# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Budget & Bidirectional DShot600 Allocation

**Design Review Package, Job #33, Rev A (pre-layout)**

---

## 0. Architecture Assumptions

Several points in the brief need clarifying before any numbers are useful.

1. **The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In every production 4-in-1 stack, the H743 runs ArduPilot and emits DShot600. Each ESC channel has its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071, plus a 3-phase gate driver. So the 48 kHz figure is the **ESC PWM frequency**, set in AM32 firmware and not by ArduPilot.
2. **"Nano" means a 20×20 mm mounting pattern** (about 25×25 mm board outline). The thermal analysis below shows that **80 A per motor is a transient (~1–3 s) rating at this size, not a continuous one.** I size the design honestly against that.
3. **The electrical operating point** is 6S LiPo (25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal), trapezoidal 6-step commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM, and one MOSFET per switch position (24 FETs total).

**Representative MOSFET** (a 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN class part; substitute datasheet values before release):

| Parameter | Symbol | Value used |
|---|---|---|
| R_DS(on) @ 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | R₂₅ | 1.0 mΩ |
| Temperature coefficient (125 °C / 25 °C) | k_T | 1.6 |
| R_DS(on) @ T_j = 125 °C | R_hot | 1.6 mΩ |
| Gate charge | Q_g | 40 nC |
| Miller charge | Q_gd | 6 nC |
| Output charge @ 25 V | Q_oss | 30 nC |
| Body-diode reverse recovery | Q_rr | 30 nC |
| Body-diode forward drop @ 80 A | V_F | 0.8 V |
| Junction-to-case thermal resistance | R_θJC | 1.5 K/W |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology

In 6-step operation, two phases conduct at any instant. Phase A high-side (Q_AH) PWMs at duty D, and phase B low-side (Q_BL) is held ON. During the PWM off-time, Q_AL is switched on as the synchronous rectifier.

| Device | Conducts for | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| Q_AH | D·T | D · I² · R |
| Q_AL (sync rect.) | (1−D)·T | (1−D) · I² · R |
| Q_BL | always | I² · R |

The duty terms cancel, so total conduction loss is **independent of duty cycle** with synchronous rectification:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{hot}\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I^2 R_{hot}$$

### 1.2 At 80 A burst

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.6\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{20.5\ W\ per\ motor}$$

### 1.3 PCB copper (not optional at 80 A)

The current path runs from the battery pad, through the planes and FETs, to the motor pad. Assume a 4-layer stack with 2 oz copper and stitched planes, giving about 0.5 mΩ effective per motor loop:

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 3.2\ \text{W per motor}$$

### 1.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

Assume t_dead = 100 ns, with two transitions per PWM cycle:

$$P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dead} f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 2(100\text{n})(48\text{k}) = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the active switch (Q_AH) hard-switches. The sync FET commutates under near-zero voltage, because its body diode is already conducting.

### 2.1 V·I overlap

Gate driver sink/source current is about 1 A at the Miller plateau, so t ≈ Q_gd / I_g ≈ 6 ns intrinsic. Allowing for gate resistance and loop inductance, use t_r + t_f ≈ 30 ns:

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Capacitive, recovery and gate terms

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Output capacitance | Q_oss · V · f | 30n × 25.2 × 48k = 0.036 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f | 30n × 25.2 × 48k = 0.036 W |
| Gate drive (2 switching FETs) | 2 · Q_g · V_GS · f | 2 × 40n × 10 × 48k = 0.038 W (dissipated in the driver/R_g) |

### 2.3 Frequency sensitivity

$$P_{sw}(f) \approx 1.56\text{ W} \times \frac{f}{48\text{ kHz}}$$

At 96 kHz the total rises by about +1.5 W per motor. 48 kHz is a reasonable trade between loss and audible noise/current ripple on low-inductance motors.

## 3. Total Loss Budget at 80 A Burst

| Component | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 20.5 W | 82.0 W |
| PCB copper | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.6 W | 2.4 W |
| Switching (V·I + Q_oss + Q_rr) | 1.5 W | 6.1 W |
| Gate drive | 0.04 W | 0.15 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 25.9 W** | **≈ 103 W** |

Output power is about 4 × 80 A × ~22 V × D. At full throttle that is roughly 7 kW, so ESC efficiency is about **98.5%**. The efficiency is fine; the problem is power density, with 103 W landing on roughly 6 cm² of PCB.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Network

```
 P_FET ──[R_θJC 1.5 K/W]── Case ──[R_θCB ~2 K/W via thermal vias]── Board node ──[R_θBA]── Ambient
                                                       │
                                                  C_board ≈ 3 J/K
```

The board heat capacity comes from about 4 g of FR4, copper and components at roughly 0.75 J/(g·K).

R_θBA (board to ambient, whole board) depends on airflow:

| Condition | R_θBA |
|---|---|
| Still air | ~15 K/W |
| Bench fan | ~6 K/W |
| In-flight, prop-wash exposed | ~3–4 K/W |

### 4.2 Junction rise above board

Electrical frequency (≥1 kHz) is much faster than the FET thermal time constants, so each FET sees its time-averaged share:

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{22.7\text{ W (FET losses only)}}{6} \approx 3.8\text{ W}$$

$$\Delta T_{JB} = 3.8 \times (1.5 + 2) \approx 13\text{ K}$$

### 4.3 Burst duration (adiabatic approximation)

All four motors are at 80 A, the ambient (in the frame) is 40 °C, and the junction design limit is 125 °C.
