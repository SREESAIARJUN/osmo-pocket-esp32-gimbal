## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path in six-step synchronous commutation

In any commutation sector, current flows through exactly two phase legs:

- **Leg A (PWM leg):** the high-side conducts for duty D. The synchronous low-side conducts for (1−D), minus dead time.
- **Leg B (static leg):** the low-side conducts for 100% of the sector.

Two MOSFET channels are therefore always in series with the motor current:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \left[ D\,R_{HS} + (1-D)R_{LS,sync} + R_{LS,static} \right] \approx 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

This approximation assumes matched FETs. It is independent of D, which is the key insight: full-throttle and part-throttle conduction losses are equal at equal current.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.004\text{–}0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C with α = 0.005: R = 1.0 mΩ × 1.5 = **1.5 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A per channel

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond / channel | P_cond ×4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.0 mΩ | 2 × 6400 × 1.0e-3 = **12.8 W** | 51.2 W |
| 100 °C | 1.375 mΩ | **17.6 W** | 70.4 W |
| 125 °C | 1.5 mΩ | **19.2 W** | 76.8 W |

Per-FET worst case is the static low-side: 6400 × 1.5 mΩ = **9.6 W** during its sector.

The low-side conducts 1/3 of the electrical cycle statically, plus sync time. Averaged per FET over a cycle, this gives about 3.2 W static plus a sync contribution. The loss is distributed, but with peaks per sector at eRPM frequency, which the FET's thermal mass filters out.

### 1.4 Additional series resistances (often dominant on nano boards)

$$P_{extra} = I^2\,(R_{shunt} + R_{trace} + R_{solder})$$

| Element | Assumed | Loss @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Shunt (per channel) | 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| Phase/bus copper, 2 oz, ~15 mm × 4 mm equivalent | ≈0.3 mΩ ×2 paths | 3.8 W |
| Pads, vias, solder | ≈0.1 mΩ | 0.64 W |

Copper resistance on a 20×20 board is comparable to the FETs. Model it explicitly using:

$$R_{trace} = \frac{\rho_{Cu}(T)\,L}{w\,t},\quad \rho_{Cu} = 1.72\times10^{-8}\,[1+0.00393(T-20)]\ \Omega\text{m}$$

For 2 oz copper (t = 70 µm), w = 4 mm, and L = 15 mm: R ≈ 0.92 mΩ per single layer. Paralleling 3 layers with via stitching gives ≈0.31 mΩ.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Per channel, per switching period:

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap (high-side)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I_{ph}\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

The transition times follow from gate charge and driver current:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd} + Q_{gs2}}{I_{g,on}},\quad I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}{R_{g,ext} + R_{g,int} + R_{drv,on}}$$

With V_drv = 12 V, V_pl = 3.5 V, and R_total = 10 Ω: I_g ≈ 0.85 A. With Q_gd + Q_gs2 ≈ 17 nC, t_r ≈ 20 ns. Take t_f ≈ 20 ns.

$$P_{VI} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{e-}9 \times 48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Body-diode dead-time conduction

$$P_{DT} = V_F\,I_{ph}\,(2\,t_{dead})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{e-}9 \times 48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

This assumes t_dead = 100 ns per edge. Minimize dead time to the shoot-through-safe value; the term scales linearly with it.

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 40\text{e-}9 \times 25.2 \times 48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

Q_rr increases with di/dt and T_j. At 80 A, use the datasheet value at the actual di/dt.

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12\,Q_{oss}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw}\times 2 = 30\text{e-}9 \times 25.2 \times 48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{0.036\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET)

$$P_{gate} = 2\,Q_g\,V_{drv}\,f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{e-}9 \times 12 \times 48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{0.058\ W}$$

### 2.6 Switching summary per channel at 80 A

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| V–I overlap | 1.94 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.08 |
| Gate (driver side) | 0.06 |
| **Total** | **≈2.7 W** |

**Frequency sensitivity:** ∂P_sw/∂f_sw ≈ 56 µW/Hz. Dropping to 24 kHz saves ~1.3 W per channel. That is about 6% of total loss, so 48 kHz is a reasonable trade for current-ripple and acoustic benefit. Conduction dominates.
