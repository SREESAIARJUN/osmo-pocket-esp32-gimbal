# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #30): System Architecture and Mathematical Models

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFET bridges directly. It runs ArduPilot as the flight controller and sends DShot600 to four ESC-side MCUs, such as the STM32G071 or AT32F421 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Those ESC MCUs generate the 48 kHz 6-step PWM. This split is how every production 4-in-1 stack works. It also matters for timing: the H743 cannot also run 4× FOC/6-step commutation loops at 48 kHz while ArduPilot runs its own scheduler.

> **Reality check, stated up front.** 80 A per channel on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board is a burst-only rating. The model below shows about 70 W dissipated board-wide at 80 A on all four channels. That is only survivable as a short transient, and only with heavy copper. Treat the "80A" label as a *thermal-capacity-limited burst*, not a continuous rating.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (6-Step, Synchronous Rectification)

### 1.1 Current Topology per Commutation Sector
In each 60° sector two legs are active:
- **Leg A (PWM leg):** the high-side (HS) conducts for fraction D. The complementary low-side (LS) conducts synchronously for fraction (1−D).
- **Leg B (return leg):** the LS is fully on for 100% of the sector.

Summing I²R over the sector gives the total MOSFET conduction loss per ESC:

$$
P_{cond,FET} = I_{ph}^2 R_{DS,hot}\big[D + (1-D) + 1\big] = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS,hot}
$$

This result is independent of duty cycle. The two current paths are always resistive, which is the key benefit of synchronous rectification.

### 1.2 Temperature-Dependent R_DS(on)
$$
R_{DS,hot} = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

| T_j | Factor | R_DS,hot |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 | 0.70 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 1.45 | 1.015 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 1.60 | 1.12 mΩ |

### 1.3 Numerical Result (T_j = 125 °C, worst case)
$$
P_{cond,FET} = 2(80)^2(1.12\times10^{-3}) = 14.34\ \text{W per ESC}
$$

**Copper paths** (two conducting paths):
$$
P_{cu} = 2\,I^2 R_{cu} = 2(6400)(0.30\times10^{-3}) = 3.84\ \text{W}
$$

**Shunt.** The DC-link shunt carries I_ph only during the D interval. Worst case is D → 1:
$$
P_{sh} = D\,I^2 R_{sh} = 6400(0.25\times10^{-3}) = 1.60\ \text{W}
$$

### 1.4 Per-Device Distribution (Hot-Spot FET)
Each FET is active for 120° of the electrical cycle.

Worst device: a LS that spends one sector as the 100% return path and one sector as the synchronous rectifier at (1−D):
$$
P_{LS,avg} = \tfrac{1}{6}I^2R\,[1 + (1-D)]
$$

At D = 0.5:
$$
P_{LS,avg} = \tfrac{1}{6}(7.17)(1.5) = 1.79\ \text{W}
$$

The instantaneous peak is I²R = 7.17 W. The die thermal time constant (around 1–5 ms) is longer than the electrical period at typical eRPM. Average power therefore governs T_j, plus a small ripple term ΔT_ripple ≈ P_pk·Z_th(t_sector).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only Leg A hard-switches in each sector. One hard-switched leg is active per ESC at any instant.

### 2.1 Transition Times (Miller Plateau Dominated)
$$
t_r \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_g} = \frac{15\ \text{nC}}{1.5\ \text{A}} = 10\ \text{ns}
$$

Add 5 ns each for loop inductance and ringing, giving **t_r + t_f ≈ 30 ns**.

### 2.2 Loss Components

**(a) V-I overlap (hard-switched HS)**
$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = \tfrac12(25.2)(80)(30\text{n})(48\text{k}) = 1.45\ \text{W}
$$

**(b) Output capacitance**
$$
P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = (40\text{n})(25.2)(48\text{k}) = 0.048\ \text{W}
$$

**(c) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead-time windows per PWM cycle)
$$
P_{dt} = 2\,V_f I_{ph} t_{dt} f_{sw} = 2(0.8)(80)(50\text{n})(48\text{k}) = 0.307\ \text{W}
$$

**(d) Reverse recovery** (LS body diode recovered by HS turn-on)
$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = (50\text{n})(25.2)(48\text{k}) = 0.060\ \text{W}
$$

**(e) Gate drive** (HS + LS of the PWM leg, dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g)
$$
P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2(60\text{n})(10)(48\text{k}) = 0.058\ \text{W}
$$
