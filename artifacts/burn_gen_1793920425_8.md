## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Architectural Clarifications

**The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production-grade design, the H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. Each motor channel is driven by a dedicated ESC MCU, typically an AT32F421, STM32G071, or EFM8 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Bidirectional DShot is the link *between* those two processors. If the H743 drove the gates directly, DShot would be unnecessary, and ArduPilot has no native 4-channel FOC/6-step motor-drive stack. This document therefore assumes:

> **H743 FC → (4× bidirectional DShot600) → 4× AT32F421 ESC MCUs → 4× 3-phase gate drivers → 24 MOSFETs**, all on one stacked or AIO nano PCB.

**80A burst on a nano board is a transient rating, not a continuous one.** Section 3 shows that the board cannot dissipate the steady-state losses. The 80A figure must be specified with a duration, which this document derives as approximately 3–5 s.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation topology

The model assumes six-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM). At any instant, exactly one high-side and one low-side MOSFET carry the phase current I, in series. During the dead time, the body diode substitutes for the synchronous FET.

Per electrical cycle, each phase leg is:
- High side for 120°, PWM at duty D. Complementary low-side sync conducts (1−D).
- Low side for 120°, fully on.
- Floating for 120°.

### 1.2 Per-MOSFET RMS current

$$
I_{rms,HS} = I\sqrt{\tfrac{D}{3}},\qquad
I_{rms,LS} = I\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{3} + \tfrac{1-D}{3}}
$$

### 1.3 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right]
$$

At T_j = 100 °C: R_DS = 1.0 × (1 + 0.006 × 75) = **1.45 mΩ**.

### 1.4 Channel conduction loss

Because two FETs are always in series, the D-terms cancel:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) + I^2 R_{Cu}
$$

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2(80)^2(1.45\text{ m}\Omega) + (80)^2(0.3\text{ m}\Omega) = 18.56 + 1.92 = \mathbf{20.5\ W}
$$

**The model is self-heating and must be iterated.** R_DS depends on T_j, which depends on P. Solve $T_j = T_{ref} + Z_{th}\,P(T_j)$ numerically. Section 3 uses the 100 °C value as the mid-burst average.

**Worst single FET.** The high-side FET dissipates its peak instantaneous loss while conducting: 80² × 1.45 mΩ = 9.3 W during its on-interval.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing high-side FET in each channel hard-switches. The sync FET switches at near-zero voltage because the body diode is already conducting.

### 2.1 Loss terms

| Term | Equation | Value per channel |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·40 ns·48 k | **1.94 W** |
| Coss | ½·C_oss·V²·f_sw = ½·1.5 nF·635·48 k | 0.023 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 50 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.060 W |
| Dead-time diode | V_SD·I·2t_dead·f_sw = 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k | **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw | 0.058 W (dissipated in driver/R_g) |
| **Total** | | **≈ 2.7 W** |

### 2.2 Design notes

- **The loss peaks at mid-throttle.** At D = 1.0 (true full throttle), PWM switching stops and P_sw drops to near zero. A burst at D ≈ 0.85–0.95 is the true worst case, so 2.7 W is used.
- **Dead-time loss is about 30% of the switching total.** Tightening t_dead from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel. Verify shoot-through margin with a scope before doing so.
- **Raising f_sw to 96 kHz** doubles the switching loss to about 5.4 W per channel but reduces current ripple. 48 kHz is a reasonable compromise for low-inductance racing motors.

### 2.3 Bulk capacitor ripple

This is the most commonly missed failure mode in 4-in-1 designs.

$$
I_{C,rms} \approx I\sqrt{D(1-D)} \;\Rightarrow\; 40\text{ A per channel at } D = 0.5
$$

With four channels at random phase alignment, the combined ripple is approximately √4 × 40 ≈ **80 A_rms worst case**. Mitigations:
- Ceramic MLCCs local to each leg: ≥ 4 × 10 µF/50 V X7R per channel.
- Low-ESR bulk capacitor on the battery pads.
- Low pack lead inductance.
