| Issue | Finding | Decision |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

**Temperature-dependent R_ds(on):**

R_ds(T_j) = R_ds,25 · [1 + α(T_j − 25)]

At T_j = 125 °C, R_ds = 1.0 mΩ × 1.5 = **1.5 mΩ**.

**Per commutation interval (one ESC channel).** In each 60° sector, one leg is PWM'd and one low-side FET is held on:

- The PWM leg's high-side FET conducts for a fraction D: I²·R·D.
- The PWM leg's low-side FET (synchronous) conducts for 1−D: I²·R·(1−D).
- The static low-side FET of the return leg conducts for the whole sector: I²·R.

So the conduction loss per channel is:

**P_cond,ch = I²·R_ds(T_j)·[D + (1−D) + 1] = 2·I²·R_ds(T_j)**

This is independent of D. That is why burst current, not throttle, sets the heat.

At 80 A and 125 °C:

P_cond,ch = 2 × 6400 × 1.5×10⁻³ = **19.2 W**

**Per FET.** Each FET carries current for 1/3 of the electrical cycle:

P_FET,avg ≈ 19.2 / 6 × (6/… ) → **≈ 3.2 W average per FET**, with 9.6 W instantaneous while conducting.

**Copper loss.** Plan 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner layers. Sheet resistance is about 0.25 mΩ/□ per 2 oz layer, so two layers in parallel give 0.125 mΩ/□. A typical phase path of about 4 squares gives:

R_Cu ≈ 0.5 mΩ, and P_Cu = 2·I²·R_Cu ≈ **6.4 W per channel**

Copper loss is not negligible. Use 3–4 paralleled layers on the power paths.
