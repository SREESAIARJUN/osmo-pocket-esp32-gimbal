# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #20): Power-Stage, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

## 0. Assumptions and Key Findings

**Assumed architecture.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It cannot also commutate four BLDC motors in real time. Each ESC channel therefore has its own commutation MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071, running AM32). The H743 drives each ESC over bi-directional DShot600. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs, not the H743.

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max (22.2 V nominal) |
| Burst current | 80 A per channel, ≤ 5 s |
| PWM | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous rectification), 6-step trapezoidal |
| Form factor | 30.5 × 30.5 mm stack, 20 × 20 mm mounting |
| MOSFET (assumed, 40 V, PDFN 3.3×3.3 class) | R_DS(on) = 1.2 mΩ max at 25 °C (V_GS = 10 V); Q_g = 40 nC; Q_gs2 = 4 nC; Q_gd = 8 nC; V_plateau ≈ 3.0 V; C_oss(er) ≈ 1.2 nF; Q_rr ≈ 40 nC; R_θJC = 1.0 K/W |
| Gate driver (assumed) | FD6288Q-class 3-phase driver, V_DRV = 10 V |

Replace the assumed MOSFET values with your chosen part's datasheet numbers. All equations are written parametrically so you can re-run them.

**Key findings:**

1. **80 A burst is feasible only as a transient.** Each channel dissipates about 28 W at 80 A, so all four channels together produce about 113 W. The board's thermal mass limits this to roughly 3–5 seconds.
2. **Continuous capability is about 25–35 A per channel** in forced airflow. A nano board cannot reject 100 W or more in steady state. Market the board as "35 A continuous / 80 A burst (5 s)" or similar.
3. **The H743's DMAMUX removes the classic F4/F7 DMA-conflict problem.** Bidirectional DShot on four motors fits comfortably, with streams to spare.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation, two phases conduct during each 60° sector:

- **High-side leg:** the PWM'd high-side FET conducts for duty D. Its complementary low-side FET (synchronous rectification) conducts for 1 − D.
- **Low-side leg:** the low-side FET of the return phase is fully on for the whole sector.

So at any instant, the motor current I flows through exactly **two channel resistances in series**, independent of D (dead time excluded).

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

R_DS(on)(T_j) = R_DS(on),25 · [1 + α (T_j − 25)], with α ≈ 0.005/K for 40 V trench FETs.

| T_j | R_DS(on) |
|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.20 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 1.65 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 1.80 mΩ |

### 1.3 Conduction loss per ESC channel

P_cond = I² · 2 · R_DS(on)(T_j) + I² · R_Cu

PCB copper path resistance (2 oz outer + 2 oz inner, battery pad to phase pad): assume R_Cu ≈ 0.5 mΩ.

| Condition | MOSFET loss | Copper loss | Total per channel |
|---|---|---|---|
| 80 A, T_j = 25 °C | 6400 × 2.4 mΩ = 15.4 W | 3.2 W | 18.6 W |
| 80 A, T_j = 125 °C | 6400 × 3.6 mΩ = **23.0 W** | 3.2 W | **26.2 W** |
| 35 A, T_j = 100 °C | 1225 × 3.3 mΩ = 4.0 W | 0.6 W | 4.6 W |

**Per-FET split at 80 A and 125 °C:**

- The fully-on low-side FET takes the worst case: 6400 × 1.8 mΩ × (1/3 electrical period) ≈ 3.84 W averaged.
- The peak instantaneous loss is 11.5 W in a single 3.3 × 3.3 mm package during its conducting sector.
- Electrical frequency is about 1–3 kHz, which is far faster than the junction-to-case thermal time constant (≈ ms). The junction therefore sees the period-averaged loss, plus a small ripple.

**Averaged per-FET loss:** each of the 6 FETs conducts for 2/3 of the electrical cycle (as high-side or low-side).

P_FET,avg ≈ 23.0 W / 6 ≈ **3.8 W per FET**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Gate drive currents

Total gate resistance R_G,tot = R_driver + R_G,ext + R_G,int ≈ 5 Ω.

- Turn-on: I_G,on = (V_DRV − V_pl) / R_G,tot = (10 − 3) / 5 = **1.4 A**
- Turn-off: I_G,off = V_pl / R_G,tot = 3 / 5 = **0.6 A**

### 2.2 Transition times

- Turn-on: t_on = (Q_gs2 + Q_gd) / I_G,on = 12 nC / 1.4 A = **8.6 ns**
- Turn-off: t_off = (Q_gs2 + Q_gd) / I_G,off = 12 nC / 0.6 A = **20 ns**

### 2.3 Loss components (only the PWM'd half-bridge switches)

| Component | Equation | Value at 80 A, 25.2 V, 48 kHz |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_on + t_off) · f_sw | ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 28.6 ns × 48 k = **1.38 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw | 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.05 W** |
| C_oss energy | C_oss(er) · V² · f_sw | 1.2 nF × 635 × 48 k = **0.04 W** |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_F · I · 2 t_dt · f_sw | 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (2 switching FETs; lost in driver/R_G) | 2 · Q_g · V_DRV · f_sw | 2 × 40 nC × 10 × 48 k = **0.04 W** |
| **Total switching (worst-case D)** | | **≈ 2.1 W per channel** |

### 2.4 Notes on the switching model

- **Dead time t_dt = 100 ns is assumed.** Reducing it to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W, but the driver propagation-delay mismatch must be verified first.
- **Overlap loss is roughly independent of D** for D in (0, 1). At D = 100% (full throttle) there is no switching, and switching loss collapses to about 0.
- **Effect of PWM frequency.** At 24 kHz, switching loss would be about 1.05 W. 48 kHz reduces motor current ripple and audible noise, at a cost of about 1 W per channel.

### 2.5 Bulk capacitor ripple current

Use I_C,rms = I · √(D(1 − D)), which peaks at **40 A rms** at D = 0.5 and 80 A.

This requires:

- Low-ESR bulk capacitance on the board, e.g. 4 × 220 µF/35 V polymer capacitors, about 3 A rms each.
- 6–8 × 10 µF/50 V X7R ceramics per channel, placed directly at the half-bridge.
- An external 1000 µF low-ESR electrolytic on the battery leads, which is mandatory.

| Conduction (FET) | 23.0 W | 4.0 W |
