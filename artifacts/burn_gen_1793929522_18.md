# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #29): Power-Stage, Thermal & Bidirectional DShot Architecture

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Feasibility Findings

These are the baseline assumptions. If any differ from your actual design, the numbers below should be recomputed.

| Parameter | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal |
| Form factor | 20×20 mm (M3/M2 "nano"), 6-layer, 2 oz copper on every layer |
| Topology | H743 flight controller (FC) board, stacked on a 4-in-1 ESC board |
| ESC MCU | 4× AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 (BLHeli_32-class) |
| Gate driver | FD6288Q-class 3-phase bootstrap driver, one per motor |
| MOSFETs | 40 V N-ch in DFN3.3×3.3, R_DS(on) = 1.0 mΩ at 25 °C / V_GS = 10 V, Q_g ≈ 60 nC |
| Motor | 14-pole, 6-step trapezoidal drive, complementary (synchronous) PWM |
| PWM frequency | 48 kHz |
| Burst current | 80 A per motor |

**Architectural clarification:** the H743 does not run the ESC. The H743 produces the DShot600 frames and captures the bidirectional telemetry. The ESC MCUs commutate the motors. Section 3 covers the H743 side; Sections 1–2 cover the ESC power stage.

**Key finding (developed below):** an 80 A × 4 simultaneous burst on a 20×20 board is thermally viable for only about 1–1.5 s. The sustainable continuous rating with prop-wash cooling is about 28–30 A per motor. Rate the product as "30 A continuous / 80 A burst ≤1 s, single-motor transient", or move to a 30×30 board with 5×6 mm FETs if 80 A must be sustained for longer.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At $T_j = 125\,°C$: $R_{hot} = 1.0 \times 1.5 = 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega$. Use the worst-case datasheet maximum in production. For a 1.0 mΩ typical part this is often around 1.3 mΩ at 25 °C, so scale accordingly.

### 1.2 Six-step synchronous commutation

In any 60° sector, current I flows through two phases:

- **PWM'd phase:** the high-side FET conducts for D·T and the low-side (synchronous) FET for (1−D)·T.
- **Fixed phase:** the low-side FET is on for the entire sector.

The current is always through exactly two R_DS(on) in series, independent of duty cycle (dead time ignored here; added in §2):

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{hot}$$

$$P_{cond,motor}(80\text{A}) = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$
