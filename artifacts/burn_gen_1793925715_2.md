# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #55): Power-Stage Thermal Model, Loss Derivation, and Bi-Directional DShot600 DMA Allocation

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Flags

**Who does what.** In an ArduPilot system the STM32H743 is the flight controller. It generates DShot600 frames and decodes bi-directional (eRPM) telemetry. The 48 kHz commutation PWM runs on each ESC's own MCU, typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The loss model below therefore applies to the ESC power stage, and the DMA model applies to the H743. Merging both onto one H743 is not an ArduPilot-supported topology.

**Feasibility flags.** Before layout begins, these need management sign-off:

1. **80 A per channel on a "nano" board is a burst-only rating.** At 80 A the board dissipates about 62 W, which a 20×20 or 30×30 mm PCB cannot reject continuously. I model it as a time-limited burst (Section 3.4).
2. **Input capacitor ripple at 80 A is about 40 A RMS.** This, more than the FETs, usually kills small 4-in-1 boards.
3. **All component values below are reference assumptions.** Replace them with your selected parts' datasheet values, especially R_DS(on)(T), Q_gd, Q_rr, and Z_th(j-c).

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

In 6-step trapezoidal (or FOC near peak), current flows at any instant through **one high-side path and one low-side path**. With synchronous rectification, the conducting leg alternates HS ↔ LS. That gives:

$$
I_{HS,rms} = I\sqrt{D}, \qquad I_{LS,rms} = I\sqrt{1-D}
$$

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

For one active half-bridge:

$$
P_{cond,leg} = I^2 R_{hot}\,D + I^2 R_{hot}\,(1-D) = I^2 R_{hot}
$$

The result is independent of D. The return leg contributes the same, so:

$$
P_{cond} = 2\,I^2 R_{hot} = 2(80)^2(1.05\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{13.44\ W}
$$

That is about 6.7 W per conducting FET pair-equivalent.

**Thermal-runaway coupling.** R_hot depends on T_j. The full expression is:

$$
R(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha (T_j-25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ /^\circ\mathrm{C}
$$

Solve iteratively with Section 3: T_j = T_pcb + P·Rθ_jc, where P depends on R(T_j).

### 2.2 Hard-Switching Loss (V–I overlap)

Only the actively switched FET incurs overlap loss. The synchronous FET switches at near-zero voltage after dead time.

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = \tfrac12(25.2)(80)(30\,\mathrm{ns})(48\,\mathrm{kHz}) = \mathbf{1.45\ W}
$$

**Rise/fall time derivation (Miller plateau).** The switching time is set by gate current through the Miller charge:

$$
t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{(V_{drv}-V_{plateau})/R_g}
$$

For example, Q_gd = 12 nC, V_drv = 10 V, V_pl = 4.5 V, and R_g,total = 6.9 Ω give t_r ≈ 15 ns.

### 2.3 Capacitive (C_oss) Loss

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5(1.5\,\mathrm{nF})(25.2)^2(48\,\mathrm{k}) = \mathbf{0.023\ W}
$$

### 2.4 Reverse-Recovery Loss

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = (50\,\mathrm{nC})(25.2)(48\,\mathrm{k}) = \mathbf{0.060\ W}
$$

The Q_rr current is also added to the HS FET turn-on loss. This is included in the margin.

### 2.5 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_f\, I\,(2t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(200\,\mathrm{ns})(48\,\mathrm{k}) = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

This term matters. Reducing t_dt to 50 ns halves it, but only if the driver's propagation delay matching allows it.

### 2.6 Gate-Drive Loss

This loss is dissipated in the driver and R_g, not in the FET junction.

$$
P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times N_{switching} = (60\,\mathrm{nC})(10)(48\,\mathrm{k})\times 2 = \mathbf{0.058\ W}
$$

### 2.7 Copper / Shunt Loss

Assume 0.5 mΩ trace plus a 0.5 mΩ shunt in the current path:

$$
P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400(1.0\,\mathrm{m\Omega}) = \mathbf{6.4\ W}
$$

This loss is often overlooked. It is why 2–3 oz outer copper is mandatory.

### 2.8 Budget Summary at 80 A Burst

| Loss term | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 13.44 W | 53.8 W |
| Switching overlap | 1.45 W | 5.8 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W | 2.4 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.08 W | 0.3 W |
| Gate drive | 0.06 W | 0.2 W |
| **FET + driver subtotal** | **15.6 W** | **62.5 W** |
| PCB copper + shunt | 6.4 W | 25.6 W |
| **Total board** | **22.0 W** | **~88 W** |

Efficiency at burst is P_out ≈ 25.2 × 80 × 4 ≈ 8 kW electrical in, so η ≈ 98.9 %. The efficiency is fine; the problem is power density.

**Optimal f_sw sanity check.** Switching-dependent loss (Sections 2.2–2.6) is about 2.2 W versus 13.4 W conduction. At 48 kHz, the stage is conduction-dominated. Lowering to 24 kHz saves only about 1.1 W per channel but increases ripple current and acoustic noise, so 48 kHz is justified.
