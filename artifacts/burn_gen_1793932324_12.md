# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Design Basis (Job #16)

**Scope:** Thermal and loss model for an 80 A burst per channel, 48 kHz switching, ArduPilot bi-directional DShot600 timer and DMA allocation, reference netlists, and thermal budget.

> **Architect's caveat up front:** The math below shows that **80 A burst per channel on a true "nano" (20×20 mm) 4-in-1 is only thermally viable for about 1 second**, even with good FETs. Before layout freeze, either redefine "burst" as ≤1 s or move to a 30×30 mm power stage. The derivation is shown so the program can make that call with numbers.

---

## 1. System Architecture

**Partitioning.** The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs directly. Commutation runs on four dedicated ESC MCUs running AM32 with bidirectional DShot and 48 kHz PWM.

```
6S LiPo (25.2 V max) ──► Power Stage (4× 3-phase bridges, 24 FETs)
                             ▲ gate drive (4× 3-ph driver)
                             │
                      4× ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32)
                             ▲ bidirectional DShot600 (1 wire each)
                             │
                      STM32H743VI (ArduPilot) ── IMU, baro, OSD, UARTs
```

Each ESC MCU runs complementary (synchronous) PWM with dead-time and back-EMF zero-cross commutation.

## 3. Conduction Loss Model

### 3.1 Temperature-corrected R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.0 mΩ × 1.45 = **1.45 mΩ**.

### 3.2 Current path per commutation step

In 6-step trapezoidal control, two phases conduct at any instant:

- **PWM leg:** current flows through the HS FET for D·T and the LS FET (synchronous rectification) for (1−D)·T. Either way, exactly one R_DS is in the path.
- **Sink leg:** the LS FET is on continuously.

So exactly two R_DS(on) are always in series, independent of duty:

$$
P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) = 2(80)^2(1.45\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{18.6\ W}
$$

### 3.3 Per-FET peak dissipation

Each FET conducts during roughly 1/3 of the electrical cycle at full current:

$$
P_{FET,inst} = I^2 R_{DS} = 9.3\ \text{W}, \qquad \bar P_{FET} \approx \tfrac{2\cdot 18.6}{6}\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}\ \text{(cycle-avg)} \approx 3.1\ \text{W}
$$

At electrical frequencies above about 100 Hz, which covers every flight regime, the die thermal time constant (~ms) averages this ripple. Use **3.1 W average per FET** with a small ripple term.

### 3.4 PCB copper loss (often ignored, should not be)

Assume a 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner paralleled bus, with an effective path of 15 mm × 6 mm × 4 layers × 70 µm:

$$
R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{A} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot 0.015}{6\times10^{-3}\cdot 4\cdot 70\times10^{-6}} \approx 0.15\ \text{mΩ (per path, @25 °C)}
$$

This is about 0.2 mΩ hot, giving ≈ **2.6 W per ESC** (two paths), plus the shared battery bus. The shared bus carries 4×80 = 320 A:

$$
P_{bus} = (320)^2 \cdot R_{bus}
$$

Every 0.05 mΩ of shared bus costs **5.1 W**. The battery pads must be large and heavily via-stitched.

## 4. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 4.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap (HS FET of the PWM leg)

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = \tfrac12(25.2)(80)(24\times10^{-9})(48\times10^3) = \mathbf{1.16\ W}
$$

### 4.2 Reverse recovery (LS body diode)

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}
$$

The induced HS turn-on current spike adds a similar order of magnitude. Budget **0.1 W**.

### 4.3 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.015\ \text{W}
$$

### 4.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_F\, I\,(2t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot 80\cdot 200\text{n}\cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

This term is the strongest argument for minimizing dead time. At 50 ns, it halves.

### 4.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET.

$$
P_{g} = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw}\times 2\ \text{(HS+LS)} = 40\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k}\cdot 2 = 0.038\ \text{W}
$$

### 4.6 Switching subtotal

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| V·I overlap | 1.16 |
| Q_rr (incl. spike) | 0.10 |
| Q_oss | 0.02 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Gate drive | 0.04 |
| **Total switching** | **1.93** |

**Sensitivity:** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it is about 0.97 W. Doubling to 96 kHz would add about 2 W per ESC.
