## 0. Architecture Decision

The STM32H743 should **not** commutate the motors directly. ArduPilot has no in-firmware BLDC commutation, and the H743 doesn't have enough advanced timers for 4×3 complementary phases with independent dead-time.

The standard and correct partition is:

```
┌────────────────────────── FC (STM32H743, ArduPilot) ──────────────────────────┐
│  TIM1 CH1..CH4 ──bidir DShot600 (single-wire, half-duplex, 3.3V)──┐            │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┼───────────┘
                                                                     ▼
┌──────────── 4-in-1 ESC: 4× independent ESC MCUs (AM32 / BLHeli_32-class) ──────┐
│  ESC MCU (e.g. AT32F421 / STM32G071) → 3-phase gate driver → 6× N-MOSFET       │
│  48 kHz center-aligned complementary PWM, sync rectification, BEMF ZC sense    │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**"Nano" vs 80A [VERIFY your target].** An 80A-burst-per-channel 4-in-1 realistically needs a 30.5×30.5 mm board with 5×6 mm PDFN FETs and 2–3 oz copper. A 20×20 "nano" footprint cannot sink the energy computed below for more than ~1–2 s. I model the 30.5 mm case and give the scaling law for smaller boards.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

With 6-step trapezoidal commutation and complementary (synchronous) PWM, current always flows through exactly two FET channels:

- one high-side or its complementary low-side in the PWM leg, and
- one fully-on low-side in the return leg.

This is duty-cycle independent, because either the HS or the synchronous LS is conducting in the PWM leg (ignoring dead time, handled in §2).

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot \left[2R_{ds}(T_j) + R_{cu}\right]$$

### 1.2 Temperature feedback

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1+\alpha (T_j-25)\right]$$

At T_j = 110°C: R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.006 × 85) = **1.51 mΩ**

$$P_{cond,ch} = 80^2 \times (2 \times 1.51 + 0.5)\,\text{m}\Omega = 6400 \times 3.52\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{22.5\ W}$$

Split:
- FETs: 19.3 W
- Copper: 3.2 W (this lands in the board, not the junctions)

### 1.3 Per-FET share

Over one electrical revolution, each of the 6 FETs carries current for 1/3 of the time:

$$P_{FET,avg} = \frac{2I^2R_{ds}}{6} = \frac{I^2R_{ds}}{3} = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

At low eRPM (e.g. the start of a punch-out), a single commutation step can dwell for milliseconds. Use the instantaneous worst-case value, **I²R_ds = 9.7 W in one FET**, for the junction transient check (§4.3).

### 1.4 Thermal-runaway stability criterion

Conduction loss is stable only if the loop gain is below 1:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\cdot R_{th,ja} = 2I^2R_{ds,25}\,\alpha\, R_{th,ja} < 1$$

With 2 × 6400 × 1e-3 × 0.006 = 0.077 W/K, this requires **R_th,ja < 13 K/W per channel**. This is easily met in a burst (thermal-mass limited) but sets the continuous rating.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches; the return-leg low-side is static during a step.

### 2.1 V–I overlap

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

The synchronous LS body diode conducts during dead time, then recovers when the HS turns on:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM period:

$$P_{dt} = 2V_{SD} I\, t_{dt} f_{sw} = 2\times0.8\times80\times100\text{n}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated in the driver and R_g, not the FETs:

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\times30\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k} = 0.029\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Frequency sensitivity

Frequency-dependent loss coefficient:

$$\frac{dP}{df} = \frac{1.94 + 0.61 + 0.08}{48\text{k}} \approx 55\ \mu\text{W/Hz}$$

- Dropping to 24 kHz saves ~1.3 W per channel.
- Going to 96 kHz costs ~2.6 W per channel.

48 kHz is a good compromise: it gives low current ripple and quiet audio on low-inductance racing motors.
