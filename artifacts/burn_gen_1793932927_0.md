# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Architecture

## 0. Assumptions and Design Review Findings

**Architectural correction.** The STM32H743 does not generate the 48 kHz phase PWM. In ArduPilot systems:

```
STM32H743 (FC, ArduPilot)  --DShot600 bidir, 4 lines-->  ESC MCU (AM32 / BLHeli_32 / Bluejay)
                                                         └─> gate drivers └─> 12 MOSFETs (3 half-bridges × 4)
```

- The **48 kHz switching loss model** applies to the ESC power stage, which the ESC MCU controls.
- The **DMA/timer model** applies to the H743.

I model both and define the interface between them.

**Critical finding: "80 A per motor" on a nano (20×20 mm) 4-in-1 is a short-burst rating, not a thermal rating.** Section 3 shows the board stays within limits for only about **1–2 s** at full simultaneous 80 A burst without forced airflow. Treat the 80 A number as a sizing input to be validated, not as a guaranteed capability.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology

In 6-step synchronous PWM, two legs carry current at any instant:

- **The PWM leg.** Current alternates between the high-side FET (duty D) and the low-side FET (1−D).
- **The static leg.** One FET is fully on.

Ripple is negligible at 48 kHz with motor inductance of roughly 10–50 µH. So at any instant exactly **two channel resistances** sit in series with the phase current, independent of D:

$$
P_{cond,FET} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

Temperature dependence:

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\left[1+\alpha (T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

### 1.2 Copper and Interconnect

The current path is battery pad → bus plane → FET → phase pad → motor wire. A realistic 2 oz, 6-layer nano layout adds:

$$
R_{Cu} \approx 0.4\text{–}0.8\ \text{m}\Omega \text{ per channel path}
$$

Copper's temperature coefficient is 0.0039 K⁻¹.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A Burst (Worst Case, T_j = 125 °C, R_max)

$$
R_{DS(on)} = 1.0 \times 1.6 = 1.6\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{FET,cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.6\text{e-}3 = 20.5\ \text{W}
$$

$$
P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.6\text{e-}3 = 3.8\ \text{W}
$$

**Per channel: about 24.3 W conduction (worst case).** The typical part at T_j = 90 °C gives about 15 W.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per channel, only the PWM leg hard-switches. Each period has one turn-on and one turn-off.

### 2.1 V–I Overlap

$$
t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g} \Rightarrow \frac{15\,\text{nC}}{1\,\text{A}} = 15\ \text{ns (each edge, + ~5 ns current-rise)} \approx 20\ \text{ns}
$$

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\text{e-}9 \cdot 48\text{e}3 = 1.94\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.2 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 400\text{e-}9 \cdot 48\text{e}3 = 1.23\ \text{W}
$$

This term is often overlooked. Reducing dead time to 100 ns halves it but raises shoot-through risk.

### 2.3 Reverse Recovery, C_oss, and Gate Drive

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{e-}9 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{e}3 = 0.06\ \text{W}
$$

$$
P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \approx 0.07\ \text{W}
$$

$$
P_{gate} = 2 \cdot Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 60\text{e-}9 \cdot 10 \cdot 48\text{e}3 = 0.06\ \text{W (dissipated mostly in driver/R}_g)
$$

### 2.4 Switching Total

$$
P_{sw,ch} \approx 1.94 + 1.23 + 0.06 + 0.07 + 0.06 \approx 3.4\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.5 Frequency Sensitivity

Frequency-dependent loss scales at about 70 mW/kHz per channel at 80 A:

| f_sw | P_sw per channel |
|---|---|
| 24 kHz | 1.7 W |
| 48 kHz | 3.4 W |
| 96 kHz | 6.8 W |

At 80 A, conduction dominates (about 88 %). The 48 kHz choice is justified for low-inductance, high-KV motors, where it reduces current ripple and audible noise.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Total Dissipation (All 4 Motors at 80 A Burst, Worst Case)

| Source | Per channel | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 20.5 W | 82 W |
| Copper | 3.8 W | 15 W |
| Switching | 3.4 W | 14 W |
| **Total** | **27.7 W** | **≈111 W** |

The typical case (T_j ≈ 90 °C, typical R_DS) is about 75 W total.

### 3.2 Steady-State Check (Proves Burst-Only)

A 20×20 mm (or 25×25 mm) board with prop-wash airflow has a board-to-ambient resistance of:

$$
R_{\theta,BA} \approx 4\text{–}8\ \text{K/W (whole board)}
$$

$$
\Delta T_{ss} = 75\ \text{W} \times 4\ \text{K/W} = 300\ \text{K} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \textbf{not sustainable}
$$

Sustainable continuous current, assuming a 70 K rise budget and R_θ = 4 K/W:

$$
P_{max}=17.5\ \text{W} \Rightarrow I_{cont}\approx\sqrt{\frac{17.5/4}{2R_{DS}+R_{Cu}}}\approx\sqrt{\frac{4.4}{2.2\text{e-}3}}\approx 45\ \text{A? }
$$

That figure is too optimistic once switching loss is included and losses are applied correctly per channel. Solving numerically with switching included and typical R_DS(on) gives **I_cont ≈ 30–40 A per channel** for a nano board. That range is consistent with real-world nano 4-in-1 continuous ratings.

### 3.3 Transient (Burst) Model: Two-Node Foster/Cauer Network

```
P_j ──[Zth_jc(t)]── T_case ──[R_spread]── T_board ──[R_θBA]── T_amb
                                              │
                                           C_board
```

**Board heat capacity.** The board carries about 3–4 g of copper plus 2 g of FR4, plus the FET masses:

$$
C_{board} \approx 3.5\cdot0.385 + 2\cdot1.1 + \text{FETs} \approx 4\text{–}5\ \text{J/K}
$$

**Board time constant:**

$$
\tau = R_{\theta,BA} C_{board} \approx 4 \times 4.5 = 18\ \text{s}
$$

**Board temperature rise:**

$$
\Delta T_{board}(t) = P R_{\theta,BA}\left(1-e^{-t/\tau}\right)
$$

**Junction rise above board** (per FET, about 6 W average; PQFN Z_th,jc(1 s) ≈ 1.5 K/W plus spreading about 2 K/W):

$$
\Delta T_{j-b} \approx 6 \times 3.5 = 21\ \text{K}
$$
