# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage, Thermal, and Bidirectional DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Flags

I have no access to any prior "Job #9" specification, so this document builds a self-consistent model from stated assumptions. Every number below is parametric. Substitute your actual MOSFET, driver, and stack-up values and the equations still hold.

**Architecture clarification.** In a 4-in-1 ESC, the STM32H743 is the flight controller. It does not commutate motors. Each ESC channel runs its own MCU, such as an AT32F421 running AM32 or an EFM8 running BLHeli_S/Bluejay. The H743 sends DShot600 to those MCUs and receives eRPM telemetry back. The 48 kHz PWM is the ESC-MCU switching frequency, not an H743 timer.

**Feasibility flag.** 80 A per channel on a "nano" (20×20 mm) board is not physically sustainable. The analysis below assumes a 30×30 mm stacked board. Even then, 80 A is a **burst rating of roughly 3 s**, not a continuous rating. Section 4 quantifies this.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Commutation Topology

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification. In any commutation state, two phases carry the full phase current *I*:

- **Phase A (PWM leg):** the high-side FET conducts for duty fraction D. The low-side FET (sync rectifier) conducts for 1−D.
- **Phase B (return leg):** the low-side FET conducts for 100% of the state.

At every instant, exactly two FETs (one per active leg) are in series with the current. Therefore:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This result is independent of D, which is the key reason synchronous rectification matters.

### 2.2 Temperature Dependence of R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.5 mΩ × 1.6 = **2.4 mΩ**.
