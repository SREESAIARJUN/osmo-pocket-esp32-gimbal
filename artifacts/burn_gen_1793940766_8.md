# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #62): Thermal, Switching & DShot Architecture

## 0. Architecture Correction and Assumptions

**Architectural point.** ArduPilot does not commutate BLDC motors. The H743 acts as the flight controller and sends bidirectional DShot600 to the ESC. The 4-in-1 ESC needs its own commutation MCUs. These can be four small MCUs, such as AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or one MCU per pair, depending on firmware support. Everything below assumes this split.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (Six-Step Trapezoidal, Synchronous Rectification)

During each 60° sector, two legs conduct:
- One leg is PWM'd. Its high-side (HS) conducts for D; its low-side (LS) synchronous rectifier conducts for 1−D.
- One LS FET in the return leg conducts continuously.

So exactly two FET channels are always in the current path:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j) + I^2 R_{cu}, \qquad R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Each phase conducts for 2/3 of the electrical cycle. Per-FET dissipation is:

$$P_{HS} = \frac{I^2 R_{ds} D}{3}, \qquad P_{LS} = I^2 R_{ds}\left[\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1-D}{3}\right]$$

**At T_j = 100 °C:**

- R_ds = 0.90 × (1 + 0.0045 × 75) = **1.20 mΩ**
- P_cond,FET = 2 × 80² × 1.20 mΩ = **15.4 W** per channel
- P_cu = 80² × 0.5 mΩ = **3.2 W** per channel
- Per-FET at D ≈ 1: P_LS = P_HS ≈ 6400 × 1.2 mΩ / 3 = **2.56 W**

At D < 1, the burden shifts to the LS FET: P_LS = 2.56 × (2 − D).

**Electrothermal feedback.** R_ds depends on T_j, so iterate:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_{board} + P(T_j^{(k)})\,R_{th,jb}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. That means one HS turn-on and one HS turn-off per period, per channel.

### 2.1 V-I overlap loss

Gate current during the plateau:

- Turn-on: I_g,on = (12 − 3.5) / 5 = 1.7 A
- Turn-off: I_g,off = 3.5 / 5 = 0.7 A

Transition times use Q_sw = Q_gd + Q_gs2 = 12 nC:

- t_r = 12 nC / 1.7 A ≈ **7 ns**
- t_f = 12 nC / 0.7 A ≈ **17 ns**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot 25.2\cdot 80\cdot 24\text{ ns}\cdot 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.16\ W}$$

### 2.2 Other switching terms

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
