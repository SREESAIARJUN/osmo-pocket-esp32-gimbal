## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous PWM

During any commutation sector, phase A is PWM'd and phase B is the return:

- **Q_AH** (high side, PWM leg) conducts for fraction D.
- **Q_AL** (low side, PWM leg, synchronous rectifier) conducts for fraction (1 − D), minus dead time.
- **Q_BL** (low side, return leg) conducts 100 % of the sector.

The instantaneous conduction loss per channel is:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 R_{hot}\left[D + (1-D) + 1\right] = 2\,I_{ph}^2 R_{hot}$$

The result is independent of D, because synchronous rectification always keeps two FETs in the current path.

### 1.2 Ripple correction

The phase inductance of a typical 2207 to 2810 motor is L_ph ≈ 15–40 µH line-to-line. Worst-case peak-to-peak ripple occurs at D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\,\mu H \times 48\,kHz} = 6.6\ A$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.6 \approx 6404\ A^2$$

The correction is +0.06 %, so it is negligible at 48 kHz. That is one justification for 48 kHz over 24 kHz.

### 1.3 Numerical result

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.65\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{21.1\ W\ per\ channel}$$

### 1.4 Per-FET averaging

Each FET carries current in only 2 of 6 sectors (120° electrical). The electrical period is short compared with the FET's thermal time constant, which is about 10 ms die-to-case. For example, a 14-pole motor at 30 kRPM has an electrical period of 286 µs. The junction therefore sees average power:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} = \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6} = 3.52\ W$$

The worst FET is the low side, because it also absorbs dead-time diode loss. That is treated in §2.

### 1.5 PCB copper conduction loss (often forgotten)

The bus path is about 25 mm from the pads to the FETs, with 4 layers of 2 oz copper (70 µm) and a 6 mm effective width:

$$R_{Cu} = \rho\frac{\ell}{w\,t\,n} = 1.72\times10^{-8}\frac{0.025}{0.006 \times 70\mu \times 4} = 0.256\ m\Omega\ (\times1.39\ at\ 140°C \rightarrow 0.36\ m\Omega)$$

Total battery current at D = 0.95 with 4 × 80 A is about 304 A:

$$P_{Cu,bus} = 304^2 \times 0.36\,m\Omega \approx \mathbf{33\ W}$$

> ⚠️ **This is the single largest hidden loss.** The bus copper dissipates more than one channel's FETs do. **Mitigation:** solder XT60 pigtails directly at the board centroid, use 6 internal layers at 2 oz for V_bus and GND, and stitch with a via field (≥ 60 × 0.3 mm vias per transition). The bus loss for the stitched 6-layer design then drops to about 18 W. The thermal model uses that 18 W.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches hard. Hard switching happens on the high-side FET. The low side sees soft (ZVS-like) turn-on after dead time, but contributes reverse recovery.
