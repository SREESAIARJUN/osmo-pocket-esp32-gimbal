# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

**Job #36, Rev A (architecture baseline)**

---

## 0. Feasibility Findings

These findings drive every number that follows, so they come first.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** An STM32H743 cannot run four sensorless BLDC loops and ArduPilot at the same time. The standard architecture is:
   - **H743**: runs ArduPilot and sends bi-directional DShot600 commands.
   - **4× ESC MCUs**: STM32G071 or AT32F421 running AM32. Each generates 48 kHz complementary PWM to a 3-phase gate driver.
   
   This document models that topology.

2. **"80A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is thermally infeasible as a sustained or simultaneous rating.** Section 3 shows that four channels at 80 A dissipate about 64 W into roughly 4 J/K of thermal mass. That is about 16 K/s of adiabatic temperature rise. 80 A per channel is achievable only as a **≤2–3 s single-burst rating** with a tightly specified duty cycle. My recommendation is to either:
   - re-rate to **80 A burst / 35 A continuous**, or
   - move to a 30.5×30.5 mm format.

3. **Battery side.** Four motors at 80 A phase current near full duty demand about 300 A from the pack. XT60/XT90 connectors, battery leads, and the capacitor bank cannot support that. Battery current must be limited in firmware:
   - ArduPilot: `BATT_CURR_LIM` / `MOT_BAT_CURR_MAX`.
   - AM32: current limit per ESC.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction loss

In 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM, two legs carry current at every instant:

- **PWM leg**: current flows through the high-side FET for D·T_sw and through the low-side FET for (1−D)·T_sw.
- **Return leg**: the low-side FET is held fully on.

So current always passes through exactly **two R_DS(on) in series**, independent of D. Including inductor ripple ΔI:

$$
P_{cond} = 2\,R_{DS}(T_j)\left(I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}\right)
$$

The ripple term is under 1% for typical motor inductance at 48 kHz, so it is neglected.
