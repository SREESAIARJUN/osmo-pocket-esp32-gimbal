# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #8): Electro-Thermal and Timing Model

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Feasibility Warnings

The architecture forces three corrections:

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot is a flight-control stack, not ESC firmware. The standard and correct partitioning is:
   - **STM32H743** (ArduPilot) generates bidirectional DShot600 on four timer channels.
   - **4× ESC MCUs** (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32-class firmware) each run 6-step commutation at 48 kHz PWM and drive one 3-phase bridge.
2. **80 A is treated as a *per-channel burst*,** which is how 4-in-1 boards are normally rated. That means 320 A board total.
3. **A "nano" board (20×20 mm mounting, ~26×26 mm PCB) cannot dissipate this continuously.** The math in §4 shows 80 A is survivable only as a **~1–2 s burst** on all four channels. Treat 80 A as a marketing-grade burst rating unless you add a heatsink and forced airflow. I recommend rating the board at **40–45 A continuous per channel**.

**Design point:**

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage | V_bus | 6S, 25.2 V max (22.2 V nominal) |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous rectification) |
| MOSFET | 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN class | R_ds(on),25 = 1.0 mΩ typ / 1.3 mΩ max @ V_gs = 10 V |
| Gate charge | Q_g, Q_sw | Q_g = 40 nC, Q_sw = Q_gs2 + Q_gd ≈ 12 nC |

| Dead time | t_dt | 50 ns per edge |

> These are representative values for this MOSFET class. **Substitute your selected part's datasheet numbers.** Every equation below is parametric.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous PWM

In any 60° commutation sector, the current path is:
- one phase is PWM-switched (high side during the on-time, its complementary low side during the off-time), and
- a second phase's low side is held on statically.

So the motor current always flows through **exactly two conducting MOSFETs**, ignoring dead time:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Ripple correction

Phase current is DC plus a triangular ripple ΔI:

$$I_{rms} = I_{dc}\sqrt{1 + \frac{1}{12}\left(\frac{\Delta I}{I_{dc}}\right)^2}, \qquad \Delta I \approx \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{emf})\,D}{L_{ph-ph}\, f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5-inch-class motor (L_ph-ph ≈ 15 µH, D = 0.9, V_bus − V_emf ≈ 4 V at burst): ΔI ≈ 5 A, so the ripple term is ≈ 0.03 %. **This term is negligible.** Use I_rms ≈ 80 A.

### 1.3 Temperature dependence (self-consistent)

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.004\text{–}0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

The worst-case design uses R_25 = 1.3 mΩ (max) and α = 0.0045 K⁻¹. Solving the coupled equations below by fixed-point iteration converges to T_j ≈ 110 °C:

$$R_{ds(on)}(110\,°C) = 1.3 \times (1 + 0.0045 \times 85) = 1.80\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.4 Result at 80 A

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.80\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{23.0\ W\ per\ channel}$$

With typical rather than maximum R_25 (1.0 mΩ → 1.38 mΩ hot): **17.7 W per channel**.

### 1.5 Per-FET distribution

Over one electrical cycle, each FET carries current for 120°. The high-side FET carries it only during D·T; its low-side partner covers (1−D)·T.

| FET role (per 120° window) | Loss share |
|---|---|
| PWM-leg high side | I²R · D |
| PWM-leg low side (sync rect.) | I²R · (1−D) |
| Static low side | I²R · 1 |

Averaged over the cycle (each FET takes each role in turn), the per-FET average is

$$P_{FET} \approx \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6} \approx 3.8\ \text{W}$$

**Peak instantaneous per-FET loss is 11.5 W** while it is the static low side. This matters for the junction transient in §4.3 because the electrical period at burst (~1–3 ms) is comparable to the die thermal time constant.

### 1.6 Copper / PCB conduction loss

For a 2 oz (70 µm) copper layer, sheet resistance is ρ_□ ≈ 0.245 mΩ/□ at 25 °C, about 0.33 mΩ/□ at 110 °C. Assume each phase path is ≈ 4 squares effective, using 4 parallel layers of 2 oz copper.

$$R_{cu} \approx 4 \times 0.33 / 4 = 0.33\ \text{m}\Omega$$

The current crosses two such paths, so:

$$P_{cu} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 0.33\times10^{-3} \approx \mathbf{4.2\ W\ per\ channel}$$

This is frequently forgotten and is not small. Add the battery-pad-to-bus path separately: 320 A total, ~0.1 mΩ → **10 W board-level**.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The high side sees hard turn-on and turn-off; the low side soft-switches, with its body diode conducting during dead time.
