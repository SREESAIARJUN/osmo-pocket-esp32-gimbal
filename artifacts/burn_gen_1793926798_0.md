## 1. FOC Inner Current Loop

### 1.1 Transforms

**Clarke transform** (amplitude invariant, with i_a + i_b + i_c = 0):

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \frac{1}{\sqrt3}(i_a + 2 i_b)
$$

**Park transform**, using electrical angle θ_e:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix} =
\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}
$$

**Electrical angle with latency compensation.** The SPI read, computation and PWM update together delay the applied voltage by about 1.5 T_s. The angle is therefore advanced:

$$
\theta_e = p\,(\theta_m - \theta_{\text{off}}) + \omega_e \cdot 1.5\,T_s
$$

### 1.2 Current PI Tuning by Pole-Zero Cancellation

Per axis, the motor's electrical plant is:

$$
G_i(s) = \frac{1}{Ls + R}
$$

Choose the PI controller as C(s) = K_p + K_i/s, with:

$$
K_p = L\,\omega_c,\qquad K_i = R\,\omega_c
$$

The controller zero then cancels the motor pole, and the closed loop becomes first-order:

$$
\frac{i}{i^*} = \frac{\omega_c}{s + \omega_c}
$$

Pick ω_c ≈ 2π · 1.5 kHz, which is below f_s/10 at f_s = 20 kHz.

**Decoupling feedforward:**

$$
v_d^{ff} = -\omega_e L i_q,\qquad v_q^{ff} = \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e \lambda_m
$$

where λ_m = Kt / (1.5 p).

**Voltage limiting.** The output vector is limited to a circle:

$$
\sqrt{v_d^2+v_q^2}\le V_{bus}/\sqrt3
$$

The d-axis has priority.

### 1.3 SVPWM by Min-Max Zero-Sequence Injection

$$
v_{off} = -\tfrac12\big(\max(v_a,v_b,v_c) + \min(v_a,v_b,v_c)\big),\qquad
d_x = \tfrac12 + \frac{v_x + v_{off}}{V_{bus}}
$$

This is mathematically equivalent to sector-based SVPWM.

## 2. Cascaded Velocity and Angle Control

### 2.1 Mechanical Plant

$$
J\dot\omega = K_t\, i_q - B\omega - \tau_{cog}(\theta) - \tau_{dist}
$$

### 2.2 Velocity PI Loop

The velocity loop sees the current loop as approximately unity gain, since ω_c is much greater than ω_v.

$$
i_q^{*} = K_{pv}\,e_\omega + K_{iv}\!\int e_\omega\,dt + i_q^{ff},\qquad e_\omega=\omega^*-\omega
$$

**Tuning by the symmetric optimum,** with crossover ω_v and spacing factor a = 3 to 4:

$$
K_{pv} = \frac{J\,\omega_v}{K_t},\qquad K_{iv} = K_{pv}\,\frac{\omega_v}{a^2}
$$

Use ω_v ≤ ω_c / 6. A typical choice is ω_v = 2π · 150 Hz.

**Feedforward current:**

$$
i_q^{ff} = \frac{J\,\alpha^* + B\,\omega^*}{K_t} + i_{cog}(\theta)
$$

**Anti-windup by back-calculation:**

$$
I_{k+1} = I_k + T_v\big[K_{iv} e_k + K_{aw}(u_{sat} - u_{unsat})\big],\qquad K_{aw}\approx \frac{1}{T_v}\sqrt{K_{iv}/K_{pv}}
$$

**Velocity feedback blending.** Commutation always uses the encoder. For stabilization, however, the rate that matters is the inertial rate of the payload. The feedback is therefore:

$$
\omega_{fb} = \text{HPF}_{f_b}\big(\hat\omega_{gyro,axis}\big) + \text{LPF}_{f_b}\big(\dot\theta_{enc}\big)
$$

The crossover f_b is about 5 Hz. Both filters are first-order and complementary, so their sum is unity.
