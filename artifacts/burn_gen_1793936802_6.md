## 0. Architectural Clarification

An STM32H743 does not normally commutate the motors directly. In this product class the work is split:

| Function | Device | Firmware |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller (attitude, DShot generation, telemetry decode) | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100) | ArduPilot |
| 4× ESC commutation, 48 kHz PWM | 4× AT32F421 / STM32G071 (one per motor) | AM32 / BLHeli_32-class |
| Gate drive | 4× FD6288Q (3-phase, bootstrapped) | — |
| Power stage | 24× N-MOSFET (6 per motor) | — |

The 48 kHz switching analysis applies to the ESC MCUs and power stage. The DShot/DMA analysis applies to the H743.

**Form-factor warning.** 80 A per channel cannot be carried continuously on a 20×20 mm "nano" board. The analysis below shows that 80 A is achievable only as a **time-limited burst**, and only on a 30.5×30.5 mm board with 2 oz+ copper. I quantify the burst limit in §3.

## 2. Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

The ESC uses trapezoidal 6-step drive with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM). At any instant two devices are in the current path: one high-side and one low-side, or, during the off-time, the complementary device of the PWM leg. So:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I^2 \, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 105 °C (first iteration):

- R_DS = 0.70 × (1 + 0.006 × 80) = **1.036 mΩ**
- P_cond = 2 × 80² × 1.036 × 10⁻³ = **13.26 W per channel**

Each device conducts for 1/3 of the electrical cycle, high and low sides combined. With 6 devices the average is:

$$P_{cond,dev} = P_{cond,ch} / 6 \approx 2.21\ \text{W}$$

**Device RMS current.** For a 120° conduction interval:

- I_RMS,dev = I·√(1/3) = **46.2 A**
- Check: I_RMS² × R × 6 / 2 = 46.2² × 1.036 m × 3 ≈ 6.63 W for each half of the path, 13.26 W total. Consistent.

### 2.2 Switching Losses (48 kHz, one hard-switched leg active)

**(a) V–I overlap.** The transition time is set by gate drive current:

- t_r ≈ t_f ≈ Q_sw / I_g = 15 nC / 1 A = 15 ns

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.452\ W}$$

**(b) Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 2\text{ nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.030\ W}$$

**(c) Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = 60\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.073\ W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead-time intervals per period):

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.614\ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive** (two switching devices). This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs:

$$P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\text{ n} \times 10 \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.058\ W}$$

**Switching subtotal:** P_sw,ch ≈ **2.23 W**

### 2.3 Other Losses (per channel)

| Source | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| Copper (phase + bus traces) | ~0.3 mΩ effective × 80² | 1.92 W |
| Bus capacitor ESR | I_cap,RMS ≈ I·√(D(1−D)) ≈ 40 A at D = 0.5; 3 mΩ ESR | 4.8 W (shared/bulk, partly external) |

The bus capacitor term is significant. A 1000 µF low-ESR external cap on the battery lead is **mandatory**.

### 2.4 Totals at 80 A Burst

| Term | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 13.26 W | 53.0 W |
| Switching | 2.23 W | 8.9 W |
| PCB copper | 1.92 W | 7.7 W |
| **Board total** (excluding external caps) | **17.4 W** | **69.6 W** |

**Efficiency check.** Output power ≈ 22.2 V × 80 A × 4 ≈ 7.1 kW, so the power stage efficiency is ≈ 99.0%. This is plausible for low-R_DS(on) FETs. The difficulty is not efficiency but power density.

### 2.5 Sensitivity to f_sw

Only V–I overlap, C_oss, Q_rr, dead-time and gate terms scale with frequency:

$$\frac{\partial P_{sw}}{\partial f_{sw}} = 2.23\text{ W} / 48\text{ kHz} = 46\text{ mW/kHz per channel}$$
