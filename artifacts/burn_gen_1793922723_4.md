# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #32): Power-Stage Model, DShot DMA Plan, and Netlist

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Design Assumptions

**Architecture correction.** The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. In every practical 4-in-1 design:

- The **H743 runs ArduPilot** (FC side).
- The **ESC side runs AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware on 4 small MCUs**, typically AT32F421 or STM32G071, one per motor.
- The interface between them is 4× bidirectional DShot600.

The model below uses that partition.

**Feasibility warning.** A "nano" (20×20 mm) board cannot dissipate the heat of 80 A per motor except as a burst of about 1–3 seconds. Section 3 quantifies this.

**Parameter caveat.** The MOSFET and driver values below are representative of the class. Before layout, replace them with your chosen datasheet values.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |

## 1. Conduction Losses

In six-step operation, two phases conduct at all times. With synchronous rectification, exactly **two MOSFETs are in series with I at every instant**:

- PWM on-time: high-side of phase A plus low-side of phase B.
- PWM off-time: low-side of phase A plus low-side of phase B.

Per motor:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.74\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{22.3\ W}$$

Copper loss per motor:

$$P_{cu} = I^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{6.4\ W}$$

**Per-FET distribution.** Each FET conducts for at most 120° of the electrical cycle.

- Instantaneous worst case is the return-leg low-side FET: I²R = 11.1 W during its sector.
- Averaged over the electrical cycle: 22.3 / 6 ≈ **3.7 W per FET**.
- This averaging is valid because the electrical frequency (hundreds of Hz) is much higher than 1/τ of the package plus copper.

**Ripple correction (optional).** With inductor ripple ΔI:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

At typical motor inductance (~10 µH), 48 kHz, and D = 0.5, ΔI ≈ 13 A. This adds less than 1%, so it is neglected.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

**Transition times** (hard-switched high-side, gate-charge method):

$$t_r = \frac{Q_{sw}}{(V_{drv}-V_{pl})/R_g} = \frac{12\,\text{nC}}{1.2\,\text{A}} = 10\ \text{ns}$$

$$t_f = \frac{Q_{sw}}{V_{pl}/R_g} = \frac{12\,\text{nC}}{0.8\,\text{A}} = 15\ \text{ns}$$

**Loss components:**

| Loss | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·25 ns·48 k | **1.21 W** |
| Output capacitance | ½·C_oss,er·V²·f_sw × 2 FETs | 0.03 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 0.05 W |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_f·I·2·t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k | **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (mostly in driver and R_g) | Q_g·V_drv·f_sw × 2 | 0.05 W |
| **Total switching-related** | | **≈ 1.95 W** |

**Layout margin.** Apply a 1.5–2× factor to the V-I overlap term for loop-inductance ring
