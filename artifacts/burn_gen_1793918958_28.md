## 0. Architectural Ground Rules and Assumptions

Two points about the requested configuration need to be settled before any numbers mean anything.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production AIO, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and emits DShot600. Four local ESC MCUs (AT32F421 / STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32) perform 6-step commutation at 48 kHz. Therefore:
   - The 48 kHz PWM frequency is an **ESC firmware parameter**.
   - Bi-directional DShot is a link **between the H743 and the ESC MCUs**.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano-class board is thermally marginal.** The math below shows it is survivable only as a short burst of a few seconds, which is consistent with how commercial AIOs are rated.

All device parameters are **representative assumptions**. Replace them with your selected MOSFET's datasheet values; every equation is parametric.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification, two phases are energized in each 60° sector:

- **Commutated leg:** the high-side (HS) FET carries current during the duty interval D. The synchronous low-side (LS) FET carries the freewheel current during (1−D).
- **Return leg:** the LS FET conducts continuously for the whole sector.

At any instant, phase current therefore flows through exactly **two FETs in series**. Including current ripple:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{ds(on)}\, k_T$$

### 1.2 Evaluation at 80 A

Ripple is a small correction at 48 kHz with typical motor inductance (ΔI ≈ 4–8 A adds less than 0.1 %), so it is neglected:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.5 = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ channel}$$

### 1.3 Per-device distribution

Each FET conducts for 120° electrical, i.e. one third of the time:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds}\, k_T}{3} = \frac{6400 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega}{3} = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

**Check:** 6 FETs × 3.2 W = 19.2 W, which matches the channel total.

### 1.4 PCB copper loss (frequently ignored, never negligible)

Battery current at full throttle is:

$$I_{bat} \approx D \sum I_{ph} \approx 0.9 \times 4 \times 80 = 288\ \text{A}$$

With an effective power-plane plus pad resistance of about 0.2 mΩ:

$$P_{Cu} = 288^2 \times 0.2\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 16.6\ \text{W}$$
