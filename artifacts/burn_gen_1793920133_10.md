# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Architecture Package (Job #10)

**Rev A, preliminary design. Numerical values depend on the parameters stated in §1 and must be re-run against the chosen MOSFET datasheet and verified on hardware.**

## 0. Feasibility Findings

Three issues affect the spec as written and should be resolved before layout.

| # | Finding | Impact | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | The H743 runs ArduPilot. It does not commutate motors. ArduPilot has no sensorless BLDC commutation firmware, and four sensorless FOC/6-step loops on the FC MCU would compete with the flight loop. | Architecture | Use **4× dedicated ESC MCUs** (AT32F421 or STM32G071) running **AM32** or BLHeli_32. The H743 talks to them over bidirectional DShot600. |
| F2 | 80 A burst × 4 motors produces about **90 W of heat** in the power stage (§2). A 20×20 "nano" board has roughly 2–4 J/K of thermal mass. | Thermal | Allow 80 A only as a ≤2–3 s burst. Use a **30.5×30.5 board** with 6-layer 2 oz copper (3 oz on the outer layers is preferred). Spec continuous current at about 40–45 A. |
| F3 | A 48 kHz PWM is high for 80 A-class FETs. Switching and dead-time losses scale linearly with frequency. | Efficiency | Keep 48 kHz only if quiet operation or low-inductance motors require it. Otherwise use 24 kHz, which cuts about 1.5 W per channel. |

## 2. Loss Model (per motor channel, 6 FETs)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

In each 60° commutation sector two phases conduct:
- **The PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for duty D and its synchronous low side for (1−D). Current flows through exactly one FET of that leg at all times.
- **The static leg:** its low side is fully on.

The conduction path is therefore always two FETs in series, independent of D:

$$
P_{cond} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = 2(80)^2(1.5\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{19.2\ W}
$$

Per-FET distribution: each FET conducts during 120° of the electrical cycle.
- **Average per FET:** P_cond / 6 ≈ 3.2 W.
- **Peak instantaneous:** I²R = 9.6 W while conducting. This sets the junction ripple and matters at low eRPM, where sectors are long.

Add the copper path. With 2 oz copper at about 0.25 mΩ per phase path: 2 × 6400 × 0.25 mΩ ≈ 3.2 W, deposited in the board rather than in the FETs.

### 2.2 Switching losses (PWM leg only, one hard-switched FET per edge)

$$
P_{sw,VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(40\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}
$$

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V f_{sw} \approx 0.030\ \text{W}
$$

$$
P_{gate} = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw}\ (\text{2 FETs switched}) = 2(50\text{n})(10)(48\text{k}) = 0.048\ \text{W}
$$

The gate loss is dissipated mainly in the driver and R_g.

### 2.3 Dead-time (body-diode) loss

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD} I\, (2 t_{dead}) f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot400\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}
$$

### 2.4 Totals

| Term | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 19.20 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching V·I | 1.94 W | 7.8 W |
| Dead-time | 1.23 W | 4.9 W |
| Q_rr + Q_oss + gate | 0.14 W | 0.6 W |
| **FET total** | **22.5 W** | **90.1 W** |
| Copper (board) | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| Bus capacitor ESR (below) | ~0.3 W | ~1.2 W |
| **Board total** | **~26 W** | **~104 W** |

Efficiency at the burst point: P_out ≈ 4 × 25.2 × 80 × D_eff. At D ≈ 0.9 that is about 7.2 kW electrical, so efficiency is roughly 98.5%. The high efficiency does not help here, because the problem is power density, not efficiency.

### 2.5 Bus capacitor ripple

$$
I_{C,rms} \approx I_{ph}\sqrt{D(1-D)}
$$

This peaks at D = 0.5, giving 40 A_rms per channel. In practice it is about 35–45 A_rms total, with partial cancellation since the four ESCs are not phase-locked.

Requirements:
- **Local MLCC bank per channel:** 4× 10 µF/50 V X7R 1210. Derate to about 3 µF effective at 25 V DC bias.
- **Bulk:** 1× 470 µF–1000 µF/35 V low-ESR electrolytic on the pigtail. This is mandatory for 6S at 80 A.

### 2.6 Effect of f_sw (sensitivity)

$$
P_{fsw\text{-dependent}} \approx (1.94+1.23+0.14)\cdot\frac{f}{48\text{k}}
$$

At 24 kHz this term drops to 1.65 W per channel. Conduction loss dominates at every practical frequency.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network (per FET, Foster/Cauer simplified)

```
P_FET ─► T_j ─[R_jc 1.0 K/W]─► T_c ─[R_cs ~2 K/W via-array to inner planes]─► T_pcb ─[R_pa]─► T_amb
                                                                                 │
                                                                          C_pcb (board mass)
```

### 3.2 Steady state (shows 80 A continuous is impossible)

Board-to-air resistance, 30.5×30.5 mm, both sides, with prop-wash at about 5 m/s:

$$
R_{pa} \approx \frac{1}{hA} = \frac{1}{(50)(2\times0.00093)} \approx 10.8\ \text{K/W}
$$

At 104 W the steady-state rise would be ≈1100 K, so the board cannot run 80 A continuously.

The continuous rating follows from allowing 90 °C rise (T_amb 35 °C, T_pcb 125 °C): P ≤ 8.3 W total. Because conduction loss scales with I², this gives I_cont ≈ 80·√(8.3/104) ≈ **23 A per motor, with no heatsink**.

With a 3 mm aluminium spreader plate and good airflow (R_pa ≈ 3 K/W): P ≤ 30 W, giving **I_cont ≈ 43 A**.
