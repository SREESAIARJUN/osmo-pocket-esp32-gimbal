# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #45): Reference Design Analysis

## 0. Architecture Assumptions

Several of these choices drive the math, so verify them before using any number below.

| Item | Assumption | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| FC MCU | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100), running ArduPilot at 400 MHz | Standard ArduPilot H7 configuration |
| ESC MCUs | 4 × AT32F421K8U7 (or STM32G071) running AM32 | The H743 does not run the ESC commutation loops. ArduPilot has no sensorless ESC firmware, so each channel needs its own MCU. |
| Bus voltage | 6S, 25.2 V max. MOSFETs rated 40 V. | Gives about 60% derating at full charge |
| MOSFETs | 6 per channel, 24 total. 40 V, R_DS(on) = 0.9 mΩ at 25 °C, PDFN 5×6. | An 80 A burst rating is not credible with 3.3×3.3 parts |
| Gate driver | 1 × FD6288Q (3-phase half-bridge) per channel | Common AM32/BLHeli_32 topology |
| PWM | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous rectification), 6-step trapezoidal | AM32 default class |
| "80 A burst" | Per channel, ≤ 5 s | See Section 3. This determines whether the design survives. |

**Feasibility flag:** "Nano" plus 4 × 80 A is thermally contradictory, which the numbers below show. A 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 board cannot hold 24 × 5×6 FETs. The minimum realistic footprint is 30.5×30.5 mm mounting on roughly a 36×36 mm board, and 80 A must be a short burst rating, not continuous.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)] , \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At a design-point junction temperature T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{DS}(110) = 0.9\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.51 = 1.36\,\text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Per-FET conduction in 6-step with complementary PWM

Each leg spends one third of the electrical period in each of three states: high-side PWM, low-side fully on, and floating. Let D be the PWM duty cycle and I the phase current.

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$
$$P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)\, I^2 R_{DS} + \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{DS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(2-D)\, I^2 R_{DS}$$

Per channel (3 legs), exactly two FETs conduct at any instant:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 3(P_{HS}+P_{LS}) = 2\, I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, D = 0.95

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| I²R | 6400 × 1.36 mΩ = 8.70 W |
| P_HS per FET | 2.76 W |
| P_LS per FET | 3.04 W (worst-case device) |
| **P_cond per channel** | **17.4 W** |
| **P_cond, 4 channels** | **69.6 W** |

### 1.4 Copper and shunt losses (often forgotten)

- Phase and bus copper, assuming 2 oz external and 2 × 2 oz internal planes, about 0.3 mΩ effective per current path: 6400 × 0.3 mΩ = **1.9 W per channel**.
- Current-sense shunt (one common 0.25 mΩ shunt), with 320 A total on the battery side: 320² × 0.25 mΩ = **25.6 W**.
  - This is not acceptable for a 4 × 80 A burst.
  - Use a 0.1 mΩ shunt, giving 10.2 W, or a Hall/magnetic current sensor.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWMing leg hard-switches. Its low-side switch is soft-switched: ZVS after dead-time body-diode conduction.

### 2.1 Hard-switching (V–I overlap)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

With V_bus = 25.2 V, I = 80 A, and t_r = t_f = 15 ns (FD6288 with about 10 Ω gate resistance and Q_g ≈ 60 nC):

$$P_{VI} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 1.45\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 0.31\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery and output capacitance

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$
$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \approx 40\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Gate drive (dissipated in driver and gate resistors, not the FETs)

$$P_{g} = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.058\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Switching totals

| Term | Per channel |
|---|---|
| V–I overlap | 1.45 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.31 W |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.10 W |
| **Total switching** | **≈ 1.86 W** |
| **4 channels** | **≈ 7.4 W** |

**Design insight:** at 80 A, conduction is about 90% of the loss. Going from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves only about 3.7 W board-wide. Lower R_DS(on) or paralleled FETs matter far more than frequency.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Total burst dissipation

$$P_{tot} = 4(17.4 + 1.9 + 1.86) + P_{shunt} + P_{logic} \approx 84.6 + 10.2 + 1.5 \approx \mathbf{96\ W}$$

The shunt term assumes the 0.1 mΩ shunt; logic is about 1.5 W.

### 3.2 Junction rise (steady-state, local)

$$T_j = T_{pcb,local} + P_{FET}(R_{\theta JC} + R_{\theta,solder+spread})$$

Taking the worst FET at P_FET = 3.04 W conduction plus 1.6 W switching (high side, when PWMing), about 3.5 W average with R_θJC = 1.0 K/W and R_spread ≈ 4 K/W:

$$\Delta T_{j-pcb} \approx 3.5 \times 5 = 17.5\ \text{K}$$

### 3.3 Board transient (lumped capacitance, burst-limiting)

For a 36×36 mm 8-layer PCB:

| Element | Heat capacity |
|---|---|
| FR4, about 4.5 g × 1.1 J/gK | 5.0 J/K |
| Copper, about 3 g × 0.385 J/gK | 1.2 J/K |
| 24 FETs plus solder | about 1.0 J/K |
| **C_th total** | **≈ 7.2 J/K** |

For R_θ,board→air, use a forced-convection estimate of about 2.5 K/W under prop wash. If unshrouded at hover it is more like 4–6 K/W.

$$T_{pcb}(t) = T_a + P R_\theta \left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right), \quad \tau = R_\theta C_{th} = 18\ \text{s}$$
