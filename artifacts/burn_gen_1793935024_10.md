# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #28): Reference Design Analysis

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Feasibility Flags

**Architecture assumption.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does not commutate the motors. Each motor is driven by a dedicated ESC MCU (one AT32F421 or STM32G071 per motor, running AM32 or BLHeli_32) through a 3-phase gate driver and six N-MOSFETs. The H743 talks to the ESC MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. This partitioning is standard, and you should keep it. Commutating four motors from the H743 while it also runs EKF3 is not viable under ArduPilot.

**Feasibility flag: 80 A burst on a "nano" board.** The calculations in §3 show about 100 W of dissipation board-wide during an all-motor 80 A burst. On a 20×20 mm board this is thermally sustainable for roughly 1 s. Treat 80 A as a single-motor or short-duration burst rating, not a continuous rating. I recommend the 30.5×30.5 mm form factor if 80 A is a marketing requirement.

**Operating point.**
- Supply: 6S, V_bus = 25.2 V (fully charged, worst case).
- PWM frequency: f_sw = 48 kHz.
- Duty cycle at burst: D = 0.9.
- Commutation: 6-step trapezoidal with complementary (synchronous) PWM on the high side.
- Phase current: I = 80 A.
