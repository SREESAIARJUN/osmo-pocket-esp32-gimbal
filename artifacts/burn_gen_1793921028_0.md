## 0. System Architecture Clarification

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. A 4-in-1 ESC has its own per-motor (or shared) ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The ESC MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM and handles commutation. The H743 does the following:

1. Emits DShot600 frames on 4 timer channels.
2. Receives the GCR-encoded eRPM telemetry back on the **same wire** (bi-directional DShot).
3. Monitors VBAT and total current.

The partitioning is:

```
 ┌────────────── FC (STM32H743VIT6) ──────────────┐      ┌──── 4-in-1 ESC ────┐
 │ TIM3_CH3/CH4, TIM5_CH1/CH2 ── DShot600 bidir ──┼─────►│ ESC MCU ×4 (AM32)  │
 │ ADC: VBAT, CURR ◄──────────────────────────────┼──────┤ 6× FET bridge ×4   │
 │ UART RX ◄── KISS/AM32 ESC telemetry (optional) ┼──────┤ shunt + amp        │
 └────────────────────────────────────────────────┘      └────────────────────┘
```

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current Path in 6-Step Complementary PWM

At any instant inside a commutation sector, current flows through exactly **two switch positions** in series:

- One high-side position (PWM-ing with its complementary low side).
- One low-side position held fully on.

The PWM leg alternates between HS (fraction D) and its synchronous LS (fraction 1−D). The loop resistance is therefore always:

$$R_{loop} = 2\,R_{ds}(T_j) + R_{shunt} + R_{Cu}$$

### 2.2 Per-Channel Conduction Loss (Flat-Top Approximation)

Assume the motor's inductance holds the phase current flat at I = 80 A:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \left[2R_{ds}(T_j) + R_{shunt} + R_{Cu}\right]$$

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

With T_j = 125 °C, R_ds = 0.80 × 1.45 = **1.16 mΩ**. Take R_Cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ for 2 oz external plus 2 oz internal pours, pads, and the motor wire joint.

$$P_{cond} = 6400 \times (2.32 + 0.25 + 0.30)\times10^{-3} = 6400 \times 2.87\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{18.4\ W}$$

Of that total, the FETs dissipate 6400 × 2.32 mΩ = **14.8 W**.

### 2.3 Per-FET Distribution

Each of the 6 positions is in the conduction path 1/3 of the electrical period. The "always-on" LS position takes the full 1/3. The PWM leg splits its 1/3 as D (HS) and 1−D (LS sync). Averaged over a full electrical revolution, every FET sees the same mean:

$$\bar P_{FET} = \frac{14.8\ \text{W}}{6} \approx \mathbf{2.5\ W}$$

The **instantaneous** peak per FET during its active sector is 3× higher, about 7.4 W. This matters for the transient Z_th at low eRPM, where a sector lasts milliseconds:

$$P_{FET,inst} = I^2 R_{ds} = 6400 \times 1.16\,\text{m}\Omega = 7.4\ \text{W}$$

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg hard-switches per sector, the PWM leg. The commutation edges themselves happen at f_comm ≪ f_sw and are neglected.

### 3.1 V·I Overlap (Hard Switching, Inductive Clamp)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus}\, I\, (t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}$$

$$P_{VI} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 3.2 Output Capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{ nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 3.3 Body-Diode Reverse Recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{ nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$

### 3.4 Dead-Time Diode Conduction (Two Dead Times per PWM Period)

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2 t_{dead}) f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{ ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 3.5 Gate Drive

This loss lands mostly in the driver and R_g, not the FET:

$$P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2\times50\text{ nC}\times10\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 3.6 Switching Totals Per Channel

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| V·I overlap | 1.94 |
| Coss | 0.05 |
| Qrr | 0.06 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| **Σ (in FETs)** | **2.66** |
| Gate drive (driver IC) | 0.05 |

### 3.7 Frequency Sensitivity

Each term above scales linearly with f_sw:

$$\frac{\partial P_{sw}}{\partial f_{sw}} = 2.66\ \text{W} / 48\ \text{kHz} \approx 55\ \text{mW/kHz}$$

Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.3 W per channel at 80 A. The cost is higher current ripple:

$$\Delta I_{pp} \approx \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5″ motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH line-to-line), D = 0.5, at 48 kHz: ΔI_pp ≈ 8.75 A. The resulting RMS ripple adds only 6400 → 6406 A² to conduction loss, which is negligible. The 48 kHz choice is justified mainly by acoustic noise and ripple torque, not by efficiency.

## 4. Total Loss Budget

| | Per channel | 4 channels (all at 80 A) |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 14.8 W | 59.2 W |
| FET switching | 2.66 W | 10.6 W |
| Shunt + copper | 3.5 W | 14.1 W |
| Gate drive + LDO + MCU | ~0.15 W | ~0.6 W |
| **Total** | **≈21.1 W** | **≈84.5 W** |

Efficiency at 80 A, 25.2 V with D ≈ 1 (full throttle):

$$P_{out} \approx 25.2\times80\ \text{W} = 2016\ \text{W} \;\Rightarrow\; \eta \approx 1 - \frac{21.1}{2016} \approx 98.9\%$$

### 4.1 Self-Consistent T_j Iteration

R_ds depends on T_j, and T_j depends on loss, so the steady state comes from iteration:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_{case} + R_{th,jc}\left[\frac{I^2 R_{ds,25}(1+\alpha(T_j^{(n)}-25))}{3}\cdot k_{sector} + P_{sw,FET}\right]$$

There is thermal runaway risk if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\cdot R_{th,ja} \ge 1 \;\Rightarrow\; I^2 R_{ds,25}\,\alpha\, R_{th,ja} \ge 1$$

For one FET with I = 80 A and R_th,ja,eff ≈ 25 K/W (PCB-mounted, no heatsink):

$$6400 \times 0.8\text{m} \times 0.0045 \times 25 / 3 \approx 0.19$$

This is below 1, so the operating point is stable and there is no runaway. Keep R_th,ja,eff < 130 K/W per FET to preserve this margin.
