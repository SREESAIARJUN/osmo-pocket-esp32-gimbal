## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step complementary PWM

In any commutation sector, two phases are active:

- **PWM leg:**
  - The high-side FET conducts for a fraction D of the period.
  - The low-side FET conducts for (1−D) as a synchronous rectifier, minus dead time.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET is held ON for 100% of the sector.

The phase current I therefore always flows through exactly **two FETs in series**.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.006 × 100) = **1.60 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Per-device and per-channel conduction loss

| Device | Loss expression | Value at 80 A, 125 °C |
|---|---|---|
| Return-leg LS FET (100% on) | I²·R_ds | 6400 × 1.6 m = **10.24 W** |
| PWM-leg HS FET | D·I²·R_ds | 10.24·D W |
| PWM-leg LS FET | (1−D−2t_dt·f_sw)·I²·R_ds | ≈10.24·(1−D) W |
| **Channel total** | 2·I²·R_ds | **20.5 W** |

Across a full electrical cycle, each of the 6 FETs is a return-leg FET for 1/3 of the time, and so on. The **cycle-averaged** per-FET loss is therefore:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{2 I^2 R_{ds}}{6}\cdot\ldots \;\Rightarrow\; \approx \frac{I^2R_{ds}}{3}\ \text{(HS+LS share)} \approx 3.4\text{ W}$$

However, the **instantaneous** peak per FET is 10.24 W for up to one sector, which lasts 1/(6·f_e).

- At high eRPM (for example f_e = 2 kHz), the sector is 83 µs. This is far shorter than the die thermal time constant, so the average value governs.
- At low eRPM or stall, the peak value governs. This is why **stall/desync protection** must cut current within roughly 50–200 ms.

### 1.4 Copper and parasitic conduction loss

$$P_{Cu} = I_{bus}^2 R_{bus} + I_{ph}^2 R_{ph} + I_{ph}^2 R_{shunt}$$

Assumed values:
- 2 oz outer layers plus 2 oz internal planes
- Phase pour ≈ 0.25 mΩ
- Bus pour share ≈ 0.15 mΩ
- Shunt 0.25 mΩ (shared DC shunt)

Result: P_Cu ≈ 6400 × (0.25 + 0.15) m + shunt share ≈ **2.6 W per channel at 80 A**. This loss lands on the PCB, not the junctions.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

The loss terms below apply per channel, and only on the **PWM leg**. The return leg does not switch within the sector.

**(a) Hard-switching V·I overlap (HS FET):**
$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 20\,\text{ns} × 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.968\ W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery of the LS body diode, dissipated in the HS FET:**
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\,\text{n} × 25.2 × 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}$$

Q_rr scales with dI/dt. Verify it at your chosen gate resistor.

**(c) Output capacitance loss:**
$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5 × 25\,\text{n} × 25.2 × 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.015\ W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction (LS FET):**
$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I \,(2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 × 80 × 200\,\text{n} × 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.614\ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive (dissipated in the driver and R_g, not the FET):**
$$P_g = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 50\,\text{n}×10×48\,\text{k} = 0.024\ \text{W per switching FET}$$

**Switching subtotal per channel ≈ 1.63 W** (FET-dissipated ≈ 1.60 W).

**Sensitivity:**
- Doubling f_sw to 96 kHz adds about 1.6 W per channel.
- Halving dead time to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel.

48 kHz is a sound compromise. It is above audible range and keeps current ripple low for low-inductance motors:

$$\Delta I_{pp} \approx \frac{V_{bus}D(1-D)}{L_{ll} f_{sw}}$$

With L_ll = 15 µH at D = 0.5, ripple is about 8.75 A.
