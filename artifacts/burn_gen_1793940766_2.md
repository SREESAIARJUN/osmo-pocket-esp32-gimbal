## 0. Architecture Clarification and Design-Review Flags

**Architectural note.** In a 4-in-1 ESC, the STM32H743 does not commutate the MOSFETs. The partitioning is:

| Layer | Device | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIH6 / VIT6 (480 MHz) | ArduPilot, bidirectional DShot600 master |
| ESC MCU (×4) | AT32F421K8U7 (AM32) or EFM8BB51 (BLHeli_S/Bluejay) | 6-step commutation, 48 kHz PWM, eRPM telemetry |
| Gate driver (×4) | FD6288Q (3-phase, 250 V, bootstrap) | High-side/low-side gate drive |
| Power stage (×24) | 40 V N-MOSFET, PDFN 5×6 or 3.3×3.3 | Inverter bridges |

The 48 kHz switching frequency is therefore set by the ESC MCU firmware (AM32 `PWM_FREQ`). The 600 kbit/s DShot link originates at the H743 timers.

**Design-review flags. These require a decision before layout.**

1. **80 A per motor on a nano form factor (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) cannot be sustained.** The analysis below shows about 31 W per channel and about 125 W per board at 80 A. On a board of this size, that figure is only survivable as a time-limited burst of roughly 1–2 s. Recommend specifying the rating as "80 A burst (≤1 s, 10% duty)" and "35–45 A continuous with airflow," or moving to a 30.5×30.5 mm board.
2. **The MOSFET part number is not yet fixed.** All calculations use the parametric assumptions in §1. They must be re-run against the selected part's datasheet values: R_DS(on)(T), Q_gd, Q_rr, Q_oss, and Z_th.
3. **The netlist in §6 is a reference design.** It has not been ERC/DRC-validated or prototype-verified. Pin assignments must be checked against the exact H743 package and the current ArduPilot `hwdef` conventions.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Current path in 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification

In each of the 6 commutation sectors, one phase is high (PWM), one is low (on), and one floats.

- **During the PWM on-time (D):** current flows through HS(phase A) → motor → LS(phase B).
- **During the PWM off-time (1−D):** current freewheels through LS(phase A, synchronous) → motor → LS(phase B).

In both states, current flows through exactly two conducting MOSFETs. Including the PWM ripple RMS term:

$$
I_{RMS}^2 = I_{DC}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}
$$

The ripple is:

$$
\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}
$$

For a typical 5-inch-class motor with L_ph ≈ 15 µH, at D = 0.5:

$$
\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{15\,\mu\text{H} \times 48\,\text{kHz}} = 8.75\ \text{A}
$$

This gives I_RMS² = 6400 + 6.4 ≈ 6406 A². The ripple correction is therefore below 0.1% at 80 A and is neglected from here on.

### 2.2 Total conduction loss per ESC channel

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I^2 R_{hot} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.95\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{24.96\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Per-MOSFET distribution (hot-spot identification)

Each phase spends 1/3 of the electrical period as the PWM leg, 1/3 as the low leg, and 1/3 floating.

$$
P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\, D\, I^2 R_{hot}
$$

$$
P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\, I^2 R_{hot} + \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)\, I^2 R_{hot}
$$

where I²R_hot = 12.48 W.

| Duty D | P_HS (W) | P_LS (W) | Hottest device |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.00 | 4.16 | 4.16 | Balanced |
| 0.80 | 3.33 | 4.99 | Low side |
| 0.50 | 2.08 | 6.24 | Low side |

**Conclusion.** Low-side FETs are the thermal hot spot during partial-throttle, high-current events. Examples are punch-outs and prop-wash recovery at around 50–80% duty. Place low-side devices nearest the thermal vias and heatsink.

### 2.4 Copper loss

$$
P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{3.2\ W\ per\ channel}
$$

This loss is distributed in the board, not the junctions. It still adds to the board's bulk temperature rise.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 3.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap (PWM high-side)

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}
$$

$$
P_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 24\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.16\ W}
$$

Gate-charge-based check:

$$
t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g}
$$

At an effective plateau current of I_g ≈ 0.8 A, this gives t_sw ≈ 12.5 ns, which is consistent with the assumed rise/fall time.
