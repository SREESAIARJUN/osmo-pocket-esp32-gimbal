# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage and DShot Design Model

**Job #24, Rev A (engineering baseline, not yet released for fabrication)**

---

## 0. Feasibility Findings and Assumptions

### Key findings

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** On every production 4-in-1 design, each ESC channel has its own MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). The H743 acts as the flight controller and sends bidirectional DShot600 to those four ESC MCUs. This document uses that architecture. Driving 12 half-bridges plus BEMF sensing directly from the H743 under ArduPilot is not supported.
2. **An 80 A per-channel burst on a "nano" (20×20 mm) board is limited by thermal capacitance, not by the silicon.** The math in §3 shows that four channels at 80 A dissipate about 85–90 W into a board with about 2–3 J/K of thermal mass. That yields roughly **1–2 s of burst**, not 10 s. The rating should be stated as "80 A / 1 s burst" unless the board grows to a 30×30 mm footprint with 5×6 mm FETs.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

In six-step commutation with synchronous rectification:

- During the PWM on-time D, current flows through HS_x and LS_y.
- During the off-time (1−D), current flows through LS_x and LS_y.

So **exactly two FETs conduct the full phase current at all times**, apart from dead time:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \left[ D\,(R_{HS}+R_{LS}) + (1-D)(R_{LS}+R_{LS}) \right] \approx 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha_R (T_j-25)]
$$

This creates electro-thermal feedback. The junction temperature is the solution of:

$$
T_j = T_c + R_{\theta jc}\cdot I^2 R_{ds,25}[1+\alpha_R(T_j-25)]
$$

$$
\Rightarrow\; T_j = \frac{T_c + R_{\theta jc} I^2 R_{25}(1-25\alpha_R)}{1 - R_{\theta jc} I^2 R_{25}\alpha_R}
$$

**Runaway condition:** the denominator reaches zero when

$$
R_{\theta jc}\, I^2\, R_{25}\, \alpha_R \geq 1
$$

With R_θjc = 1.5 K/W, the loop gain is 1.5 × 6400 × 0.001 × 0.0045 = 0.043. That is far from runaway at the die level. **The board is the bottleneck, not the junction.**

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_ds | Loss per conducting FET | P_cond per channel | P_cond for 4 channels |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 6.4 W | 12.8 W | 51 W |
| 100 °C | 1.34 mΩ | 8.6 W | 17.2 W | 69 W |
| 125 °C | 1.45 mΩ | 9.3 W | 18.6 W | 74 W |

### 1.4 Copper loss (not negligible on a nano board)

Use 2 oz (70 µm) copper, with phase and bus pours about 4 mm wide, about 15 mm long, on 3 parallel layers:

$$
R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{w\,t\,n} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot 0.015}{0.004\cdot 70\times10^{-6}\cdot 3} \approx 0.31\text{ mΩ}
$$

At 100 °C, R_Cu rises to about 0.40 mΩ. With roughly two such segments in the path, this adds **about 5 W per channel**. Use 8-layer stacking and stitch-via the phase pours aggressively.
