**Architecture assumption.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller. Each of the four motor channels has its own small ESC MCU (e.g. AT32F421 running AM32, or a BLHeli_32-class part). That ESC MCU generates the 48 kHz 6-step/complementary PWM. The H743 only generates DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry. The H743 should not commutate motors directly: ArduPilot does not support that, and it would compromise flight-loop determinism.

> ⚠️ **Feasibility flag.** 80 A per motor on a nano-format board is only achievable as a short transient. Section 2.6 shows that sustained 4×80 A dissipates about 85 W into a board with a heat capacity of roughly 3–5 J/K. That gives about 2 s to thermal limit with no airflow benefit. Before quoting "80 A burst" on a datasheet, define it as one of:
> (a) 80 A per motor for ≤ 1–2 s at 25 °C ambient, or
> (b) 80 A **total** across 4 motors.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_hot = 1.0 mΩ × 1.5 = **1.5 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current path per motor

With 6-step trapezoidal commutation and synchronous (complementary) rectification, the motor current always flows through exactly two FETs in series:

- During the PWM on-time: high-side FET of phase A, then the motor, then low-side FET of phase B.
- During the PWM off-time: low-side FET of phase A (synchronous freewheel), then the motor, then low-side FET of phase B.

So the instantaneous conduction loss per motor is independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Over one electrical revolution, each phase's high-side FET conducts for 120° (gated by duty D), and its low-side FET conducts for 120° plus the freewheel share.

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I^2 R, \qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\left[1 + (1-D)\right] I^2 R$$

**Worst case at full throttle (D → 1):** the load is evenly spread, so P ≈ 19.2 / 6 ≈ **3.2 W per FET**.

**Worst case at D ≈ 0.5:** the low-side FETs carry more, P_LS ≈ ½ · 9.6 ≈ **4.8 W** while P_HS ≈ 1.6 W. This is why low-side FETs should get the best copper (see §5).
