# STM32H743 Nano AIO: 4-in-1 ESC + Flight Controller (Job #18)

## Architecture Review

This document derives the 80 A burst thermal model, the 48 kHz switching-loss model and the ArduPilot bi-directional DShot600 timer/DMA allocation, then gives the netlists. Three findings shape everything below. Two of them change the requirements, so they come first.

| # | Finding | Impact |
|---|---------|--------|
| A1 | **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz PWM.** The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bi-directional DShot600. Each motor bridge is commutated by a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. This design uses one AT32F421 per motor (four total), which is the common 4-in-1 pattern. | The switching-loss model applies to the ESC MCU's PWM. The H743 timer budget covers DShot only. |
| A2 | **80 A per motor cannot be sustained on a 20×20 mm "nano" footprint.** The analysis below shows about 27 W per channel at 80 A. | 80 A is treated as a **≤5 s burst** on a **30.5×30.5 mm** power board, stacked with the 20×20 FC. If the hard requirement is a single 20×20 board, the realistic limit is about 35–40 A continuous and 55 A burst. |
| A3 | **DShot DMA buffers must avoid DTCM.** On the H7, DMA1/DMA2 cannot reach DTCM. The buffers also need cache maintenance or non-cacheable placement. | ArduPilot handles this when the hwdef is correct (see Section 3). |

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ /^\circ\mathrm{C}
$$

At T_j = 125 °C with the max datasheet value:

$$
R_{DS}(125) = 1.5 \times 1.55 = 2.33\ \mathrm{m\Omega}
$$

A typical-at-hot value of about **1.9 mΩ** is used for the nominal model. The worst case uses 2.33 mΩ.

### 1.2 Current path in a BLDC bridge

Consider trapezoidal (6-step) drive with complementary PWM. Two phases conduct at any instant. During the ON-time the motor current flows through one high-side FET and one low-side FET. During the OFF-time it free-wheels through the synchronous low-side FET instead of a diode. Either way, **two channels are always conducting**.

Conduction loss is therefore effectively independent of duty cycle:

$$
P_{cond} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j) \quad \text{(per motor)}
$$

With sinusoidal/FOC drive, the RMS current per phase is spread across three legs. The result is within about 5 % of the trapezoidal value, so 6-step is kept as the conservative case.

$$
P_{cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.9\,\mathrm{m\Omega} = 6400 \times 3.8\,\mathrm{m\Omega} = \mathbf{24.3\ W\ per\ motor}
$$

At the worst-case 2.33 mΩ this rises to **29.8 W**.

### 1.3 Copper and shunt losses

- **Copper:** 2 oz outer layers plus 2×2 oz inner pours, about 0.35 mΩ effective per phase path pair. This gives 6400 × 0.35 mΩ = **2.2 W**.
- **Shunt:** a single 0.25 mΩ low-side DC shunt per motor (for the current-sense telemetry) adds 6400 × 0.25 mΩ = **1.6 W**. If this is too much, use a bus-level hall sensor or a 0.1 mΩ shunt instead.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model (48 kHz)

All terms below are for one motor, which has one commutating half-bridge plus one static leg.

**(a) V–I overlap, hard-switched edge**

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus}\, I\,(t_r+t_f)\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\,\mathrm{ns} \times 48\,\mathrm{kHz} = 0.97\ \mathrm{W}
$$

The edge times come from Miller-plateau charge:

$$
t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,R_{g,tot}}{V_{CC}-V_{plateau}}, \qquad R_{g,tot} = 2.2 + 1.0_{int} + 3_{drv}
$$

$$
t_r \approx \frac{9\,\mathrm{nC} \times 6.2\,\Omega}{10-4.5} \approx 10\ \mathrm{ns}
$$

So t_r + t_f ≈ 20 ns, which confirms the assumption.

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction**

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I \cdot 2t_{dead}\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\,\mathrm{ns} \times 48\,\mathrm{k} = 0.61\ \mathrm{W}
$$

**(c) Reverse recovery**

$$
P_{rr} = V_{bus}\, Q_{rr}\, f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 40\,\mathrm{nC} \times 48\,\mathrm{k} = 0.048\ \mathrm{W}
$$

**(d) Output capacitance** (Q_oss form, valid for nonlinear C_oss)

$$
P_{oss} = V_{bus}\, Q_{oss}\, f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 30\,\mathrm{nC} \times 48\,\mathrm{k} = 0.036\ \mathrm{W}
$$

**(e) Gate drive**

This loss is dissipated mainly in the driver and the gate resistors, not in the FET. Two FETs switch per PWM period.

$$
P_{g} = 2\, Q_g V_{CC} f_{sw} = 2 \times 45\,\mathrm{nC} \times 10 \times 48\,\mathrm{k} = 0.043\ \mathrm{W}
$$

### 2.1 Loss budget at 80 A burst (T_j ≈ 125 °C)

| Term | Per motor | Four motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 24.3 W | 97.2 W |
| Copper + shunt | 3.8 W | 15.2 W |
| Switching overlap | 0.97 W | 3.9 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W | 2.4 W |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.08 W | 0.34 W |
| Gate drive | 0.04 W | 0.17 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 29.8 W** | **≈ 119 W** |

Two design conclusions follow:

- **Conduction dominates at 96 %.** Raising f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz costs only about 1.6 W per motor. So 48 kHz is justified, because it lowers current ripple and reduces audible noise on low-inductance motors.
- **Spend the money on R_DS(on), not switching speed.** Paralleling two FETs per switch would halve the 24.3 W conduction term to 12.2 W, but it does not fit in 30.5×30.5 with 24 FETs.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network (Cauer reduced to 3 nodes, per FET)

```
P_FET ──[Zth_jc 1.2K/W, τ≈2ms]──● T_case ──[Rth_c-pcb 2.5K/W]──● T_board ──[Rth_board-air]── T_amb
                                                                        │
                                                                   C_board (shared)
```

- **Per-FET power:** 29.8 W across 4 conducting FETs is about **6.1 W** for each conducting FET.
- **Junction rise over the board:**

$$
\Delta T_{j-b} = 6.1 \times (1.2 + 2.5) = 22.6\ \mathrm{K}
$$

This includes about 30 thermal vias (0.3 mm) under each drain pad. It reaches steady state in tens of milliseconds, so treat it as quasi-static.

### 3.2 Board thermal capacitance (the dominant node during a burst)

The 30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz power board with FETs and caps weighs about 7 g.

$$
C_{board} = \sum m_i c_{p,i} \approx (3.5\,\mathrm{g\ Cu}\times0.385) + (3.5\,\mathrm{g\ FR4/parts}\times1.1) \approx 5.2\ \mathrm{J/K}
$$

The board-to-air resistance depends on airflow:

| Condition | R_th,b-a |
|---|---|
| Still air | ≈ 18 K/W |
| Prop wash, ~8 m/s | ≈ 6 K/W |

The board temperature then follows a first-order response:

$$
T_b(t) = T_{b0} + P_{tot}R_{ba}\left(1-e^{-t/\tau}\right), \qquad \tau = R_{ba}C_{board}
$$

With prop wash, τ = 6 × 5.2 = **31 s**.
