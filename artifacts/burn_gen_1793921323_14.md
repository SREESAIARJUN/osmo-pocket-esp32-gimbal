## 0. Architecture Clarification

The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs. A 4-in-1 ESC uses four dedicated ESC MCUs (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32), one per motor. Each runs its own 48 kHz PWM and commutation loop.

```
VBAT ──┬─► 4× [ESC MCU → 3-ph gate driver → 6× N-MOSFET] ─► Motors 1–4
       │            ▲  bidirectional DShot600 (1 wire each)
       └─► Buck 5V/3V3 ─► STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ── TIM1 CH1–4
```

The thermal and switching model therefore applies to the ESC section. The DMA model applies to the H743.

**Design assumptions:**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V (max), 22.2 V nominal |
| Burst current | 80 A per motor (phase/line current), ≤ 2 s |

| Copper | 2 oz outer / 2 oz inner, 6-layer |

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{DS} = 1.2\,\text{mΩ}\times(1+0.0055\times85) = 1.76\ \text{mΩ}$$

Because R_DS(on) rises with temperature, conduction loss feeds back on itself. Solve iteratively (§3.3).

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

In any 60° sector, two legs are active:
- **Leg A (PWM leg):** the high side conducts for D·T_sw; the low side (synchronous rectifier) conducts for (1−D)·T_sw.
- **Leg B (return leg):** the low side conducts 100%.

At any instant, exactly two MOSFETs are in series with the motor current. So the per-ESC conduction loss is independent of D (ignoring dead time):

$$P_{cond,ESC} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.76\,\text{mΩ} = \mathbf{22.5\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-device loss (rotational average)

Over one electrical revolution, each switch carries current for 120° (1/3 of the time). Half of that is as a static conductor and half as a PWM-pair member. The average loss per FET is therefore:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} = \frac{P_{cond,ESC}}{6}\cdot 2 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2}... = \frac{22.5}{6}\approx 3.75\ \text{W}$$

The peak instantaneous loss in a statically-on FET is I²R = 11.3 W. At 30 krpm with 14 poles, f_e = 3.5 kHz, so a sector lasts about 48 µs. That is far below the FET's thermal time constant (ms range), so the junction sees the average plus a small ripple of about 1–2 K.

### 1.4 PCB copper / shunt loss

Assume a path resistance of about 0.5 mΩ per ESC (bus plane, phase pad, solder, motor-wire pad) and a 0.25 mΩ shunt:

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.75\,\text{mΩ} = 4.8\ \text{W}$$

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches hard: the high side switches, and the low side commutates through its body diode during dead time.

| Mechanism | Equation | Value (per ESC) |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap (hard switching) | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·40 ns·48 k | **1.94 W** |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss·V·f_sw = 40 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.05 W |
| Reverse recovery (LS body diode) | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 50 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.06 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_SD·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k | **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (2 switching FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_DRV·f_sw = 2·50 nC·10·48 k | 0.05 W (dissipated mostly in driver and R_g) |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.7 W** |

**Frequency sensitivity:** switching losses scale linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz they drop to about 1.35 W. The benefit of 48 kHz is lower phase-current ripple and quieter low-inductance motors. The cost is about 1.3 W per ESC at 80 A.

**Ringing check:** with a power-loop inductance of L_loop ≈ 2 nH and di/dt = 80 A / 20 ns = 4 A/ns:

$$V_{spike} = L\,\frac{di}{dt} = 8\ \text{V}\ \Rightarrow\ V_{DS,pk}\approx 33\ \text{V}$$

This leaves about 17% margin on a 40 V FET. The layout must keep 0402/0603 MLCC decoupling within 2 mm of each half-bridge.
