**Architecture flag 1: who generates the PWM.** The H743 does **not** generate the 48 kHz PWM. Each ESC channel runs its own MCU, typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071, running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 only sends bidirectional DShot600 commands. The two domains are therefore modelled separately (Sections 1–3 versus Section 4).

**Architecture flag 2: form factor versus 80 A.** "Nano" usually means a 20×20 mm stack.
- A 20×20 board physically fits only 3.3×3.3 mm FETs, with roughly 1.5–2 mΩ R_DS(on).
- On that footprint, an **80 A burst is thermally limited to about 1 s** (Section 3.4).
- 80 A for several seconds requires the **30.5×30.5 mm** form factor with 5×6 FETs.

I model the 30.5 mm case as the baseline and give the 20×20 result as a contrast.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j)=R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\quad \alpha\approx0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 100 °C: R_DS = 1.0 × 1.45 = **1.45 mΩ**.
At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = **1.60 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step with complementary PWM

At any instant, exactly two phase legs carry the phase current I_ph:
- One high-side FET is either ON or freewheeling through its complementary low-side FET.
- One low-side FET is solidly ON.

So at every instant two channel resistances are in series, independent of duty cycle D:

$$P_{cond,ch}=2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

The relationship between phase current and battery current depends on duty:

$$I_{ph}=\frac{I_{bus}}{D}$$

This means partial throttle with high torque is the worst case per bus amp. The 80 A rating is interpreted as **phase** current.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

$$P_{cond,ch}=2(80)^2(1.45\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{18.6\ W\ per\ channel}$$

Distribution across the six FETs of one channel:
- Each FET conducts for 120° of the electrical cycle.
- Average loss per FET = 18.6 / 6 ≈ **3.1 W**.
- Instantaneous loss in a conducting FET = I²R = **9.3 W**, which drives the junction ripple at low eRPM.

Across four channels: **P_cond,total = 74.3 W**.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing high-side FET hard-switches. Its complementary low-side FET commutates through its body diode during the dead time (near-ZVS turn-on).

### 2.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap

The transition time is set by the Miller plateau, using a plateau voltage of about 4.5 V:

$$t_r\approx\frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{G,src}}=\frac{10\,\text{nC}}{\approx0.65\,\text{A}}\approx15\ \text{ns},\qquad t_f\approx\frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{G,snk}}\approx 10\text{–}15\ \text{ns}$$

$$P_{sw}=\tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw}=\tfrac12(25.2)(80)(30\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{k})=\mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Body-diode conduction during dead time

$$P_{dt}=V_{SD}\,I_{ph}\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw}=0.8\cdot80\cdot200\,\text{ns}\cdot48\,\text{k}=\mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}=40\,\text{nC}\cdot25.2\cdot48\,\text{k}=\mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

This is dissipated mostly in the hard-switching FET.

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss}\approx Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}=\mathbf{0.06\ W}$$
