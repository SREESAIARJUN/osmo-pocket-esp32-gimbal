## 1. Conduction Loss Model (Trapezoidal BLDC, Synchronous Rectification)

During any 60° commutation sector, two phases conduct:

- **Return leg:** the low-side FET is on 100% of the sector.
- **Active leg:** PWM at duty D. The high-side FET conducts for D, and the synchronous low-side conducts for (1−D).

**Per-channel conduction loss** (duty-independent, because the active leg always has exactly one FET on):

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{hot}\big[\underbrace{D + (1-D)}_{\text{active leg}} + \underbrace{1}_{\text{return leg}}\big] = 2 I^2 R_{hot}$$

**Per-FET average loss.** Each FET is in a conducting role for 1/3 of the electrical cycle:

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R\, D,\qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}I^2R\,\big[1 + (1-D)\big]$$

The low-side FETs run hotter at partial throttle.

**At 80 A, using R_hot = 2.25 mΩ:**

- P_cond,ch = 2 × 6400 × 0.00225 = **28.8 W per channel**
- Instantaneous dissipation in a single conducting FET: I²R = **14.4 W**
- Four channels: **115.2 W**

**Additional copper and shunt losses:**

- Phase and bus traces: R_cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ per path, adding about 1.9 W per channel.
- Shunt: 0.25 mΩ, adding about 1.6 W at 80 A.

Budget roughly **+3.5 W per channel**.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the active leg switches. There is one hard-switched commutation pair per PWM cycle.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | 0.5·25.2·80·20 ns·48 k = **0.968 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 30 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.036 W** |
| Coss | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw | **0.024 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2t_d·f_sw | 0.8·80·100 ns·48 k = **0.307 W** |
| Gate drive (driver loss) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw | **0.038 W** |
| **Total P_sw per channel** | | **≈ 1.37 W** |

**Totals at 80 A burst:**

- Per channel: P_tot = 28.8 + 1.37 + 3.5 ≈ **33.7 W**
- Board (4 channels): **≈ 135 W**

**Design insight:** Conduction dominates (about 85%). Two consequences follow:

- Reducing f_sw from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves only about 0.7 W per channel.
- Paralleling two FETs per switch position halves P_cond, saving about 14 W per channel. This is the only lever that materially changes burst capability.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Junction-to-board transient

With a single-pulse Z_th(j-pcb) of about 1.2 K/W at t = 1 s (PQFN on 2 oz copper with vias):

ΔT_j-pcb = 14.4 W × 1.2 ≈ **17 K above local board temperature**

### 3.2 Board as a lumped thermal capacitance

| Element | Mass | c_p | C_th |
|---|---|---|---|
| FR4 (9.3 cm² × 1.6 mm, ρ = 1.85) | 2.75 g | 1.1 J/g·K | 3.0 J/K |
| Copper (4 × 70 µm) | 2.3 g | 0.385 | 0.9 J/K |
| 24 FETs, caps, solder | ~1.5 g | ~0.7 | 1.0 J/K |
| **Total C_board** | | | **≈ 4.9 J/K** |

### 3.3 Convective cooling in prop wash

Assuming h ≈ 75 W/m²·K over both faces (A = 1.86×10⁻³ m²):

G_conv = h·A ≈ 0.14 W/K, so R_th(b-a) ≈ 7.2 K/W

### 3.4 Burst-time equation

$$T_b(t) = T_a + P\,R_{th}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right),\qquad \tau = R_{th}C = 7.2 \times 4.9 \approx 35\ \text{s}$$

**80 A burst (P = 135 W):**

- Time for the board to rise from 40 °C to 110 °C is set by t ≈ −τ·ln(1 − ΔT/(P·R_th)), with ΔT/(P·R_th) = 70/972.
- This gives t ≈ **2.6 s**.
- At that point T_j ≈ 110 + 17 = **127 °C**, which is within the 150–175 °C absolute maximum but leaves little margin.

**Burst rating: 80 A for no more than 2 s from a 40 °C start, with a cooldown of at least 3τ (about 100 s at hover load).**

### 3.5 Continuous rating

With T_b,max = 100 °C and T_a = 40 °C, P_max = 60 / 7.2 ≈ 8.3 W. Adding the ~2 W of switching and miscellaneous losses that do not scale with I², and solving 8·I²·R_hot ≈ 8.3 − 2:

$$I_{cont} \approx \sqrt{\tfrac{6.3}{8 \times 0.00225}} \approx \mathbf{19\text{–}20\ A}\ \text{per motor}$$

Realistic marketing figures would be "**20 A continuous / 80 A burst (2 s)**". An 80 A continuous claim would be physically false for this footprint.

### 3.6 Firmware protection

Implement an I²t estimator on each ESC MCU:

- Track T̂ = T̂ + (P(I)·dt − (T̂ − T_a)·G·dt)/C.
- Derate current when T̂ exceeds 105 °C.
- Report temperature through the bidirectional DShot EDT channel.
