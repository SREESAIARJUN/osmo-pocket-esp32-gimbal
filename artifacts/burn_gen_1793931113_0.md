## 0. Architecture Reality Check (Read First)

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production AIO design, ArduPilot runs on the H743 and sends DShot over on-board traces to dedicated ESC MCUs. These run AM32 or BLHeli_32 on parts such as the AT32F421 or STM32G071. This package assumes **4× AT32F421K8U7 running AM32**, one per channel.
2. **"Nano" and "80A burst" conflict.** A 20×20 mm board cannot carry 80A per channel. Four channels at 80A dissipate about 88 W, as derived below. This package assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz board with 5×6 mm PDFN MOSFETs**. On a 20×20 board, derate to about 35–45A burst.
3. **"Burst" must be time-bounded.** As shown in §3, the board is adiabatically limited to roughly **2–4 s** at 80A on all four channels. The ESC firmware temperature limit and ArduPilot `MOT_` current limiting must enforce this.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (Per ESC Channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

**Temperature-dependent on-resistance:**

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.0 × (1 + 0.5) = **1.5 mΩ**.

**Ripple correction.** The phase ripple is:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L\,f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\,\mu H \times 48\,kHz} = 6.56\ \text{A}$$

The RMS current is:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2\left(1+\frac{(\Delta I_{pp}/I)^2}{12}\right) = 6400 \times 1.00056 \approx 6404\ \text{A}^2$$

The ripple contribution is negligible at this inductance. It becomes significant for L < 5 µH, as with low-kV pancake motors.

**6-step conduction.** At any instant two legs conduct:
- The **PWM leg** alternates between the high side (fraction D) and the synchronous low side (fraction 1−D). It therefore always conducts through one R_DS.
- The **static leg** conducts its low side continuously, through one R_DS.

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) = 2 \times 6404 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

**Per-FET average.** The electrical period is short compared with the die and package thermal time constants. Over a full commutation cycle (6 sectors), each of the 6 FETs therefore averages:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} = 19.2/6 = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

**Copper loss.** Add PCB copper loss: P_Cu = I²·R_trace. A 1 mΩ phase and bus path adds 6.4 W per channel. **Layout copper is as important as FET selection** at 80A. Use stacked 2 oz pours on 4+ layers and solder-wire pads directly behind the FETs.

### 2.2 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Its hard-switched FET sees full V_bus and I.

**(a) V–I overlap:**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 50\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{2.42\ W}$$

Sanity check on t_r: t_r ≈ Q_gd / I_g,Miller. With 10 nC and ~0.4 A at the Miller plateau (driver plus R_g = 10 Ω), t_r ≈ 25 ns. ✔

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period):**

$$P_{dt} = V_f\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}$$

**(c) Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 50\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

**(d) Output capacitance (both FETs in the switching leg):**

$$P_{oss} = 2 \times \tfrac12 C_{oss}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1.5\,\text{nF} \times 635 \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.046\ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive.** This is mostly dissipated in the driver and R_g, not the FET die:

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g\,V_{gs}\,f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\,\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.058\ W}$$

$$P_{sw,total} \approx \mathbf{2.84\ W\ per\ channel}$$

### 2.3 Loss Summary

| Loss term | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (T_j = 125 °C) | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching + dead time + Q_rr + C_oss | 2.84 W | 11.4 W |
| **Semiconductor total** | **≈ 22.0 W** | **≈ 88 W** |
| PCB copper (1 mΩ assumed) | 6.4 W | 25.6 W |
