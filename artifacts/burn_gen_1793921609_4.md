# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #5): Electrothermal and DShot Timer Model

## 0. Feasibility Summary

Some constraints govern everything below:

1. **The H743 is the flight controller MCU, not the ESC MCU.** Every 4-in-1 ESC runs commutation on separate low-cost MCUs (AT32F421, STM32G071 or EFM8, running AM32 or BLHeli). The H743 only generates bidirectional DShot600 and decodes eRPM telemetry. The architecture below reflects that split.
2. **80 A per motor in a "nano" (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) footprint is not thermally sustainable.** The math in §3 shows about 110 W of dissipation at 4×80 A. That figure is valid only as a **burst rating of roughly 1.5 to 3 s**, set by the board's heat capacity. Spec it as "80 A burst (≤2 s), ~35–45 A continuous," or move to a 30.5×30.5 mm form factor.
3. **Component values are representative assumptions** (a 40 V, ~1 mΩ-class DFN5×6 MOSFET and an FD6288-class driver). Rerun the equations with datasheet values for the real BOM. The netlists are reference designs, not a released, DRC-checked production netlist.

## 2. Loss Model per ESC Channel

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

In 6-step commutation, two phases conduct at any instant. With synchronous rectification, the current flows continuously through either the PWM leg's high-side switch or its complementary low-side switch, plus the static leg's low-side switch. Conduction loss is therefore **independent of duty cycle**:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_m^2\,R_{ds,hot} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.8\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{23.0\ W}$$

Each FET's share over an electrical cycle is:

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} = \frac{P_{cond}}{6} = 3.84\ \text{W}$$

The instantaneous loss in a conducting FET is I²R = 11.5 W. At 30 k eRPM the electrical period is about 2 ms. That is comparable to the die thermal time constant (~1–5 ms), so add a ripple term to T_j (see §3.3).

**Phase current ripple.** The ripple adds an RMS term. With winding inductance L ≈ 10 µH and worst case D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}D(1-D)}{L f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{10\mu \times 48k} = 13.1\ A_{pp}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_m^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 6400 + 14.3$$

The ripple contribution is +0.2%, so it is negligible.

### 2.2 Switching Loss (hard-switched PWM leg)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2}V_{bus}I_m(t_r+t_f)f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.3 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f\,I_m\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.4 Reverse Recovery

$$P_{rr} = V_{bus}\,Q_{rr}\,f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 50\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

### 2.5 Output Capacitance

$$P_{oss} = 2 \times \tfrac{1}{2}C_{oss}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.03\ W}$$

### 2.6 Gate Drive (dissipated in the driver and R_g, not the FET)

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g\,V_{GS}\,f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.7 Per-Channel Total

| Loss term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction | 23.0 |
| Switching | 1.94 |
| Dead time | 0.61 |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.14 |
| **Total per channel** | **25.7 W** |
| **4 channels** | **102.8 W** |

### 2.8 Board-Level (Non-FET) Losses

| Element | Model | Loss at 320 A total |
|---|---|---|
| Current shunt | 0.25 mΩ × 320² | 25.6 W ⚠ |
| Battery copper (4 oz, ~0.15 mΩ effective) | R·I² | 15.4 W |
| Bus capacitor ESR | see §2.9 | ~1–2 W |

The shunt alone dissipates 25.6 W. Use **0.1 mΩ or a Hall sensor** (e.g., ACS772/TMCS1100-class, or rely on per-ESC current telemetry). With a 0.1 mΩ shunt the shunt loss is 10.2 W.

**Grand total at 4×80 A burst: ≈ 125 W (with 0.1 mΩ shunt) to 145 W (with 0.25 mΩ shunt).**

### 2.9 DC-Link Capacitor Ripple Current

Per channel at D = 0.5:

$$I_{C,rms} = I_m\sqrt{D(1-D)} = 40\ A$$

The four ESC MCUs run unsynchronized, so the channel ripples add in RMS:

$$I_{C,total} \approx 40\sqrt{4} = 80\ A_{rms}$$

**Required:** low-ESR MLCC bank at the FETs (≥ 8 × 10 µF/50 V X7R per channel, placed less than 3 mm from the half-bridges), plus an external low-ESR electrolytic (≥ 1000 µF/35 V) on the battery leads. The external cap is mandatory to suppress lead-inductance spikes.
