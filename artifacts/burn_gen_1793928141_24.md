## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Assumptions

Three issues affect every number below and need sign-off first.

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** It runs ArduPilot and emits four bi-directional DShot600 streams. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. AM32 on an AT32F421 is assumed here. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs, not by the H743.

2. **"80 A burst" is ambiguous.**
   - **Per channel:** 320 A total from the pack. This is not credible on a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm board, as Section 3 shows.
   - **Total:** 20 A per channel. This is a routine design.

   I model **80 A per channel** as the worst case. Because all losses scale with I² (conduction) or I (switching), you can rescale directly. At 20 A per channel, conduction loss drops 16×.

3. **MOSFET parameters are placeholders.** They are representative of a 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm PDFN device and must be replaced with your selected part's datasheet values before release.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

R_ds(T_j) = R_ds,25 · [1 + α(T_j − 25)], with α ≈ 0.0055 K⁻¹ for this class of device.

At T_j = 110 °C:

R_ds = 1.2 mΩ × 1.47 ≈ **1.8 mΩ**

### 1.2 Six-step commutation with synchronous rectification

In each 60° sector, phase A is PWM'd and phase B's low-side FET is held on.

- During D·T_sw: current flows through HS_A and LS_B.
- During (1−D)·T_sw: current freewheels through LS_A (synchronous) and LS_B.

Current therefore always flows through exactly two channels in series:

P_cond,bridge = I_rms² · R_ds(T_j) · [D + (1−D) + 1] = 2 · I_rms² · R_ds(T_j)

The ripple-corrected RMS current is:

- I_rms² = I_ph² + ΔI²/12
- ΔI = V_bus · D(1−D) / (L_ph · f_sw)

For a typical 5″-class motor with L_ph ≈ 15 µH:

ΔI ≈ 25.2 × 0.25 / (15 µH × 48 kHz) ≈ 8.75 A

The ripple contribution, ΔI²/12 ≈ 6.4 A², is negligible against 6400 A².

**Result per channel:**

P_cond = 2 × 6400 × 1.8 mΩ = **23.0 W**

Add the copper path (motor pads, phase pours, and a ~0.3 mΩ ground/bus share):

P_Cu ≈ 6400 × 0.3 mΩ ≈ **1.9 W**
