# STM32H743 AIO 4-in-1 ESC Flight Controller (Job #21): System Architecture and Mathematical Model

## 0. Scope, Topology, Assumptions

### 0.1 Topology

DShot is the protocol *between* a flight controller and an ESC. So "H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC + bidirectional DShot600" implies this split:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──DShot600 bidir──► 4× ESC MCU (AT32F421 / AM32) ──► 4× 3-phase bridges (24 FETs)
                                               48 kHz PWM, 6-step / sync. rectification
```

- The 48 kHz switching frequency belongs to the ESC MCUs.
- The timer/DMA allocation belongs to the H743.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Hot R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)] = 1.2\,\text{mΩ}\,[1+0.006\cdot 85] = 1.81\ \text{mΩ}$$

### 1.2 Conducting Path

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, two phases conduct at every instant.

- The PWM leg alternates current between its HS FET (fraction D) and its LS FET (fraction 1−D).
- The opposite leg's LS (or HS) FET is held on.

Therefore exactly **two R_ds(on) are always in series**, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

Inductor ripple is typically ΔI ≈ 10–20 % of 80 A, which adds less than 0.4 % to the loss, so it is neglected here:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.81\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{23.2\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET Loss

Each phase conducts 120° of every 360°.

- **Instantaneous** (while in the conduction window, held-on FET): 80² × 1.81 mΩ = **11.6 W**
- **Cycle average:** 23.2 W / 6 = **3.87 W/FET**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. Per motor:

$$P_{sw} = \underbrace{\tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}}_{\text{V–I overlap}} + \underbrace{Q_{rr} V f_{sw}}_{\text{reverse recovery}} + \underbrace{\tfrac12 Q_{oss} V f_{sw}}_{\text{C}_{oss}} + \underbrace{2 V_f I\, t_{dead} f_{sw}}_{\text{dead-time diode}} + \underbrace{2 Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw}}_{\text{gate drive}}$$

| Term | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| Overlap | 0.5·25.2·80·30 ns·48 k | 1.452 W |
| Q_rr | 60 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.073 W |
| Q_oss | 0.5·40 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.024 W |
| Dead-time | 2·0.8·80·100 ns·48 k | 0.614 W |
| Gate (HS + LS) | 2·50 nC·10 V·48 k | 0.048 W* |
| **P_sw,motor** | | **≈ 2.21 W** |

*The gate loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET.

**Design levers:**
- Dead-time diode conduction is the second-largest term. Cutting t_dead from 100 ns to 50 ns saves 0.31 W per motor.
- Dropping f_sw to 24 kHz halves P_sw. However, it raises current ripple and acoustic noise.

## 3. Total Power Budget

| | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 23.2 W | 92.8 W |
| Switching | 2.2 W | 8.8 W |
| Shunt (0.2 mΩ low-side, 80 A²) | 1.3 W | 5.1 W |
| PCB copper (≈0.3 mΩ effective path) | 1.9 W | 7.7 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 28.6 W** | **≈ 114 W** |

**Efficiency at burst:** 4 × 80 A × 22 V (nominal effective) ≈ 7 kW of motor input power, so the bridge efficiency is about 98.4 %. The efficiency is fine. **The issue is entirely heat density.**

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Steady State: Not Achievable

A 20×20 mm board in prop-wash has R_θ,board-amb ≈ 3–5 K/W:

$$\Delta T = 114\ \text{W} \times 4\ \text{K/W} \approx 456\ \text{K}$$

**80 A × 4 continuous on a nano board is physically impossible.** The 80 A rating can only be a *burst* rating. It must be defined by a time limit.
