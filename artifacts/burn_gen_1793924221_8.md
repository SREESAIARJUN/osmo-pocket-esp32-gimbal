# STM32H743 AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC): 80 A Burst Thermal, 48 kHz Switching, and Bidirectional DShot600 Design

**Document scope:** analytical models, worked numbers, DMA/timer allocation, and reference netlists. All component values are representative assumptions. Before you build anything, check them against the datasheets of the parts you actually procure.

---

## 0. Architecture Correction

The STM32H743 does **not** drive the MOSFETs. A viable AIO design splits the work across two domains:

| Domain | Device | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (480 MHz) running ArduPilot | Attitude control and bidirectional DShot600 output/telemetry capture |
| ESC (×4, or 1 multi-channel) | AT32F421 / STM32G071 running AM32 (or BLHeli_32) | 48 kHz complementary PWM, commutation, BEMF sensing |
| Gate drive | FD6288Q (or equivalent 3-phase bootstrap driver), one per motor | High-side and low-side gate drive |
| Power stage | 6 × N-MOSFET per motor (24 total) | 3-phase bridge |

The 48 kHz switching frequency is set in the ESC firmware. ArduPilot only sees the DShot link.

### Design assumptions

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, worst case 25.2 V |
| MOSFET | 40 V, DFN 3.3×3.3 |
| R_DS(on) | 2.0 mΩ max at 25 °C, 10 V V_GS |
| R_DS(on) temperature coefficient | α ≈ 0.0045 /K |
| Q_g / Q_gd / Q_oss / Q_rr | 30 nC / 8 nC / 25 nC / 30 nC |
| Package thermal resistance R_θJC | 1.5 K/W |
| Board | 36 × 36 mm (30.5 mm mount), 8-layer, 2 oz outer and inner copper |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal with synchronous (complementary) PWM, dead time 100 ns |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

Evaluating at the design point T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{DS} = 2.0 \times (1 + 0.0045 \times 85) = 2.77\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Conducting-path analysis

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, the motor current always flows through exactly **two** MOSFET channels in series:

- **ON interval (D):** high-side of phase A, then the motor, then low-side of phase B.
- **OFF interval (1−D):** low-side of phase A (synchronous freewheel), then the motor, then low-side of phase B.

The current therefore always passes through two R_DS(on), regardless of duty cycle:

$$\boxed{P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)}$$

At 80 A:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.77\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{35.4\ W\ per\ motor}$$

### 1.3 Per-device distribution

Averaged over one electrical revolution, each phase is active 2/3 of the time. The six FETs therefore share the loss roughly equally, about **5.9 W per FET on average**. Within each 60° sector, however, two FETs carry 17.7 W each.

At the burst levels below (~400 Hz electrical at high RPM), this sector-level loading is filtered by the junction-to-case thermal time constant (~ms). The average figure is valid for T_j estimation, but add about 3–5 K of ripple.

### 1.4 Copper and shunt losses

| Element | Resistance | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Power plane / trace path (B+ to FET to phase pad, and return) | ≈0.5 mΩ | 3.2 W |
| Current shunt (if fitted) | 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switching device in each motor channel hard-switches. The fixed low-side and the synchronous FET are soft or ZVS.

### 2.1 V-I overlap

First, estimate the gate current during the Miller plateau:

$$I_g \approx \frac{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}{R_{g,ext} + R_{g,int}} = \frac{10 - 3}{5 + 1} = 1.17\ \text{A}$$

The Miller interval is then t ≈ Q_gd / I_g ≈ 7 ns. Adding current-rise time and layout inductance, use t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 15 ns.

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Remaining switching terms

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Body-diode dead time | V_f · I · 2t_dt · f = 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 kHz | 0.61 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f = 30 nC × 25.2 × 48 kHz | 0.036 W |
| Output capacitance | Q_o
