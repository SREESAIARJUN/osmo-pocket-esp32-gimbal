# Job #52: STM32H743 Nano FC with Integrated 4-in-1 ESC
## Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, Bidirectional DShot600 Timer/DMA Allocation, and Reference Netlist

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Feasibility

No MOSFET, gate driver, or board size was specified, so this document uses representative parameters. Every derived number traces back to the parameter table in §1, so you can substitute your real datasheet values and recompute.

**Architectural clarification.** In a 4-in-1 ESC the STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. Each ESC channel, or a shared ESC MCU, runs firmware such as AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 commands and receives eRPM telemetry back on the same wire. The loss model in §2–§4 therefore applies to the ESC power stage, and §5 applies to the H743.

**Feasibility flag: "80 A burst" on a nano board.** The numbers below show roughly 80 W of total board dissipation at a 4×80 A burst. No 20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm board can sustain that. It is achievable only as a time-limited burst, and §4.4 derives that time limit. The specification must therefore state the burst duration and duty cycle, not just the current. My recommendation is to either:
- define "80 A burst" as **≤ 2–3 s per motor, ≤ 10 % duty**, or
- move to a 30.5×30.5 mm footprint for continuous ratings above 45 A.

**Commutation model.** Six-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM, which is the AM32/BLHeli_32 default.
- In any 60° sector, two phases conduct and one floats.
- One leg is PWM-switched (HS on for D, LS on for 1−D).
- The other leg has its LS held fully on.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds(25)}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0055 × 100) = **1.55 mΩ**.

### 2.2 Bridge conduction loss per motor channel

**PWM leg.** The current I flows through the HS for fraction D and through the synchronous LS for (1 − D):

$$P_{PWM\,leg} = I^2 R_{ds} D + I^2 R_{ds}(1-D) = I^2 R_{ds}$$

**Static leg.** The current flows continuously through one LS:

$$P_{static\,leg} = I^2 R_{ds}$$
