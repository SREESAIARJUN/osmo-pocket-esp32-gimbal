# STM32H743 Nano AIO with 4-in-1 ESC: Architecture, Loss Model, BDShot Allocation, Netlists

## 0. Architectural Corrections

Two points in the brief need correcting before any modelling.

**1. The H743 should not commutate the motors.** The H743 has only 2 advanced timers (TIM1, TIM8) plus HRTIM and TIM15/16/17. That cannot cleanly produce 12 complementary, dead-timed pairs alongside the flight-control workload. It would also make DShot meaningless, since DShot is the FC-to-ESC protocol.

The correct partition is:
- **H743**: ArduPilot flight controller, sending DShot600 with bidirectional (EDT/eRPM) telemetry.
- **4× ESC MCUs**: AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071, running AM32 (or BLHeli_32). Each switches its own 3-phase bridge at 48 kHz.

**2. 80 A on a nano board is a burst rating, not continuous.** The thermal maths below shows a 30.5 × 30.5 mm board sustains roughly **20–25 A/channel continuous**. 80 A is survivable for only about **2–4 s**. All numbers are derived so you can substitute your own FET and stack-up.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Commutation geometry
In 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification, two of the three legs carry the phase current at any instant. During PWM-on the current flows through HS(A) and LS(B). During PWM-off it flows through LS(A) (synchronous rectification) and LS(B).

Either way, **exactly two R_ds(on) are in series** with the motor current, independent of duty cycle. The motor current is near-DC within a commutation interval, so I_rms ≈ I_phase.

$$P_{cond,FET} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

### 1.2 Thermal-electrical iteration (I = 80 A)
Iterating junction temperature against R_ds(on):

| Iteration | T_j (°C) | R_ds (mΩ) | P_cond,FET (W) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 25 | 1.00 | 12.8 |
| 1 | 90 | 1.36 | 17.4 |
| 2 | 110 | 1.47 | 18.8 |
| 3 | 115 | 1.50 | **19.2** |

The iteration converges if the board heat-sinks the FETs. If it does not, it runs away (see §3).

### 1.3 Ohmic adders
$$P_{sh} = I^2 R_{sh} = 6400 \times 0.25\text{ m}\Omega = 1.6\ \text{W}$$
$$P_{cu} = I^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\text{ m}\Omega = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

**Conduction total per channel at 80 A ≈ 24.0 W.**
