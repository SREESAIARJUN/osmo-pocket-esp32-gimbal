## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

Three points affect everything downstream.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In a "nano stack" the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and generates DShot. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. This design uses 4× AT32F421 or STM32G071, one per motor. The interface between FC and ESC is 4× bi-directional DShot600 plus power.
2. **"80A burst" is defined here as 80A RMS motor phase current per channel, all four channels at once.** Vendors often quote the peak of one channel, which is far easier. This document uses the hard case.
3. **A nano footprint cannot dissipate this power at steady state.** As shown below, 80A on all four channels is limited to roughly **2–3 seconds**. This is a thermal-mass problem, not a heatsinking problem. Firmware current and temperature limits are mandatory, not optional.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification

At any instant, exactly two phases carry current I_ph:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET for fraction D, then the synchronous low-side FET for fraction (1−D).
- **Static leg:** the low-side FET, on for the whole 60° sector.

So exactly **two FET channels are always in series** with the motor current, regardless of duty.

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

### 1.2 Per-FET RMS current

Each FET is involved for one-third of the electrical cycle.

- **Static low-side role:** current flows 1/3 of the cycle at duty 1, so its loss share is (1/3)·I²R.
- **PWM high-side role:** current flows 1/3 of the cycle at duty D. The complementary (1−D) portion lands on the same leg's low-side FET.
