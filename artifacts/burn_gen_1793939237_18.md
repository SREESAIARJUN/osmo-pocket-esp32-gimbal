## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

Two points need to be settled first because they change the design.

**1. The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In a 4-in-1 stack, the H743 runs ArduPilot and *generates* DShot. Each motor channel is commutated by its own ESC MCU, typically an STM32G071 or AT32F421 running AM32, or an EFM8 running BLHeli_S/Bluejay. The 48 kHz PWM, dead-time, and MOSFET losses belong to the ESC side. This document models both sides and keeps them separate.

**2. "80A on a nano (20×20 mm) stack" is only physically credible as a short transient.** Section 3 shows the adiabatic limit is a few seconds. Treat 80 A as a *burst* rating (≤2–3 s), not continuous.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-step (trapezoidal) current path

In any 60° commutation sector, exactly two half-bridges carry current I.

- **PWM leg:** the HS conducts for D·T_sw. With complementary PWM, the LS conducts for (1−D)·T_sw (synchronous freewheel).
- **Return leg:** the LS is held ON for the whole sector.

Per sector, the instantaneous I²R summed over all conducting devices is:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\left[D + (1-D)\right]_{\text{PWM leg}} + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)_{\text{return leg}} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

This is independent of duty cycle. This is the key result: synchronous rectification makes total channel conduction loss duty-agnostic.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence (self-consistent)

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0053\ \text{K}^{-1} \Rightarrow k_T(110^\circ C) \approx 1.45
$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.45\times10^{-3} = 18.56\ \text{W per channel}
$$

### 1.4 Per-device distribution (hottest die)

Each switch participates for 1/3 of the electrical cycle. At high throttle (D → 1), the HS of the PWM leg dominates:

- Instantaneous HS loss while active: 80² × 1.45 mΩ = **9.28 W**
- Electrical-cycle average for one HS: 9.28 × D × (1/3)... + return-leg LS role

$$
\bar P_{FET,max} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}(9.28)(D) + \tfrac{1}{3}(9.28) \approx 6.2\ \text{W at } D \approx 1
$$

This is conservative because each die serves both PWM-leg and return-leg roles across the cycle.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. There are two hard edges per period (HS turn-on and HS turn-off). The LS commutates softly through its body diode during dead-time.

### 2.1 V-I overlap (hard switching)

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 1.94\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I \,(2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

The recovery current is dissipated in the HS at turn-on.

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.4 Output capacitance (HS turn-on discharges its own C_oss and charges the LS C_oss)

$$
P_{oss} \approx C_{oss(er)} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1.5\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = 0.046\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (lost in the driver, not the FET)

$$
P_{gate} = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.6 Channel totals at 80 A burst

| Loss term | W / channel | W × 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (2I²R) | 18.56 | 74.2 |
| V-I switching | 1.94 | 7.8 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 | 2.4 |
| Q_rr | 0.05 | 0.2 |
| C_oss | 0.05 | 0.2 |
| Gate (driver) | 0.05 | 0.2 |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, if fitted) | 1.60 | 6.4 |
| Copper/vias (~0.3 mΩ path) | 1.92 | 7.7 |
| **Total** | **≈ 24.8 W** | **≈ 99 W** |

At 80 A and 25.2 V, the channel delivers about 2 kW of input power, so electrical efficiency is about 98.8 %. The *absolute* 99 W, however, is far more than a nano board can sink continuously.

### 2.7 Sensitivity

Conduction dominates (≈75 %). The levers that matter, in order:

1. R_ds(on): two paralleled dies halve P_cond.
2. Copper thickness.
3. Lowering f_sw only helps about 10 %. 48 kHz is fine.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network (per FET → board → air)

```
P_FET ──[Zth_jc(t) ≈0.3 K/W @10ms, 1.2 K/W ss]──●Tc
        ──[Rth_c-pcb ≈ 2 K/W per DFN via-array]──●Tpcb (shared node, C_board)
        ──[Rth_pcb-air ≈ 6–10 K/W board total, prop-wash dependent]── T_amb
```

### 3.2 Board thermal capacitance (20×20 or 30×30 ESC, 6-layer 2 oz)

| Component | Calculation | Heat capacity |
|---|---|---|
| Copper | ~2.5 g × 0.385 J/gK | 0.96 J/K |
| FR4 | ~3.0 g × 1.1 J/gK | 3.3 J/K |
| MOSFETs + solder | | ~0.4 J/K |
| **C_board total** | | **≈ 4.7 J/K** |

### 3.3 Burst endurance (time constant ≫ burst, so treat as adiabatic)

$$
\tau = R_{th,pcb-air} C_{board} \approx 8 \times 4.7 \approx 38\ \text{s}
$$

$$
\Delta T_{pcb}(t) = P R_{th}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right) \approx \frac{P\,t}{C}
$$

With all four channels at 80 A (99 W), starting from T_pcb = 50 °C and a limit of T_pcb = 110 °C (leaves headroom to T_j):

$$
t_{max} = \frac{C\,\Delta T}{P} = \frac{4.7 \times 60}{99} \approx 2.8\ \text{s}
$$

**Steady state is impossible:** 99 W × 8 K/W = 792 K
