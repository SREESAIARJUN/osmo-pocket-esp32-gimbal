# Job #62: STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC, 80 A Burst
## Architecture, Loss Model, Thermal Model, DShot/DMA Allocation, and Reference Netlist

This is a design-basis document. All device parameters are stated as assumptions. Before layout freeze, replace them with the datasheet values of the parts you actually select. The models are written so you can do that substitution directly.

## 0. Architecture Decision

The STM32H743 cannot and should not commutate the motors directly. Four sensorless BLDC controllers need 12 complementary PWM pairs with dead-time, plus back-EMF zero-cross comparators and ADC timing. That would conflict with ArduPilot's scheduler and gyro loop. The industry-standard and only practical partition is:

| Block | Device (class) | Firmware |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIH6 (TFBGA100) or VIT6 (LQFP100) | ArduPilot (ChibiOS), custom `hwdef.dat` |
| ESC MCU ×4 | STM32G071 / AT32F421 class | AM32 (supports bidirectional DShot, 48 kHz PWM) |
| Gate driver ×4 | 3-phase half-bridge driver, e.g. FD6288Q class | — |
| Power stage ×4 | 6× 40 V N-MOSFET, PDFN5×6 (or 3.3×3.3 for nano) | — |

The "48 kHz switching" is therefore set in AM32 (PWM frequency parameter), not by the H743. The H743 only produces DShot600 frames and decodes the eRPM telemetry.

**Reality check on "nano" plus 80 A.** The math in §3 shows that 4 × 80 A on a nano footprint is a burst-only rating of a few seconds. It requires 2 oz+ copper, 6–8 layers, and ideally a heat-spreader. Continuous current on 20×20 / 25.5×25.5 boards is realistically 25–45 A per channel. Market the rating accordingly.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction topology

With 6-step trapezoidal commutation and synchronous rectification (AM32 complementary PWM), two FETs carry the phase current at every instant:

- the high-side (or its complementary low-side) of the PWM-ed phase, and
- the low-side of the return phase.

So regardless of duty D:

$$
P_{cond}= 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j) + I_{ph}^2 R_{trace}
$$

### 2.2 Temperature-coupled R_ds(on)

R_ds depends on T_j, which depends on loss. The fixed point is:

$$
T_j = T_{ref} + P_{FET}(T_j)\,Z_{th}, \quad R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{25}[1+\alpha_T(T_j-25)]
$$

The closed form for the conduction-dominated case is:

$$
T_j=\frac{T_{ref}+k\,R_{25}(1-25\alpha_T)}{1-k\,R_{25}\alpha_T},\qquad k = I^2 Z_{th}\cdot(\text{share factor})
$$

If the denominator approaches 0, the result is **thermal runaway**. This is the design check for any Z_th you obtain from layout simulation.

### 2.3 Conduction loss at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0045 × 100) = 1.45 mΩ

$$
P_{cond}=2(80^2)(1.45\,\text{m}\Omega) + 80^2(0.3\,\text{m}\Omega)=18.56+1.92=20.5\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.4 Switching losses at 48 kHz

**Hard-switching V·I overlap** (one hard-switched FET per PWM period):

$$
P_{sw}=\tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}=\tfrac12(25.2)(80)(40\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz}) = 1.94\ \text{W}
$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$
P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V f_{sw}=60\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k}=0.073\ \text{W}
$$

**Output capacitance** (two FETs per bridge leg):

$$
P_{oss}=2\cdot\tfrac12 C_{oss,er}V^2 f_{sw}=1.5\text{n}\cdot635\cdot48\text{k}=0.046\ \text{W}
$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two edges per period):

$$
P_{dt}=V_{SD} I\,(2t_{dead}) f_{sw}=0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{n}\cdot48\text{k}=0.61\ \text{W}
$$

**Gate drive** (mostly dissipated in the driver and gate resistors):

$$
P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2\cdot50\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k}=0.048\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.5 Totals

| Loss | W / channel | W / 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 18.56 | 74.2 |
| Conduction (copper) | 1.92 | 7.7 |
| Switching overlap | 1.94 | 7.8 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 | 2.4 |
| Q_rr + C_oss + Q_g | 0.17 | 0.7 |
| **Total** | **≈23.2** | **≈92.8** |
