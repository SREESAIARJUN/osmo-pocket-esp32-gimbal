## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

Two points need correcting before the math, because they change the model.

**1. The H743 does not switch the MOSFETs.** In a 4-in-1 stack, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs. Each ESC MCU (AT32F421, STM32G071, or GD32E230 class, running AM32 or BLHeli_32) generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM through a 3-phase gate driver. The 48 kHz figure belongs to the ESC firmware. The DShot rate belongs to the H743. The two domains are modeled separately below.

**2. "Nano" and "80 A burst" conflict.** A 20×20 mm stack cannot shed approximately 70 W of total loss. This design therefore assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm ESC board**. The 80 A rating is valid only as a **time-limited burst** (target ≤ 5 s), not a continuous rating. The burst duration follows from thermal capacitance (§3).

**Baseline parameters.** These are representative of a 40 V, 5×6 mm, sub-1 mΩ trench MOSFET. Replace them with values from your chosen part's datasheet.

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 25.2 V | 6S full charge (worst case for switching loss) |
| I_ph | 80 A | Burst phase current (trapezoidal plateau) |
| R_ds(on),25 | 0.80 mΩ | at V_GS = 10 V |
| k_T | 1.5 | R_ds(on) multiplier at T_j = 125 °C |
| t_r + t_f | 20 ns | effective V–I overlap time with chosen R_g |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths under 6-step commutation

With 6-step trapezoidal drive, two phases carry I_ph at any instant. One leg is the PWM leg, which runs complementary/synchronous. The other is the static leg, with its low or high side held fully on.

In each 60° sector:
- **PWM leg:** the high side conducts for fraction D and the low side (synchronous rectifier) for 1−D.
- **Static leg:** one FET conducts continuously.

The instantaneous conducting resistance is therefore always R_ds in series twice, independent of D (dead time excluded):

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j) \qquad R_{ds(on)}(T_j)=R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

With k_T = 1.5 at 125 °C:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2(80)^2(1.2\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{15.36\ W}$$

### 1.2 Ripple correction

The phase-current ripple adds I_rms² = I_ph² + ΔI²/12:

$$\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

Take L_ph = 15 µH, V_bemf ≈ 0.85·V_bus and D ≈ 0.9. Then ΔI ≈ 4.7 A, giving ΔI²/12 ≈ 1.8 A². That is a 0.03% correction, so it is negligible at 80 A. It becomes significant only for very low-inductance motors (below about 3 µH).

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution (needed for the junction temperature)

Over one electrical revolution, each leg conducts 240° (120° high side + 120° low side), so each FET is on for 1/3 of the revolution. The worst-case FET is a low side that spends its 120° as part of the static leg and part as the synchronous rectifier:

$$P_{FET,max} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\cdot 1.0 = \tfrac{1}{3}(6400)(1.2\text{ m}\Omega) = 2.56\ \text{W}$$

### 1.4 Shunt and copper

| Loss | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Phase shunt (0.25 mΩ, if per-phase sensing) | 2 · 80² · 0.25 m | 3.2 W/ch |
| Single DC-link shunt (0.25 mΩ) | 80² · 0.25 m (at D ≈ 1) | 1.6 W/ch |
| Copper, 4 oz outer layers, ~0.5 mΩ path | 80² · 0.5 m | 3.2 W/ch |

**Recommendation:** use a single DC-link shunt per channel, or one shared shunt with per-channel estimation in ESC firmware.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. Using the synchronous scheme, there is one hard-switched high-side FET per channel.

### 2.1 V–I overlap

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = \tfrac12(25.2)(80)(20\text{ ns})(48\text{ k}) = \mathbf{0.968\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

The low-side body diode conducts during dead time and is then hard-commutated:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = (50\text{ nC})(25.2)(48\text{ k}) = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

Q_rr scales with di/dt and I_F. At 80 A, use the datasheet curve rather than the 25 °C headline value; doubling it is a reasonable hot-case estimate.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = (60\text{ nC})(25.2)(48\text{ k}) = 0.073\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

This term is often forgotten:

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\,I_{ph}\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 400\text{ ns}\cdot 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

It exceeds P_VI. Trimming dead time to 100 ns (if the gate driver and layout allow) halves it.

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated in R_g and the driver, not in the FET:

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2(60\text{ nC})(10)(48\text{ k}) = 0.058\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Per-channel and total budget (80 A burst, T_j = 125 °C)

| Term | W/channel |
|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 15.36 |
| V–I switching | 0.97 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 |
| Q_rr (2× hot) | 0.12 |
| Q_oss | 0.07 |
| Gate | 0.06 |
| DC-link shunt | 1.60 |
| Copper | 3.20 |
| **Total per channel** | **≈ 22.6** |
| **4-in-1 total** | **≈ 90 W** |

Efficiency at 4 × 80 A × ~21 V delivered (≈ 6.7 kW) is about 98.7%. The absolute 90 W is the problem, not the efficiency.
