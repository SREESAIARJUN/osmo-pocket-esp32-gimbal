# STM32H743 AIO 4-in-1 ESC "Nano" FC: 80 A Burst Thermal, 48 kHz Switching and Bidirectional DShot600 Design

**Document:** Job #4 Architecture Baseline, Rev A

## 0. Architecture Decisions and Flagged Risks

Three findings shape everything below. Resolve them before layout.

### Finding 1: The H743 cannot be the ESC commutation controller

Bidirectional DShot implies two parties: a flight controller sending commands and ESC MCUs replying with telemetry. The H743 also lacks the resources to drive 4 motors directly:

| Resource | Needed for 4× 3-phase sync-rect | H743 has |
|---|---|---|
| Complementary PWM pairs with hardware dead-time | 12 | 6 (TIM1, TIM8) plus HRTIM (5 pairs) = 11 |
| BEMF comparators | 4×3 | 2 comparators |

ArduPilot also does not run commutation firmware.

**Baseline adopted:**
- STM32H743 (FC, ArduPilot) sends DShot600 to 4× AT32F421 (or STM32G071).
- The ESC MCUs run AM32.
- Each ESC MCU drives 1× FD6288Q and 6× N-MOSFETs.

### Finding 2: "80 A" on a nano board is a short burst, not a continuous rating

Section 3 shows the board heats at roughly 18 K/s at 4×80 A. Expected figures:
- **Burst:** about 2–3 s.
- **Realistic continuous:** about 25–30 A per motor.

Specify and label the product accordingly.
