# STM32H743 AIO 4-in-1 ESC (20×20 "Nano") – Job #28 Engineering Model

## 0. Assumptions and a Feasibility Finding

The thermal model shows that **80 A burst on all four channels is not physically achievable on a 20×20 mm board.** It is marginal even on a single channel for more than about 2 to 3 seconds. ## 1. Conduction Loss Model

In 6-step operation with synchronous rectification, two legs are active at any instant:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side (HS) FET conducts for a fraction D of each period. Its complementary low-side (LS) FET conducts for 1−D.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET is on continuously.

This gives the following losses:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Each FET carries this current for one third of the electrical cycle in each role. Averaged over the cycle:

$$\bar P_{FET} = \frac{2 I^2 R_{ds}}{6} = \frac{I^2R_{ds}}{3}, \qquad \hat P_{FET} = I^2 R_{ds}\ \text{(peak, during its conduction interval)}$$

**At I = 80 A and T_j = 100 °C:**

- R_ds = 1.5 × (1 + 0.0065 × 75) = **2.23 mΩ**
- P_cond,ch = 2 × 6400 × 2.23 m = **28.6 W per channel**
- P̄_FET = **4.76 W** average
- P̂_FET = **14.3 W** instantaneous

**Copper losses must be added.** A 2 oz outer + 2 oz inner phase pour, about 8 mm long and 3 mm wide, is roughly 0.3 mΩ. That adds 6400 × 0.3 m ≈ **1.9 W per phase path.** Battery input current would be 4 × 80 = 320 A. That is beyond any nano pad, XT30 connector or 14 AWG lead.
