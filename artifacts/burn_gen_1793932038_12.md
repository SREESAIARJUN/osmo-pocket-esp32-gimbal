## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Distribution (6-step, 120° conduction)

In each 60° sector, one high-side FET (HS) of leg *a* and one low-side FET (LS) of leg *b* carry the phase current *I*. With complementary PWM at duty *D*:

- The HS of leg *a* conducts for fraction *D* of its 1/3 electrical period.
- The LS of leg *a* (synchronous rectifier) conducts for (1−*D*) of that same 1/3 period.
- The LS of leg *b* is on for the full 1/3 period.

Per-FET RMS currents averaged over an electrical cycle:

$$
I_{HS,rms}^2 = \frac{D\,I^2}{3}, \qquad
I_{LS,rms}^2 = \frac{I^2}{3}\left[1 + (1-D)\right]\cdot\frac{1}{2}\ \text{(averaged over its two roles)}
$$

At any instant exactly two channels of R_DS(on) are in the current path. The total per-ESC conduction loss is therefore independent of *D*:

$$
P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

### 1.2 Temperature-Dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

This is a self-consistent (electro-thermal) problem: P depends on T_j, and T_j depends on P. It must be iterated, as in §4.3.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond per ESC | P_cond per FET (avg, worst) | 4 ESCs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.20 mΩ | 15.4 W | 5.1 W | 61 W |
| 100 °C | 1.74 mΩ | 22.3 W | 7.4 W | 89 W |
| 125 °C | 1.92 mΩ | **24.6 W** | 8.2 W | **98 W** |

### 1.4 PCB Copper Conduction (Frequently Ignored)

The current path is: battery pad → bus plane → HS drain → phase pour → motor pad, plus the return. For a 25 mm board, 2 oz outer layers plus stitched inner layers give a realistic R_Cu,path ≈ 0.4–0.6 mΩ per ESC current loop at 80 °C.

$$
P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 3.2\ \text{W per ESC}
$$

The shared battery-input copper carries 4×80 = 320 A at full burst. Even 0.15 mΩ of shared copper dissipates **15 W** there.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing leg switches (one HS/LS pair per sector). In hard-switched inductive commutation the **HS** takes the V-I overlap loss, while the LS sees body-diode and Q_rr effects.

### 2.1 Gate Current During the Miller Plateau

$$
I_{G,on} = \frac{V_{DRV}-V_{pl}}{R_{drv,src}+R_G+R_{G,int}} = \frac{10-3}{3+2+1} = 1.17\ \text{A}
$$

$$
I_{G,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{drv,snk}+R_G+R_{G,int}} = \frac{3}{2+2+1} = 0.60\ \text{A}
$$

$$
t_{on} = \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{G,on}} = 10.3\ \text{ns}, \qquad t_{off} = \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{G,off}} = 20.0\ \text{ns}
