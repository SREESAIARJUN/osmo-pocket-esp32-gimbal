# STM32H743 Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: 80 A Burst Thermal Model, 48 kHz Switching Losses, and Bidirectional DShot600 DMA Plan

## 0. Architecture Clarification

The STM32H743 should be the **flight controller MCU** running ArduPilot. It should not drive the power stage directly. In a production 4-in-1 stack, each ESC channel runs on a dedicated low-cost MCU (e.g., STM32G071 or AT32F421 running AM32/BLHeli_32) or one MCU per ESC board. The H743 talks to them over bidirectional DShot600. The model below follows that split:

```
[6S LiPo] ──► [4-in-1 ESC: 4× MCU + 4× 3-phase driver + 24 MOSFETs] ◄── DShot600 bidir ──► [STM32H743 FC]
```

**Feasibility warning.** 80 A burst per channel is only plausible on a 30×30 mm or larger board with heavy copper and very low R_DS(on) MOSFETs. On a 20×20 "nano" board, the numbers below show it is not credible beyond about 1 s. All component values are representative assumptions. Replace them with your selected parts' datasheet values before release.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

In 6-step operation, two MOSFETs carry the phase current at any instant: the PWM leg (high side, or its synchronous low side during off-time) plus the static low-side leg.

$$P_{cond} = 2 \cdot I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot R_{DS(on)}(T_j),\qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j - 25)] \approx k_T R_{25}$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.6\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{20.5\ W\ per\ channel}$$

Ripple correction: if inductor ripple ΔI_pp is significant, use
$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \Delta I_{pp}^2/12.$$
For 40 A pk-pk ripple this adds +2.1 % and is negligible at 80 A.

Per-FET time average: each FET conducts 120° of 360°, so average conduction loss per FET is about 20.5 / 6 ≈ **3.4 W**. Peak instantaneous loss is 10.2 W per conducting FET.

**PCB copper loss** (not negligible at 80 A). A 2 oz trace has R_sheet ≈ 0.25 mΩ/□. Assume 4 parallel layers and a 3-square path.
$$R_{Cu} \approx \frac{0.25 \cdot 3}{4} \cdot 1.3\ (\text{at }90^\circ\text{C}) \approx 0.24\ \text{m}\Omega$$
$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \cdot 0.24\text{m} \approx \mathbf{1.6\ W\ per\ channel}$$

## 3. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

### 3.1 V–I overlap (hard-switched high side)
$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 3.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction
$$P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I_{ph} \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 600\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.84\ W}$$

This is as large as the switching loss. Cutting dead time to 150 ns saves about 0.9 W per channel. It is the cheapest optimization available.

### 3.3 Reverse recovery
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$
Q_rr also adds turn-on current overshoot, which increases P_VI by roughly 5–10 %.

### 3.4 Output capacitance
$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \times 2 = 0.5\cdot 40\text{n}\cdot 25.2\cdot 48\text{k}\cdot 2 = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$
