# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Thermal, Switching, and DShot Timer Model

This model rests on several assumptions, listed first because they change the numbers:

1. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In ArduPilot systems the H743 runs the flight stack and sends bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs. These are typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32, and each one generates the 48 kHz PWM and six-step commutation. The architecture below keeps that split.
2. **80A on a 20×20 nano board is a burst rating only.** The math below gives roughly 20W of loss per channel and 80W+ per board at 80A. A 20×20 board has no meaningful steady-state path for that. Expect about 3–8 seconds, set by thermal mass, not a continuous rating. Most 20×20 designs are honestly rated 35–45A continuous.
3. **The component parameters are representative placeholders.** Replace them with your selected MOSFET and driver datasheet values before signoff.


## 2. MOSFET Conduction Losses at 80A Burst

### 2.1 Reference parameters (assumed)

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| MOSFET | 40V N-ch, PDFN 5×6 | – |
| R_DS(on) at 25 °C, V_GS = 10V | R₂₅ | 1.0 mΩ |
| Temperature coefficient | α | 0.0045 /K (≈1.4× at 115 °C) |
| Gate charge | Q_g | 50 nC |
| Output charge at 25V | Q_oss | 35 nC |
| Reverse recovery charge | Q_rr | 40 nC |
| Body diode V_F at 80A | V_F | 0.85 V |
| Junction-to-case thermal resistance | R_θJC | 1.0 K/W |
| Bus voltage (6S full) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| Dead time | t_dt | 200 ns per edge |

### 2.2 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right]$$

At T_j = 115 °C: R_DS = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0045 × 90) = **1.405 mΩ**.

### 2.3 Six-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary PWM

In each 60° sector, one phase is PWM-switched and another phase's low-side FET is held on.

| FET | Conducting fraction | RMS² current |
|---|---|---|
| Active high-side (HS) | D | I²·D |
| Synchronous low-side, same leg | 1 − D | I²·(1 − D) |
| Return low-side | 1 | I² |

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{DS}\left[D + (1-D) + 1\right] = 2 I^2 R_{DS}$$

The result is independent of duty cycle. With synchronous rectification, current always flows through exactly two channels.

At I = 80A:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.405\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{17.98\ W}$$

### 2.4 PCB copper and shunt losses

- Board path (4 oz inner/outer planes, about 0.25 mΩ round trip per channel): 80² × 0.25 mΩ = **1.60 W**
- Current shunt: 80² × 0.25 mΩ = **1.60 W**

**Board design note:** a single 0.25 mΩ shunt carrying the summed current of four motors (up to 320A burst) would dissipate 25.6W. Use one shunt per channel, or a lower value such as 0.1 mΩ with an INA240 or equivalent amplifier.

## 3. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

### 3.1 Hard-switching transition loss (HS FET)

The driver is a 3-phase driver rated about 1.5A source / 1.8A sink. Effective transition times are t_r ≈ 15 ns and t_f ≈ 15 ns.

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 3.2 Dead-time body diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.85 \times 80 \times 400\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.31\ W}$$

To reduce this, cut dead time to 100–150 ns if your driver and gate loop allow it. That saves about 0.5W per channel.

### 3.3 Reverse recovery, output capacitance, and gate drive losses

| Loss term | Equation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw = 40n × 25.2 × 48k | 0.048 W |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss · V_bus · f_sw = 35n × 25.2 × 48k | 0.042 W |
| Gate drive (2 switching FETs, 10V) | 2 · Q_g · V_GS · f_sw | 0.048 W (dissipated in driver/gate resistors) |

### 3.4 Loss budget per channel at 80A

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (2 FETs) | 17.98 |
| Transition switching | 1.45 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.31 |
| Reverse recovery + output capacitance | 0.09 |
| Gate drive | 0.05 |
| Copper + shunt | 3.20 |
| **Total per channel** | **≈ 24.1 W** |
| **Total, 4 channels at 80A** | **≈ 96 W** |

At 48 kHz, conduction dominates: about 75% of losses come from I²R, not switching. Raising f_sw to 96 kHz adds only about 2.8W per channel. The highest-leverage fix is paralleling MOSFETs, i.e. 12 → 24 FETs. That halves conduction loss, but the extra FETs are hard to fit on a 20×20 board.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Per-FET dissipation

The worst-case FET is the return low-side, which conducts 100% of the time in its sector:

$$P_{FET,max} = I^2 R_{DS} = 6400 \times 1.405\text{m} = 9.0\text{ W (plus dead-time share)} \approx 9.7\text{ W}$$

Commutation rotates this load across all six FETs. The time-averaged loss per FET is (17.98 + 2.85)/6 ≈ **3.5 W**. Electrical rotation is far faster than the thermal time constants, so the per-FET average is what heats the board.

### 4.2 Cauer/Foster network

```
Tj ──[Rθjc 1.0 K/W]── Tcase ──[Rθcb 0.5 K/W (solder+vias)]── Tboard ──[Rθba]── Tamb
        Cj≈0.05 J/K                  Cb (shared copper mass)
```

**Junction rise above board:**

- Average: 3.5 W × 1.5 K/W = **5.3 K**
- Instantaneous worst-sector: 9.7 W × 1.5 K/W ≈ **14.6 K**

This is a ceiling for a single sector. In reality the junction thermal capacitance smooths it.

### 4.3 Board thermal mass (adiabatic burst model)

Estimated board composition for a 20×20 board with an 8-layer, 4 oz design:

- Copper ≈ 4.5 g → 4.5 × 0.385 = 1.73 J/K
- FR4 ≈ 3.0 g → 3.0 × 1.1 = 3.3 J/K
- MOSFETs and other parts ≈ 2 g → ~1.0 J/K
- **Total C_b ≈ 6 J/K**

Under prop wash (~10 m/s), the board-to-ambient resistance is R_θba ≈ 4 K/W, so the time constant is τ = R·C = 24 s.

For a step load:

$$T_b(t) = T_{amb} + P R_{\theta ba}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right)$$

With P = 96W, the steady-state asymptote would be 96 × 4 = 384 K above ambient. That number is physically impossible to reach. It shows that 80A on all four motors simultaneously is strictly a burst rating.

**Time to board limit** (T_amb = 35 °C, board limit 105 °C so the junction stays at or below about 125 °C):

$$t = -\tau \ln\!\left(1 - \frac{70}{384}\right) = 24 \times 0.201 = \mathbf{4.8\ s}$$

**Burst duration by load:**

| Scenario | P_total | Time to board 105 °C |
|---|---|---|
| 4 × 80A | 96 W | ~4.8 s |
| 4 × 60A | ~56 W | ~9 s |
| 4 × 40A | ~26 W | ~24 s |
| 4 × 30A | ~16 W | Steady-state ΔT ≈ 64 K, so continuous is marginal |

Practical rating to publish: **30–35A continuous / 80A burst for 3 s at ≤ 35 °C ambient, with airflow.**

### 4.4 Derating firmware hooks

- **ESC side:** AM32 temperature limit about 110 °C, plus a current limit using its PID.
- **ArduPilot side:**
  - Use the bidirectional DShot extended telemetry (EDT) temperature together with `ESC_TLM`.
  - Set `BATT_CURR_LIMIT`/`BATT_MAX_AMPS` if your battery monitor supports it.
  - Log `ESC[n].Temp` and alarm at 100 °C.
