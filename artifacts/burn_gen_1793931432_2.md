# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage and DShot Engineering Model

**Job #51 design note, Rev A (preliminary).**

---

## 0. Architecture Assumptions

The brief combines several items that live on different processors, so the partitioning is stated first:

| Function | Processor | Firmware |
|---|---|---|
| Flight control, DShot600 master, bidirectional eRPM decode | STM32H743 (400 MHz) | ArduPilot (ChibiOS) |
| Commutation, **48 kHz PWM**, telemetry response | 4× ESC MCU (e.g., AT32F421 / STM32G071) | AM32 or BLHeli_32-class |
| Gate drive | 4× 3-phase half-bridge driver (FD6288Q-class) | none |
| Power stage | 24× N-ch MOSFETs, 6 per motor | none |

The H743 does not switch the bridges. The 48 kHz switching analysis applies to the ESC MCU and power stage. The DShot/DMA analysis applies to the H743.

**Physical feasibility flag.** A "nano" footprint (20×20 to 30.5×30.5 mm) cannot sustain 4 × 80 A. Two reasons, quantified below:

- **Thermal:** about 90 W dissipation at 80 A on all four channels.
- **Input path:** 320 A through solder pads and a connector rated about 60 A continuous.

I model 80 A as a **short per-channel burst**. The rating must be stated with a duration (§2.6). The geometry modeled is a **30×30 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz board**. A 20×20 board fares considerably worse.

## 2. Loss Model at 80 A Burst

### 2.1 Temperature-Dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At the design point T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{ds}=1.2\times(1+0.0045\cdot85)=1.66\text{ m}\Omega$$

### 2.2 Conduction Loss (Six-Step Trapezoidal, Complementary PWM)

Two legs are active in each 60° sector:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for D, and the synchronous low-side FET conducts for (1 − D).
- **Static leg:** the low-side FET conducts for the full sector.

Per motor:

$$P_{cond}=I^2R_{ds}[D+(1-D)]+I^2R_{ds}+I^2R_{cu}=I^2(2R_{ds}+R_{cu})$$

$$P_{cond}=80^2\,(2\cdot1.66+0.4)\times10^{-3}=6400\times3.72\times10^{-3}=\mathbf{23.8\ W/motor}$$

The FET-only portion is 21.2 W.

### 2.3 Switching Loss at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. Hard-switching overlap:

$$P_{sw}=\tfrac12V_{bus}I(t_r+t_f)f_{sw}=0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot27\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k}=1.31\text{ W}$$

Remaining terms:

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Coss | Q_oss·V·f | 0.030 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f | 0.024 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_SD·I·2t_dt·f | 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k = 0.61 W |
| Gate drive (2 FETs switching) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f | 0.04 W (dissipated in the driver/gate resistors) |
| **Total P_sw** | | **≈ 2.0 W/motor** |

**Design levers:**

- Dead-time diode loss (0.61 W) is about 30% of switching loss. Trimming dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per motor, if shoot-through margin is verified on the scope.
- Raising f_sw to 96 kHz doubles P_sw to about 4 W per motor. That is why 48 kHz is a sensible ceiling here.
