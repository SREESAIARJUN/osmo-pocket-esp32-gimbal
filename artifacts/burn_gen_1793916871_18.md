### 0.1 Architecture correction

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. A 4-in-1 ESC runs its own MCU or MCUs, which do the 48 kHz PWM and commutation. The H743 is the flight controller and sends bidirectional DShot600 to the ESC. This is the standard split, and the rest of this document follows it:

| Block | Device (reference) | Role |
|---|---|---|
| FC MCU | STM32H743VIH6 (400 MHz) | ArduPilot, DShot600 TX, eRPM capture |
| ESC MCU ×4 | AT32F421K8U7 (AM32) or 1× STM32G071 per 2 ch | 6-step trapezoidal, 48 kHz complementary PWM |
| Gate driver ×4 | FD6288Q (3-phase, bootstrap) | ≈1.0 A source / ≈1.5 A sink effective |
| MOSFET ×24 | 40 V N-ch, 3.3×3.3 PQFN class | Single FET per switch |
| Bus | 6S LiPo, V_bus,max = 25.2 V | |

## 1. Conduction Topology (6-Step, Complementary / Synchronous Rectification)

At any instant, two phases conduct:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side (HS) switch conducts for D·T_sw. The low-side (LS) of the same leg conducts the freewheel current for (1−D)·T_sw, minus dead time.
- **Static leg:** the LS switch is fully on.

So the motor current always flows through exactly **two** channels in series:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

Over one electrical revolution, each phase spends 1/3 of the time as the PWM leg, 1/3 as the static low leg, and 1/3 floating. The per-switch conduction duty is therefore:

$$
\delta_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}D, \qquad \delta_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D) + \tfrac{1}{3}
$$

### 1.1 RMS current including ripple

With peak-to-peak ripple ΔI and ripple ratio r = ΔI / I:

$$
I_{rms} = I\sqrt{1 + \frac{r^2}{12}}
$$

For a typical 5" motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH):

$$
\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{2L f_{sw}}
$$

This gives r ≈ 0.15 near full throttle. I_rms/I is then 1.0009, which is negligible, so I_rms ≈ I is used below.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_0\,[1 + \alpha(T_j - 25)]
$$

At T_j = 100 °C:

$$
R_{DS} = 1.2 \times 1.45 = 1.74\ \text{m}\Omega
$$
