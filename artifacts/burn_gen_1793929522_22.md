# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with Integrated 4-in-1 ESC (Nano Class)
## Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, Bi-Directional DShot600 DMA Plan, and Reference Netlist

I have no prior context for "Job #22," so this analysis states its assumptions explicitly. **Section 0 is the most important part.** The math shows that an 80 A per-motor rating on a nano board is only a short burst rating. The board cannot sustain it.

## 0. Architectural Reality Check

| Item | Implication |
|---|---|
| **The H743 does not switch the MOSFETs.** | In any 4-in-1 design, each motor has its own ESC MCU (e.g., AT32F421 running AM32). That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM and does commutation. The H743 only sends DShot600 frames and receives eRPM telemetry. |
| **80 A × 4 motors at full throttle draws about 320 A from the battery (about 8 kW at 6S).** | No nano-class connector, battery lead, or input plane can carry this. A practical system draws roughly 80 to 120 A total from the battery. |
| **The thermal result (Section 3)** | About 133 W of total dissipation at 80 A per motor. The board survives this for only about 1 to 3 s. The sustainable level is about 20 to 25 A per motor continuous. |

Specify the product as **"80 A burst (≤1 s, 10% duty)"** and derive the continuous rating from Section 3.

## 2. Loss Model (per motor)

### 2.1 Conduction loss

With synchronous rectification, the phase current always flows through exactly two channels in series:
- During the on-time: one high-side FET plus the opposite low-side FET.
- During freewheel: two low-side FETs.

Conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle D:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds,25}\,k(T_j)$$

Since R_ds depends on T_j and T_j depends on loss, solve self-consistently:

$$T_j = T_{board} + \frac{P_{cond}+P_{sw}}{6}\,(R_{\theta jc}+R_{\theta cb})$$

Results at 80 A:

| T_j | k | P_cond per motor |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 | 20.5 W |
| 100 °C | 1.45 | **29.7 W** |
| 125 °C | 1.60 | 32.8 W |

### 2.2 Switching loss at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd high-side FET hard-switches. The low-side commutates softly, apart from reverse recovery.

**Hard-switching overlap loss:**
$$P_{ov} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f)f_{sw} = \tfrac12(25.2)(80)(30\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz}) = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

**Output capacitance:**
$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.023\ \text{W}$$
