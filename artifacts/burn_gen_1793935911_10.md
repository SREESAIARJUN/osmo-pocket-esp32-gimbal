# STM32H743 AIO + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #37): Power Stage, Thermal, and Bi-Directional DShot Allocation

## 0. Assumptions and Architecture Corrections

I don't have the Job #37 requirements, BOM, or layout files. This model is fully parameterized, and the numbers use representative values that you should replace with your datasheet values.

| Parameter | Value used | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | 6S, V_bus = 25.2 V (full charge) | Worst case for switching loss |
| Burst current | 80 A per motor, all 4 simultaneously | Worst case |
| f_sw | 48 kHz | Center-aligned, complementary (synchronous) PWM |
| MOSFET | 40 V, 3.3×3.3 DFN class | R_ds(on),25 = 1.4 mΩ @ V_gs = 10 V; Q_g = 40 nC; t_r + t_f ≈ 40 ns with the driver; Q_rr ≈ 30 nC; V_f ≈ 0.8 V |

| Board | Nano form factor, 20×20 mm (4-layer, 2 oz outer copper) | |
| Ambient | 40 °C | T_j,max design limit 125 °C, board limit ~110 °C |

**Architecture points:**

1. **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz motor PWM.** In an ArduPilot AIO, the H743 sends DShot600 frames to the ESC MCUs (typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). Those MCUs generate the 48 kHz complementary PWM, commutation, and the eRPM telemetry. ArduPilot has no FOC/6-step motor drive on the FC MCU. The power stage math below therefore applies to the ESC side, and the timer/DMA section applies to the H743.
2. **80 A on a 20×20 board is a seconds-scale burst rating, not a continuous rating.** Section 3 quantifies this. I'd flag it as a requirements risk before layout.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_ds = 1.4 × (1 + 0.006 × 85) = **2.11 mΩ**

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step synchronous drive

In each commutation sector, current flows through one high-side and one low-side device of two different phases. With synchronous rectification, the PWM'd leg passes current through either its HS FET (duty D) or its LS FET (1−D). So exactly **two FET channels are always in series**:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j) + I^2 R_{Cu}$$

- FET term: 80² × 2 × 2.11 mΩ = 6400 × 4.22 mΩ = **27.0 W**
- Copper/shunt term (assume R_Cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ, including the shunt and the pads): 6400 × 0.3 mΩ = **1.9 W**

$$P_{cond,motor} \approx 28.9\ \text{W}$$

### 1.3 Per-device split

Over an electrical cycle each of the 6 FETs carries current for about 1/3 of the time. Average conduction per FET is therefore 27.0 / 6 × 2 ≈ **9 W while conducting, ~4.5 W cycle-averaged**. Electrical frequency is far above the junction thermal time constant at high eRPM, so the averaged value is valid. At **low eRPM** (startup or a stalled motor), one FET can hold the full 80 A for tens of ms. Check that case against the Z_th(j-c) transient curve.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches hard. The commutation events (≤ a few kHz electrical) are negligible by comparison.

| Mechanism | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 k | **1.94 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k | **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 30 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | **0.036 W** |
| C_oss | ½·C_oss,eff·V²·f_sw (C_oss ≈ 1 nF) | **0.015 W** |
| Gate charge (dissipated mostly in the driver) | Q_g·V_gs·f_sw × 2 FETs = 40 nC × 10 × 48 k × 2 | **0.038 W** |

$$P_{sw,motor} \approx 2.64\ \text{W}$$

**Sensitivity.** Overlap loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it falls to ~1.3 W total, at 96 kHz it rises to ~5.3 W. Dead-time loss is the second-largest term, so tightening dead time to 50 ns (if the driver and FET permit it without shoot-through) saves ~0.3 W per motor.

## 3. Total Loss and Thermal Calculation

### 3.1 Loss budget (80 A burst, all four motors)

| Term | Per motor | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 27.0 W | 108.0 W |
| Copper/shunt | 1.9 W | 7.6 W |
| Switching | 2.6 W | 10.6 W |
| ESC MCU, drivers, regulators, H743 | — | ~2 W |
| **Total** | **~31.5 W** | **~128 W** |

Input power at full duty is 25.2 × 320 ≈ 8.06 kW, so efficiency is ~98.4%. Electrically that is good, but 128 W must go into a 4 cm² board.

### 3.2 Steady-state check (shows that continuous 80 A is impossible)

Convective area is both sides of a 20×20 mm board: A = 8 × 10⁻⁴ m². With strong prop wash, h ≈ 100 W/m²K:

$$R_{th,b\text{-}a} = \frac{1}{hA} = 12.5\ \text{K/W} \;\Rightarrow\; \Delta T_{ss} = 128 \times 12.5 = 1600\ \text{K}$$

The board cannot sustain 80 A. Steady-state allowance for a 70 K rise is 70/12.5 = **5.6 W total**. Solving I²·(2R_ds + R_Cu)·4 + P_sw ≈ 5.6 W gives **~16–18 A continuous per motor**, which is consistent with real 20×20 ESCs.

### 3.3 Transient (burst) model

First-order lumped model:

$$C_{th}\frac{dT}{dt} = P - \frac{T - T_a}{R_{th}}, \quad \tau = R_{th}C_{th}$$

Thermal mass estimate: PCB + copper + 12 FETs + components ≈ 3 g at ~0.9 J/g·K, so **C_th ≈ 2.7–3 J/K** and τ ≈ 12.5 × 3 ≈ 37 s. Because the burst is much shorter than τ, convection is negligible and the board heats almost adiabatically:

$$\frac{dT}{dt} \approx \frac{128}{3} \approx 43\ \text{K/s}$$

Junction rise above the board, per FET with R_th(j-c) ≈ 1.5 K/W plus ~2 K/W to the board: ~4.5 W × 3.5 ≈ **16 K**.

**Allowable burst duration:**

$$t_{burst} = \frac{(T_{j,max} - \Delta T_{j\text{-}b}) - T_a}{dT/dt} = \frac{(125 - 16) - 40}{43} \approx \mathbf{1.6\ s}$$
