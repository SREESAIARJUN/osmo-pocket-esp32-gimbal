## 0. Architecture Decision and Assumptions

### 0.1 Critical architecture point

The H743 runs ArduPilot. It does **not** commute the motors. Sensorless six-step commutation needs about 4 independent advanced-timer PWM sets and comparator/ADC zero-cross detection with µs-level latency. ArduPilot does not provide that on the FC MCU.

The correct AIO architecture is:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──bidir DShot600 ×4──► 4× ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32 firmware)
                                              └─► gate driver ─► 6 MOSFETs ─► motor
```

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-step current path

In 120° trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM on the high side:

- **On-time (D):** current flows through HS(phase A) → motor → LS(phase B).
- **Off-time (1−D):** current freewheels through LS(phase A) (sync-rect) → motor → LS(phase B).

At every instant, **exactly two FET channels plus one shunt and the copper path** carry I. The duty cycle cancels:

$$
P_{cond,FET} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

Dead time removes a small fraction from synchronous conduction. That fraction is counted separately in §2.3.

### 1.2 Distribution per device

| Device | Conduction share |
|---|---|
| HS(A) | D · I²R |
| LS(A) | (1−D) · I²R |
| LS(B) | 1 · I²R (full 60° interval) |

Averaged over a full electrical cycle, each phase has a 1/3 duty as the "PWM leg" and 1/3 as the "return leg". So the **worst single FET is the return-leg low side**, at I²R for 1/3 of the cycle.

**Hot-spot rule:** when the motor is stalled or at low eRPM, commutation is slow. The return-leg LS then sees the full I²R for a long interval, and its thermal time constant (~100 ms for package + pad) is long enough to reach the instantaneous value. **Design the worst FET for 1 × I²R continuous during a burst.**

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_ds | P per conducting FET | 2-FET path |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.20 mΩ | 7.68 W | 15.4 W |
| 100 °C | 1.74 mΩ | 11.1 W | 22.3 W |
| 125 °C | 1.92 mΩ | 12.3 W | 24.6 W |

Additional resistive losses at 80 A:

- Copper: I²·R_cu = 6400 × 0.5 mΩ = **3.2 W**
- Shunt: 6400 × 0.25 mΩ = **1.6 W**

**Per-channel conduction at T_j = 125 °C: ≈ 29.4 W.**

### 1.4 Thermal-runaway check

Because R_ds rises with temperature, conduction loss feeds back into junction temperature:

$$
T_j = T_c + R_{\theta jc}\, I^2 R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

Solving for T_j:

$$
T_j = \frac{T_c + R_{\theta jc} I^2 R_{25}(1-25\alpha)}{1 - R_{\theta jc} I^2 R_{25}\alpha}
$$

Stability requires the denominator to stay positive: R_θjc · I² · R_25 · α < 1.

With R_θjc = 1.0 K/W: 1.0 × 6400 × 0.0012 × 0.006 = **0.046 ≪ 1**. The die is stable. In practice, runaway is governed by the case-to-ambient path (R_θca), not by the die (§4).

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-leg high side hard-switches. The sync-rect low side switches at near-zero V_ds (ZVS-like) because its body diode is already conducting after dead time.

### 2.1 V-I overlap (hard-switching HS)

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}
= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance

Loss from the HS C_oss plus charging the LS C_oss:

$$
P_{oss} = 2 \times \tfrac12 C_{oss(er)} V^2 f_{sw} = 1\,\text{nF} \times 635 \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.03\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction (LS)

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

### 2.4 Reverse recovery

This loss is dissipated mostly in the HS at turn-on:

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}
$$

At 80 A, Q_rr rises. Derating by 2× gives **0.10 W**.
