## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At the design limit T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{DS} = 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega \times (1 + 0.005 \times 85) = 1.71\,\text{m}\Omega$$

Copper and solder add resistance per current path:

- PCB trace/via: R_cu ≈ 0.25 mΩ
- Phase-pad: R_pad ≈ 0.05 mΩ

### 1.2 Six-step trapezoidal with complementary PWM

During each 60° sector:

- **Leg A (PWM leg):** the high-side conducts for D·T. The low-side conducts synchronously for (1−D)·T.
- **Leg B (sink leg):** the low-side is on for 100 % of the sector.

The phase current I therefore always flows through exactly two FETs in series:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS}\cdot 1 = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}$$

This result is independent of duty cycle, which is a key benefit of synchronous rectification.

At 80 A and 110 °C:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.71\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{21.9\ W}$$

Adding copper loss (I² × 0.3 mΩ = 1.9 W) gives **P_cond,ch ≈ 23.8 W**.

### 1.3 Worst single device (thermal hotspot)

The sink-leg low-side FET carries 100 % of the current for its sector:

$$P_{FET,max} = I^2 R_{DS} = 6400 \times 1.71\text{m} = \mathbf{10.9\ W}\ \text{(instantaneous, during its 120° conduction)}$$

Averaged over an electrical revolution, each FET conducts for 1/3 of the time. Its 120° block is long compared with the junction thermal time constant at low eRPM, so size for the 10.9 W instantaneous value.

### 1.4 Ripple correction

Phase-current ripple adds to the RMS current:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

For a low-inductance motor (L_ph = 15 µH line-to-line, D = 0.5):

- ΔI = 25.2 × 0.25 / (15 µ × 48 k) = 8.75 A
- The correction term ΔI²/12 = 6.4 A², which is <0.1 % of I². This is negligible at 80 A, but it matters at light load.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg commutates. The sink leg is static within the sector. Per channel:

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

The low-side body diode recovers when the high-side turns on:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}$$
