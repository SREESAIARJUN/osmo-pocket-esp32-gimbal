## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Key Findings

An STM32H743 does not commutate the motors directly in any production ESC design. This document assumes the following split:

| Block | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIH6 / VIT6 (LQFP100) | ArduPilot, sends bi-directional DShot600 to ESC |
| ESC control | 4× AM32-capable MCU (e.g. STM32G071 or AT32F421), one per motor | Sensorless FOC/trapezoidal commutation, 48 kHz PWM |
| Gate drive | 4× FD6288Q-class 3-phase half-bridge driver | 12 V gate drive, bootstrap high side |
| Power stage | 24× N-MOSFET (12 per side, 1 per switch), 40 V, 5×6 DFN | 6S (25.2 V max) |

**Operating point:** V_bus = 25.2 V (worst-case 6S), I_burst = 80 A per motor, f_sw = 48 kHz, dead time t_d = 100 ns.

> ⚠️ **Key finding: physical feasibility.** 4 × 80 A simultaneous burst is ~8 kW of input power and ~80 W of ESC dissipation. Section 3 shows that a **20×20 mm** board cannot sustain this for more than ~1–2 s. A **30.5×30.5 mm** board with a bonded aluminium spreader reaches ~8 s. "80 A burst" must be specified with a duration and an ambient temperature, or the rating is meaningless.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths

With synchronous rectification (complementary PWM, the AM32 default), every commutation step has exactly **two FETs conducting the full phase current** at every instant:

- the high-side FET of the "+" phase **or** its low-side partner during the off-time, and
- the low-side FET of the "−" phase.

The conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle, apart from the dead-time interval handled in §2.4:

$$
P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2 \cdot R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + I_{ph}^2 \cdot \left(R_{shunt} + R_{Cu}\right)
$$
