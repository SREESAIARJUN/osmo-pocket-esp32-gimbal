# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Nano): Electro-Thermal Model and DShot Timer Architecture

**Job #18, Rev A (Architecture Baseline)**

---

## 0. Critical Findings

1. **80 A per channel cannot be sustained on a nano board.** The model below shows ≈115 W of total ESC dissipation at 4 × 80 A. That gives an adiabatic burst window of only about 2–4 s from a 40 °C start. Continuous capability on a 30.5 mm board is about 35–40 A per channel. On 20 × 20 mm it is lower. "80 A burst" must be specified with a duration (recommend ≤2 s) and a duty cycle.
2. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. These are typically STM32G071, AT32F421, or GD32. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs (AM32 supports 24–48 kHz). This document assumes that partitioning.
3. **48 kHz is a trade-off, not free.** Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 80 A it adds about 1.9 W per channel versus about 0.95 W at 24 kHz. This is acceptable because conduction loss dominates by about 14×.
4. **Every MOSFET, PCB, and airflow parameter below is a stated assumption.** Replace them with datasheet values once the BOM is frozen. All equations are parameterized.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-corrected on-resistance

$$R_{DS,hot} = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha_R (T_j - 25)] = 1.4\,\text{mΩ} \times (1 + 0.0045 \times 100) = 2.03\,\text{mΩ}$$

### 2.2 Current path in six-step commutation with synchronous rectification

During each 60° sector, phase X high-side PWMs and phase Y low-side is held on.

- During **t_on**: current flows through HS_X and LS_Y.
- During **t_off**: current flows through LS_X (synchronous rectification) and LS_Y.

Current therefore always flows through exactly **two channels** of R_DS. This holds independent of duty cycle D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{DS,hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS,hot} = 2I^2 R_{DS,hot}$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.03\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{26.0\ W}$$

### 2.3 Copper interconnect loss (often omitted)

Assume about 0.3 mΩ of effective path resistance per channel. This covers 2 oz inner planes, vias, and motor pads.

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.3\times10^{-3} = 1.9\ \text{W/ch}$$

### 2.4 Current shunt (if per-channel sensing is used)

With a 0.25 mΩ shunt carrying battery-side current I_bat ≈ D·I_ph (D ≈ 0.85 at burst):

$$P_{shunt} \approx (0.85 \times 80)^2 \times 0.25\times10^{-3} \approx 1.2\ \text{W/ch}$$

**Recommendation:** use a single bus shunt instead. At 272 A it dissipates about 18.5 W at 0.25 mΩ, so an alternative is needed. Better options are a Hall-effect sensor or a 0.1 mΩ shunt (about 7.4 W, burst only).

## 3. Switching Loss Model (f_sw = 48 kHz)

Only the PWM'd high-side FET hard-switches. The synchronous low-side FET commutates at near-zero voltage through dead time, but its body diode incurs reverse-recovery and conduction loss.

| Term | Equation | Value per channel |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f = ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 30 ns × 48 k | **1.452 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f = 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.048 W |
| C_oss | ½·Q_oss·V·f = ½ × 30 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.018 W |
| Dead-time diode | V_SD·I·2t_dt·f = 0.8 × 80 × 100 ns × 48 k | 0.307 W |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f = 2 × 30 nC × 10 × 48 k | 0.029 W (mostly in driver) |
| **Total** | | **≈ 1.85 W** |
