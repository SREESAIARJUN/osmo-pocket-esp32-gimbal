# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #14): Design Model

Two things need settling before the math.

1. **Partitioning.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller (FC) MCU running ArduPilot. It does not commutate the motors. Each of the four ESC channels has its own small MCU (e.g., AT32F421 running AM32, or BLHeli_32). The H743 drives them over bidirectional DShot600. The math below follows this architecture.
2. **"80A burst" on a nano board is thermally impossible to sustain.** The model shows it is only achievable for a few seconds and is limited by board heat capacity, not by the silicon. The burst duration should be published honestly.

All component values are **stated assumptions**. Replace them with your datasheet values and re-run the equations. The netlist is a reference topology, not a fab-ready release.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

R_ds(Tj) = R₂₅ · [1 + α(Tj − 25)]

At Tj = 110 °C: R = 1.3 mΩ × [1 + 0.0055 × 85] = **1.91 mΩ**

### 2.2 Bridge conduction with complementary PWM

During any 60° commutation sector:
- **Leg A** (PWM leg): the high side conducts for D·T, and the low side synchronous-rectifies for (1−D)·T.
- **Leg B** (return leg): the low side is on for 100% of the sector.

The load current therefore always flows through exactly two MOSFET channels, independent of duty cycle:

P_cond,FET = I² · R_ds · [D + (1−D) + 1] = **2 · I² · R_ds(Tj)**

At 80 A and 1.91 mΩ:

P_cond,FET = 2 × 6400 × 1.91 × 10⁻³ = **24.4 W per channel**

### 2.3 Copper loss

P_cu = I² · R_cu = 6400 × 0.5 × 10⁻³ = **3.2 W per channel**

This term is significant. On nano boards, 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner pours plus stitched vias are mandatory. Each 0.1 mΩ removed saves 0.64 W per channel.

### 2.4 Electrothermal convergence

Because R_ds rises with Tj, solve by fixed-point iteration:

Tj(n+1) = T_board + P_FET(Tj(n)) · R_θ,j-board

Starting at Tj = 25 °C (1.3 mΩ → 16.6 W conduction), the solution converges to the 110 °C value in 3–4 iterations when the board is at ~95 °C. **Always quote the hot value.**

## 3. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

### 3.1 Transition times from gate charge

With a plateau voltage of ~4.5 V and a 10 V drive:

- Turn-on: t_r ≈ Q_gd / I_g,on ≈ 10 nC / 0.7 A ≈ **14 ns**
- Turn-off: t_f ≈ Q_gd / I_g,off ≈ 10 nC / 1.0 A ≈ **10 ns**

(The effective gate currents are reduced by series gate resistance and loop inductance.)

### 3.2 Loss terms per channel (only the PWM-ing leg switches)

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 24 ns × 48 k | **1.16 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw = 50 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.06 W |
| Coss charge/discharge | Q_oss · V · f_sw = 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.05 W |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_f · I · 2t_dt · f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 400 ns × 48 k | **1.23 W** |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver/R_g) | Q_g · V_drv · f_sw × 2 FETs | 0.06 W |
| **Total switching-related** | | **≈ 2.56 W** |

Dead time costs as much as the switching edges. Trimming t_dt from 200 ns to 100 ns saves ~0.6 W per channel. This is a tunable AM32/BLHeli parameter, but go only as low as your measured gate-drive margins allow without shoot-through.

Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz the total would be ~1.3 W. The 48 kHz choice buys lower current ripple and quieter motors at the cost of ~1.3 W per channel.

## 4. Total Loss Budget at 80 A Burst

| Item | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| MOSFET conduction (hot) | 24.4 W | 97.6 W |
| Copper | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| Switching + dead time + gate | 2.6 W | 10.2 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, if low-side sensing) | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 31.8 W** | **≈ 127 W** |

Corresponding efficiency: 4 × 80 A × 25.2 V × D. At D ≈ 0.9 that is ~7.3 kW in, so ~98.3% efficient. The problem is not efficiency. **It is dumping 127 W into roughly 10 g of PCB.**
