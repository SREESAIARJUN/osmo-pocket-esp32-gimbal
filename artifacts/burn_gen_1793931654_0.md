# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Engineering Model and Reference Design

## 0. Architecture Baseline and Assumptions

The design depends on several assumptions. Replace them with your actual BOM datasheet values before release.

**Architecture correction.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller MCU. It does not commutate the motors. Each ESC channel needs its own ESC MCU (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071) running AM32 or BLHeli_32, so the H743 never sees the 48 kHz PWM. It only emits bidirectional DShot600 and decodes eRPM telemetry. The model below is therefore split:

- **Power stage (ESC MCUs):** conduction and switching losses at 48 kHz.
- **H743:** DShot timer/DMA allocation under ArduPilot.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

R_ds(T_j) = R_ds,25 · [1 + α(T_j − 25)]

At T_j = 100 °C: 1.2 mΩ × 1.41 ≈ **1.7 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current path in 120° trapezoidal drive

During each 60° electrical step, one leg PWMs and another leg's low-side is held on. With synchronous rectification, exactly two FETs carry current at every instant:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side conducts for D, then the low-side (sync) conducts for (1 − D).
- **Static leg:** the low-side is on 100 %.

Total conduction loss per ESC channel:

P_cond,ch = 2 · I² · R_ds(T_j)

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each leg spends 1/3 of the electrical period as the PWM leg, 1/3 as the static low leg, and 1/3 floating.

| FET | Conduction fraction | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| High-side | D/3 | I² · R · D/3 |
| Low-side | (1 − D)/3 + 1/3 | I² · R · (2 − D)/3 |

The low-side FET is the hot spot at low duty.

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 100 °C

- P_cond,ch = 2 × 6400 × 1.7 mΩ = **21.8 W per channel**
- Worst-case low-side FET (D → 0): 6400 × 1.7 m × 2/3 = **7.3 W**
- High-side FET at D = 1: 6400 × 1.7 m / 3 = **3.6 W**

Note the positive feedback: rising T_j raises R_ds, which raises loss. This is a thermal-runaway loop. Solve it iteratively with the thermal model in §3.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the hard-switched FET (the PWM-leg high-side) takes the V·I overlap loss. The sync FET switches at near-zero voltage, but it adds reverse recovery and dead-time losses.

### 2.1 Transition time

Gate current during the Miller plateau:

I_g = (V_drv − V_pl) / R_g = (10 − 3.5) / 5 = 1.3 A

Ideal switching time:

t_sw = (Q_gs2 + Q_gd) / I_g = 12 nC / 1.3 A ≈ 9.2 ns

Derate for source inductance and ringing. A realistic figure on a dense ESC layout is t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 20 ns.

### 2.2 Loss terms per channel

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V·I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f | ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 k = **1.94 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f | 40 n × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.05 W** |
| Output capacitance | E_oss · f | 0.4 µ × 48 k = **0.02 W** |
| Dead-time body diode | V_f · I · 2 · t_dt · f | 0.8 × 80 × 200 n × 48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2 · Q_g · V_drv · f | 2 × 50 n × 10 × 48 k = **0.05 W** (dissipated in driver/R_g) |
| **Total** | | **≈ 2.67 W** |

The dead-time loss is the main lever here. Cutting t_dt from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel.

### 2.3 Combined burst loss

| Scope | Loss |
|---|---|
| Per channel | 21.8 + 2.7 ≈ **24.5 W** |
| 4-in-1 total | **≈ 98 W** |
| Plus shunts, traces, connectors | +5–10 W, so **≈ 105 W** |

Shunt loss example: a 0.25 mΩ shunt at 80 A dissipates 1.6 W.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Junction-level: Foster network

Z_th,j-a(t) = Σ R_i · (1 − e^(−t/τ_i))

Typical DFN5x6 on 8-layer, 2 oz copper:

| Stage | R_i (K/W) | τ_i |
|---|---|---|
| Die | 0.3 | 0.5 ms |
| Die-attach + case | 1.2 | 10 ms |
| PCB spreading | 15 | 2 s |
| PCB to air | 25 | 60 s |

Hot low-side FET at 7.3 W:

- Junction-to-PCB rise within ~50 ms: 7.3 × 1.5 ≈ **11 K above local copper**. This is acceptable.
- The binding limit is the **board's bulk temperature**, below.
