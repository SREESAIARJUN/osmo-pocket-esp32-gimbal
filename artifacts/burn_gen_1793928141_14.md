# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

## 0. Architecture Review: Problems with the Spec as Written

Three problems need to be resolved before any netlist is useful.

1. **The H743 should not run the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 stack, each ESC channel (or a shared ESC MCU) runs its own commutation firmware, such as AM32 on an AT32F421 or BLHeli_32 on an STM32G071. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to those MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCU, not the H743. I have modeled it this way.
2. **80 A burst per motor does not fit a 20×20 "nano" stack.** The math in §2–3 shows a 4-motor burst dissipates about 65 W into roughly 1.5 J/K of board heat capacity. That is about 45 K/s adiabatic, giving a usable burst window of about 1–2 s. **The recommendation is a 30.5×30.5 mm ESC with 5×6 mm FETs, rated 60 A continuous / 80 A burst at ≤ 2 s.** The 20×20 variant should be limited to about 35–45 A burst.
3. **The netlists are reference designs, not release artifacts.** They contain no layout, DRC, or SPICE verification. Use them as a starting topology for the schematic capture in KiCad or Altium.

## 2. Loss Model, Per Motor Channel

### 2.1 Conduction losses

With 6-step commutation and complementary (synchronous) PWM, current always flows through **two** FETs in series: one high-side and one low-side, with the PWM leg alternating between its HS and LDS devices. The total conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.13\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{14.5\ W}$$

The **worst single FET** is the non-PWM (continuously on) high- or low-side device of the active pair:

$$P_{FET,max,cond} = 80^2 \times 1.13\,\text{m}\Omega = 7.2\ \text{W}$$

The PWM-leg FETs share the remainder as D·I²R and (1–D)·I²R.

### 2.2 Switching losses at 48 kHz

The transition times are set by Miller charge divided by driver current:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g}} \cdot \frac{V_{drv}}{V_{drv}-V_{pl}} \approx \frac{10\,\text{nC}}{1.5\,\text{A}}\cdot 1.47 \approx 10\ \text{ns}$$

Adding R_g (≈2 Ω internal + 4.7 Ω external) and loop inductance, a realistic design value is **t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 15 ns**.

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{in} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

This loss lands entirely in the PWM-leg high-side FET.

### 2.3 Dead-time (body diode) loss

$$P_{dt} = 2 \cdot V_f \cdot I \cdot t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 2 \times 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

Reverse-recovery (Q_rr ≈ 40 nC) adds:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{in} f_{sw} = 40\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.05\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Capacitive and gate losses

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{in} f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 50\,\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.03\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{gate} = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} \times 2\ (\text{PWM leg pair}) = 60\,\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\,\text{k} \times 2 = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

The gate loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g, not in the FET.

### 2.5 Copper and shunt losses

- Phase and DC-bus copper: 2 oz outer + 2 oz inner layers, ~0.3 mΩ effective path → 80² × 0.3 m = **1.9 W**.
- Current shunt (if used): 0.25 mΩ on the shared DC bus. This is counted once for the whole stack, in §2.6.

### 2.6 Totals

| Loss | Per motor @ 80 A | 4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 14.5 W | 58.0 W |
| Switching | 1.45 W | 5.8 W |
| Dead time + Q_rr | 0.66 W | 2.6 W |
| C_oss + gate | 0.09 W | 0.4 W |
| Copper | 1.9 W | 7.6 W |
| Shunt (bus 320 A worst case, 0.25 mΩ) | — | 25.6 W (realistically the bus averages ~150 A → 5.6 W) |
| **Total (realistic)** | **≈18.6 W** | **≈80 W** |

Silicon-only loss is ≈16.7 W per motor and ≈67 W for the stack. **Efficiency at burst** is P_out ≈ 25.2 V × 80 A × ~0.9 effective duty ≈ 1.8 kW per motor, giving η ≈ 99% electrically. The issue is *heat density*, not efficiency.

**Design lever:** conduction dominates at 87% of silicon loss. Paralleling two FETs per switch cuts P_cond to 7.3 W per motor (half the current through each of two devices: 2 × (I/2)²R = I²R/2). It costs about 2× Q_g, which is negligible here. Lowering f_sw to 24 kHz only saves about 1 W. **Do not reduce f_sw for thermal reasons; parallel FETs or use lower-R_ds parts.**

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Junction-to-board, worst FET

Per-FET loss is P = 7.2 (cond) + 1.45 (sw) + 0.3 (dt share) ≈ **9.0 W**. This is the PWM high-side FET at high duty.

| Path | R_th |
|---|---|
| R_θJC (PDFN5×6, bottom pad) | 1.0 K/W |
| Solder + via array (≥12 × 0.3 mm vias) | 1.5 K/W |
| Spreading in 4-layer 2 oz copper (local) | 4–6 K/W steady state |

**Junction-to-local-copper rise** is 9 W × 2.5 K/W = **22.5 K**. This is effectively instantaneous; the thermal time constant is under 100 ms.

### 3.2 Transient board heating (lumped capacitance)

**Board heat capacity, 30.5 × 30.5 mm, 1.6 mm, 4 × 2 oz layers:**
- Copper: 9.3 cm² × 4 × 70 µm = 0.26 cm³ → 2.33 g × 0.385 J/gK = 0.90 J/K
- FR4: 1.49 cm³ × 1.85 g/cm³ = 2.75 g × 1.1 J/gK = 0.66 J/K (heats slowly, so count about half)
- FETs, capacitors, and solder: ≈0.4 J/K

$$C_{th} \approx 1.6\ \text{J/K}$$

For a 20×20 board, C_th ≈ 0.7 J/K.

**Heat removed by prop wash** (h ≈ 80 W/m²K over both faces): 2 × 9.3 cm² × 80 = 0.15 W/K, so R_amb ≈ 6.7 K/W.

$$\tau = R_{amb}\,C_{th} = 6.7 \times 1.6 \approx 11\ \text{s}$$

**Temperature rise during a 4-motor 80 A burst (P = 80 W):**

$$\Delta T(t) = P R_{amb}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right)$$

| Burst duration | ΔT_board (30.5 mm) | T_j worst (T_amb 35 °C + 22.5 K) | 20×20 board |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 s | 24 K | 82 °C | 57 K → 115 °C ⚠ |
| 1.0 s | 47 K | 105 °C | 114 K → **fails** |
| 2.0 s | 89 K | 147 °C ⚠ (T_j max 150/175 °C) | fails |
| Steady state | 536 K | — | — |
