# Job #61: STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (80 A Burst) System Architecture Model

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Flags

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 does not switch the MOSFETs. The partition is:

| Layer | Device | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (ArduPilot) | Generates bi-directional DShot600 and decodes eRPM telemetry |
| ESC logic (×4) | AT32F421K8U7 running AM32, or EFM8BB21 running Bluejay | Commutation and 48 kHz complementary PWM |
| Gate drive (×4) | FD6288Q (3-phase half-bridge driver with integrated bootstrap) | High-side and low-side drive |
| Power stage (×4) | 6× 40 V N-MOSFET (DFN3.3×3.3) per motor | Three half-bridges per motor |

So "switching at 48 kHz" belongs to the ESC MCUs. The H743's job is DShot timing and DMA.

**Feasibility flags (read before layout).**
1. "80 A burst" is interpreted as **80 A per motor**. That is 320 A at the battery and roughly 8 kW at 6S.
2. On a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm nano board, this is a **seconds-scale transient rating**, not a sustained one. The model below shows the board can tolerate about **1.5–3 s** at 80 A per motor without forced cooling.
3. A realistic continuous rating is about 35–45 A per motor on 25.5 mm. Size the product claims to the model, not the other way around.

### 1.2 Conduction Losses

In any 60° sector, current flows from leg A to leg B:
- **Leg A (PWM leg):** the high-side conducts for D and the low-side (synchronous rectifier) conducts for 1−D.
- **Leg B (return leg):** the low-side is on continuously.

$$
P_{cond,motor} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

The result is independent of duty cycle, which is a useful property of synchronous 6-step drive.

Temperature correction:
$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$
At T_j = 125 °C this gives 1.0 mΩ × 1.5 = 1.5 mΩ.

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W}
$$

**Per-FET average.** Each FET conducts for 120° electrical out of 360°, and eRPM ≫ 1/τ_thermal, so losses average across the six FETs:
$$
P_{cond,FET} \approx 19.2/6 = 3.2\text{ W}
$$

**PCB copper conduction** (must not be ignored at 80 A). Assume a phase path of 2 oz, 4 mm wide, 15 mm long, doubled on 2 layers:
$$
R_{Cu} = \rho \frac{L}{A} = 1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot\frac{0.015}{2\times(4\times10^{-3}\cdot 70\times10^{-6})} \approx 0.46\text{ m}\Omega
$$
Two phase paths carry current, and the hot-copper factor is about 1.3:
$$
P_{Cu} \approx 2 \times 6400 \times 0.46\text{ m}\Omega \times 1.3 \approx \mathbf{7.7\ W/motor}
$$

**Shunt.** With 0.25 mΩ in the low-side common return (one shunt per motor; see §5 for the board-level shunt option):
$$
P_{shunt} \approx 80^2 \times 0.25\text{ m}\Omega \approx 1.6\text{ W}
$$

### 1.3 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches.

**(a) V·I overlap (hard turn-on and turn-off of the high-side):**
$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction (low-side, two transitions per period):**
$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

**(c) Reverse recovery:**
$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 20\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{ k} = 0.024\text{ W
