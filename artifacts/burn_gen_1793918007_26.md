**Feasibility warning (read first).** 80 A per motor on a "nano" form factor (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) is not thermally achievable as a sustained rating. Sections 2–4 show that the bridge dissipates roughly 100 W at 4×80 A. That figure is only survivable as a seconds-long burst, and only with a heat spreader. Real 80A-class 4-in-1 boards are 30.5×30.5 mm or larger. I've kept the 80A burst target but sized the thermal model honestly. The continuous rating that falls out is roughly 30–40 A per motor.

## 1. Bridge Conduction Model

### 1.1 Which FETs conduct when

In 120° trapezoidal commutation with complementary PWM, two phases are active in each 60° sector and one floats.

- **PWM-on interval:** the high-side (HS) FET of phase A and the low-side (LS) FET of phase B conduct.
- **PWM-off interval:** the LS FET of phase A (synchronous rectification) and the LS FET of phase B conduct.

So at every instant exactly two FETs carry the phase current I. This gives an exact, duty-independent bridge result:

$$
P_{cond,bridge} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

### 1.2 Per-FET duty fractions (needed for hot-spot analysis)

Over one electrical period, each FET is in the current path for:

$$
\delta_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}D, \qquad \delta_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D) + \tfrac{1}{3}
$$

$$
P_{cond,FET} = \delta \cdot I_{rms}^2 \cdot R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$
