## 0. Scope, Architecture Assumptions, and Feasibility Warnings

Several points in the brief need clarifying before the math, because they change the results.

1. **The STM32H743 is the flight controller MCU, not the ESC MCU.** No production 4-in-1 ESC runs commutation on the H743. The reference architecture is:
   - **FC:** STM32H743VIT6 running ArduPilot.
   - **ESC:** 4× independent commutation MCUs (AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071GBU6) running AM32.
   - **Gate drivers:** 4× three-phase gate drivers (e.g., Fortior FD6288Q class).
   - **Power stage:** 24× N-MOSFETs.
   
   The FC sends DShot600 to the ESC MCUs. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs, not the H743.

2. **"80 A" on a nano (20×20 mm mount, ~25×25 mm board) is a short-burst rating only.** The model in §3 shows a burst limit of about 2 s from a 40 °C start with all four channels at 80 A. Continuous operation is roughly 12–18 A per channel with prop-wash airflow. Market an 80 A label only with an explicit burst duration.

3. **All device parameters below are representative values for a 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN trench MOSFET.** Substitute the datasheet values of the selected part and re-run the equations. The equations are exact for the stated model. The numbers are only as good as the inputs.
