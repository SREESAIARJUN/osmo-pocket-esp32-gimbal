## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology current path

With complementary (synchronous) PWM in 6-step commutation, exactly **two series MOSFET channels** carry I_ph at every instant:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side switch conducts for a fraction d of each period. The low-side synchronous rectifier conducts for (1−d), minus dead time.
- **Return leg:** the low-side switch is held fully on.

So the instantaneous conduction loss per motor is independent of duty:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS}(T_j)=R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

### 1.2 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS(T_j) | P_cond per motor | P_cond for 4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W | 51.2 W |
| 100 °C | 1.34 mΩ | 17.2 W | 68.6 W |
| 115 °C | 1.41 mΩ | **18.0 W** | **72.0 W** |

Temperature feedback means you should iterate R_DS(T_j) together with the thermal model in §3. Use the 115 °C row for design.

### 1.3 Per-device distribution

Over one electrical revolution, each phase leg spends 1/3 of the time as the PWM leg, 1/3 as the return leg, and 1/3 floating.

$$P_{HS,avg} = \tfrac{1}{3} d\, I^2R, \qquad P_{LS,avg} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-d)\,I^2R + \tfrac{1}{3}I^2R$$

At d → 1 (full-throttle burst), these sum to:
- P_HS ≈ P_LS ≈ 3.0 W per device average
