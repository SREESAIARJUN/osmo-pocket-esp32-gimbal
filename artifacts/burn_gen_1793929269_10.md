## Electro-Thermal Model, DShot600 Bidirectional DMA Allocation, and Reference Netlists

## 0. Scope, Architecture, and Assumptions

### 0.1 Architectural clarification

In a 4-in-1 stack, the STM32H743 does not generate the 48 kHz bridge PWM. It sends DShot600 commands. Each ESC's own MCU (for example an AT32F421 running AM32, or a BLHeli_32-class target) runs the 48 kHz commutation PWM and drives the gate drivers. The model is therefore split into three domains:

| Domain | Owner | Covered in |
|---|---|---|
| Power stage losses (conduction and switching at 48 kHz) | ESC MCU + MOSFETs | §1–§3 |
| Thermal (burst transient) | PCB + FETs | §4 |
| DShot600 bidirectional timing and DMA | STM32H743 / ArduPilot | §5–§6 |
| Hardware netlists | Both boards | §7 |

### 0.2 Feasibility flag

An 80 A per-motor burst on a nano form factor (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm mounting) is at the edge of physical feasibility. The analysis below shows it is viable only as a short burst (about 1–3 s), not as a continuous rating. Treat "80 A" as a datasheet burst figure and validate it with thermocouple and IR testing (§4.5).

## 1. Commutation Model (6-step trapezoidal, synchronous PWM)

At any instant two phases conduct:

- **Switching leg:** high side (HS) is on for duty D. Low side (LS) synchronously rectifies for 1−D, with a dead time t_dt at each edge.
- **Return leg:** LS is on continuously for the 60° sector.

Each FET conducts 120° of every 360° electrical cycle, i.e. a one-third conduction fraction.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

### 2.2 Instantaneous loss per motor

$$P_{cond,mot}= \underbrace{I^2R_{ds}D + I^2R_{ds}(1-D)}_{\text{switching leg}} + \underbrace{I^2R_{ds}}_{\text{return leg}} = 2I^2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This result is independent of D, because synchronous rectification replaces body-diode conduction except during dead time (counted in §3).

### 2.3 Per-FET average

The electrical period at flight RPM is far shorter than the thermal time constants, so averaging is valid:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET}=\frac{2I^2R_{ds}}{6}=\frac{I^2R_{ds}(T_j)}{3}$$

This averaging is not valid at stall or at very low eRPM, where one FET can carry the full I²R for milliseconds.

### 2.4 Numerical results at 80 A

| T_j (°C) | R_ds (mΩ) | P_cond per motor (W) | P_cond per FET (W) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 | 1.00 | 12.8 | 2.13 |
| 75 | 1.30 | 16.6 | 2.77 |
| 100 | 1.45 | 18.6 | 3.09 |

### 2.5 Additional copper and shunt losses

- **Bus shunt (if fitted, 0.5 mΩ):** P = I_bus² · R_sh. With 4 motors at 80 A and D ≈ 0.85, I_bus ≈ 272 A, giving 37 W. A single bus shunt is unworkable at this current. Use per-ESC low-side sensing, or the ESC's BEMF/FET-based current estimate, with 0.2–0.25 mΩ shunts per channel (≈1.3–1.6 W each).
- **Phase copper:** for a 2 oz, 6-layer stack with 2 mm effective width × 4 layers over 8 mm, R ≈ 0.25 mΩ, so P ≈ 1.6 W per phase pair per motor.
