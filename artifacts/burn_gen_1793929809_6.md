# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC with 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage, Thermal, and Bi-Directional DShot Model (Job #21)

## 0. Architectural Ground Truth (Read First)

Three constraints shape the rest of this design.

1. **The H743 cannot commutate the motors under ArduPilot.** ArduPilot does not run sensorless BLDC commutation. The board must be an AIO with **four dedicated ESC MCUs**, for example AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 talks to them over bi-directional DShot600, as on every production AIO.
2. **80 A × 4 on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board is a transient-only rating.** The thermal model in Section 3 shows this sustains about **4–5 s** of simultaneous burst, with about **29 A/motor continuous** in prop-wash cooling. The datasheet should quote it that way.
3. **Device parameters are representative design values.** They describe a 40 V-class, ~1.5 mΩ, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN MOSFET. Replace them with your selected part's datasheet values, and re-run the equations, before layout freeze.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per motor)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, current always flows through **one high-side position and one low-side position** in series. One is the PWM'd leg (HS/LS sharing D and 1−D); the other is the static leg.

**RMS current with ripple:**

$$
\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L\,f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \cdot 0.25}{20\,\mu H \cdot 48\,kHz} = 6.56\ \text{A (worst case, } D=0.5)
$$

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.6 \approx 6404\ \text{A}^2
$$

Ripple contributes less than 0.06%, so it is negligible at 48 kHz. This is one argument *for* 48 kHz over 24 kHz.

**Temperature-corrected resistance at T_j = 125 °C:**

$$
R_{pos}(125) = 0.75\,m\Omega \cdot (1.0045)^{100} = 0.75 \cdot 1.567 = 1.18\ m\Omega
$$

**Conduction loss:**

$$
P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{pos} = 6404 \cdot 2.35\,m\Omega = \mathbf{15.1\ W}
$$

**Copper loss:**

$$
P_{cu} = 6404 \cdot 0.3\,m\Omega = \mathbf{1.9\ W}
$$

**Per-FET distribution.** Each phase spends 120° in a high-side interval and 120° in a low-side interval. Average dissipation per FET is about 15.1 W / 12 ≈ **1.26 W**. The worst FET is the static-leg FET, which sees full current for 1/3 of the electrical cycle:

$$
P_{FET,peak\text{-}avg} = \tfrac{1}{3}\cdot 80^2 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot 2.35\,m\Omega \approx 2.5\ \text{W}
$$
