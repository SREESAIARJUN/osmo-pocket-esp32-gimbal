# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #41): System Architecture, Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and Bidirectional DShot Mapping

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and a Key Architectural Clarification

**The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 stack, the H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600. Each ESC channel has its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, which generates the 48 kHz PWM. This document models both domains and the link between them:

```
[STM32H743 / ArduPilot] --4x BDShot600 (1-wire, half-duplex)--> [4x ESC MCU, e.g. AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32]
                                                                     │ 48 kHz 6-step PWM
                                                                     ▼
                                                        [3-ph gate driver] -> [6x N-MOSFET] -> motor
```

**Baseline parameters.** These are assumed. Replace them with your BOM datasheet values; every equation below is parametric.

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 25.2 V | 6S fully charged (worst case for switching loss) |
| I_ph | 80 A | Burst phase current, per channel |
| f_sw | 48 kHz | ESC PWM frequency |
| R_ds(on),25 | 1.2 mΩ | 40 V N-MOSFET, PDFN 3.3×3.3 class, V_GS = 10 V |
| α | 0.0045 /K | Temperature coefficient of R_ds(on) |
| T_j | 110 °C | Design junction temperature for the loss calculation |
| t_r, t_f | 15 ns each | Gate driver ≈1 A peak, Q_gd ≈ 10 nC |
| Q_rr | 40 nC | Body diode reverse recovery |
| Q_oss | 30 nC | Output charge at 25 V |
| Q_g | 50 nC | Total gate charge at 10 V |
| V_F | 0.8 V | Body diode forward voltage at 80 A |
| t_dt | 100 ns | Dead time per edge |
| R_shunt | 0.25 mΩ | Per-channel current shunt |
| R_cu | 0.6 mΩ | Effective PCB copper per conduction path |

> ⚠️ **Voltage margin:** 40 V MOSFETs on 6S give only 1.59× margin over 25.2 V. Regenerative braking spikes require a TVS plus ≥1000 µF of external low-ESR bulk capacitance. Otherwise move to 60 V parts, which roughly doubles R_ds(on) and conduction loss.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)] = 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega \times [1+0.0045\times 85] = 1.66\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Six-step current paths

In trapezoidal commutation, two phases conduct in each of the 6 sectors. With active (synchronous) freewheeling:

- **High-side FET, PWM leg:** conducts I for duty D.
- **Complementary low-side FET:** conducts I for (1 − D), minus dead time.
- **Low-side FET, return leg:** conducts I for 100% of the sector.

The current path therefore always contains exactly two MOSFETs in series, regardless of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j) = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.66\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{21.2\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-device split

Each FET occupies an active role for 2 of 6 sectors. The average per FET is:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} = \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6} = \frac{I^2R_{ds}}{3} = \mathbf{3.54\ W}$$

The worst FET is the return-leg low side. Its instantaneous dissipation is I²R = **10.6 W** for the full sector. This is the value to apply to Z_th,jc.

### 1.4 Copper and shunt loss

$$P_{cu} = 2 I^2 R_{cu} = 2 \times 6400 \times 0.6\,\text{m}\Omega = 7.7\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{shunt} = I^2 R_{sh} = 6400 \times 0.25\,\text{m}\Omega = 1.6\ \text{W}$$

> Copper loss is about 36% of FET conduction loss. On nano boards it is routinely the dominant uncounted term. Use 2 oz copper on inner layers and parallel the phase pours across ≥4 layers.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The return-leg low side is static during the sector.

**(a) V·I overlap loss in the hard-switched high side, linear approximation:**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction in the low side, two edges per cycle:**

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**(c) Reverse recovery, dumped into the high side at turn-on:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

**(d) Output capacitance, both FETs in the leg:**

$$P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

**(e) Gate drive, dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, two switching FETs:**

$$P_{g} = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{sw,ch} \approx \mathbf{2.19\ W}$$

**Design levers.** Dead-time loss (b) is about 28% of switching loss.

- Cutting t_dt from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel.
- This requires validating shoot-through margin against driver propagation skew.
- At 48 kHz, switching loss is only about 10% of conduction loss. Lowering f_sw to 24 kHz buys little thermally and worsens current ripple and acoustic noise.
