# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #31): Architecture, Loss Model, DShot DMA Plan, and Netlists

## 0. Feasibility Finding

The model below shows that **80 A per channel on a 20×20 / 25.5×25.5 mm nano 4-in-1 is a transient (burst) rating, not a thermal steady state.** At 80 A on all four channels the board dissipates about 95 W, and nano-scale copper cannot shed that continuously. The design is therefore specified as:

| Rating | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Burst | 80 A/ch for ≤ 1.5 s (all 4 ch) or ≤ 4 s (1 ch) | §1.6 transient model |
| Continuous | ~35–40 A/ch with prop-wash airflow | §1.7 |
| Bus | 6S (25.2 V max), 40 V MOSFETs | §1.8 margin |

These parameters are assumptions you can substitute: R_DS(on) = 1.2 mΩ @25 °C (PDFN 5×6, 40 V class), Q_g = 50 nC, Q_rr = 40 nC, t_r + t_f = 20 ns, dead time 50 ns. **Re-run every equation with the datasheet values of the part you actually source.**

**Partitioning.** The H743 is the flight controller. Each ESC channel runs its own MCU (AT32F421 with AM32, which supports bidirectional DShot). The H743 does not commutate motors.

## 1. Thermal and Loss Model (per channel unless noted)

### 1.1 Current path
With 6-step trapezoidal commutation and synchronous (complementary) PWM, current always flows through **two conducting MOSFET positions**: one high-side (or its complementary low-side during the off-time) plus one low-side of the return phase. So the effective conduction resistance is 2·R_DS(on)(T_j).

Phase RMS current ≈ motor current I_m, because the flat-top trapezoid has a crest factor near 1. Battery current is I_bat ≈ D·I_m. Size the FETs for I_m, not I_bat.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 100 °C: R = 1.2 mΩ × 1.45 = **1.74 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Conduction loss @ 80 A

$$P_{cond} = I_m^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j) = 6400 \times 3.48\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{22.3\ W}$$

Copper and solder loss: allow ≈ 0.5 mΩ of path (2 oz outer, 4×2 oz inner). This gives 6400 × 0.5 mΩ = **3.2 W**, spread across the board rather than in the FETs.

Electrothermal feedback: because R rises with T_j, solve iteratively:

$$T_j = T_c + R_{\theta jc}\, P_{FET}(T_j)$$

This converges if

$$\alpha\, R_{\theta jc}\, P_{FET} < 1$$

Check: 0.006 × 1.5 × 11 ≈ 0.1. It converges with margin, so there is no runaway at 80 A.

### 1.4 Switching losses @ f_sw = 48 kHz, V_bus = 25.2 V

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f = 0.5·25.2·80·20 ns·48 k | 0.968 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f = 40 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.048 W |
| C_oss | E_oss·f ≈ ½·Q_oss·V·f = 0.5·30 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.018 W |
| Dead-time body diode | V_f·I·2·t_dt·f = 0.8·80·2·50 ns·48 k | 0.307 W |
| Gate drive (in driver) | Q_g·V_gs·f·2 = 50 nC·10·48 k·2 | 0.048 W |
| **P_sw total** | | **≈ 1.39 W** |

At 48 kHz, switching is about 6 % of the loss. Conduction dominates. The levers are therefore R_DS(on), paralleled FETs, and copper, not edge rate. Slower edges (~40 ns) are acceptable and improve EMI at a cost of only about +1 W.

### 1.5 Totals @ 80 A burst

| | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 22.3 W | 89.2 W |
| Switching + diode | 1.39 W | 5.6 W |
| Copper | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| **Total** | **26.9 W** | **≈ 108 W** |

Efficiency check: P_out ≈ 25.2 × 80 × D. At D = 0.9 that is about 1.8 kW per channel, so ESC efficiency is about 98.5 %. The problem is power density, not efficiency.

### 1.6 Transient (burst) model

**Board heat capacity.** The board is roughly 6 g of FR4, copper, FETs, and caps:

$$C_{th} \approx \textstyle\sum m c_p \approx 6\,\text{g}\times 0.65\ \text{J/gK} \approx 3.9\ \text{J/K}$$

**Lumped board model.** First-order RC from board to ambient:

$$\Delta T_{board}(t) = P\,R_{\theta ba}\,(1-e^{-t/\tau}),\quad \tau = R_{\theta ba}C_{th}$$

With R_θba ≈ 6 K/W in prop-wash airflow, τ ≈ 23 s. For bursts much shorter than τ, the board heats almost adiabatically:

$$\Delta T \approx \frac{P\,t}{C_{th}} = \frac{108\,t}{3.9} \approx 28\ \text{K/s}$$

**Junction rise above board** (per FET, about 11 W each, two conducting):

$$\Delta T_{jb} = P_{FET}(R_{\theta jc}+R_{\theta cb}) = 11 \times (1.5 + 2.5) \approx 44\ \text{K}$$

This rise settles within about 50–200 ms (Z_θ of the die and package).
