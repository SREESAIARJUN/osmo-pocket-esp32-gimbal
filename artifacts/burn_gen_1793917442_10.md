# Job #45: STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (80 A Burst) System Architecture

## 0. Architectural Ground Rules and Scope Corrections

Four facts constrain the whole design.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot does not run ESC firmware. Each motor channel needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 generates bidirectional DShot600 and decodes the eRPM telemetry. The 48 kHz PWM frequency is an ESC-MCU setting, not an H743 timer.
2. **"80 A burst" is treated as per motor channel, for at most 10 s.** That implies roughly 290 A peak battery current at high duty. A true 20×20 mm "nano" format cannot carry that. This document therefore assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz copper stack**, with the FC and ESC on separate boards connected by a board-to-board connector. A single-board 20×20 mm version is not thermally viable at 80 A (see §2.6).
3. **MOSFET parameters are representative.** They match a typical 40 V, approximately 0.7 mΩ PDFN 5×6 or 3.3×3.3 class device. Substitute the datasheet values for your selected part before freezing the design. Every equation is written so you can do this.
4. **Netlists are reference topology, not a signed-off production netlist.** They must go through ERC/DRC and a pin-by-pin datasheet check.

## 2. Thermal and Loss Model

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

In 6-step drive, two half-bridges carry I_ph in series at any instant. With complementary PWM, the PWM'd leg's current alternates between the high-side FET (fraction D) and the low-side FET (fraction 1−D). The total conducting resistance in the loop is therefore always 2·R_ds, independent of D:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds(on)}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)] \approx k_T R_{ds,25}
$$

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 80^2 \times 2 \times (1.5\times0.70\text{ mΩ}) = 6400\times 2.1\text{ mΩ} = \mathbf{13.44\ W}
$$

Add the copper path from bus plane, through the FETs, to the phase pad, at roughly 0.3 mΩ effective per channel: P_Cu = 6400 × 0.3 mΩ ≈ **1.92 W**. This is board heat, not junction heat.

**Per-FET averaging.** Each FET conducts during 120° of the 360° electrical cycle. At 80 A the motor is near full eRPM, typically more than 1 kHz electrical, which is fast compared with the FET thermal time constant (tens of ms). The average is therefore valid:

$$
P_{FET,avg} = \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6}\times 2 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2}\ \text{(two conducting FET positions shared across 6 devices)} \approx \frac{13.44}{6} = 2.24\ \text{W}
$$

The instantaneous peak per conducting device is about 6.7 W. This value is used for the short-time Z_θ check in §2.5.

**Self-consistency loop.** R_ds depends on T_j, which depends on P. Iterate:

$$
T_j^{(n+1)} = T_{board} + P_{FET}\big(R_{ds}(T_j^{(n)})\big)\cdot R_{\theta J\text{-}board}
$$

This converges in 2–3 iterations. A thermal runaway criterion exists: if ∂P/∂T_j · R_θ ≥ 1, there is no stable solution.

### 2.2 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Hard switching occurs only in the PWM'd leg. Per channel:

**(a) V–I overlap.** Only the hard-switched FET incurs this loss. The complementary FET switches with ZVS through the body diode.

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\times25.2\times80\times40\text{ ns}\times48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

**(b) Output capacitance (C_oss).**

$$
P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 60\text{ nC}\times25.2\times48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.073\ W}
$$

**(c) Reverse recovery.** Q_rr energy is dumped into the opposite FET at turn-on.

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{ nC}\times25.2\times48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}
$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction.** There are two edges per PWM period.

$$
