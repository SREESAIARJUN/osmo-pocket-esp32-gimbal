# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and Bidirectional DShot600 Timer/DMA Allocation

## 0. Assumptions and Key Findings

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It sends bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs (e.g., AT32F421 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). Each ESC MCU drives a 3-phase bridge of 6 N-MOSFETs through a gate driver. The H743 does not commutate the motors, so its timers carry only DShot traffic. The 48 kHz switching frequency belongs to the ESC MCUs.

**Main finding.** An "80 A burst" rating on a nano (20×20 or 30.5×30.5) board is a **seconds-scale transient rating** set by the board's thermal capacitance, not by MOSFET junction limits. The model below gives about **33 W of loss per ESC at 80 A**, or about **134 W board-level** with all four motors bursting. Convective cooling sustains only about 8–10 W, which corresponds to roughly **20–25 A continuous per motor**. Burst duration must be held to about 1–2 s. Section 3 quantifies this.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (per ESC)

### 1.1 Current paths in trapezoidal 6-step with complementary PWM

In any 60° commutation sector, two phases conduct:

- **Sourcing phase** (PWM): the high-side FET conducts for duty D; the synchronous low-side FET conducts for 1−D.
- **Sinking phase**: the low-side FET is held on for 100 % of the sector.

With complementary (synchronous) rectification, phase current I_ph flows through exactly two FET channels at every instant:

$$
P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}\cdot 1 = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

The result is independent of D, which is a useful property of synchronous 6-step drive.

Temperature dependence:

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + k\,(T_j - 25)\right], \quad k \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

### 1.2 At 80 A

| Condition | P_cond |
|---|---|
| Cold (25 °C) | 2 × 80² × 1.6 mΩ = **20.5 W** |
| Hot (125 °C) | 2 × 6400 × 2.4 mΩ = **30.7 W** |
| PCB copper, 2 paths | 2 × 6400 × 0.4 mΩ = **5.1 W** |

**Electrothermal feedback.** R_ds rises with T_j, and loss rises with R_ds. The positive-feedback fixed point is:

$$
T_j = T_a + R_{th,ja}\cdot 2I^2R_{25}[1+k(T_j-25)]
\;\Rightarrow\;
T_j = \frac{T_a + 2I^2R_{25}R_{th}(1-25k)}{1 - 2I^2R_{25}R_{th}k}
$$

Thermal runaway occurs when the denominator reaches 0, i.e. when 2I²R₂₅·R_th·k ≥ 1. At 80 A with R₂₅ = 1.6 mΩ, that gives runaway at **R_th ≥ 9.8 K/W** (per-ESC share). A nano board in prop-wash sits near 7 K/W for the *whole* board (Section 3), so the per-ESC share is ~28 K/W. **Continuous 80 A operation is thermally unstable; it is inherently burst-only.**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz (per ESC)

Only one leg hard-switches per PWM period: the PWM'd high side, with its complementary low side acting as synchronous rectifier.

### 2.1 V·I overlap (hard switching)

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r + t_f) f_{sw}
= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

Estimate t_r and t_f from the gate charge:

$$
t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}+Q_{gs2}}{I_{g}}, \quad I_g = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}{R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}}
$$

With V_plateau ≈ 4.5 V, R_g,total ≈ 4 Ω, and Q_gd + Q_gs2 ≈ 25 nC: I_g ≈ 1.4 A, so t ≈ 18 ns, which is consistent with the assumption.

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{ nC}\times25.2\times48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

The recovery current also adds to high-side turn-on loss. That adds roughly 10 % to P_VI and is already covered by the 2.0 W total below.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss(er)} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5\times1.5\text{ nF}\times635\times48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.023\ W}
$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per period:

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD} I_{ph}\,(2t_{dead}) f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{ ns}\times48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

This term is significant. Tuning dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns halves it, provided the driver's propagation skew allows.

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in the driver and R_g, not the FET)

$$
P_{gate} = 2\,Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2\times30\text{ nC}\times10\times48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.029\ W}
$$

### 2.6 Commutation loss

There are 6 commutations per electrical revolution. A 14-pole motor at 30 krpm gives f_e = 3.5 kHz and 21 k commutations/s.
