# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC Nano Stack: Power Stage, Thermal, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Design Package

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

No component data was supplied for Job #75, so the parameters below are a stated baseline. Replace them with your BOM's datasheet values. The equations are general, and the numbers are worked examples.

**Two architecture points need to be settled first.**

1. **The H743 runs ArduPilot, not the ESC.** A 4-in-1 ESC uses its own MCU(s), for example 4× AT32F421 running AM32, or a single BLHeli_32 MCU. The H743 generates DShot600 frames and decodes the eRPM telemetry those MCUs send back. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCU.
2. **80 A on a nano (20×20 mm) board is a burst rating only.** This document works through the physics. The result is that 4×80 A is thermally sustainable for roughly 1–3 seconds. The input copper and connectors become the real limit before the MOSFETs do.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

The ESC uses 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation with synchronous rectification and complementary PWM. During any commutation sector, exactly two FETs carry phase current:

- **PWM on (duty D):** high-side of phase X plus low-side of phase Y.
- **PWM off (1−D):** low-side of phase X (freewheeling, synchronous) plus low-side of phase Y.

So the current always flows through two channels:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

This total is independent of D. However, the low-side FETs carry a larger share when D is low. At D → 0, the Y low-side conducts 100% of the time.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

R_DS(on) rises with junction temperature:

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

$$R = 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega \times (1 + 0.0045 \times 85) = 1.38\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Electro-thermal fixed point

Junction temperature depends on loss, and loss depends on junction temperature:

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}\,R_{th(j\text{-}pcb)}, \qquad P_{FET} = I^2 R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Solving for T_j in closed form:

$$T_j = \frac{T_{pcb} + I^2 R_{25}\,R_{th}\,(1-25\alpha)}{1 - \alpha\,I^2 R_{25}\,R_{th}}$$

**Stability condition (thermal runaway check):**

$$\alpha\,I^2 R_{25}\,R_{th} < 1$$

At 80 A: 0.0045 × 6400 × 0.001 × 1.5 = 0.043. This is far below 1, so the FET itself is stable. The real limit is T_pcb rising, covered in Section 4.

Worked example for the worst-case low-side FET at 100% conduction share, with T_pcb = 90 °C:

$$T_j = \frac{90 + 9.6\,(0.8875)}{1 - 0.0432} = 103.0\ ^\circ\text{C}$$

$$P_{FET} = 6400 \times 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega \times [1 + 0.0045 \times 78] = 8.65\ \text{W}$$

### 1.4 Per-motor conduction loss at 80 A

Using the hot value R ≈ 1.38 mΩ:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.38\times10^{-3} = 17.7\ \text{W}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg is hard-switched per PWM period (the PWM'd high-side and its complementary low-side). Each loss term is evaluated at V_bus = 25.2 V and I = 80 A.

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ · V_bus · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw | ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 20 ns × 48 k = **0.97 W** |
| Coss charge/discharge | Q_oss · V_bus · f_sw | 30 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.036 W** |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_f · I · 2t_dt · f_sw | 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw (Q_rr ≈ 40 nC) | **0.048 W** |
| Gate drive (6 FETs, incurred in driver) | 6 · Q_g · V_GS · f_sw | 6 × 30 nC × 10 × 48 k = **0.086 W** |

$$P_{sw,motor} \approx 1.75\ \text{W}$$

**Design levers:**
- Dead-time loss scales linearly with t_dt. Reducing it from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per motor.
- Overlap loss scales with f_sw. Running at 24 kHz halves it, at the cost of higher ripple current and more motor iron loss.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Lumped heat capacity of the stack

| Mass | Heat capacity |
|---|---|
| ESC PCB: 20×20×1.6 mm FR4 (1.85 g/cm³, 1.1 J/g·K) | 1.3 J/K |
| 8 layers of 2 oz copper (≈ 2.0 g Cu, 0.385 J/g·K) | 0.77 J/K |
| 12 FETs, capacitors, connectors | ≈ 0.8 J/K |
| **C_th total** | **≈ 2.9 J/K** |

Steady-state convection with propwash (h ≈ 80 W/m²K over about 12 cm² of exposed area, both sides plus edges):

$$R_{th,amb} \approx 10\ \text{K/W}$$

### 4.2 First-order transient

$$T_{pcb}(t) = T_a + P\,R_{th,amb}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right), \qquad \tau = R_{th,amb}\,C_{th} \approx 29\ \text{s}$$

For P = 100 W, the steady-state asymptote would be +1000 K, so the board is effectively adiabatic during a burst:

$$\frac{dT}{dt} \approx \frac{P}{C_{th}} = \frac{100}{2.9} \approx 34\ \text{K/s}$$

### 4.3 Allowed burst duration

$$t_{burst} = \frac{C_{th}\,(T_{pcb,max} - T_{start})}{P}$$

| Start temperature | Limit | Burst time |
|---|---|---|
| 40 °C | 105 °C (board/connector limit, keeps T_j < 125 °C) | **≈ 1.9 s** |
| 60 °C (after hover) | 105 °C | **≈ 1.3 s** |

**Continuous rating.** Solve P_cont = ΔT / R_th,amb = 65/10 = 6.5 W total. Working backwards, that gives roughly 15–18 A continuous per motor on a true nano stack.

**Recommendation:** Specify the product as "≈20 A continuous / 80 A for ≤1 s burst." Enforce this in firmware with an I²t limiter, either in the ESC (AM32 current limit) or in ArduPilot (`BATT_*` limits plus `MOT_*`
