Each loop runs 4–5× slower than the loop it wraps, and each loop's bandwidth is at most 1/5 of the inner loop's bandwidth. That separation is what lets the cascaded loops be designed one at a time.

## 1. Field-Oriented Current Loop (20 kHz)

### 1.1 Transforms

Electrical angle: $\theta_e = p\,(\theta_m - \theta_{\text{off}})$, where $p = 7$ pole pairs.

**Clarke transform (amplitude-invariant):**
$$i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}(i_a + 2 i_b)$$

**Park transform:**
$$\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}$$

**Inverse Park transform:**
$$v_\alpha = v_d\cos\theta_e - v_q\sin\theta_e,\qquad v_\beta = v_d\sin\theta_e + v_q\cos\theta_e$$

### 1.2 Plant and Decoupling

The PMSM equations in the rotor frame are:
$$v_d = R i_d + L_d \dot i_d - \omega_e L_q i_q$$
$$v_q = R i_q + L_q \dot i_q + \omega_e L_d i_d + \omega_e \lambda_m$$

Adding the feedforward terms $v_d^{ff} = -\omega_e L_q i_q$ and $v_q^{ff} = \omega_e(L_d i_d + \lambda_m)$ leaves two decoupled first-order plants:
$$G_i(s) = \frac{1}{L s + R}$$

### 1.3 PI Design by Pole–Zero Cancellation

Choose the current-loop bandwidth $\omega_c$. Typically $\omega_c \le 2\pi f_{\text{pwm}}/20$, i.e. about 2π·1 kHz at 20 kHz PWM.

$$K_p = L\,\omega_c,\qquad K_i = R\,\omega_c\quad\Rightarrow\quad \frac{i}{i^*} = \frac{\omega_c}{s+\omega_c}$$

### 1.4 Discrete Form with Delay and Anti-Windup
