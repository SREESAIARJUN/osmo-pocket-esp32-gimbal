## 0. Architectural Ground Rules and Reality Check

Three points shape the rest of this document.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot does not run ESC firmware. The correct partition is:
   - **H743** runs ArduPilot (flight control). It sends DShot600 frames and receives bidirectional eRPM telemetry.
   - **Four ESC MCUs** run AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical parts are the AT32F421 or STM32G071, each with a 3-phase gate driver and 6 to 12 MOSFETs.
   - The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs, not the H743.
2. **"Nano" and "80A burst per channel" conflict thermally.** Section 3 shows that a 30×30 mm board dissipating about 100 W rises by roughly 20 K/s or more. The 80A rating is only defensible as a **≤2 s burst**, with paralleled FETs and a 30.5×30.5 mm or larger footprint. A 20×20 mm "nano" cannot carry it.
3. **Design voltage:** 6S LiPo, so V_bus = 25.2 V (full) and 22.2 V (nominal). This requires 40 V MOSFETs. 30 V parts have insufficient margin for regenerative braking and ringing spikes.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (Per ESC Channel)

### 2.1 Commutation Assumption

The model assumes trapezoidal (6-step) drive with complementary synchronous rectification, which is the AM32 default.

- At any instant, two phase legs carry current I. One leg is PWM-switching. The other leg's low side (or high side) is held fully on.
- With synchronous rectification, the switching leg's current alternates between the high-side FET (fraction D) and the low-side FET (fraction 1−D). Each active leg therefore always presents one R_ds(on) in the path.

### 2.2 Temperature-Dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{ds}(125) = 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.60 = 1.92\,\text{m}\Omega$$

### 2.3 Conduction Loss

For N_p FETs paralleled per switch position:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,\frac{R_{ds}(T_j)}{N_p} + 2\,I^2 R_{cu}$$

| Configuration | FET term | Copper term | **P_cond** |
|---|---|---|---|
| N_p = 1 (6 FETs/ESC) | 2·6400·1.92 m = 24.6 W | 3.84 W | **28.4 W** |
| N_p = 2 (12 FETs/ESC) | 12.3 W | 3.84 W | **16.1 W** |

Copper is now about 24% of the loss with N_p = 2. Mitigations: use 2 oz inner planes, put phase pads on all layers, and stitch the vias.

### 2.4 Switching Loss at 48 kHz (Hard-Switched Leg Only)

**Transition times (Miller-plateau approximation):**

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,src}} + t_{ri} \approx 4 + 11 \approx 15\text{ ns},\qquad t_f \approx 12\text{ ns}$$

**(a) V-I overlap**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(27\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.31\ W}$$

**(b) C_oss loss** (an effective Q_oss model is better if available)

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw}\times 2 = 0.5(1\text{n})(635)(48\text{k})(2) = \mathbf{0.03\ W}$$

**(c) Reverse recovery** (Q_rr scales with di/dt and I; derated ×1.5 at 80 A)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 45\text{nC}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead-time intervals per PWM period)

$$P_{dt} = V_f\,I\,(2t_{dt})f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive** (two switching FETs per active leg; dissipated in the driver and gate resistors)

$$P_g = N_p\cdot 2\cdot Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = N_p\cdot 2\cdot 40\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k} = 0.038\,N_p\text{ W}$$

**Effect of paralleling on (a) to (d):**

- Total Q_gd and C_oss roughly double, so P_VI rises to about 1.6 W unless the gate resistors are reduced.
- P_dt is roughly unchanged, because the same current is split across paralleled body diodes.
