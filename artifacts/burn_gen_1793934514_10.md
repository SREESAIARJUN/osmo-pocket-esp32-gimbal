# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #30): Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Timer/DMA Architecture

**Document class:** Architecture baseline, revision A
**Scope:** Loss and thermal derivation at 80 A burst per channel and 48 kHz PWM; ArduPilot bi-directional DShot600 timing and timer/DMA allocation; reference netlists.

> **Up-front finding.** The math below shows that a true nano form factor (20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm) cannot sustain 80 A per channel on all four motors simultaneously for more than about 1–2 s without exceeding junction limits. At 80 A, 6S, and 48 kHz, each channel dissipates about 22 W, and the board has only about 3–5 J/K of thermal mass. The model is still valid. The burst-duration limit is derived in §3.6 and should be enforced in ESC firmware, not assumed away. All component parameters below are representative values that must be replaced with values from the datasheets of the parts actually selected.

**Commutation scheme:** Six-step trapezoidal with complementary (synchronous) PWM. At any instant, current flows through exactly two FET channels: one high side of phase A and one low side of phase B. During PWM off-time, the complementary low-side FET of the switching leg conducts instead of the high side. The current path therefore always consists of **two conducting FETs**. Body-diode conduction occurs only during dead time.

## 2. Loss Model (Per Channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

The temperature-corrected on-resistance is:

$$R_{ds}(T_J) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_J - 25)\right], \qquad \alpha = \frac{k_T - 1}{100} = 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

The RMS current through the switching high-side FET is:

$$I_{rms,HS} = I_{pk}\sqrt{D/3}$$

The factor of 1/3 arises because each leg conducts for 120° of each 360° electrical cycle.

Because two FETs are always in the path, the total channel conduction loss is independent of D:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{pk}^2\, R_{ds}(T_J)$$

The ΔI-ripple correction is $I_{rms}^2 = I_{pk}^2 + \Delta I^2/12$. It is negligible at 48 kHz with typical 5–30 µH drone motor inductance (below 1 %).

**Numeric result at T_J = 125 °C:**

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.6\ \text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{20.48\ W\ per\ channel}$$

This equals about **3.41 W per FET** averaged over six FETs. The instantaneous dissipation of a conducting FET is 10.24 W, but each FET conducts for only about one third of the electrical cycle. At electrical frequencies above about 500 Hz, which applies to any spinning motor, the thermal mass averages this out.

**Self-consistency loop.** R_ds depends on T_J, which in turn depends on R_ds:

$$T_J = T_{ref} + R_\theta\, P(T_J) \;\Rightarrow\; T_J = \frac{T_{ref} + R_\theta\left[P_0(1 - 25\alpha) + P_{sw}\right]}{1 - R_\theta P_0\,\alpha}$$

Here $P_0 = 2 I^2 R_{ds,25}$. Thermal runaway occurs if $R_\theta P_0 \alpha \ge 1$. With $P_0 = 12.8$ W, this gives the runaway threshold **R_θ ≥ 13 K/W** (channel-level). Stay well below it.

### 2.2 Switching Loss (Hard-Switched Edges, 48 kHz)

Only the PWM leg switches. One FET turns on and one turns off per period.

**V–I overlap:**
$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{pk}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.968\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery** (the complementary FET's body diode conducts during dead time and must recover):
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$

**Output capacitance** (the energy stored in C_oss is dumped at turn-on):
$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \cdot 2 = 60\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.073\ W}$$

The factor of 2 accounts for both FETs in the leg.

**Dead-time body-diode conduction:**
$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I_{pk}\,(2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{n}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.614\ W}$$
