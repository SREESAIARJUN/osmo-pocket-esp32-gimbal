# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #11): Architecture & Mathematical Model

## 0. Architecture Assumptions

The numbers below depend on parts that have not yet been selected. Each assumption is stated so it can be swapped for real datasheet values.

| Item | Assumption | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Topology | H743 runs ArduPilot (FC only). Four separate ESC MCUs (AT32F421 or STM32G071, AM32/BLHeli_32-class) drive the power stage. | ArduPilot does not run FOC/six-step on the H7. DShot implies discrete ESC MCUs. |
| Bus voltage | 6S, V_bus,max = 25.2 V | Worst-case switching loss |
| "80A burst" | 80 A **phase** current per ESC, ≤10 s duty | Industry rating convention |
| MOSFET (×24) | 40 V, R_ds(on),25°C = 1.2 mΩ, Q_g = 40 nC, Q_rr = 40 nC, Q_oss = 50 nC, R_θJC = 1.0 K/W | Typical 3.3×3.3 / 5×6 DFN class |
| Gate driver | FD6288Q-class, ~1.5 A source / 1.8 A sink, V_GS = 10 V | Common in AM32 designs |
| PWM | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous rectification), dead time 100 ns per edge | |
| Board | 30.5×30.5 mm mount, 8-layer, 2 oz outer / 2 oz inner power planes | |

**Flag before going further.** If "nano" means 20×20 mm, 24 MOSFETs at 80 A burst is not physically credible. The copper cross-section and thermal mass are roughly halved, and the burst-time result in §3 drops below ~1.5 s. I recommend 30×30 for an 80 A target, or derating to about 45 A burst on 20×20.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.2 mΩ × 1.5 = **1.8 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-step with complementary PWM

In each commutation sector, one leg is PWM-switching (HS/LS alternate) and one leg is held on (LS on). Current I_ph always flows through exactly two conducting channels:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph,rms}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

PWM ripple adds a correction:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12},\qquad \Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5-inch motor (L_ph ≈ 20 µH) at D = 0.5: ΔI ≈ 12.6/(20e-6 × 48e3) ≈ 13 A, so ΔI²/12 ≈ 14 A². This is negligible against 6400 A² (+0.2%).

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.8\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{23.0\ W\ per\ ESC}$$

Per FET (two conducting, worst case): **11.5 W**.

### 1.3 Copper and shunt losses (board-level)

| Path | R | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| Phase traces, 2 oz, ~8 mm, 4 layers paralleled, per ESC | ~0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W × 4 = 6.4 W |
| Battery pad → bus planes (I_bat ≈ 4 × D × 80 ≈ 240 A peak at D = 0.75) | ~0.05 mΩ | 2.9 W |
| Current shunt (0.25 mΩ, battery path) | 0.25 mΩ | 14.4 W ⚠ |

**Recommendation:** for the shunt, use 0.1 mΩ (5.8 W) or Hall/low-side per-ESC sensing. 14 W in a shunt is a design-killer.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Its loss splits across the HS (hard-switched) and LS FETs.

### 2.1 V-I overlap (hard switching)

$$t_{r,f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd} + Q_{gs2}}{I_{gate}} \approx \frac{15\text{ nC}}{0.75\text{ A}} = 20\text{ ns}$$

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery, output capacitance, dead time, gate drive

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw | 40n × 25.2 × 48k = **0.048 W** |
