# STM32H743 AIO with 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model for Job #7

**80A Burst Thermal, 48kHz Switching, and ArduPilot Bi-directional DShot600 Allocation**

---

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Feasibility Warning

Several premises in the brief need correcting before any math is meaningful.

### 0.1 The H743 cannot run the motors itself

The STM32H743 serves as the flight controller. Each motor still needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071, plus a 3-phase gate driver. The H743 cannot run 4× sensorless 6-step/FOC loops while also running ArduPilot. The architecture assumed throughout is:

`H743 (ArduPilot) → 4× bidir DShot600 → 4× ESC MCU → 4× gate driver → 24× MOSFET`

### 0.2 "80A burst" on a nano board is thermally marginal to infeasible

On a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm board, 4×80A means ~92 W of FET dissipation (derived below) and up to ~320 A of battery current at full duty. Under those conditions:
- No XT60 or nano-board power entry survives this.
- The burst window is limited to roughly 2–3 seconds.

This document therefore treats **80A as per-motor phase current, short burst (≤2 s), with one or two motors at peak**, and shows quantitatively where the limits are.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At 125°C: R = 1.2 × 1.6 = **1.92 mΩ**. A planning value of **1.68 mΩ** at ~100°C is used where noted.

### 1.2 Six-step trapezoidal with synchronous rectification (AM32 default)

At any instant, current flows through exactly two devices:
- **PWM on-state:** high-side of leg A + low-side of leg B.
- **PWM off-state:** low-side of leg A (synchronous freewheel) + low-side of leg B.

Per motor:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Each device conducts about 1/3 of the electrical period at D→1, so per device:

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}}{3}$$

**Numbers at I = 80 A, R = 1.68 mΩ:**

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Conduction loss per motor | 2 × 6400 × 0.00168 = **21.5 W** |
| Conduction loss per device | **3.58 W** |
| Conduction loss, 4 motors at burst | **86 W** |

Add current ripple:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I \approx \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

For a 10 µH phase at D = 0.5: ΔI ≈ 13 A, which adds 14 A² (+0.2%). This is negligible at 80 A.

**FOC variant (sinusoidal):** with peak phase current Î,

$$P_{cond,motor} = 3\left(\tfrac{\hat I}{\sqrt2}\right)^2 R_{DS} = 1.5\,\hat I^2 R_{DS}$$

### 1.3 Electro-thermal coupling (closed form)

Combine P = I²R₂₅[1+α(T_j−25)]·k (with k = 1/3 per FET) and T_j = T_c + P·R_th,jc. Solving gives:

$$T_j = \frac{T_c + kI^2R_{25}(1-25\alpha)R_{th}}{1 - kI^2R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_{th}}$$

The thermal runaway condition is k·I²·R₂₅·α·R_th ≥ 1. Per FET this is 2560 × 0.0012 × 0.006 × 1.5 = 0.028, so the device-level margin is large. **Runaway risk lives at board level**, where R_th is 10–100× larger (see §3).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In each 60° sector, only one leg hard-switches. Its high-side FET carries the V·I overlap loss. The complementary low-side FET switches near zero voltage, but its body diode conducts during dead time, and its Q_rr is dumped into the high-side FET at turn-on.

### 2.1 Loss terms (per motor, per active switching leg)

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 22 ns × 48 kHz | **1.06 W** |
| Reverse recovery | V·Q_rr·f_sw = 25.2 × 40 nC × 48 kHz | **0.048 W** |
| C_oss discharge | ½·C_oss·V²·f_sw = ½ × 1.5 nF × 635 × 48 kHz | **0.023 W** |
