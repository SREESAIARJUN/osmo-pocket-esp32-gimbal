## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, the motor current always flows through exactly two FETs in series:

- **PWM on-time:** high-side of phase A plus low-side of phase B.
- **PWM off-time:** low-side of phase A plus low-side of phase B (freewheel through the synchronous FET).

The conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence of R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ /\mathrm{K}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 0.75 mΩ × 1.55 ≈ **1.16 mΩ**. Use 1.2 mΩ for the worst case.

There is also a gate-drive derating. At V_GS = 10 V from a 12 V driver minus the bootstrap diode drop, the datasheet value holds. Below 7 V, R_DS(on) can rise by 20–40%, so the bootstrap must be sized correctly (§4.3).

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| Term | Expression | Value |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (hot) | 80² × 2 × 1.2 mΩ | **15.36 W** |
| FET conduction (cold, 25 °C) | 80² × 2 × 0.75 mΩ | 9.60 W |
| PCB copper path (bus → FET → phase pad, R_pcb ≈ 0.5 mΩ) | 80² × 0.5 mΩ | **3.20 W** |
| Phase pad / solder joint (≈ 0.1 mΩ ×2) | 80² × 0.2 mΩ | 1.28 W |

**Per motor, hot: P_cond ≈ 19.8 W.** Of this, 15.4 W is in silicon and 4.5 W is in copper.

The loss is distributed as 7.7 W per conducting FET. Commutation rotates the load across all 6 FETs, so the time-averaged dissipation per FET is about 7.7 × (2/6) × 2 ≈ 5.1 W. Instantaneously, the dissipation sits on 2 FETs for 1/6 of an electrical period. At burst eRPM (> 1 kHz electrical), that interval is ≪ the die thermal time constant (~ms), so the averaging assumption holds.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only one half-bridge per motor is PWM-switching at any instant.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

With V = 25.2 V, I = 80 A, and t_r + t_f = 40 ns (R_g ≈ 2–4 Ω with FD6288 drive):

$$P_{VI} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\times10^{-9} \times 48\,000 = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 60\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,000 = 0.073\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw}\cdot 2 \approx 50\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,000 = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

Two dead-time intervals occur per PWM period:

$$P_{dt} = V_F I_{ph} \cdot 2 t_{dead} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 2(200\,\text{ns}) \times 48\,000 = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

Reducing dead time to 100 ns halves this term, but it raises shoot-through risk. Validate the setting on the bench with a current probe on the bus.

### 2.5 Gate drive

$$P_g = Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} \cdot 2 = 60\,\text{nC} \times 12 \times 48\,000 \times 2 = 0.069\ \text{W}$$

This loss lands mostly in R_g and the driver, not the FET.

|---|---|
| V–I overlap | 1.94 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 |
| Q_rr | 0.07 |
| Q_oss | 0.06 |
| Gate | 0.07 |
| **Σ P_sw** | **3.37 W** |
