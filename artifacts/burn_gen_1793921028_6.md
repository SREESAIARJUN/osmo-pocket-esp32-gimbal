# STM32H743 AIO 4-in-1 ESC Nano Flight Controller (Job #5): System Design Specification

**Scope:** Power-stage loss model at 80 A burst, 48 kHz switching analysis, thermal transient model, ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 timer/DMA allocation, and reference netlists.

**Assumptions:** 6S (25.2 V max), 20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm stack-compatible AIO. Device parameters are representative placeholders, so substitute the selected parts' datasheet values before sign-off.

---

## 0. Architectural Ground Truth

Two constraints shape everything downstream:

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot has no on-FC ESC firmware. Each of the four channels needs a dedicated ESC MCU (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or an EFM8/BB51 running BLHeli) driving a 3-phase gate driver. The H743 talks to them via bidirectional DShot600.
2. **80 A per channel on a nano footprint is burst-only.** Section 3 shows roughly 87 W of total power-stage dissipation at 4×80 A. That is thermally sustainable for seconds, not minutes. The design target is a **10 s burst, 35–40 A continuous per channel**.

```
VBAT ─┬─ Bulk + MLCC bank ─┬─ ESC1 (MCU + 3φ driver + 6 FETs) ── Motor 1
      │                   ├─ ESC2 ...                        ── Motor 2
      │                   ├─ ESC3 ...                        ── Motor 3
      │                   └─ ESC4 ...                        ── Motor 4
      ├─ 4× shunt/current sense → ESC MCUs (+ summed to H743 ADC)
      └─ Buck 10 V (VTX/driver), Buck 5 V → LDO 3.3 V (H743, IMU)
H743 TIM3 CH1–4 ──(bidir DShot600, single wire each)── ESC1–4 signal pins
```

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)] , \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\text{–}0.007\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

**Placeholder FET:** 40 V, 5×6 mm PowerPAK/DFN, R_DS,25 = 1.0 mΩ at V_GS = 10 V, α = 0.006.

At T_j = 110 °C: R_DS = 1.0 × (1 + 0.006·85) = **1.51 mΩ**

### 1.2 Six-step / synchronous rectification

In each commutation sector, two phase legs conduct. With complementary (synchronous) PWM at duty D:

- PWM leg: high-side carries D·I², low-side carries (1−D)·I².
- Return leg: low-side is fully on, carrying I².

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{rms}^2 R_{DS} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 1.3 Ripple correction (48 kHz)

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph\text{-}ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

Worst case at D = 0.5, with L = 20 µH (typical 2207-class motor):

ΔI_pp = 25.2 × 0.25 / (20e-6 × 48e3) = **6.56 A**

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{avg}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.6 \approx 6403.6\ \text{A}^2$$

Ripple adds less than 0.1 % at 80 A. It can be neglected at high current but matters for loss at low throttle.

### 1.4 Result at 80 A, T_j = 110 °C

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 6403.6 \times 1.51\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.3\ W}$$

**Electro-thermal fixed point:** R_DS depends on T_j, which depends on P. Iterate:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_a + P\!\left(R_{DS}(T_j^{(k)})\right)\cdot R_{th,ja}$$

This converges when α·P_25·R_th < 1 (thermal-runaway criterion). Check: 0.006 × 12.8 W × 1.5 K/W ≈ 0.12 < 1, so it converges.

### 1.5 Copper / PCB losses (frequently ignored, not negligible)

Using 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner layers, effective phase-path resistance is about 0.4 mΩ per channel (via arrays, pads, and solder joints included).

P_Cu = 2 × 6400 × 0.4e-3 = **5.1 W per channel**

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-chopping leg switches hard. The return leg is static within a sector.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard switching, inductive clamp)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

With t_r + t_f = 40 ns (driver ~1.5 A source/sink, R_g ≈ 4.7 Ω, Q_gd ≈ 8 nC):

P_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40e-9 × 48e3 = **1.94 W**

**Edge-time derivation:**

$$t_{r/f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,(R_{drv}+R_g)}{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}$$

With V_drv = 10 V, V_pl ≈ 4.5 V, Q_gd = 8 nC, R_total = 7 Ω, this gives about 10 ns ideal. Add 2–3× for common-source inductance in the 5×6 package, which explains the 20 ns per edge used above.

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

(t_dt = 100 ns per edge.)

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

Q_rr rises with di/dt and T_j, so derate ×2 when hot: about 0.1 W.

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated mostly in driver and R_g, not the FETs)

$$P_g = 6 \times Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 6 \times 50\text{ nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.144\ W/ch}$$

### 2.6 Per-channel and total budget (80 A, 25.2 V, T_j = 110 °C)

| Loss term | Per channel (W) | ×4 (W) |

| FET conduction | 19.3 | 77.2 |
