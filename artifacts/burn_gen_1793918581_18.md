## 0. Architectural Corrections

Two constraints change the scope of this job.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each motor channel (or the whole board) has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical parts are the AT32F421 and STM32G071. The **48 kHz PWM is an ESC-firmware parameter**. The H743 only generates DShot600 and decodes bidirectional eRPM telemetry. The model below follows this split.

**2. 80 A per motor on a nano (20×20 mm) footprint is a burst rating, not a thermal rating.** Section 3 shows the board reaches its thermal limit in about **1–2 s** at 4×80 A, and sustains only about **15–25 A per motor** continuously. Treat "80 A" as a ≤1 s transient spec, and design overcurrent firmware to enforce it.

**Assumed component values.** All component parameters below are representative values for 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN MOSFETs and FD6288-class drivers. **Replace them with your selected parts' datasheet worst-case values before sign-off.**

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

In 6-step commutation, two phases conduct at any instant. With synchronous rectification, the phase current always flows through exactly **one high-side-path FET and one low-side-path FET**. One is the active switch. The other is either the opposite active switch or the complementary freewheeling FET.

Ignoring dead-time intervals (handled in §2.3):

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\, I_{ph,rms}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

Include current ripple through the RMS value:

$$I_{ph,rms} = I_{ph}\sqrt{1 + \tfrac{1}{12}\left(\tfrac{\Delta I_{pp}}{I_{ph}}\right)^2}$$

The ripple ΔI_pp comes from motor inductance:

$$\Delta I_{pp} \approx \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\, D}{2 L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5" motor, L_ph is about 15–30 µH line-to-line. At 48 kHz the ratio ΔI/I stays below about 0.3 at 80 A, so the RMS correction is under 1%. Neglect it.

The temperature dependence forms a feedback loop:

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

**Numeric result (hot, conservative):**

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.92\,\text{m}\Omega = 2 \times 12.29 = \mathbf{24.6\ W}$$

Average per FET is P_cond/6 ≈ **4.1 W**. The hottest FET, the low side of the PWM leg at low duty, can carry roughly 1.5× the average, about **6 W**.
