## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

**The H743 does not commutate the motors.** It is the flight controller running ArduPilot. Each ESC channel has its own commutation MCU, because sensorless 48 kHz 6-step control with zero-cross detection needs dedicated comparators and timers per motor. ## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step commutation

With complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM, motor current always flows through exactly two conducting devices:

- **On-time (duty D):** high-side of phase A plus low-side of phase B.
- **Off-time (1 − D):** low-side of phase A (synchronous rectifier) plus low-side of phase B.

The total conduction loss per channel is therefore independent of duty cycle. Duty only redistributes heat between the devices:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Each phase leg conducts for 2/3 of an electrical revolution. Averaged over a revolution, the loss per device is:

- High-side: $\tfrac{1}{3} D \cdot I^2 R$
- Low-side: $\tfrac{1}{3}(2-D) \cdot I^2 R$

At low throttle with high current, which happens during hard braking or bidirectional (3D) operation, the **low-side devices run hotter**. Place them closest to the thermal vias.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha (T_j-25)\right] = 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega\,[1 + 0.007\cdot 85] = 1.914\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.914\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{24.5\ W}$$

### 1.3 Copper loss

The phase and bus path has an estimated resistance of about 0.5 mΩ on 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner pours of a 20×20 / 30×30 board:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

### 1.4 Self-consistent junction temperature

R_DS(on) rises with temperature, which raises loss, which raises temperature. Iterate:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_{case} + R_{\theta JC}\cdot \tfrac{1}{2}P_{ch}\!\left(T_j^{(n)}\right)$$

The loop converges when this condition holds:

$$\alpha \cdot R_{\theta JC} \cdot I^2 R_{25} < 1 \;\Rightarrow\; 0.007 \cdot 1.5 \cdot 7.68 = 0.081 \ll 1$$

The condition is met, so there is **no thermal runaway at the device level**. Runaway risk exists only at the board level, through the case temperature (Section 4).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Gate-drive-limited transition times

Gate current during the Miller plateau:

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_g} = \frac{10-3.5}{5.3} = 1.23\ \text{A}, \qquad I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_g} = 0.66\ \text{A}$$

Transition times:

$$t_r = \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{g,on}} = \frac{8\,\text{nC}}{1.23} = 6.5\ \text{ns}, \qquad t_f = \frac{8\,\text{nC}}{0.66} = 12.1\ \text{ns}$$

> The turn-off asymmetry matters. Adding a fast-off diode in parallel with R_g (or using a split R_g of 4.7 Ω on / 1 Ω off) brings t_f down to about 5 ns.

### 2.2 Loss components per channel

Only one leg switches at a time; the other conducting leg is static during a 6-step sector.

| Mechanism | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ V_bus · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = ½ · 25.2 · 80 · 18.6 ns · 48 k | **0.90 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw = 30 nC · 25.2 · 48 k | 0.036 W |
| C_oss discharge | ½ C_oss · V_bus² · f_sw = ½ · 600 pF · 635 · 48 k | 0.009 W |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_F · I · 2t_dt · f_sw = 0.8 · 80 · 100 ns · 48 k | **0.31 W** |
| Gate drive (2 switching FETs) | 2 · Q_g · V_drv · f_sw = 2 · 30 nC · 10 · 48 k | 0.029 W |
| **Total switching-related loss** | | **≈ 1.28 W** |

### 2.3 Total loss at 80 A burst

| | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 24.5 W | 98.0 W |
| Copper | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| Switching + dead time + gate | 1.3 W | 5.1 W |
| **Total** | **29.0 W** | **116 W** |
| Efficiency (P_out ≈ 25.2 · 80 · D, at D ≈ 1) | ≈ 98.6 % | |

**Key design insight:** at 80 A, conduction loss is about 95 % of the total. Raising PWM from 24 kHz to 48 kHz adds only about 0.6 W per channel. To cut heat, choose lower R_DS(on) or parallel FETs. Lowering f_sw buys little, while 48 kHz improves current ripple and motor smoothness.
