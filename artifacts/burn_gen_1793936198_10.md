# STM32H743 AIO 4-in-1 ESC: Loss Model, DShot Timer Allocation, and Schematic Netlists

## 0. Scope Corrections

Three points in the spec need correcting before any numbers are useful.

**1. The H743 should not drive the power stage.** ArduPilot has no field-oriented control (FOC) or six-step commutation running on the flight-controller MCU. A 4-in-1 AIO uses four small ESC MCUs (AT32F421 or STM32G071) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 talks to them over bi-directional DShot600. The 48 kHz switching frequency is an ESC-firmware setting (AM32 `PWM_FREQ`), not an H743 timer. The architecture below follows this split.

**2. 80 A burst per channel on a nano board is thermally marginal.**
- The math in Section 2 gives about 21 W per ESC and about 84 W total at full burst.
- On a 20×20 board, that bursts for roughly 1 to 2 s.
- On a 30.5×30.5 board, it bursts for roughly 4 s.
- Neither can sustain it. Spec it as "80 A, ≤ 2 s burst, 45 A continuous with airflow" or the board will fail qualification.

**3. Four channels at 80 A means 320 A from the battery.** No XT60 or XT90 and no nano-scale power pad survives that. Realistic designs budget about 150 to 200 A total, which is what a real prop and motor combination actually draws.

**Assumptions used throughout:**
- 6S pack, V_bus = 25.2 V
- Complementary (synchronous) trapezoidal PWM
- Example MOSFET class: 40 V, R_DS(on) ≈ 1.0 mΩ at 25 °C, PDFN 5×6
- These are representative values. **Replace them with your actual datasheet values.**

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| R_DS(on),25 | 1.0 mΩ | on-resistance at 25 °C |
| k_T | 1.5 | multiplier for R_DS(on) at T_j ≈ 110 °C |
| t_r + t_f | 20 ns | switching transition time (at the chosen gate resistance) |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

**Current path.** In six-step commutation, at any instant one high-side FET in one leg and one low-side FET in another leg carry the phase current. With synchronous rectification, the freewheel path also flows through a FET channel instead of a diode. The conduction resistance is therefore effectively two FETs in series for the whole period:

$$P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2 \cdot R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

**Temperature dependence.** On-resistance rises with junction temperature:

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0058\ \text{K}^{-1} \Rightarrow k_T \approx 1.5 \text{ at } 110\,°\text{C}$$

**Ripple current.** Phase ripple is set by motor inductance L_ph, assumed here as about 8 µH line-to-line:

$$\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}, \qquad I_{rms} = \sqrt{I_{DC}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}}$$

At D = 0.5 and V_bemf ≈ 12 V, ΔI ≈ 17 A. This gives I_rms ≈ 80.15 A, so ripple is negligible at 48 kHz. Ripple is one of the reasons to run 48 kHz instead of 24 kHz.

**Result:**

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ ESC}$$

**Per-FET split.** Each FET conducts about 1/3 of an electrical cycle:
- Instantaneous dissipation in a conducting FET: 9.6 W
- Average per FET: 3.2 W

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing leg switches hard. There is one hard-switched commutation cell per ESC at any time.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw | 0.5·25.2·80·20 ns·48 k = **0.968 W** |
| C_oss discharge | Q_oss·V·f_sw | 60 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.073 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 50 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.060 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_SD·I·2·t_dead·f_sw | 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k = **0.614 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs of the cell) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw | 2·50 nC·10·48 k = **0.048 W** (dissipated in the driver and R_G) |

**Totals:**

$$P_{sw,total} \approx 1.76\ \text{W/ESC}$$

$$P_{ESC} \approx 19.2 + 1.76 \approx \mathbf{21.0\ W}, \qquad P_{4\text{-}in\text{-}1} \approx \mathbf{84\ W}$$

**Sensitivity to switching frequency:**

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial f_{sw}} \approx 36.6\ \mu\text{W/Hz}$$

Going from 24 kHz to 48 kHz therefore costs about 0.9 W per ESC. Dead time is the largest controllable switching term. Trimming it from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per ESC, if the driver propagation-delay matching allows it.

**Bus capacitor RMS current:**

$$I_{C,rms} = I\sqrt{D(1-D)} \le 40\ \text{A per ESC at } D = 0.5$$

The four ESC PWM phases are uncorrelated, so their contributions add roughly in quadrature: about 80 A RMS worst case on the shared bulk bank. This requires:
- Distributed ceramic capacitors at each leg (≥ 6× 10 µF/50 V X7R 1210)
- Plus a low-ESR external electrolytic (≥ 1000 µF/35 V) on the power leads

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Steady-State Network (per FET)

The thermal path is a series chain:

```
T_j ─[R_θJC 1.2]─ T_case ─[R_θ,pad+vias 2.5]─ T_PCB ─[R_θ,spread 6–10]─ T_board ─[R_θ,BA]─ T_amb
```

- **FET junction rise above board:** 3.2 W × (1.2 + 2.5 + 6) ≈ 31 K.
- **Board to air, still air:** a 30.5×30.5 board (two sides, about 18.6 cm²) at h ≈ 15 W/m²K gives R_θBA ≈ 36 K/W.
  - 84 W × 36 K/W ≈ 3000 K. Steady state at 80 A is impossible.
- **Board to air, prop-wash:** h ≈ 80 to 120 W/m²K gives R_θBA ≈ 5 K/W.
  - Allowable steady load ≈ (100 − 40) / 5 ≈ 12 W.
  - That corresponds to about 30 to 35 A per ESC continuous.
