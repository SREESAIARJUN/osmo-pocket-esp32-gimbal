# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and DShot Timer Plan

## 0. Architecture Findings

Three issues affect everything downstream.

**1. The H743 cannot commutate four BLDC motors itself.**
- Four motors with 6-step drive need 12 complementary PWM outputs with dead-time.
- The H743 has only two advanced timers with complementary outputs and dead-time (TIM1, TIM8), giving 3 complementary pairs each, so 6 pairs.
- HRTIM adds 10 outputs, but they would also have to carry back-EMF sensing and ArduPilot's scheduler at the same time.
- The architecture used in practice, and adopted here, is: **H743 runs ArduPilot → bi-directional DShot600 → four ESC MCUs** (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32), one per motor.

**2. "80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is thermally bounded to well under one second.** Section 3 shows this. The spec must be written as an I²t limit, not as a current.

**3. The netlist below is a reference design topology.** It is not a verified, fab-ready netlist. Before layout, validate pin numbers against the exact parts' datasheets and validate the timer/DMA map with ArduPilot's `hwdef` DMA resolver.

## 2. Loss Model (per motor)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

In each 60° commutation step:
- One phase's high-side (HS) FET switches PWM at duty D, with its low-side (LS) FET switching complementarily at (1−D).
- A second phase's LS FET is held fully on.
- Each phase leg therefore spends 1/3 of the electrical cycle as the PWM leg and 1/3 as the "LS-on" leg.

**Mean-square current in each device:**

$$I_{HS}^2 = I^2\cdot\frac{D}{3}, \qquad I_{LS}^2 = I^2\cdot\frac{(1-D)+1}{3}$$

**Per-FET loss at 80 A, D = 0.95:**

$$P_{HS} = 6400 \times 2.56\text{e-}3 \times \tfrac{0.95}{3} = 5.19\ \text{W}$$
$$P_{LS} = 6400 \times 2.56\text{e-}3 \times \tfrac{1.05}{3} = 5.73\ \text{W}$$

**Per-motor total.** With synchronous rectification, exactly two channels are always conducting, so the total is independent of D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2I^2R_{DS}(T_j) = 32.8\ \text{W}$$

**Copper/trace loss.** Add this term separately:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{trace}$$

With 0.5 mΩ of effective path, it adds ≈ 3.2 W per motor at 80 A. This term is not negligible on a nano board.

### 2.2 Switching losses

At any instant, only one FET per motor is hard-switching.

| Mechanism | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·40 ns·48 kHz | 1.94 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_F·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·200 ns·48 kHz | 0.61 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 0.04 W |
| C_oss charge (×2 devices) | 2·½·C_oss,er·V²·f_sw | 0.03 W |
| **P_sw,motor** | | **≈ 2.62 W** |

**Gate-drive power.** This is dissipated in the driver/regulator, not the FET:

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 14.4\ \text{mW per FET}$$

That is ≈ 0.09 W per motor.

**Switching-time sensitivity.** The 40 ns figure assumes about 1 A of gate drive:

$$t \approx Q_{gd}/I_g$$

Weak drivers (e.g., 0.3 A) give t_r + t_f ≈ 130 ns. That raises V-I overlap loss to ≈ 6.3 W per motor.

### 2.3 Totals at 80 A on all four motors

| Item | Per motor | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 32.8 W | 131.1 W |
| Switching | 2.6 W | 10.5 W |
| Copper (0.5 mΩ) | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| **Total** | **38.6 W** | **≈ 154 W** |

Electrical input power is about 4 × 25.2 × 0.95 × 80 ≈ 7.66 kW. Power-stage loss is therefore ≈ 2.0 %. The percentage is efficient, but the absolute heat is enormous for 400 mm² of board.

**Loss fraction and the frequency trade-off:** at 80 A, conduction loss dominates switching loss by roughly 12:1.
- Dropping f_sw to 24 kHz saves only about 1.3 W per motor.
- Dropping R_DS(on) by 30 % saves about 10 W per motor.
- **Spend the BOM budget on R_DS(on) and copper, not on frequency.**
