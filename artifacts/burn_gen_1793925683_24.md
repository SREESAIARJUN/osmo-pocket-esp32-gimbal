## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Architectural Findings

Three findings change the spec. Read these first.

| # | Finding | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | **The H743 cannot commutate 4 motors directly.** It has only 2 advanced timers (TIM1, TIM8) with complementary outputs, which covers 2 three-phase bridges. | The architecture is H743 (ArduPilot) driving 4 ESC MCUs (AT32F421 / STM32G071 class running AM32) over bidirectional DShot600. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs, not the H743. |
| F2 | **An 80 A burst per channel is not thermally sustainable on a "nano" (20×20 mm) footprint.** The board can absorb it only as a short transient. The §3 analysis gives about 4–6 s from 40 °C to the 110 °C board limit. | Recommend a 30.5×30.5 mm ESC board, or limit burst to ≤3 s with firmware current limiting. The 20×20 mm stack is limited to about 35–45 A burst. |
| F3 | **Bus-capacitor ripple current, not the FETs, is the hidden limiter.** Worst case is about 40 A RMS per channel at D = 0.5. | Bulk polymer capacitors plus distributed MLCCs per channel are mandatory (see §2.6). |

## 1. Commutation Model (6-step trapezoidal, complementary PWM)

At any instant, one leg is PWM-switching (HS chopping, LS synchronous rectification), one leg has its LS fully on, and one leg floats (BEMF sense). The phase current I flows through exactly two conducting switches at all times, ignoring dead time:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = I^2\,\frac{R_{ds}(T_j)}{N_{par}}\,\big[D + (1-D)\big] + I^2\,\frac{R_{ds}(T_j)}{N_{par}} = \frac{2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)}{N_{par}}$$

Each switch position is active for 1/3 of the electrical period. The average per-FET conduction loss is:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{2I^2R_{ds}}{6\,N_{par}^2}\cdot N_{par} = \frac{I^2 R_{ds}}{3\,N_{par}}$$

**Note on RMS.** The phase current is quasi-square, so I_RMS,ph = I·√(2/3), which is consistent with the above. Burst at full throttle sets I_dc ≈ D·I_ph with D → 1.
