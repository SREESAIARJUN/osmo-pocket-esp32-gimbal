## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

### 0.1 Partitioning

The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. In any ArduPilot-compatible design:

- **The FC is the H743.** It runs ArduPilot and generates DShot600 on its timers.
- **The 4-in-1 ESC has four dedicated ESC MCUs.** Typical parts are the AT32F421 or STM32G071, running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Each one owns the 48 kHz complementary PWM and dead-time for its six FETs.
- **The H743 has an HRTIM, but ArduPilot has no on-chip motor-control path.** Driving 24 FETs from the FC is out of scope.

The loss and thermal model below therefore applies to the **ESC board**. The DMA and timer model applies to the **FC**.

### 0.2 Feasibility Flag

80 A per channel on a "nano" (20×20 mm) stack is a **burst-only** rating. Section 3 shows the board heat-soaks in seconds. Confirm with the customer whether "80 A" means per-channel or aggregate. That distinction changes the design class entirely.

## 1. Conduction Losses

### 1.1 Current Path

With synchronous rectification in 6-step (trapezoidal) drive, the phase current always flows through **two conducting FETs in series**:

- **During PWM on-time:** the high-side FET of phase A and the low-side FET of phase B.
- **During PWM off-time:** the low-side FET of phase A (synchronous) and the low-side FET of phase B.

This holds regardless of duty cycle D. The conduction loss per ESC channel is therefore:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

### 1.2 Evaluation at T_j = 125 °C

$$R_{DS(on)} = 0.70 \times 1.5 = 1.05\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.05\times10^{-3} = 13.44\ \text{W}$$

### 1.3 Copper Loss

$$P_{cu} = I_{ph}^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\times10^{-3} = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

### 1.4 Per-FET Distribution

Over one electrical cycle, the six FETs share the conduction loss. Each FET is active for roughly one third of the period:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{13.44}{6} \approx 2.24\ \text{W (average)}$$

Peak instantaneous dissipation in a single FET is about 6.7 W.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing leg (one high/low pair) switches hard. Each loss term below is per channel.

### 2.1 V–I Overlap

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot40\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k} = 1.94\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse Recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Output Capacitance (Two Devices)

$$P_{oss} = 2\cdot\tfrac12 Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 0.05\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM period:

$$P_{dt} = V_f I_{ph}\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate Drive (Two Devices, Dissipated Mostly in Driver and Gate Resistors)

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Total Switching Loss

$$P_{sw,tot} \approx 2.72\ \text{W per channel}$$

### 2.7 Sensitivity

- **Overlap loss** scales with f_sw. At 24 kHz it falls to about 1 W.
- **Dead-time loss** is also proportional to f_sw. Cutting t_dt to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel. Do this only if the shoot-through margin allows it.
