> **Key finding:** At these assumptions, 80 A is a **sub-second transient rating, not a continuous one**. The full burst dissipates about 106 W across the board, and a 20×20 board cannot reject that heat. The burst duration limit is derived in §3. Please review the burst-rating marketing claim against the numbers below before committing it to a datasheet.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 110 °C, using the max datasheet value:

$$R_{ds} = 1.3\ \text{m}\Omega \times 1.425 = 1.85\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Bridge conduction

With synchronous rectification in 6-step commutation, current always flows through **exactly two conducting channels**:
- one leg is PWMing, with its high-side FET or complementary low-side FET conducting;
- one leg's low-side FET is held on.

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j) = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.85\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{23.7\ W\ per\ motor}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each FET conducts during ⅓ of the electrical cycle. At burst conditions the electrical period is far shorter than the junction thermal time constant (ms scale), so averaging is valid:

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds} = 3.95\ \text{W}$$

Duty cycle D splits this between the high-side and low-side FETs of the PWM leg. Worst case is D near 1, which concentrates the loss in the high side.

### 1.4 Battery current versus phase current

$$I_{batt} \approx D \cdot I_{ph}$$

The 80 A phase burst therefore does **not** mean 80 A from the battery, except at full throttle.

### 1.5 Interconnect copper loss

Assume shunt plus traces total about 0.6 mΩ per phase path:

$$P_{Cu} \approx I_{ph}^2 \times 0.6\ \text{m}\Omega \approx 3.8\ \text{W per motor}$$

This term is often ignored, but it is significant on nano boards.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches hard. Terms below are per motor.

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap (t_r + t_f ≈ 40 ns) | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw | ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 k = **1.94 W** |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_f · I · 2t_dt · f_sw | 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw | 50 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.06 W** |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss · V · f_sw | 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.05 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs active) | 2 · Q_g · V_GS · f_sw | 2 × 40 nC × 10 × 48 k = **0.04 W** (dissipated in driver/gate resistor) |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.70 W** |

**Switching-time estimate.** With a ~1 A driver and Q_gd ≈ 10 nC, the Miller plateau lasts about 10 ns per edge. Adding ring time with a ~4.7 Ω gate resistor gives roughly 20 ns per edge. This is consistent with the t_r + t_f ≈ 40 ns used above.

**Frequency sensitivity.** Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw: about 56 mW per kHz per motor.
