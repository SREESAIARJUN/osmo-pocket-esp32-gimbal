# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Electrothermal and DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and a Feasibility Flag

I don't have the Job #39 specification, so every parameter below is a stated assumption that you can replace. The equations are parameterized for that purpose.

**Architectural assumption.** The STM32H743 cannot practically commutate four sensorless BLDC motors itself. The standard and assumed architecture is:

- **H743** runs ArduPilot as the flight controller.
- **4× AT32F421 (or STM32G071)** run AM32 firmware as the ESC MCUs.
- **4× FD6288Q** three-phase gate drivers.
- **24× N-channel MOSFETs** form the power stage.

| Parameter | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal |
| PWM frequency | 48 kHz, 6-step trapezoidal, complementary (synchronous) PWM |
| MOSFET | 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN, R_ds(on) = 1.2 mΩ at 25 °C, Q_g = 40 nC, Q_rr = 40 nC, Q_oss = 30 nC |
| Rise/fall time (gate R ≈ 4.7 Ω) | t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 15 ns |
| Dead time | 100 ns |
| Board | 20×20 mm "nano" form factor, 8 layers, 2 oz copper |
| Ambient | 40 °C, with propwash cooling |

**⚠ Feasibility flag (read before layout).** "80 A burst" must be clarified:

- **80 A per ESC (320 A total):** physically not viable on a 20×20 mm board. The calculation in §1.4 shows roughly a 2-second adiabatic limit. That requirement needs a 30×30 mm or larger power board with 5×6 mm FETs.
- **80 A total (20 A per ESC):** comfortable.

I compute both cases.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step commutation

In 6-step commutation, two phases conduct at any instant:

- one high-side FET (the PWM-switched leg, with its complementary low side conducting during the off-time under synchronous rectification), and
- one static low-side FET.

So the motor current always flows through exactly **two channel resistances in series**.

**Conduction loss per ESC:**

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

**Temperature dependence of R_ds(on):**

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.0065\ /\mathrm{K}$$

At T_j = 105 °C: R ≈ 1.2 mΩ × 1.52 ≈ **1.82 mΩ**.

**Per-FET average loss.** Each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical cycle (120°):

$$P_{cond,FET} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,I^2\,R_{ds(on)}$$

### 1.2 PCB copper loss (frequently ignored, and significant here)

$$R_{Cu} = \rho_{Cu}(T)\cdot\frac{L}{w\cdot t\cdot N_{layers}},\qquad \rho_{Cu,100°C} = 2.2\times10^{-8}\,\Omega\,\mathrm{m}$$

Example path: L = 15 mm, w = 4 mm, 70 µm copper, 4 paralleled layers.

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{2.2\times10^{-8}\times0.015}{0.004\times70\times10^{-6}\times4} \approx 0.29\ \mathrm{m\Omega}$$

There are about three such segments per phase loop (battery pad → bus, bus → FETs, FETs → motor pad), giving **R_Cu,loop ≈ 0.9 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Results

| Case | I per ESC | P_cond, FETs (per ESC) | P_Cu (per ESC) | Total, 4 ESCs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A: 80 A per ESC | 80 A | 2·6400·1.82e-3 = **23.3 W** | 6400·0.9e-3 = **5.8 W** | **116 W** |
| B: 80 A total | 20 A | 2·400·1.82e-3 = **1.46 W** | 0.36 W | **7.3 W** |

### 1.4 Thermal capacity of the board (adiabatic burst limit)

| Component | Calculation | Heat capacity |
|---|---|---|
| FR4 | 20×20×1.6 mm = 0.64 cm³ × 1.85 g/cm³ = 1.18 g × 1.1 J/g·K | 1.30 J/K |
| Copper | 4 cm² × 70 µm × 8 layers = 0.224 cm³ × 8.96 g/cm³ = 2.0 g × 0.385 J/g·K | 0.77 J/K |
| FETs, components, solder | estimate | ~0.4 J/K |
| **C_th, total** | | **≈ 2.5 J/K** |

**Case A, including switching losses from §2 (≈ 125 W total):**

$$\frac{dT}{dt} = \frac{125}{2.5} \approx 50\ \mathrm{K/s}$$

From 40 °C to a 110 °C board limit, **t_burst ≈ 1.4 s**, before considering junction rise. **This is not a credible "burst" rating.**

**Case B (≈ 15 W total including switching):**

$$\frac{dT}{dt} \approx 6\ \mathrm{K/s}$$

The steady-state calculation in §3 governs this case.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In 6-step commutation, only **one leg switches** at a time. The switching node commutates between the high-side FET and the low-side FET's body diode or channel.

$$P_{sw} = \underbrace{\tfrac{1}{2}V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw}}_{\text{V–I overlap}} + \underbrace{Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}}_{\text{output cap}} + \underbrace{Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}}_{\text{reverse recovery}} + \underbrace{2V_F\,I\,t_{dt}\,f_{sw}}_{\text{dead-time diode}} + \underbrace{2Q_gV_{gs}f_{sw}}_{\text{gate, in driver}}$$

Inputs: V = 25.2 V, f = 48 kHz, V_F = 0.8 V, V_gs = 10 V.

| Term | Case A (80 A) | Case B (20 A) |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap: 0.5·25.2·I·30 ns·48 k | 1.45 W | 0.36 W |
| Q_oss: 30 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.036 W | 0.036 W |
| Q_rr: 40 nC·25.2·48 k (scales roughly with I) | 0.048 W | ~0.02 W |
| Dead time: 2·0.8·I·100 ns·48 k | 0.61 W | 0.15 W |
| Gate: 2·40 nC·10 V·48 k (dissipated in driver and R_g) | 0.038 W | 0.038 W |
| **P_sw per ESC** | **≈ 2.2 W** | **≈ 0.6 W** |

**Why 48 kHz rather than 24 kHz.** Switching loss roughly doubles. In exchange, current ripple ΔI ≈ V/(4·L·f) halves on low-inductance motors (L ≈ 15 µH), which reduces copper RMS heating and audible noise. For a 6S, low-kV build this is a reasonable trade.

**Per-ESC total:**

- Case A: 23.3 + 5.8 + 2.2 ≈ **31 W** (≈ 125 W for the board)
- Case B: 1.46 + 0.36 + 0.6 ≈ **2.4 W** (≈ 10 W for the board)
