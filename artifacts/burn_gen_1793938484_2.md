# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Reference Design Model (Job #19)

## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

**Architecture clarification:** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and generates bi-directional DShot600. It does not commutate the motors. Each motor channel needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, for example 4× AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 never sees phase currents directly. It sees eRPM telemetry and the ESC current-sense sum. All numbers below assume this two-tier architecture.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

**Commutation scheme:** Six-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. At every instant, one high-side FET and one low-side FET carry the full phase current I_ph. Each FET conducts for 120° electrical, which is one third of the period.

**Temperature-dependent on-resistance:**

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\quad \alpha\approx0.0045\,/\mathrm{K}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_hot = 1.3 mΩ × 1.45 = **1.89 mΩ**.

**Instantaneous conduction loss per motor channel** (two devices in the current path):

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{hot} = 2(80)^2(1.89\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{24.2\ W}$$

**Average loss per FET** (one-third duty, with the high-side/low-side split absorbed by synchronous rectification):

$$P_{cond,FET} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,I_{ph}^2 R_{hot} = \mathbf{4.03\ W}$$

**Copper path loss:** Phase and bus traces are assumed to be about 0.3 mΩ per current path.

$$P_{Cu} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 3.8\ \text{W per channel}$$

This loss is spread across the board, not concentrated at the FETs.

**Battery current versus phase current:** I_bat ≈ D · I_ph. An 80 A phase burst at D ≈ 0.9 draws about 72 A per motor and about 290 A for the whole quad. Your XT60/XT90 connector, capacitor bank and current shunt must be rated for this.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switched pair dissipates switching loss. The low-side FET switches with zero voltage across it (ZVS), apart from the dead-time interval.

**Gate currents:**

$$I_{g,on}=\frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_g}=1.2\,\mathrm{A},\qquad I_{g,off}=\frac{V_{pl}}{R_g}=0.8\,\mathrm{A}$$

**Transition times:**

$$t_{on}=\frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}}=8.3\,\mathrm{ns},\qquad t_{off}=\frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,off}}=12.5\,\mathrm{ns}$$

**Loss components per channel:**

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_on+t_off)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·20.8 ns·48 k | 1.01 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 0.036 W |
| C_oss discharge | ½·C_oss·V²·f_sw | 0.015 W |
| Dead-time body diode | V_f·I·2t_dt·f_sw | 0.61 W |
| Gate drive (6 FETs) | 6·Q_g·V_drv·f_sw (dissipated in driver and R_g) | 0.086 W |
| **Total switching-related loss** | | **≈1.76 W** |

**Design note:** At 48 kHz, dead-time loss is about 60% of the V–I overlap loss. Reducing t_dt from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel. Gate-ring margin must be verified on the bench before doing this.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Junction-to-board rise (local)

The local thermal resistance from junction to the surrounding board is:

$$R_{\theta j\text{-}b} = R_{\theta jc} + R_{\theta,solder+spread} \approx 1.5 + 8 = 9.5\,\mathrm{K/W}$$

This assumes ≥12 thermal vias of 0.3 mm diameter under each drain pad.

$$\Delta T_{j\text{-}b} = 5.4 \times 9.5 \approx \mathbf{51\ K}$$

The FET time constant is short (under about 1 s), so this is effectively a steady offset during a burst.
