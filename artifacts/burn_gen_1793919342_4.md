## 0. Architecture Corrections & Scope Flags (read first)

Three points affect everything downstream.

| # | Issue | Resolution adopted |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The STM32H743 does not drive the power stage.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each ESC channel has its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32 commutation firmware. The H743 runs ArduPilot only. | Architecture uses **1× STM32H743 (FC) + 4× ESC MCUs** (STM32G071 or AT32F421 class, AM32 firmware). The H743 talks to them via bidirectional DShot600. |
| 2 | **"Nano" (20×20 mm) and 80 A burst per motor are physically incompatible.** You cannot fit 24 FETs, 4 drivers, and 4 MCUs on 20×20 mm, and the copper cannot carry 320 A. | The model below is computed for a **30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz board**. Section 3.6 shows why 20×20 fails. If "nano" is a hard requirement, the burst rating must drop to about 35–40 A. |
| 3 | **Component values are assumed.** No BOM was supplied. | All MOSFET and driver parameters are representative 40 V DFN5×6 class values (Table 1.1). Re-run the equations with your actual datasheet values. Every formula is parametric for that reason. |

### 1.2 Commutation Scheme

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM on the active leg. In any 60° sector:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for duty D. The low-side FET of the same leg conducts for (1−D), synchronous-rectified.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET is held ON for 100% of the sector.
- **Floating leg:** off, used for BEMF zero-crossing sensing.

**Consequence:** current always flows through exactly **2 FETs in series**, independent of D.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

$$
P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{ds,hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{ds,hot} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j)
$$

The temperature coefficient must be included. Otherwise the model under-predicts loss by about 33%.

$$
R_{ds(on)}(T_j) \approx R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0059\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W}
$$

**Positive feedback check:** dP/dT_j = 2·I²·R₂₅·α = 75 mW/K per channel. This is stable as long as the thermal path removes heat faster than about 0.075 W/K, which it does (section 3). Thermal runaway is not a risk at 80 A, but it becomes one above about 110 A.

### 2.2 Hard-Switching Loss (V–I Overlap)

Only the PWM-leg high-side FET hard-switches. The synchronous low-side FET commutates with near-zero V_ds after dead time.
