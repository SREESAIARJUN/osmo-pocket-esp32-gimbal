| Form factor | "Nano" = 20×20 mm mounting pattern, 6-layer 2 oz board |

> ⚠️ **Feasibility flag.** As shown in §3, 4 × 80 A on a 20×20 board dissipates about 77 W. Thermally, the board can survive roughly 1–3 s at that load before it is near its limits. There is also a footprint problem: 24 FETs with sub-mΩ R_ds(on) do not fit in 400 mm². Commercial 20×20 AIOs are typically rated 13–20 A continuous and 25–35 A burst. Either relax the rating or move to a 30.5×30.5 / 36×36 board. The math below lets you evaluate either option.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 0.9 mΩ × 1.5 = **1.35 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

In each 60° commutation sector, two phases carry current I:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for duty D. The synchronous low-side FET conducts for (1 − D) while freewheeling.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET conducts for 100% of the sector.

The current therefore always flows through exactly two channel resistances, independent of D:

$$P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)\,\big(1 + \tfrac{1}{12}r^2\big)$$
