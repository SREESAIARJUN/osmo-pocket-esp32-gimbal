## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)]$$

- Typical α ≈ 0.005 /K for trench silicon (≈1.5× at 125 °C) **[DS: normalized R_DS(on) vs T_j curve]**.
- At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.0 × 1.5 = **1.5 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current path (6-step trapezoidal, complementary/synchronous PWM)

At any instant, two phases carry current. In the PWM-on interval, current flows through the high-side switch of phase A and the low-side switch of phase B. In the PWM-off interval, it flows through the low-side (synchronous rectifier) of phase A and the low-side of phase B.

Exactly two MOSFET channels carry the phase current at all times, independent of duty D:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\, I_{ph,rms}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 1.3 RMS correction for ripple

The motor winding inductance L_ph produces a triangular ripple:

$$\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}, \qquad I_{rms} = \sqrt{I_{avg}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}}$$

For a typical 2207 motor at 1700–1950 KV (L_ph ≈ 15 µH), with V_bus = 25.2 V, D = 0.5, and back-EMF ≈ 12 V:

$$\Delta I = \frac{13.2 \times 0.5}{15\times10^{-6} \times 48\times10^{3}} \approx 9.2\ \text{A} \;\Rightarrow\; I_{rms} = \sqrt{80^2 + 9.2^2/12} = 80.04\ \text{A}$$

The ripple correction is negligible at 80 A.

### 1.4 Result

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ ESC}\quad(\text{at } T_j = 125\,°\text{C})$$

At T_j = 25 °C (start of burst), the same calculation gives 12.8 W. Section 4 handles this electro-thermal coupling iteratively.
