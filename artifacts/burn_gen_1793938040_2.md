## 2. Electrical Loss Model (Per Motor)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

Assume 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM on the switching leg. At any instant, current flows through:
- the PWM leg (high side for D, low side for 1−D), and
- the static low side of the return phase.

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{hot} = 2\,I^2 R_{hot}
$$

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.8\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{23.0\ W}
$$

Two FETs carry this at any instant, so each "hot" FET dissipates **≈11.5 W instantaneous**. Averaged over commutation, each FET conducts about 1/3 of the time.

**Electro-thermal coupling.** R_ds depends on T_j, so iterate:

$$
R(T_j) = R_{25}[1 + k(T_j - 25)], \quad k \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

Converge P → T_j → R. At 80 A, two iterations typically settle within 3%.

### 2.2 Switching Loss at 48 kHz

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = 0.5·25.2·80·35 ns·48 k | **1.69 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 40 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.048 W |
| C_oss | ½·C_oss·V²·f_sw | 0.015 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2t_dead·f_sw = 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k | **0.61 W** |
| Gate charge (2 FETs in leg) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw | 0.029 W (dissipated in driver/R_g) |
| **Total switching** | | **≈2.4 W** |

### 2.3 Copper, Shunt, and Interconnect

Battery current per motor is I_bat ≈ D·I_ph. At D ≈ 0.9 this gives 72 A per motor, or **~288 A total** for the quad.

- **Shunt.** A 0.2 mΩ shunt at 288 A dissipates **16.6 W**. Use a 0.1 mΩ shunt (8.3 W) or Hall/phase-shunt sensing. This is often the forgotten hotspot.
- **Phase traces.** Plan for at least 0.3 mΩ per phase path, which dissipates about 1.9 W per motor at 80 A. Use stacked vias, ≥4 parallel layers, and solder-filled pads.

### 2.4 Totals at Burst

| | Per motor | Quad |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 23.0 W | 92 W |
| Switching | 2.4 W | 9.6 W |
| Copper | ~1.9 W | ~7.6 W |
| Shunt (0.1 mΩ) | | ~8.3 W |
| **Total** | **~27 W** | **~118 W** |

**Efficiency check.** Output is about 288 A × 25.2 V ≈ 7.26 kW, so stage efficiency is ≈98.4%. That looks good on paper, but 118 W into a ~2.5 J/K board is the real limit.
