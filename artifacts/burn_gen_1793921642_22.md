## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Flags

I need to resolve two points before the math.

**1. Where the 48 kHz PWM lives.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600. It does **not** commutate the motors. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU, assumed to be AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 firmware. That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM to a 3-phase gate driver. The H743 timer/DMA plan (§4) and the 48 kHz switching model (§2) therefore belong to different silicon.

**2. "80 A burst" on a nano board is at the edge of physics.** Both interpretations are modeled below:
- **Case A:** 80 A per motor channel (320 A aggregate). On a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm board this is limited to roughly 1–3 s by heat and copper.
- **Case B:** 80 A aggregate (20 A per channel). This is comfortably feasible.

My recommendation is to rate the product as **"40 A continuous / 55 A 5 s burst per channel" on a 30.5×30.5 mm board with heatsink**. An honest 80 A per channel requires larger copper, as §3 shows.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-Step Trapezoidal Commutation with Synchronous Rectification

At any instant, two phases carry current I. Leg X is PWM'd (high-side ON for D, low-side ON for 1−D). Leg Y has its low-side held ON 100%.

The per-channel conduction loss is:

$$P_{cond,ch} = \underbrace{I^2 R\,D + I^2 R\,(1-D)}_{\text{PWM leg}} + \underbrace{I^2 R}_{\text{static leg}} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

This result is **independent of duty cycle** when synchronous rectification is enabled (AM32 default). The phase current I equals the DC-link current divided by D. In a burst near full throttle, D → ~1, so I ≈ I_bus.

### 1.2 Temperature Dependence

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)]$$

### 1.3 PCB Copper Loss

Assume a battery → FET → phase pad path resistance R_Cu ≈ 0.5 mΩ per channel (2 oz, short 6–8 mm pours, both directions).

Copper resistance rises about 0.393 %/K:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu}(T)$$
