### Architectural correction (important)

On a 4-in-1 ESC the STM32H743 does **not** switch the MOSFETs. Each ESC channel, or each pair of channels, has its own MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32/S, for example an AT32F421 or STM32G071. That MCU drives a 3-phase gate driver such as an FD6288Q-class part. The responsibilities split as follows:

- **H743 (ArduPilot):** generates bidirectional DShot600 and captures the eRPM telemetry.
- **ESC MCU:** generates the 48 kHz PWM, commutation and dead time.

The power-loss model below applies to the ESC half, and the DMA model applies to the H743.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.5 × 1.6 = **2.4 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current-path analysis: six-step with complementary (synchronous) PWM

In each 60° commutation sector, one phase is PWM-switched and a second phase's low side is held on statically. The third phase floats.

- **During PWM on-time (D):** current flows through HS(A) and LS(B). That is two FETs in series.
- **During off-time (1−D):** current flows through LS(A) (synchronous rectification) and LS(B). That is also two FETs in series.

The motor current therefore always passes through exactly two R_ds(on) elements, whatever the duty cycle:

$$\boxed{P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I_{ph,rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)}$$

### 1.3 Per-device split per electrical cycle

Each leg conducts for 120° of the electrical cycle, half of it as the PWM leg and half as the static low side.

| Device | Conduction fraction | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| High-side FET | D/3 | I²·R·D/3 |
| Low-side FET, synchronous part | (1−D)/3 | I²·R·(1−D)/3 |
| Low-side FET, static part | 1/3 | I²·R/3 |

### 1.4 Numerical evaluation (I_ph = 80 A, T_j = 125 °C, D → 1)

- Per ESC channel: P_cond = 2 × 80² × 2.4 mΩ = **30.7 W**
- Per high-side FET: 6400 × 2.4 m / 3 = **5.1 W**
- Per low-side FET: also about **5.1 W**
- All four channels at 80 A simultaneously: **≈123 W**

**Reality check.** If "80 A burst" means **80 A total** (≈20 A per motor), losses scale with I². Each channel then dissipates 2 × 20² × 2.4 m ≈ **1.9 W**, or about **7.7 W** for the board. That is a realistic 20×20 nano design point. 80 A *per channel* is only survivable for sub-second bursts; see §3.

Relationship between battery and phase current: in six-step operation I_batt ≈ D·I_ph. At high throttle I_ph ≈ I_batt. At partial throttle the phase current exceeds the battery current, which is why low-duty, high-load cases such as punch-outs from hover can be thermally worse than they appear on the battery current sensor.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The HS FET takes the V–I overlap loss, and the complementary LS commutates through its body diode during dead time.
