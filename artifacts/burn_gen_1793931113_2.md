## 0. Architectural Ground Truth and Assumptions

Two points need settling before any math.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot on the H743 cannot also run four real-time 6-step or FOC loops at 48 kHz. The correct partition is:
   - **FC domain:** STM32H743 running ArduPilot, which sends bi-directional DShot600 to the ESCs.
   - **ESC domain:** four independent ESC MCUs (STM32G071 or AT32F421 running AM32, or EFM8 running BLHeli_S/Bluejay). Each has a 3-phase gate driver and 6 N-channel MOSFETs.

2. **"80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 or 30.5×30.5 mm) board is thermally possible only for seconds.** Section 3 quantifies this. The numbers show that 80 A is a transient rating, not a continuous one.

**Assumed design parameters.** Replace these with your chosen parts' datasheet values. Every equation below is parametric.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, phase current always flows through **two conducting devices in series**:

- the PWM leg (HS during on-time, LS during off-time), and
- the static low-side of the return phase.

Therefore, regardless of duty cycle D:

$$P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2 \cdot R_{ds(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right] \approx k_T R_{25}$$

For flat-top trapezoidal current, I_rms ≈ I_pk = 80 A:

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times (1.5 \times 0.70\,\text{m}\Omega) = 6400 \times 2.1\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{13.44\ W\ per\ channel}$$

**Per-device average.** Each FET conducts approximately 1/3 of the electrical cycle (HS or LS, with the PWM split between them), so:

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx 13.44 / 6 \approx 2.24\ \text{W}$$

**Electrothermal coupling.** Loss raises T_j, which raises R_ds(on). Iterate using T_j = T_board + P·R_θ until converged. The loop is stable if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T} \cdot R_\theta < 1$$

With α ≈ 0.4 %/K and R_θ,j-board ≈ 15 K/W per FET, the loop gain is ≈ 0.13. It is stable but adds about 15 % to the cold estimate. The k_T = 1.5 figure already covers this.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The return-leg LS is static within a commutation sector.

### 2.1 V-I overlap (hard turn-on and turn-off of HS)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (LS body diode, after dead time)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{ k} = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

Q_rr scales with I and di/dt. At 80 A, derate pessimistically by ×2, giving 0.12 W.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss,eq} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} \times 2 = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{ nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{ k} \times 2 = 0.046\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period)

$$P_{DT} = V_{SD} I \cdot 2 t_{dead} f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in driver plus gate resistors, not the FET die)

$$P_G = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2 = 80\text{ nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{ k} \times 2 = 0.077\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Totals per channel at 80 A, 6S, 48 kHz

| Loss term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction | 13.44 |
| V-I switching | 1.94 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Q_rr (derated) | 0.12 |
| C_oss | 0.05 |
| Gate (driver side) | 0.08 |
| **Total per channel** | **≈ 16.2 W** |
| **4 channels** | **≈ 65 W** |

At 1.6 kW per channel output (25.2 V × 80 A × D≈0.8), this is about 98.7 % efficiency. The *absolute* dissipation in a ~9 cm² board is the problem.

### Design levers

- **Switching frequency.** Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.3 W per channel at the cost of acoustic noise and current ripple.
- **Gate drive and dead time.** Stronger gate drive (lower t_r, t_f) and shorter dead time (50 ns) attack 2.1 and 2.4.
- **Conduction.** Conduction dominates at 82 %. Paralleling FETs (12 per channel) halves P_cond, but there is rarely room on a nano board.
