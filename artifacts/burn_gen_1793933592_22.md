**Architecture correction.** In a 4-in-1 ESC design, the **STM32H743 does not generate the 48 kHz bridge PWM**. Each ESC has its own MCU, typically one AT32F421 or STM32G071 per motor running AM32 or BLHeli_32. That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM and handles commutation. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to the ESC MCUs over **bi-directional DShot600**. The physics below is split along that boundary.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

With trapezoidal 6-step commutation and synchronous rectification (complementary PWM), there are always **two MOSFETs in series** with the motor current:
- During the ON portion (duty D): the high-side of the driven phase plus the low-side of the return phase.
- During the OFF portion (1 − D): the low-side of the driven phase (sync rectifier) plus the low-side of the return phase.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

$$R_{DS}(125\,°C) = 1.0 \times 1.5 = 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Per-channel conduction loss

Phase current RMS includes PWM ripple ΔI_pp:

$$I_{rms} = \sqrt{I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}}$$

Ripple is negligible at 80 A for typical motor inductance (ΔI ≈ 5 A gives +0.03 A), so I_rms ≈ 80 A.

$$P_{cond,FET} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j) = 6400 \times 3.0\ \text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

$$P_{cond,Cu} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot R_{path} = 6400 \times 0.5\ \text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$
