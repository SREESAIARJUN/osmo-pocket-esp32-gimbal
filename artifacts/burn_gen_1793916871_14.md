# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC: Systems Architecture Package

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Early Warnings

I don't have prior documentation for "Job #45," so this package is built from first principles. Every component value is a stated assumption. Replace the parameters in **Table 0** with your selected parts' datasheet values; all equations stay valid.

**Architecture clarification.** On a 4-in-1 stack, the STM32H743 is the **flight controller**. It sends DShot and receives eRPM telemetry. It does not run motor commutation. Commutation runs on per-motor ESC MCUs, typically AM32 or BLHeli_32 firmware on an STM32G071 or AT32F421. The 48 kHz switching figure is an ESC-firmware parameter. The DShot/DMA allocation is an H743/ArduPilot parameter. I treat them as two coupled subsystems.

> **⚠ Feasibility flag (stated up front):** 80 A per motor on a 20×20 mm or 30.5×30.5 mm board with no heatsink is **thermally burst-limited to roughly 1–3 s at 6S**. Section 3 derives this. If marketing requires "80 A burst," define it contractually, e.g. *"80 A for 1 s, 25 °C ambient, 6S, 10 s recovery at ≤20 A."*

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation with synchronous rectification

In any 60° commutation sector, two phases carry current: one sourcing, one sinking.

- The PWM-switched leg alternates between its HS FET (duty D) and its complementary LS FET (duty 1−D).
- The other leg's LS FET (or HS, depending on PWM scheme) conducts continuously for that sector.

Because synchronous rectification keeps a channel conducting during both PWM states, the current always passes through **exactly two channels in series**:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

The model is built up in four steps:

1. **Temperature dependence of on-resistance:**
$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right]$$

2. **RMS current including PWM ripple.** Ripple is triangular, with peak-to-peak ΔI:
$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I \approx \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{2 L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$
The 2L term arises because two phase windings are in series. As an example, take L_ph = 15 µH, D = 0.5, and f_sw = 48 kHz. Then ΔI ≈ 4.4 A, which adds only 0.02 A² relative to 6400 A². It is negligible at 80 A, but not at light load with low-inductance motors.

3. **Per-FET average.** Each phase conducts 240° electrical out of 360°, split between HS and LS. Averaged over an electrical cycle, the 6 FETs share the loss approximately equally:
$$\bar P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{2 I^2 R_{ds}}{6} = \frac{I^2 R_{ds}}{3}$$
The *instantaneous* loss in a conducting FET approaches I²R_ds. This matters because the DFN die thermal time constant (~1–5 ms) is comparable to the electrical period at low eRPM.

4. **Worked numbers** at 80 A and T_j = 125 °C:
   - R_ds(125 °C) = 1.2 mΩ × 1.5 = **1.8 mΩ**
   - P_cond,motor = 2 × 6400 × 0.0018 = **23.0 W**
   - Average per FET ≈ 7.7 W per leg pair, or **3.8 W per FET**
   - Peak instantaneous per FET = 6400 × 0.0018 = **11.5 W**

### 1.2 Electro-thermal coupling (runaway check)

Because R_ds rises with T_j, the stable operating point must satisfy:

$$T_j = T_a + R_{\theta JA}\, \bar P_{FET}(T_j) \quad\Rightarrow\quad T_j = \frac{T_a + R_{\theta JA}\, k\,(1 - 25\alpha)}{1 - R_{\theta JA}\, k\, \alpha}, \quad k = \frac{I^2 R_{ds25}}{3}$$

- **Stability condition:** R_θJA · k · α < 1.
- With k = 2.56 W and α = 0.005, the steady-state limit is **R_θJA < 78 K/W**. This is easily met, so there is no true runaway.
- However, a realistic R_θJA of ~25–35 K/W per FET on a shared, crowded nano PCB gives T_j ≫ 150 °C in steady state.
- **Conclusion: 80 A is transient-only.** See Section 3.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switched leg incurs switching loss: one HS/LS pair per motor at any instant.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss (hard-switched HS turn-on/turn-off)

Gate currents during the Miller plateau:

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{pl}}{R_{g,ext} + R_{g,int}} = \frac{7}{5.7} = 1.23\ \text{A}, \qquad I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}} = 0.53\ \text{A}$$

$$t_{on} = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = 7.3\ \text{ns}, \qquad t_{off} = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,off}} = 17.0\ \text{ns}$$
