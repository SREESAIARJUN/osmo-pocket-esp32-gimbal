## 0. Feasibility Verdict

**A 20×20 mm board cannot sustain 80 A on all four channels for more than about 1 s.** With realistic 3.3×3.3 mm MOSFETs, total dissipation at 4×80 A is about 125 W, and the board's thermal mass is about 2 J/K. The derivation is in §3.

Choose one of three paths:

| Option | Burst rating that closes thermally | Change required |
|---|---|---|
| A | 80 A for ≤0.8 s (punch-out only), 35 A continuous | Stay at 20×20, enforce firmware current limit |
| B | 80 A for ~4 s, 50 A continuous | Move to 30.5×30.5, 5×6 mm FETs, Al heat-spreader |
| C | 80 A for ~1.5 s, 45 A continuous | 25.5×25.5 "whoop-plus" mounting, 8L 2oz, 3.3×3.3 FETs |

All the math below is parameterized so you can re-run it for any option.

**Architecture note.** The H743 does not switch the bridges. In a 4-in-1 AIO, each channel runs its own ESC MCU (AM32 or BLHeli_32 class, e.g. AT32F421). The ESC MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM, and the H743 talks to it over bidirectional DShot600. The design therefore has two firmware domains:
- **ArduPilot on the H743**
- **AM32 on 4× AT32F421**

## 2. Loss Model per ESC Channel

### 2.1 Commutation topology

The model assumes six-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM on the active high-side leg. Under that scheme, at every instant the phase current flows through **exactly two FET channels**:
- either HS_x + LS_y during the ON interval,
- or LS_x + LS_y during the OFF interval (synchronous freewheel).

Each of the 6 FETs is involved for 1/3 of the electrical period.
