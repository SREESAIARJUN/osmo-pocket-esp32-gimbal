## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and a Feasibility Flag

No MOSFET, gate driver, board stack-up, or burst duration was specified, so I have parameterized every equation and then worked a numeric example with representative values. **Substitute your datasheet values before signing off.**

**Feasibility flag:** 80 A burst per channel on a "nano" 4-in-1 (20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm) is far beyond what the copper can absorb, even for a few seconds. Section 3 quantifies this. Shipping nano 4-in-1 ESCs are typically rated 35–45 A burst per channel. 80 A realistically needs a 30.5×30.5 mm board or larger, 2–3 oz inner copper, and forced airflow.

**Architecture clarification:** The H743 runs ArduPilot and generates bidirectional DShot600. The **48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC's own MCUs** (AM32 / BLHeli_32 class, e.g. STM32G071 or AT32F421), not by the H743. The two halves are modeled separately.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)]$$

### 1.2 Six-step commutation with synchronous rectification

At any instant, the motor current flows through two energized phases: one high-side and one low-side path. With complementary (synchronous) switching, the freewheeling current flows through the opposite FET of the PWM'd leg instead of its body diode. So, outside dead time, **exactly two FET channels carry I_ph at all times**, regardless of duty cycle D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Per-FET average loss: each FET carries current for 1/3 of the electrical cycle, split between D (high side) and 1−D (low side). Averaged over the bridge:

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} = \frac{P_{cond,motor}}{6}$$

**The hotspot is not the average.** During a commutation sector at high throttle (D → 1), a single high-side FET carries the full current. For thermal-transient sizing of that worst FET, use:

$$P_{cond,FET,worst} \approx I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)\cdot\tfrac{1}{3}\ \text{(steady)}, \qquad \text{up to}\ I^2 R_{DS}\ \text{instantaneous}$$

With multiple FETs in parallel per switch position (N_p), divide R_DS by N_p.

### 1.3 Electrothermal fixed point

R_DS depends on T_j, and T_j depends on loss. Solve self-consistently:

$$T_j = T_a + P(T_j)\cdot Z_{th}(t)
\;\Rightarrow\;
T_j = \frac{T_a + Z_{th}\,(P_0 - 25\,\alpha P_0) + \dots}{1 - \alpha\,P_0\,Z_{th}}, \qquad P_0 = I^2\cdot 2R_{25}$$

**Thermal runaway condition:** the denominator reaches zero when α·P₀·Z_th ≥ 1. Use this as a hard design check.

### 1.4 Worked example (80 A, T_j assumed 125 °C)

- R_DS(125 °C) = 1.2 mΩ × 1.5 = **1.8 mΩ**
- P_cond,motor = 80² × 2 × 1.8 mΩ = 6400 × 3.6 mΩ = **23.0 W per motor**
- Runaway check: α·P₀·Z_th = 0.005 × 15.4 W × Z_th, which requires Z_th < 13 K/W. At steady state this is easily violated on a nano board, so the design depends on burst duration.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per motor, each PWM period has one hard-switched turn-on and one hard-switched turn-off on the active leg.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard switching)

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2} + Q_{gd}}{I_g} = \frac{10\,\text{nC}}{0.5\,\text{A}} = 20\ \text{ns (each edge)}$$

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (body diode of the complementary FET)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 30\,\text{nC}\times25.2\times48\,\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

Q_rr scales with di/dt and I_F, so at 80 A expect 2–3× the datasheet value, roughly **0.1 W**.

### 2.3 Output-capacitance energy

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,C_{oss,er}\,V_{bus}^2\,f_{sw}\times 2 = 0.6\,\text{nF}\times635\times48\,\text{k} = 0.018\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f\,I\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times400\,\text{ns}\times48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

This term is significant and is often ignored. Reducing t_dt to 100 ns (AM32 dead-time setting, if the driver allows) halves it.

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors)

$$P_{gate} = Q_g\,V_{GS}\,f_{sw}\times6 = 30\,\text{nC}\times10\times48\,\text{k}\times6 = 0.086\ \text{W per motor}$$

### 2.6 Total loss budget (burst, per motor, T_j = 125 °C)

| Loss term | Watts |
|---|---|
| Conduction | 23.0 |
| V–I switching | 1.94 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 |
| Reverse recovery | 0.10 |
| C_oss | 0.02 |
| Gate (FET side) | ~0.04 |
| **Per motor** | **≈ 26.3 W** |
| **4 motors** | **≈ 105 W** |
| Plus shunts, copper, bulk-cap ESR | +5–10 W |

**Efficiency at burst:** P_out ≈ 25.2 V × (80 A × D) per motor. Conduction dominates (~87%), so the lever is lower R_DS / parallel FETs, not lower f_sw. Dropping to 24 kHz saves only ~1.6 W per motor and costs audible noise and current ripple.
