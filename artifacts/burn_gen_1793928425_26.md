## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Flags

**The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design, the H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600. Four ESC MCUs (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32) generate the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The "48 kHz switching" therefore refers to the ESC-MCU PWM frequency, not an H743 timer.

**80A on a nano (20×20 mm) footprint is not thermally credible.** Section 3 shows that 4×80A dissipates over 100 W into roughly 3–5 J/K of thermal mass. The model below supports 80A only as a short burst (on the order of 1–2 s) on a 30.5×30.5 mm board. Continuous rating will be roughly 30–40A per channel. I recommend that Job #22 be re-scoped to 30×30 if 80A burst is a hard requirement.

## 2. Loss Model: 6-Step Trapezoidal with Synchronous Complementary PWM

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

During one 60° sector, current I_m flows through three switches:
- the high-side FET of leg A (PWM, duty D),
- the low-side FET of leg A while freewheeling (1 − D),
- the low-side FET of leg B (on for the full sector).

The instantaneous bridge conduction loss is therefore:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = I_m^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I_m^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This result is independent of duty. That is a key result: you cannot throttle your way out of conduction loss at a given current.

**Per-FET distribution.** Each leg spends 120° as the high/PWM leg, 120° as the low leg, and 120° floating:

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I_m^2 R_{ds}, \qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(2-D)\, I_m^2 R_{ds}$$
