# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Budget, and ArduPilot Bi-directional DShot600 Allocation

## 0. Two Corrections Before the Model

**Where the 48 kHz switching happens.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller MCU. It does not switch the MOSFETs. In a 4-in-1 ESC, each motor channel has its own small ESC MCU (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). That ESC MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 only sends DShot600 commands and receives eRPM telemetry. The model below keeps those two domains separate.

**"80A burst" must be defined.** There are two possible readings:

| Interpretation | Electrical power at 6S (25.2 V) | Realistic on a nano AIO? |
|---|---|---|
| **A: 80 A per motor** | ~8 kW total | **No.** It exceeds what the copper, connectors, and thermal mass of a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm board can survive beyond about 1–2 s. |
| **B: 80 A total (20 A per motor)** | ~2 kW total | Yes. This is the normal class for nano AIOs. |

I derive the model symbolically, then evaluate both cases. Case A is included to show where the design fails.

| Reverse recovery charge | Q_rr | 60 nC | |
| Output charge | Q_oss | 50 nC | |
| Total gate charge | Q_g | 60 nC | Gate drive at 10 V |
| Junction-to-case thermal resistance | R_θJC | 1.2 K/W | |
| Case-to-PCB thermal resistance (with local spreading) | R_θC-PCB | 5 K/W | Strongly layout dependent |

**Commutation scheme:** 6-step trapezoidal with synchronous rectification (the AM32/BLHeli default). At any instant two phases conduct, so current I flows through exactly **two MOSFETs in series**: either the high-side switch of one leg, or the complementary low-side switch, plus the return path. Only one leg is PWM-switching at a time.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Per motor bridge

$$
P_{cond}(T_j) = 2\, I_{ph,rms}^2\, R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

With synchronous rectification, the factor of 2 is independent of duty cycle. Current flows through the high-side switch during the on-time and through the low-side channel during the off-time, so the conduction path always contains two R_DS(on) elements.

### 2.2 Thermal feedback and runaway condition

Junction temperature depends on the loss it produces:

$$
T_j = T_{PCB} + P_{FET}\,(R_{\theta JC}+R_{\theta C\text{-}PCB})
$$

Substituting the temperature-dependent loss gives a closed-form steady state:

$$
T_j = \frac{T_{PCB} + K(1-25\alpha)}{1 - K\alpha}, \qquad K = I^2 R_{25}\, R_{\theta,total}\, d_{FET}
$$

Here d_FET is the fraction of time a given FET carries current. That is about 1/3 for 6-step operation, split between the high- and low-side devices.

**Stability criterion:** Kα < 1. If Kα ≥ 1, there is no steady state and the device enters thermal runaway.

### 2.3 Copper loss on the PCB

PCB copper loss is often forgotten, but it is significant at these currents:

$$
P_{Cu} = I^2 \frac{\rho_{Cu}\, L}{w\, t\, n_{layers}}
$$

For example, take a 15 mm path, 3 mm width, 2 oz copper (70 µm), and 4 parallel layers. The resistance is about 0.30 mΩ, which matches a third FET in series.

### 2.4 Evaluation at T_j = 125 °C (R_DS(on) = 1.45 mΩ)

| | Case A (80 A/motor) | Case B (20 A/motor) |
|---|---|---|
| P_cond per bridge | 2 × 6400 × 1.45e-3 = **18.6 W** | 2 × 400 × 1.45e-3 = **1.16 W** |
| P_Cu per motor | 6400 × 0.30e-3 = **1.9 W** | **0.12 W** |

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg commutes per PWM cycle. The loss components are:

$$
P_{sw} = \underbrace{\tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}}_{\text{V–I overlap}}
+ \underbrace{Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}}_{\text{C}_{oss}}
+ \underbrace{Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}}_{\text{reverse recovery}}
+ \underbrace{2\,V_f\, I\, t_{dt}\, f_{sw}}_{\text{dead-time diode}}
$$

Gate drive loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET:

$$
P_{gate} = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw}
$$

### Evaluation

| Term | Case A (80 A) | Case B (20 A) |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap: 0.5 × 25.2 × I × 40 ns × 48 k | 1.94 W | 0.48 W |
| C_oss: 50 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.06 W | 0.06 W |
| Reverse recovery: 60 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.07 W | 0.07 W |
| Dead-time diode: 2 × 0.8 × I × 200 ns × 48 k | 1.23 W | 0.31 W |
| **P_sw total** | **3.30 W** | **0.92 W** |
| Gate drive (in the driver) | 0.06 W | 0.06 W |

### Design levers

- **Dead time** is the biggest single lever in Case A. Halving it to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per motor.
- **Switching frequency:** dropping from 48 kHz to 24 kHz halves P_sw but increases current ripple and motor iron loss. 48 kHz is reasonable for low-inductance quad motors.

## 4. Total Loss Budget

| | Case A (80 A/motor) | Case B (20 A/motor) |
|---|---|---|
| Per motor (conduction + Cu + switching) | 18.6 + 1.9 + 3.3 = **23.8 W** | 1.16 + 0.12 + 0.92 = **2.2 W** |
| Four motors | **95 W** | **8.8 W** |
| Efficiency | 1 − 95/8064 = 98.8 % | 1 − 8.8/2016 = 99.6 % |

The efficiency in Case A looks fine on paper. The real problem is that 95 W must leave a board weighing a few grams.
