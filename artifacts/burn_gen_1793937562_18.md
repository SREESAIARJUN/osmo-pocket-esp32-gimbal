# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and Bidirectional DShot600 DMA Architecture

**Document:** Job #21, Architecture Baseline Rev A
**Scope:** FC MCU (STM32H743), 4× ESC MCUs, 24 power MOSFETs, gate drive, sensing, ArduPilot timer/DMA mapping

---

## 0. Feasibility Finding (Read First)

Before the math, one result shapes everything else:

> **An 80 A per-motor burst on a nano (20×20 mm mounting, ~26×26 mm outline) AIO is not thermally or electrically sustainable beyond roughly 1–3 seconds.** At 80 A the power stage dissipates about **33 W per motor (about 130 W total)** in a board with roughly 1–2 J/K of effective heat capacity.

This document therefore does three things:

1. It defines "80 A burst" as a **time-limited rating**: 80 A for ≤1 s at 25 °C ambient with 50% duty recovery.
2. It derives the **continuous rating** that the copper and packages can actually support (about 18–22 A per motor with airflow).
3. It specifies firmware current/thermal limiting so the burst rating cannot be abused.

If the product requirement is truly sustained 80 A per motor, the form factor must move to at least 30.5×30.5 mm with 5×6 mm MOSFETs, or to a separate stacked 4-in-1 ESC.

## 3. Conduction Loss Model

### 3.1 Commutation topology

AM32 uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with **complementary (synchronous) PWM**. At any instant:

- **Phase A (PWM leg):** the high-side conducts for D·T_sw, and the low-side conducts (synchronous rectification) for (1−D)·T_sw.
- **Phase B (return leg):** the low-side is continuously ON for the 60° electrical sector.
- **Phase C:** floating (BEMF sensing).

The DC-link current in the PWM leg is I. Because one switch of the PWM leg always carries I, the conduction loss per motor is independent of D:

$$
P_{cond,PWM\ leg} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

$$
P_{cond,return\ leg} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

$$
\boxed{P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)}
$$

### 3.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

| T_j | R_DS(on) |
|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.60 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 2.14 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 2.32 mΩ |
| 150 °C | 2.50 mΩ |

### 3.3 Ripple correction

Phase inductance of typical 5" motors is L_ph ≈ 15–40 µH (line-to-line ≈ 2·L_ph). Worst-case ripple occurs at D = 0.5:

$$
\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus} \cdot D(1-D)}{2L_{ph}\, f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{2 \times 20\mu H \times 48\,kHz} = 3.28\ \text{A}
$$

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 0.9 \approx 6401\ \text{A}^2
$$

The correction is negligible at 80 A (<0.02%). It matters only below about 10 A.

### 3.4 Conduction loss at 80 A

$$
P_{cond,motor}(125\,°C) = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.32\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{29.7\ W}
$$

$$
P_{cond,motor}(25\,°C) = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.6\,\text{m}\Omega = 20.5\ \text{W}
$$

### 3.5 PCB copper conduction loss (frequently ignored, significant at 80 A)

The current path per motor is: battery pad → bus plane → HS drain → phase node → motor pad, plus the return through the LS source → GND plane.

For 2 oz copper (70 µm), the sheet resistance at 25 °C is:

$$
R_\square = \frac{\rho_{Cu}}{t} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}}{70\times10^{-6}} = 0.246\ \text{m}\Omega/\square
$$

- **Phase trace** from MOSFET to motor pad: ~6 mm long × 3 mm wide = 2 squares. With 4 layers paralleled through via stitching (2 oz outer + 2× 1 oz inner, effective ≈ 0.08 mΩ/□), R ≈ 0.16 mΩ.
- **Bus/GND share:** ≈ 0.10 mΩ per motor path.

$$
P_{Cu,motor} \approx 80^2 \times (2 \times 0.16 + 0.10)\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 2.7\ \text{W}
