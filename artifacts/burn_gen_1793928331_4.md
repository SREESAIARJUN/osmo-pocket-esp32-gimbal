# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC "Nano": Power-Stage, Thermal, and Bi-Directional DShot Architecture

## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility Constraints

Several points in the brief need to be settled before the math, because they change the results.

1. **Two MCU domains.** In this architecture the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and generates DShot600. It does not commutate the motors. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU (AT32F421, STM32G071 or EFM8 running AM32 or BLHeli_32/Bluejay). The 48 kHz PWM, dead-time and gate drive live on the ESC MCUs. All switching math below applies to that domain.
2. **"80 A burst" is interpreted as 80 A per motor channel.** That implies up to 320 A of battery current. As shown in §3, a 20×20 mm (or even 25.5×25.5 mm) board cannot carry this for more than about 1–2 s. Commercial "80 A" ratings are usually per channel, on 30.5×30.5 mm boards with heatsinks, for about 10 s. I give the model and then the honest operating envelope.
3. **Component values are assumed, not datasheet-verified.** They are representative of a 40 V N-channel MOSFET in PDFN3.3×3.3 and a 6S system. Before committing the design, replace them with the actual datasheet values for your selected parts.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation topology
The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM, as AM32 uses. In any 60° sector:

- **Leg A (switching):** the high-side FET conducts for duty D and the low-side synchronous FET for (1 − D).
- **Leg B (static):** the low-side FET is on for 100% of the sector.
- **Leg C:** floating (BEMF sensing).

### 1.2 Instantaneous conduction loss per channel

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 R_{hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{rms}^2 R_{hot} = 2\,I_{rms}^2 R_{hot}
$$

$$
R_{hot} = k_T R_{25} + R_{pcb} = 1.6(1.5) + 0.3 = 2.7\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

Current ripple adds to the RMS value:

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12},\qquad \Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph} f_{sw}}
$$

Example: L_ph = 15 µH and D = 0.9 at V_bemf ≈ 20 V give ΔI_pp ≈ 6.5 A. The ripple term is then 3.5 A², which is negligible against 6400 A². So:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2(80^2)(2.7\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{34.6\ W}
$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution
Each FET conducts about one third of the electrical cycle at high duty:

$$
P_{cond,FET} = \frac{I^2 R_{hot}}{3} = \frac{6400 \times 2.7\text{m}}{3} = \mathbf{5.76\ W}
$$
