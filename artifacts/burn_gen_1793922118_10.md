## 0. Architectural Corrections

Two issues need resolving before the math matters.

**1. The H743 cannot be the ESC commutation controller under ArduPilot.**
ArduPilot is a flight-control stack. It does not perform BLDC commutation, BEMF zero-cross detection, or FOC. A viable architecture is:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot FC) ──4× Bi-Dir DShot600──► 4× ESC MCUs (AM32 / BLHeli_32-class)
                                                  e.g. AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071GBU6
                                                  ──► 4× 3-phase gate drivers ──► 24 MOSFETs
```

**2. "Nano" + 80A burst per channel is thermally marginal.**
Section 3 shows that a 20×20 or 30.5×30.5 mm board can survive roughly a 1–2 s burst at 80A/channel, not sustained operation. The 80A figure must be specified with a duration (e.g., "80A for 1 s, 10% duty cycle"). Otherwise the requirement is undefined.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

The ESC uses trapezoidal 6-step drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM. At any instant, current flows through:

- the active leg (the high-side for duty D, then the low-side for 1−D, so the leg is always conducting), and
- the low-side of the return leg, which is held on.

So there are always **two R_DS(on) in series** with the phase current:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125°C, R_DS = 1.5 mΩ:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

**Per-FET distribution.** Averaged over a commutation cycle, each of the 6 FETs carries ≈ P_cond/6 ≈ 3.2 W. The instantaneous peak is higher: during a 60° sector, the held-on low-side carries the full I²R ≈ 9.6 W. For bursts shorter than one electrical revolution, use the peak.

**Copper loss:**

$$P_{cu} = I_{ph}^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

**Thermal runaway check.** Conduction loss rises with T_j. Solving self-consistently with θ the effective thermal resistance:

$$T_j = T_a + \theta \cdot 2I^2 R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

$$T_j = \frac{T_a + \theta k (1 - 25\alpha)}{1 - \theta k \alpha}, \quad k = 2I^2R_{25}$$

This is stable only when **θ·k·α < 1**. With k = 12.8 W and α = 0.005, that requires θ < 15.6 K/W. That bound is easy to meet, but in practice it shows conduction loss keeps climbing as the board heats.

### 2.2 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches: one high-side/low-side pair per sector.

**(a) V–I overlap (hard switching):**

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{drv}} = \frac{10\,\text{nC}}{1\,\text{A}} = 10\,\text{ns per edge}$$

To cover the Q_gs2 region and driver/layout inductance, use t_r + t_f ≈ 40 ns as a realistic figure.

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery** (low-side body diode, after dead time):

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}$$

The Q_rr-induced extra turn-on current also adds overlap loss. Datasheets often understate this, so budget about 2× → 0.07 W.

**(c) Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.024\ W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction (2 edges per period):**

$$P_{dt} = V_F I_{ph} \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive** (dissipated in the driver and gate resistor, not the FET):

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2 = 60\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = \mathbf{0.058\ W}$$

### 2.3 Loss Summary @ 80 A, T_j = 125°C

| Loss | Per ESC | ×4 Channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 19.20 W | 76.8 W |
| Copper | 3.20 W | 12.8 W |
| V-I switching | 1.94 W | 7.8 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W | 2.4 W |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.09 W | 0.4 W |
| Gate drive | 0.06 W | 0.2 W |
| **Total** | **≈25.1 W** | **≈100 W** |

At burst, conduction losses dominate (~89%). 48 kHz switching costs only about 2.6 W per channel. **Lowering R_DS(on) is the only lever that matters at 80A.** Moving to a 0.6 mΩ FET in a 5×6 package saves ~7.7 W per channel but won't fit a 20×20 board.

### 2.4 DC-Link Capacitor Ripple

The input RMS ripple per channel is:

$$I_{C,rms} = I_{ph}\sqrt{D(1-D)}$$

This peaks at D = 0.5 with **40 A rms per channel**. Four unsynchronized channels can reach roughly 40·√4 = 80 A rms worst case if their phases align.

The local MLCC bank (e.g., 8× 10 µF/50 V X7R 1206 per channel) cannot absorb this alone. Mandate a **low-ESR bulk electrolytic or polymer at the battery pads** (≥ 1000 µF / 35 V, plus a TVS such as SMBJ30A).
