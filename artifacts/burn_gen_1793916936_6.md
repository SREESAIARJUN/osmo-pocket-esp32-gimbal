## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Design Assumptions

Several architectural facts shape everything below.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In a 4-in-1 ESC, each motor has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bi-directional DShot600 to those MCUs. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs, not by the H743.
2. **"80A burst" on a nano board (20×20 mm) is an aggressive claim.** Section 3 shows it is thermally feasible only for roughly 1 s bursts. Treat the 80 A figure as a transient rating, never a continuous one.
3. **Every numeric parameter not tied to a specific datasheet is a design assumption.** Replace each with values from the final BOM before sign-off. All values are tabulated so the model can be re-run.

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, the current at any instant flows through:
- one high-side FET (phase A), and
- one low-side FET (phase B).

During PWM off-time, the complementary low-side of phase A freewheels the same current. The net effect is that **two R_ds(on) are always in series** with the motor current, independent of duty cycle D:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds(on)}(T_j)
$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$
R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$
R_{ds} = 1.8\,\text{mΩ} \times 1.5 = 2.7\,\text{mΩ}
$$

### 1.3 Ripple correction

The phase current carries a triangular ripple Δi on top of its average:

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I^2\left(1 + \frac{r^2}{12}\right), \qquad r = \frac{\Delta i}{I}
$$

The phase inductance determines the ripple:

$$
\Delta i = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}
$$

For a typical 5" motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH) at high throttle, r ≈ 0.1. The ripple correction is therefore below 0.1% and is neglected.
