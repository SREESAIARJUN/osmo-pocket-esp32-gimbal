### Architectural correction

The STM32H743 should not drive the power stages directly. It is the flight-controller MCU running ArduPilot.

- Each of the four ESC channels gets its own small motor MCU. Use an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, which natively supports bidirectional DShot and EDT.
- The H743 talks to the ESCs only over bidirectional DShot600.

This is the correct partitioning for latency, firmware support, and fault isolation.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A burst)

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.5 mΩ × 1.45 = **2.18 mΩ**

### 2.2 Current path in trapezoidal synchronous drive

During any 60° commutation sector, current flows through exactly two FETs at every instant:

- **PWM-on:** high-side of phase X, then the low-side of phase Y.
- **PWM-off (synchronous rectification):** low-side of phase X, then the low-side of phase Y.

The path therefore always contains two R_ds(on) elements, independent of duty D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 2.3 Per-FET distribution (for hotspot analysis)

Over one electrical cycle, each phase conducts for 2/3 of the time.

| FET | Conduction fraction | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| High-side | D/3 | P_HS = I²R·D/3 |
| Low-side | (1−D)/3 + 1/3 | P_LS = I²R·(2−D)/3 |

At low throttle, low-side FETs are the hotspots. At 80 A burst (D → ~0.9), the load is roughly balanced.

### 2.4 Numbers at 80 A

- **Instantaneous per conducting FET:** 80² × 2.18 mΩ = **13.95 W**
- **Per motor:** 2 × 13.95 = **27.9 W**
- **Four motors simultaneous burst:** **111.6 W**

### 2.5 Copper and connection losses

Assume a 15 mm path, 4 parallel 2 oz layers, 6 mm wide on V_BUS and GND. Use ρ_Cu = 1.72 µΩ·cm and 1.39 at 100 °C.

- Sheet resistance at 2 oz: ≈ 0.245 mΩ/□ → ×1.39 = 0.34 mΩ/□ hot.
- Path = 2.5 □ / 4 layers ≈ 0.21 mΩ per leg. Supply plus return ≈ 0.42 mΩ.
- Phase pads and wire joints ≈ 0.3 mΩ.
- **P_Cu ≈ 80² × 0.72 mΩ ≈ 4.6 W per motor.**

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

The total per switching leg (one PWM'd half-bridge per motor) is:

$$P_{sw} = \underbrace{\tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f}_{\text{V-I overlap}} + \underbrace{Q_{rr} V f}_{\text{reverse recovery}} + \underbrace{\tfrac12 C_{oss(er)} V^2 f}_{\text{C}_{oss}} + \underbrace{2 V_{SD} I\, t_{dt} f}_{\text{dead-time diode}} + \underbrace{Q_g V_{GS} f}_{\text{gate (in driver)}}$$

| Term | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| Overlap | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 27 ns × 48 k | **1.306 W** |
| Q_rr | 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.048 W |
| C_oss | 0.5 × 1.5 nF × 635 × 48 k | 0.023 W |
| Dead time | 2 × 0.8 × 80 × 50 ns × 48 k | 0.307 W |
| Gate (×2 FETs) | 2 × 40 nC × 10 V × 48 k | 0.038 W (driver) |
| **Total per motor** | | **≈ 1.72 W** |

**Conclusion:** At 80 A, conduction (27.9 W) dominates switching (1.7 W) by about 16:1. Raising PWM to 96 kHz costs only about 1.7 W per motor. L
