## 0. Executive Summary and Feasibility Verdict

Before deriving anything, three architectural facts need to be fixed. Each one changes the math.

1. **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz motor PWM.** In every production AIO of this class, each motor bridge is commutated by a dedicated ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071 running AM32, or EFM8 running BLHeli_S). The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to those MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. The design below follows that partitioning:
   - The 48 kHz switching-loss model applies to the ESC power stage.
   - The DMA/timer model applies to the H743.

2. **"80 A burst" must be defined.** I define it as **80 A RMS phase current per channel, for a bounded duration**. That is how ESC ratings are conventionally quoted. It is *not* battery current.

3. **80 A on a nano (20×20 mm) board is not physically sustainable.** The thermal model in §3 shows:
   - The board can survive 80 A on all four channels for about **1.8 s**.
   - Continuous capability is about **25–28 A/channel** under prop-wash cooling.
   - Any "80 A" rating on a 20×20 board is a sub-2-second rating.

   If the requirement is a genuine multi-second 80 A, the board must grow to 30.5×30.5 mm or larger, with 5×6 mm MOSFETs. §3.6 quantifies this.
