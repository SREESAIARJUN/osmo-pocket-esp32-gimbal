# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #56): Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and ArduPilot BDShot600 Timer/DMA Plan

**Bottom line:** An 80 A per-motor burst rating on a nano-format board is not credible. The model below produces about 100 W of heat from the power stage and copper. A 26 × 26 mm board can only shed about 15 W continuously. The board survives roughly 1.5–3 s of burst from a cool start. Realistic ratings for this format are about 35 A continuous and 45–50 A burst for 2–3 s. The "80 A" figure only holds as a sub-second transient that an FET datasheet would allow.

**What is a starting point, not a final design:**

- The netlists are reference-design topology. Part numbers are representative and must be confirmed against datasheets, stock, and your layout before fab.
- Timer and DMA assignments follow ArduPilot ChibiOS conventions but must be checked against the current `hwdef` docs and on the bench with a logic analyzer.

## 0. System Architecture Assumptions

The H743 does **not** commutate the motors. The 48 kHz PWM comes from separate ESC MCUs, which the H743 commands over bidirectional DShot.

| Block | Choice | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| FC MCU | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100), 480 MHz (rev V) or 400 MHz | Runs ArduPilot |
| ESC MCUs | 4 × AT32F421K8U7 (QFN32), one per motor | Runs AM32. STM32G071 is an alternative |
| Gate driver | 4 × FD6288Q (3-phase half-bridge, QFN24) | Bootstrap high side, Vgs about 10–12 V |
| MOSFETs | 24 × N-ch, 40 V, DFN3.3×3.3 or 5×6, about 1.2 mΩ at 10 V/25 °C | 6 per motor |
| Bus | 6S LiPo: 22.2 V nominal, 25.2 V max | All losses use 25.2 V as worst case |
| Modulation | Trapezoidal 6-step, complementary (synchronous) PWM, 48 kHz | AM32 setting |
| Board | 26 × 26 mm, 20 × 20 mm mount, 8-layer, 2 oz outer / 2 oz inner power | "Nano" class |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step complementary PWM

In each 60° sector, phase A's high side is PWM'd at duty D while phase B's low side is held fully on.

- During D·T_sw, current flows Q_AH → motor → Q_BL.
- During (1−D)·T_sw, current freewheels through the synchronous rectifier: Q_AL → motor → Q_BL. The dead-time portion is covered in §2.

So at every instant, **two FETs carry the full DC-link-equivalent phase current I_ph**:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

Averaged over an electrical revolution, the six FETs share this equally:

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} = \frac{2 I_{ph}^2 R_{DS(on)}}{6} = \frac{I_{ph}^2 R_{DS(on)}}{3}$$

The instantaneous per-FET loss is up to 3× the average. However, at eRPM above about 20k the electrical period (under 3 ms) is far shorter than the die thermal time constant (about 10–50 ms). The average governs the junction temperature except at very low RPM or stall. **Stall or desync is the worst case**, because one pair of FETs takes all the loss.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence (positive feedback)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.004\text{–}0.006\ /°C$$

Taking R₂₅ = 1.2 mΩ, α = 0.005, and T_j = 100 °C:

$$R_{DS(on)} = 1.2 \times 1.375 = 1.65\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A burst

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.65\times10^{-3} = 21.1\ \text{W}$$
