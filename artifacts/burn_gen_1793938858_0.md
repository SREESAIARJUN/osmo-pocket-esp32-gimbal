# Job #9: STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 80A-Burst ESC

**Architecture, Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, DShot DMA Plan and Reference Netlist (Rev A, Design Baseline)**

---

## 0. Architectural Corrections and Scope Assumptions

These points determine whether the rest of the design is valid.

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design that runs ArduPilot, the H743 runs ArduPilot and *emits* bidirectional DShot600. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The baseline here uses an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32. That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM, handles BEMF/zero-cross commutation, and returns eRPM telemetry. The loss model (§2–3) applies to the ESC power stage. The DMA model (§4) applies to the H743.
2. **"Nano" plus 80 A burst conflicts with thermal physics.**
   - A 20×20 mm (M2) board cannot carry 4×80 A.
   - A 30.5×30.5 mm board can carry 80 A *per channel* only for **≤ ~5–10 s** with airflow.
   - The battery bus would see up to 320 A, which no XT60/XT90 lead or 30×30 board copper can sustain.

   The design therefore targets **80 A per channel burst (≤10 s), 45–55 A continuous per channel, and a 30.5×30.5 mm form factor**. The nominal stack-total burst limit is ~200 A.
3. **Operating point:** 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V maximum (22.2 V nominal). Synchronous rectification, trapezoidal 6-step drive, f_sw = 48 kHz, T_amb = 40 °C.
4. **Reference MOSFET** (representative 40 V, 5×6 mm or 3.3×3.3 mm class, e.g. 40 V/≤1 mΩ parts). Replace these with your chosen part's datasheet values before release.

## 1. Power-Stage Topology per Channel

- 3 half-bridges (6 N-FETs) per motor, 24 FETs total.
- 3-phase gate driver (e.g. FD6288Q class), V_drv = 10–12 V, R_g(on) = 4.7 Ω, R_g(off) = 2.2 Ω with a diode.
- In 6-step commutation two phases conduct at any instant:
  - One high-side/low-side **pair is PWM-switched** (complementary, with dead time t_d).
  - One low-side FET is **held ON**.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_ds ≈ 0.95 × 1.51 = **1.43 mΩ**.

### 2.2 Current path

The phase current I_ph is approximately the DC-link current I_DC. Ripple is neglected because the motor inductance is ≫ the value needed at 48 kHz. Over a PWM period with duty D:

- The switched leg passes current through the HS FET for D·T and through the LS FET (synchronous rectification) for (1−D)·T. This leg therefore always contributes exactly one R_ds.
- The return leg's LS FET conducts 100% of the time.

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2\,\big[D\,R_{ds} + (1-D)\,R_{ds}\big] + I_{ph}^2 R_{ds} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This holds independent of D, which is the virtue of synchronous rectification.

Add the copper and shunt path:

$$P_{path} = I_{ph}^2\,(R_{Cu} + R_{shunt})$$

with R_Cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ (2 oz outer + 2 oz inner pours) and R_shunt = 0.25 mΩ (or 0 Ω if using phase-current-sense-free AM32).

### 2.3 At 80 A, T_j = 110 °C

| Term | Calculation | Power |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 2 × 80² × 1.43 mΩ | **18.3 W** |
| Copper + shunt | 80² × 0.55 mΩ | **3.5 W** |
| **Total conduction per channel** | | **21.8 W** |

Heat concentrates in two FETs, ~9.2 W each.

## 3. Switching Loss Model (f_sw = 48 kHz)

Only the PWM leg switches. Each period has one HS turn-on and one turn-off, both hard-switched, while the LS is soft-switched ZVS-like after body-diode conduction.

### 3.1 V–I overlap

Gate current during the Miller plateau:

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}{R_{g,on} + R_{g,int}} = \frac{12 - 4.5}{4.7 + 1.0} \approx 1.32\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{plateau}}{R_{g,off} + R_{g,int}} = \frac{4.5}{3.2} \approx 1.41\ \text{A}$$

Transition times:

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd} + Q_{gs2}}{I_g}$$

With Q_gs2 ≈ 6 nC: t_on ≈ 13.6 ns and t_off ≈ 12.8 ns. Add ~5 ns of di/dt-limited current rise for loop inductance L_loop ≈ 2 nH: t_r+t_f ≈ 36 ns.

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I_{ph}\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 36\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.74\ W}$$

### 3.2 Capacitive (Q_oss) loss

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 55\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.067\ W}$$

### 3.3 Reverse recovery (LS body diode → HS turn-on)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

At 80 A, Q_rr rises with di/dt. Budget 2× for this: **0.10 W**.

### 3.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\,I_{ph}\,(2\,t_d)\,f_{sw}$$

With t_d = 200 ns: 0.8 × 80 × 400 ns × 48 k = **1.23 W**.

Dead time is the dominant controllable loss. AM32 dead-time settings at 48 kHz should be tuned to the minimum safe value, and should be verified with a scope on the switch node.

### 3.5 Gate drive (all 6 FETs; complementary pair active; commutation-averaged)

$$P_g = Q_g\,V_{drv}\,f_{sw} \times 2 = 60\text{n} \times 12 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = \mathbf{0.069\ W}$$

Most of this is dissipated in R_g and the driver, not in the FET.

### 3.6 Switching total per channel

| Term | Power |
|---|---|
| V–I overlap | 1.74 W |
| Q_oss | 0.07 W |
| Q_rr (2× margin) | 0.10 W |
| Dead time | 1.23 W |
| Gate drive | 0.07 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 3.2 W** |

### 3.7 Grand total at 80 A burst

$$P_{ch} = 21.8 + 3.2 \approx \mathbf{25\ W/channel}$$

| Scenario | Stage Loss | Other Loads | Total Board Heat | Efficiency |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 channels × 80 A | ~100 W | H743 @ 480 MHz ≈ 0.6 W; 4× ESC MCU ≈ 0.2 W; BEC (5 V/3 A buck, η≈90%) ≈ 1.7 W; 10 V/12 V driver supply ≈ 0.5 W | **≈ 103 W** | 1 − 100/(4×80×25.2×D_eff) ≈ 98.5% at full throttle |

Efficiency looks high, but 100 W in ~9 cm² of PCB is the governing problem.

### 3.8 Scaling law (for firmware current limits)

$$P_{ch}(I) \approx 2R_{ds}(T_j)\,I^2 + (R_{Cu}+R_{sh})\,I^2 + \left[\tfrac12 V (t_r+t_f) + 2 V_{SD} t_d\right] f_{sw}\, I + (Q_{oss}+Q_{rr}+2Q_g\tfrac{V_{drv}}{V})V f_{sw}$$

The fitted form is P(I) ≈ 3.4e-3·I² + 0.040·I + 0.24 W. At 50 A this gives ≈ 10.7 W per channel.

Dropping f_sw to 24 kHz saves ~1.5 W per channel at 80 A, at the cost of current ripple and audible noise. Treat this as a design lever.
