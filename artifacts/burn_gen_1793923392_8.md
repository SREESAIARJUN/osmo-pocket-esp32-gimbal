**Architectural clarification.** The H743 does not commutate the motors. Each ESC channel needs its own MCU, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32. The H743 talks to those MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. Both halves are covered below.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-step (trapezoidal) operation with synchronous rectification

At any instant two phases carry the current I:

- **PWM leg:** the high side conducts for a fraction D of the time. The complementary low side conducts for (1 − D), apart from dead time.
- **Return leg:** the low side is held fully on.

Summing the conduction losses in the bridge:

$$
P_{cond,bridge} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

This result does not depend on duty cycle, which makes it a useful sizing rule.

R_ds(on) rises with junction temperature:

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

### 1.2 Numerical result at 80 A

At T_j = 105 °C, R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.005 × 80) = 1.4 mΩ.

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.4\text{ mΩ} = 17.9\ \text{W per ESC}
$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each FET conducts about one third of the electrical period. An example electrical frequency is 30,000 rpm with 7 pole pairs, giving 3.5 kHz. The die thermal time constant is on the order of milliseconds, so averaging over the electrical cycle is valid:

$$
P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{2 I^2 R_{ds}}{6} = 3.0\ \text{W}
$$

The exception is startup and stall. At very low eRPM a single FET can carry the full I²R = 9 W continuously. Desync and stall protection must cut current within about 100 ms.

### 1.4 PCB copper loss (frequently ignored)

Assume a path resistance from battery pad through the bus, FET, phase pad and back of about 0.4 mΩ:

$$
P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{path} \approx 6400 \times 0.4\text{ mΩ} = 2.6\ \text{W per ESC}
$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. The loss terms are:

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48k = **1.94 W** |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss · V · f_sw | 50 nC × 25.2 × 48k = **0.06 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw | **0.05 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_SD · I · 2 t_dt · f_sw | 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2 · Q_g · V_gs · f_sw | **0.05 W** (dissipated in driver and R_g) |
| **Switching total** | | **≈ 2.7 W per ESC** |

The 48 kHz trade-off is visible in the numbers: overlap loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it falls to about 1.35 W total, but motor current ripple rises. The ripple is:

$$
\Delta I \approx \frac{V(1-D)D}{L\, f_{sw}}
$$

### 2.6 Transient thermal model (lumped RC)

**Heat capacity of a 20×20 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz board:**

| Component | Volume / mass | Heat capacity |
|---|---|---|
| Copper | 8 × 0.07 mm × 400 mm² = 224 mm³ → 2.0 g × 0.385 J/g·K | 0.77 J/K |
| FR-4 | 640 mm³ → 1.18 g × 1.1 J/g·K | 1.30 J/K |
| FETs, caps, solder | — | ≈ 0.3 J/K |
| **C_th total** | | **≈ 2.4 J/K** |

**Thermal resistance:** board to ambient in prop wash, R_th,ba ≈ 4 K/W (measure this; it varies roughly 2–8 K/W with airflow).

The board temperature follows:

$$
T_{board}(t) = T_a + P\,R_{th}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right), \qquad \tau = R_{th} C_{th} \approx 9.6\ \text{s}
$$

The steady-state rise (P·R_th) is 93 W × 4 K/W = 372 K. That is not survivable, so the design is burst-limited. Time to go from 35 °C to 100 °C:

$$
t = -\tau \ln\!\left(1 - \frac{\Delta T}{P R_{th}}\right)
$$

| Scenario | P | Time to ΔT = 65 K |
|---|---|---|
| All 4 ESCs at 80 A | 93 W | **≈ 1.85 s** |
| 1 ESC at 80 A | 23 W | **≈ 9.4 s** |
| 4 ESCs at 35 A (continuous check) | ≈ 19 W | Steady state ≈ 76 K rise → **marginal** |

**Junction above board:** take R_th,j-pcb ≈ 1.5 K/W for a DFN3333 on a via array. With P_FET ≈ 3.5 W, the junction sits about 5 K above the board. The junction limit is not the binding constraint; board heat capacity is.

**Firmware implications:**
- Enforce an I²t burst limit of about 80 A for no more than 1.5 s with all four channels loaded.
- Use the ESC's on-die temperature sensor plus the NTC described in §4. Derate starting at 90 °C.
