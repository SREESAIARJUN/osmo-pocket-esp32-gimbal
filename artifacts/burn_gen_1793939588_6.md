# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC: 80 A Burst Loss Model, 48 kHz Switching, and ArduPilot BDShot600 Timer/DMA Plan

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Caveats

I don't have access to Job #72's BOM or prior design files. Everything below is a **parametric model** with representative component values. Before release, substitute your actual MOSFET datasheet parameters and validate the result against a thermal-camera burst test.

**Architecture (the standard split):**
- **Flight controller.** STM32H743 running ArduPilot. It generates DShot600 and receives bidirectional eRPM telemetry.
- **4-in-1 ESC.** Four ESC MCUs (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32) drive the bridges. The H743 does **not** commutate the motors. If Job #72 was intended as a single-MCU FC that also commutates four motors, that is a different and much harder design, and the timer plan in §4 would not apply.

**Electrical assumptions:**

| Parameter | Value | Note |

**Feasibility warning.** 80 A per motor on a "nano" (20×20 or 30×30) 4-in-1 board is **burst-only**. The math below shows it is thermally limited to a few seconds.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Per-switch duty in 6-step commutation

In each 60° sector, one phase is high-side PWM (duty D), one phase is low-side ON, and one phase floats. Averaged over a full electrical cycle, each phase leg sees:

- High-side FET: conducts for fraction **D/3**.
- Low-side FET: conducts for **1/3** (its own low-side-ON interval) plus **(1−D)/3** (synchronous-rectification freewheel).

Per-leg conduction loss:

$$P_{cond,leg} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\left[\frac{D}{3} + \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1-D}{3}\right] = \frac{2}{3}I^2R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Per ESC (three legs). This equals two channels conducting at every instant, and is independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2I^2R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Note that the low side carries the most heat: **(2−D)/3** versus **D/3** for the high side. Place the best thermal path under the low-side FETs.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right]$$

### 1.3 Results at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond per ESC | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W | 51.2 W |
| 110 °C | 1.38 mΩ | 17.7 W | 70.7 W |
| 150 °C | 1.56 mΩ | 20.0 W | 80.0 W |

### 1.4 Copper and shunt losses (often forgotten)

- **Phase traces.** 2 oz copper is about 0.25 mΩ/sq. About 4 squares per phase gives ~1 mΩ per path, and two paths conduct at once:
  - 2 × 6400 × 0.001 ≈ **12.8 W per ESC**.
  - This is comparable to the FET loss. Use 3–4 oz copper, stitched pours, and solder-loaded or bus-bar phase pads.
- **Current shunt.** A 0.25 mΩ shunt carries the battery current I_bat = Σ(I·D):
  - At 4×80 A and D = 0.9, I_bat ≈ 288 A.
  - Loss is about **20.7 W**, and only during the burst.
  - Consider Hall-effect sensing (e.g., ACS7xx) or a 0.1 mΩ shunt with a higher-gain amplifier.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches; its low-side FET acts as the synchronous rectifier. Per ESC:

$$P_{sw} = \underbrace{\tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f}_{\text{V–I overlap}} + \underbrace{Q_{rr}Vf}_{\text{recovery}} + \underbrace{\tfrac12 Q_{oss}Vf}_{\text{C}_{oss}} + \underbrace{2V_f I t_{dt} f}_{\text{dead time}} + \underbrace{2Q_gV_{GS}f}_{\text{gate}}$$

| Term | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | 0.5 · 25.2 · 80 · 30 ns · 48 k | 1.45 W |
| Q_rr | 40 nC · 25.2 · 48 k | 0.05 W |
| Q_oss | 0.5 · 50 nC · 25.2 · 48 k | 0.03 W |
| Dead time | 2 · 0.8 · 80 · 100 ns · 48 k | 0.61 W |
| Gate (driver side) | 2 · 60 nC · 10 V · 48 k | 0.06 W |
| **Total per ESC** | | **≈ 2.2 W** |
| **Total ×4** | | **≈ 8.8 W** |

### 2.1 Edge-time check

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{drive}} = \frac{12\text{ nC}}{1.5\text{ A}} \approx 8\text{ ns per Miller plateau}$$

Adding the Q_gs portion gives about 15 ns per edge, which is consistent with the assumed 30 ns total.

### 2.2 Design levers

- Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it is about 1.1 W per ESC, but you get more motor ripple current and audible noise.
- Dead-time loss is significant. Reduce t_dt to 50–70 ns if the driver propagation-delay matching allows it.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Total burst dissipation

Worst case, T_j ≈ 110 °C, all four motors at 80 A:

| Source | Loss |
|---|---|
| FET conduction | 70.7 W |
| Switching | 8.8 W |
| Copper | ~51 W (2 oz); ~26 W (4 oz) |
| Shunt | ~20 W (0.25 mΩ) |
| **Total** | **≈ 125–150 W** (4 oz copper / Hall sensing ≈ 105 W) |

### 3.2 Two-level transient model

**Level 1: Junction to board (fast, Foster network).**

$$Z_{th,jb}(t) = \sum_i R_i\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau_i}\right)$$

Typical PDFN 3.3×3.3 values, R_th,jc ≈ 1.5 K/W:

| Stage | R_i | τ_i |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.2 K/W | 0.1 ms |
| 2 | 0.6 K/W | 2 ms |
| 3 | 0.7 K/W | 20 ms |

The worst-case low-side FET dissipates I²R·(2−D)/3 plus a share of switching loss: about 6400 · 1.38 mΩ · 0.37 ≈ 3.3 W plus ~0.5 W ≈ **3.8 W**. This gives ΔT_jb ≈ 3.8 × 1.5 ≈ **6 K**. The junction-to-board rise is not the bottleneck.

**Level 2: Board lumped capacitance (slow).**

$$C_{board} = \sum m_ic_i \approx 7\text{ g FR4} \cdot 1.1 + 3\text{ g Cu} \cdot 0.385 + \text{FETs/solder} \approx 9.5\text{ J/K (30×30)}$$

$$T_{board}(t) = T_a + P R_{ba}\left(1 - e^{-t/(R_{ba}C)}\right)$$

Take R_ba ≈ 4 K/W in prop wash, so τ ≈ 38 s.

For t much shorter than τ, the rise is approximately ΔT ≈ P·t/C. At P = 105 W (optimized board):

| Burst | ΔT_board | T_j (T_a = 35 °C) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 s | 11 K | ~52 °C |
| 3 s | 32 K | ~73 °C |
| 5 s | 52 K | ~93 °C |
| 8 s | 79 K | ~120 °C (stop here) |
