## 0. Architectural Clarifications

Three issues need resolving before any math, because they change the model.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** If the H743 drove the gate drivers directly, there would be no DShot link to discuss. The correct partition is:

| Layer | Device | Firmware | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIH6 (400 MHz) | ArduPilot | Attitude control, DShot600 master, BDShot RPM telemetry consumer |
| ESC (×4) | AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071 (one per motor) | AM32 | 6-step commutation, 48 kHz complementary PWM, BDShot responder |

**2. "80 A" means phase current per motor, as a burst.** On a 20×20 mm or even 30.5×30.5 mm board, 80 A cannot be sustained. Section 3 shows that 80 A is thermally a burst of roughly 1–2 s at most. The realistic continuous rating is about 25–35 A per motor, depending on airflow. Spec-sheet "80 A burst" figures on nano boards are generally marketing numbers.

**3. Battery current is a separate problem.** With four motors at 80 A phase current and duty D ≈ 0.85, the battery current is approximately 4 × 80 × 0.85 ≈ 272 A. No nano-format power entry (XT30/XT60, AWG14 pads) supports that. The design target in this document is therefore:

- **Per-motor phase current:** 80 A peak burst for ≤ 1 s, 35 A continuous at 15 m/s airflow.
- **Pack:** 6S (25.2 V max).
- **Aggregate battery current:** 160 A burst, 100 A continuous.

**Board format assumed:** 30.5×30.5 mm ESC (M3 mounting), 8-layer, 2 oz on all layers, stacked under a 20×20 or 30.5 mm H743 FC.

$$R_{DS}(T_j)=R_{DS,25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big]$$
