## 0. Architecture Decisions and Feasibility Flags

Three issues must be resolved before the math is meaningful.

**1. The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.**
ArduPilot is a flight-control stack and does not run sensorless BLDC commutation. The correct architecture is:
- **H743** runs ArduPilot and outputs bi-directional DShot600.
- **4× ESC MCUs** (e.g., AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32-class firmware) each drive a 3-phase gate driver and 6 MOSFETs.

The derivations below assume this split.

**2. "80 A burst" is a per-channel transient rating, not a sustained one.**
On a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm board, the thermal model in §3 shows:
- Roughly **45 A continuous** per channel with prop-wash airflow.
- Roughly **2 s at 80 A** before the board reaches its thermal limit.

**3. Battery-side current is the real system limit.**
Four channels at 80 A and near-full duty draw about 320 A from the pack. That exceeds the following by a wide margin:
- An XT60 connector (≈60 A continuous).
- Typical 12–14 AWG leads.
- Any realistic 20×20 bus-copper cross-section.

ArduPilot battery/current limiting (`BATT_*`, `MOT_BAT_CURR_MAX`) **must** be configured. The 80 A figure is valid only as an asymmetric single-motor or short-yaw-transient rating.
