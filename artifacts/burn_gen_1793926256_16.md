# Job #5: STM32H743 Nano AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC

**Engineering Basis Document: Thermal, Switching, and Bi-directional DShot Architecture**

> **Feasibility note:** The headline requirement (80 A burst × 4 motors on a 20×20 mm "nano" board at 48 kHz) is far beyond what the physics allows for anything but very short bursts. The model below quantifies that limit rather than hiding it. Section 4 gives the maximum burst duration. Section 8 lists the changes needed to make the design viable.

## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

| Item | Assumption |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C, R_ds = 1.05 × 1.45 ≈ **1.52 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Per-motor conduction loss

In 6-step drive with synchronous rectification, two legs carry the phase current at every instant. Exactly one FET conducts in each of those legs: either the high side (HS) or the low side (LS) of the PWM leg, plus the static FET of the opposite leg. So two R_ds are always in series:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j) \;+\; I^2 R_{Cu}$$

| Term | At 80 A, T_j = 125 °C |
|---|---|
| FETs: 6400 × 2 × 1.52 mΩ | **19.5 W** |
| Copper: 6400 × R_Cu, with R_Cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ (phase trace + motor pad, 2 oz × 4 layers) | **1.9 W** |
| **Total per motor** | **21.4 W** |

### 1.3 Distribution across the FETs

Each phase leg is active for 2/3 of an electrical cycle. Averaged over a revolution, the 19.5 W spreads across 6 FETs, giving **≈3.25 W average per FET**.

The worst-case instantaneous load is higher. At low eRPM, one FET can sit in the static (100 % on) position for a long time. For burst analysis, assume a single FET can see I²R_ds = 6400 × 1.52 mΩ = **9.7 W**.

### 1.4 Convergence of the loss and temperature loop

Loss depends on temperature, and temperature depends on loss. Solve iteratively:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_a + P\big(T_j^{(k)}\big)\cdot R_{th,ja}$$

The loop converges only if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\,R_{th,ja} = I^2\,2R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_{th,ja} < 1$$

At 80 A, I²·R₂₅·α = 6400 × 1.05e-3 × 0.0045 ≈ 0.030 W/K per FET. Thermal runaway therefore occurs if R_th,ja exceeds **≈33 K/W** for a statically conducting FET. A nano board without a heatsink sits close to that limit. This is a hard design constraint.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg of each motor switches. Hard switching occurs on the HS FET, and the LS FET commutates through its body diode.

### 2.1 Transition times

Using the Miller-plateau charge and the driver current:

$$t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{G,src}},\quad I_{G,src} = \frac{V_{DRV} - V_{plateau}}{R_{G,int}+R_{G,ext}+R_{drv}} = \frac{10 - 3.2}{1 + 4.7 + 2.5} \approx 0.83\ \text{A}$$

This gives t_r ≈ 12 nC / 0.83 A ≈ 14.5 ns. Adding package and loop-inductance slowing, the design value is **t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 20 ns**.

### 2.2 Loss components per motor (V = 25.2 V, I = 80 A)

| Mechanism | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f = ½·25.2·80·40e-9·48e3 | **1.94 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f = 30e-9·25.2·48e3 | 0.036 W |
| C_oss | ½·C_oss·V²·f (×2 FETs) = 1.5e-9·635·48e3 | 0.046 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2t_d·f = 0.8·80·200e-9·48e3 | **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver and R_G) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f = 2·40e-9·10·48e3 | 0.038 W |
| **Total switching** | | **≈2.67 W** |

### 2.3 Scaling insight

Switching loss is proportional to f_sw. Dropping from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves about 1.3 W per motor (about 5 W per board).

Conduction loss dominates by about 7:1. The higher 48 kHz frequency improves current ripple and acoustics for low-inductance motors, but it is not the thermal problem.
