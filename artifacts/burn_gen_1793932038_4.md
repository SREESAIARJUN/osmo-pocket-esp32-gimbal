## 1. Conduction Loss Model

In 6-step operation, two phases carry current at any instant. The current path is always two series FETs:
- **Switched leg:** high-side during D, low-side (synchronous rectification) during 1−D, i.e., one FET at a time.
- **Grounded leg:** low-side, on continuously.

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This is duty-independent, a useful property of synchronous trapezoidal drive. Dead-time diode conduction is handled separately in Section 2.

**Temperature coupling.** R_ds depends on T_j, so solve iteratively. Converged design point is T_j ≈ 110 °C:

$$R_{ds}(110^\circ C) = 1.4\,\text{mΩ} \times [1 + 0.0055(85)] = 2.05\,\text{mΩ}$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.05\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{26.2\ W\ per\ channel}$$

**PCB copper loss.** Assume a 30×30 mm board with 2 oz copper in 4 parallel planes and about 15 mm effective path per phase:

$$R_{Cu} \approx \frac{\rho L}{A} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot 0.015}{(4\cdot 70\,\mu m)\cdot 4\,mm} \approx 0.23\ \text{mΩ (hot: } \approx 0.3\ \text{mΩ)}$$

$$P_{Cu} \approx 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.3\,\text{mΩ} \approx \mathbf{3.8\ W\ per\ channel}$$

Copper loss is non-negligible at 80 A. Use full-plane pours and stitched vias.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the switched leg's HS/LS pair commutates. Each PWM period contains one turn-on and one turn-off of hard-switched current.

**(a) V–I overlap loss**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 30\,\text{ns} \cdot 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

Transition time comes from the Miller plateau, where V_pl ≈ 4 V:

$$t \approx \frac{Q_{gd} \cdot R_g}{V_{drv} - V_{pl}} = \frac{8\,\text{nC}}{1.5\,\text{A}} \approx 5{-}15\,\text{ns}$$

30 ns total is conservative and includes layout inductance.

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction**

$$P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 400\,\text{ns} \cdot 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

This term is comparable to the V–I overlap loss. Cutting dead time from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per channel. Validate shoot-through margin on a scope first.

**(c) Reverse recovery**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\,\text{nC} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\,\text{kHz} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

**(d) Output capacitance**

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.03\ \text{W}$$

**(e) Gate drive** (dissipated in the driver and R_g, not the FET)

$$P_g = 2 \cdot Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 50\,\text{nC} \cdot 10 \cdot 48\,\text{kHz} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### Loss Budget per Channel @ 80 A

| Loss term | W |
|---|---|
| FET conduction (2 FETs) | 26.2 |
| V–I switching | 1.45 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.08 |
| PCB copper | 3.8 |
| **Total per channel** | **≈ 32.8 W** |
| **All 4 channels at burst** | **≈ 131 W** |

Efficiency is about 1 − 32.8 / (25.2 × 80 × D_eff). At full throttle this is ~98% electrically, but 131 W is unsustainable for a nano board.

## 3. Thermal Model (Transient: 80 A Is a Burst, Not a Rating)

### 3.1 Two-node RC network

```
P_FET ──► [T_j] ──R_θJB──► [T_board, C_b] ──R_θBA──► T_amb
                 1.5 K/W                     ~8 K/W (still air) / ~3 K/W (prop wash)
```

### 3.2 Board thermal capacitance

Assume ~7 g of populated board: ~3 g Cu and ~4 g FR4/components.

$$C_b = 3(0.385) + 4(1.1) \approx 5.6\ \text{J/K} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \approx 1.4\ \text{J/K per channel quadrant}$$

### 3.3 Junction offset

The hottest FET (grounded low-side, on 100% of the time) dissipates I²R ≈ 13.1 W plus a share of switching loss:

$$\Delta T_{jb} \approx 14\,\text{W} \times 1.5\,\text{K/W} \approx 21\ \text{K}$$
