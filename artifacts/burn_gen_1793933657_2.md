# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Engineering Design Package (Job #57)

## 0. Architecture Decisions and Scope Corrections

Three constraints shape everything that follows.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In any ArduPilot-compatible design, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and emits DShot. Each motor (or the 4-in-1 as a group) needs a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM and does sensorless commutation. This design uses **4× STM32G071** ESC MCUs, one per motor, because that gives independent fault containment.
2. **"80A" and "nano" conflict physically.** At 80 A per motor, total power-stage dissipation is about 130 W (Section 3). A 20×20 mm board cannot carry that current through copper or sink that heat, even for a short burst. The power stage is therefore specified on a **30.5×30.5 mm mounting pattern with a ~42×42 mm outline**. The H743 FC section can still be stacked or co-located in nano form. Section 4.5 quantifies why 20×20 fails.
3. **"80A burst" is defined here as 80 A per motor for ≤ 5 s.** The continuous rating is derived separately and is much lower.

### Design Basis

| Parameter | Value |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Path

In 6-step trapezoidal drive, one phase is PWM'd high, one is held low, and one floats.

- **ON interval (duty D):** current flows through HS(phase A) and LS(phase B).
- **OFF interval with synchronous rectification:** current flows through LS(phase A) and LS(phase B).

At every instant, **exactly two MOSFETs carry the full phase current**. Without synchronous rectification, the body diode would conduct during the off-time at ~0.8 V × 80 A = 64 W per motor. That is unacceptable, so complementary PWM is mandatory.

### 1.2 Temperature-Dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j)=R_{DS,25}\,\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha\approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.0 mΩ × 1.55 = **1.55 mΩ**.

Add package and spreading parasitics of PCB copper and solder per FET path, about 0.25 mΩ, giving **R_eff ≈ 1.8 mΩ**.

### 1.3 RMS Current Including Ripple

Phase current is approximately DC within a commutation step, with triangular PWM ripple:

$$I_{rms}=\sqrt{I_{dc}^2+\frac{\Delta I^2}{12}}$$

Ripple: ΔI = V·D(1−D)/(L·f_sw). Worst case is D = 0.5, with L_phase ≈ 10 µH (typical 2207 to 2812 class motor):

$$\Delta I=\frac{25.2\cdot0.25}{10\times10^{-6}\cdot48\,000}=13.1\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{rms}=\sqrt{80^2+13.1^2/12}=80.09\ \text{A}$$

The ripple contribution is negligible (<0.2 %).

### 1.4 Conduction Loss per Motor

$$P_{cond}=I_{rms}^2\cdot 2R_{eff}=80.09^2\times 2\times 1.8\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{23.1\ W}$$

Averaged over a full electrical revolution, each of the 6 FETs conducts:

- **HS:** 1/3 of the period × D
- **LS:** (1/3 of the period × (1−D) as sync rectifier) + (1/3 of the period as the held-low leg)

LS FETs are therefore the hottest.

$$P_{LS}=\tfrac{1}{3}I^2R(2-D),\qquad P_{HS}=\tfrac{1}{3}I^2R\,D$$

At D = 0.9 (high throttle burst), with I²R = 11.55 W:

| FET | Formula | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| Each LS | 11.55 × 1.1 / 3 | **4.23 W** |
| Each HS | 11.55 × 0.9 / 3 | **3.47 W** |

Check: 3 × 4.23 + 3 × 3.47 = 23.1 W ✓

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Hard-Switching Transition (HS FET of the PWM'd Phase)

Gate current during the Miller plateau:

- Turn-on: I_g,on = (V_drv − V_plateau)/(R_g,ext + R_g,int) = (10 − 3)/(4.7 + 1.2) = 1.19 A
- Turn-off: I_g,off = V_plateau/(R_g,off + R_g,int) = 3/(2.2 + 1.2) = 0.88 A

The turn-off path uses a diode-bypassed 2.2 Ω resistor.

Transition times:

- t_r ≈ Q_gd/I_g,on = 9 nC/1.19 A = **7.6 ns**, plus the Q_gs2 portion ≈ 3 ns → **~11 ns**
- t_f ≈ 9 nC/0.88 A = **10.2 ns**, plus ≈ 3 ns → **~13 ns**

Overlap loss:

$$P_{sw,VI}=\tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}=\tfrac12(25.2)(80)(24\times10^{-9})(48\,000)=\mathbf{1.16\ W}$$

### 2.2 Output Capacitance Loss

$$P_{oss}=\tfrac12 Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}\cdot 2 = 35\text{nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.04\ W}$$

### 2.3 Body-Diode Reverse Recovery

The LS body diode conducts during dead time, then is reverse-recovered by HS turn-on:

$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}=40\text{nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

The recovery charge also adds overlap energy to the HS FET. Allow about +0.15 W.

### 2.4 Dead-Time Diode Conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM cycle:

$$P_{dt}=V_F I\,(2t_{dt})f_{sw}=0.8\times80\times200\text{ns}\times48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate Drive Loss

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET die. Only the HS/LS pair actively switching counts:

$$P_g=2\,Q_gV_{drv}f_{sw}=2\times50\text{nC}\times10\times48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.048\ W}$$
