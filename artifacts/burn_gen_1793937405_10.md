> **Engineering warning:** 4 × 80 A on a 25×25 mm ESC is at the physical edge of what is achievable. The math below shows that roughly 85 W of simultaneous dissipation makes this a **thermal-capacitance-limited burst rating**, not a steady-state rating. Market it, and set firmware current limits, accordingly.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

With 6-step synchronous PWM, phase current at every instant flows through exactly **one high-side-position device and one low-side-position device in series**. During the PWM off-time, the complementary FET on the PWM leg conducts in place of the high-side FET. Therefore, independent of duty cycle D:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot \left[\,2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + R_{Cu} + R_{shunt,eff}\,\right]
$$

During the commutation plateau of a trapezoidal drive, I_ph,rms ≈ I_ph,peak = 80 A. This is conservative, because commutation dips reduce the true RMS slightly.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence of R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\left(\frac{T_j + 273}{298}\right)^{\kappa}, \qquad \kappa \approx 2.0\text{–}2.4 \;\text{(use datasheet normalized curve)}
$$

With κ = 2.3:

| T_j | Multiplier | R_DS(on) |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 | 0.90 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 1.68 | 1.51 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 1.94 | 1.74 mΩ |

A simpler linear form, R(T) = R₂₅[1 + α(T − 25)] with α ≈ 0.0075 /K, gives 1.58 mΩ at 125 °C. The power-law form above is more conservative.

### 1.3 Per-motor conduction loss at 80 A

Parasitic estimates **[A]**:
- PCB copper plus solder, per conduction path: R_Cu ≈ 0.30 mΩ (2 oz, 8-layer, short pours).
- Shunt (Section 3.3): 0.25 mΩ low-side DC shunt. It carries phase current only during the high-side on-time, so the effective value is R_sh,eff ≈ D · 0.25 mΩ. At D ≈ 0.75, that is ≈ 0.19 mΩ.
