## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature‑dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds25}\left(\frac{T_j+273}{298}\right)^{2.3}$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_ds ≈ 0.9 mΩ × 1.67 = **1.50 mΩ**. The solve in §3.4 is iterative because R_ds depends on T_j.

### 1.2 Six‑step current sharing

During each 60° sector, two legs conduct:

- **PWM leg, high‑side FET:** on for fraction D, carrying I.
- **PWM leg, low‑side FET (synchronous rectification):** on for (1 − D − 2t_dead·f_sw), carrying I.
- **Return leg, low‑side FET:** on 100 %, carrying I.

Total conduction loss per motor (dead‑time correction is negligible here):

$$P_{cond,motor}=I^2R_{ds}\,[D+(1-D)+1]=2I^2R_{ds}$$

This is **independent of duty cycle**, a useful property of synchronous six‑step. Each of the 6 FETs takes every role over an electrical cycle, so the average per FET is:

$$P_{cond,FET,avg}=\tfrac{1}{3}I^2R_{ds}$$

The instantaneous peak in a single FET (return‑leg low side) is I²R_ds.

**Ripple correction.** Phase‑current ripple adds a factor (1 + r²/12), where r = ΔI/I. Motor inductance L_ph is roughly 5–15 µH for 2207‑class motors. With ΔI = V_bus·D(1−D)/(L·f_sw) ≈ 25.2·0.25/(10 µH·48 kHz) ≈ 13 A, r ≈ 0.16. The correction is +0.2 %, which is negligible.
