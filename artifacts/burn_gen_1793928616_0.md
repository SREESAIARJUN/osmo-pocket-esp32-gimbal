## 0. Architecture Corrections and Design-Basis Assumptions

These points determine whether the numbers below are meaningful.

1. **The H743 is not the ESC MCU.** No 4-in-1 ESC commutates motors from an H743. The realistic topology has two parts:
   - **FC board:** STM32H743VIT6 running ArduPilot. It generates bidirectional DShot600 and decodes eRPM.
   - **ESC board:** four independent ESC MCUs (AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071GBU6) running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, each driving 3 half-bridges.

   The 48 kHz PWM in this document is generated by the ESC MCUs, not the H743.

2. **"Nano" vs 80 A burst.** On a 20×20 mm board, 80 A per channel (320 A aggregate, about 8 kW at 6S) is only possible as a short **adiabatic burst** of a few seconds. Section 3 quantifies this. I model a 20×20 board and give a 30.5×30.5 comparison.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Per-FET conduction in 6-step complementary PWM

Each phase spends 120° high, 120° low and 120° floating per electrical cycle. When a phase is "high," its high-side (HS) switch is on for duty D and its low-side (LS) switch conducts the freewheel current for (1−D). When the phase is "low," its LS switch is on continuously.

$$P_{HS} = I_{ph}^2 R_{ds,hot}\cdot\frac{D}{3}$$

$$P_{LS} = I_{ph}^2 R_{ds,hot}\cdot\frac{1 + (1-D)}{3} = I_{ph}^2 R_{ds,hot}\cdot\frac{2-D}{3}$$

The sum per leg is $\tfrac{2}{3}I^2R$. Over three legs, the bridge loss is:

$$\boxed{P_{cond,bridge} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds,hot}}$$

This is independent of D, which matches intuition: two switches are always in the current path.

**Hot resistance**, with self-consistent iteration on T_j:

$$R_{ds,hot} = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0067\ /K \Rightarrow k_T(100^\circ C)\approx 1.5$$

$$R_{ds,hot} = 1.2\ \text{m}\Omega \times 1.5 = 1.8\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Numerical results at 80 A

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| I²R per conducting switch | 6400 × 1.8 m = 11.52 W (instantaneous) |
| P_HS at D = 0.95 | 11.52 × 0.317 = 3.65 W |
| P_LS at D = 0.95 | 11.52 × 0.350 = 4.03 W (worst FET) |
| **P_cond per channel** | **23.0 W** |
| **P_cond, 4 channels** | **92.2 W** |

### 1.3 Copper and shunt conduction

- **Phase/bus copper:** for a 2 oz (70 µm) inner+outer layer stack, R_□ ≈ 0.25 mΩ/□ per layer. A 4-layer parallel path of about 3 squares gives ≈ 0.19 mΩ → 80² × 0.19 m ≈ **1.2 W per channel**.
- **Current shunt:** a 0.25 mΩ shunt (shared on the battery input) carries the full 320 A burst, so P = 320² × 0.25 m = **25.6 W**.
  - **Recommendation:** use a Hall sensor (e.g., ACS781/TMCS1100-class is insufficient at 320 A; use an INA-based 0.1 mΩ shunt across 4 parallel 2512 resistors), or a per-channel 0.5 mΩ shunt in each low-side return. The latter carries the average battery current only.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one HS switch per channel PWMs at any instant. Its complementary LS switch handles dead-time diode conduction and reverse recovery.

### 2.1 Gate current and transition times

$$I_{G,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_G} = \frac{10-3}{10}=0.70\ \text{A},\qquad I_{G,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_G}=0.30\ \text{A}$$

$$t_r = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{G,on}} = \frac{12\ \text{nC}}{0.70} = 17.1\ \text{ns},\qquad t_f = \frac{12\ \text{nC}}{0.30}=40\ \text{ns}$$

Driver sink/source limits apply. For example, the FD6288Q is about 1.5 A/1.8 A and does not limit here. The EG3112 is not limiting either.

**V–I overlap (hard switching, inductive load):**
