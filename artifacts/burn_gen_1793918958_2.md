## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 is the **flight controller** MCU. It does not commutate motors directly. Each ESC channel uses its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, for example the AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 talks to each ESC MCU over **bidirectional DShot600**, one wire per motor. This partitioning is standard practice. The 48 kHz switching analysis therefore applies to the ESC MCU's PWM, and the DShot/DMA analysis applies to the H743.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model at 80 A Burst

### 1.1 Commutation topology

The ESC uses six-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM). At any instant two phases carry the current I:

- During PWM **on-time** (duty D): high-side FET of phase A and low-side FET of phase B conduct.
- During PWM **off-time** (1−D): low-side FET of phase A (synchronous) and low-side FET of phase B conduct.

In both intervals the current flows through exactly **two FET channels in series**. Conduction loss is therefore independent of D, apart from the dead-time intervals handled in §2.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

R_DS(on) rises with junction temperature:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

Per-channel conduction loss, including the copper path resistance R_cu:

$$P_{cond}(T_j) = I^2 \left[ 2R_{DS}(T_j) + R_{cu} \right]$$

Take R_cu ≈ 0.4 mΩ. This covers the phase pad, two vias arrays, and the bus plane share for a 2 oz nano layout. Measure it on the first article with a 4-wire milliohm meter.

### 1.3 Self-consistent junction temperature

Junction temperature depends on loss, and loss depends on junction temperature. The two must be solved together.

Per-FET average loss: each FET conducts roughly 1/3 of the electrical period. High-side FETs conduct 1/3 × D. Low-side FETs conduct 1/3 plus the synchronous-rectification share. At high throttle (D → 1), the per-FET worst case is approximately:

$$P_{FET} \approx \frac{I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)}{3} \cdot k_{imb}, \qquad k_{imb} \approx 1.2 \text{ (low-side imbalance at D < 1)}$$

Junction temperature:

$$T_j = T_{board} + P_{FET}\cdot R_{th,j\text{-}pcb}$$

Assume R_th,j-pcb ≈ 1.5 K/W (clip package soldered to a 2 oz plane with a thermal via array).

**Fixed-point iteration** at I = 80 A, T_board = 90 °C (a realistic mid-burst value):

| Iteration | T_j (°C) | R_DS (mΩ) | P_FET (W) | New T_j (°C) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 90 | 1.163 | 2.98 | 94.5 |
| 1 | 94.5 | 1.181 | 3.02 | 94.5 |

The iteration converges at **T_j ≈ 95 °C, R_DS ≈ 1.18 mΩ**.

Per-channel conduction loss:

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \times (2 \times 1.18 + 0.4)\ \text{m}\Omega = 6400 \times 2.76\ \text{m}\Omega \approx \mathbf{17.7\ W}$$

Of this, about 15.1 W is in silicon and 2.6 W is in copper.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switching phase leg switches at f_sw. The commutation-rate switching, at a few kHz electrical, is negligible by comparison. Inductive hard switching is assumed.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 33\text{ns} \times 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.60\ W}$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance loss

The Coss energy of the switching node is dissipated in the turning-on FET:

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 45\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.027\ W}$$

Because the transition is hard-switched, use the full Q_oss·V for a conservative estimate. That gives 0.054 W, which is still small.

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

The synchronous FET body diode conducts during dead time and then recovers when the high side turns on:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

Q_rr scales with di/dt and current. At 80 A, derate by about 2×, giving roughly **0.10 W**.

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM period:

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I \,(2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

> **Design lever:** this loss is comparable to the V–I overlap loss. Cutting dead time to 100 ns halves it. That is only safe if the driver's propagation-delay mismatch plus the FET t_d(off) leaves margin against shoot-through. Verify on the bench with a current probe on the bus capacitor.

### 2.5 Gate drive loss

Two FETs per leg are switched each period. Most of this power dissipates in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET.

$$P_{gate} = 2 Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 55\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.053\ W}$$

For all 4 channels this totals 0.21 W, drawn from the gate-drive regulator. Size that regulator accordingly.

### 2.6 Per-channel switching summary at 80 A

| Term | Power (W) |
|---|---|
| V–I overlap | 1.60 |
| Coss | 0.05 |
| Reverse recovery (derated) | 0.10 |
| Dead-time diode conduction | 1.23 |
| Gate (driver side) | 0.05 |
| **Total switching** | **≈ 3.0** |
