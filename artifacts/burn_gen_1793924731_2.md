# STM32H743 Flight Controller + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Reference Architecture (Job #10)

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

The "Job #10" requirements package was not provided, so this design rests on the assumptions below. Change any of them and the equations in §1–§3 recompute directly.

| Parameter | Assumed value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V max | 8S needs 60 V MOSFETs; R_ds(on) roughly doubles |
| "80A burst" | Per-channel **phase** current, ≤10 s | At high duty, phase current ≈ battery current |
| Architecture | H743 flight controller running ArduPilot → DShot600 bidir → 4-in-1 ESC running AM32/BLHeli_32 | The H743 does **not** drive the gates. Each ESC channel has its own MCU (e.g., AT32F421). |
| Commutation | Trapezoidal 6-step, complementary (synchronous) PWM | PWM frequency f_sw = 48 kHz |
| MOSFET | 40 V, R_ds(on) = 1.0 mΩ at 25 °C, Vgs = 10 V, DFN5×6 class | Q_g ≈ 60 nC, Q_rr ≈ 40 nC, Q_oss ≈ 50 nC |
| Form factor | 30.5×30.5 mm stack, 8-layer, 2 oz copper | A 20×20 "nano" board is analyzed in §2.4 |

> **Engineering flag up front:** 80A per channel on a nano-format board is **not** a steady-state rating under any realistic cooling. The model below shows it is a thermal-capacitance-limited burst of roughly 3–6 s. The math below quantifies this.

## 1. Loss Model (Per ESC Channel)

### 1.1 Conduction Losses

In 6-step commutation, two phase legs are active at any instant. With synchronous rectification, current always flows through exactly one MOSFET per active leg:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

Temperature dependence:

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0055/\text{K}$$

At T_j = 115 °C, this gives R ≈ 1.0 × 1.5 = **1.5 mΩ**.

$$P_{cond} = 2(80)^2(1.5\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

**Thermal runaway check.** Because R_ds(on) rises with temperature, loss and temperature feed back on each other. The loop is stable only if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\,R_{th} = 2I^2R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_{th,ja} < 1$$

With per-FET R_th ≈ 15 K/W effective, the loop gain is 3.2 W × 0.0055 × 15 ≈ 0.26. This is stable, but it amplifies the temperature rise by 1/(1−0.26) ≈ 1.35×.

**Per-FET share.** Each FET conducts during 1/3 of the electrical cycle, so P_cond,FET ≈ 19.2/6 = **3.2 W** on average. Instantaneous dissipation in the conducting FET is 9.6 W.

### 1.2 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-modulated leg switches. With V = 25.2 V, I = 80 A, and t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 15 ns (2 A driver, R_g ≈ 4.7 Ω, Q_gd ≈ 10 nC):

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | ½·25.2·80·30 ns·48 k = **1.45 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2t_dt·f_sw (V_f = 0.8 V, t_dt = 100 ns) | **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | **0.05 W** |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss·V·f_sw | **0.06 W** |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver and R_g) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw | **0.06 W** |
| **P_sw total** | | **≈ 2.23 W** |

Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz, P_sw ≈ 1.1 W. At 96 kHz, P_sw ≈ 4.5 W. The 48 kHz choice trades about 1 W per channel for lower current ripple and acoustic noise.

### 1.3 Parasitic Losses

These terms are commonly omitted but must be included.

**Current shunt.** Assume a shared 0.25 mΩ shunt carrying 4 × 80 = 320 A:

$$P = 320^2 \times 0.25\text{ mΩ} = \mathbf{25.6\ W}$$

> **Design action:** Use a 0.1 mΩ shunt (10.2 W) or Hall/phase sensing.

**Copper.** For bus and phase pours of about 0.3 mΩ per channel path, P ≈ 80² × 0.3 m ≈ **1.9 W per channel**.

### 1.4 Totals at Burst

| | Per channel | ×4 board |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching | 2.2 W | 8.9 W |
| Copper | 1.9 W | 7.7 W |
| Shunt (0.1 mΩ) | — | 10.2 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 23.3 W** | **≈ 103.6 W** |

Electrical output is about 4 × 80 A × 25 V ≈ 8 kW, so efficiency is about 98.7%. Even so, ~100 W dissipated in roughly 9 cm² is the binding constraint.
