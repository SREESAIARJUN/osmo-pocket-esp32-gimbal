# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Model and DShot Architecture

**Document:** Job #1, Rev A (preliminary design basis)

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and One Architectural Correction

The STM32H743 should not commutate the motors. In every production 4-in-1 ESC, each motor channel needs a dedicated small MCU, such as an STM32G071 or AT32F421 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 cannot service four sensorless 6-step commutation loops, with back-EMF zero-cross detection, while also running ArduPilot.

The architecture modelled here is therefore:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──bi-dir DShot600 x4──► 4 × ESC MCU (AM32, 48 kHz PWM) ──► 4 × 3-phase bridges (24 MOSFETs)
```

All component parameters below are **stated design assumptions**, not values from a particular datasheet. Replace them with your selected parts and re-run the equations. The model is parametric for that reason.

| Symbol | Meaning | Assumed value |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | Bus voltage (6S, full charge) | 25.2 V |
| I_burst | Burst phase current, per motor | 80 A |
| f_sw | ESC PWM frequency | 48 kHz |
| R_ds(on),25 | MOSFET on-resistance at 25 °C (40 V, 3.3×3.3 DFN class) | 1.6 mΩ |
| α | R_ds(on) temperature coefficient | ≈ 0.0045 /K (×1.5 at 125 °C) |
| t_r, t_f | Switching rise / fall times (approx. 2 A gate driver) | 10 ns each |
| Q_oss | Output charge | 30 nC |
| Q_rr | Body-diode reverse-recovery charge | 40 nC |
| Q_g | Total gate charge | 40 nC |
| t_dt | Dead time | 100 ns |
| V_SD | Body-diode forward voltage | 0.8 V |
| R_θJC | Junction-to-case thermal resistance | 1.5 K/W |
| V_GS | Gate drive voltage | 10 V |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Topology
The model assumes trapezoidal 6-step commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. At any instant two phase legs conduct:

- **Leg A (chopped leg):** the HS FET is on for duty D, and the LS FET carries freewheel current for 1−D.
- **Leg B (return leg):** the LS FET is on for the full 60° commutation sector.

### 1.2 Per-device conduction loss

$$P_{HS,A} = D\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j), \quad P_{LS,A} = (1-D)\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j), \quad P_{LS,B} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) \quad \text{(independent of } D\text{)}$$

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

### 1.3 Numerical result at 80 A

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per channel | P_cond, 4 channels |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.6 mΩ | 20.5 W | 82 W |
| 100 °C | 2.14 mΩ | 27.4 W | 110 W |
| 125 °C | 2.4 mΩ | **30.7 W** | **123 W** |

The **hottest single device** is LS_B, which conducts continuously within its sector:

- During the sector, I²R = 6400 × 2.4 mΩ = **15.4 W**.
- Averaged over one electrical revolution, each FET conducts for 1/3 of the time: P_avg ≈ 5.1 W.
- However, at low eRPM the sector time (ms scale) approaches the die thermal time constant (τ_die ≈ 1–5 ms). Design to the **15.4 W instantaneous** figure in that regime.

### 1.4 Copper and PCB losses (often forgotten)
At 80 A, every 0.1 mΩ of trace or via resistance adds 0.64 W.

- **Requirement:** total bus-plus-phase copper ≤ 0.3 mΩ per channel.
- **Recommended stack-up:** ≥ 2 oz outer layers, 4–6 parallel power layers, via arrays of at least 20 × 0.3 mm vias per FET pad.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the chopped leg switches, and in synchronous mode the HS FET takes the hard-switching loss. The loss components are:

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$
$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw}$$
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw}$$
$$P_{dt} = 2\, V_{SD}\, I\, t_{dt}\, f_{sw}$$
$$P_{g} = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \quad \text{(dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors)}$$

| Term | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| V·I overlap | 0.5·25.2·80·20 ns·48 k | 0.968 W |
| C_oss | 30 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.036 W |
| Q_rr | 40 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.048 W |
| Dead-time diode | 2·0.8·80·100 ns·48 k | 0.614 W |
| Gate (driver side) | 2·40 nC·10·48 k | 0.038 W |
| **Total per channel** | | **≈ 1.70 W** |

### 2.1 Per-channel total and the 48 kHz trade-off

$$P_{ch} = P_{cond} + P_{sw} \approx 30.7 + 1.7 = 32.4\ \text{W at } T_j = 125\,°\text{C}$$

At 80 A, conduction dominates (about 95%). Going from 24 kHz to 48 kHz roughly doubles switching loss, adding about 0.85 W per channel. This is acceptable given the benefits:

- lower current ripple, which reduces motor copper loss;
- quieter operation;
- better low-throttle resolution.

The **dead-time term is the largest switching contributor.** Reducing t_dt to 50 ns, if your driver and FET Q_g permit it without shoot-through, saves about 0.3 W per channel.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network (per hot FET)
```
T_j ─[R_θJC 1.5 K/W]─ T_case ─[R_θCB ≈ 2 K/W local spreading]─ T_board ─[R_θBA]─ T_amb
       C_die ~0.02 J/K           C_local ~0.3 J/K                C_board
```

The transient response is modelled with a Foster network:

$$Z_{th}(t) = \sum_i R_i\,(1 - e^{-t/\tau_i})$$

### 3.2 Junction rise above the board (hot device)

$$\Delta T_{j-b} = 15.4\ \text{W} \times (1.5 + 2) \approx 54\ \text{K (steady within sector)}$$

### 3.3 Board bulk rise during a burst (lumped capacitance)
Assume a 30×30 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz board, with FETs and solder included:

- Copper: ≈ 6 g × 0.385 J/g·K ≈ 2.3 J/K
- FR-4: ≈ 4 g × 0.9 J/g·K ≈ 3.6 J/K
- **Total C_board ≈ 6 J/K**

In the adiabatic case (valid for t ≪ τ_board-to-air, and ignoring airflow):

$$\Delta T_{board}(t) = \frac{P_{total}\,t}{C_{board}}$$
