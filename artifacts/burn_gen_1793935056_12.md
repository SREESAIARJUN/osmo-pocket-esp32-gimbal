## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Feasibility Issues

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller (FC) MCU. It does not commutate the motors. Each ESC channel (or a shared ESC MCU) runs its own firmware, here assumed to be AM32 on an STM32G071 or AT32F421. The H743 sends DShot600 commands to the ESC MCUs and receives eRPM telemetry back over the same wires (bidirectional DShot).

**Feasibility issues to review before layout:**

| Issue | Impact |
|---|---|
| 80 A per motor × 4 = **320 A battery burst** | Battery pads, XT60/XT90 connector and leads, and the shunt all become first-order design problems. |
| Nano form factor (assumed 30.5 mm mount, ~37×37 mm board) | Thermal mass supports roughly **5 s bursts** and **~30 A continuous** per motor (derived below). It cannot sustain 80 A continuously. |
| Bus capacitor ripple of ~80 A RMS worst case | No small-format capacitor bank handles this indefinitely. A large external low-ESR electrolytic at the battery pads is mandatory. |
| 6S (25.2 V max) with inductive spikes | Requires 40 V MOSFETs minimum. |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Commutation assumptions
The ESC uses six-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. In each electrical cycle, each phase spends:

- 120° as the **PWM leg**: the high-side FET (HS) conducts for duty D, the low-side FET (LS) freewheels for (1 − D);
- 120° as the **return leg**: LS is fully on;
- 120° **floating**.

### 1.2 RMS current per FET
For phase current I (flat-top approximation):

$$I_{HS,rms}^2 = I^2\frac{D}{3}, \qquad I_{LS,rms}^2 = I^2\left[\frac{1-D}{3} + \frac{1}{3}\right] = I^2\frac{2-D}{3}$$
