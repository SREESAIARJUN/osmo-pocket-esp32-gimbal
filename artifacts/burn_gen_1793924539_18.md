# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #34): Design Model

## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Feasibility Verdict

**Partitioning**
- The **STM32H743** runs ArduPilot and generates bidirectional DShot600.
- Each ESC channel has its own MCU, for example an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32. Each one drives its 3-phase bridge at 48 kHz PWM.
- The H743 does **not** switch the FETs directly, and no shipping ArduPilot build supports that.

**Feasibility flag (must go to the program owner)**

| Variable | Value |
|---|---|
| Burst current | 80 A per channel |
| Supply | 6S |
| Form factor | "Nano" (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm mounting) |
| Dissipation during burst | ~37 W per channel, ~150 W board total |

The lumped thermal model in §2 shows this is only survivable for roughly **1–2 s**, and only with paralleled FETs and a 30.5×30.5 mm or larger board. My recommendation is to re-spec as one of:
- 55–60 A continuous / 80 A for 1 s on a 30×30 board, or
- 80 A on a 35×35 board or larger.

**Data provenance.** All component values below are representative and must be replaced with your selected parts' datasheet values before tape-out. Each assumption is labeled **[A#]**.

### 1.2 Conduction Loss

In 6-step operation, two phase legs carry current at any instant:
- **PWM leg:** current flows through the high-side FET for duty D and the synchronous low-side for 1−D. Either way, one R_DS is in the path the whole period.
- **Fixed leg:** the low-side FET conducts continuously.

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot R_{DS,hot} \cdot (1 + 1) = 2 I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.4\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{30.7\ W}$$

With N FETs in parallel per position, R_DS scales as R/N:

| Configuration | Conduction loss |
|---|---|
| N = 1 (6 FETs per channel) | 30.7 W |
| N = 2 (12 FETs per channel) | 15.4 W |

Parasitic I²R losses:

| Element | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| Shunt | 80² × 0.5 mΩ | 3.2 W |
| Copper | 80² × 0.8 mΩ | 5.1 W |

### 1.3 Switching Loss at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-leg high side hard-switches. The synchronous low side turns on after dead time, so it is effectively zero-voltage switched.

**Hard-switching (V–I overlap)**
$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction**
$$P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I \cdot (2 t_{dt}) \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery**
$$P_{rr} = V \cdot Q_{rr} \cdot f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ W$$

**Output capacitance**
$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 V Q_{oss} f_{sw} = 0.018\ W$$

**Gate drive** (two FETs switching, dissipated in the driver and gate resistors)
$$P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ W$$

$$P_{sw,total} \approx \mathbf{3.3\ W}$$

### 1.4 Per-Channel and Board Totals at 80 A Burst

| Loss term | N=1 FET/position | N=2 FETs/position |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 30.7 W | 15.4 W |
| Switching + dead time + Q_rr + Q_oss + gate | 3.3 W | ~3.6 W (higher Q_g, Q_oss) |
| Shunt | 3.2 W | 3.2 W |
| Copper | 5.1 W | 5.1 W |
| **Per channel** | **42.3 W** | **27.3 W** |
| **Board (×4)** | **169 W** | **109 W** |
| Efficiency at 25.2 V × 80 A = 2016 W/ch | 97.9 % | 98.6 % |

**Trade-off note:** dropping f_sw to 24 kHz saves only ~1.6 W per channel. Conduction loss dominates, so the main levers are FET count and R_DS. Keep 48 kHz for acoustic and current-ripple benefits.
