# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #21): Architecture, Loss Model, DShot/DMA Allocation, Netlists

## 0. Architecture Decisions and Stated Assumptions

**The H743 does not commutate the motors.** A 48 kHz, 4-motor sensorless FOC/6-step workload with BEMF zero-cross detection is handled by four dedicated ESC MCUs (AT32F421 running AM32). The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to each ESC over bidirectional DShot600. This is the only architecture ArduPilot supports for bidir DShot RPM telemetry, and it isolates flight control from power-stage faults.

> ⚠️ **Reality check up front.** 4 × 80 A means 320 A from the battery. That exceeds XT60 (≈60 A continuous) and XT90 ratings, and most 6S pack C-ratings at nano-class capacity. The analysis below shows that "80 A burst" on a 25.5 mm board is valid only as a **seconds-scale transient**. The design must enforce this in firmware.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current-path topology (6-step, synchronous)

In any 60° sector, one phase's high side (HS) is PWM-ing, one phase's low side (LS) is held on, and the third phase floats.

- During D·T_sw, current flows through HS(a) and LS(b).
- During (1−D)·T_sw, the complementary LS(a) freewheels with LS(b).

There are always **exactly two R_DS(on) in series** in the current path, independent of duty:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j) \quad\text{(minus the dead-time interval, handled in §2)}
$$

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right],\qquad \alpha = \frac{1.5 - 1}{100} = 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

### 1.3 Self-consistent junction temperature

The resistance depends on T_j, and T_j depends on the loss, so we iterate:

$$
T_j = T_{board} + P_{FET}\cdot R_{th(j\text{-}board)}, \qquad R_{th(j\text{-}board)} \approx 2.0\ \text{K/W (DFN3.3 + solder + via array)}
$$

| Iteration | T_j (°C) | R_DS(on) (mΩ) | P_cond,ch (W) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 25 | 1.20 | 15.4 |
| 1 | 100 | 1.65 | 21.1 |
| 2 | 115 | 1.74 | 22.3 |
| 3 (converged) | ~118 | 1.76 | **22.5 W** |

### 1.4 Distribution across the six FETs

Each FET conducts for roughly 1/3 of the electrical period: 120° as HS (shared with LS by duty) plus 120° as LS. Average loss per FET:

$$
\bar P_{FET,cond} \approx \frac{22.5}{6} = 3.75\ \text{W}
$$

The peak instantaneous loss is in the full-on LS: 80² × 1.76 mΩ = **11.3 W** for one 120° interval. At typical electrical frequencies (>300 Hz) that interval is under 1.1 ms. This is much shorter than the DFN thermal time constant (~10–50 ms), so the junction sees approximately the average plus a small ripple of a few K.

### 1.5 PCB copper loss (frequently ignored, not negligible)

**Phase and bus copper (per channel):**

- 2 oz copper sheet resistance ≈ 0.25 mΩ/□ at 25 °C, rising to ~0.33 mΩ/□ at 100 °C.
- The phase path is ~3 squares on 4 paralleled layers ≈ 0.25 mΩ.
- P = 80² × 0.25 m ≈ **1.6 W per channel**.

**Battery bus (shared):**

- 320 A through ~0.2 mΩ of pad, plane, and vias gives P = 320² × 0.2 m ≈ **20 W** concentrated at the battery pads.
- This is a dominant hotspot. Use ≥ 8 layers of bus plane and large pad-via fields, and solder the pigtail directly.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per channel, only the PWM-ing half-bridge switches hard. One hard-switched leg is active at a time.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard-switching, inductive load)

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = \tfrac12(25.2)(80)(25\times10^{-9})(48\times10^3) = \mathbf{1.21\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery of the complementary FET body diode

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

The added turn-on current spike (I_rr·V·t_rr/2) is included in a 1.5× margin below.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two transitions per period)

$$
P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot100\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.307\ W}
$$
