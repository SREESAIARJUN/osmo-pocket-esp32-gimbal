## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

The STM32H743 is the **flight controller (FC) MCU**. It runs ArduPilot and generates DShot. The 4-in-1 ESC needs its own per-motor MCUs, which run the commutation and the 48 kHz PWM. Typical choices are AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or EFM8/BB51 running Bluejay. An H743 cannot commutate four BLDC motors and run ArduPilot at the same time. The design below therefore covers both boards of the stack.

| Parameter | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_nom = 22.2 V, V_max = 25.2 V (use 25.2 V for worst case) |
| Burst current | 80 A per motor (phase/DC current), ≤ a few seconds |
| PWM | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous rectification), 6-step trapezoidal |
| MOSFET | 40 V, PDFN3.3×3.3. R_DS(on),25 = 1.0 mΩ @ V_GS = 10 V. Q_g = 50 nC, Q_rr = 50 nC, Q_oss = 30 nC, R_θJC = 1.2 K/W |
| Switching edges | t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 20 ns (dedicated half-bridge driver, ~1.5 A peak) |
| Dead time | 100 ns per edge |
| Board | 20×20 mm (nano), 1.6 mm FR-4, 6 layers × 2 oz Cu |

**Reality check up front:** 80 A per motor on a 20×20 mm board is a **burst-only** rating. Section 3 shows the board thermal mass allows roughly 1–5 s at that current, depending on how many motors are loaded.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\left(\frac{T_j + 273}{298}\right)^{\kappa},\qquad \kappa \approx 2.0\text{–}2.5
$$

With κ = 2.3:

| T_j | R_DS(on) |
|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 1.68 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 1.95 mΩ |

In practice, a linear fit is often used instead: R_DS(on) ≈ R₂₅·[1 + 0.0045·(T_j − 25)], which gives 1.45 mΩ at 125 °C. Use the datasheet's normalized R_DS(on) vs. T curve, and iterate (Section 3.4).

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

At any instant exactly two phases conduct. During the ON interval the current flows through high-side A and low-side B. During the OFF interval (after dead time) it flows through low-side A (synchronous) and low-side B. **The current always passes through exactly 2 MOSFETs, independent of duty cycle.**

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) + I_{ph}^2\,R_{Cu}
$$

R_Cu is the PCB plus solder-joint resistance of the full current loop, estimated at ≈ 0.3 mΩ for 2 oz × 6 layers on a nano board.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 110 °C (R_DS ≈ 1.40 mΩ, linear model)

$$
P_{cond,FET} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.40\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{17.9\ W}
$$

$$
P_{Cu} = 80^2 \cdot 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{1.9\ W}
$$

### 1.4 Per-device share

Each of the six FETs is active about one third of the electrical cycle. Averaged:

$$
P_{cond,per\,FET} \approx \frac{17.9}{6} \approx 3.0\ \text{W}
$$

The **instantaneous** dissipation while a FET conducts is 80² × 1.4 mΩ = 9.0 W. Use this for the junction ripple analysis.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing high-side/low-side pair of the active phase hard-switches. The non-PWM low side stays on for 60° of the cycle.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard-switched edges)

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I_{ph}\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}
= \tfrac12 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\,\text{ns} \cdot 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

For more accuracy, replace (t_r + t_f) with the Miller-plateau times from Q_gd and the driver current:

$$
t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,R_{G,tot}}{V_{DRV} - V_{plateau}}
$$

### 2.2 Body-diode reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 50\,\text{nC} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}
$$

Add roughly the same amount again for the extra turn-on current spike in the opposing FET: ≈ 0.12 W total.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = Q_{oss}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 30\,\text{nC}\cdot 25.2\cdot 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}
$$

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_F\,I_{ph}\cdot 2\,t_{dt}\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\,\text{ns}\cdot 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors.

$$
P_{g} = 2\,Q_g\,V_{DRV}\,f_{sw} = 2\cdot 50\,\text{nC}\cdot 10\cdot 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$
