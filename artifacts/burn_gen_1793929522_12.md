## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Feasibility Finding

### 0.1 Architecture Correction

The STM32H743 should **not** commutate the motors. ArduPilot has no ESC firmware path, and running four 48 kHz six-step or FOC loops beside the EKF would compromise both. The baseline architecture is:

| Block | Device | Firmware |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIH6 (LQFP100 / TFBGA100) | ArduPilot (ChibiOS) |
| ESC ×4 | AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071 (one per motor) | AM32 / BLHeli_32-compatible |
| Gate driver ×4 | 3-phase half-bridge driver, FD6288Q-class (bootstrap, ~1.0/0.8 A) | n/a |
| MOSFETs ×24 | 40 V, ~0.7 mΩ class, PDFN 5×6 or 3.3×3.3 (6 per ESC) | n/a |
| Link | Bi-directional DShot600 (eRPM returned on the same wire) | n/a |

### 0.3 Feasibility Finding (Read First)

**Finding:** 80 A per channel on a 20×20 mm "nano" board is **not thermally sustainable** for more than about 1–2 s. Section 3 derives this. You will see "80 A burst" on 30.5 mm boards in the market; those ratings rely on larger copper area, heavier construction, and short, loosely specified burst windows. Section 5 gives the realistic ratings.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-Step Synchronous Commutation

In each 60° sector, three devices carry current:

- **High-side A** switches with duty *D*.
- **Low-side A** (complementary, synchronous rectifier) conducts for (1 − D), minus dead time.
- **Low-side B** is on continuously for the sector.

The phase current therefore always passes through exactly two channels:

$$
P_{cond,ESC} = I_{ph}^2 \, R_{ds}(T_j)\,\big[D + (1-D) + 1\big] = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

Ripple correction (triangular ripple ΔI):

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}
$$

At 48 kHz with typical motor inductance (~10–30 µH line-to-line), ΔI/I_ph is below 10%. The correction is therefore under 0.1% and is ignored.

### 1.2 Temperature Dependence

Linearised model:

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \qquad \alpha = \frac{k_T - 1}{100} = 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

Because P_cond raises T_j, which raises R_ds, the loss must be solved self-consistently:

$$
T_j = T_{ref} + R_{\theta}\cdot 2 I^2 R_{25}\left[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\right]
\;\Rightarrow\;
T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + 2I^2R_{25}R_\theta(1-25\alpha)}{1 - 2I^2R_{25}R_\theta\,\alpha}
$$

**Thermal-runaway condition:** the denominator reaches zero when

$$
2I^2R_{25}R_\theta\,\alpha \ge 1.
$$

At 80 A this happens for R_θ ≥ 0.93 K/W. Any realistic nano-board R_θ (≈ 15 K/W, see §3) far exceeds this. **There is no steady state at 80 A; only a transient exists.**

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A (T_j = 100 °C Operating Point)

- R_ds(100 °C) = 0.70 × (1 + 0.006 × 75) = **1.015 mΩ**
- P_cond = 2 × 80² × 1.015 × 10⁻³ = **13.0 W per ESC**

Per-device split within one sector:

| Device | Loss |
|---|---|
| HS-A | D · I²R |
| LS-A | (1 − D) · I²R |
| LS-B | 6.5 W |

LS-B is the hottest device instantaneously. Averaged over a full electrical cycle, each FET is active 120°, so its average loss is P_cond / 6 + its share of switching loss.

**Low-eRPM caveat:** at stall or very low eRPM (e.g. a throttle punch from idle), sector dwell can approach the FET's thermal time constant (~ms). A single FET can then see the full 6.5 W without rotation averaging. Size the design for this case.

## 2. Switching and Ancillary Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd high-side device hard-switches. The synchronous low side turns on after its body diode conducts, so it switches at near-zero voltage (ZVS-like).

### 2.1 V–I Overlap

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I_{ph}\,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}
= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}
$$

Here t_r ≈ t_f ≈ Q_gd / I_gate,plateau. With the driver's ~0.8 A sink current, t_f grows. Re-derive using:

$$
t \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,(R_{drv}+R_g+R_{g,int})}{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}
$$
