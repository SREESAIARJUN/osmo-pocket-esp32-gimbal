# STM32H743 AIO 4-in-1 ESC Nano Flight Controller: System Architecture & Analysis

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Red Flags

Some spec assumptions need to be stated up front because they drive everything else.

| Item | Assumption used | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Form factor | 25.5 × 25.5 mm board, 20 × 20 M2 mounting, stacked FC/ESC or single 8-layer AIO | "Nano" class |
| Battery | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal | 4S changes switching numbers (Section 3) |
| ESC topology | 4 × dedicated ESC MCUs (AM32-class, e.g. AT32F421) driven by the H743 over DShot | The H743 runs ArduPilot only. It does not commutate motors. |
| "80 A burst" | Per motor, short duration | **Red flag:** see 0.1 |
| PWM frequency | 48 kHz is an ESC-firmware parameter (AM32 supports it) | Unrelated to DShot rate |
| MOSFET parameters | Representative 40 V, 3.3 × 3.3 / 5 × 6 DFN class | **Substitute your actual datasheet values.** All numbers below are parametric. |

### 0.1 The spec as written is not physically achievable on this form factor

- **Total current:** 4 × 80 A = 320 A. That exceeds any XT60 or XT30 connector and any nano-class battery lead (12–14 AWG). The battery would also sag severely.
- **Burst duration:** With the thermal mass of a 25.5 mm board, the model in Section 4 shows 80 A on all four channels is sustainable for about **1–2 s**.
- **Recommended datasheet language:** "80 A per channel, ≤1 s, single channel; 4 × 45 A ≤10 s; 4 × 25–30 A continuous with prop-wash airflow." Section 4 justifies these numbers.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Commutation model

Assume 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification. At any instant, exactly two phase legs carry the phase current $I$:

- **Leg A** PWM-switches: the high-side is on for duty $D$, and the complementary low-side is on for $1-D$.
- **Leg B** holds its low-side fully on.
- The third leg floats (BEMF sensing).

So, ignoring dead time, there are always exactly **two FET channels in series with the current**:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \left[ D\,R_{HS}(T_j) + (1-D)\,R_{LS}(T_j) + R_{LS}(T_j) \right]$$

With identical FETs this simplifies, independent of $D$:

$$\boxed{P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)}$$

### 2.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big], \quad \alpha \approx 0.004\text{–}0.006\ \mathrm{K^{-1}}$$

Use the datasheet normalized curve. Typically $R(125^\circ\text{C})/R(25^\circ\text{C}) \approx 1.5$, which gives $\alpha = 0.005$.

### 2.3 Parasitic copper and solder path

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{path}, \qquad R_{Cu}(T) = R_{20}[1 + 0.00393(T-20)]$$

The phase pad, internal planes, and vias in series amount to about 0.3 mΩ per path. There are two paths in series, plus the battery-bus share.

### 2.4 Worked numbers (single FET per position)

Inputs: $R_{DS(on),25,max} = 1.3\ \text{mΩ}$, $T_j = 125^\circ$C, so $R = 1.95\ \text{mΩ}$.

| Quantity | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction per channel | $2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.95\text{m}$ | **24.96 W** |
| Copper (0.6 mΩ @ 80 °C) | $80^2 \cdot 0.6\text{m}\cdot 1.24$ | 4.75 W |
| Hottest single FET (static low-side) | $80^2 \cdot 1.95\text{m}$ | 12.5 W |

Design implication: 12.5 W in one DFN is beyond what it can shed continuously. Two options follow.

- **Paralleled FETs (2 per position, 12 per channel):** conduction drops to **12.5 W per channel** and 6.2 W per FET. This is the standard approach for any rating above 50 A, if layout area permits. It probably won't on 25.5 mm with 4 channels; 30.5 mm is realistic.
- **Single-FET design:** the realistic rating is about 45–55 A burst.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at $f_{sw}$ = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Per channel:

$$P_{sw} = \underbrace{\tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}}_{\text{V-I overlap}} + \underbrace{Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw}}_{\text{reverse recovery}} + \underbrace{2\cdot\tfrac12 C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw}}_{\text{output cap}} + \underbrace{2 V_F I\, t_{dt} f_{sw}}_{\text{dead-time diode}} + \underbrace{2 Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw}}_{\text{gate (in driver)}}$$
