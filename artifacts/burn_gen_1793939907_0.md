# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Thermal Model and Bidirectional DShot600 Timer/DMA Architecture

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

### Architectural correction

The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does **not** commutate the motors. Each ESC channel needs its own MCU running AM32, BLHeli_32 or Bluejay (for example, AT32F421 or STM32G071). The 48 kHz PWM frequency is therefore an **ESC firmware setting**, not an ArduPilot one. ArduPilot only produces the DShot600 command stream and decodes the bidirectional eRPM replies.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 100 °C: R_ds = 1.5 mΩ × 1.30 = **1.95 mΩ**.

Use about 2.1 mΩ to include PCB and solder resistance in the current path. This is a positive-feedback loop, which is why burst behaviour is so sensitive to starting temperature (see section 4.3).

### 1.2 Trapezoidal (6-step) commutation with synchronous rectification

In any commutation state, current flows through two phase legs:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for duty D, and the low-side FET conducts for 1−D (synchronous rectification).
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET conducts for 100 % of the time.

The total conduction loss per channel is therefore:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

The result is independent of duty cycle. Ripple adds a factor of (1 + r²/12), where r = ΔI/I. At 48 kHz with typical phase inductance, r is about 0.2, so the correction is under 1 % and is neglected.

**At 80 A:**

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.1\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{26.9\ W\ per\ channel}$$

- The worst single FET (the return-leg low side) dissipates 13.4 W while it conducts.
- Averaged over the 6 commutation steps, each of the 6 FETs on a channel carries current about 1/3 of the time.

**FOC / sinusoidal drive.** With a peak current of I_pk and three legs active, P_cond ≈ (3/2)·I_pk²·R_ds. At equal RMS current this gives a similar total, but the loss is spread more evenly across the FETs.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. With synchronous rectification there is one hard-switched FET per transition.

| Loss term | Equation | Value per channel |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 30 ns × 48 k | 1.45 W |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_f·I·(2·t_dt)·f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 400 ns × 48 k | 1.23 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 30 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.036 W |
| C_oss charging | ½·C_oss·V²·f_sw | 0.015 W |
| Gate drive (2 FETs, dissipated in driver and R_g) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw | 0.038 W |
| **Total switching loss** | | **≈ 2.77 W** |

Key observations:

- **Dead time is nearly as costly as the overlap loss.** Reducing t_dt from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per channel at 80 A. AM32 exposes a dead-time setting. Tune it on the real board while watching gate waveforms; do not choose it blindly.
- **Switching frequency trade-off.** Moving from 48 kHz to 24 kHz halves P_sw to about 1.4 W. The cost is more audible noise and higher current ripple on low-inductance motors.
- **Switching loss scales with current.** P_sw is proportional to I, while P_cond is proportional to I².

## 3. Total Loss Budget

### Per channel

$$P_{ch}(I) = 2I^2R_{ds}(T_j) + I\left[\tfrac12 V(t_r+t_f) + 2V_f t_{dt}\right] f_{sw} + \text{(fixed terms)}$$

$$P_{ch}(80\,\text{A}) \approx 26.9 + 2.77 \approx \mathbf{29.7\ W}$$
