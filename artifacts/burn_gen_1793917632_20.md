# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #4): System Architecture, Loss Model, DShot/DMA Allocation, and Netlists

> **Read this before anything else.** All MOSFET, driver and board parameters below are **design assumptions** for a generic 40 V DFN3.3×3.3 part class. They are not quotes from a specific datasheet. Before release you must substitute the values from the datasheets you actually select. The equations are exact for the model stated, but the numbers are only as good as their inputs.
>
> **The headline result is negative:** 80 A per motor, on all four motors simultaneously, is **not thermally achievable on a nano-class board** for more than about 1 to 2 seconds with single FETs per switch. Section 4 quantifies this, and Section 5 gives the design changes that close the gap.

## 1. System Architecture

### 1.1 Partitioning

The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. ArduPilot does **not** perform BLDC commutation, so each motor bridge needs its own ESC MCU. The architecture is therefore:

```
                 ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  VBAT 6S ──┬──► │ 4× ESC channel: AT32F421 (AM32) + 3-ph gate  │──► M1..M4
            │    │ driver + 6× N-FET + shunt + BEMF dividers    │
            │    └───────▲──────────────────────────────────────┘
            │            │ 4× bidirectional DShot600 (single wire, 3V3)
            │    ┌───────┴───────────────┐
            └──► │ STM32H743VIT6 @400MHz │◄── IMU (SPI1), Baro (I2C2),
     5V/3V3 BEC  │ ArduPilot             │    OSD/SD/UARTs
                 └───────────────────────┘
```

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Board | 30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz on all layers. A 20×20 board is substantially worse; see §4.4. |
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus,max = 25.2 V |
| Drive | 6-step trapezoidal with synchronous rectification (AM32 default), f_sw = 48 kHz |
| Burst rating | 80 A phase-DC-equivalent per motor |

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Conducting Path in 6-Step Synchronous Operation

At every instant, exactly **two** devices carry the phase current I:

- the PWM leg's high-side FET during D, or its low-side FET (synchronous rectification) during 1−D, and
- the opposite leg's low-side FET, which is on for the full 60° sector.

This gives:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

### 2.2 Per-Device Distribution Within a Sector

| Device | Loss |
|---|---|
| Static low-side FET | I²R_ds (the worst device) |
| PWM high-side FET | D·I²R_ds |
| PWM low-side FET (synchronous) | (1−D)·I²R_ds |

Averaged over a full electrical revolution, each of the 6 FETs conducts for 1/3 of the period. The **hotspot**, however, is governed by the 60° sector peak, because the FET thermal time constant (about 1 to 10 ms) is comparable to the sector time at low eRPM.

### 2.3 Numbers at 80 A, with Electro-Thermal Feedback

Iterate T_j, starting from a 125 °C guess:

- R_ds(125 °C) = 1.2 × (1 + 0.005 × 100) = **1.80 mΩ**
- P_cond,motor = 2 × 80² × 1.80e-3 = **23.0 W**
- Static low-side FET during its sector: 80² × 1.8e-3 = **11.5 W**
