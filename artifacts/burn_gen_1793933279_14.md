## 0. Architecture Decisions and a Feasibility Check

### 0.1 System partitioning

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. It runs ArduPilot and emits 4× bidirectional DShot600. Four dedicated ESC MCUs (AT32F421K8 or STM32G071, running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware) perform the 48 kHz complementary PWM and BEMF commutation. This is how every production 4-in-1 works. Commutating four sensorless motors from the flight-control MCU at 48 kHz would wreck ArduPilot's scheduler.

```
 6S VBAT ──┬──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
           │                                              │
     [Buck 5V/3A]──[LDO 3V3]── STM32H743VIH6 (TFBGA100)    │
           │                    │ TIM3 CH1..CH4 (bidir DShot600)
           │                    ▼                         │
           │        ┌─ ESC MCU1 ─ FD6288Q ─ 6×FET ─ M1    │
           │        ├─ ESC MCU2 ─ FD6288Q ─ 6×FET ─ M2  ◄─┘
           │        ├─ ESC MCU3 ─ FD6288Q ─ 6×FET ─ M3
           │        └─ ESC MCU4 ─ FD6288Q ─ 6×FET ─ M4
           └── shunt + INA → H743 ADC (CURR), divider → ADC (VBAT)
```

### 0.2 Two interpretations of the "80 A burst" spec

| Interpretation | Per-motor current | Verdict on a 20×20 / 25.5×25.5 board |
|---|---|---|
| **A: 80 A per channel** | 80 A | **Not physically credible.** About 28 W per channel and 112 W per board (derived in §3). The board has roughly 3 J/K of thermal mass, so the adiabatic rise is about 37 K/s. Combined with junction rise, the FETs would exceed rating in under 2 s even with ideal copper. |
| **B: 80 A board total** | 20 A per motor | **Feasible** for short bursts, with 2 oz+ copper, 6-layer stackup, and prop-wash cooling. |

I derive the model parametrically, evaluate both cases, and **recommend specifying Case B** (20 A/ch continuous-burst, 80 A aggregate) unless the board grows to 30.5×30.5 mm or larger with a heat spreader. If the product brief truly says 80 A per channel on a nano form factor, push back on it before layout.
