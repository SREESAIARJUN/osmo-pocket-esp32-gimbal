# STM32H743 Nano AIO with 4-in-1 ESC: 80 A Burst Loss Model, Thermal Budget, and Bi-Directional DShot600 DMA Plan

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and One Architectural Correction

**Context.** I don't have records for "Job #57", so this document states every assumption. Replace the values in the parameter table (§1) with your actual BOM datasheet values. All equations are written so the numbers drop straight in.

**Architecture correction.** ArduPilot running on the STM32H743 does **not** commutate motors or generate the 48 kHz bridge PWM. In a real AIO design:

- The **H743** is the flight controller. It sends bi-directional DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry.
- The **ESC MCU(s)** generate the 48 kHz complementary PWM to the gate drivers. These are typically one AT32F421 or STM32G071 per motor running AM32, or a single-MCU BLHeli_32-class design.

The power-stage math (§2–§4) therefore applies to the ESC side, and the DMA/timer math (§5) applies to the H743 side.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current path

The ESC uses trapezoidal (6-step) commutation with synchronous rectification. At any instant, current flows through:

- one high-side device, and
- one low-side device.

During the PWM off-time, the complementary FET on the switched leg conducts instead of its body diode. As a result, the conduction loss is approximately independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 2.2 Ripple correction

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad
\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{2 L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}$$

Example: with L_ph = 10 µH, D = 0.9 and V_bemf = 21 V, the ripple is ΔI ≈ 3.9 A. The added term is ΔI²/12 ≈ 1.3 A², which is negligible against 6400 A². It matters only on very low-inductance motors.

### 2.3 Temperature coupling (fixed-point solution)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + \frac{P_{cond}+P_{sw}}{2}\, R_{\theta JC}$$

Iterate these two equations to convergence. Three iterations are sufficient.

**Result at the design point T_j ≈ 110 °C:**

- R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.006 × 85) = **1.51 mΩ**
- P_cond = 80² × 2 × 1.51 mΩ = **19.3 W per motor channel**

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per PWM period, only one leg switches: one hard turn-on edge and one hard turn-off edge.

| Loss term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 k | 1.94 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw | 0.036 W |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss · V · f_sw (conservative estimate) | 0.048 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f · I · 2 t_dt · f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 100 ns × 48 k | 0.31 W |
| Gate drive (2 switching FETs) | 2 · Q_g · V_gs · f_sw | 0.048 W |
| **Total switching-related loss** | | **≈ 2.38 W** |

### 3.1 Totals

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Per-channel loss | 19.3 + 2.38 ≈ **21.7 W** |
| 4-in-1 total (all motors at 80 A simultaneously) | **≈ 87 W** |
| Plus shunts (0.5 mΩ, I²R) | +3.2 W per channel |
| Plus PCB copper | +2–4 W |
| **Worst-case board dissipation** | **~100 W** |

### 3.2 Sensitivity

Each loss term scales as follows:

- **Conduction:** ∝ I² · R_ds. This term dominates at about 89% of the total.
- **Switching:** ∝ V · I · f_sw. Moving from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves only about 1.2 W per channel.

**Lever to prioritize:** R_ds(on) and gate voltage, not switching frequency.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Junction-to-board rise

Each conducting FET dissipates about 10.85 W.

- Steady-state junction-to-case rise: ΔT_jc = 10.85 × 1.2 = **13 K**.
- For a 1–5 s burst, Z_θJC(t) is approximately equal to R_θJC, because the junction-to-case time constant is around 100 ms or less.

### 4.2 Board as a lumped thermal mass (transient)

**Heat capacity estimate:**

| Component | Mass | Specific heat | Heat capacity |
|---|---|---|---|
| FR4 | ~5 g | 1.1 J/g·K | 5.5 J/K |
| Copper | ~4 g | 0.385 J/g·K | 1.5 J/K |
| Components | — | — | ~1 J/K |
| **Total C_th** | | | **≈ 8 J/K** |

**Board-to-air resistance:**

- In a typical prop-wash duct: R_θ,b-a ≈ 3 K/W.
- With no airflow: about 8 K/W.

**Governing equations:**

$$C_{th}\frac{dT_{pcb}}{dt} = P_{tot} - \frac{T_{pcb}-T_{amb}}{R_{\theta,b\text{-}a}}$$

$$T_{pcb}(t) = T_{amb} + P R\,(1-e^{-t/RC}), \qquad \tau = RC \approx 24\ \text{s}$$

**Result with all four motors at 80 A** (P = 87 W, T_amb = 35 °C):

| Burst duration | T_pcb | T_j |
|---|---|---|
| 1 s | 35 + 87×3×(1−e^(−1/24)) = 45.7 °C | ≈ 59 °C |
| 3 s | 65.7 °C | ≈ 79 °C |
| 5 s | 84.5 °C | ≈ 98 °C |
| 10 s | 125 °C | ≈ 138 °C (**exceeds limit**) |

Here T_j = T_pcb + 13 K, plus about 2 K of iteration correction for R_ds rise.

### 4.3 Allowable burst time

The burst time to reach the junction limit is:

$$t_{max} = -\tau \ln\!\left(1 - \frac{T_{j,max} - \Delta T_{jc} - T_{amb}}{P R}\right)$$

With T_j,max = 150 °C and an 25 K derating margin (target 125 °C):

**t_max ≈ 8.2 s** (all four motors at 80 A).
