# STM32H743 AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC): 80 A Burst Thermal, 48 kHz Switching, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Design Package

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

Two premises need correcting before the math.

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the FETs.** In any 4-in-1 design, each ESC channel has its own MCU (AM32 on STM32G071/AT32F421, or BLHeli_32/Bluejay). These run gate drive, back-EMF sensing and the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bi-directional DShot600 to those four ESC MCUs.
2. **80 A per channel on a nano (20×20 mm) board is not thermally sustainable.** The model below shows it is a sub-second burst rating at best. This is the most important design finding.

Working assumptions (replace with your BOM values):

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S, full) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Burst phase current | I | 80 A |
| MOSFET (40 V, DFN3.3×3.3 class) | R_ds(on),25 | 2.0 mΩ |
| R_ds(on) temperature coefficient | α | 0.005 /K |
| Rise + fall time | t_r + t_f | 10 + 10 ns |
| Dead time | t_dt | 100 ns |
| Body diode forward voltage | V_f | 0.8 V |
| Reverse recovery charge | Q_rr | 30 nC |
| Output capacitance | C_oss | 1.0 nF |
| Gate charge / drive voltage | Q_g / V_gs | 40 nC / 10 V |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

**Temperature-dependent on-resistance:**

R_ds(on)(T_j) = R₂₅ · [1 + α(T_j − 25)]

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 2.0 × 1.5 = **3.0 mΩ**.

**Current path.** In trapezoidal six-step drive, two phases conduct at all times:
- The non-PWM leg holds one low-side FET on continuously.
- The PWM leg alternates between the high-side FET (fraction D) and the synchronous low-side FET (fraction 1−D).

So one FET is always in each half of the path, independent of duty:

P_cond,ch = I² · R_HS · D + I² · R_LS · (1−D) + I² · R_LS ≈ **2 · I² · R_ds(on)(T_j)**

**At 80 A:**

| T_j | R_ds(on) | P_cond per channel | P_cond, 4 channels |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 2.0 mΩ | 25.6 W | 102 W |
| 125 °C | 3.0 mΩ | **38.4 W** | **154 W** |

**Thermal runaway check.** dP/dT_j = 2I²R₂₅α = 0.128 W/K. This is stable only if 0.128 × R_th,ja < 1, i.e. R_th,ja < 7.8 K/W per channel. A nano board in still air exceeds that, so the board is runaway-prone without propwash cooling.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw | 0.5 · 25.2 · 80 · 20 ns · 48 kHz = **0.97 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f · I · 2t_dt · f_sw | 0.8 · 80 · 200 ns · 48 kHz = **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw | 30 nC · 25.2 · 48 kHz = **0.036 W** |
| C_oss discharge | ½ · C_oss · V² · f_sw (×2 for HS+LS) | ≈ **0.030 W** |
| Gate drive (mostly in driver/R_g) | 2 · Q_g · V_gs · f_sw | **0.038 W** |
| **Total switching per channel** | | **≈ 1.69 W** |

Dead time is the largest controllable term after overlap: going from 100 ns to 200 ns doubles it to 1.23 W.

The choice of 48 kHz costs about 1.7 W/ch versus about 0.85 W at 24 kHz. That is small next to conduction losses, so keep 48 kHz for current ripple and acoustics.

**Total at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C:**

P_ch ≈ 38.4 + 1.7 ≈ **40.1 W**, so P_board ≈ **160 W** (plus shunt and trace losses; see §4).

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Junction-level network (per FET, Foster approximation)

T_j(t) = T_pcb + P_FET · Σ R_i(1 − e^(−t/τ_i))

Typical DFN3.3 values:

| Stage | R_i | τ_i |
|---|---|---|
| Die | 0.3 K/W | 0.5 ms |
| Die attach / clip | 0.6 K/W | 5 ms |
| Package to pad | 0.6 K/W | 50 ms |

P_FET ≈ 20 W (two conducting FETs share the ~40 W per channel), with T_j − T_pcb ≈ 30 K at steady state. The junction tracks the PCB closely after about 100 ms, so **the board's bulk heat capacity is the real limit.**

### 3.2 Board heat capacity (20×20 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz outer / 1 oz inner)

| Component | Mass | c_p | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copper (~0.17 cm³) | 1.5 g | 0.385 J/g·K | 0.58 J/K |
| FR-4 (0.64 cm³) | 1.2 g | 0.6 J/g·K | 0.71 J/K |
| 24 FETs + passives | ~1.5 g | ~0.7 J/g·K | ~1.0 J/K |
| **Total** | | | **C_th ≈ 2.3 J/K** |

### 3.3 Burst duration (adiabatic)

t_burst = C_th · ΔT / P

Starting at T_pcb = 50 °C, limited to T_pcb = 100 °C (T_j ≈ 130 °C):

| Load | P | t_burst |
|---|---|---|
| All 4 channels at 80 A | 160 W | **≈ 0.7 s** |
| All 4 channels at 50 A | ~60 W | ≈ 1.9 s |
| All 4 channels at 30 A | ~22 W | ≈ 5.2 s (≈ steady state with propwash) |

### 3.4 Steady state

With strong propwash, R_th,board-amb ≈ 5–8 K/W. ΔT = 50 K therefore allows roughly 6–10 W, about **15–20 A continuous per channel**.

**Conclusion:** Market the rating as "80 A / 0.5 s burst, 20 A continuous." Better, move to a 30.5 mm layout or 4 FET pairs per phase to quadruple copper area and halve R_ds(on).

## 4. Ancillary Losses

| Element | Calculation | Loss at 320 A (4×80 A) |
|---|---|---|
| Battery shunt, 0.1 mΩ | I²R | **10.2 W** (needs a 4–6 W-rated shunt; at burst it is pulse-rated) |
| Battery pads/traces, ~0.3 mΩ effective | I²R | **≈ 30 W** |
| Bulk capacitor ripple | I_cap,rms ≈ I·√(D(1−D)) = 40 A at D = 0.5 | Needs ≥ 2× 1000 µF low-ESR external cap plus 4×10 µF/50 V MLCC per channel |

At burst, the copper traces dissipate about as much as one ESC channel. Use 2 oz on all power planes and stitch the battery pads with ≥ 40 vias.
