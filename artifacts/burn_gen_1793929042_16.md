## 0. Architecture Clarification and Headline Finding

**The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In a 4-in-1 ESC, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. These are typically STM32G071 or AT32F421 parts, four in total. Each ESC MCU:
- generates the 48 kHz PWM for its gate driver, and
- performs commutation.

The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to the four ESC MCUs over bi-directional DShot600. So the system splits into two analytical domains:

| Domain | Owner | Analysed in |
|---|---|---|
| Power stage (conduction, switching, thermal) | ESC MCU + gate driver + 24 MOSFETs | §1–§3 |
| Command/telemetry (DShot600 BDShot) | STM32H743 timers + DMA | §4 |

> ⚠️ **Headline finding:** A *simultaneous* 80 A burst on all four channels produces about **100 W** of dissipation. A nano-class board (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) has only about 3–5 J/K of thermal mass. Uncooled, that is **≈20–25 K/s** of temperature rise, so 80 A is sustainable for only **≈3–4 s** before Tj approaches 125 °C. Section 3 quantifies this and lists mitigations. The "80 A burst" rating must be specified with a duration and a channel count (per-motor vs. all-motor), or the rating is not physically meaningful.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-step (trapezoidal) bridge current paths

In each of the 6 commutation steps, current I_ph flows from V_bat through the high-side (HS) FET of phase X, the motor, and the low-side (LS) FET of phase Y. AM32 uses complementary (synchronous) PWM on the switching leg, so:

| FET | Conducts during step | Loss in step |
|---|---|---|
| HS, switching leg | PWM on-time, fraction D | I²·R·D |
| LS, switching leg (sync rect.) | off-time, fraction 1−D | I²·R·(1−D) |
| LS, static leg | entire step | I²·R |

Summing the three rows gives the total bridge conduction loss per motor. The loss is independent of D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

Each FET is active in 2 of 6 steps, so averaged over an electrical cycle:

$$\overline{P}_{cond,FET} = \frac{I_{ph}^2 R_{DS(on)}(T_j)}{3}$$

**Phase vs. battery current.** In six-step drive, I_bat ≈ D·I_ph. The 80 A rating is therefore defined here as **phase current**. At partial throttle, phase current exceeds battery current, so a battery-side 80 A limit does not protect the FETs.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right]$$

The coefficient α ≈ 0.006 /K **[A]**. This is typical for 30–40 V trench FETs, which show a normalised R of about 1.6 at 125 °C.

### 1.4 Self-consistent solution at 80 A

The junction temperature sets R_DS(on), and R_DS(on) sets the loss that heats the junction. The table below evaluates the loss at three candidate Tj values.

| T_j (°C) | R_DS(on) (mΩ) | P_cond/motor (W) | P_cond/FET avg (W) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 | 1.20 | 15.4 | 2.56 |
| 85 | 1.63 | 20.9 | 3.48 |
| 110 | 1.81 | **23.2** | 3.87 |

Calculation at 110 °C: P = 2 × 80² × 1.81 mΩ = **23.2 W per motor**, or **92.7 W for four motors**.

**Thermal runaway check.** Runaway occurs when ∂P/∂T × R_θ ≥ 1:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T} = 2I^2R_{25}\alpha = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.2\times10^{-3} \times 0.006 = 0.092\ \text{W/K per motor}$$

With a per-motor junction-to-air resistance of R_θ ≈ 8 K/W in prop wash **[A]**, the product is 0.74 < 1. The system is **stable, but marginal**. Without airflow (R_θ ≳ 11 K/W), it runs away.
