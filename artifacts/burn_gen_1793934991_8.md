# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #44): Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Model, and Bi-Directional DShot600 DMA Architecture

## 0. Architectural Clarifications and Design Assumptions

Two system-level facts shape the rest of the analysis.

1. **The STM32H743 does not switch the MOSFETs.** In a 4-in-1 stack, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU (AT32F421, STM32G071, or EFM8 running AM32/BLHeli_32/Bluejay). That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The H743 only produces DShot600 frames and decodes eRPM telemetry. The loss model (§1–2) therefore belongs to the ESC board. The DMA model (§4) belongs to the FC.
2. **80 A per motor on a 20×20 mm ESC is at or beyond physical limits.** Commercial 20×20 ESCs are rated for about 40–60 A burst. This design assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm ESC** paired with a 20×20 FC, or a 20×20 ESC with a strictly limited burst duration (≤2 s). The math below shows why.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step complementary PWM

During each 60° commutation sector, one phase is PWM'd (high-side at duty D, with its low-side as the complementary synchronous rectifier) and a second phase's low-side is held fully ON. The third phase floats (BEMF sensing).

At any instant, exactly **two** FETs carry the full phase current I:

- **Interval D:** HS(PWM phase) + LS(held phase)
- **Interval 1−D:** LS(PWM phase, sync-rect) + LS(held phase)

So the total conduction loss per motor is **independent of duty** (neglecting dead time):

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

### 1.2 Temperature-corrected R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.0 × 1.5 = **1.5 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Numbers

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W}
$$

Add PCB copper and solder losses. Assume 0.3 mΩ per current loop (2 oz inner, 3 oz outer):

$$
P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.3\times10^{-3} = 1.92\ \text{W}
$$

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

Averaged over a full electrical cycle, each phase leg conducts 2/3 of the time:

$$
I_{RMS,phase} = I\sqrt{2/3} = 65.3\ \text{A}
$$

The worst-case **instantaneous** FET (the held-ON low side) sees 80 A × 1.5 mΩ = **9.6 W** during its 120° window. This value drives the junction temperature ripple calculation in §3.4.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd half-bridge switches. There is one switching node per motor per sector.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}
= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.968\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance loss

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss,er} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} \times 2 = 1.5\text{n}\times 635\times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.046\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

The sync-rect FET's body diode conducts during dead time, then recovers when the HS turns on:

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}
$$

The induced HS turn-on loss is often 2–3× this value. Budget **0.1 W**.

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction (dominant secondary loss)

$$
P_{dt} = V_f\, I \,(2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times600\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.84\ W}
$$

> **Design lever:** reducing dead time from 300 ns to 100 ns saves about 1.2 W per motor at 80 A. Verify shoot-through margin against your driver's propagation-delay mismatch.

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in driver and R_g, not the FET)

$$
P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2 = 40\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k}\times2 = 0.038\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.6 Loss summary at 80 A burst, T_j = 125 °C

| Loss term | Per motor (W) | 4 motors (W) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 19.20 | 76.8 |
| PCB copper | 1.92 | 7.7 |
| Switching (overlap) | 0.97 | 3.9 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.84 | 7.4 |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.15 | 0.6 |
| Gate drive | 0.04 | 0.2 |
| **Total** | **24.1** | **96.5** |

At 6S × 320 A (about 8 kW input), this is roughly 98.8% efficiency. That is excellent electrically, but about 96 W must be dissipated into a board weighing a few grams. **Conduction dominates at 80% of the loss**, so R_DS(on) and copper are the levers that matter. Raising or lowering f_sw changes less than 5% of the total.

### 2.7 Frequency sensitivity

$$
\frac{\partial P_{total}}{\partial f_{sw}} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) + V_f I (2t_{dt}) + Q_{rr}V + C_{oss}V^2 \approx 61\ \mu\text{W/Hz}
$$

Moving from 24 to 96 kHz therefore changes the loss by about ±2.9 W per motor.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Thermal network (per hottest FET, Cauer approximation)

```
 P_FET ──► [Tj] ─R_JC=1.5─► [Tcase] ─R_CB=0.5─► [Cu plane] ─R_spread─► [Board bulk C_b] ─R_conv─► T_amb
              │C_j≈0.02J/K      │C_case≈0.08J/K        │                     │C_b                 
```

### 3.2 Board bulk heat capacity

For a 30.5×30.5 mm, 1.6 mm, 8-layer board with 2–3 oz copper:

- FR4: about 2.6 g × 1.1 J/gK ≈ 2.9 J/K
- Copper: about 4.5 g × 0.385 J/gK ≈ 1.7 J/K
- FETs, capacitors, solder: about 0.6 J/K
- **C_b ≈ 5.2 J/K**
