## 1. Commutation Model (Basis for Every Loss Term)

The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM on the active high-side phase. This is the AM32/BLHeli_32 default.

During any 60° electrical sector:

- **Phase A (PWM phase):** the high-side FET conducts for D·T_sw. The low-side FET synchronously rectifies for (1−D)·T_sw.
- **Phase B (return phase):** the low-side FET conducts for 100% of the sector.
- **Phase C:** floating, used for back-EMF zero-cross sensing.

At every instant, phase current I flows through exactly **two** conducting channels in series. Over one electrical revolution, each FET position is active for 1/3 of the time. Because of the complementary PWM, the conduction time is split between the high-side and low-side FETs of a given leg.

## 2. Conduction Losses

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\left[1 + \alpha_R\,(T_j - 25)\right]$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

R_ds = 1.2 mΩ × (1 + 0.0045 × 100) = **1.74 mΩ**

### 2.2 Instantaneous and per-ESC conduction power

Two channels are always in series, so:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

At I = 80A and T_j = 125 °C:

P_cond,ESC = 2 × 6400 × 1.74×10⁻³ = **22.3 W**

The average per FET, with the loss spread over 6 positions, is 22.3 / 6 = **3.71 W**.

The worst-case single FET is the one that holds the return-phase low side at 100% while the adjacent leg runs PWM. Its loss is (1/3) × I²R ≈ 3.7 W averaged over a revolution. However, it reaches **11.1 W instantaneous** for 1/3 of the electrical period.
