## 0. Architecture Review: Read First

Three findings change the design before any math applies.

| # | Finding | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | The H743 should **not** commutate the motors. A 4-in-1 ESC uses 1 MCU per motor (e.g., AT32F421 / STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32) or a single G4-class MCU. The H743 is the flight controller and talks bidirectional DShot600 to the ESC MCUs. | Defines the partition used in this document. |
| A2 | **80A per motor on a "nano" (20×20 mm) board is thermally impossible for any sustained duration.** Section 3 shows roughly 120 W of total dissipation into a board with a heat capacity of about 2 J/K. Typical nano-class realistic ratings are 15–25A continuous and 30–40A burst. | The 80A rating must be either (a) a ≤1 s burst with stated conditions, or (b) moved to a 30.5×30.5 mm board with 5×6 mm FETs. |
| A3 | Bidirectional DShot needs **input capture**. Complementary `TIMx_CHyN` pins cannot capture. Many H743 reference designs put motors on `TIM8_CH2N/CH3N`, which breaks bidirectional DShot. | Motor pins must be assigned to `CHy` (non-N) outputs (Section 5). |

The rest of this document models the full 80A case so the numbers expose the limits.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (Per Motor)

### 2.1 Commutation Topology

The model assumes trapezoidal 6-step commutation with synchronous complementary PWM:

- At any instant, **2 half-bridges** carry the full phase current I.
- In the PWM leg, the high-side FET conducts for D·T and the low-side FET (synchronous rectification) conducts for (1−D)·T.
- The second leg's low-side FET is held on.
- Each FET is active for 120° electrical (1/3 of the electrical period).

### 2.2 Conduction Loss

Hot resistance:

$$R_{hot} = R_{25}\cdot k_T = 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.5 = 2.25\,\text{m}\Omega$$

Conduction loss in the PWM leg. The sum of the high-side and synchronous low-side terms is independent of D:

$$P_{cond,PWM} = I^2 R_{hot} D + I^2 R_{hot}(1-D) = I^2 R_{hot}$$

Loss in the static low-side leg:

$$P_{cond,static} = I^2 R_{hot}$$

Total per motor:

$$P_{cond} = 2 I^2 R_{hot} = 2 \times 6400 \times 2.25\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{28.8\ W}$$

**Ripple correction.** Worst case is at D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L_{LL} f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\times10^{-6} \times 48\times10^{3}} = 6.56\,\text{A}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.6$$

The ripple term adds about 0.06%, so it is negligible at 48 kHz. This is one argument for 48 kHz over 24 kHz, where ΔI doubles.

### 2.3 Switching Loss (Hard-Switched Leg Only)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\times10^{-9} \times 48\times10^{3} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 2.4 Output Capacitance Loss

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\times10^{-9}\times 25.2 \times 48\times10^{3} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}$$

### 2.5 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM period:

$$P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\times10^{-9} \times 48\times10^{3} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

Reverse recovery Q_rr adds roughly 0.1–0.3 W. That falls within the model uncertainty, so it is lumped in here.

### 2.6 Gate Drive (Driver/LDO Dissipation)

Two FETs switch each PWM cycle:

$$P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 30\times10^{-9}\times 10 \times 48\times10^{3} = \mathbf{0.029\ W}$$
