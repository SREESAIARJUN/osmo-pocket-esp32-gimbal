### Architectural correction

ArduPilot runs on the H743 flight controller. It does not run the ESC power stage. The 48 kHz PWM and commutation are generated by four separate ESC MCUs running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 only sends and receives bidirectional DShot600 on four timer channels. The model below is split along that boundary.

### Feasibility warning (Section 3 quantifies this)

80 A per channel on a 20×20 mm board is a burst-only rating. It lasts seconds, not tens of seconds. The physics yields roughly 20 A continuous per channel in propwash on a 20×20 mm board. Treat "80 A" as a marketing burst number and design the firmware current limits around it.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in six-step synchronous PWM

At any instant, one phase is driven high and another low. With complementary PWM at duty D:

- **On-state:** current flows through the high-side FET of phase A and the low-side FET of phase B (two FETs in series).
- **Freewheel:** the high-side turns off and the phase-A low-side turns on (synchronous rectification). Current then flows through the two low-sides, which is still two FETs in series.

The bridge therefore always presents **2 × R_ds(on)** in the current path, independent of D.

### 1.2 Equations

$$R_{ds,hot} = k_T \cdot R_{ds(on),25} = 1.5 \times 0.70\,\text{m}\Omega = 1.05\,\text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph}^2 \, R_{ds,hot}$$

Per-FET distribution, with each phase leg active for 120° (1/3) of the electrical period:

$$P_{HS} = \frac{1}{3} I^2 R_{ds,hot}\, D \qquad P_{LS} = \frac{1}{3} I^2 R_{ds,hot}\,(2 - D)$$

The low-side carries more because it conducts during its own 120° interval and also during freewheel. Low-side FETs run hotter at low throttle.

PCB copper (phase bus plus battery bus) adds its own loss. Assume about 0.5 mΩ effective per channel path for 2 oz inner/outer layers on a nano board:

$$P_{Cu,ch} = I^2 R_{Cu}$$

### 1.3 Results at 80 A burst

| Term | Equation | Value per channel |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 2 × 80² × 1.05 mΩ | **13.44 W** |
| Copper conduction | 80² × 0.5 mΩ | **3.20 W** |

Battery current relates to phase current through the duty cycle: I_batt ≈ D · I_ph. At D = 0.9 that is about 72 A from the pack per motor, or about 290 A total at a full four-motor burst. Check your XT60/XT90, pigtail, and pack C-rating against this.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

With complementary PWM, only the PWM-ing high-side is hard-switched. The synchronous low-side turns on after the dead time with its body diode already conducting, so it is zero-voltage switched.

### 2.1 Hard-switching overlap (high-side)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 35\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.69\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (low-side body diode, dumped into the high-side)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$

At 80 A, Q_rr scales up. Use the datasheet value at the actual di/dt; it can reach 2–3× this.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 60\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction (low-side)

$$P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dead} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

This term is significant. Cutting dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns saves 0.3 W per channel. Tune it in AM32 if the driver propagation-delay mismatch allows.

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in driver and gate resistors, not the FETs)

$$P_{gate} = 2\, Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2 \times 80\,\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.077\ W}$$

### 2.6 Switching loss totals

| Term | W per channel |
|---|---|
| Hard-switch overlap | 1.69 |
| Q_rr | 0.06 |
| Q_oss | 0.04 |
| Dead time | 0.61 |
| Gate (driver) | 0.08 |
| **Switching subtotal** | **2.48** |

Frequency trade-off: at 24 kHz the switching subtotal halves to about 1.24 W. Current ripple rises, though, which matters for low-inductance racing motors. 48 kHz is a reasonable choice for low-L motors on 6S.
