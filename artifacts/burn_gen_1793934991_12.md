# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #25): Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Budget, Bi-directional DShot600 Timer/DMA Plan, and Reference Netlist

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design, the H743 is the flight controller. Each ESC channel has its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 sends DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry back over the same wire. This document models that architecture. The "48 kHz" figure is therefore the ESC MCUs' PWM frequency, not an H743 timer.

**2. 80 A per channel on a nano board is a burst rating, not a continuous one.** Section 3 shows it is thermally limited to roughly 1–3 seconds depending on board size. Treat "80 A burst" as a 1–2 s rating.

**3. Assumed design parameters.** Every result below scales directly with these. Substitute your actual datasheet values.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path
In 6-step trapezoidal drive, two phases conduct at a time. With synchronous rectification, the current always flows through exactly two MOSFET channels:
- the high side of phase A (or its complementary low side during off-time), and
- the low side of phase B.

There is no diode-only freewheel interval apart from dead time.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.6 mΩ × 1.45 = **2.32 mΩ**.

### 1.3 RMS current including PWM ripple
The peak-to-peak ripple, which is worst at duty D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I=\frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}=\frac{25.2\times0.25}{20\,\mu\text{H}\times48\,\text{kHz}}=6.56\ \text{A}_{pp}$$

$$I_{rms}^2=I^2+\frac{\Delta I^2}{12}=6400+3.6=6403.6\ \text{A}^2$$

The ripple contribution is negligible (0.06 %).

### 1.4 Conduction loss per ESC channel

$$P_{cond}=I_{rms}^2\cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)=6403.6\times4.64\,\text{m}\Omega=\mathbf{29.7\ W}$$

Per-FET split: the PWM leg's high-side and low-side FETs share their ~14.9 W in proportion to D and 1−D. The fixed low-side FET carries the full 14.9 W for its 120° conduction interval. Averaged over a commutation cycle, each of the 6 FETs dissipates about 29.7/3 ≈ 9.9 W.

The worst instantaneous case is a single FET at **~14.9 W** conduction. This value drives the junction calculation in Section 3.

### 1.5 Board-level total
All four channels at 80 A: 4 × 29.7 = **118.8 W** of conduction loss.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per channel, only one leg is PWM-switching at any time. With synchronous rectification, the high-side FET hard-switches. The low-side FET switches near zero voltage because its body diode clamps during dead time.

**(a) V–I overlap (hard switching, high side)**

$$t_r\approx t_f\approx\frac{Q_{sw}}{I_g}=10\ \text{ns}$$

$$P_{VI}=\tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}=\tfrac12(25.2)(80)(20\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{k})=\mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two edges per cycle)

$$P_{dt}=V_f\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw}=0.8\times80\times200\,\text{ns}\times48\,\text{k}=\mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**(c) Reverse recovery** (low-side diode, at high-side turn-on)

$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}=30\,\text{nC}\times25.2\times48\,\text{k}=\mathbf{0.036\ W}$$

**(d) C_oss charge/discharge** (both FETs in the leg)

$$P_{oss}=2\cdot\tfrac12 C_{oss,eq}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw}=1.2\,\text{nF}\times635\times48\,\text{k}=\mathbf{0.037\ W}$$
