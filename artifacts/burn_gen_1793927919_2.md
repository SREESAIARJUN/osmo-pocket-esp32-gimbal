# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC: Engineering Model (Job #64)

**Document class:** Reference design. All component values are engineering estimates. Verify every figure against current datasheets and bench measurements before release.

## 0. Architectural Clarification

Before the math, the partitioning needs to be clear, because the request mixes two separate processor domains:

| Function | Executes on | Firmware |
|---|---|---|
| Attitude control, mixer, DShot600 generation, bidirectional eRPM decode | **STM32H743** (FC) | ArduPilot |
| 48 kHz commutation PWM, back-EMF zero-cross detection, MOSFET gate control | **4× ESC MCUs** (e.g. AT32F421 / STM32G071) | AM32 or BLHeli_32 |

The H743 never generates the 48 kHz bridge PWM. The thermal and switching models apply to the ESC board. The DMA model applies to the FC.

**Form-factor warning.** A true 20×20 mm "nano" stack cannot sustain 4×80 A. The copper cross-section and MOSFET footprint don't fit. This design assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm ESC** (5×6 mm PDFN FETs) paired with a 20×20 or 30.5 mm FC. The 80 A figure is treated as a **burst rating of seconds**, not continuous. Section 3 quantifies this.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Commutation topology

AM32 and BLHeli_32 drive 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM. At any instant, motor current flows through **exactly two conducting devices** in series:
- one high-side or its complementary low-side on the PWM leg, and
- one static low-side on the return leg.

Duty cycle D splits the PWM-leg conduction between its HS (fraction D) and LS (fraction 1−D). The sum is constant, so total conduction loss is independent of D.

### 2.2 Conduction loss

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)] = 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega\,(1+0.0045\cdot 85) = 1.38\,\text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j) = 80^2 \cdot 2.76\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{17.7\ W}
$$

$$
P_{pcb} = I^2 R_{pcb} = 6400 \cdot 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.2\ W}
$$

Ripple correction: with phase-current ripple ΔI, use $I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \Delta I^2/12$. With typical 5″–7″ motor inductance (~20–50 µH) at 48 kHz, ripple is a few amps. The correction is under 1 % and is neglected.

### 2.3 Switching loss (hard-switched HS turn-on and turn-off)

Transition time is dominated by the Miller plateau:

$$
t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{drv,src}} = \frac{10\,\text{nC}}{1.0\,\text{A}} \approx 10\,\text{ns}
$$

$$
t_{f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{drv,snk}} \approx 7\,\text{ns}
$$

In layout, plan for about 15 ns each, which accounts for gate resistance and common-source inductance.

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot30\,\text{ns}\cdot48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}
$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two dead-times per period)

$$
P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot600\,\text{ns}\cdot48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.84\ W}
$$

This is larger than the switching loss. Cutting dead time to 150 ns, if the gate driver and FET permit, saves about 0.9 W per channel.

### 2.5 Reverse recovery, C_oss and gate charge

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\,\text{nC}\cdot25.2\cdot48\,\text{k} = 0.06\,\text{W}
$$

$$
P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 C_{oss,eff} V^2 f = 0.5\cdot1.5\,\text{nF}\cdot635\cdot48\,\text{k} = 0.02\,\text{W}
$$

$$
P_{gate} = 2\cdot Q_g V_{gs} f = 2\cdot60\,\text{nC}\cdot10\cdot48\,\text{k} = 0.06\,\text{W}
$$

Gate-drive loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET.
