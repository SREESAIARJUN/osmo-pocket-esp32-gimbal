## 0. Architecture Clarification

The STM32H743 is the **flight controller** MCU. It does not run the motor commutation.

- A 4-in-1 ESC at 48 kHz PWM uses **four dedicated ESC MCUs**, typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 (or BLHeli_32-class firmware).
- The H743 generates **DShot600 commands** and receives **bidirectional eRPM telemetry**.
- The ESC MCUs generate the **48 kHz complementary PWM** for the gate drivers.

```
LiPo 6S ──┬── Bulk caps ── 4× (3-phase bridge, 6 FETs) ── Motors
          │                     ▲ gate driver ▲
          │              ESC MCU ×4 (AM32, 48 kHz PWM)
          │                     ▲ DShot600 bidir (1 wire each)
          └── 5V/10V BECs ── STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ── IMU/Baro/OSD/Flash
```

### ⚠️ Feasibility flag (read first)

"80 A burst" **per motor** means up to **~320 A of battery current** at full duty. On a nano (20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm) board, that is far beyond what the copper, the FET packages, and the battery lead can sustain for more than about 1 s (see Section 3).

- I model 80 A per motor as specified, and derive the allowable burst duration.
- If the requirement is actually **80 A total**, divide all I² terms by 16. The design becomes comfortable.
- **Confirm this with the customer before layout.**

## 2. Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

With 6-step synchronous rectification, two FETs are always in the current path:

- the PWM leg (high-side during D, its complementary low-side during 1 − D), and
- the low-side of the return leg, which is fully on.

The current therefore always sees 2 · R_ds(on):

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2 \cdot R_{ds(on)} \cdot k_T$$

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.5 = 6400 \times 3.6\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{23.0\ W}$$

Correction terms:

- **PWM ripple:** I²_rms = I²_dc + ΔI²/12. With motor phase inductance ~10 µH at 48 kHz, ΔI ≈ 10–20 A pk-pk, so the term adds < 1 %. Ignored.
- **PCB/busbar copper:** R_cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ per path (2 oz, short pours) gives 80² × 0.3 m ≈ **1.9 W**. This is not negligible and is tracked separately.

### 2.2 Switching losses (hard-switched, inductive load)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two transitions per period)

$$P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

Cutting dead time to 100 ns halves this term. Do so only after verifying shoot-through margin on the scope.
