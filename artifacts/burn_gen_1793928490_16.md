## 0. Feasibility Findings (Read First)

Three conclusions from the analysis below affect product definition. They should be resolved before schematic capture.

1. **The H743 does not switch the motors.** The 48 kHz PWM is generated by four dedicated ESC MCUs running AM32 or BLHeli_32, one per motor. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to them over bidirectional DShot600. The 48 kHz figure is an ESC-side parameter, and the loss model treats it that way.

2. **"80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) footprint is thermally unsustainable beyond about 1–2 s.**
   - The model shows about 28 W of dissipation per ESC channel, or about 112 W total with all four motors at burst.
   - The board's steady-state thermal resistance to air is about 15 K/W.
   - **Recommendation:** rate the product at 80 A burst for ≤1 s per channel, with a 35–45 A continuous rating. Alternatively, move to a 25.5×25.5 or 30.5×30.5 mm footprint.
   - Marketing must not publish "80 A" without a stated duration.

3. **Component density is the real constraint.** A 20×20 board must hold the H743 (TFBGA100, 8×8 mm), 4 ESC MCUs, 4 gate drivers, and 12 power MOSFETs. Two consequences follow:
   - A two-board stack (FC over ESC) is strongly preferred.
   - A single-board AIO forces the FC section onto the bottom side, directly under the heat source.
