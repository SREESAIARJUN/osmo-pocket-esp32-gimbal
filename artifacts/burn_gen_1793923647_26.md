# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model

**Scope:** 80 A burst conduction losses, 48 kHz switching losses, thermal model, ArduPilot bi-directional DShot600 timer and DMA allocation, and reference netlists.

**Status:** Engineering model. Component values are assumptions until datasheets are frozen and verified on the bench.

## 0. Design Assumptions and Architecture

| Parameter | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max (22.2 V nominal) | Worst case taken at 25.2 V |
| Burst current | 80 A per motor (phase RMS ≈ DC-link-equivalent in 6-step) | Burst, not continuous |
| PWM | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous rectification) | AM32/BLHeli_32-class firmware |

The H743 never drives the bridges directly. It emits DShot600 to four ESC MCUs and receives eRPM telemetry on the same wire.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

| T_j | R_DS(on) |
|---|---|
| 25 °C | 0.90 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 1.27 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 1.40 mΩ |

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

At any instant, motor current flows through exactly two FETs:

- **ON interval (duty D):** HS of phase A and LS of phase B.
- **OFF interval (1 − D):** LS of phase A (sync rect) and LS of phase B.

Therefore the per-motor conduction loss is independent of D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 \cdot 2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

**At 80 A, T_j = 100 °C:**
$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.27\,\text{m}\Omega = 16.3\ \text{W}$$

The worst single FET is the LS of the "return" phase. It conducts 100 % of the time during its 120° commutation window, so its instantaneous loss is:
$$P_{FET,peak} = 80^2 \times 1.27\,\text{m}\Omega = 8.1\ \text{W}$$

Each FET conducts about 1/3 of the electrical cycle. Its average loss is therefore about 2.7 W, but its die sees 8.1 W pulses.

### 1.3 Copper and shunt losses (per motor)

| Element | R | Loss @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase pad + 2 oz trace/plane (≈ 0.15 mΩ per path × 2) | 0.30 mΩ | 1.9 W |
| Battery plane share (common, ÷4 per motor) | — | (see below) |
| Shunt (if per-ESC sensing), 0.25 mΩ | 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |

The common battery input at 320 A burst (4 × 80 A) through about 0.1 mΩ of pad and plane gives P = 320² × 0.1 mΩ ≈ 10 W.

> ⚠️ **320 A aggregate burst through XT60 + 12 AWG leads is outside connector ratings.** In practice all four motors rarely peak at the same time. Spec the battery path for 150–200 A burst and enforce the limit in firmware (`BATT_CURR_LIM` / ESC current limit).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the **hard-switched HS FET** of the active phase incurs significant V·I overlap loss. The sync-rect LS switches near-ZVS, apart from dead-time and Q_rr.

### 2.1 V·I overlap

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I \,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}$$

Rise and fall times are set by the gate drive (Miller plateau). With Q_gd ≈ 12 nC, V_plateau ≈ 4.5 V, and drive (12 V − 4.5 V)/(4.7 Ω + R_drv≈3 Ω) ≈ 1 A:

$$t_r \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g} \approx 12\ \text{ns}$$

$$P_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 24\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 1.16\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 2 \times 100\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

At high di/dt, Q_rr grows. Budget 2× margin, which gives 0.12 W.

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss,eq} V^2 f = 0.5 \times 1.2\text{nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = 0.018\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

The power is mostly dissipated in R_g and the driver, not the FET:
$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f \times 2\ \text{(complementary pair)} = 55\text{nC}\times 10 \times 48\text{k}\times 2 = 0.053\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Per-motor loss summary @ 80 A, 25.2 V, 48 kHz, T_j = 100 °C

| Loss | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (2 FETs) | 16.3 |
| Switching overlap | 1.16 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Q_rr (2× margin) | 0.12 |
| C_oss + gate | 0.07 |
| Phase copper | 1.9 |
| **Total per motor** | **≈ 20.2 W** |
| **4 motors** | **≈ 81 W** (+ ~10 W battery path) |

**Efficiency check:** P_out ≈ 25.2 V × 80 A × D_eff. Losses are about 1 % of input per motor at full duty, but they are all concentrated in roughly 4 cm².

**Frequency sensitivity:** switching-related loss is ≈ 1.95 W at 48 kHz versus ≈ 0.98 W at 24 kHz. At 80 A, conduction dominates (≈ 81 %). Raising f_sw is cheap; R_DS(on) is what matters.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network (per FET, Cauer-reduced to Foster for the calc)

```
P_FET ─► [Zth j-c: Foster 3-term] ─► Tc ─► [R pad-plane] ─► T_board ─► [R board-air] ─► T_amb
                                                         └── C_board (shared, all 24 FETs)
```

| Element | Value |
|---|---|
| Zth(j-c) Foster: r₁=0.05 K/W τ₁=50 µs; r₂=0.25 K/W τ₂=1 ms; r₃=0.6 K/W τ₃=10 ms | R_thJC ≈ 0.9 K/W |
| R(case→board), 5×6 pad + thermal vias (≥ 12 × 0.3 mm) | ≈ 3 K/W per FET |
| Board heat capacity: ~6 g (Cu ≈ 2.5 g @ 0.385 J/gK, FR4 ≈ 3.5 g @ 1.1 J/gK) + FET bodies | **C_b ≈ 5 J/K** |
| R(board→air), prop-wash forced convection, 36 × 36 mm both sides | **R_ba ≈ 6–8 K/W** |
