## 1. Power-Stage Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 1.1 Conduction losses

In trapezoidal six-step drive, two phases conduct during each 60° sector:
- **Phase A (PWM phase):** the high-side FET conducts for D, and the low-side FET synchronously rectifies for (1 − D).
- **Phase B (return phase):** the low-side FET is held on for 100 % of the sector.

So at any instant, the current flows through two FET channels in series:

$$
P_{cond} = I_b^2 R_{hot}\big[D + (1-D)\big] + I_b^2 R_{hot}(1) = 2\,I_b^2 R_{hot}
$$

The result does not depend on D. This is a property of synchronous rectification.

Temperature-corrected resistance:

$$
R_{hot} = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)] \approx 0.70\ \text{m}\Omega \times 1.5 = 1.05\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.05\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{13.44\ W}
$$

**Hottest individual FET.** Each low-side FET is the held-on return path for 1/3 of each electrical cycle, and the synchronous-rectifier path for part of another 1/3. The peak instantaneous dissipation in one device is:

$$
P_{FET,peak} = I_b^2 R_{hot} = 6.72\ \text{W}
$$

Over a full electrical cycle, the dissipation averages across the 6 FETs to about 13.44 / 6 ≈ 2.24 W each. For thermal design, use the per-device average. At motor eRPM, the commutation period is far shorter than the FET thermal time constant (~ms).

**PCB copper conduction.** With 2 oz copper, a 25 mm path, 6 mm effective width, and two layers in parallel, the estimate is:

$$
R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{w t} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot 0.025}{0.006\cdot 70\times10^{-6}\cdot 2} \approx 0.51\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

Hot copper (+40 %) gives 0.72 mΩ, so:

$$
P_{Cu} \approx 80^2 \times 0.72\times10^{-3} \approx \mathbf{4.6\ W}\ \text{per motor path}
$$

Treat this as an upper bound. It depends heavily on layout. A current-sense shunt, if fitted (typically 0.25–0.5 mΩ on the shared ground return), adds:

$$
P_{shunt} = I_{total}^2 R_{shunt}
$$

At 320 A × 0.25 mΩ that is **25.6 W**. **Recommendation:** on an 80 A-burst nano design, sense current with Hall/magnetic sensing or use a 0.1 mΩ shunt, and accept the resolution penalty.

### 1.2 Switching losses (hard-switched high-side FET)

**Turn-on/turn-off V–I overlap:**

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_b (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.935\ W}
$$

**Body-diode reverse recovery** (low-side diode recovers when the high side turns on):

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}
$$

**Output capacitance discharge:**

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{ nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.023\ W}
$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead-time intervals per period):

$$
P_{dt} = V_F I_b (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.614\ W}
$$

**Gate drive** (all 6 FETs switching, assessed conservatively). This loss lands in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FETs:

$$
P_{gate} = 6\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 6 \times 50\text{ nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.144\ W}
$$

In six-step drive only 1–2 legs actually switch, so the realistic value is about 0.05 W.

### 1.3 Per-channel and board totals

| Loss term | Per motor (W) | ×4 motors (W) |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 13.44 | 53.76 |
| Switching overlap | 1.94 | 7.74 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 | 2.46 |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.08 | 0.33 |
| Gate drive | 0.14 | 0.58 |
| **FET subtotal** | **16.21** | **64.9** |
| Copper (estimate) | 4.6 | 18.4 |
| **Total power stage** | **~20.8** | **~83 W** |

Plus about 1.5 W of logic: H743 ≈ 0.6 W at 480 MHz, 4× ESC MCUs, regulators.

**Efficiency at burst.** Output power is roughly 4 × 80 A × 22.2 V × D_eff. At full throttle that is about 7.1 kW electrical in, so losses are about 1.2 %. This is efficient, but the absolute heat is what kills a nano board.

**Frequency sensitivity:**

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial f_{sw}} \approx \frac{1.935+0.614+0.083}{48\text{ k}} = 54.8\ \mu\text{W/Hz per channel}
$$

Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.3 W per channel (5.3 W per board). Rising to 96 kHz costs about 2.6 W per channel. At 80 A, conduction loss dominates, so 48 kHz is a reasonable choice.
