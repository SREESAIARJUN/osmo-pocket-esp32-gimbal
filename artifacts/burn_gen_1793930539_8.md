## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

**Partitioning.** The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. It runs ArduPilot (flight controller side) and sends bi-directional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs. Each ESC MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or BLHeli_32-class firmware) generates the 48 kHz six-step PWM and returns eRPM telemetry. All loss math below is per ESC channel and then aggregated.

**Baseline assumptions.** Replace these with your datasheet values; every equation is parametric.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage | V_bus | 25.2 V | 6S, full charge |
| Burst phase current | I_ph | 80 A | Per channel, DC-equivalent during conduction |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz | |
| MOSFET | — | 40 V, 3.3×3.3 DFN class | 6 per channel, 24 total |

> ⚠️ **Feasibility flags (read first):**
> 1. Four channels × 80 A gives about 256 A of battery current at D ≈ 0.8. On a nano (20×20 or 30×30) board, this is a **seconds-scale burst rating only**. It is not continuous.
> 2. A 40 V FET on 6S leaves about 15 V of margin for inductive overshoot. A low-ESR bulk capacitor at the pads and snubber/layout discipline are mandatory.
> 3. The netlist below is a **reference topology**. Pin numbers must be verified against the exact datasheet revisions before layout.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C, R_DS = 1.0 × 1.75 = **1.75 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-step conduction with synchronous rectification

During any commutation sector, three devices carry I_ph:

- **High-side FET, PWM leg:** conducts for fraction D.
- **Low-side FET, same leg:** complementary, conducts for (1−D).
- **Low-side FET, return leg:** fully on.

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}$$

This result is **independent of D**, which is a useful property of synchronous six-step drive.

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.75\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{22.4\ W\ per\ channel}\quad (T_j = 125\,°C)$$

At T_j = 25 °C, the same expression gives 12.8 W. This is why the thermal model must be solved iteratively (§3.3).

### 1.3 Per-device averaging

The electrical frequency is roughly 300 Hz–2 kHz. The die thermal time constant is on the order of milliseconds, so each FET sees approximately the time-averaged share:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} = \frac{P_{cond,motor}}{6} = 3.73\ W$$

A ripple term exists at low eRPM. At startup or stall, one FET can hold the full I²R for an entire sector. Use the Z_θJC(t) curve at t = 1/(6·f_e).

### 1.4 Copper and interconnect losses (frequently omitted)

| Path | Estimated R | Loss at burst |
|---|---|---|
| Phase pad → FET node, per channel | 0.3 mΩ | 80² × 0.3 m = 1.9 W |
| Battery pads → bus plane (shared) | 0.2 mΩ | 256² × 0.2 m = **13.1 W** |
| Shunt (if fitted) | 0.25 mΩ | 256² × 0.25 m = 16.4 W, so use per-channel shunts or hall sensing |

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

The high-side PWM FET is the hard-switched device.

### 2.1 V–I overlap

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\times25.2\times80\times27\text{ ns}\times48\text{ kHz} = 1.31\ W$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.024\ W$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery of the low-side body diode

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.060\ W$$

Most of this loss is dissipated in the high-side FET. Q_rr rises with di/dt and T_j.

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction (low side)

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\,I_{ph}\cdot 2t_{dt}\cdot f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{ ns}\times48\text{ kHz} = 0.61\ W$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

Most of this power is dissipated in the driver and R_g, not in the FET.

$$P_g = Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 0.029\ W \text{ per switching FET}$$

### 2.6 Switching summary per channel

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| P_VI | 1.31 |
| P_oss | 0.02 |
| P_rr | 0.06 |
| P_dt | 0.61 |
| P_g (×2 FETs) | 0.06 |
| **Total** | **≈ 2.06 W** |
