# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage, Thermal, and DShot DMA Architecture

## 0. Feasibility Finding

The first-order math shows that **80 A burst per channel is not thermally survivable on a nano (20×20 mm) 4-in-1 stage**. The model below proves this and then proposes a defensible rating. The derivations are general, so you can substitute your actual MOSFET datasheet values.

**Architecture assumptions (correct these if they differ from Job #36):**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max (22.2 V nominal) |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal, synchronous complementary PWM (AM32/BLHeli_32 class) |
| PWM frequency | f_sw = 48 kHz |
| ESC MCUs | 4× AT32F421 (one per channel) running AM32. The H743 is the flight controller only. |
| Gate driver | 3× half-bridge drivers per channel (FD6288Q-class, 3-phase), 10 V gate drive |
| MOSFET (placeholder) | 40 V N-ch, DFN3.3×3. R_ds(on),25 = 1.3 mΩ; Q_g = 45 nC; Q_rr = 40 nC; Q_oss = 60 nC; t_r = t_f = 15 ns at the chosen R_g; V_SD = 0.8 V; R_θJC = 1.2 K/W |
| Dead time | t_dt = 50 ns per edge |
| FC/ESC form factor | 20×20 mm, M2 mounts, 8-layer 2 oz |

> A 40 V FET on 6S leaves only about 37% margin over 25.2 V. Ringing at 80 A di/dt will exceed this. **Use 60 V FETs for 6S production**, and accept roughly 1.6–2× the R_ds(on) for the same package. The numbers below are therefore optimistic.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path
In 6-step synchronous mode, each 60° sector has one phase PWM-ing (high-side HS and low-side LS alternate) and one phase's LS held fully on. At any instant, **exactly two FETs carry the phase current I_ph**:

- During the D interval: HS(A) + LS(B)
- During the (1−D) interval: LS(A) + LS(B), with synchronous freewheeling

So the instantaneous conduction loss per channel is always:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Each FET conducts over 120° of the 360° electrical period, so the average per FET is:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6}\;\text{(averaged over the 6 FETs)} = \frac{I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}}{3}$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence
$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\text{–}0.006\ /\text{K}$$

At T_j = 125 °C with α = 0.005: R_ds ≈ 1.3 × 1.5 = **1.95 mΩ**. Use 2.0 mΩ.

Because R_ds rises with temperature, conduction loss feeds back on itself and can run away thermally.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A burst

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.0\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{25.6\ W}$$
$$P_{cond,FET} \approx 8.5\text{ W instantaneous on the 2 active FETs; } 4.27\text{ W average}$$

### 1.4 PCB copper loss (often ignored, but significant at this current)
Phase and bus copper on a 20 mm board: estimate about 0.5 mΩ in series per current path (2 oz, via arrays, pads).

$$P_{Cu,ch} \approx 80^2 \times 0.5\text{ m}\Omega = 3.2\text{ W}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing HS FET hard-switches. Its complementary LS turns on into its own conducting body diode (soft, ZVS-like) but incurs reverse recovery.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V-I overlap
$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I_{ph}\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}$$
$$= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 40\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{ k} = 0.048\text{ W}$$

Q_rr grows with di/dt and T_j. Budget 2–3× this value, about 0.12 W.

### 2.3 Output capacitance
$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2}\,Q_{oss}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 60\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{ k} = 0.036\text{ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction
$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\,I_{ph}\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ k} = 0.31\text{ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate charge
This loss is dissipated mostly in R_g and the driver, not the FET:
$$P_g = Q_g\,V_{GS}\,f_{sw} = 45\text{ nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{ k} = 0.022\text{ W per switching FET}$$

### 2.6 Total switching loss per channel
$$P_{sw,ch} \approx 1.45 + 0.12 + 0.04 + 0.31 + 0.02 \approx \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.7 Frequency sensitivity

| f_sw | P_sw,ch | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| 24 kHz | 0.97 W | Higher motor ripple and audible noise |
| 48 kHz | 1.94 W | Specified |
| 96 kHz | 3.9 W | Not justified |
