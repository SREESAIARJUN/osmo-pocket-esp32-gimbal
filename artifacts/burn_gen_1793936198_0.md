# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model & Allocation Report

**Architecture baseline:** The STM32H743 is the flight controller. Each motor is commutated by its own ESC MCU (AT32F421 running AM32), because the H743 cannot practically commutate four BLDC motors while also running ArduPilot. The H743 talks to the ESC MCUs over bi-directional DShot600.

> **Feasibility warning:** An 80 A per-motor burst on a "nano" footprint (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) is at the edge of physical feasibility. The model below shows the burst is limited to about **1 s**, not the 5–10 s implied by typical marketing ratings. All component values are **representative assumptions** that you must replace with your BOM datasheet values.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

**Current path.** In 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM, two FETs always carry the phase current:

- **PWM on:** the high-side FET of phase A and the low-side FET of phase B conduct.
- **PWM off:** the low-side FET of phase A (synchronous rectifier) and the low-side FET of phase B conduct.

The bridge conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

**Temperature dependence.** R_DS(on) rises with junction temperature:

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

Because R_DS(on) rises with temperature, conduction loss feeds back on itself. Iterate until the result converges, and check for thermal runaway:

$$\frac{\partial P_{cond}}{\partial T_j} \cdot R_{th,ja} < 1$$

**At 80 A:**

| Junction temperature | R_DS(on) | P_cond per ESC channel |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.2 mΩ | 2 × 6400 × 0.0012 = **15.4 W** |
| 125 °C | 1.9 mΩ | 2 × 6400 × 0.0019 = **24.3 W** |

At 125 °C, the hottest FET carries most of this, about **12.2 W**.

**Copper loss.** Phase and bus copper adds to the FET loss. For a 2 oz (70 µm) trace that is 4 mm wide and 15 mm long:

$$R = \rho L / (w t) = 1.72\times10^{-8} \times 0.015 / (0.004 \times 70\times10^{-6}) \approx 0.92\ \text{m}\Omega$$

This is comparable to the FET resistance. Stacking the phase copper on 4 parallel layers brings it to about 0.23 mΩ, which adds **≈ 1.5 W per channel at 80 A**.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

The loss terms below are per ESC channel. Only the PWM-ed half-bridge switches hard.

### 3.1 V–I overlap loss

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I\,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = 0.97\ \text{W}$$

### 3.2 Reverse-recovery loss

$$P_{rr} = V_{bus}\, Q_{rr}\, f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 40\text{ nC} \times 48\text{ kHz} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 3.3 Output-capacitance loss (both FETs of the switching leg)

$$P_{oss} = 2 \times \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1\text{ nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{ kHz} = 0.030\ \text{W}$$

### 3.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I \,(2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

### 3.5 Gate drive loss

This loss is dissipated in the driver, not the FET:

$$P_g = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} \times N_{switching} = 40\text{ nC} \times 10\text{ V} \times 48\text{ kHz} \times 2 \approx 0.04\ \text{W}$$

### 3.6 Loss budget at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

| Term | Per ESC channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 24.3 W | 97.2 W |
| Copper | 1.5 W | 6.0 W |
| V–I overlap | 0.97 W | 3.9 W |
| Dead time | 0.61 W | 2.4 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.12 W | 0.5 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 27.5 W** | **≈ 110 W** |

**Takeaways:**

- Conduction dominates, at about 90% of the total. Going from 24 kHz to 48 kHz costs only about +0.8 W per channel.
- Higher PWM frequency is therefore justified for current ripple and acoustic benefits.
- Lower R_DS(on) and thicker copper matter far more than switching speed.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Lumped thermal network

```
P_FET ──[R_jc 1.2 K/W]──[R_solder/via 0.6 K/W]── T_board (C_board) ──[R_ba]── T_amb
```

### 4.2 Board heat capacity (20×20 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz)

| Material | Calculation | Heat capacity |
|---|---|---|
| Copper | 4 cm² × 6 × 70 µm = 1.68×10⁻⁷ m³ → 1.5 g × 385 J/kg·K | 0.58 J/K |
| FR4 | ≈ 1.0 g × 1100 J/kg·K | 1.10 J/K |
| FETs and passives | — | ≈ 0.20 J/K |
| **C_board** | | **≈ 1.9 J/K** |

### 4.3 Burst duration (adiabatic, all 4 motors at 80 A)

$$\frac{dT_{board}}{dt} = \frac{P}{C} = \frac{110}{1.9} \approx 58\ \text{K/s}$$

The junction runs above the board by about 12.2 W × 1.8 K/W ≈ **22 K**. With T_j,max = 150 °C and a design limit of 135 °C, the board may reach about 113 °C.

$$t_{burst} = \frac{(113 - 45)}{58} \approx \mathbf{1.2\ s}$$

This assumes a 45 °C starting temperature and no airflow.

### 4.4 Continuous rating

With prop-wash convection of h ≈ 60 W/m²·K over both faces (8 cm²), R_ba ≈ 21 K/W. That supports only about **3–4 W continuous** at ΔT = 70 K.

This means a sustained current of about **15–18 A per motor** with all four motors loaded. The figure rises significantly with a heat spreader or a clamped frame.
