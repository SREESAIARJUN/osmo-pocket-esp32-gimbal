# High-Rate FOC and Sensor Fusion for a 3-Axis Gimbal (Job #1)

I don't have the hardware details of Job #1, so this design rests on the assumptions below. Every equation is written in terms of these parameters, so you can substitute your real values.

## 0. Assumptions and Loop Architecture

| Item | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Motors | Gimbal BLDC/PMSM, e.g. GBM4108 class, 11 pole pairs, 12N14P |
| Position sensor | 14-bit absolute magnetic encoder (AS5048/MA730 class) |
| IMUs | 2× ICM-42688-P on SPI, on the camera plate, mounted symmetrically |
| MCU | Cortex-M4F/M7 with single-precision FPU (STM32G4/H7 class) |
| Current sensing | Low-side or inline shunts, ADC triggered at PWM center |

The control loops form a rate hierarchy:

```
 20 kHz  ── FOC current loop (PI id/iq, SVPWM)         [PWM-center ISR]
  8 kHz  ── IMU sampling (both ICM-42688-P), notch/LPF  [IMU INT ISR]
  4 kHz  ── Velocity (rate) PI + cogging feedforward
  1 kHz  ── AHRS (Mahony on fused IMU) + angle P loop
```

Each loop's bandwidth is at least 4–5× below the loop it commands. The rate loop uses the camera-mounted fused gyro as its feedback. This is what rejects base disturbances. The encoder provides commutation and joint-limit and follow-mode information.

## 1. FOC Current Loop

### 1.1 Transforms

Clarke transform (amplitude-invariant, with i_a + i_b + i_c = 0):

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \frac{1}{\sqrt3}(i_a + 2 i_b)
$$

Park transform, where θ_e = p·θ_m − θ_offset:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix}=
\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}
$$

The inverse Park transform is the transpose of this matrix.

### 1.2 PMSM Plant in the dq Frame

$$
v_d = R i_d + L_d \dot i_d - \omega_e L_q i_q
$$

$$
v_q = R i_q + L_q \dot i_q + \omega_e L_d i_d + \omega_e \lambda_m
$$

$$
\tau = \tfrac32\, p\,\lambda_m\, i_q = K_t\, i_q \quad\text{(surface PM, } L_d = L_q = L\text{)}
$$

### 1.3 PI Gains by Pole–Zero Cancellation

The plant is $G(s) = 1/(Ls+R)$. Use a PI controller:

$$
C(s) = K_p + \frac{K_i}{s}
$$

Choose the gains as

$$
K_p = L\,\omega_c,\qquad K_i = R\,\omega_c.
$$

The PI zero then cancels the plant pole and the closed loop becomes $\omega_c/(s+\omega_c)$.

The current-loop bandwidth must satisfy $\omega_c \le 2\pi f_{PWM}/20$. At 20 kHz this is ≈ 6.3 krad/s, i.e. about 1 kHz.

Gimbal motors typically have R ≈ 5–15 Ω and L ≈ 2–5 mH. That gives an electrical pole R/L ≈ 3 krad/s.

### 1.4 Decoupling, Back-EMF Feedforward, and Voltage Limit

$$
v_d^* = PI_d(e_d) - \omega_e L\, i_q
$$

$$
v_q^* = PI_q(e_q) + \omega_e (L\, i_d + \lambda_m)
$$

The voltage vector is limited to the linear SVPWM circle, $|v| \le V_{bus}/\sqrt3$:

- d-axis has priority: $v_d$ is clamped to ±V_max.
- q-axis gets the remainder: $v_q$ is clamped to $\pm\sqrt{V_{max}^2 - v_d^2}$.

### 1.5 Anti-Windup by Back-Calculation (Tustin Discretization)

$$
u_k = K_p e_k + I_k
$$

$$
\bar u_k = \text{sat}(u_k)
$$

$$
I_{k+1} = I_k + K_i T_s\, e_k + K_{aw}(\bar u_k - u_k)
$$

A typical choice is $K_{aw} = K_i T_s / K_p$.
