## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-step trapezoidal with synchronous rectification

In any commutation sector, two phases conduct:
- Phase A is PWM'd. Current flows through HS_A during D·T and through LS_A (synchronous) during (1−D)·T.
- Phase B's low side LS_B is on continuously.

So the motor current always flows through exactly **two FETs in series**:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds,hot} + I_{rms}^2 \cdot R_{Cu}$$

At 80 A, phase current ripple is small at 48 kHz with typical motor inductance (≥15 µH), so I_rms ≈ I_avg = 80 A.

$$P_{FET,cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.36\,\text{m}\Omega = 17.4\ \text{W}$$

Copper path (bus planes, phase pads, solder joints) at R_Cu ≈ 0.4 mΩ:

$$P_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.4\,\text{m}\Omega = 2.6\ \text{W}$$

### 1.2 Per-FET distribution (worst device)

Each phase is the "PWM" phase for 1/3 of the electrical cycle, "continuous LS" for 1/3, and floating for 1/3.

$$P_{LS} = I^2 R\left[\tfrac{1}{3}(1-D) + \tfrac{1}{3}\right], \qquad P_{HS} = I^2 R\cdot\tfrac{1}{3}D$$

At D = 0.9, a typical burst at full throttle:

| Device | Expression | Result |
|---|---|---|
| LS FET | 8.70 W × (0.033 + 0.333) | 3.19 W |
| HS FET | 8.70 W × 0.30 | 2.61 W |

Here 8.70 W = 6400 × 1.36 mΩ. HS also carries switching loss (next section).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per motor, one half-bridge switches at any time. Using V = 25.2 V and I = 80 A:

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = 0.5·25.2·80·30 ns·48 k | **1.45 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k | **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 50 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.06 W |
| Coss | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw | 0.02 W |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver/R_g) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw = 2·60 nC·10·48 k | 0.06 W |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.2 W / motor** |

Notes:
- Overlap loss lands mostly in the HS FET of the PWM'd phase.
- Dead-time diode loss lands in the LS FET.

**Sensitivity.**
- Overlap loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 96 kHz it doubles to about 2.9 W.
- Dead time is the hidden cost. Cutting t_dt from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per motor.

### 2.1 Totals

| Scenario | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 17.4 W | 69.6 W |
| Copper | 2.6 W | 10.4 W |
| Switching | 2.2 W | 8.8 W |
| **Total** | **22.2 W** | **≈ 89 W** |

Efficiency check: at 22.2 V × 0.9 × 80 A ≈ 1.6 kW per motor, the 22 W loss is about 1.4%. That is efficient, but it is concentrated in about 9 cm².

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Steady state: not feasible at 80 A

The local thermal path per FET is:

| Segment | Value |
|---|---|
| R_θJC (die to pad) | 1.0 K/W |
| Solder + via array to inner planes (≥20 × 0.3 mm vias) | 3–5 K/W |
| Board to air, 30×30 mm, prop-wash convection h ≈ 50–80 W/m²K, both sides | **≈ 7–11 K/W for the whole board** |

With 89 W into a board with about 9 K/W to air, the steady-state rise would be roughly 800 K. 80 A on all four channels is **burst-only**, governed by thermal capacitance.

### 3.2 Transient (10 s burst)

**Lumped board heat capacity.**
- FR4: about 3.5 g × 0.9 J/gK ≈ 3.2 J/K.
- Copper: about 4 g × 0.385 ≈ 1.5 J/K.
- FETs, solder, and components: about 0.8 J/K.
- Total: **C_board ≈ 5.5 J/K**.
- Time constant: τ = R·C ≈ 9 × 5.5 ≈ 50 s, so a 10 s burst is nearly adiabatic.

$$\Delta T_{board}(t) = P\,R_{th}\left(1-e^{-t/\tau}\right)$$

| Case | P | ΔT after 10 s |
|---|---|---|
| All 4 motors at 80 A | 89 W | 89·9·(1−e^{−0.2}) ≈ **145 K** ❌ |
| 1 motor at 80 A, 3 at 30 A | 22.2 + 3·3.5 ≈ 33 W | ≈ **54 K** ✓ (marginal from a 40 °C start) |
| All 4 at 80 A for 2 s | 89 W | ≈ **31 K** ✓ |

**Junction above board (worst FET).** Using about 3.6 W at Z_θ ≈ 4 K/W gives about 15 K.

Constraint for T_j ≤ 150 °C:

$$T_{amb} + \Delta T_{board} + \Delta T_{jb} \le 150\,°\text{C}$$

**Conclusion.** "80 A burst" is credible as a **per-channel, ≤10 s** rating, or as a **4-channel, ≤2–3 s** rating. It is not credible as 4 × 80 A for 10 s. On a 20×20 "nano" footprint, C_board drops about 2.2×, so the burst time halves accordingly. Specify the rating in exactly these terms, or the product will fail in punch-outs.

**Mitigations, in order of impact:**
1. Use FETs with lower R_ds(on), such as 0.5 mΩ class in 3.3×3.3 mm dual-cooled or top-side-cool packages. Loss scales linearly.
2. Use 3 oz outer copper with a 4 oz bus.
3. Use top-side-cooling packages with a 0.5 mm aluminium spreader. This adds about 3 J/K per gram of Al × 0.9.
4. Implement firmware thermal foldback using the ESC NTC and an I²t limit (AM32 supports current limit).
