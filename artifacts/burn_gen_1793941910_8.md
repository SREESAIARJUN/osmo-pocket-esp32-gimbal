# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #65): Reference Design Package

## 0. Architecture Decisions and Design Risks

**The H743 cannot directly commutate four motors while running ArduPilot.** The H743 is the flight controller. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU, which receives bidirectional DShot600 from the H743. This is the standard AIO topology and the only one ArduPilot supports.

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──DShot600 bidir──► 4× ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32 or BLHeli_32)
                                              └─► 3-phase gate driver ──► 6× N-MOSFET ──► motor
```

**80 A burst per channel on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board is outside the envelope of any shipping product.** The math below shows why. I have modelled it rigorously so you can see exactly what must change. My recommendation is to either:

- re-spec to about 40–55 A burst per channel, or
- move to a 30.5×30.5 mm board with 3 oz outer copper and heavy-copper bus bars.

**All component parameters below are representative values, not quotations from a specific datasheet.** Each is marked with a † symbol. Replace them with values from your selected parts before tape-out.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS25}\left(\frac{T_j + 273}{298}\right)^{\kappa},\quad \kappa \approx 2.0\text{–}2.4\ (\text{use } 2.2)
$$

At T_j = 125 °C, the multiplier is (398/298)^2.2 ≈ 1.89. This gives R_DS ≈ 1.32 mΩ.

### 2.2 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

In each 60° sector, one phase is PWM-switched and one phase is held low. Current always flows through exactly two conducting channels:

| Phase leg | Device | Conducts for |
|---|---|---|
| Switched leg | High-side FET | Fraction D |
| Switched leg | Low-side FET (sync rect) | Fraction 1 − D |
| Static leg | Low-side FET | Fraction 1 |

The total conduction loss per ESC channel is therefore independent of duty cycle:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2 I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

The losses are distributed across the 6 FETs. Each FET is active for 2 of 6 sectors, so the average per FET is:

$$
\bar P_{cond,FET} = \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6}
$$

The worst-case instantaneous FET is the static low-side device, which carries I²R_DS for 120° electrical.

### 2.3 Copper losses

Model the phase and bus trace resistance as R_Cu. For a 2 oz copper trace (~0.25 mΩ/sq at 25 °C), the PCB path from battery pad through the bridge to the motor pad is typically 0.4–0.8 mΩ on a nano board. Copper resistance also rises with temperature (α_Cu = 0.0039/K):

$$
P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu}(1+\alpha_{Cu}\,\Delta T)
$$

### 2.4 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond,ch | Per FET (average) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 0.70 mΩ | 8.96 W | 1.49 W |
| 100 °C | 1.15 mΩ | 14.7 W | 2.45 W |
| 125 °C | 1.32 mΩ | 16.9 W | 2.82 W |

Copper loss with R_Cu = 0.6 mΩ at ΔT = 80 K: 6400 × 0.6 m × 1.31 ≈ **5.0 W per channel**.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. The high-side FET hard-switches, and the low-side sync FET switches near zero voltage (it conducts through its body diode during dead time).

| Loss component | Equation | Value at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap (hard switching) | ½·V_bus·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | ½·25.2·80·27 ns·48 k = **1.31 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V_bus·f_sw | 30 n·25.2·48 k = **0.036 W** |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss·V_bus·f_sw (both FETs in leg, approx.) | 35 n·25.2·48 k = **0.042 W** |
| Dead-time body diode | V_F·I·2t_dt·f_sw | 0.8·80·200 n·48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (dissipated mostly in driver and R_G) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw | 2·40 n·10·48 k = **0.038 W** |
| **Total switching loss per channel** | | **≈ 2.04 W** |

**Frequency sensitivity:** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz this drops to about 1.0 W. The trade-off is higher current ripple in low-inductance quad motors and more audible noise.

**Reducing dead-time loss:** shortening dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel. This is only safe if the measured t_d(off) plus driver propagation skew leaves margin against shoot-through.

## 4. Total Burst Loss Budget

Calculated per channel at 80 A, with T_j converged near 115 °C:

| Loss source | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 16.0 W | 64.0 W |
| Switching | 2.0 W | 8.2 W |
| PCB copper | 5.0 W | 20.0 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ†, if a per-channel shunt is used) | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| Gate driver, LDO, MCU | 0.2 W | 0.8 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 24.8 W** | **≈ 99 W** |

**About 100 W dissipated in roughly 6–10 cm³ of board.** This is why 80 A can only ever be a burst rating, never a continuous one.

## 5. Thermal Model

### 5.1 Network

```
P_FET ──► R_θJC (1.2 K/W) ──► T_case ──► R_θCB (solder/via, ~0.8 K/W) ──► T_board (lumped C_board)
                                                                               │
                                                     R_θBA (convection, prop-wash) ──► T_amb
```

### 5.2 Board thermal capacitance

For a 30×30 mm, 6-layer, 1.6 mm board with 2/1/1/1/1/2 oz copper:

| Element | Calculation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Cu mass | ≈ 9 cm² × 8 oz-equivalent × 0.0347 mm/oz × 8.96 g/cm³ | ≈ 2.2 g → 0.85 J/K |
| FR4 mass | ≈ 9 × 0.16 × 1.85 | ≈ 2.7 g → 3.0 J/K |
| Components and solder | | ≈ 1.0 J/K |
| **C_board** | | **≈ 4.9 J/K** |

For a 20×20 mm board, scale by area to roughly 2.2 J/K.

The convective path is R_θBA ≈ 3–6 K/W with prop-wash. Use 4 K/W.

### 5.3 Transient solution

**Board temperature (lumped):**

$$
T_b(t) = T_a + P_{tot} R_{\theta BA}\left(1-e^{-t/\tau}\right),\qquad \tau = R_{\theta BA}C_{board}
$$

**Junction temperature:**

$$
T_j(t) = T_b(t) + P_{FET,hot}\,\big(R_{\theta JC}+R_{\theta CB}\big)
$$

Here P_FET,hot is the static low-side FET's instantaneous loss: I²·R_DS ≈ 8.4 W during its 120° window. When the electrical period is much shorter than the FET thermal time constant, use the average of about 2.8 W plus the switching share.
