# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage & DShot Architecture (Job #67)

> **Scope and honesty notes before the math**
> 1. **Topology:** On every production 4-in-1 design, the STM32H743 is the **flight controller** MCU. It sends bidirectional DShot600 to **four dedicated ESC MCUs** (e.g. STM32G071 or AT32F421 running AM32/BLHeli_32). Those ESC MCUs generate the 48 kHz complementary PWM. An H743 cannot sensibly run 4× sensorless 6-step commutation plus the ArduPilot stack. The architecture below reflects this split.
> 2. **"80 A burst" on a nano board:** On a 20×20 mm board, 80 A per channel is not physically credible. The copper cross-section and thermal mass are insufficient. I model a **30.5×30.5 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz** board. All equations are parametric, so you can substitute your real board.
> 3. **Netlist status:** The netlist is a **reference topology**, not a released, verified design. Pin numbers must be checked against the datasheets of the parts you actually procure.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current path in complementary (synchronous) 6-step

During each 60° commutation sector, two legs carry the phase current:
- **PWM leg:** the high side (HS) conducts during D, and the synchronous low side (LS) conducts during (1−D).
- **Return leg:** the LS is on for the full sector.

So, independent of duty cycle, **two R_ds(on) are always in series with I_ph**:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j) + I_{ph}^2 R_{Cu}$$

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\right]$$

### 2.2 Self-consistent junction temperature

Loss and junction temperature depend on each other, so solve iteratively:

$$T_j = T_{board} + P_{FET,avg}\,R_{\theta JC}, \qquad P_{FET,avg}=\frac{P_{cond}+P_{sw}}{6}$$

At T_j ≈ 125 °C, R_ds = 1.5 mΩ × 1.45 = **2.18 mΩ**:

$$P_{cond,FET-pair} = 2(80)^2(2.18\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{27.9\ W\ per\ motor}$$

Copper adds to this. Assume R_Cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ for phase and bus plane segments plus solder, giving 6400 × 0.3 mΩ = **1.9 W**.

**Conduction total ≈ 29.8 W per motor.**

### 2.3 Worst single FET (instantaneous)

The return-leg LS carries the full current for 1/3 of the electrical period:
- Instantaneous loss: 6400 × 2.18 mΩ = 14 W.
- Its 1/3-duty average is ≈ 4.7 W.

At typical eRPM, sectors are about 10–100 µs. This is much shorter than the die-to-case thermal time constant (~ms). Therefore the **averaged** value governs T_j:

ΔT_jc ≈ (4.7 + 0.4) × 1.5 ≈ **7.6 K**

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg per motor switches at a time (the PWM leg: one HS/LS pair).

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = 0.5·25.2·80·30 ns·48 k | **1.45 W** |
| Dead-time diode | V_f·I·2·t_dead·f_sw = 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k | **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 30 nC·25.2·48 k | **0.036 W** |
| C_oss | 2·(½·C_oss·V²·f_sw) | **0.046 W** |
| Gate drive (driver side) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw | **0.038 W** |
| **Total P_sw** | | **≈ 2.2 W per motor** |

**Key insight:** At 48 kHz, conduction (~30 W) is **≈ 93 % of loss**. Lowering f_sw buys little. Lowering R_ds(on) and copper resistance is the lever. 48 kHz is justified by low motor ripple current and acoustic considerations, not efficiency.

### Totals at the 4×80 A burst

$$P_{total} = 4\times(29.8+2.2) \approx \mathbf{128\ W}$$
