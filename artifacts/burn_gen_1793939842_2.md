# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #14): Engineering Design Package

**Covers:** 80 A burst thermal model, 48 kHz switching losses, ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 timer/DMA allocation, and reference netlists.

## 0. Architecture Clarification

The STM32H743 should **not** commutate the motors directly. ArduPilot has no field-oriented control or 6-step commutation stack for the H7, and running 24 gate signals at 48 kHz with ADC-synchronous BEMF sensing would starve the flight-control scheduler.

The design therefore uses this partition:

```
┌──────────────────────────┐   DShot600 (bidir, 1-wire ×4)   ┌──────────────────────────────┐
│ STM32H743VIT6 @ 480 MHz  │ ───────────────────────────────▶ │ 4× AT32F421 / STM32G071      │
│ ArduPilot (Copter)       │ ◀─────── eRPM telemetry (GCR) ── │ AM32 / BLHeli_32 firmware    │
│ IMU, Baro, OSD, RX, VTX  │                                  │ 4× FD6288Q 3-ph gate driver  │
└──────────────────────────┘                                  │ 24× N-MOSFET, 48 kHz PWM     │
                                                              └──────────────────────────────┘
```

**Assumptions** (substitute your datasheet values; all equations are parametric):

| Symbol | Meaning | Assumed value |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 6S nominal / max | 22.2 V / 25.2 V |
| I_ph | Burst phase current, per channel | 80 A, ≤ 5 s |
| I_cont | Continuous per channel | 45 A |
| f_sw | PWM frequency | 48 kHz |
| R_ds(on),25 | MOSFET (40 V, 3.3×3.3 or 5×6 class) | 1.0 mΩ @ V_GS = 10 V |
| α_R | R_ds(on) hot multiplier @ T_j = 125 °C | 1.5 |
| t_r, t_f | Switching edges at gate driver strength | 10 ns, 10 ns |
| Q_rr | Body diode reverse-recovery charge | 50 nC |
| V_f | Body diode forward drop | 0.8 V |
| t_dt | Dead time (per edge) | 200 ns |
| Q_g | Total gate charge | 60 nC |
| V_drv | Gate drive voltage | 10 V |
| R_θJC | Junction to board pad | 1.0 K/W |

> ⚠️ **80 A per channel is a burst rating only on a nano (≈30.5×30.5 mm) board.** The model below shows it is thermally capacity-limited (adiabatic), not steady-state-limited.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Six-step (trapezoidal) commutation

At any instant two phases conduct: one high-side FET is PWM'd at duty *D*, and one low-side FET is fully on. With synchronous rectification (complementary PWM, which AM32 uses), the freewheeling current flows through the opposite FET of the PWM'd leg rather than its body diode.

Current paths per PWM period:

- **On-time (D):** HS_a → motor → LS_b
- **Off-time (1−D):** LS_a (sync) → motor → LS_b

LS_b conducts 100 % of the time, and the PWM leg conducts 100 % shared between HS_a and LS_a. Therefore:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{ds,hot}\,\big[D + (1-D)\big] + I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{ds,hot} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds,hot}
$$

$$
R_{ds,hot} = \alpha_R\, R_{ds,25} \quad\text{or more precisely}\quad R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\big(1 + \kappa\,(T_j - 25)\big),\ \ \kappa \approx 0.005\ /\text{K}
$$

### 1.2 Numerical result

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W}
$$

**Per-FET average.** Each FET carries current for 120° of 360° electrical:

$$
P_{FET,avg} = \frac{19.2}{6}\times\frac{\text{(path occupancy)}}{} \approx 3.2\ \text{W}
$$

The worst case is a low-side FET during its 120° window, which sees I²R = 9.6 W instantaneous. At low eRPM (launch or punch-out from hover), the electrical period exceeds the FET's thermal time constant. You must therefore design for the instantaneous value against Z_θJC(t), not the average.

### 1.3 PCB copper conduction (often forgotten)

For a phase trace of length *l*, width *w*, copper thickness *t* (2 oz = 70 µm), and layer count *n*:

$$
R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho_{Cu}(T)\, l}{w\, t\, n}, \qquad \rho_{Cu,100°C} = 2.2\times10^{-8}\ \Omega\cdot\text{m}
$$

Example: l = 8 mm, w = 4 mm, 4 layers × 70 µm gives R_Cu ≈ 0.157 mΩ, so P ≈ 2 × 80² × 0.157 m = **2.0 W/ch**.

### 1.4 Shunt loss (if per-channel sensing)

With R_sh = 0.25 mΩ, I_DC,bus ≈ D·I_ph. Worst case at D → 1:

$$
P_{sh} = (D\, I_{ph})^2 R_{sh} \le 1.6\ \text{W}
$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches; one hard-switched transition pair per period.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap

$$
P_{sw,VI} = \tfrac12\, V_{bus}\, I_{ph}\,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}
= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}
$$

A more exact form uses the gate-driver plateau model:

$$
t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\, R_{g,tot}}{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}},\qquad
t_{f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\, R_{g,tot}}{V_{plateau}}
$$

This shows that turn-off is usually the slower edge. Size R_g,off (with a diode bypass) accordingly.

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$
P_{dt} = 2\, V_f\, I_{ph}\, t_{dt}\, f_{sw} = 2\times0.8\times80\times200\text{n}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}
$$

> This loss exceeds the V–I overlap. Reducing dead time from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W/ch, which is the single most effective firmware tweak (AM32 "dead time" setting). Validate shoot-through margin with scope measurements first.

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = V_{bus}\, Q_{rr}\, f_{sw} = 25.2\times50\text{n}\times48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}
$$

There is a secondary effect: Q_rr adds to HS turn-on current, giving roughly V·Q_rr·f extra again (~0.06 W).
