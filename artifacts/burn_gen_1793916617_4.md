## 0. Scope, System Partitioning and Assumptions

Two points need agreement before the numbers are useful.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 stack, each ESC runs its own MCU on firmware such as AM32 or BLHeli_32. That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM and runs commutation and dead-time. The STM32H743 flight controller running ArduPilot only:
- emits bidirectional DShot600 commands, and
- captures the eRPM telemetry the ESC sends back.

The document is therefore split into three parts:
- **ESC power stage:** loss and thermal model (Sections 1–3).
- **FC timer/DMA architecture:** Section 4.
- **Interconnect and power-stage netlists:** Section 5.

**2. "Nano" and 4 × 80A are in tension.** A 20×20 mm board cannot sustain 4 × 80 A. Even a 30.5×30.5 mm board can only do so for a few seconds (Section 3 quantifies this). I treat 80 A as a **per-motor phase-current burst rating**, not continuous, and size the board as a 36×36 mm, 30.5 mm-mount 4-in-1. If "nano" is a hard mechanical constraint, the burst rating must be derated. Section 3.4 shows how.
