# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal, and DShot Design

**Job #46 design note, Rev A (Draft)**

This note covers the power-stage loss model, the thermal model, the bidirectional DShot600 timer and DMA plan, and reference netlists. Treat it as a draft for review.

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

### 0.1 Architecture

The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does not commutate the motors. Each of the four motor channels has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, typically an STM32G071 or AT32F421. That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 talks to the ESCs only through bidirectional DShot600.

If the intent really is for the H743 to drive the bridges directly, that is a different firmware architecture that ArduPilot does not support. Please flag it now if so.

### 0.3 Sanity Flag

80 A burst on a nano-class (20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm) 4-in-1 board is at or beyond physical limits. The model below shows that the limit is thermal capacitance, so it is time-limited to seconds. Section 2.4 shows the result.

## 1. Power-Stage Loss Model (per motor, 3-phase bridge, 6 FETs)

### 1.1 Commutation Model

The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM on the switching leg. At any instant the current flows through exactly two FETs:

- the PWM leg: either the high-side FET (fraction D of the time) or the synchronous low-side FET (fraction 1−D);
- the static low-side FET of the return phase.

Each leg is active for 1/3 of the electrical period.

### 1.2 Conduction Losses

The effective resistance of one current path is:

$$
R_{eff} = k_T R_{ds(on),25} + R_{pcb} = 1.5(1.5) + 0.3 = 2.55\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

Including inductor current ripple ΔI (peak-to-peak), the RMS current is:

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2\left(1 + \frac{r^2}{12}\right),\qquad r = \frac{\Delta I}{I_{ph}}
$$

The ripple amplitude follows from the motor's phase-to-phase inductance L_ll:

$$
\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - E)\,D}{L_{ll}\, f_{sw}}
$$

Here E is the back-EMF. At full throttle (D → 1), V_bus − E is small and so is r.

**Per-FET conduction loss, averaged over one electrical cycle:**

$$
P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I_{rms}^2 R_{eff}
$$

$$
P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\left[(1-D) + 1\right] I_{rms}^2 R_{eff}
$$

In the low-side term, (1−D) is the synchronous-rectification share and the 1 is the static-return share.

**Per-motor total conduction loss:**

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 3\left(P_{HS} + P_{LS}\right) = 2\, I_{rms}^2 R_{eff}
$$

The total is independent of D.

**Numerical result** (taking r ≈ 0.2, so the ripple factor is 1.0033):

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.0033 \times 2.55\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{32.7\ W}
$$

At D ≈ 1, the hottest FETs each dissipate roughly 32.7 / 6 ≈ 5.5 W. At part throttle, the low-side FETs carry more of the loss than the high-side FETs.

### 1.3 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Hard switching occurs at high-side turn-on, because the low-side body diode must recover. Turn-off is partly cushioned.

| Term | Expression | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V·I overlap | ½ V_bus I (t_r + t_f) f_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 k | 1.94 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr V_bus f_sw = 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.05 W |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss V_bus f_sw (approximation of ½C_oss V² for nonlinear C_oss) | 0.04 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | 2 V_SD I t_dt f_sw = 2 × 0.8 × 80 × 50 ns × 48 k | 0.31 W |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver and R_g, not in the FET) | 2 Q_g V_gs f_sw | 0.03 W |
| **Total per motor** | | **≈ 2.37 W** |

The overlap term dominates and scales linearly with f_sw. For comparison:

- 24 kHz: about 1.2 W
- 96 kHz: about 4.7 W

Raising f_sw improves motor current ripple (and therefore copper and iron loss and acoustic noise). The cost is about 1.2 W per motor for every additional 24 kHz.
