## 0. Architecture Findings to Resolve First

These issues change the design, so they come before the math.

**Finding 1: The H743 should not commutate the motors.**
ArduPilot has no ESC commutation firmware. The STM32H743 also has only two advanced timers with complementary outputs and dead-time (TIM1 and TIM8), plus HRTIM. That is not enough to cleanly run 12 complementary half-bridges. The production architecture is therefore:

- **H743 (ArduPilot):** sends bidirectional DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry.
- **4× ESC MCUs (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071, running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware):** each runs 48 kHz complementary PWM and 6-step or FOC commutation.

All loss calculations below apply to the ESC power stage regardless of which MCU drives it.

**Finding 2: "80A burst" on a 20×20 mm board is limited to roughly 1–7 seconds.**
The model in Section 3 shows this. Treat 80A as a transient rating with an explicit time limit, not a continuous rating. Marketing figures for nano boards usually omit this time limit.

**Finding 3: All component values are representative.**
The values below are typical of 40V DFN3.3×3.3 MOSFETs and 2oz 6–8 layer stackups. Before release, replace every parameter tagged **[DS]** with figures from the datasheet of the part you actually select.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC)

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

With 6-step commutation and synchronous rectification, two phases conduct at any instant. During the PWM-on interval, the current path is high-side FET (phase A) → motor → low-side FET (phase B). During PWM-off, the complementary low-side FET of phase A carries the current instead. Either way, the current always flows through exactly two FETs:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{ph,rms}^2 \, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

On-resistance rises with junction temperature:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 100 °C:

$$R_{ds} = 1.0 \times (1 + 0.0045 \times 75) = 1.34\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.34\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{17.1\ W}$$

Copper losses add to this:

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

**Thermal runaway check.** Conduction loss depends on R_ds, R_ds depends on T_j, and T_j depends on loss. Iterate the loop:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_{board} + P_{FET}(T_j^{(n)})\cdot R_{\theta jb}$$

A stable solution requires:

$$\alpha \cdot P_{FET} \cdot R_{\theta jb} < 1$$

Here that is 0.0045 × 10 × 3 = 0.135, so the loop converges and there is no electrothermal runaway at the FET level.

### 2.2 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing leg switches hard.

**Hard-switching V–I overlap:**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead-time intervals per period):

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = V_{bus} Q_{rr} f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 30\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}$$

**Output capacitance** (both FETs in the leg):

$$P_{oss} = 2\cdot\tfrac12 C_{oss} V^2 f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.030\ W}$$

**Gate drive** (both FETs in the leg; this power is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors):

$$P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.038\ W}$$

$$P_{sw,total} \approx \mathbf{2.17\ W\ per\ ESC}$$

**Frequency sensitivity.** Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it is about 1.1 W. At 96 kHz it is about 4.3 W. At 48 kHz, switching is about 11% of total loss, so conduction dominates. Lowering R_ds therefore has far more effect than lowering f_s
