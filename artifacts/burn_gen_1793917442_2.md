# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with Integrated 4-in-1 ESC: Engineering Model

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Red Flags

I have no records of "Job #41." The parameters below are stated assumptions; replace them with your actual BOM values. All equations are parameterized so the numbers update directly.

Three architectural issues to resolve before layout:

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production AIO (Matek, Holybro, SpeedyBee, etc.), the H743 runs ArduPilot and emits DShot. Each 4-in-1 ESC section has its own MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32, or EFM8 running Bluejay) plus gate drivers. The H743 cannot run 4× field-oriented or 6-step commutation at 48 kHz alongside the flight stack without major real-time risk. The architecture below follows that split.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is not thermally credible beyond about 1 second** if all four channels burst simultaneously. Section 3 quantifies this. If the spec means 80 A total (≈20 A per motor), the design is comfortable.
3. **4 × 80 A = 320 A battery current** exceeds any XT30/XT60 connector and 20×20 copper. Confirm whether the spec is per channel or aggregate.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation Topology (6-step trapezoidal, synchronous/complementary PWM)

At any instant, two phases conduct. Current flows through exactly one high-side path and one low-side path:

- The PWM phase alternates between its high-side (on-time D) and its complementary low-side (off-time 1−D).
- The sink phase low-side is on continuously for its 120° sector.

So at every instant, motor current I passes through exactly two R_ds(on) in series, regardless of D.

### 1.2 Temperature-Dependent Resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

### 1.3 Per-Channel and Per-FET Conduction Loss

Per channel:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Per FET, averaged over an electrical cycle. Each phase leg conducts 2/3 of the cycle, shared between its two FETs:

$$P_{cond,HS} = \frac{I^2 R_{ds}}{3}\left(D + D\right) \cdot \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot 2 = \frac{2}{3} D\, I^2 R_{ds}\quad\text{(for PWM-on-high scheme, approx.)}$$

For thermal sizing, use the bounding case: per FET ≈ (1/3)·I²·R_ds, with six FETs sharing the 2·I²·R_ds total.

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_ds(on) | P_cond per channel | Per FET (avg) | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.20 mΩ | 15.4 W | 2.6 W | 61.4 W |
| 100 °C | 1.61 mΩ | 20.6 W | 3.4 W | 82.4 W |
| 125 °C | 1.74 mΩ | 22.3 W | 3.7 W | 89.1 W |

Additional series loss: PCB copper and shunt. A 2 oz, 4-layer-paralleled power path of about 0.3 mΩ per channel adds about 1.9 W per channel at 80 A. A 0.25 mΩ low-side shunt (if used) adds 1.6 W.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing phase leg switches. That is one hard-switched FET per channel at any time.

### 2.1 Components

**V-I overlap (hard switching):**
$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2}(25.2)(80)(30\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz}) = 1.45\text{ W}$$

**Output capacitance:**
$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss} V^2 f_{sw} = 0.5(1\text{ nF})(635)(48\text{ k}) = 0.015\text{ W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = (30\text{ nC})(25.2)(48\text{ k}) = 0.036\text{ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction:**
$$P_{dt} = V_f I (2 t_{dead}) f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(100\text{ ns})(48\text{ k}) = 0.31\text{ W}$$

**Gate drive** (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g):
$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 40\text{ nC}(10)(48\text{ k}) = 0.019\text{ W per FET}$$

### 2.2 Totals

| Component | Per channel at 80 A |
|---|---|
| V-I overlap | 1.45 W |
| C_oss | 0.02 W |
| Q_rr | 0.04 W |
| Dead time | 0.31 W |
| Gate (×2 switching FETs) | 0.04 W |
| **P_sw total** | **≈1.85 W** |

At 80 A burst the throttle is usually near D ≈ 1, and many ESC firmwares stop PWM-ing at 100%. Switching loss then collapses toward zero. The figure above is the conservative partial-throttle burst case.

### 2.3 Sensitivity

$$\frac{\partial P_{VI}}{\partial t_{sw}} \approx 48\text{ mW/ns}$$

A weak driver (t_sw = 60 ns) roughly doubles switching loss to about 2.9 W per channel. Going from 24 kHz to 48 kHz doubles it again. 48 kHz is justified only if you need low current ripple for small, low-inductance motors.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Total Dissipation (T_j ≈ 100 °C, all four channels at burst)

$$P_{total} = 4\,(20.6 + 1.9_{Cu} + 1.85_{sw}) \approx 97\text{ W}$$

Add the H743 (≈0.5 W at 400 MHz), regulators (≈1–2 W), and ESC MCUs (≈0.1 W) for a total of **≈100 W**.
