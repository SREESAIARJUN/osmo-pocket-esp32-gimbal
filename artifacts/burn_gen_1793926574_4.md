## 0. Architecture Clarifications (Read First)

1. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each ESC channel has its own MCU (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or a BLHeli_32-class target). That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM and does commutation. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to the 4 ESC MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. The rest of this document assumes that split.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano/20×20 mm board is a thermal-capacity problem, not a steady-state one.** The math in §3 shows that the realistic burst window is about **2–4 seconds**, not continuous. If the spec intends 80 A continuous, the board must grow (30.5×30.5 mm minimum), use paralleled FETs, or add a heatsink.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At a design junction temperature of T_j = 100 °C:

$$R_{ds}(100) = 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega \times (1 + 0.005 \times 75) = 1.65\,\text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Current path

In 6-step trapezoidal drive, two phases are active at any instant:
- The **PWM leg** conducts through its HS FET during the on-time (D) and through its LS FET during the off-time (1−D, synchronous rectification).
- The **return leg** conducts through its LS FET continuously.

So **exactly two FETs are always in series with the motor current**, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

With ripple-free phase current, I_rms ≈ I = 80 A:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.65\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{21.1\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each FET is active for 120° of every 360° electrical cycle. Its average share is therefore 21.1 / 6 ≈ **3.5 W**.

The **instantaneous** dissipation while a FET conducts is:

$$I^2 R = 6400 \times 1.65\times10^{-3} = 10.6\ \text{W}$$

This instantaneous figure is the one used for the junction-transient check in §3.3.

### 1.4 Ripple correction (optional)

Peak-to-peak phase ripple is:

$$\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}\, D(1-D)}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}$$

For a typical L_ph = 20 µH at D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\times10^{-6} \times 48\times10^{3}} = 6.6\ \text{A}$$

The resulting RMS correction is:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.6$$

This is negligible (<0.1 %), so it is ignored below.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the **PWM leg** switches. Its HS FET hard-switches, and its LS FET soft-switches through the body diode during dead time.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (HS FET)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I\, (t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}$$
$$P_{VI} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\times10^{-9} \times 48\,000 = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (LS body diode → HS turn-on)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 40\times10^{-9} \times 25.2 \times 48\,000 = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

A second-order term is added to HS turn-on, but it is negligible at this Q_rr.

### 2.3 Output capacitance (C_oss)

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss}\, V_{bus}^2\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\times10^{-9} \times 635 \times 48\,000 = \mathbf{0.02\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2\, t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\times10^{-9} \times 48\,000 = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**Design lever:** cutting dead time to 50 ns halves this loss. Validate the shoot-through margin against the driver's propagation-delay mismatch before doing so.

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET:

$$P_g = Q_g\, V_{gs}\, f_{sw} \times 2\ \text{FETs} = 50\times10^{-9} \times 10 \times 48\,000 \times 2 = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

$$P_{sw,motor} = 1.45 + 0.05 + 0.02 + 0.61 + 0.05 \approx \mathbf{2.2\ W}$$
