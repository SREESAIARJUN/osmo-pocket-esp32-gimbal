# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage, Thermal, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Model

## 0. Architectural Assumptions

**The STM32H743 is the flight controller, not the motor controller.** A production 4-in-1 ESC runs commutation on dedicated MCUs, either one per motor or one quad device. Typical choices are the AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to those MCUs over bi-directional DShot600. Running FOC/6-step for four motors at 48 kHz on the H743 alongside the EKF3 is possible in principle, but no ArduPilot firmware supports it. I model the conventional split.

**"Nano" form factor and 80 A burst.** I take "nano" to mean a 20×20 mm mounting pattern on a board around 25×25 mm. An "80 A burst" on that footprint is only survivable as a short adiabatic event, and the model below quantifies exactly how short. Every device parameter marked **[P]** is a placeholder that must be replaced with your selected MOSFET's datasheet values before release.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology

The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM, which is the AM32/BLHeli_32 default.

At any instant two phases carry current I:
- **The PWM phase** alternates between its high-side FET (on-time D) and its low-side FET (off-time 1−D).
- **The return phase** keeps its low-side FET on continuously for the 60° sector.

So the current always flows through exactly two channels, independent of duty:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j) + I_{ripple,rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature-Corrected R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R = 1.0 × (1 + 0.006 × 85) = **1.51 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Ripple Term

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

Take L_ph = 15 µH (a typical 2207 motor, line-to-line) at worst-case D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{15\times10^{-6} \times 48000} = 8.75\,\text{A}$$

$$I_{ripple,rms} = \frac{\Delta I_{pp}}{\sqrt{12}} = 2.53\,\text{A}$$

The ripple term is 2.53² / 80² ≈ 0.1 % of the DC term, so it is negligible at burst.

### 1.4 Result at 80 A

$$P_{cond,motor} = 6400 \times 2 \times 1.51\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.3\,W}$$

$$P_{cond,total} = 4 \times 19.3 = \mathbf{77.3\,W}$$

Per conducting FET this is about **9.7 W** instantaneous.

### 1.5 Copper and Shunt Losses

| Item | Assumption | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| Phase trace and via path | ~0.3 mΩ per phase path, 2 phases active | 80² × 0.6 mΩ = 3.8 W per motor |
| Current shunt | 0.25 mΩ, sees battery current I_bat = D × I_ph | Duty-dependent |

The phase path resistance assumes 2 oz outer copper and an internal plane stack. Copper losses add **about 15 W** across the board at 80 A, which is not negligible.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Hard-Switching Overlap (PWM-Phase High-Side FET)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\,W}$$

### 2.2 Secondary Terms

| Term | Formula | Value (per motor) |
|---|---|---|
| Output capacitance | 2 × ½ C_oss,er V² f | 0.046 W |
| Reverse recovery (low-side body diode) | Q_rr V f | 0.048 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f I · 2t_dt · f | 0.307 W |
| Gate drive (2 switching FETs) | 2 Q_g V_GS f | 0.048 W |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.39 W** |

Totals:
- Four motors: **≈ 9.6 W**.
- Switching loss scales roughly linearly with current: k_sw ≈ 0.030 W/A per motor.

### 2.3 Design Insight

At 48 kHz, switching loss is only about 11 % of conduction loss at burst current. The frequency is therefore not the thermal problem; R_DS(on) is.

Raising f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz costs about 1.2 W per motor. In exchange it halves ripple, which reduces motor iron and copper loss and audible noise. That is a reasonable trade.
