## 0. Scope Corrections and Design Assumptions

Two points need settling before the math, because they change the architecture.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design, and in ArduPilot's model, the STM32H743 is the flight controller. It emits DShot and receives eRPM telemetry. Each ESC channel, or a shared ESC MCU, runs AM32, BLHeli_32 or Bluejay on its own MCU (AT32F421, STM32G071 or EFM8BB51). This document therefore covers:
- the **FC board** (H743, ArduPilot), and
- the **ESC board** (one AT32F421 or STM32G071 per channel running AM32, with 24 MOSFETs).

This holds whether the two are stacked or merged into a single AIO PCB.

**2. "Nano" and "80 A burst" conflict physically.** A 20×20 mm board cannot carry 4 × 80 A. This design assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm mounting pattern on a ~38×40 mm ESC PCB**. Section 3 shows that even this size survives 80 A on all four channels for only about **3 s**. "80 A burst" should be specified as *per channel, ≤3 s, ≤10 % duty*.
