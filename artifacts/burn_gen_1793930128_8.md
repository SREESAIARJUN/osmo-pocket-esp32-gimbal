## 0. Architecture Corrections and Governing Assumptions

Three issues must be resolved before layout. They change the design.

| # | Issue | Resolution |
|---|-------|-----------|
| 1 | The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. A 4-in-1 ESC needs a dedicated MCU per channel running AM32 or BLHeli_32 for 48 kHz PWM, BEMF sensing and bidirectional DShot. | Use the H743 as the flight controller. Add 4× AT32F421 (or STM32G071) ESC MCUs running AM32. AM32 supports bidirectional DShot and 48 kHz PWM natively. |
| 2 | 80 A per channel on a 20×20 mm "nano" footprint is not thermally achievable (see §3). An H743 in LQFP100 is 14×14 mm on its own. | Use a 2-board stack: a 20×20 FC with the H743 (TFBGA100 or LQFP100) and a 30×30 4-in-1 ESC. If 20×20 is a hard requirement, derate to 40–45 A burst. |
| 3 | "80 A burst" must be defined. Four channels × 80 A at 6S is about 8 kW and roughly 320 A of battery current. | Define the burst as **80 A on any single channel for ≤5 s**. The 4-channel simultaneous burst is analyzed separately (§3.4). |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{ds} = 1.5 \times 1.45 = 2.175\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Six-step synchronous commutation

In trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM, two FETs are always in the current path:

- the PWM leg, where current alternates between the high-side FET (for D) and the low-side synchronous FET (for 1−D);
- the low-side FET of the return leg, which conducts continuously.
