## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\,\text{K}^{-1} \Rightarrow k_{125}=1.5$$

### 1.2 Current path

In 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation with synchronous rectification, current always flows through one high-side-equivalent and one low-side-equivalent device in series. Complementary PWM means the on-time and off-time devices sum to a continuous path:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j) + I^2 R_{Cu}$$

At 80 A and T_j = 125 °C:

$$P_{cond,FET} = 2(80^2)(1.5\,\text{m}\Omega) = 19.2\ \text{W per channel}$$

### 1.3 Copper loss

Assume ~8 mm effective path per side, 2 oz (70 µm), ~3 mm average width, two paralleled layers:

$$R_{Cu}=\frac{\rho L}{wt}=\frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot 0.016}{0.003\cdot 70\times10^{-6}\cdot 2}\approx 0.66\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{Cu}=6400 \cdot 0.66\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 4.2\ \text{W per channel}$$

### 1.4 Per-device instantaneous loss

The worst device carries the full current for its 1/3 commutation window:

$$P_{FET,inst} = I^2R_{ds}(T_j) = 9.6\ \text{W}$$

Averaged over a full electrical cycle (each leg conducts 2/3 of the time, split by duty D between high and low side):

$$\bar P_{FET,HS} \approx \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R\, D\ \ ,\ \ \bar P_{FET,LS}\approx \tfrac{1}{3}I^2R(1-D)$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Switching losses occur only in the hard-switched (PWM'd) device per channel. The synchronous partner sees soft switching except for dead time.

| Term | Equation | Value per channel |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw | ½·25.2·80·20 ns·48 k = **0.97 W** |
| C_oss discharge | Q_oss·V·f_sw | 40 n·25.2·48 k = **0.048 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 30 n·25.2·48 k = **0.036 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2t_dt·f_sw | 0.8·80·100 ns·48 k = **0.31 W** |
| Gate drive (lost in driver) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw | 2·50 n·10·48 k = **0.048 W** |
| **Subtotal** | | **≈ 1.41 W** |

**Key insight.** At 80 A, conduction dominates by roughly 14:1. Raising f_sw to 96 kHz adds only about 1.4 W per channel. Lowering R_ds(on) is the lever that matters.

Optimal frequency trade-off. Motor current ripple scales as $\Delta I \approx \frac{V_{bus}D(1-D)}{L_{ph}f_{sw}}$. For low-inductance racing motors (~10–20 µH phase-to-phase), 48 kHz keeps ripple near 7–13 A at D = 0.5. That is why 48 kHz is the AM32/BLHeli_32 default.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Lumped RC network

```
P_FET ──[Rθjc 1.2 K/W]──┬──[Rθc-pcb 0.8 K/W]──┬── Board node (C_b) ──[Rθb-a]── T_amb
                        Cj (~0.05 J/K)         │
                                               └── shared by 24 FETs + Cu + shunts
```

### 4.2 Board thermal capacitance

Assume an effective mass of about 4 g (FR4 + copper + FETs + caps), with blended c_p ≈ 0.88 J/g·K:

$$C_b \approx 3.5\ \text{J/K}$$

### 4.3 Board-to-ambient resistance

Assume h ≈ 80 W/m²K in prop wash and two faces of 4×10⁻⁴ m² each:

$$R_{\theta b\text{-}a}=\frac{1}{hA}=\frac{1}{80 \cdot 8\times10^{-4}}\approx 15.6\ \text{K/W},\qquad \tau_b = R C \approx 55\ \text{s}$$

### 4.4 Burst response

Since t_burst ≪ τ_b, the board is nearly adiabatic:

$$\Delta T_b(t)=P R_\theta\left(1-e^{-t/\tau_b}\right)\approx \frac{P\,t}{C_b}=\frac{105}{3.5}\,t \approx 30\ \text{K/s}$$

The junction rides on top of the board temperature:

$$T_j = T_{amb} + \Delta T_b + P_{FET,inst}(R_{\theta jc}+R_{\theta c\text{-}pcb}) = T_{amb}+\Delta T_b + 9.6\cdot 2.0$$

Note that R_ds(on) rises as T_j rises, which feeds back into loss. Iterate the model, or use $R(T)$ inside a time-step loop.

|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 s | 15 K | ~69 °C | OK |
| 1.0 s | 30 K | ~84 °C | OK |
| 2.0 s | 60 K | ~114 °C | Marginal |
