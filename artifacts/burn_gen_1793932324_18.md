## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_0\left(\frac{T_j + 273}{298}\right)^{k}$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_DS = 1.2 mΩ × (383/298)^2.3 = 1.2 × 1.78 ≈ **2.13 mΩ**

A linear approximation, R₀[1 + α(T−25)] with α ≈ 0.007 K⁻¹, gives 1.91 mΩ. The power-law form is used because it is more conservative.

### 1.2 Six-step trapezoidal BLDC with complementary (synchronous) PWM

- In any 60° sector, two phases carry current I_ph. One leg is PWM-switched (high side for D, low side for 1−D, synchronous rectification). The other leg's low (or high) FET is held fully on.
- Current therefore always flows through **exactly two R_DS(on)** in series, regardless of D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j)$$

- Each of the 6 FETs carries current for 1/3 of the electrical period. Averaged over a cycle:

$$\overline{P}_{cond,FET} = \frac{P_{cond,motor}}{6} = \frac{I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}}{3}$$

- Ripple correction: with phase inductance L and ripple Δi = V(1−D)D/(L·f_sw),

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta i^2}{12}$$

  For typical 5–15 µH motors at 48 kHz this adds less than 1 %, so it is neglected below.

### 1.3 Results (T_j = 110 °C, R_DS = 2.13 mΩ)

| Case | I_ph | P_cond / motor | P_cond / FET (avg) | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P | 80 A | 6400 × 4.26 mΩ = **27.3 W** | 9.1 W | **109 W** |
| T | 20 A | 400 × 4.26 mΩ = **1.70 W** | 0.57 W | **6.8 W** |

**Self-consistency:** R_DS depends on T_j, which depends on loss. Iterate T_j(n+1) = T_ref + P(T_j(n))·R_θ until it converges. If (∂P/∂T)·R_θ ≥ 1, the loop has **no stable solution (thermal runaway)**:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T} = P\cdot\frac{k}{T_j+273}$$

For Case P per FET: 9.1 × 2.3/383 = 0.055 W/K. Runaway therefore occurs if the effective R_θ per FET exceeds about **18 K/W**, which is reachable on a nano board with no airflow.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Per commutation sector there is one hard-switched transition pair: the high side hard-switches, and the low side soft-switches after its body diode conducts.

| Term | Formula | Case P (80 A, 25.2 V) | Case T (20 A) |
|---|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | ½·25.2·80·35 ns·48k = **1.69 W** | 0.42 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw (Q_rr scales roughly with I) | 40 nC·25.2·48k = **0.048 W** | ~0.02 W |
| C_oss (both FETs in leg) | 2 × ½·C_oss·V²·f_sw | 1.5 nF·635·48k = **0.046 W** | 0.046 W |
| Dead-time body diode | V_SD·I·2t_dt·f_sw | 0.8·80·100 ns·48k = **0.31 W** | 0.077 W |
| Gate drive (in driver/LDO, ×2 FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw | 2·50 nC·10·48k = 0.048 W | 0.048 W |
| **Total switching / motor** | | **≈ 2.14 W** | **≈ 0.61 W** |

**Notes:**
- The 48 kHz choice costs roughly 2× the switching loss of 24 kHz. In exchange it gives quieter operation and lower current ripple on low-inductance (high-KV) motors. At 80 A, switching is only about 7 % of total loss, so f_sw is not the lever that matters. Conduction is.
- Increasing t_dt beyond about 50 ns increases the diode term linearly. Use the smallest dead time the gate driver (e.g. FD6288Q fixed internal ~200 ns, or AM32 configurable) allows without shoot-through.

### 2.1 Total Loss Summary

| | Per motor | 4 motors | Efficiency (at P_out ≈ V·I·D, D ≈ 0.9) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Case P | 27.3 + 2.1 = **29.4 W** | **≈ 118 W** | ≈ 98.4 % |
| Case T | 1.70 + 0.61 = **2.3 W** | **≈ 9.2 W** | ≈ 99.5 % |

The efficiency looks excellent, but 118 W into a 25 mm board is a thermal problem, not an efficiency problem.
