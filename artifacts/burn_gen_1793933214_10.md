# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model (Job #15)

**Architecture assumption:** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It sends bidirectional DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs (for example AT32F421 running AM32), which drive the bridges. The H743 does not commutate motors directly, because its timers and latency budget belong to the flight stack.

**Parameter assumption:** Every parameter marked *[A]* is an assumed value. Replace it with datasheet values from your chosen parts before you freeze the design.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

In 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification, two switch positions always carry the phase current:

- the active high side or its complementary low side during PWM-off, and
- the return-leg low side.

The PWM leg's current alternates between its high side (duty D) and low side (1−D). Total conduction loss is therefore independent of D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2 \cdot k_T \cdot R_{ds(on),25}$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.6 \times 1.0\,\text{mΩ} = \mathbf{20.5\ W}$$

**Per-FET values:**

- Instantaneous peak while conducting: 80² × 1.6 mΩ = **10.2 W**. Use this against Z_th(j-c) over one electrical period.
- Average: conduction rotates over 120° segments, so roughly P_total/6 ≈ **3.4 W per FET**.

**Battery-side caveat:** I_batt ≈ D · I_ph. If "80 A" refers to battery current at partial throttle, then I_ph = 80/D and losses grow as 1/D². Your current-limit firmware (AM32 current protection) must clamp phase current, not just battery current.

**Copper loss** must be added to the FET loss:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 \cdot \frac{\rho L}{w t}$$

- 2 oz copper (70 µm), 5 mm wide, 15 mm path: R ≈ 0.72 mΩ per layer.
- With 4 paralleled internal layers: ≈ 0.18 mΩ, giving **~1.2 W per motor**.
- Add the shunt: a 0.25 mΩ shunt at 80 A dissipates **1.6 W**.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg is hard-switched at a time.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½ V I (t_r + t_f) f_sw | ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 20 ns × 48 k = **0.968 W** |
| Dead-time diode | V_SD · I · 2 t_dead · f_sw | 0.8 × 80 × 100 ns × 48 k = **0.307 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw | 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.048 W** |
| Output capacitance | ½ Q_oss · V · f_sw | **0.018 W** |
| Gate drive (driver/R_g) | 2 Q_g V_GS f_sw | 2 × 30 nC × 10 × 48 k = **0.029 W** |
| **Total** | | **≈ 1.37 W per motor** |

The dominant switching term is V-I overlap. Halving t_r/t_f with a stronger driver and lower R_g helps, but it raises dV/dt and ringing. Budget a phase-node RC snubber, which adds about C·V²·f ≈ 2.2 nF → 0.07 W.

| Item | Per motor | ×4 |
|---|---|---|

| Switching | 1.4 W | 5.5 W |
| Copper + shunt | 2.8 W | 11.2 W |
| Gate driver / LDO / MCU | ~0.3 W | 1.2 W |
| **Total** | **~25 W** | **~100 W** |

**Efficiency check:** 4 × 80 A × ~24 V ≈ 7.7 kW input, so efficiency ≈ 98.7%. That is electrically fine. The problem is thermal density.

## 5. Thermal Model

### 5.1 Junction to case (fast transient)

Assume R_th(j-c) ≈ 1.5 K/W *[A]*:

$$ΔT_{j-c,peak} ≈ 10.2\,W × Z_{th}(t_{seg})$$

For segments shorter than 1 ms, Z_th is approximately 0.3–0.5 K/W, giving **≈ 3–5 K ripple**. This is negligible. The board-level lumped mass is what governs.

### 5.2 Board lumped capacitance (burst-limited)

Example: a 20×20 mm "nano" board with 8 layers, 2 oz copper, and 24 FETs.

| Contributor | Mass | Specific heat | Heat capacity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copper | ~2.5 g | 0.385 J/g·K | 0.96 J/K |
| FR4 | ~2 g | 1.1 J/g·K | 2.2 J/K |
| Components | ~1.5 g | 0.8 J/g·K | 1.2 J/K |
| **Total C_th** | | | **≈ 4.4 J/K** |

**Convection:** prop-wash h ≈ 100 W/m²K over A ≈ 2 × 4×10⁻⁴ m², so G ≈ 0.08 W/K and R_th(b-a) ≈ 12.5 K/W. The time constant τ = R·C ≈ 55 s is much longer than the burst, so the burst is effectively adiabatic:

$$\frac{dT}{dt} ≈ \frac{P}{C_{th}} = \frac{100}{4.4} ≈ \mathbf{23\ K/s}$$

**Burst budget:** starting from 50 °C and limiting the board to 110 °C (keeping T_j ≤ ~125 °C with margin):

$$t_{burst,max} ≈ \frac{60}{23} ≈ \mathbf{2.6\ s}\ \text{(all four motors simultaneously)}$$

**Steady state** is limited to P ≈ 60 K / 12.5 K/W ≈ **4.8 W total**. That corresponds to roughly I_ph,rms ≈ 17 A per motor continuous.

### 5.3 Design verdict

80 A burst on a 20×20 board is only credible for sub-3 s events. Continuous operation is not credible. Options:

1. **Grow to 30.5×30.5 mm.** C_th rises ~2.3×, giving ~6 s bursts.
2. **Use two paralleled FETs per position.** Conduction loss halves, but footprint area doubles.
