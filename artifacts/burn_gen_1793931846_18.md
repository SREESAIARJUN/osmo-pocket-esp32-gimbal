# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage, Thermal, and Bidirectional DShot600 Design Package

**Project:** Job #22, STM32H743 flight controller with integrated 4-in-1 ESC

---

## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility Findings

I don't have the original Job #22 requirements, so this package uses the assumptions below. Correct any that are wrong and the numbers re-derive directly from the equations.

| Parameter | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max (22.2 V nominal) |
| Burst current | 80 A **per motor**, 4 motors simultaneously (worst case: 320 A bus) |
| PWM frequency | f_sw = 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous) 6-step |
| MOSFETs | 40 V class, PDFN 3.3×3.3, R_DS(on) = 1.0 mΩ max at 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V, Q_g ≈ 40 nC, Q_rr ≈ 50 nC, Q_oss ≈ 40 nC, R_θJC ≈ 1.5 K/W |
| ESC firmware | AM32 on a per-motor ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071 class) |
| FC firmware | ArduPilot, H743 running at 400 MHz (ArduPilot default) |
| Ambient / board inlet | 35 °C, with prop-wash airflow |

### Critical design-review findings

1. **"Nano" form factor (20×20 mm) is not viable for this spec.** 24 power FETs (3.3×3.3 mm), 4 gate drivers, 4 ESC MCUs, bulk capacitance, and an LQFP100/TFBGA H743 cannot coexist on about 4 cm² of routable area. Two alternatives:
   - **30.5×30.5 mm stack:** FC board plus a separate 4-in-1 ESC board. This is my recommendation.
   - A single 30×30 AIO with a reduced rating (about 40 A continuous / 55 A burst).
2. **The H743 does not drive the gates.** In every production architecture, the H743 sends DShot to four dedicated ESC MCUs, which run commutation and the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 lacks enough advanced-control timers and ADC-synchronous paths to run four sensorless BLDC loops alongside the flight stack. This package follows that architecture.
3. **4×80 A simultaneous burst is thermally time-limited to about 2–3 s** (derived in §3). A single-motor 80 A burst is sustainable for much longer.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At the design point T_j = 115 °C:

$$R_{DS(on)}(115) = 1.0\,\text{mΩ}\times(1+0.0055\times 90) \approx 1.5\ \text{mΩ}$$

### 1.2 Current path in synchronous 6-step

At any instant, exactly one high-side FET (phase A) and one low-side FET (phase B) carry the phase current I.

- During the PWM on-time, current flows through HS_A and LS_B.
- During the off-time with synchronous rectification, current flows through LS_A and LS_B.

So **two R_DS(on) are always in series**, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + I_{rms}^2 \cdot R_{trace+shunt}$$

The phase-current ripple term adds roughly 1–3 %:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \tfrac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

$$P_{FET,cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ motor}$$

Copper and shunt contribution (0.25 mΩ shunt plus about 0.3 mΩ phase copper on 2 oz inner layers):

$$P_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.55\times10^{-3} = 3.5\ \text{W}$$

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Per motor, conduction (FET) | 19.2 W |
| Per motor, copper/shunt | 3.5 W |
| Per FET, time-averaged over electrical cycle (÷6) | 3.2 W |
| Hottest FET, instantaneous peak during its 120° window | ~9.6 W (1/3 of commutation period) |
| **Board total, 4 motors** | **90.8 W** |

Because the electrical frequency is high (14-pole motor at 25 krpm gives about 2.9 kHz), the FET thermal mass averages the 120° window. So the 3.2 W average per FET is the correct figure for T_j, with a small ripple of about 2–3 K.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the PWM'd half-bridge hard-switches. For the low-side-commutated phase, B is static. Per motor, use one hard-switched node.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I\,(t_r+t_f)\, f_{sw}$$

The gate driver sources and sinks about 1.5 A into a Miller plateau charge Q_gd ≈ 12 nC, giving t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 15 ns:

$$P_{sw} = 0.5\times25.2\times80\times30\times10^{-9}\times48\,000 = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$
