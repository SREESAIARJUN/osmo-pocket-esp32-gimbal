# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #7): Design Analysis

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

Two points in the brief need correcting before the math:

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each ESC channel runs its own firmware (AM32 or BLHeli_32) on a small MCU such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071. That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot600 frames to the ESC MCUs. This document models the system on that basis.
2. **80 A burst per channel on a "nano" (20×20 mm) board is not thermally credible.** Section 3 shows that even a 30.5×30.5 mm board only survives 80 A for about 5 to 8 seconds. I model the 30.5 mm board and give derating factors for 20 mm.

**Representative parameters.** These are not a specific part. Substitute your chosen MOSFET's datasheet values.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

In 6-step commutation, two phases conduct at any instant. During the PWM on-time, current flows through one high-side and one low-side FET. During the off-time, synchronous rectification makes it flow through two low-side FETs. Either way, current always passes through two R_DS(on) in series.

**Per-channel conduction loss:**

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{on}(T_j)$$

$$R_{on}(T_j) = R_{on,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At burst current:

| Condition | Calculation | P_cond per channel |
|---|---|---|
| Cold (R_on = 1.0 mΩ) | 6400 × 2.0 mΩ | 12.8 W |
| Hot (R_on = 1.5 mΩ) | 6400 × 3.0 mΩ | **19.2 W** (design case) |

**Per-FET average.** Each FET conducts for 120° of the 360° electrical cycle:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{1}{3} \cdot I^2 R_{on} = \frac{6400 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega}{3} = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

A positive feedback loop exists: R_on rises with temperature, which raises loss, which raises temperature further. This is iterated in Section 3.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg per channel is PWM-switching at a time. The other active leg is held statically on.

**(a) V·I overlap loss:**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 1.94\ \text{W}$$

**(b) Output capacitance loss:**

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

**(c) Reverse recovery loss:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 60\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.07\ \text{W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period):**

$$P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

**(e) Gate drive loss.** This dissipates in the driver, not the FET:

$$P_g = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 60\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.03\ \text{W per switching FET}$$

### Loss Budget at 80 A Burst (Hot)

| Loss | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.20 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching overlap | 1.94 W | 7.8 W |
| Coss + Qrr | 0.13 W | 0.5 W |
| Dead time | 0.61 W | 2.4 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 21.9 W** | **≈ 87.5 W** |

Conduction dominates at about 88% of the total. The switching-frequency trade-off follows from this:

- Raising f_sw from 24 kHz to 48 kHz adds only about 1.3 W per channel.
- In exchange, the higher frequency reduces current ripple and motor heating.

The losses that scale with f_sw are:

$$P_{f}(f_{sw}) = f_{sw}\left[\tfrac{1}{2}V I (t_r+t_f) + (Q_{oss}+Q_{rr})V + 2V_f I t_{dt}\right] \approx 56\ \mu\text{J} \times f_{sw}$$

**Efficiency at burst:**

- Electrical input power: 4 × 25.2 V × 80 A = 8064 W
- Efficiency: 1 − 87.5/8064 ≈ 98.9%

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Junction Rise Above Board

The hottest FET is a switching high-side FET. Its average loss is about 3.2 W conduction plus about 0.9 W switching share, so roughly 4.1 W.

$$\Delta T_{jc} = P_{FET} \cdot R_{\theta JC} = 4.1 \times 1.5 \approx 6\ \text{K}$$

### 3.2 Burst: Lumped Transient Model

**Board heat capacity (30.5 mm):**

| Item | Mass | Specific heat | Capacity |
|---|---|---|---|
| PCB (8-layer, 2 oz copper) | ≈ 8 g | 0.6 J/g·K | ≈ 4.8 J/K |
| 24 FETs plus aluminum spreader | ≈ 5 g | 0.9 J/g·K | ≈ 4.5 J/K |
| **Total** | | | **C_th ≈ 9.3 J/K** |

**Steady-state thermal resistance to air.** Convection in prop wash is h ≈ 50 W/m²·K over about 30 cm² of effective area:

$$R_{\theta SA} \approx \frac{1}{50 \times 0.003} \approx 6.7\ \text{K/W}, \quad \tau = R C \approx 62\ \text{s}$$

**Temperature response to a burst:**

$$T_{board}(t) = T_a + P R_{\theta SA}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right)$$

Because the burst is much shorter than τ, the response is nearly adiabatic: dT/dt ≈ 87.5 / 9.3 ≈ 9.4 K/s.
