## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Assumptions

I have no prior spec sheet for Job #57, so every parameter below is stated explicitly. Swap in your actual BOM values and the equations still hold.

**Partitioning.** The H743 is the flight controller. Each ESC needs its own commutation MCU, typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32. One G071/AT32F421 per motor is the standard 4-in-1 topology. The H743 drives the four ESC MCUs over bidirectional DShot600.

**Topology.** 6S LiPo, 3-phase bridge per motor, 6 MOSFETs per motor, 24 FETs total. Six-step (trapezoidal) commutation with complementary PWM (synchronous rectification) at 48 kHz.

**"80 A burst".** This is interpreted as 80 A phase current per motor, simultaneously on all 4 motors, for a short duration.

> ⚠️ **Feasibility flag (important).** The model below shows that 80 A × 4 on a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm nano board is thermally a burst of roughly 2–3 seconds, not a sustained rating. I recommend publishing it as "80 A burst (≤2 s), ~35–40 A continuous" and enforcing it in firmware (Section 5).

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{ds,hot} = 0.80 \times (1 + 0.006 \times 100) = 1.28\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Current path in six-step with synchronous rectification

During any 60° sector, current flows through two phases. With complementary PWM:

- During the ON time, current flows through HS(A) and LS(B).
- During the OFF time, current flows through LS(A) (synchronous freewheel) and LS(B).

In both cases exactly two FET channels carry the phase current. The conduction loss per motor is therefore independent of duty cycle, apart from the dead-time interval, which is handled in Section 2.4.

### 1.3 RMS correction for current ripple

Peak-to-peak ripple, using line-to-line inductance:

$$\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}\, D(1-D)}{L_{LL}\, f_{sw}}$$

The worst case is at D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{30\,\mu\text{H} \times 48\,\text{kHz}} = 4.38\ \text{A}_{pp}$$

The RMS current is then:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 6400 + 1.6 \approx 6401.6\ \text{A}^2$$

The ripple correction is under 0.03%, so it is negligible at 48 kHz. This is a key benefit of the high PWM frequency.

### 1.4 Conduction loss result

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2 R_{ds,hot} = 6400 \times 2.56\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{16.38\ W}$$

The per-FET average assumes each FET conducts about 1/3 of the electrical cycle, so the loss is evenly shared across the 6 FETs:

$$P_{cond,FET} = 16.38 / 6 = 2.73\ \text{W}$$

The instantaneous loss in a FET during its conduction window is 8.19 W.

Electrical period check: at 30,000 rpm with 14 poles, f_e = 3.5 kHz, so T_e = 0.29 ms. This is much shorter than the die thermal time constant (~1–5 ms), so per-FET averaging is valid. The superimposed junction ripple is:

$$\Delta T_{ripple} \approx P_{inst}\, Z_{th}(0.1\,\text{ms}) \approx 8.19 \times 0.15 \approx 1.2\ \text{K}$$
