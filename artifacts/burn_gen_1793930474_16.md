# STM32H743 AIO (4-in-1 ESC + FC), Job #19: Power-Stage, Thermal & Bi-Dir DShot Design Package

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Stated Assumptions

Three issues in the brief need resolving first, because they change the math.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** At a 48 kHz PWM carrier, 6-step BEMF commutation needs a dedicated MCU per motor (or a 4-channel ESC MCU), with comparator and ADC sync per channel. The standard and correct split is:
   - **STM32H743** runs ArduPilot. It outputs bi-directional DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry.
   - **4× AT32F421 (or STM32G071)** run AM32. AM32 supports a 48 kHz PWM carrier natively.

   The "48 kHz switching" in this package is therefore an ESC-firmware parameter, not an ArduPilot one.
2. **80 A burst on a nano/AIO footprint is not thermally sustainable on all four channels at once.** Section 3 proves this. The design target I adopt is **80 A on one channel for ≤ 1 s** (transient spike), not a quad-simultaneous rating.
3. **No MOSFET was specified.** I use a representative 40 V, 1 mΩ-class 3.3×3.3 mm part. Every result below is parametric, so substitute your datasheet values.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology

AM32 uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. In each 60° sector:

- The **PWM leg** has its high-side FET on for duty D and its low-side FET on for (1−D). The low-side acts as a synchronous rectifier.
- The **return leg** has its low-side FET on for 100% of the sector.
- The third leg floats and is used for BEMF zero-cross sensing.

Neglecting the dead-time fraction, phase current always flows through **exactly two channels**. Per motor channel:

$$P_{cond}=I_{ph,rms}^2\Big[\underbrace{D\,R_{HS}+(1-D)R_{LS}}_{\text{PWM leg}}+\underbrace{R_{LS}}_{\text{return}}\Big]\approx 2\,I_{ph,rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS}(T_j)=R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

This makes the model self-consistent. P depends on T_j, and T_j depends on P, so we iterate (§3.3).

### 1.3 Ripple correction

The phase ripple at 48 kHz with inductance L_ph is:

$$\Delta I=\frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5" motor with L_ph ≈ 15 µH at mid-throttle (V_bus − V_bemf ≈ 10 V, D = 0.5):

$$\Delta I \approx \frac{10\times0.5}{15\,\mu\text{H}\times48\,\text{kHz}} \approx 6.9\ \text{A}$$

The RMS of the triangular ripple adds:

$$I_{rms}=\sqrt{I^2+\frac{\Delta I^2}{12}}$$

For 80 A this is +0.025%, which is negligible. Ripple matters for capacitors, not FETs.

### 1.4 Result at 80 A

At T_j = 125 °C, R_DS = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0055 × 100) = **1.55 mΩ**.

$$P_{cond}=2\times80^2\times1.55\,\text{m}\Omega=\mathbf{19.8\ W}\ \text{per channel}$$

The loss splits as ≈ 9.9 W in the return-leg low-side FET, and ≈ 9.9 W shared between the PWM-leg high-side and low-side FETs in proportion to D and (1−D).

**The return-leg low-side FET is the hot spot.**

Add the PCB copper loss. A 2 oz power trace (70 µm) that is 4 mm wide and 8 mm long has:

$$R=\frac{\rho L}{wt}=\frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\times0.008}{0.004\times70\times10^{-6}}\approx0.49\ \text{m}\Omega$$

Running through two path segments on two layers in parallel gives ≈ 0.5 mΩ, so **+3.2 W at 80 A**.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (f_sw = 48 kHz)

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The return leg is static within the sector.

**(a) V-I overlap:**

$$P_{VI}=\tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}=0.5\times25.2\times80\times25\,\text{ns}\times48\,\text{kHz}=\mathbf{1.21\ W}$$

The overlap time comes from the Miller charge and the gate current, t ≈ (Q_gs2 + Q_gd) / I_G. Doubling the gate resistance roughly doubles this term.

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction:**

$$P_{dt}=V_F\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw}=0.8\times80\times200\,\text{ns}\times48\,\text{kHz}=\mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**(c) Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}=50\,\text{nC}\times25.2\times48\,\text{kHz}=\mathbf{0.06\ W}$$
