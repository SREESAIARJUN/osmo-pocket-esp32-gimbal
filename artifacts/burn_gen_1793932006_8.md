## 0. Assumptions and Corrections

These assumptions underpin every number below. Verify them before using any result.

| # | Item | Assumption / Correction |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | **Architecture** | The H743 runs ArduPilot and **does not** commutate motors. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 (STM32G071 or AT32F421 class) that generates the 48 kHz PWM. | A2 | **Supply** | 6S LiPo, V_in = 25.2 V max (worst case for switching loss). |

| A6 | **Form factor** | A "nano" board is assumed to be 20×20 mm or at most 30×30 mm. **Physics warning:** an 80 A/motor 4-in-1 on 20×20 mm is not thermally sustainable. The model below shows why and gives the burst limit. |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.004\text{–}0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C, the multiplier is k_T ≈ 1.5, so R_DS ≈ 1.5 mΩ.

### 1.2 Per motor channel

In six-step operation, current flows through one high-side FET and one low-side FET:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

- With synchronous rectification, the off-time current circulates through the complementary FET (also R_DS), so this holds independently of duty cycle.
- If you use diode freewheeling instead, replace the off-time term with V_SD·I·(1−D).

**At burst:**

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2(80)^2(1.5\times10^{-3}) = 19.2\ \text{W}$$

**Per FET:** each FET conducts about 1/3 of the electrical period, giving an average of 19.2/6 = 3.2 W and an instantaneous loss of 9.6 W.

### 1.3 PCB copper conduction (often forgotten)

Per-layer sheet resistance for 2 oz (70 µm) copper:

$$R_\square = \rho/t = 1.72\times10^{-8}/70\times10^{-6} = 0.246\ \text{m}\Omega/\square$$

Assume a phase path of about 4 squares and a 4-layer parallel pour (6-layer, 2 oz stack):

- R_Cu ≈ 0.25 mΩ per path, ×2 paths (high side and low side)
- P_Cu ≈ 2 × 80² × 0.25e-3 ≈ **3.2 W/channel** (comparable to a FET; do not ignore it)

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

| Term | Formula | Value / channel |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V_in·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw, with t_r + t_f = 20 ns | ½·25.2·80·20 ns·48 k = **0.97 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V_in·f_sw | 50 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.06 W** |
| Output capacitance | ½·C_oss·V_in²·f_sw (one switching FET pair) | ½·1.5 nF·635·48 k = **0.02 W** |
| Dead-time body diode | V_SD·I·2·t_dt·f_sw, with t_dt = 100 ns | 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (from the driver) | Q_g·V_GS·f_sw·n_switching (2 FETs PWM'd) | 60 nC·10·48 k·2 = **0.06 W** |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 1.7 W** |

**Rise/fall time estimate.** With driver source/sink R_g,total ≈ 5 Ω, Miller charge Q_gd ≈ 12 nC and V_drv − V_plateau ≈ 6 V:

- I_g ≈ 1.2 A
- t ≈ Q_gd / I_g ≈ 10 ns per edge, which matches the 20 ns total assumed above.

**Sensitivity:**
- Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it is about 0.85 W; at 96 kHz about 3.4 W.
- Conduction loss dominates by more than 10×, so **lowering R_DS(on) is the main lever.**

## 3. Total Burst Loss Budget

| Component | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (T_j = 125 °C) | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| PCB copper | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| Switching | 1.7 W | 6.8 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, total-current shunt at I_bat ≈ 4×80×D, with D ≈ 0.8) | — | ≈ 16 W |
| **Total** | | **≈ 112 W** |

The shunt sees I_bat ≈ 256 A at burst. That is a further argument that a single 4-in-1 shunt cannot handle 4×80 A. Consider per-channel sensing or a Hall sensor.

**Efficiency check:** P_out ≈ 25.2 × 256 ≈ 6.45 kW, so η ≈ 98.3%. That efficiency is fine; the problem is power *density*.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Junction-to-case (steady within one electrical cycle)

Electrical frequency is at most a few kHz, far faster than the die thermal time constant (≈ ms), so use the average per-FET loss:

$$\Delta T_{jc} = P_{FET,avg}\, R_{\theta JC} = 3.2 \times 1.2 \approx 3.8\ \text{K}$$

The junction is not the bottleneck. **The board is.**

### 4.2 Board lumped-capacitance (adiabatic burst) model

Estimate the heat capacity of a 20×20 mm, 6-layer 2 oz board with 24 FETs:

| Material | Mass | Heat capacity |
|---|---|---|
| Copper | 6 × 0.4 cm² ... ≈ 4 cm² × 420 µm × 8.96 g/cm³ ≈ 1.5 g | × 0.385 J/g·K = 0.58 J/K |
| FR4 | 4 cm² × 0.16 cm × 1.85 g/cm³ ≈ 1.2 g | × 1.1 J/g·K = 1.3 J/K |
| FETs, caps, solder | ≈ 1.5 g | × 0.7 J/g·K ≈ 1.0 J/K |
| **Total** | | **C_th ≈ 2.9 J/K** |

Rise during a burst:

$$\Delta T_{board}(t) = \frac{P\,t}{C_{th}} \quad \text{(adiabatic upper bound)}$$

$$\Delta T(2\,\text{s}) = \frac{112 \times 2}{2.9} \approx 77\ \text{K}$$

### 4.3 First-order model with convection

$$\Delta T(t) = P\,R_{\theta BA}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right),\quad \tau = R_{\theta BA}\,C_{th}$$

With prop-wash forced convection, h ≈ 50–80 W/m²·K over about 8 cm² of two-sided area:

- R_θBA ≈ 1/(65 × 8e-4) ≈ **19 K/W**
- τ ≈ 55 s

**Maximum burst time** (T_amb = 40 °C, board limit 110 °C, so ΔT = 70 K; τ ≫ t, so approximately adiabatic):

$$t_{burst} \approx \frac{C_{th}\,\Delta T}{P} = \frac{2.9 \times 70}{112} \approx \mathbf{1.8\ s}$$

**Sustainable continuous loss** = 70/19 ≈ **3.7 W total**. Since P ∝ I², this gives:

$$I_{cont} \approx 80\sqrt{3.7/112} \approx \mathbf{14.5\ \text{A/motor}}$$
