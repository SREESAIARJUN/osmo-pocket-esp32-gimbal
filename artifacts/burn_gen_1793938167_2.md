# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #42): Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Budget, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Allocation

---

## 0. Architectural Corrections

Three issues affect the deliverable, so they come first.

1. **The STM32H743 does not generate the 48 kHz PWM.** In a 4-in-1 ESC, each motor (or the whole stack) is commutated by a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical parts are the AT32F421, STM32G071 or GD32E230. The H743 runs ArduPilot and only emits DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry. The switching-loss model below therefore belongs to the ESC MCU and power stage, and the DMA model belongs to the H743.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano-class board (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) is not thermally credible beyond about 1–2 s.** The math in §3 shows this. Treat 80 A as a transient rating with an explicit time limit, not a spec point.
3. **No datasheet-locked BOM was provided for Job #42.** All device parameters below are stated assumptions for a representative 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm N-channel MOSFET. Substitute your real values into the equations. The model is built so this takes one pass.

## 2. Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

With 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation and synchronous rectification, the phase current always flows through exactly two MOSFETs in series:
- one high-side device, or its complementary low-side device during the PWM off-time;
- one low-side return device.

$$
P_{cond} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS(on),25}\, k_T
$$

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.5 = \mathbf{28.8\ W}
$$

**Per-FET average.** Each device carries current for 120° of the electrical cycle, so the 28.8 W is shared across 6 FETs over time. That gives about 4.8 W average per FET, with instantaneous peaks of 14.4 W.

**PCB copper (often forgotten).** Assume 2 oz copper with roughly 0.5 mΩ of effective path from battery pad to FET to motor pad, per phase path, ×2:

$$
P_{Cu} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega \approx \mathbf{6.4\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Switching Loss (hard-switched high-side)

**Gate currents during transitions:**

$$
I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{pl}}{R_{g,tot}} = \frac{6}{4} = 1.5\,\text{A}
\qquad
I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{g,tot}} = 1.0\,\text{A}
$$

**Intrinsic transition times:**

$$
t_{on} = \frac{Q_{gs2} + Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = 6.7\,\text{ns}
\qquad
t_{off} = \frac{Q_{gs2} + Q_{gd}}{I_{g,off}} = 10\,\text{ns}
$$

**Layout penalty.** Common-source inductance of about 0.5–1 nH at 80 A roughly doubles these times. Use t_r,eff = 15 ns and t_f,eff = 20 ns.

**V–I overlap loss:**

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I_{ph}\, (t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}
= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 35\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.69\ W}
$$

**Reverse recovery** (low-side body diode, recovered by the high-side turn-on):

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 60\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.073\ W}
$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss,eff}\, V_{bus}^2\, f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.012\ W}
$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (2 edges per period):

$$
P_{dt} = V_F\, I_{ph} \cdot 2\, t_{dead}\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}
$$

**Gate drive** (dissipated in the driver and R_g, not the FET). Only the active pair switches, but budget all 6 FETs:

$$
P_{gate} = 6\, Q_g\, V_{drv}\, f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.086\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Per-Motor and Board Totals (80 A, 6S, 48 kHz)

| Term | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 28.8 W | 115.2 W |
| Copper | 6.4 W | 25.6 W |
| V–I switching | 1.69 W | 6.8 W |
| Dead time | 1.23 W | 4.9 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.17 W | 0.7 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 38.3 W** | **≈ 153 W** |

**Key insight.** At 80 A, conduction loss is about 90 % of the total. Lowering f_sw from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves only about 1.5 W per motor. The levers that matter are R_DS(on) (paralleling FETs or using a larger package) and copper weight.
