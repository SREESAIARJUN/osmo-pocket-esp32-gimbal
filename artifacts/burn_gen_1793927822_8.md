# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #8): Architecture, Loss Model, DShot DMA Plan, Netlist

## 0. Scope Corrections and Assumptions

Several things need to be settled before the math.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 stack, each motor has its own ESC MCU (AT32F421, STM32G071 or EFM8BB51). That MCU runs AM32, BLHeli_32 or Bluejay. The 48 kHz phase PWM is generated there. The H743 only sources bidirectional DShot600 and consumes eRPM telemetry. This document models both domains but keeps them separate.
2. **80 A burst on a "nano" (20×20 mm) power stage is not thermally credible** for more than about 1 s with all four channels loaded. The model in §2 shows this quantitatively. I treat 80 A as a *per-channel, single-channel, short-burst* rating and derive the allowable burst duration.
3. **Component parameters are representative, not datasheet-verified.** Substitute your BOM's actual R_DS(on)(T), Q_g, Q_rr, C_oss and Z_th curves before sign-off. All formulas are parametric so you can do this directly.

## 1. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 1.1 Conduction topology

The ESC uses 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM:

- During each 60° electrical sector, current flows through exactly one high-side and one low-side leg.
- Within a PWM period, the high side conducts for D·T. The complementary low side of the same half-bridge conducts for (1−D)·T.
- The opposite-phase low side conducts continuously for the full sector.

At any instant, the motor current therefore passes through **exactly two channels**, independent of D. This is the key simplification for conduction loss.

### 1.2 Conduction loss

R_DS(on) depends on junction temperature:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

Per-channel conduction loss is then:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.50 mΩ | 19.2 W |
| 100 °C | 2.06 mΩ | 26.4 W |
| 125 °C | 2.25 mΩ | **28.8 W** |

This positive feedback (higher T_j, higher R_DS, higher P) is solved iteratively in §2.4.

**Per-FET distribution.** Each phase leg is active for 2 of 6 sectors. On average each of the 6 FETs carries about 1/6 of the per-channel loss share, i.e. ≈ 4.8 W at 125 °C. At commutation frequencies above roughly 100 Hz, the FET die thermal time constant (ms range) averages the 120° conduction windows. Peak-to-mean T_j ripple is under 3 K at any useful eRPM.

### 1.3 Copper / interconnect loss

Assume a 4 oz outer + 2 oz inner stack, with an effective 0.25 mΩ per channel from battery pad through FETs to phase pad:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.25\text{ m}\Omega = 1.6\text{ W}$$

Also add the shunt if used, e.g. 0.25 mΩ, for another **1.6 W**.

### 1.4 Switching loss at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing half-bridge switches.

**(a) V–I overlap, hard-switched high side:**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = 0.97\text{ W}$$

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction, two edges per period:**

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ k} = 0.61\text{ W}$$

**(c) Reverse recovery of the low-side body diode:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{ k} = 0.048\text{ W}$$

This is current-dependent. At 80 A, Q_rr may be 2–3× its datasheet test-point value. Budget **0.15 W**.

**(d) Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \approx 30\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{ k} = 0.036\text{ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive** (dissipated in driver and gate resistors, not the FET):

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2 = 40\text{ nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{ k} \times 2 = 0.038\text{ W}$$

$$P_{sw,total} \approx 0.97 + 0.61 + 0.15 + 0.04 \approx \mathbf{1.77\text{ W}}$$

**Frequency trade-off.** Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it is ≈ 0.9 W. At 96 kHz it is ≈ 3.5 W. At 80 A, conduction dominates (≈ 94 %), so 48 kHz is acceptable. The thermal argument for lowering f_sw is weak; the argument for 48 kHz (lower current ripple, quieter motors) holds.

### 1.5 Per-channel and board totals (80 A burst, T_j = 125 °C)

| Loss | W / channel |
|---|---|
| Conduction (2 FETs) | 28.8 |
| Switching (all terms) | 1.8 |
| Copper + shunt | 3.2 |
| **Total** | **≈ 33.8 W** |
| **4 channels simultaneously** | **≈ 135 W** |

ESC efficiency at this point: P_out ≈ 25.2 × 80 ≈ 2016 W per channel, giving η ≈ 98.3 %. The efficiency looks excellent; the problem is entirely heat *density* (135 W into 4 cm²).
