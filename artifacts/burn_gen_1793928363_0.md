## 1. Field-Oriented Control (Inner Current Loop)

### 1.1 Coordinate transforms

The electrical angle is $\theta_e = p\,(\theta_m - \theta_{\text{offset}}) \bmod 2\pi$.

**Clarke transform** (amplitude-invariant, with $i_a + i_b + i_c = 0$):
$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \frac{1}{\sqrt3}\left(i_a + 2 i_b\right)
$$

**Park transform:**
$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d \\ i_q\end{bmatrix} =
\begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e \\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha \\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}
$$

**Inverse Park transform:**
$$
v_\alpha = v_d\cos\theta_e - v_q\sin\theta_e,\qquad v_\beta = v_d\sin\theta_e + v_q\cos\theta_e
$$

### 1.2 Plant and current PI design

The PMSM voltage equations in the dq frame are:
$$
v_d = R i_d + L\frac{di_d}{dt} - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad
v_q = R i_q + L\frac{di_q}{dt} + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e \lambda_m
$$

With decoupling feedforward, each axis reduces to $G(s) = 1/(Ls+R)$. A PI controller $C(s) = K_p + K_i/s$ cancels the plant pole when $K_i/K_p = R/L$. The open loop then becomes $\omega_{ci}/s$, and the closed loop is first-order with bandwidth $\omega_{ci}$:
$$
\boxed{K_{p,i} = L\,\omega_{ci},\qquad K_{i,i} = R\,\omega_{ci}}
$$

With the assumed values: $K_{p,i} = 18.85\,\text{V/A}$ and $K_{i,i} = 52{,}779\,\text{V/(A·s)}$.

**Delay constraint.** The total delay (PWM update plus sampling) is about $1.5\,T_i = 60\,\mu s$. The phase margin is approximately $90^\circ - \omega_{ci}\cdot 1.5T_i\cdot\frac{180}{\pi} = 90 - 32.4 = 57.6^\circ$, which is acceptable.

**Decoupling plus back-EMF feedforward:**
$$
v_d^{ff} = -\omega_e L i_q,\qquad v_q^{ff} = \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e\lambda_m,\quad \lambda_m = \tfrac{2}{3}\tfrac{K_t}{p}
$$

**Voltage limiting.** Use a circular limit $\sqrt{v_d^2+v_q^2} \le V_{dc}/\sqrt3$ with d-axis priority. The integrators use back-calculation anti-windup (Section 2.2).

### 1.3 Space-Vector PWM (min/max zero-sequence injection)

$$
v_a = v_\alpha,\quad v_b = -\tfrac12 v_\alpha + \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}v_\beta,\quad v_c = -\tfrac12 v_\alpha - \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}v_\beta
$$
$$
v_0 = -\tfrac12\left(\max(v_a,v_b,v_c) + \min(v_a,v_b,v_c)\right),\qquad
D_x = \tfrac12 + \frac{v_x + v_0}{V_{dc}}
$$

This is mathematically identical to sector-based SVPWM and gives 15.5% more linear range than sinusoidal PWM.
