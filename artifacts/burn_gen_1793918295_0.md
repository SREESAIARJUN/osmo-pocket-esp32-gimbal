# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #6): Architecture, Loss Model, and DShot DMA Plan

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Feasibility

Two architectural facts shape the rest of this document:

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design, the H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bi-directional DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs. These are typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32, and they drive the half-bridges at 48 kHz. The model below reflects that split.
2. **80 A per motor is a burst rating only on a nano (≤30.5 mm) board.** The math below shows this explicitly. It is limited to roughly 2–5 s, depending on airflow. Continuous ratings for this form factor land around 35–45 A.

**Baseline parameters.** These are representative values. Substitute your actual BOM datasheet values.

|---|---|---|

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

With 6-step trapezoidal commutation and synchronous (complementary) PWM, the motor current always flows through exactly **two MOSFET channels**:

- the active high-side switch, or its complementary low-side during the PWM off-time, and
- the low-side switch of the return phase.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 135 °C:

$$R_{DS} = 1.0 \times (1 + 0.0055 \times 110) = 1.6\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Losses per ESC channel and per FET

**Instantaneous conduction loss per ESC channel:**

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.6\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{20.5\ W}$$

**Per-FET loss.** Each switch carries current for 120° electrical (one third of the cycle):

$$P_{FET,peak} = I^2 R_{DS} = 10.2\ \text{W (while conducting)}$$

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}\cdot 10.2 = 3.4\ \text{W}$$

**Copper and shunt losses.** Assume 0.3 mΩ of trace/via resistance per current path and a 0.25 mΩ shunt:

$$P_{Cu} \approx 80^2 \cdot 0.55\times10^{-3} = 3.5\ \text{W per channel}$$

This loss is non-negligible, so use heavy copper: 2 oz minimum on the outer layers.

### 1.4 Electro-thermal coupling and runaway criterion

The FET heats itself through its own on-resistance:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + R_{th}\,I^2 R_{DS,25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Solving for T_j:

$$\boxed{T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + R_{th} I^2 R_{25}(1-25\alpha)}{1 - R_{th} I^2 R_{25}\alpha}}$$

Thermal runaway occurs when

$$R_{th}\,I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha \ge 1$$

At I = 80 A this gives:

$$R_{th,crit} = \frac{1}{6400 \times 10^{-3} \times 0.0055} = 28\ \text{K/W}$$

The effective junction-to-board resistance is about 4.5 K/W (Section 3.2), well below this limit. **Runaway is not the limiting factor. Bulk board heating is.**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Gate drive current and switching time

Gate current during the Miller plateau:

$$I_g = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}{R_{g,total}} = \frac{10 - 3}{5} = 1.4\ \text{A}$$

Miller transition time:

$$t_{Miller} = \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g} \approx 5.7\ \text{ns}$$

Adding the Q_gs2 interval and driver delay, use t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 12 ns.

### 2.2 Loss components (one PWM-switching leg)

| Mechanism | Equation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·24 ns·48 kHz | 1.16 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 40 nC·25.2·48 kHz | 0.05 W |
| C_oss charge | 2·½·C_oss·V²·f_sw | 0.05 W |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_F·I·2·t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·200 ns·48 kHz | 0.61 W |
| Gate drive (6 FETs, dissipated in the driver) | 6·Q_g·V_drv·f_sw | 0.14 W |
| **Total switching-related** | | **≈ 2.0 W** |

### 2.3 Total per channel and per board

**Per ESC channel at 80 A burst:**

$$P_{ch} = 20.5 + 2.0 + 3.5 \approx \mathbf{26\ W}$$

**Whole 4-in-1 board** (all four motors at 80 A, worst case):

$$P_{tot} \approx 104\ \text{W}$$

Add roughly 1.5 W for the H743, regulators, and logic.

**Design note.** At 48 kHz, conduction loss dominates (about 80% of the total). Dropping to 24 kHz saves only about 1 W per channel and worsens motor ripple. Effort is better spent on lower R_DS(on), parallel FETs, and copper.
