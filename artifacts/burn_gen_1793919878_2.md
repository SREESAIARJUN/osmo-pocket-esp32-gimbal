# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Systems Architecture Package (Job #56)

**Scope:** 80 A burst conduction loss, 48 kHz switching loss, thermal model, ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 timer/DMA allocation, and a reference netlist.

---

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Flags

Three points need to be settled before the math, because they change the results.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production AIO, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot600 to **four dedicated ESC MCUs**, one per channel. Typical choices are the AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or the EFM8BB51 running Bluejay. Bidirectional DShot exists because the ESC is a separate processor. This document follows that architecture.

2. **80 A on a "nano" (20×20 mm) board is burst-only and marginal.** The thermal model below shows that in a 20×20 footprint, an 80 A per-channel burst is limited to **about 2–4 seconds**, set by board heat capacity. I recommend one of:
   - qualifying for 80 A **burst at 5 s maximum** on a **30.5×30.5 mm** board, with a ~45–55 A continuous rating, or
   - de-rating to about 40 A burst if 20×20 is mandatory.

3. **All device parameters below are design assumptions.** Replace them with datasheet values from the selected MOSFET before release. The equations stay valid; only the numbers change.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-Dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

$$R_{DS}(125\,°C) = 1.0 \times 1.5 = 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Six-Step Synchronous Commutation

During each 60° electrical sector, two legs carry current:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side (HS) FET conducts for duty D; the synchronous low-side (LS) FET conducts for 1 − D, minus dead time.
- **Return leg:** the LS FET is held on for the full sector.

Current flows through exactly **two channel resistances** at all times, independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \left[ D R_{HS} + (1-D) R_{LS} \right] + I^2 R_{LS} \approx 2 I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ channel}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET Distribution (Worst-Case FET)

Each FET conducts for 120° of the 360° electrical cycle.

Instantaneous loss while conducting:

$$P_{FET,inst} = I^2 R_{DS} = 6400 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = 9.6\ \text{W}$$

Cycle-averaged loss:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx 9.6/3 = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

At D ≈ 1, the HS FET of the active leg carries this. At low D, the burden shifts to the sync LS FET.

### 1.4 Copper and Interconnect Loss

Assume about 0.3 mΩ per phase path, covering 2 oz inner planes, vias and the solder pad:

$$P_{Cu,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.3\times10^{-3} = 3.84\ \text{W}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

These losses apply per channel. Only the PWM leg switches.

### 2.1 Hard-Switching V–I Overlap

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\times10^{-9} \times 48\times10^{3} = \mathbf{1.452\ W}$$

### 2.2 Body Diode Reverse Recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\times10^{-9} \times 25.2 \times 48\times10^{3} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Output Capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\times10^{-9} \times 25.2 \times 48\times10^{3} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-Time Diode Conduction (Two Edges per Period)

$$P_{dt} = V_F I \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\times10^{-9} \times 48\times10^{3} = \mathbf{0.614\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate Drive (Dissipated in Driver and Gate Resistors, Not the FET)

$$P_{g} = 2 Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\times10^{-9} \times 10 \times 48\times10^{3} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Switching Subtotal

$$P_{sw,ch} = 1.452 + 0.048 + 0.036 + 0.614 + 0.048 \approx \mathbf{2.20\ W}$$

**Design lever:** dead-time loss is about 28% of switching loss. Cutting t_dt to 50 ns, which AM32 and modern gate drivers support, saves about 0.3 W per channel.

## 3. Total Loss Budget at 80 A Burst

| Loss Term | Per Channel | ×4 Channels |
|---|---|---|
| MOSFET conduction | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| PCB copper | 3.84 W | 15.4 W |
| Switching (all terms) | 2.20 W | 8.8 W |
| ESC MCU, gate driver LDO, current shunt (est.) | 0.4 W | 1.6 W |
| H743 + BEC (FC side, shared) | — | ~1.5 W |
| **Total** | **~25.6 W** | **~104 W** |

**Efficiency at burst:** P_out per channel is about 25.2 × 80 × D. At full throttle, that is about 2 kW per channel, so η ≈ 98.7%. The problem is not efficiency. The problem is that roughly 100 W of heat lands on a few grams of PCB.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Junction-to-Case (Fast Time Constant): Not the Limit

- Electrical frequency at 30k RPM with 7 pole pairs: f_e = 3.5 kHz.
- Conduction interval per FET: T_e/3 ≈ 95 µs.
- Transient thermal impedance at 100 µs: Z_θJC ≈ 0.05 K/W (typical PQFN).

The junction ripple over the commutation cycle is small:

$$\Delta T_{ripple} \approx (9.6 + 1.5)\ \text{W} \times 0.05\ \text{K/W} \approx 0.6\ \text{K}$$
