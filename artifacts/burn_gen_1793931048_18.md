# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #25): Power-Stage Loss Model, DShot Timer Allocation and Reference Netlist

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and a Feasibility Warning

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller (FC). The 4-in-1 ESC uses its own MCU (one per motor or one shared), running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware with bi-directional DShot. The H743 does not commutate the motors. Combining both on one stack is normal. Making the H743 drive the bridges directly is not viable with ArduPilot.

**Baseline parameters.** Replace these with your real BOM values.

| Symbol | Meaning | Value |
|---|---|---|

| I_b | Burst phase current per motor | 80 A |
| f_sw | PWM frequency | 48 kHz |
| R_ds25 | MOSFET R_DS(on) at 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V (40 V DFN3.3×3.3 class) | 1.2 mΩ |
| α | R_DS(on) tempco (≈1.5× at 125 °C) | 0.005 /K |
| t_r, t_f | Rise and fall times at the chosen gate resistor | 15 ns each |
| Q_g | Gate charge | 45 nC |
| Q_rr | Body-diode reverse-recovery charge | 40 nC |
| Q_oss | Output charge | 50 nC |
| V_SD | Body-diode forward voltage | 0.8 V |
| t_dt | Dead time per edge | 100 ns |
| R_cu | Copper path per motor (bus + phase pads) | 0.5 mΩ |

> **Feasibility warning.** "80 A burst" on a 20×20 or 30×30 nano 4-in-1 is a marketing-class figure. The model below shows about 23 W per motor at 80 A, so roughly 90 W total if all four motors burst together. That load sits on a PCB with a few joules per kelvin of thermal mass. The design is survivable only for about 1–2 s bursts. Treat these numbers as limits to validate, not targets to advertise.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

| T_j | R_ds |
|---|---|
| 100 °C | 1.65 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 1.80 mΩ |

### 1.2 Current-path accounting (6-step, synchronous)

In each 60° commutation sector, one phase is PWM'd high, one phase is held low, and one floats.

- **PWM'd leg:** the high-side (HS) conducts for fraction D. The synchronous low-side (LS) conducts for 1−D, minus dead time.
- **Held leg:** the LS conducts continuously.

Each leg serves both roles across the six sectors. Averaging over one electrical cycle, each phase leg sees:

$$P_{HS}=\frac{I^2R_{ds}D}{3},\qquad P_{LS}=\frac{I^2R_{ds}(1-D)}{3}+\frac{I^2R_{ds}}{3}$$

Summing over three legs gives the per-motor conduction loss:

$$P_{cond,motor}=I^2R_{ds}\big[D+(1-D)+1\big]=2\,I^2R_{ds}\quad(\text{independent of }D)$$

$$P_{cu}=I^2R_{cu}$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| Case | R_ds | P_cond (FETs) | P_cu | Total per motor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cold (25 °C) | 1.20 mΩ | 15.4 W | 3.2 W | 18.6 W |
| Hot (100 °C) | 1.65 mΩ | 21.1 W | 3.2 W | 24.3 W |
| Hot (125 °C) | 1.80 mΩ | 23.0 W | 3.2 W | 26.2 W |

Thermal runaway is self-limiting only if the loop gain stays below 1:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\cdot R_{th} = 2I^2R_{ds25}\,\alpha\,R_{th,ja} < 1$$

At 80 A this gives 0.077 W/K × R_th. Stability therefore requires R_th,ja per motor below about 13 K/W, which is easily met. Runaway is not the concern here; total energy is.

### 1.4 Per-FET worst case

The hottest single device is the LS FET, at about (2−D)/3 · I²R. At D = 0.5 and 100 °C:

$$P_{LS}=0.5\times6400\times1.65\text{ m}=5.3\text{ W}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches. Per motor:

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard switching, inductive load)

$$P_{VI}=\tfrac12 V_{bus} I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw}=\tfrac12(25.2)(80)(30\text{n})(48\text{k})=1.45\text{ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (LS body diode recovering at HS turn-on)

$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}=40\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k}=0.048\text{ W}$$

This term is often dominated by the turn-on current overshoot it causes, not the loss itself. Budget an extra 10–20 % on P_VI.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss}\approx Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}=50\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k}=0.06\text{ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period)

$$P_{dt}=V_{SD}\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw}=0.8\times80\times200\text{n}\times48\text{k}=0.61\text{ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated mostly in R_g and the driver, not the FET)

$$P_g=2\,Q_gV_{GS}f_{sw}=2\times45\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k}=0.043\text{ W}$$

### 2.6 Switching total

$$P_{sw,motor}\approx1.45+0.05+0.06+0.61+0.04\approx\mathbf{2.2\text{ W}}$$

Note that P_sw falls to about 0 at D = 1 (100 % throttle, no PWM edges, only commutation). Mid-throttle sustained hover is where switching matters.

### 2.7 Per-motor and system total at 80 A, T_j ≈ 100 °C

$$P_{motor}\approx24.3+2.2\approx\mathbf{26.5\text{ W}},\qquad P_{4\times}\approx\mathbf{106\text{ W}}$$

$$\eta\approx1-\frac{26.5}{25.2\times80\times D_{eff}}$$

At D_eff ≈ 0.8 this gives η ≈ 98.4 %. Efficiency looks excellent while absolute heat is brutal.
