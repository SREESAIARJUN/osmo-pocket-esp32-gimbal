# STM32H743 AIO with 4-in-1 ESC: Power Stage, Thermal, and Bidirectional DShot Reference Design

**Engineering Basis Document, Job #24**

> **Scope and assumptions**
>
> 1. In a 4-in-1 AIO, the **STM32H743 is the flight controller MCU**. It generates DShot600 and decodes bidirectional eRPM telemetry. Each ESC channel's 48 kHz commutation/PWM runs on a **dedicated ESC MCU** (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32). The H743 does not drive gates directly.
> 2. All device parameters below are **assumed design values** representative of 40 V, ~1 mΩ-class 5×6 / 3.3×3.3 MOSFETs. Substitute your selected part's datasheet values; the equations are parameterized for that purpose.
> 3. Battery: **6S Li-ion/LiPo**, V_bus = 25.2 V max (design worst case), 22.2 V nominal.
> 4. "80 A burst" means **80 A RMS phase current per ESC channel, all four channels simultaneously**. This is the worst case.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Topology
With 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation and **synchronous complementary PWM** (AM32 default), two phase legs carry current at any instant:

- **PWM leg:** the high side conducts for fraction D and the low side (synchronous rectifier) for fraction 1−D.
- **Return leg:** the low side is fully on.

The current therefore always flows through exactly **two MOSFET channels in series**, independent of D.

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 R_{ds,hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{ph}^2 R_{ds,hot} = 2\,I_{ph}^2 R_{ds,hot}
$$

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ channel}
$$

Each of the 6 FETs conducts for about 1/3 of an electrical cycle, so:

$$
P_{cond,FET,avg} = \frac{19.2}{6} = 3.2\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.2 Electrothermal Coupling
R_ds(on) rises with junction temperature:

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ /\text{K}
$$

This creates positive feedback. For stability (no thermal runaway), the loop gain must be below 1:

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\, Z_{th,ja} = 2 I_{ph}^2 R_{ds,25}\,\alpha\, Z_{th,ja} < 1
$$

With 80 A and Z_th,ja,effective = 15 K/W (per-FET share of the board), the per-FET gain is about 3.2 W × 0.005 × 15 ≈ 0.24. That is stable, but it is **not negligible**. Iterate the operating point or use R_ds,hot directly, as done here.

### 2.3 PCB Copper Loss

$$
P_{Cu,ch} = I_{ph}^2 R_{pcb} = 6400 \times 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{1.92\ W}
$$

On a 2 oz/3 oz 6–8 layer nano board this is often underestimated. Use stitched parallel planes and avoid any neck-downs narrower than about 5 mm at phase pads.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the **PWM leg** hard-switches. The return leg commutes at the electrical frequency, which is negligible at f_e ≪ f_sw.

### 3.1 V·I Overlap (Hard Switching)

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}
= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

### 3.2 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I_{ph}\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

### 3.3 Reverse Recovery

$$
P_{rr} = V_{bus} Q_{rr} f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 50\,\text{nC} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}
$$

### 3.4 Output Capacitance (C_oss)
This term is already partially captured in t_r/t_f. As an explicit estimate with Q_oss ≈ 60 nC:

$$
P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 60\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{kHz} = 0.036\ \text{W}
$$

### 3.5 Gate Drive
This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FETs.

$$
P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\,\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\,\text{kHz} = 0.048\ \text{W per channel}
$$

### 3.6 Frequency Sensitivity
The frequency-dependent loss scales linearly with switching frequency:

$$
P_{f}(f_{sw}) = \left[\tfrac{1}{2}V I (t_r+t_f) + 2 V_{SD} I t_{dt} + V Q_{rr} + \ldots\right] f_{sw} \approx 54\ \mu\text{J} \times f_{sw}
$$

| f_sw | Frequency-dependent loss per channel |
|---|---|
| 24 kHz | 1.3 W |
| 48 kHz | 2.6 W |
| 96 kHz | 5.2 W |

At 80 A, **conduction dominates (~85 %)**, so raising f_sw for smoother low-throttle behavior costs comparatively little at burst.
