## 0. Architectural Clarification and Assumptions

**Correction to the job brief.** The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. On every production-grade AIO "FC + 4-in-1 ESC" design, the board is split into two subsystems:

| Subsystem | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743 at 480 MHz, running ArduPilot | Generates 4× bidirectional DShot600 and decodes eRPM telemetry |
| ESC ×4 | Dedicated ESC MCU per motor (e.g. AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or EFM8 running BLHeli_S/Bluejay) plus a 3-phase gate driver and 6 N-MOSFETs | 6-step commutation at 48 kHz PWM, BEMF zero-cross detection, DShot decode and telemetry reply |

The "48 kHz switching" therefore lives in the ESC firmware (AM32 supports 24–48 kHz). The "DMA timer allocation" lives in the H743.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification

During any 60° commutation sector, one phase leg is PWM-switched (say phase A), one phase is held low (phase B), and one phase floats (phase C).

- **A-high** conducts for duty fraction D.
- **A-low** conducts the freewheel current for (1 − D), because synchronous rectification is used.
- **B-low** conducts continuously.

The motor current I_m therefore always flows through exactly **two** MOSFET channels in series:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = I_m^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I_m^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$
