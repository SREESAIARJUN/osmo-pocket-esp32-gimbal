# STM32H743 Nano AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC): Burst Thermal, Switching Loss, and Bidirectional DShot Architecture (Job #32)

## 0. Feasibility Finding: Resolve This First

The math below shows that **80 A burst per motor channel cannot be sustained on a 20×20 mm nano board** for more than about 1 s from a warm start. Total burst dissipation reaches about 130 W into a board with roughly 3 J/K of thermal mass.

Before layout, the requirement must be pinned down as one of these:

| Interpretation | Feasible on 20×20? | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| 80 A **per channel** phase-current burst | No. Above 120 °C Tj in about 1 s | Move to a 30.5×30.5 or larger 4-in-1 with a heatsink |
| 80 A **total battery burst** (about 20 A per channel) | Yes, with margin | Proceed on the nano form factor |
| 80 A per channel for under 200 ms (e.g., punch-outs) | Marginal | Requires firmware current limiting and thermal derating |

All numbers below are parameterized, so they can be re-run for either case. The parameters marked *assumed* must be replaced with datasheet values for the selected MOSFET.

## 1. System Partitioning

- **Flight controller (H743):** runs ArduPilot and generates 4× DShot600 with bidirectional eRPM telemetry.
- **ESC section:** 4× independent ESC MCUs (e.g., AT32F421 running AM32, or an EFM8/BB21 with BLHeli_S/Bluejay). Each has a 3-phase gate driver and 6 N-MOSFETs.
- **Key point:** the 48 kHz PWM is generated by the **ESC MCUs**, not the H743. The H743 only sends throttle commands. Keep the two domains logically separate in the netlist.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Topology assumptions
- 6-step trapezoidal commutation with **synchronous (complementary) PWM**. This is the AM32/BLHeli default.
- During each 60° commutation sector, exactly two legs conduct:
  - The PWM leg: high-side on for fraction D, low-side on for (1−D) via synchronous rectification.
  - The return leg: low-side fully on.

The phase current I_ph therefore always flows through exactly **two** channel resistances, independent of D (neglecting dead time):

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot \left[\,2R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + R_{Cu} + R_{shunt}\right]$$

### 2.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

### 2.3 Bus vs. phase current
In trapezoidal drive, I_bus ≈ D · I_ph. An "80 A" rating must state which one is meant.

- At full throttle (D → 1) the two are equal.
- At partial throttle, phase current exceeds bus current. This is the worst case for an 80 A *bus* limit at D = 0.5, which gives 160 A phase.

### 2.4 Worked numbers (80 A phase, per channel)

**Assumed parameters** (40 V, 3.3×3.3 DFN-class MOSFET, required for 20×20):

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| R_DS(on),25 | 1.6 mΩ |
| R_Cu (phase pour + vias + pads) | 0.4 mΩ |
| R_shunt | 0.5 mΩ (one per channel, if current sensing is used) |
| Tj (design point) | 100 °C, giving R_DS(on) = 1.6 × 1.45 = 2.32 mΩ |

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \times (2 \times 2.32 + 0.4 + 0.5)\ \text{m}\Omega = 6400 \times 5.54\ \text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{35.5\ W/channel}$$

Breakdown:
- MOSFETs: 29.7 W total, about 14.8 W per conducting FET.
- Copper: 2.6 W.
- Shunt: 3.2 W. That is far beyond a 0805 shunt's 0.25–0.5 W rating, so the shunt must be omitted, or replaced with a 2512 / 0.2 mΩ part.

**Thermal-runaway check:**
- dP/dTj = 2 I² R₂₅ α = 2 × 6400 × 1.6 mΩ × 0.006 = 0.123 W/K.
- Loop gain = (dP/dT) · Rθ_ja,FET ≈ 0.123 × 2.5 ≈ 0.31, which is below 1. The design is stable, but losses grow about 45% from cold to hot.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. Per PWM period there is one high-side turn-on and one high-side turn-off. The low-side commutates through its body diode during dead time.

$$P_{sw} = \underbrace{\tfrac{1}{2}V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r + t_f) f_{sw}}_{\text{V-I overlap}} + \underbrace{Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}}_{\text{reverse recovery}} + \underbrace{Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}}_{\text{C}_{oss}\text{ (HS+LS)}} + \underbrace{2V_F I_{ph}t_{dt}f_{sw}}_{\text{dead-time diode}} + \underbrace{2Q_gV_{GS}f_{sw}}_{\text{gate drive}}$$

### 3.1 Overlap time estimate

$$t_{r,f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g}},\qquad I_g \approx \frac{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}{R_g + R_{drv}}$$

With Q_gd = 8 nC (*assumed*), a 10 V driver, a 3.5 V plateau, R_g + R_drv = 5 Ω:
- I_g ≈ 1.3 A, so t ≈ 6 ns intrinsic.
- With layout inductance and driver limits, use **t_r = t_f = 15 ns**.

### 3.2 Worked numbers (6S, V_bus = 25.2 V, 80 A)

| Term | Expression | Result |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 30 ns × 48 k | 1.45 W |
| Reverse recovery (Q_rr = 30 nC) | 30 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.036 W |
| C_oss (Q_oss = 40 nC, both FETs, conservative) | 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.048 W |
| Dead time (V_F = 0.8 V, t_dt = 100 ns) | 2 × 0.8 × 80 × 100 ns × 48 k | 0.61 W |
| Gate drive (Q_g = 50 nC, 10 V, 2 FETs) | 2 × 50 nC × 10 × 48 k | 0.048 W (mostly in driver/R_g) |
| **Total P_sw** | | **≈ 2.2 W/channel** |

**Observations:**
- At 48 kHz, switching loss is only about 6% of the total. **Conduction dominates by roughly 16:1.**
- Raising f_sw buys little. Halving R_DS(on) is the only meaningful lever.
