## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\quad \alpha\approx0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 0.70 × 1.60 = **1.12 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Conduction per channel (6-step, synchronous)

During each 60° sector, two legs carry the phase current I:

- **PWM leg:** the high side conducts for D and the low side conducts for (1−D).
- **Return leg:** the low side conducts 100 %.

These sum to a time-weighted conduction of D + (1−D) + 1 = 2 MOSFETs on at all times:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) + \frac{I_{ripple,pp}^2}{12}\cdot 2R_{DS}$$

The ripple term is negligible here (under 1 % for motor inductance of 10 µH or more at 48 kHz).

$$P_{cond,ch}=2(80)^2(1.12\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{14.3\ W}$$

### 1.3 Copper and interconnect loss

Assume a battery-pad → FET → motor-pad path of R_Cu ≈ 0.5 mΩ per channel (2 oz, 6 layers paralleled, with via farms). Copper TCR is 0.0039/K, giving about 0.65 mΩ at 100 °C.

$$P_{Cu,ch}=I^2R_{Cu}=6400\times0.65\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{4.2\ W}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-leg high side hard-switches. The low side commutates through its body diode during dead time.

| Mechanism | Equation | Value (25.2 V, 80 A, 48 kHz) |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f | ½·25.2·80·30n·48k = **1.45 W** |
| Dead-time diode (V_F = 0.8 V, 2 edges) | V_F·I·2t_dt·f | 0.8·80·400n·48k = **1.23 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f | 50n·25.2·48k = **0.06 W** |
| C_oss | ½·C_oss(er)·V²·f | 0.5·1.5n·635·48k = **0.02 W** |
| Gate charge (dissipated mostly in driver) | Q_g·V_GS·f per FET | 60n·10·48k = 0.029 W × 2 switching FETs = **0.06 W** |
| **Total switching per channel** | | **≈ 2.8 W** |

**Sensitivity:**

$$\frac{\partial P_{sw}}{\partial f}\approx 58\ \text{µW/Hz}$$

Every extra 10 kHz costs about 0.6 W per channel. Dead-time loss is comparable to overlap loss. Cutting dead time to 100 ns with a fast driver and low gate loop inductance recovers about 0.6 W per channel.

### 2.1 Totals

| | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 14.3 W | 57.2 W |
| Switching + diode | 2.8 W | 11.2 W |
| Copper | 4.2 W | 16.8 W |
| **Total** | **21.3 W** | **≈ 85 W** |

Efficiency at 80 A × 4 × 25.2 V × ~0.75 effective duty is about 6 kW in, so 85 W lost gives ~98.6 % efficiency. Efficiency is not the problem; **heat density** is.

### 2.2 Hottest-device check

The worst FET is the return-leg low side: it carries 80 A for its full 120° interval. Its average over an electrical cycle:

$$P_{LS,avg} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}(80^2)(1.12\text{m}) + \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)(80^2)(1.12\text{m}) + \text{diode share} \approx 2.4+0.6+0.4 = \mathbf{3.4\ W}$$

With R_θJC ≈ 1.0 K/W, junction-to-case rise is about 3.4 K. The die is fine. The thermal limit is board-to-ambient.

At low eRPM (under ~200 Hz electrical), the per-sector conduction time exceeds the package thermal time constant (~10–50 ms). Use the instantaneous 7.2 W for that FET in transient Z_θJC checks during hard punch-outs from hover.
