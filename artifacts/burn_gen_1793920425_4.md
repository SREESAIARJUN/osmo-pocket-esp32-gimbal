**Engineering flag up front:** "80A burst" on a nano-class board is a few-second rating, not a continuous one. Section 3 quantifies this. The realistic continuous rating is about 25–30 A per channel unless active cooling is used.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Parameters

| Symbol | Meaning | Value |
|---|---|---|
| I_ph | Phase current (burst) | 80 A |
| R_ds,25 | R_DS(on) max at 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | 1.2 mΩ |
| α | R_DS(on) temperature coefficient | 0.0045 /K |
| T_j | Design junction temperature | 100 °C |
| R_cu | PCB copper + solder path per conducting leg | 0.15 mΩ |

### 1.2 Temperature-Corrected R_DS(on)

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)] = 1.2\,(1 + 0.0045 \cdot 75) = 1.605\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

### 1.3 Commutation Topology

With six-step (trapezoidal) drive and complementary synchronous PWM, two phase legs conduct at any instant:

- **PWM leg:** current alternates between the high-side FET (duty D) and the low-side synchronous FET (1−D). Total I²R is independent of D, ignoring dead time.
- **Return leg:** one FET conducts continuously.

$$
P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 \left[ D\,R_{ds} + (1-D)\,R_{ds} \right] + I_{ph}^2 R_{ds} = 2\,I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.605\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{20.5\ W\ per\ channel}
$$

Adding copper loss:

$$
P_{cu} = 2\,I^2 R_{cu} = 2 \cdot 6400 \cdot 0.15\times10^{-3} = 1.9\ \text{W}
$$

### 1.4 Ripple Correction (Optional Refinement)

With phase inductance L_ph and switching frequency f_sw, the peak-to-peak ripple at D = 0.5 is ΔI = V_bus/(4 L f_sw). For L = 20 µH at 48 kHz, ΔI = 25.2/(4·20e-6·48e3) ≈ 6.6 A. The RMS correction is:

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}
$$

This adds about 3.6 A², which is under 0.06 %, so it is negligible at 80 A.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (f_sw = 48 kHz)

Only the PWM leg switches. Parameters: V_bus = 25.2 V (worst case 6S full), I = 80 A.

| Loss term | Equation | Parameters | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | t_r = t_f = 15 ns | **1.45 W** |
| Dead-time body diode | V_f·I·2·t_dt·f_sw | V_f = 0.8 V, t_dt = 200 ns | **1.23 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | Q_rr = 40 nC | 0.05 W |
| Output capacitance | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw | Q_oss = 30 nC | 0.02 W |
| Gate drive (mostly in driver) | Q_g·V_GS·f_sw·2 | Q_g = 50 nC, V_GS = 10 V | 0.05 W |
| **Total P_sw** | | | **≈ 2.8 W/ch** |

Worked overlap term:

$$
\tfrac{1}{2}(25.2)(80)(30\times10^{-9})(48\times10^{3}) = 1.45\ \text{W}
$$

**Design insight:** dead-time loss is nearly as large as overlap loss. Dropping dead time from 200 ns to 100 ns (if the driver and FET Q_gd permit) saves about 0.6 W per channel. Switching rise time follows t ≈ Q_gd / I_gate, so a stronger gate driver or a lower gate resistor trades loss for EMI and ringing.

### 2.1 Loss Summary at 80 A Burst

| Item | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| MOSFET conduction | 20.5 W | 82.0 W |
| Copper | 1.9 W | 7.6 W |
| Switching | 2.8 W | 11.2 W |
| Bus capacitor ESR (see 2.2) | ~0.5 W | ~2 W |
| **Total** | **~25.7 W** | **~103 W** |

Efficiency check: output power is about 4 × 80 A × ~22 V × D ≈ 7 kW at full duty, so efficiency is roughly 98.5 %. The efficiency is fine; the problem is the heat density.
