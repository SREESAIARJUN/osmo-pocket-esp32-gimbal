## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Reality Check

The architecture needs one clarification before the math, because it governs everything downstream.

**The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** It runs ArduPilot and sends DShot600 frames. Each of the four ESC channels has its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM, does commutation, and returns eRPM telemetry over the same wire (bi-directional DShot).

**"80A burst" on a nano form factor is a transient rating, not a continuous one.** Section 3 shows that the board can absorb roughly 1 to 2 s at 80A on all four channels before junction limits are reached. Any specification sheet for this product must state the burst duration and duty cycle.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{ds}(125) = 1.1 \times (1 + 0.005 \times 100) = 1.65\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Current paths in six-step commutation with synchronous rectification

During any 60° commutation sector, phase A is PWM-driven, phase B is held low, and phase C floats.

| FET | Conduction fraction |
|---|---|
| High-side A | D (duty cycle) |
| Low-side A | 1 − D (synchronous freewheel, AM32 complementary mode) |
| Low-side B | 1 (always on during sector) |

The energy per sector is therefore:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{ds}\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This result is independent of duty cycle, which is a useful property of complementary PWM.

At 80A:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.65\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{21.1\ W}$$

### 1.3 Copper and interconnect loss

$$P_{Cu,ch} = I^2 R_{Cu,path} = 6400 \times 0.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

### 1.4 Electro-thermal feedback (fixed-point solution)

Because R_ds depends on T_j and T_j depends on loss, the junction temperature is a fixed point:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + R_{th}\cdot P(T_j)$$

The loop converges only if:

$$R_{th}\cdot \frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j} = R_{th}\cdot 2 I^2 R_{ds,25}\,\alpha < 1$$

With the per-FET effective R_th ≈ 9.5 K/W from Section 3, the loop gain is:

$$9.5 \times (6400 \times 1.1\times10^{-3} \times 0.005)/3 \approx 0.11$$

This is stable with no thermal runaway at 80A. Steady state, however, is unreachable for thermal-capacity reasons, not runaway (see Section 3).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap (high-side PWM FET)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (low-side body diode, at high-side turn-on)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{nC}\times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

The reverse-recovery current also adds to the high-side turn-on overlap. In practice, 0.1 to 0.3 W of extra P_VI is common. Mitigate it by keeping the power loop inductance below 1 nH.

### 2.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{nF}\times 635 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.023\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

Two FETs commutate per PWM cycle:

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2\times50\text{nC}\times10\times48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

This loss is mostly dissipated in the gate driver and R_g, not in the FET die.
