## 0. Scope, Architecture Assumptions and Key Findings

Before the math, these assumptions must be fixed. Every number below follows from them, so change any one and recompute.

### 0.1 Architecture

The STM32H743 is the **flight controller**. It does not commutate motors directly. Each ESC channel has its own local MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071-class running AM32 or BLHeli_32-compatible firmware), a 3-phase gate driver, and 6 N-MOSFETs. The H743 talks to those MCUs via bidirectional DShot600.

Driving 12 half-bridges plus BEMF sensing directly from the H743 is possible in principle. It is not how 4-in-1 boards are built, and it would make the ArduPilot DShot discussion moot.

### 0.3 Headline Findings

1. **80 A per channel is only a burst rating.** It is thermally sustainable for roughly 1–2 seconds on a 20×20 board.
2. **Continuous capability is about 25–35 A per channel.** This depends heavily on airflow and on heat sinking through the battery and motor wires.
3. **Conduction loss dominates, at about 80% of total loss at 80 A.** Lowering R_ds(on) and copper resistance matters far more than tuning 48 kHz switching.
4. **DShot600 bidirectional on the H743 is comfortably feasible.** Place all 4 motors on one advanced timer, use 1 UP-DMA stream for output plus channel DMA for capture, all routed via DMAMUX.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Paths

With complementary (synchronous) PWM in 6-step commutation, current always flows through exactly **two FETs in series**:

- **ON interval (duty D):** high-side of phase A, then motor, then low-side of phase B.
- **OFF interval (1–D):** low-side of A (sync rectification), then motor, then low-side of B.

Either way the series resistance is 2·R_ds(on). The per-channel conduction loss is therefore nearly **duty-independent**:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \left(2R_{ds}(T_j) + R_{cu}\right)$$

### 1.2 Temperature Dependence

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha(T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.0 mΩ × (1 + 0.006 × 100) = **1.6 mΩ**

This is a positive feedback loop: higher T_j raises R_ds, which raises P, which raises T_j. Solve it as a fixed point:

$$T_j = T_a + Z_{th}\cdot P(T_j) \;\Rightarrow\; T_j = \frac{T_a + Z_{th}P_0(1-25\alpha)}{1 - Z_{th}P_0\alpha}$$

where P_0 is the loss at R_ds,25.

**Thermal runaway** occurs if Z_th · P_0 · α ≥ 1.
