# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #3): 80 A Burst Loss Model, 48 kHz Switching Model, BDShot600 DMA Plan

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and a Feasibility Warning

**Read this section first.** The numbers below show that an 80 A per-channel burst on a 20×20 mm nano 4-in-1 cannot be thermally sustained beyond about 1–2 seconds.

### 0.1 Architectural correction

The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot on the flight controller. It does not commutate the motors. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, for example an AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 talks to those MCUs over bidirectional DShot600.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path and per-channel loss

In 6-step operation with synchronous rectification, exactly two MOSFETs carry the phase current at any instant:
- the PWM leg, either its high side or its complementary low side, and
- the low side of the return phase.

So the per-channel conduction loss is independent of duty cycle:

```
P_cond,ch = I² · 2 · R_ds(T_j)
R_ds(T_j) = R_ds25 · [1 + α(T_j − 25)]      α ≈ 0.005 /°C  →  k_T ≈ 1.5 at 125 °C
```

| Case | R_ds | P_cond per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|---|
| Typ, 25 °C | 1.0 mΩ | 6400·2·0.0010 = **12.8 W** | 51.2 W |
| Typ, 125 °C | 1.5 mΩ | **19.2 W** | 76.8 W |
| Max, 125 °C | 1.95 mΩ | **25.0 W** | 99.8 W |

### 1.2 Per-device split

Each FET conducts for 120° of the electrical cycle:
- The low sides of non-PWM legs see the full 1/3 conduction.
- The PWM leg's high and low sides split their share by D and (1−D).

Averaged across the 6 FETs, the hottest-typ case (19.2 W) works out to about **3.2 W per FET**. A low-side FET at low duty is the worst individual case, at roughly 4–5 W.

### 1.3 Copper and shunt adders

Each 80 A phase loop crosses about 0.3 mΩ of total PCB copper (2 oz, short pours). That adds 6400 · 0.3 m ≈ **1.9 W per channel**. Use a single 0.25 mΩ shunt for aggregate current, not per channel.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 V–I overlap

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. Its complementary partner switches with zero voltage across it, after the body diode has conducted.

```
I_g,on  = (V_drv − V_plateau)/R_g = (12 − 3)/16 ≈ 0.56 A  →  t_r ≈ Q_gd/I_g,on  ≈ 10.7 ns
I_g,off =  V_plateau/R_g         = 3/16       ≈ 0.19 A  →  t_f ≈ Q_gd/I_g,off ≈ 32 ns

P_VI  = ½ · V_bus · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw
      = ½ · 25.2 · 80 · 43e-9 · 48e3 = 2.08 W
```

### 2.2 Secondary switching terms

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Dead-time body diode | V_f · I · 2t_dt · f_sw = 0.9·80·200 ns·48 kHz | 0.69 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw | 0.05 W |
| C_oss | 2 · ½ · C_oss(er) · V² · f_sw | 0.05 W |
| Gate drive (in driver) | 2 · Q_g · V_drv · f_sw | 0.05 W |
| **P_sw per channel** | | **≈ 2.9 W** |

### 2.3 Sensitivity

- P_VI scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz, switching loss drops to about 1.5 W.
- Turn-off dominates. A Schottky or PNP turn-off bypass on the gate resistor (R_g,off ≈ 3 Ω) brings t_f to about 6 ns and P_VI to about 0.8 W.
- The trade-off is higher dV/dt, so check gate ringing and layout.

## 3. Total Burst Dissipation

| Item | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction, typ hot | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching | 2.9 W | 11.6 W |
| Copper / shunt | 1.9 W | 7.6 W |
| ESC MCU, drivers, LDO | 0.3 W | 1.2 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 24.3 W** | **≈ 97 W** |

That is ~97 W into a 20×20 mm board while delivering about 4 × 80 × 22 ≈ 7 kW. Efficiency looks fine at about 98.6%. The problem is heat density.
