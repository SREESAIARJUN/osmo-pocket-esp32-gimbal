# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #58): Power-Stage, Thermal and DShot Timing Design Basis

## 0. Architecture Correction and Scope

**The STM32H743 should not commutate the motors.** In every production AIO "4-in-1" design, commutation is handled by four dedicated ESC MCUs (AT32F421, STM32G071 or EFM8BB51) running AM32 or BLHeli. The proposed partition is:

| Block | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 @ 400 MHz, ArduPilot | Attitude control. Sends bidirectional DShot600 to ESCs and decodes eRPM telemetry. |
| ESC MCU ×4 | AT32F421K8U7 (AM32) | 6-step BEMF commutation at 48 kHz center-aligned PWM |
| Gate driver ×4 | FD6288Q (3-phase, bootstrap) | Gate drive at about 10 V |
| Power stage ×4 | 6× 40 V N-MOSFET, PDFN 3.3×3.3 | Three half-bridges per motor |

**Feasibility flag.** "80 A burst" is interpreted below as **80 A per motor channel**, for worst-case analysis. On a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board, this is only survivable for about 1–3 seconds. Section 3 quantifies this. If 80 A is meant as the board total (20 A per motor), every loss figure drops by roughly 16×.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification

During each 60° commutation sector, two legs are active:

- **PWM leg:** the HS and LS FETs alternate complementarily. Current flows through HS for duty D and through LS (synchronous) for 1−D.
- **Return leg:** the LS FET is on continuously.

At any instant, exactly two FET channels carry I_ph. With synchronous rectification, the result is independent of duty:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

### 1.2 Distribution per FET

Each FET carries current for 120° of the electrical cycle (1/3 of the time):

$$P_{cond,FET} = \tfrac{1}{3} I_{ph}^2 R_{hot}$$

The split between HS and LS within the PWM leg depends on duty. At high duty, HS FETs run hotter. Size for the full 1/3 share each.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

$$P_{cond,FET} = 6400 \times 1.5\text{m}/3 = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

### 1.4 Copper loss (not negligible)

Assume 2 oz copper, 2.5 mm wide, 15 mm path, with four layers paralleled on the power net:

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{w\,t} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\times0.015}{0.0025\times70\mu\text{m}} \approx 1.47\ \text{m}\Omega \text{ per layer}$$

Four layers in parallel give about 0.37 mΩ per path segment. The current passes through two segments:

$$P_{Cu,ch} \approx 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.37\text{m} \approx 4.7\ \text{W}$$

At 80 A, copper loss competes with FET loss. Use stitched power planes and solder-bus the battery pads.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Per channel:

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw | ½·25.2·80·20 ns·48k = **0.968 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 40n·25.2·48k = **0.048 W** |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss·V·f_sw | **0.048 W** |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_SD·I·2t_dt·f_sw | 0.8·80·200n·48k = **0.614 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs, mostly dissipated in driver/R_g) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw | 2·40n·10·48k = **0.038 W** |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 1.72 W** |

**Key observation.** At 48 kHz, switching loss is only about 8% of conduction loss. The dead-time loss term is the one worth optimizing. Reducing t_dt to 50 ns, if the driver and FET Q_g allow it without shoot-through, saves about 0.3 W per channel.

### 2.1 Total dissipation (80 A burst, all four motors)

| Source | Per channel | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching | 1.72 W | 6.9 W |
| Copper | 4.7 W | 18.8 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, if fitted) | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 27.2 W** | **≈ 109 W** |

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Junction rise above the PCB (per FET, transient)

$$T_j = T_{PCB} + P_{FET}\cdot Z_{th,j\text{-}pcb}(t_p)$$

Worst FET: 3.2 W conduction + ~0.9 W switching share ≈ 4.1 W.

For PDFN 3.3×3.3, Z_th(j→pcb) is roughly 1.5 K/W (R_θJC) plus 6–8 K/W spreading at a pulse width of about 1 s. Taking ~8 K/W gives:

$$\Delta T_{j\text{-}pcb} \approx 4.1 \times 8 \approx 33\ \text{K}$$

### 3.2 Board-level lumped (RC) model

$$C_{th}\frac{dT_{pcb}}{dt} = P_{tot} - \frac{T_{pcb}-T_{amb}}{R_{th,conv}}$$

**Heat capacity.** A 25.5 mm board with 6 layers of 2 oz copper weighs about 6 g:
C_th ≈ (4 g FR4 × 1.1) + (2 g Cu × 0.385) ≈ **5.2 J/K**

**Convection under prop wash.** With h ≈ 80 W/m²K on both faces of a 0.00065 m² board:
R_th,conv ≈ 1/(80 × 0.0013) ≈ **9.6 K/W**, giving τ = R·C ≈ 50 s.
