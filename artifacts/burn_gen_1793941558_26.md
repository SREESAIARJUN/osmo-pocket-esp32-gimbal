## 0. Architecture Correction and Feasibility Flags

Three points need resolving before any math.

**1. The STM32H743 should not commutate the motors.** Production 4-in-1 ESCs run a dedicated small MCU per motor (or one per pair), running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical choices are the AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller and talks to those ESC MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. The rest of this document uses that partition:

```
[STM32H743 FC / ArduPilot] --4x BDShot600--> [4x AT32F421 / AM32] --> [4x 3-phase gate driver] --> [24x N-MOSFET]
```

**2. "80 A burst" on a nano board (20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm) is a seconds-scale rating.**
- Four motors at 80 A would draw a 320 A battery burst. XT30 and XT60 leads, 14–16 AWG wire, and the board copper cannot carry that continuously.
- Section 3 shows roughly **1–5 s of adiabatic headroom**, depending on how many motors burst at once.
- Specify the product as "80 A single-channel burst, ≤2 s" and include a firmware thermal and current limit. Do not use a bare "80 A" rating.

**3. Operating point.** The model assumes:
- 6S battery: V_bus,max = 25.2 V.
- 40 V-rated FETs, which leave about 37% margin for ringing on a 25.2 V bus. Validate this with layout-level double-pulse testing.
- f_PWM = 48 kHz.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Commutation Topology

The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification and complementary PWM on the active high side.

In each 60° sector:
- Phase A high side (HS) switches at duty D.
- Phase A low side (LS) conducts for the complementary fraction (1−D).
- Phase B LS is held fully on.

Instantaneous conduction resistance in the current path is therefore:

$$
R_{path} = D\cdot R + (1-D)\cdot R + R = 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

### 1.2 Temperature-Corrected On-Resistance

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

At a design-limit junction temperature of T_j = 125 °C:

$$
R(125) = 1.3\,\text{m}\Omega\times1.45 = 1.885\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

### 1.3 RMS Current Including PWM Ripple

Peak-to-peak ripple current:

$$
\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{L\,f_{sw}},\qquad \text{worst case at } V_{bemf}\approx V_{bus}(1-D),\ D=0.5
$$

$$
\Delta I_{max} = \frac{25.2\times0.25}{30\,\mu\text{H}\times48\,\text{kHz}} = 4.38\ \text{A}
$$

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 6400 + 1.6 \approx 6401.6\ \text{A}^2
$$

Ripple adds about 0.03%, so it is negligible at 48 kHz.
