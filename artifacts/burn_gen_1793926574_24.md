## 0. Architecture Assumptions

These assumptions drive every number below. Change any of them and the numbers must be re-derived.

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Topology | **H743 runs ArduPilot (FC only).** Each motor has its own ESC MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 class, running AM32) driving a 3-phase gate driver. The H743 does not commutate motors, so the 48 kHz PWM lives in the ESC MCUs. |
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max (worst case for switching loss), 22.2 V nominal |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal, complementary (synchronous) PWM, f_sw = 48 kHz |
| MOSFET | Representative 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN. R_DS(on),25 = 1.2 mΩ, Q_gd ≈ 8 nC, Q_g ≈ 40 nC, Q_rr ≈ 30 nC, C_oss,er ≈ 800 pF, V_SD ≈ 0.8 V, R_θJC ≈ 1.5 K/W |
| Board | 30.5×30.5 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz copper |
| Burst | 80 A **phase current per channel**, all four channels simultaneously (worst case) |

**Feasibility flag.** 80 A per channel on a 20×20 mm "nano" board is not thermally credible. Even at 30.5×30.5 mm it is a burst rating of a few seconds, not a continuous rating. Section 3 proves this quantitatively. Replace the MOSFET placeholders with your selected part's datasheet values; derating with a 60 V part (higher R_DS(on)) is strongly advised for 6S regen spikes.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous PWM
During any 60° commutation sector, three devices carry current. Let the phase current be I and the duty cycle be D:

| Device | Conduction fraction | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| Active leg high side | D | I²·R·D |
| Active leg low side (sync rectification) | 1−D | I²·R·(1−D) |
| Return leg low side | 1 | I²·R |

Summing the three rows gives the per-channel conduction loss, which is **independent of D**:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence
$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

$$R = 1.2 \times (1 + 0.006 \times 85) = 1.81\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Copper and shunt losses
- Current shunt: R_sh = 0.25 mΩ, so P_sh = 80² × 0.25 m = **1.60 W**.
- PCB copper (two current paths ≈ 0.15 mΩ each, 2 oz with via stitching): P_Cu ≈ 2 × 6400 × 0.15 m = **1.92 W**.

### 1.4 Result at 80 A
$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.81\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{23.2\ W}\ \text{per channel}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz, 25.2 V)

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard-switched, one switching leg per sector)
Transition time is set by the Miller charge and the gate drive current. Assuming I_g ≈ 0.5 A:

$$t_r \approx t_f \approx Q_{gd}/I_g \approx 15\ \text{ns}$$

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Body-diode reverse recovery
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance
$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,er} V^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 800\text{p} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.012\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction
With t_dt = 200 ns per edge and two edges per period:

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

Dead time costs almost as much as switching. Tuning AM32 dead time to the minimum safe value is the cheapest efficiency gain available.
