# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #41): Design Model

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

No MOSFET, motor, stack size, or battery was specified, so this model uses representative values. **Every number marked ⚠ must be replaced with the datasheet value for the parts you actually select.** The equations hold for any parts; only the substitutions change.

> **Architectural flag:** 80 A per channel on a "nano" (20×20 mm) footprint is not credible. There is not enough copper cross-section or thermal mass, and you cannot fit 24 FETs with adequate spacing. This model assumes the 30.5×30.5 mm class. If the product truly must be 20×20 mm, derate to roughly 35–45 A burst.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path
- In 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation, phase current at any instant flows through one high-side FET of one phase and one low-side FET of another.
- With complementary PWM, during the off-time (1−D) the freewheeling current flows through the *synchronous* low-side FET instead of the body diode.
- The series path therefore always contains exactly **two channels' worth of R_DS(on)**:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot \left(2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + R_{Cu}\right)
$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence of R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}\ ⚠
$$

At a design junction temperature T_j = 110 °C:

$$
R_{DS(on)} = 0.7\,(1+0.006\cdot85) = 0.7 \cdot 1.51 = 1.06\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

### 1.3 Ripple correction
Peak-to-peak ripple current, using worst-case duty D = 0.5:

$$
\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L\,f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2\cdot0.25}{20\,\mu\text{H}\cdot48\,\text{kHz}} = 6.56\ \text{A}
$$

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I_{DC}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.6 = 6403.6\ \text{A}^2
$$

The ripple contribution is negligible (0.06%). This is a benefit of the high 48 kHz switching frequency.

### 1.4 Copper path resistance
Estimate R_Cu for battery pad → bus plane → FET → phase pad. For 4 oz copper, sheet resistance is ≈ 0.12 mΩ/□ at 25 °C, rising to ≈ 0.16 mΩ/□ at 100 °C. For about 2 squares of effective path, R_Cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ ⚠. This must be verified with a PDN/IR-drop solver.

### 1.5 Result

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 6403.6 \cdot (2\cdot1.06 + 0.3)\times10^{-3} = 6403.6 \cdot 2.42\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{15.5\ W}
$$

Of this, 13.6 W is in the FETs and 1.9 W is in the copper.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

With synchronous rectification, only the active (control) FET sees hard-switched V·I overlap. The sync FET switches at ~0 V (ZVS) after the dead time but contributes body-diode and reverse-recovery losses.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard switching)

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw}
$$

Gate-driver-limited transition times through the Miller plateau, with R_g,total ≈ 3 Ω:

$$
t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,R_g}{V_{drv}-V_{plat}},\quad Q_{gd}=15\text{ nC},\ V_{drv}=10,\ V_{plat}=4.5
$$

This gives t_r ≈ 8.2 ns; with driver current limiting, take t_r ≈ 12 ns and t_f ≈ 10 ns ⚠.

$$
P_{VI} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot22\times10^{-9}\cdot48\times10^3 = \mathbf{1.06\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two dead times per period)

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD}\,I\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot400\times10^{-9}\cdot48\times10^3 = \mathbf{1.23\ W}
$$

This is significant. Dropping the dead time to 100 ns, which needs a driver with good propagation-delay matching, halves it.

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 40\times10^{-9}\cdot25.2\cdot48\times10^3 = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = Q_{oss}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw}\ \text{(conservative)} = 50\times10^{-9}\cdot25.2\cdot48\times10^3 = \mathbf{0.060\ W}
$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in driver and R_g, not the FET)

$$
P_{gate} = 2\,Q_g\,V_{drv}\,f_{sw} = 2\cdot60\times10^{-9}\cdot10\cdot48\times10^3 = 0.058\ \text{W per channel}
$$

### 2.6 Switching total
P_sw,ch ≈ 1.06 + 1.23 + 0.05 + 0.06 = **2.40 W per channel**.

### 2.7 Frequency trade-off
- Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw: at 24 kHz it would be ≈ 1.2 W.
- At 24 kHz, however, ripple doubles (13 A pp), and motor iron and copper losses rise.
- 48 kHz is a reasonable choice for low-inductance racing motors.
