## 0. Architectural Clarification

There are two separate processor domains in an "STM32H743 4-in-1 ESC" product. The math only makes sense if they are kept apart.

| Domain | Silicon | Firmware | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIH6 (400 MHz) | ArduPilot | Generates bi-directional DShot600, decodes eRPM |
| ESC (×4 channels) | Typically AT32F421 / STM32G071 / GD32E230 (one per motor, or one dual-motor MCU) | AM32 / BLHeli_32 / Bluejay-class | Commutation, 48 kHz PWM, gate drive, telemetry reply |

The H743 does **not** switch the power MOSFETs. The 48 kHz switching loss model applies to the ESC MCUs and gate drivers. The DMA/timer model applies to the H743.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current distribution in 6-step commutation

In each 60° electrical sector, exactly one high-side and one low-side FET carry the phase current. Over a full electrical cycle, each phase leg conducts for 240°: 120° high, 120° low, 120° floating.

With complementary PWM at duty *D*, during a phase's high-side 120° window:
- High-side (HS) conducts for fraction *D*.
- Low-side (LS) of the same leg free-wheels (synchronous rectification) for fraction (1−D).

The RMS current per FET is therefore:

$$
I_{rms,HS}^2 = \frac{I_{ph}^2}{3}\,D,\qquad
I_{rms,LS}^2 = \frac{I_{ph}^2}{3}\,(1-D) + \frac{I_{ph}^2}{3}
$$

The second LS term covers the LS FET's own 120° commutation window, where it is held fully on in high-side-PWM schemes. Schemes differ, so treat this as worst case.

### 1.2 Hot R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big]\approx 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega\times1.5 = 2.25\,\text{m}\Omega
$$

### 1.3 Conduction loss, worst case at full throttle (D → 1)

$$
P_{cond,FET} = \frac{I_{ph}^2}{3}R_{DS(on),hot} = \frac{6400}{3}\times2.25\times10^{-3} = 4.80\ \text{W}
$$

Because two FETs conduct at any instant, the per-motor total is simply:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on),hot} = 2\times6400\times2.25\times10^{-3} = 28.8\ \text{W}
$$

$$
P_{cond,board} = 4\times28.8 = 115.2\ \text{W}\quad(\text{all four motors at }80\text{ A simultaneously})
$$

### 1.4 Additional series resistances

These are frequently ignored but are first-order on a nano board:

| Element | Typical R | Loss at 80 A per motor |
|---|---|---|
| Phase pad + copper pour (2 oz, ~10 mm) | 0.3 mΩ | 1.9 W |
| Current shunt (if per-phase or per-ESC) | 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| Battery path (shared, 320 A total) | 0.5 mΩ | **51 W** (I²R at 320 A) |

**Red flag:** 4 × 80 A means 320 A from the battery. That exceeds any XT60 or XT90 continuous rating and most 6S pack C-ratings. Real systems never see all four motors at 80 A simultaneously. The 80 A figure is a per-channel burst rating, usually defined at 10 s or less, with the other channels lower.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Switching transition times (gate-charge method)

During the Miller plateau, the gate current is:

$$
I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_g} = \frac{10-4}{5} = 1.2\ \text{A},\qquad
I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_g} = 0.8\ \text{A}
$$

$$
t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = 6.7\ \text{ns},\qquad
t_{f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,off}} = 10\ \text{ns}
$$
