# STM32H743 Nano AIO (20×20) with 4-in-1 ESC: Design Package, Job #23

## 0. Architectural Corrections and Scope

Three constraints shape everything below:

1. **The H743 is not the ESC controller.** It runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. Each motor's commutation runs on a dedicated ESC MCU (the reference design uses 1× AT32F421 or STM32G071 per motor running AM32, or a quad-MCU arrangement). Bi-directional DShot is the link between them. Driving 12 MOSFETs at 48 kHz from the H7 while it runs EKF3 is not viable under ArduPilot.
2. **20×20 mm with H743 plus 4-in-1 requires a stack.** Board A carries the FC (STM32H743VIH6, TFBGA100, 8×8 mm). Board B carries the 4-in-1 ESC. A single-board AIO cannot fit 24 power FETs, 4 drivers, 4 ESC MCUs and the H7 at this size.
3. **80 A burst is thermally a ~1–2 s rating per motor, not a sustained 4-motor rating.** Section 3 proves this. Spec-sheet language must say "80 A burst (≤1 s, single motor)" or the claim is indefensible.

The component values below are representative parametric placeholders. **Verify every one against the selected datasheets** before layout freeze.

## 2. Mathematical Loss Model (per motor, 6-step trapezoidal, complementary PWM)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

During any commutation sector, current I flows through two legs:

- **Switched leg (PWM, duty D):** the high-side FET conducts for D. The synchronous low-side conducts for (1−D).
- **Fixed leg:** the low-side FET is on 100%.

At every instant, exactly two channels carry I. So:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds(on),25}\,k_T$$

The phase-current ripple adds an RMS correction:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}$$

With ΔI_pp ≈ 10 A (low-inductance racing motor at 48 kHz), the correction term is 8.3 A², which is negligible against 6400 A².

**At 80 A:**

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.5\text{m} \times 1.6 = \mathbf{30.7\ W}$$

This splits as follows:
- Switched-leg HS: D·15.4 W
- Switched-leg LS: (1−D)·15.4 W
- Fixed-leg LS: 15.4 W

**The fixed-leg low-side FET is the hottest single device: 15.4 W for 1/3 of an electrical cycle.**

### 2.2 Hard-Switching Loss (one HS FET commutates per PWM period)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 1.45\ W$$

### 2.3 Reverse-Recovery and Output-Capacitance Loss

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ W$$

$$P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 0.030\ W$$

### 2.4 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f\,I\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.85 \times 80 \times 400\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 1.31\ W$$

### 2.5 Gate Drive (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors)

$$P_g = Q_g\,V_{gs}\,f_{sw} \times N_{switching} = 40\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = 0.038\ W$$

### 2.6 Total per Motor at 80 A Burst

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction | 30.70 |
| V-I switching | 1.45 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.31 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.08 |
| Gate | 0.04 |
| **Total** | **≈ 33.6 W** |

Efficiency check at 80 A, D = 0.8: P_out ≈ 25.2 × 0.8 × 80 ≈ 1613 W, so the loss is about 2%. Electrically this is fine. The difficulty is purely thermal.

**General closed form (for sweeps):**

$$P_{motor}(I,T_j) = 2I^2R_{25}\left[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\right] + I\,f_{sw}\left[\tfrac{1}{2}V(t_r+t_f) + 2V_f t_{dt}\right] + V f_{sw}(Q_{rr}+Q_{oss}) + 2Q_gV_{gs}f_{sw}$$

Here α ≈ 0.006 /K, which gives k_T ≈ 1.6 at 125 °C.

**Note on positive feedback.** R_ds(on) depends on T_j. The model must be iterated, because thermal runaway occurs if

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\,R_{th} \ge 1$$

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Steady State (shows why 80 A cannot be continuous)

Per-FET path:
- Junction→case (bottom): R_jc ≈ 1.2 K/W
- Case→PCB through solder and thermal vias: ≈ 3 K/W
- PCB→ambient for a 20×20 board share with prop-wash: ≈ 6 K/W per motor-quadrant

For the hottest FET (15.4 W at 1/3 duty, about 5.1 W average plus its share of the spread board heating):

$$T_j \approx T_a + P_{FET}(R_{jc}+R_{cs}) + P_{quad}R_{sa} = 35 + 5.1(4.2) + 33.6(6) \approx 258\ °C$$

**This fails.** Continuous rating must come from the inverse calculation. Solving for T_j ≤ 125 °C with T_a = 35 °C gives P_quad ≤ ~13 W, which corresponds to **I_cont ≈ 45–50 A per motor**. That is the honest continuous number.

Board B estimate: 6-layer, 2 oz, plus FETs. Mass ≈ 3 g.

$$C_{th} \approx m\,c_p \approx 3\text{ g} \times 0.9\ \text{J/(g·K)} \approx 2.7\ \text{J/K (whole board)}$$
