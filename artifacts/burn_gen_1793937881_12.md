**Revision:** A0 (reference architecture). **Status:** for design review, not release.

## 0. Architectural Assumptions and Design Risks

These assumptions shape every number below. Change any of them and the model must be re-run.

| Parameter | Assumed value | Note |

| Ambient | 35 °C | |

**Critical design risks, flagged up front:**

- **R1 (form factor).** The math below shows 80 A per motor generates about 125 W of total loss. A 20×20 mm "nano" board cannot sink that, even for a short burst. The minimum credible format is 30.5×30.5 mm with 5×6 mm DFN MOSFETs. The 20×20 option is only viable at about 35–40 A burst.
- **R2 (power isolation).** Co-locating an H743 (IMU, baro) with 320 A switching on one PCB requires strict ground-partitioning and IMU thermal isolation (Section 4).
- **R3 (component validation).** Part values below are representative. Verify all Rds(on), Qg, and Rθ figures against the specific datasheet revision you procure.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in six-step synchronous commutation

At any instant one phase is driven high via PWM, one is held low, and one floats. With synchronous rectification, the current path always runs through exactly two conducting FETs:

- **During the PWM on-time (D):** HS(phase A) → motor → LS(phase B).
- **During the PWM off-time (1−D):** LS(phase A, synchronous) → motor → LS(phase B).

So the instantaneous conduction loss is independent of duty cycle (excluding dead-time):

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2 \cdot R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature-corrected Rds(on)

Use a 40 V DFN5×6 class part (e.g., Infineon BSC014N04LS or the Toshiba TPH1R204PB class):

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25} \cdot \left(1 + \alpha\right)^{(T_j - 25)}, \qquad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \mathrm{/K}$$

With R₂₅ = 1.4 mΩ (max at Vgs = 10 V) and Tj = 125 °C:

$$R_{DS(on)}(125^\circ C) = 1.4\ \mathrm{m\Omega} \times 1.0045^{100} \approx 1.4 \times 1.566 = 2.19\ \mathrm{m\Omega}$$

**Gate drive note.** The FD6288Q-class drivers used on most ESCs provide about 10–12 V (bootstrapped from a 12 V rail). Do **not** use a 5 V gate drive. Rds(on) at Vgs = 4.5 V is about 1.5× worse.

### 1.3 Ripple correction

Phase inductance is L ≈ 15 µH (typical 2207–2810 class motor). At D = 0.5 (worst case):

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus} \cdot D(1-D)}{L \cdot f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{15\times10^{-6} \times 48\times10^{3}} = 8.75\ \mathrm{A}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{DC}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 6.4 = 6406.4\ \mathrm{A^2}$$

The ripple contribution is about 0.1%, so it is negligible at 48 kHz. That is one benefit of 48 kHz over 24 kHz.

### 1.4 Conduction loss result

$$P_{cond,motor} = 6406.4 \times 2 \times 2.19\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{28.1\ W\ per\ motor}$$

$$P_{cond,total} = 4 \times 28.1 = \mathbf{112.2\ W}$$

### 1.5 Per-FET loss distribution

Each FET conducts for about 1/3 of the electrical cycle (high side, weighted by D and 1−D for its synchronous partner). For thermal averaging, assume the electrical period is much shorter than the die thermal time constant (~1–5 ms). At 30,000 RPM with 14 poles, f_elec = 3.5 kHz, so the assumption holds.

$$\bar{P}_{FET,cond} \approx \frac{P_{cond,motor}}{6} = 4.68\ \mathrm{W}$$

The worst-case low-side FET at high duty can reach about 1.5× the average, roughly **7 W**.

### 1.6 Shunt and copper losses

- **Current-sense shunt.** At 0.25 mΩ, P = 80² × 0.25 m = **1.6 W per motor**. Consider a single 0.1 mΩ main bus shunt instead.
- **Copper.** For 2 oz copper over a 15 mm trace at 8 mm wide, R ≈ 0.62 m × 15/8 × 0.5 ≈ 0.6 mΩ. Phase copper adds about 0.7 mΩ per path, giving **≈ 4.5 W per motor**. This is not negligible: use 8 layers of 2 oz copper, with all inner layers stitched as parallel power planes.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one half-bridge switches per commutation step.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I \,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}$$

With an FD6288Q driver, R_g,ext = 4.7 Ω, and FET Qgd ≈ 7 nC: t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 12 ns.

$$P_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 24\times10^{-9} \times 48\times10^{3} = 1.16\ \mathrm{W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

With Vf = 0.8 V and t_dt = 100 ns per edge (two edges per cycle):

$$P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\times10^{-9} \times 48\times10^{3} = 0.61\ \mathrm{W}$$
