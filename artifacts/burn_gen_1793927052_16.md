# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #16): Design Model and Reference Netlist

## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility Flags

The numbers below come from an explicit parameter set (§1). Swap in your actual MOSFET datasheet values and re-run. The equations are general, but the outputs depend on those parameters.

Three architecture issues need to be fixed before layout.

| # | Issue | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | The H743 does not do the 48 kHz switching. On every 4-in-1 design, each ESC channel has its own MCU (e.g. AT32F421 running AM32, or EFM8 running BLHeli_S/Bluejay). The H743 only sends bidirectional DShot600 to those MCUs. | The switching-loss model (§3) applies to the ESC MCU firmware's PWM. The DMA model (§5) applies to the H743. |
| F2 | "80A burst" is per-channel and non-simultaneous. Four channels at 80 A phase current and high duty implies about 250–320 A battery current (>6 kW at 6S). A nano board, its power traces, and an XT60 (≈60 A continuous) cannot carry that. | The burst rating must be specified as single-channel, time-limited, and duty-limited. |
| F3 | A 20×20 mm board cannot reject tens of watts at steady state. | §4 shows 80 A is a transient rating of roughly 1–4 s. Continuous rating should be about 25–35 A per channel. |

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

**Commutation basis.** With 6-step trapezoidal drive (AM32/BLHeli) and synchronous rectification ("complementary PWM"), two conducting devices are always in the current path. During ON, those are the high side of phase A and the low side of phase B. During OFF, they are the low side of phase A (sync-rectifying) and the low side of phase B. So the channel conduction loss is independent of duty, to first order:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This loss is shared across six FETs over an electrical revolution. On average, each FET carries about 1/3 of it:

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}\cdot 2 I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j) \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} \;=\; \tfrac{1}{3} I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

The worst-case instantaneous FET (the high side at near-100% duty) carries the full I²R.

**At 25 °C:**
- I²·R = 6400 × 1.2 mΩ = **7.68 W**
- P_cond,ch = **15.4 W**
- Average per FET ≈ **2.56 W**
- Peak per FET = **7.68 W**

**Temperature feedback (closed form).** Substitute T_j = T_a + P·R_th into P = I²R₂₅[1+α(T_j−25)] and solve:

$$T_j = \frac{T_a + I^2R_{25}R_{th}(1-25\alpha)}{1 - I^2R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_{th}}$$

The thermal-runaway criterion is:

$$I^2R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_{th} \ge 1$$

For one average FET (2.56 W, isolated R_th,ja ≈ 25 K/W on dense 2 oz copper):

$$T_j = \frac{40 + 2.56\cdot25\cdot0.875}{1-2.56\cdot0.005\cdot25} = \frac{96}{0.68} \approx 141\,^\circ C$$

This exceeds the 125 °C design limit, so 80 A is not a continuous rating even before board-level heat coupling (§4). At 150 °C, R_ds ≈ 1.95 mΩ (+63%), which gives **P_cond,ch ≈ 25 W**.

## 3. Switching Losses at 48 kHz (Per Channel, One PWM-ing Leg)

| Mechanism | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw | ½·25.2·80·20 ns·48 k = **0.97 W** |
| Dead-time diode | V_f·I·2t_dt·f_sw | 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 50 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.06 W** |
| Coss | Q_oss·V·f_sw | 30 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.04 W** |
| Gate drive (6 FETs, driver side) | N·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw | 6·30 nC·10·48 k = **0.09 W** |
| **Total P_sw,ch** | | **≈ 1.77 W** |

Strictly, only the two FETs of the PWM-ing leg toggle at f_sw, so the gate-drive term is conservative.

**Sensitivity to switching frequency.** The switching terms scale linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz, P_sw ≈ 0.9 W. At 96 kHz, it is ≈ 3.5 W.

At 80 A, conduction dominates (≈ 93%). 48 kHz is a good choice for low-inductance, high-KV motors (lower current ripple, quieter). The extra ~0.9 W versus 24 kHz is acceptable.
