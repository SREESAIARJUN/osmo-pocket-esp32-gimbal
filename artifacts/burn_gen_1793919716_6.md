## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in six-step synchronous PWM

At any instant, two phases are active:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side (HS) conducts for duty D. The low-side (LS) conducts for (1−D) as a synchronous rectifier, so current always flows through exactly one FET of this leg.
- **Return leg:** the LS is held on continuously.

Two FETs are therefore always in series with the motor current:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

This is independent of D, apart from dead-time diode intervals, which are handled in §2.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right]$$

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond per channel at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.500 mΩ | 2·6400·0.0015 = **19.2 W** |
| 100 °C | 2.006 mΩ | **25.7 W** |
| 125 °C | 2.175 mΩ | **27.8 W** |

### 1.3 Electrothermal fixed point

Because R_DS(on) rises with temperature, the true junction temperature is the self-consistent solution of:

$$T_j = T_a + R_{th}\,P(T_j) \;\Rightarrow\; T_j = \frac{T_a + R_{th}\,P_0\,(1-25\alpha)}{1 - R_{th}\,P_0\,\alpha}, \quad P_0 = 2I^2R_{25}$$

Thermal runaway occurs when R_th·P_0·α ≥ 1. With P_0 = 19.2 W per channel, the runaway threshold is R_th ≥ 11.6 K/W, using the per-channel effective R_th to ambient.

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

Over one electrical revolution, each of the 6 FETs carries its share. The per-FET average is approximately P_cond/6. That gives **4.6 W per FET at 125 °C**. Instantaneous peaks are about 3× that (13.9 W) while a FET is in an active interval.

### 1.5 Non-FET conduction losses (often larger than people expect)

| Element | Value | Loss at 4×80 A (320 A battery-side, ~D-weighted) |
|---|---|---|
| Current shunt (shared) | 0.25 mΩ | I²R at 320 A: **25.6 W** (use 0.1 mΩ or Hall/per-phase sensing) |
| 2 oz Cu bus planes, ~0.3 mΩ effective | 0.3 mΩ | ~**30 W** at 320 A bus |
| Phase pads/traces per channel | ~0.2 mΩ | 80²·0.2 mΩ = 1.3 W/ch |

Battery current is I_bus ≈ D·I_phase. At full-throttle burst, D → 1, so 320 A battery current is real.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Hard-switching transition loss (PWM leg HS)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I \,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}$$

$$t_{r,f} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_g}, \quad I_g = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}{R_g + R_{drv}}$$
