**Architectural correction.** The H743 does not drive the bridges, and at 48 kHz it should not. Production 4-in-1 designs use one small ESC MCU per motor running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to those MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. The design below follows that split:

- **FC domain:** STM32H743 running ArduPilot.
- **ESC domain:** 4 × AT32F421K8U7 running AM32, 4 × FD6288Q 3-phase gate drivers, 24 × MOSFETs.

**Feasibility warning.** 80 A on a nano-class AIO is a **burst-only rating, about 1–2 s**. Section 3 shows that continuous operation at this level is physically impossible on this board.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_J) = R_{25}\,\big[1 + \alpha (T_J - 25)\big],\quad \alpha \approx 0.004\text{ K}^{-1}$$

At T_J = 150 °C: R = 1.5 mΩ × 1.5 = **2.25 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current path per channel

At any instant during a commutation step, the phase current flows through:

- the PWM-ing high-side FET (for a fraction D of the period), or its complementary low-side FET (for 1 − D), plus
- the continuously-on low-side FET of the return phase.

With synchronous rectification, exactly **two FETs are always in series**, independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_J)$$

### 1.3 Copper loss

Add the copper path from the battery pad, through the bridge, to the motor pad. Assume a 15 mm path of 3 mm width, 4 paralleled 2 oz layers, plus vias. This gives R_Cu ≈ 0.4 mΩ per channel (including return).

$$P_{Cu,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{Cu}\cdot D_{eff}$$

The worst-case bound uses D_eff = 1, giving 80² × 0.4 mΩ = 2.6 W.

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A

| Term | Per channel | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction: 80² × 2 × 2.25 mΩ | **28.8 W** | 115.2 W |
| Copper (worst case) | 2.6 W | 10.2 W |

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing half-bridge hard-switches. The return-phase LS FET is static during the step.

### 2.1 V–I overlap

Transition time is set by gate charge and gate current:

$$t_{r,f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g},\qquad I_g = \frac{V_{DRV} - V_{plateau}}{R_g + R_{g,int}}$$

With V_DRV = 10 V, V_plateau ≈ 4 V, and R_g,total ≈ 6 Ω, the gate current is I_g ≈ 1 A, so t ≈ 8 ns from Q_gd alone. Allowing for driver limits, Miller and layout effects, assume t_r + t_f ≈ 40 ns.

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f)f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot40\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per period. With t_dt = 100 ns and V_F = 0.8 V:

$$P_{dt} = 2\,V_F I_{ph} t_{dt} f_{sw} = 2\cdot0.8\cdot80\cdot100\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$
