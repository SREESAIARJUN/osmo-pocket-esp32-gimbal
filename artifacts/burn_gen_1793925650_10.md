## 0. Feasibility Findings

These issues change the design, so they come first.

| # | Finding | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | **The H743 cannot run the ESC.** ArduPilot does not commutate motors. A 4-in-1 needs dedicated ESC MCUs running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. | Architecture is H743 (flight control) plus 4× AT32F421 or STM32G071 (one per motor), linked by DShot. |
| F2 | **80 A per motor in a "nano" (20×20 mm) footprint is not thermally or physically credible.** Section 3 computes about 25 W per motor (about 100 W total) at 80 A. A 20×20 board has roughly 1–2 J/K of thermal mass. | Minimum recommended format is **30.5×30.5 mm** with 2-oz (preferably 3-oz) inner copper. Treat 80 A strictly as a ≤1–2 s burst rating. Specify sustained current around 45–55 A. |
| F3 | **Package current limits.** A 3.3×3.3 mm FET's bond-wire/clip rating, not R_DS(on), often caps current. | Use 5×6 mm (PDFN/LFPAK) FETs with ≥100 A package rating, or 2× paralleled 3.3×3.3 per switch. |
| F4 | **The H743 shares the board with 100 W of heat.** Its T_J(max) is 125 °C (140 °C on the "3" temperature-grade variants). | Place the MCU on the opposite board edge from the FETs, or use a stacked FC/ESC configuration. A true single-board AIO at 80 A is not advised. |

The rest of this document models the 30.5×30.5 mm AIO variant.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current paths

With 6-step trapezoidal commutation and complementary (synchronous) PWM, exactly two legs carry the phase current at every instant:

- **On-time (D):** the high-side FET of phase A and the low-side FET of phase B conduct.
- **Off-time (1−D):** the low-side FET of phase A (synchronous rectification) and the low-side FET of phase B conduct.

In both intervals, two FET channels carry I. Conduction loss is therefore approximately independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,FET} = 2\, I^2\, R_{25}\, k(T_J)$$

### 2.2 Numbers at 80 A

- I² = 6400 A²
- At T_J = 25 °C: P = 2 × 6400 × 1.0 mΩ = **12.8 W**
- At T_J = 125 °C: P = 12.8 × 1.6 = **20.5 W**

**Copper loss:**

$$P_{cu} = I^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

This is often ignored and it should not be. The phase pads and shunt path on a 30×30 board are a major heat source.

### 2.3 Current-sense shunt (if per-motor)

A 0.25 mΩ shunt adds 6400 × 0.25 mΩ = **1.6 W**, which is significant. Use a single bus shunt (sensing the summed current) or a low-side 0.2 mΩ 2512 shunt. Do not use per-phase shunts on this board.

### 2.4 Thermal feedback (self-consistent T_J)

R_DS(on) depends on temperature, so solve iteratively:

$$T_J = T_A + R_{\theta}\,\big[2I^2R_{25}(1+\alpha(T_J-25)) + P_{other}\big]$$

This yields a closed-form solution:

$$T_J = \frac{T_A + R_\theta\left[2I^2R_{25}(1-25\alpha) + P_{other}\right]}{1 - R_\theta\, 2I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha}$$

**Thermal runaway criterion:** the solution exists only when the denominator stays positive:

$$R_\theta \cdot 2 I^2 R_{25}\alpha < 1$$

At 80 A: 2·6400·0.001·0.006 = 0.0768 W/K, so R_θ (per motor) must be below **13 K/W** in steady state. The board in still air sits close to that limit, which is another reason 80 A must be burst-only.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switched leg incurs switching loss. That is one half-bridge per motor at any instant.

### 3.1 V–I overlap (hard switching, inductive load)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

The transition times derive from:

$$t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{G}} ,\quad I_G = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}{R_G+R_{G,int}}$$

### 3.2 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{nC}\times 25.2\times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 3.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

### 3.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 3.5 Gate drive (dissipated mostly in driver and R_G)

$$P_G = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\times50\text{nC}\times10\times48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$
