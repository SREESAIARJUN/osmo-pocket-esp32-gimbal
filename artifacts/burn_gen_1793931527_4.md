# STM32H743 AIO with 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal and Bidirectional DShot Architecture (Job #11)

---

## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

### Partitioning
ArduPilot runs on the STM32H743 and does **not** commutate motors. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs on the same PCB, and each ESC MCU runs its own commutation firmware. This document assumes AT32F421 MCUs running AM32 (a common choice). Any design that drives the gate drivers directly from the H743 is out of scope for ArduPilot.

### Baseline Parameters

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-Dependent Resistance

R_ds(T_j) = R_ds25 · [1 + α (T_j − 25)]

At T_j = 110 °C: R = 1.5 mΩ × 1.425 = **2.14 mΩ**

### 1.2 Per-FET Duty Under 6-Step Trapezoidal Drive

The model assumes complementary (synchronous) PWM. Over one electrical cycle, each phase leg spends:
- 1/3 of the time **driven high**: the high-side FET conducts for a fraction D, and the low-side FET synchronously freewheels for (1 − D).
- 1/3 of the time **driven low**: the low-side FET conducts the full current.
- 1/3 of the time **floating**: this is the BEMF sensing window.

Average conduction fractions:
- δ_HS = D / 3
- δ_LS = (1 − D)/3 + 1/3

Per-FET conduction loss:

P_cond,FET = I_pk² · R_ds(T_j) · δ

### 1.3 Per-ESC Total

With synchronous rectification, exactly two FETs carry the current at every instant, independent of D:

P_cond,ESC = 2 · I² · R_ds(T_j)

At 80 A: P_cond,ESC = 2 × 6400 × 2.14 mΩ = **27.4 W**

At full throttle (D → 1), each active FET averages 6400 × 2.14 mΩ / 3 = **4.57 W**.

### 1.4 Copper and Shunt Losses (Often Underestimated)

- **Phase-trace / pad resistance:** R_cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ per path for 2 oz outer layers plus 2 oz inner layers in parallel on a nano board. This adds about 2 W per ESC at 80 A.
- **Shunt:** a 0.25 mΩ shunt adds I² R = **1.6 W per ESC**, or about 6.4 W aggregate if each ESC has its own shunt.
- **Recommendation:** use a single 0.25 mΩ shunt on the shared battery bus, sized for total current. Per-ESC shunts are not justified here.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches; the low-side leg is static during its 1/3 interval.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw | ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 k = **1.94 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw | 50 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.06 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f · I · 2 t_dt · f_sw | 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k = **0.61 W** |
| C_oss (both FETs) | 2 × ½ · C_oss · V² · f_sw | **0.05 W** |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver) | 2 · Q_g · V_drv · f_sw | **0.05 W** |
| **Total per ESC** | | **≈ 2.7 W** |

### Sensitivity to Frequency
- Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw.
- At 24 kHz it falls to about 1.35 W, but current ripple and acoustic noise worsen.
- 48 kHz is a reasonable choice for low-inductance 2306/2207-class motors.

### Dead Time
Dead-time loss (0.61 W) is the largest controllable term after V-I overlap.
- Tune the AM32 dead-time setting to the measured driver propagation delay.
- Do not use the default value blindly. A 50 ns reduction saves about 0.3 W per ESC.

### Loss Distribution Per Leg
- **High-side FET:** about 4.57 W conduction plus about 2.0 W switching = **6.6 W** (hottest device)
- **Low-side FET:** about 4.57 W conduction plus about 0.6 W dead-time diode loss = **5.2 W**

## 3. Aggregate Burst Power and Thermal Model

### 3.1 Power Budget

| Source | Per ESC | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 27.4 W | 109.6 W |
| Switching | 2.7 W | 10.8 W |
| Copper | 2.0 W | 8.0 W |
| Shunt (single bus shunt) | n/a | ≈ 4–6 W |
| **Total** | | **≈ 135 W** |

### 3.2 Two-Stage Thermal Model

The model has two stages:
1. **Board stage (lumped, adiabatic during burst):** the ESC section of a 30.5 mm nano board, including FETs, copper and FR4, has an effective heat capacity of C_board ≈ 5 J/K. This assumes an 8-layer, 2 oz board of about 6 g with no heatsink.
2. **Junction stage (transient impedance over local copper):** the junction rises above the local board copper by P_FET · Z_th,j-b(t). For a small PQFN on via-stitched copper, take Z_th,j-b(1 s) ≈ 8 K/W. This combines R_th,jc ≈ 1.5 K/W with spreading resistance.

Board temperature during a burst of duration t:

T_board(t) = T_amb + (P_total / C_board) · t

Junction temperature:

T_j(t) = T_board(t) + P_HS · Z_th,j-b(t)

### 3.3 Worked Result

- **Board heating rate:** 135 / 5 = 27 K/s
- **Junction delta over board:** 6.6 W × 8 K/W ≈ 53 K
- **Maximum burst duration:** solve T_j = 150 °C:

40 + 27t + 53 = 150  →  **t_burst,max ≈ 2.1 s** (from 40 °C ambient, no airflow credit)

### 3.4 Engineering Verdict

1. **The 80 A rating is a ~1–2 s burst figure on a nano board and must not be specified as continuous.**
   - Steady state with prop-wash airflow: R_th,board-air ≈ 3–4 K/W.
   - Sustainable total power is about (110 − 40)/3.5 ≈ 20 W.
   - That corresponds to roughly **30–35 A per motor continuous**.
2. **R_ds(on) thermal runaway is self-limiting but significant.** Because R rises with temperature, iterate the model:

   T_j → R_ds → P → T_j

   This converges about 8–12 % above the 110 °C estimate. Run the loop in your spreadsheet; do not stop at a single pass.
