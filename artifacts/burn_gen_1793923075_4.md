## Engineering Design Package, Rev A (Preliminary)

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Flags

| Item | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Topology** | The STM32H743 is the **flight controller**. It does not commutate motors. A 4-in-1 ESC needs its own MCU per motor, typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 talks to those MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. This document assumes that split. |
| **"80A burst"** | Interpreted as **80A per motor**, which is 320A total. At 6S (25.2V) that is about **8 kW**. |
| **Connector** | XT60 is rated about 60A continuous, so it cannot carry this load. |
| **"Nano" form factor** | A 20×20 board cannot carry this current. It cannot do so continuously, and it cannot even do so for a burst longer than about 1 s (see §2.5). Commercial 20×20 AIOs are rated roughly 25–45A per motor. **Recommendation:** use a 30.5×30.5 board, or re-spec the burst to 40–50A. |
| **48 kHz PWM** | Feasible. Switching losses are small relative to conduction losses at this current (§2.3). |

## 2. Loss Model

### 2.1 Conduction Model (trapezoidal / 6-step, complementary PWM)

In any commutation sector, two phases conduct:

- **Leg A (PWM leg):** the high-side conducts for duty D, and the synchronous low-side conducts for 1−D.
- **Leg B (return leg):** the low-side is on for the whole sector.

The current path therefore always contains exactly **two** R_ds(on) in series, independent of duty cycle:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = I^2 \cdot \left[ D\,R_{HS} + (1-D)\,R_{LS} + R_{LS} \right] \approx 2\,I^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j)
$$

Temperature dependence:

$$
R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds(on),25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

At 125 °C:

$$
R_{ds(on)} = 1.8 \times 1.6 = 2.88\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.88\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{36.9\ W}
$$

**Distribution per FET.** Each FET is active for 2 of 6 sectors, half as a PWM switch and half as the return switch:

$$
P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{36.9}{6}\cdot\ldots \approx 6.1\ \text{W (average)}, \qquad P_{FET,inst,max} = I^2 R = 18.4\ \text{W (instantaneous)}
$$

**PCB copper loss** must be added. For 20 mm of 2 oz copper, 6 mm wide, on 4 layers in parallel (about 0.2 mΩ per phase path, ×2):

$$
P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.4\times10^{-3} \approx 2.6\ \text{W per motor}
$$

### 2.2 Shunt Loss (if per-motor current sensing is used)

$$
P_{shunt} = I^2 R_{sh} = 80^2 \times 0.25\ \text{m}\Omega = 1.6\ \text{W}
$$

Alternative: a single 0.1 mΩ shunt on the battery lead. With 320A flowing it dissipates 10.2 W, which is not viable on a nano board. **Recommendation:** use per-motor shunts, or omit sensing on the burst path.

### 2.3 Switching Losses at 48 kHz (PWM leg only)

**V-I overlap (hard-switched, inductive load):**

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I\,(t_r+t_f)\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 1.94\ \text{W}
$$

**Output capacitance (C_oss):**

$$
P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}
$$

**Reverse recovery (Q_rr)** of the low-side body diode:

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}
$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two edges per period):

$$
P_{dt} = V_f\, I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 1.23\ \text{W}
$$

**Gate drive** (only the two complementary gates of the PWM leg are switched at f_sw):

$$
P_{gate} = 2\, Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2 \times 40\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.038\ \text{W}
$$

This power is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs.

**Total switching per motor:**

$$
P_{sw,motor} \approx 1.94 + 0.05 + 0.06 + 1.23 + 0.04 = \mathbf{3.32\ W}
$$

**Sensitivity:** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw.

| f_sw | P_sw per motor |
|---|---|
| 24 kHz | 1.7 W |
| 96 kHz | 6.6 W |

At 80A, conduction loss dominates switching loss by about **11:1**. Raising f_sw to 48 kHz is not the problem.

Dead-time optimisation is the cheapest win. Cutting t_dt from 200 ns to 100 ns saves 0.6 W per motor.

### 2.4 Loss Budget at 80A Burst

| Loss term | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (hot) | 36.9 W | 147.6 W |
| Switching (48 kHz) | 3.3 W | 13.3 W |
| PCB copper | 2.6 W | 10.4 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ) | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| **Total** | **44.4 W** | **≈178 W** |

**Efficiency check:**

$$
P_{out} \approx 4 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times D
$$

At full throttle the output is about 8 kW, so efficiency is about 97.8%. That sounds good, but 178 W has to be absorbed by a board weighing roughly 10 g.
