# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage and DShot Design Model

This document covers the conduction and switching loss model at 80 A burst, the thermal behavior during and after a burst, ArduPilot bi-directional DShot600 timer and DMA allocation on the H743, and a reference netlist.

**Scope.** The H743 runs ArduPilot only. Motor commutation runs on dedicated ESC MCUs (AM32 on AT32F421 or STM32G071). The H743 never drives gates directly. The 48 kHz figure is the ESC MCU's PWM frequency.

**Before you rely on the numbers:**
- The component values (MOSFET R<sub>DS(on)</sub>, Q<sub>g</sub>, gate-driver choice) are representative assumptions. Substitute datasheet values for your actual bill of materials.
- The netlist is a reference topology, not a verified production schematic. It needs ERC/DRC and a design review.

| Parameter | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V<sub>bus,max</sub> = 25.2 V | 4S: scale V-dependent terms by 16.8/25.2 |
| Burst current | 80 A per motor channel | see flag below |
| MOSFET | 40 V N-ch, PQFN 3.3×3.3, R<sub>DS(on),25</sub> = 1.0 mΩ, Q<sub>g</sub> = 50 nC, Q<sub>rr</sub> ≈ 60 nC, C<sub>oss</sub> ≈ 1.5 nF, R<sub>θJC</sub> ≈ 1.5 K/W | e.g. 40 V class trench FETs |
| Gate driver | 3-phase, 10 V drive (FD6288Q class), 2 Ω source / 1 Ω sink effective | |
| PWM | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous) | AM32 default range |
| Dead time | 100 ns per edge | |
| PCB | 30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz outer / 2 oz inner power | |

> **Feasibility flag.** 80 A per channel × 4 channels gives a 320 A bus current, about 8 kW at 6S.
> - That is far beyond an XT60 (60 A continuous), a 20×20 "nano" board, and any realistic battery lead.
> - On a 20×20 board, 80 A should be read as a total-board or single-channel instantaneous rating, valid for well under a second.
> - The math below is per channel. Use it to determine the burst duration a given board can actually survive (Section 3).

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

With six-step commutation and complementary PWM, two phase legs are active at any instant. Current always flows through exactly two MOSFETs:
- **PWM on-time:** high-side of phase A → motor → low-side of phase B.
- **PWM off-time:** the synchronous low-side of phase A freewheels with the low-side of phase B. Diode conduction only occurs during dead time.

So the series resistance is independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + I_{ph}^2 R_{Cu}$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

| T<sub>j</sub> | R<sub>DS(on)</sub> | 2·I²·R at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W |
| 100 °C | 1.375 mΩ | 17.6 W |
| 125 °C | 1.50 mΩ | **19.2 W** |
| 150 °C | 1.625 mΩ | 20.8 W |

This positive feedback (hotter FET → higher R → more heat) is the thermal-runaway term solved in Section 3.

### 1.3 PCB copper loss

Sheet resistance of 2 oz copper is about 0.25 mΩ/□ at 25 °C. Assume the phase path is about 4 squares, paralleled over 2 layers, plus vias:

$$R_{Cu} \approx \frac{4 \times 0.25}{2} + 0.1_{vias} = 0.6\ \text{m}\Omega \;\Rightarrow\; P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.6\text{m} = 3.84\ \text{W}$$

Copper heats too (α<sub>Cu</sub> = 0.0039/K), so this becomes about 4.5 W at 70 °C board temperature. This term is commonly forgotten. It is the main reason "nano" boards fail at the pads rather than in the FETs.

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

Over one electrical revolution, each high-side FET conducts for 120° (gated by duty D). Its low-side partner conducts the complementary (1−D) during those 120°, plus its own 120° low-side interval. For D ≈ 1 at burst, the average loss per FET is roughly:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx \frac{2 I^2 R}{6} \cdot 2 = \frac{19.2}{3} \approx 6.4\ \text{W (peak-conducting FETs)},\quad \approx 3.2\ \text{W average over all six}$$
