# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #47): Architecture, Loss Model, DShot DMA Allocation, Netlist and Thermal Budget

## 0. Architecture and Feasibility

### 0.1 Partitioning

The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs. It is the flight controller running ArduPilot. Each of the four motor channels has its own ESC MCU, which commutates the bridge and answers bi-directional DShot.

```
                    ┌────────────── STM32H743 (ArduPilot, 400–480 MHz) ──────────────┐
 IMU (SPI1/SPI4) ──►│  TIM1 CH1..CH4 ── DShot600 BIDIR ───────────────────────────── │
 Baro/Mag (I2C)  ──►│  DMAMUX1 → DMA1/DMA2 streams                                   │
                    └──────┬─────────┬─────────┬─────────┬──────────────────────────┘
                           │M1       │M2       │M3       │M4      (3V3 single wire each)
                     ┌─────▼──┐ ┌────▼───┐ ┌───▼────┐ ┌──▼─────┐
                     │ESC MCU │ │ESC MCU │ │ESC MCU │ │ESC MCU │  e.g. AT32F421 / STM32G071 (AM32)
                     │3-ph drv│ │3-ph drv│ │3-ph drv│ │3-ph drv│  e.g. FD6288Q-class
                     │6×FET   │ │6×FET   │ │6×FET   │ │6×FET   │  40 V, DFN3.3×3.3 or 5×6
                     └────────┘ └────────┘ └────────┘ └────────┘
```

### 0.2 Reality check on "80 A burst" in a nano form factor

A nano board is about 20×20 mm mounting, with a PCB of roughly 30×30 mm.

- At 6S, 80 A per channel is about 2 kW per motor, or 320 A from the battery. That is not survivable as a sustained load on this board.
- The model below shows that 80 A per channel is a burst rating of roughly 1–2 s on this geometry.
- The common marketing interpretation of "80 A burst" on nano AIOs is closer to 80 A total.

I evaluate the per-channel case as the worst case. All equations are parametric, so you can re-run them with your actual MOSFET datasheet.

> **Assumption flag.** The device parameters in §1 are representative values for a 40 V, ~1.2 mΩ DFN MOSFET class. They are not taken from a specific part number. Replace them with your BOM part's datasheet values before signing off.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current paths

Under 6-step (trapezoidal) drive with synchronous rectification, at any instant one phase is PWM-ing, one phase is low-side-on, and one phase floats.

| Leg | Conduction pattern | Loss contribution |
|---|---|---|
| PWM leg | High side conducts for D, low side (sync rect) for 1−D | I²R·D + I²R·(1−D) = I²R |
| Return leg | Low side on continuously | I²R |

The per-motor total is therefore independent of duty:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha_R (T_j - 25)]$$

### 2.2 Ripple correction

Phase-current ripple adds to the RMS current:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

Assume L_ph = 15 µH (typical 5" class motor, line-to-line ≈ 2L) and D = 0.5:

ΔI_pp = 25.2 · 0.25 / (30 µH · 48 kHz) ≈ 4.4 A, giving ΔI²/12 ≈ 1.6 A². This is negligible against 6400 A².

### 2.3 Conduction loss at 80 A

| Condition | R_ds | P_cond per motor |
|---|---|---|
| Cold, 25 °C | 1.2 mΩ | 2 · 6400 · 1.2 mΩ = 15.4 W |
| Hot, T_j ≈ 135 °C (×1.5) | 1.8 mΩ | 2 · 6400 · 1.8 mΩ = **23.0 W** |

### 2.4 Thermal-runaway coupling

Because R_ds depends on temperature, the junction temperature must be solved iteratively. The fixed point converges only if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\,R_\theta = 2I^2 R_{ds,25}\,\alpha_R\,R_\theta < 1$$

At 80 A this gives 2 · 6400 · 1.2e-3 · 0.0045 · R_θ = 0.069 · R_θ. **Steady state therefore runs away for any R_θ greater than about 14 K/W per motor-bridge.** The nano board exceeds this (see §5), which is why 80 A can only be a transient rating.

## 3. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Each term below is per motor, with one hard-switched leg active.

### 3.1 V-I overlap

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 20\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.97\ \text{W}$$

### 3.2 Coss loss

$$P_{oss} = V_{bus}\,Q_{oss}\,f_{sw} = 25.2 \cdot 30\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

### 3.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = V_{bus}\,Q_{rr}\,f_{sw} = 25.2 \cdot 40\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

The induced current spike also adds to P_VI. A ×1.2 margin on 3.1 covers this.
