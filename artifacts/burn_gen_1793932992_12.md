# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Systems Architecture Package (Job #54)

I have no prior context for "Job #54," so this document starts from stated assumptions. Every number below is parametric, so you can substitute your actual BOM values.

**The headline finding:** an **80 A-per-channel burst on a nano (20×20 mm) AIO board is thermally infeasible beyond about 1.5 seconds**, even with ideal parts. The math is in §2. Either the rating is redefined (single-motor or short-burst), the format grows to 30.5×30.5 mm with paralleled FETs, or the rating drops to the industry-typical 35–45 A burst for 20×20.

## 1. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 1.1 Conduction losses (6-step trapezoidal, synchronous)

In each 60° sector, two phases carry the current I:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for duty D, and the low-side FET (synchronous) conducts for 1−D.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET is continuously on.

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{hot}\big[D + (1-D)\big] + I^2 R_{hot} = 2\,I^2 R_{hot}
$$

This result is independent of duty cycle. Adding board copper resistance (about 0.3 mΩ per current path):

$$
P_{cond} = 2(80)^2(1.6\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{20.5\ W} \quad (+\,3.8\text{ W copper} \Rightarrow 24.3\text{ W total path})
$$

The copper loss heats the PCB directly, not the junctions.

### 1.2 Switching losses at 48 kHz (one hard-switched leg)

| Mechanism | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½ · V_bus · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = ½ · 25.2 · 80 · 40 ns · 48 k | **1.94 W** |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_f · I · 2t_dt · f_sw = 0.8 · 80 · 400 ns · 48 k | **1.23 W** |
| Reverse recovery | V_bus · Q_rr · f_sw = 25.2 · 50 nC · 48 k | 0.06 W |
| C_oss charge/discharge | C_oss · V² · f_sw (both FETs) | 0.05 W |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver and R_g) | 2 · Q_g · V_GS · f_sw | 0.08 W |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 3.3 W** |
