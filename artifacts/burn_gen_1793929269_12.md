## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Flag

**Two-tier architecture.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller, not the motor commutation engine. The industry-standard 4-in-1 ESC uses four small ESC MCUs (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32), one per motor. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 commands to those MCUs. This document assumes that architecture.

```
6S LiPo ──► Power stage (4× 3-phase bridges, 24 FETs)
                 ▲ gate drive
          4× ESC MCU (AM32) ◄── bidirectional DShot600 ──► STM32H743 (ArduPilot)
```

**Feasibility flag: 80 A burst on a "nano" (20×20 mm) board is not credible.**

- The math below shows about 20 W per channel at 80 A, or about 80 W total.
- A 20×20 board has roughly 2.6 J/K of thermal mass.
- That combination produces about 30 K/s of temperature rise.
- **Recommendation:** use a 30.5×30.5 mm form factor for 80 A burst, or derate the nano to about 45 A burst / 35 A continuous. The model below lets you verify either choice.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Path
In 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation with synchronous rectification, two phases conduct at any instant:
- One leg is PWMing: during on-time the high-side FET conducts; during off-time the synchronous low-side FET conducts.
- The other leg's low-side FET is held on.

As a result, **phase current always flows through exactly two R_ds(on) in series**, independent of duty cycle (dead time is handled separately in §2).

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{ds}(T_j) + I_{rms}^2 \cdot R_{Cu}$$

### 1.2 Temperature Dependence
$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha_R (T_j-25)\right]$$

At a target T_j of 110 °C:

$$R_{ds} = 1.0\,(1+0.0045\cdot 85) = 1.38\ \text{mΩ}$$

### 1.3 Copper Path
Model the phase pads, bus planes and pad-to-via spreading as R_Cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ per channel. That is achievable with 4 internal layers of 2 oz copper.

### 1.4 Result at 80 A (I_rms ≈ I_dc for flat-top trapezoidal)
