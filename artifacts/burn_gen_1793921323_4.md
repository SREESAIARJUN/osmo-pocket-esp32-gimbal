# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model (Job #15)

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Feasibility

Before the math, the architecture needs to be stated clearly. Some of the requirements, as written, are not physically achievable on a nano board.

**Topology.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does not commutate the motors. Each motor channel uses a dedicated ESC MCU (AT32F421 running AM32) driving a 3-phase gate driver (FD6288Q-class) and six N-channel MOSFETs. The H743 talks to the ESC MCUs using bidirectional DShot600.

**"80A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm mount, roughly 30×30 mm board).** This is a marketing number, not a thermal rating.
- Four motors at 80A would draw roughly 250–320A from the battery at high duty. The battery pads, XT60 connector, and board copper cannot carry that.
- The model below shows the board survives about 1–3 seconds at 80A on one or two channels.
- I recommend specifying it as: **"80A per channel, ≤1 s, single channel; 25A continuous with airflow."**

**Parameters used in all calculations below:**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| V_bus (6S, full charge) | 25.2 V |
| f_sw | 48 kHz |
| MOSFET | 40 V, 3.3×3.3 DFN class |
| R_ds(on) at 25 °C, V_gs = 10 V | 1.2 mΩ |
| Q_g | 45 nC |
| Q_rr | 40 nC |
| Q_oss | 35 nC |
| V_f (body diode) | 0.8 V |
| R_θJC | 1.5 K/W |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

**Current path.** In 6-step commutation with synchronous PWM, phase current always flows through exactly two MOSFETs:
- The PWM'd leg: the high-side for duty D, then the synchronous low-side for 1−D.
- The return leg's low-side, which is on for 100% of the sector.

**Total conduction loss per motor:**

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

**Temperature dependence of R_ds(on):**

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 115 °C:

$$R_{ds} = 1.2 \times 1.495 = 1.79\ \text{m}\Omega$$

**Result at 80A:**

$$P_{cond} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.79\times10^{-3} = 22.9\ \text{W per motor}$$

**Copper loss.** Phase traces and pads add roughly 0.3 mΩ per phase path, two phases in series:

$$P_{Cu} = 2 \cdot 6400 \cdot 0.3\times10^{-3} \approx 3.8\ \text{W}$$

**Electrothermal coupling.** R_ds depends on T_j, which depends on loss. Solve iteratively:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_c + R_{\theta JC} \cdot P_{FET}(T_j^{(n)})$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches hard: one hard-switched transition pair per period.

| Loss term | Equation | Value at 80A, 25.2 V |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f | ½ · 25.2 · 80 · 24 ns · 48k = **1.16 W** |
| Reverse recovery | V · Q_rr · f | 25.2 · 40 nC · 48k = **0.05 W** |
| Output capacitance | ½ · Q_oss · V · f · 2 | **0.04 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f · I · 2t_d · f | 0.8 · 80 · 400 ns · 48k = **1.23 W** |
| Gate drive (driver/R_g, not FET) | 2 · Q_g · V_gs · f | 2 · 45 nC · 10 · 48k = **0.04 W** |
| **Total switching-related** | | **≈ 2.5 W** |

**Key insight: at 48 kHz, dead-time loss is as large as overlap loss.** Two ways to recover about 1 W per channel:
- Tighten dead time to about 100 ns. Verify there is no shoot-through at the hot gate threshold.
- Drop to 24 kHz. This halves all switching terms, at the cost of more audible noise and current ripple.

**Per-motor total at 80A burst:**

$$P_{motor} \approx 22.9 + 3.8 + 2.5 = 29.2\ \text{W}$$

**Board totals:**
- All four channels at 80A simultaneously: ≈117 W. This is non-physical given battery current limits.
- Realistic worst case, two channels at 80A plus two at 30A: ≈66 W.

**Hottest FET.** The return-leg low-side carries the full I²R continuously within its sector:

$$P_{FET,max} = I^2 R_{ds} \approx 11.5\ \text{W} \;\Rightarrow\; \Delta T_{JC} = 11.5 \times 1.5 \approx 17\ \text{K}$$

## 3. Thermal Model (Transient-Dominated)

A nano board has no meaningful steady-state capacity at these power levels. It is a heat capacitor.

**Lumped capacitance (30×30 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz copper):**

| Component | Calculation | C_th |
|---|---|---|
| Copper (900 mm² × 8 × 70 µm ≈ 504 mm³, 4.5 g) | 4.5 g × 0.385 J/g·K | 1.73 J/K |
| FR4 (~2.5 g) | 2.5 g × 0.6 J/g·K | 1.50 J/K |
| 24 FET packages + components | | ~0.5 J/K |
| **Total** | | **≈ 3.7 J/K** |

**Convective resistance to ambient:**
- Still air: R_θBA ≈ 25 K/W
- Under prop wash: R_θBA ≈ 6–8 K/W

**Board temperature, first-order model:**

$$T_b(t) = T_a + P\,R_{\theta BA}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right), \quad \tau = R_{\theta BA}\,C_{th} \approx 26\ \text{s (prop wash)}$$

Because t ≪ τ for bursts, the adiabatic approximation applies:

$$\frac{dT_b}{dt} \approx \frac{P}{C_{th}}$$

**Burst time budget** (start at T_b = 45 °C, limit T_j = 125 °C, so T_b,max ≈ 125 − 17 − margin ≈ 100 °C):

| Load case | P_total | dT/dt | Time to limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 channel at 80A | 29 W | 7.9 K/s | **≈ 7 s** |
| 2 channels at 80A | 58 W | 15.7 K/s | **≈ 3.5 s** |
| 4 channels at 80A | 117 W | 31.6 K/s | **≈ 1.7 s** |

**Continuous rating** (prop wash, R_θBA = 7 K/W, ΔT = 55 K):

$$P_{cont} \approx 55/7 \approx 7.9\ \text{W total}$$

This works out to roughly 17–20A per channel with all four loaded. That is the honest continuous number.

**Firmware protection.** Implement this RC model in the AM32 temperature/current limit. The ESC MCU's NTC reports board temperature through EDT telemetry. Derate current when:

$$T_b + R_{\theta JC} \cdot P_{FET} > 115\ ^\circ\text{C}$$
