# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #62): Power-Stage Loss Model, DShot Timer/DMA Plan, and Reference Netlist

## 0. Feasibility Findings

Two problems in the brief need resolving before layout.

1. **The thermal envelope does not close.** Four channels at 80 A on a 20×20 mm board dissipate about 85 W. With no heatsink, the board reaches its limit in roughly 2 seconds (§3). The 80 A figure is achievable only as a single-channel burst of about 1 second, or on a 30×30 board with a heat spreader. I recommend re-rating to **45 A continuous / 60 A burst per channel (10 s)** on 20×20, or keeping 80 A on a 30.5×30.5 mm format.
2. **The STM32H743 should not commutate the motors.** All ArduPilot-compatible ESC firmware (AM32, BLHeli_32-class) runs on dedicated ESC MCUs. The architecture below therefore uses:
   - the **H743 as the flight controller**, running ArduPilot and generating bidirectional DShot600;
   - **4× AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32** as the ESC controllers;
   - **FD6288Q**-class 3-phase gate drivers.

Everything below assumes this split.

**Design assumptions:**
- 6S LiPo: 25.2 V maximum, 22.2 V nominal.
- MOSFETs: 40 V, R_DS(on) = 1.0 mΩ typical at 25 °C, PDFN3.3×3.3, Q_g = 40 nC, Q_gd = 8 nC, Q_rr = 30 nC, C_oss(eff) = 1.5 nF.
- Six-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM.
- PWM frequency f_sw = 48 kHz.
- Board: 8-layer, 2 oz copper.

Replace these values with the datasheet values of the part actually selected.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

In six-step operation, motor current always flows through **two FETs in series**:
- the PWM leg (high-side during t_on, its complementary low-side during t_off);
- the static low-side of the return phase.

The per-channel conduction loss is therefore:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + I_{ph}^2 R_{Cu}$$

The temperature dependence of R_DS(on) is:

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ /\text{K}$$

At T_j = 110 °C this gives R_DS(on) ≈ 1.51 mΩ.

**Copper path resistance:** a 3 mm-wide, 2 oz pour over roughly 15 mm, paralleled on 2 layers, gives R_Cu ≈ 0.6 mΩ.

**At I_ph = 80 A:**

| Term | Calculation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 2 · 6400 A² · 1.51 mΩ | 19.3 W |
| Copper | 6400 A² · 0.6 mΩ | 3.8 W |
| **Per channel** | | **23.1 W** |

**Positive-feedback check:** R_DS(on) rises with T_j, which raises the loss, which raises T_j. The loop is stable only if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\cdot R_{th} < 1$$

Here ∂P/∂T_j = 2 · I² · R₂₅ · α = 0.077 W/K. The loop is stable for R_th < 13 K/W, which is satisfied, but the thermal margin is thin at burst current.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only one leg switches at a time in six-step operation.

### 2.1 Hard-switching (V–I overlap)

The transition time is set by the gate-driver current through the Miller plateau:

$$t_{r,f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g}}$$

With FD6288Q drive (about 1.0 A source / 1.5 A sink) and Q_gd = 8 nC: t_r ≈ 8 ns and t_f ≈ 6 ns. Adding gate-resistor and layout parasitics gives t_r + t_f ≈ 30 ns in practice.

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 30\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = 1.45\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{DT} = V_F\, I_{ph}\, 2 t_{dt}\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 2(100\text{n}) \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Output-capacitance loss

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V^2 f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot1.5\text{n}\cdot635\cdot48\text{k} = 0.023\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate charge

This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs.

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\cdot40\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k}= 0.038\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Switching total

**P_sw ≈ 2.16 W per channel.**

At 48 kHz, conduction loss dominates by about 10:1. Raising f_sw to 96 kHz for quieter low-KV motors costs only about 2.1 W more per channel.

### 2.7 Ripple-current check (DC-link capacitor)

$$I_{C,rms} \approx I_{ph}\sqrt{D(1-D)}\ \Rightarrow\ \text{at } D=0.5:\ 40\ \text{A}_{rms}\ \text{per active channel}$$

Bulk electrolytics cannot carry this current on a nano board. Use **4× 10 µF/50 V X7R (1210) per channel**, placed within 2 mm of the half-bridges. Add **1× 470 µF/35 V low-ESR off-board** on the battery leads; this capacitor is mandatory.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Total dissipation (80 A, all 4 channels)

$$P_{tot} = 4(23.1 + 2.16) \approx 101\ \text{W}$$

Excluding copper loss (which is spread across the planes), the dissipation concentrated in the FETs is about 86 W.

### 3.2 Junction rise (steady flux path, per FET)

The hot pair per channel carries about 10 W each.

| Thermal element | Value |
|---|---|
| R_th(j-c) | 1.2 K/W |
| R_th(c-pcb), via array of 9× 0.3 mm vias | 1.5 K/W |
