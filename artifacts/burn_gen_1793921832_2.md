## 0. Architecture Decision

The STM32H743 cannot run four 6-step or FOC motor-control loops under ArduPilot. ArduPilot has no native ESC firmware. The design is therefore a **split architecture**:

| Block | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP-100, 480 MHz) | ArduPilot, DShot600 master, bi-directional eRPM capture |
| ESC MCUs (×4) | STM32G071GBU6 or AT32F421K8U7 | AM32 firmware, 48 kHz complementary PWM, BEMF commutation |
| Gate drivers (×4) | Fortior FD6288Q (3-phase, 250 V, bootstrap) | High-side/low-side drive |
| Power MOSFETs (×24) | 40 V, ~1.0 mΩ, PDFN5x6 (e.g., class of BSC010N04LS / NVMFS5C410N) | 3 half-bridges per motor |

**Form-factor warning.** A 20×20 mm "nano" board cannot physically host 24 power FETs plus four ESC MCUs and carry 4×80 A. All calculations below assume a **30.5×30.5 mm** board with 8-layer construction and 2 oz copper (3 oz on inner power layers). Section 2.6 shows that 80 A is a **seconds-scale burst rating**, not a continuous one.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step with synchronous rectification

At any commutation sector, two phase legs carry current I. One leg is chopped (complementary PWM) and the other is held static.

- **Chopped leg:** the high-side FET conducts for D·T, and the low-side FET (synchronous) conducts for (1−D)·T.
- **Static leg:** one FET conducts 100 % of the sector.

### 1.2 Per-motor conduction loss

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

The result is independent of duty cycle, which is the useful property of synchronous rectification.

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = \alpha_R \cdot R_{ds,25} = 1.5 \times 1.0\ \text{m}\Omega = 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.0015 = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ motor}
$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each FET is active for 1/3 of the electrical cycle. The electrical frequency f_e = eRPM/60 is in the kHz range at burst, which is far faster than the FET thermal time constants (≥ ms). Averaging is therefore valid:

$$
\bar P_{FET,cond} \approx \frac{P_{cond}}{6} = 3.2\ \text{W}
$$

The instantaneous peak per FET is I²R = 9.6 W.

### 1.4 Copper loss (frequently ignored, not negligible)

Each phase current path is roughly 15 mm of 3 oz copper, 5 mm wide, on two parallel layers.

$$
R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{w\,t} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\times0.015}{0.005\times105\times10^{-6}} \approx 0.49\ \text{m}\Omega\ \text{per layer}
$$

Two parallel layers give 0.25 mΩ. A positive temperature coefficient of +0.39 %/K at 90 °C gives about 0.31 mΩ. Two segments (in and out) carry I:

$$
P_{Cu} \approx 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.31\times10^{-3} \approx \mathbf{4.0\ W\ per\ motor}
$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the chopped leg switches. That is one hard-switched commutation pair per PWM period.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.968\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{ nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{ nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}
$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$
P_{dt} = 2\, V_{SD}\, I\, t_{dt}\, f_{sw} = 2\times0.8\times80\times200\text{ ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.229\ W}
$$

This term exceeds the overlap loss. Cutting dead time to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per motor, but this must be validated against shoot-through on the scope.

### 2.5 Gate drive loss

The loss is drawn from the driver supply. Only the two FETs in the chopped leg switch at a time, averaged over sectors:

$$
P_{gate} = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\times30\text{ nC}\times10\times48\text{k} = 0.029\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.6 Total burst budget

| Loss term | Per motor (W) | ×4 motors (W) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 19.20 | 76.8 |
| Copper | 4.00 | 16.0 |
| V–I switching | 0.97 | 3.9 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 | 4.9 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.10 | 0.4 |
| Gate | 0.03 | 0.1 |
| **Total** | **25.5** | **≈102 W** |

The efficiency at burst is calculated as follows. Input power is approximately V_bus·I·D. At D ≈ 0.9, that is 1814 W per motor, so the loss is about 1.4 %, which is acceptable electrically. The problem is purely thermal density: 102 W into roughly 9 cm².

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network (lumped, Cauer-form)

```
P_FET ──[R_θJC 1.5 K/W]── T_pad ──[R_spread 0.8 K/W]── T_board(C_b) ──[R_θBA]── T_amb
                           (per FET)                      (lumped)
```

### 3.2 Board thermal capacitance

The 30.5 mm 8-layer board has about 2.4 g of copper and 4.5 g of FR4/components.

$$
C_b \approx 2.4\times0.385 + 4.5\times1.1 \approx 5.9\ \text{J/K}
$$

A bolted aluminum heat spreader of 3 g adds 2.7 J/K, which would give C_b ≈ 8.6 J/K.

### 3.3 Board-to-ambient resistance

R_θBA is about 4 K/W in prop-wash at roughly 10 m/s. It is about 12 K/W in still air.

### 3.4 Transient response

$$
\tau = R_{\theta BA} C_b = 4 \times 5.9 = 23.6\ \text{s}
$$

$$
T_b(t) = T_{amb} + P\,R_{\theta BA}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right)
$$

The steady-state asymptote is 102 × 4 = 408 K rise, which is not survivable. The burst is therefore limited by time:

$$
t_{max} = -\tau \ln\!\left(1 - \frac{T_{b,max} - T_{amb}}{P R_{\theta BA}}\right)
$$
