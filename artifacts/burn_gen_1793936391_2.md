# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage, Thermal, and Bi-Directional DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

The model below rests on the following decisions and assumptions. Replace the bracketed values with your selected part datasheets before sign-off.

**System partitioning**
- **The STM32H743 does not perform motor commutation.** In this architecture it runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600.
- **Each ESC channel has its own MCU** (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. That MCU does the 48 kHz complementary PWM.
- If Job #16 is a true AIO where the H743 drives the FETs, the DMA section changes completely. Flag it if so.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.2 × 1.45 = **1.74 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current path in synchronous 6-step

In every 60° sector, exactly two legs are active. The current always flows through **two conducting devices in series**:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET during D, then the low-side FET (synchronous rectification) during 1−D.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET at 100%.

Therefore the channel conduction loss is independent of duty, to first order:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 1.3 Per-device split

Over one electrical cycle, each phase is a PWM leg for 120° and a return leg for 120°:

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,D\,I^2 R_{ds}$$

$$P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,(1-D)\,I^2 R_{ds} + \tfrac{1}{3}\,I^2 R_{ds}$$

**Implication:** low-side FETs run hotter, up to 2/3 of the I²R per phase at low duty. Give them the thermal priority in layout (most vias, largest copper).

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per channel | Worst LS FET (D → 0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.20 mΩ | 15.4 W | 5.1 W |
| 100 °C | 1.61 mΩ | 20.6 W | 6.9 W |
| 125 °C | 1.74 mΩ | **22.3 W** | **7.4 W** |

### 1.5 Copper and shunt losses

**PCB copper:** take path resistance R_pcb ≈ [0.4 mΩ] (pads + pours + phase pad).

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{pcb} \approx 2.6\ \text{W per channel}$$

**Current shunt:** use a common shunt of 0.2 mΩ (4-term Kelvin, e.g., 2512).

- At 4 × 80 A = 320 A: P_shunt = 320² × 0.2 m = **20.5 W**. That is not survivable on a 2512.
- **Recommendation:** use R_sh = 0.1 mΩ, or a Hall sensor (e.g., ACS7xx-class) for the bus.
- Alternatively, rate current sense for a 200 A bus.
- At 0.1 mΩ and 320 A: V_sh = 32 mV, P = 10 W burst. This needs a gain-50 amplifier into the 3.3 V ADC.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. That means one hard-switched commutation cell per channel at any time, i.e., two transitions per PWM period.

### 2.1 Overlap (V·I crossover) loss

Estimate the transition times from the gate charge:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd} + Q_{gs2}}{I_{g,on}}, \qquad t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd} + Q_{gs2}}{I_{g,off}}$$

With FD6288 (≈0.3 A source / 0.6 A sink in practice), a gate resistor R_g = 4.7 Ω, and Q_gd ≈ 8 nC, take t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 15 ns.

$$P_{ov} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$
