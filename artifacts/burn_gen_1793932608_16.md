# Job #37: STM32H743 Nano FC + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC
## Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, BDShot600 DMA Allocation, and Reference Netlist

---

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Verdict

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 cannot directly commutate four BLDC motors in this product class. ArduPilot runs on the H743 as the flight controller. Each of the four ESC channels has its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32 (typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071). The H743 talks to those MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. All analysis below assumes this split.

**Feasibility verdict (stated up front so the math can be read in context).**

- **Continuous 80 A per motor is not achievable on a nano-format board.** The steady-state temperature rise works out to more than 400 K.
- **80 A is achievable only as a timed burst** of roughly 2.5–4 s, limited by the thermal capacitance of the board.
- **The firmware must enforce this limit.** It needs a current × time (I²t) limiter and a board-temperature derate.

The rest of this document derives those numbers.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Six-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification

At every instant, the phase current I_ph flows through exactly two switch positions in series:

- **PWM'd leg:** during the on-time (duty D), current flows through the high-side FET. During the off-time (1−D), it flows through the low-side FET acting as a synchronous rectifier.
- **Non-PWM'd leg:** the opposite low-side (or high-side) FET carries current for the entire 60° sector.

Because both PWM states always have one FET in the path, conduction loss per motor is **independent of duty cycle**:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j) \;+\; I_{ph}^2 R_{cu}
$$

with the temperature dependence

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

### 2.2 Per-FET RMS current

Each position conducts for 120° of every 360° electrical cycle. The PWM'd positions split that interval as D and 1−D. Averaged over a full electrical cycle (AM32 alternates which side is PWM'd), the per-FET RMS current is:

$$
I_{FET,rms} \approx I_{ph}\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{3}} = 0.577\,I_{ph} = 46.2\ \text{A}
$$

### 2.3 Numerical evaluation at I_ph = 80 A

| T_j | R_DS(on) | 2·I²·R | Copper I²R_cu | P_cond per motor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 0.75 mΩ | 9.60 W | 2.56 W | 12.16 W |
| 100 °C | 1.14 mΩ | 14.6 W | 2.56 W | 17.2 W |
| 125 °C | 1.275 mΩ | 16.3 W | 2.56 W | 18.9 W |

Per FET at 125 °C: 16.3 W / 6 ≈ **2.72 W** (time-averaged).

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches. Each PWM period has one hard-switched turn-on and one hard-switched turn-off (inductive clamped load).

### 3.1 Gate current during the Miller plateau

$$
I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{dr}-V_{pl}}{R_{src}+R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}} = \frac{7}{10} = 0.70\ \text{A}
$$

$$
I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{snk}+R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}} = \frac{3}{7} = 0.43\ \text{A}
$$

### 3.2 Transition times

$$
t_{on} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = \frac{16\,\text{nC}}{0.70\,\text{A}} = 22.9\ \text{ns}
\qquad
t_{off} \approx \frac{16\,\text{nC}}{0.43\,\text{A}} = 37.3\ \text{ns}
$$
