# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model (Job #52)

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

The STM32H743 should be the **flight controller** MCU. An 80A ESC needs local, sub-microsecond commutation control, so each motor (or the stack) also needs a dedicated ESC MCU, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32. The H743 drives the ESCs over bidirectional DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry back.

All numbers below come from the stated assumptions. Swap in your actual datasheet values. The equations are general, but the component parameters are placeholders.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step / trapezoidal drive

During any 60° electrical sector, current flows through exactly two switch positions:
- **The PWM'd leg.** The high-side conducts for duty fraction D. The low-side carries the current for (1 − D) under synchronous rectification.
- **The return leg.** One low-side FET is held on for the entire sector.

At any instant, therefore, the current passes through **two R_ds(on) in series**:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

### 1.2 Per-FET allocation

Over a full electrical cycle, each phase leg is the PWM leg for 1/3 of the time and the return leg for 1/3 of the time. Averaged per FET:

$$P_{HS,avg} = \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I^2 R_{ds}, \qquad P_{LS,avg} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D) I^2 R_{ds} + \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{ds}$$

The low-side FETs work harder. They carry both the return current and the freewheeling current, so **low-side placement should get the best copper**.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80A, T_j = 115 °C

- R_ds(115 °C) = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0055 × 90) ≈ **1.5 mΩ**
- P_cond,motor = 2 × 6400 × 0.0015 = **19.2 W**
- Per FET at D = 0.9 (full-throttle burst):
  - HS: (1/3)(0.9)(6400)(0.0015) = 2.88 W
  - LS: (1/3)(0.1)(9.6) + (1/3)(9.6) = 0.32 + 3.2 = 3.52 W

### 1.4 Electro-thermal convergence

R_ds depends on T_j, which depends on loss. Iterate the following until ΔT_j < 1 K. It typically converges in 3–4 passes.

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_a + P(T_j^{(n)}) \cdot Z_{th}(t_b)$$

**Thermal runaway criterion.** The loop is stable only if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\, Z_{th} = 2 I^2 R_{ds,25}\,\alpha\, Z_{th} < 1$$

Per FET (≈3.5 W at R_ds,hot), this gives ∂P/∂T ≈ 0.013 W/K. With an effective Z_th of 30 K/W, the loop gain is about 0.4. This is stable, but the gain doubles if Z_th doubles because the copper is poor.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per motor, only the PWM'd leg switches. That means one hard-switched commutation pair per PWM period.

| Loss term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½ V I (t_r + t_f) f_sw | ½·25.2·80·30 ns·48k = **1.45 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr V f_sw | 40 nC·25.2·48k = **0.048 W** |
| C_oss | ½ Q_oss V f_sw | 0.5·50 nC·25.2·48k = **0.030 W** |
| Body diode, dead time | V_f I (2 t_dt) f_sw | 0.8·80·400 ns·48k = **1.23 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs/cycle) | 2 Q_g V_gs f_sw | 2·60 nC·10·48k = **0.058 W** (dissipated in driver + R_g) |
| **Total switching-related** | | **≈ 2.8 W/motor** |

**Key insight:** dead-time diode conduction is almost as large as overlap loss. Reducing t_dt from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per motor. This is a firmware setting in AM32 or BLHeli_32, and the margin depends on driver propagation skew.

**Frequency sensitivity:** switching loss scales linearly with f_sw.

- 24 kHz: ≈ 1.4 W
- 96 kHz: ≈ 5.6 W

At 48 kHz, switching loss is about 13% of total loss.

The ESC output power is roughly 4 × 25.2 × 0.9 × 80 ≈ 7.3 kW at the motor terminals. That gives an efficiency of about **98.6%**, which is good. The remaining ~100 W, however, is concentrated in a few cm².

### 4.2 Board-level (lumped) transient

- Copper: ≈ 4 g × 0.385 J/(g·K) = 1.5 J/K
