# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC (Job #31): Power, Thermal and Bi-Directional DShot Design Package

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Design Flags

You did not provide the BOM, board outline, or Job #31 requirements, so this package is built on the assumptions below. Replace them with your actual values. Every equation is parametric, so the numbers update directly.

| Parameter | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal |
| Burst current | 80 A RMS phase current per motor, 2 s burst, 4 motors simultaneously (worst case) |
| PWM | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous rectification), dead time 100 ns |
| MOSFET (example class) | 40 V, R_DS(on) = 1.0 mΩ at 25 °C / 10 V V_GS, Q_g = 50 nC, Q_rr = 40 nC, t_r + t_f = 20 ns effective at 80 A, V_SD = 0.8 V, PDFN 5×6 |
| ESC MCU | AM32 or BLHeli_32 class (STM32G071 / AT32F421), one per channel |
| FC MCU | STM32H743 running ArduPilot, timer kernel clock 200 MHz (400 MHz core config) |

**Design flags to resolve before layout:**

1. **The H743 does not drive the gates.** In a "4-in-1 ESC" architecture, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU doing commutation. The H743 only sends DShot and receives eRPM telemetry. If Job #31 actually intends the H743 to commutate all four motors directly (FOC/6-step on one MCU), that is a fundamentally different design. The H743 has only 2 advanced timers (TIM1/TIM8) for complementary PWM, which is not enough for 4 motors × 3 phases.
2. **80 A burst on a "nano" (20×20 mm) footprint is not physically credible.** As §3 shows, the board dissipates about 85 W at 4×80 A. The numbers below assume a **30.5×30.5 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz** ESC board, and even that is marginal. I recommend specifying 80 A as a **≤1 s single-motor peak**, with 50–55 A as the 4-motor burst rating.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big], \quad \alpha \approx 0.004\text{–}0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS ≈ 1.0 mΩ × 1.5 = **1.5 mΩ**. Use this hot value; the loss model is self-heating and must be iterated (§3.4).

### 1.2 Six-step trapezoidal bridge with synchronous PWM

In any 60° commutation sector, two legs conduct current I:

- **Static leg:** one FET held fully on. Loss = I²·R_DS.
- **PWM leg:** the high-side conducts for D, and the low-side (sync rectifier) for 1−D. Loss = D·I²R + (1−D)·I²R = I²·R_DS.

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) \quad (\text{independent of duty})$$

Each of the 6 FETs carries current one-third of the electrical cycle. The **per-FET** average is:

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{2 I^2 R_{DS}}{6}\ \text{(average)}, \qquad P_{cond,FET,peak} = I^2 R_{DS}\ \text{(during its sector)}$$

**At 80 A, 125 °C:**
- P_cond,bridge = 2 × 6400 × 1.5 mΩ = **19.2 W per channel**
- Per-FET average = 3.2 W; instantaneous peak = 9.6 W

### 1.3 Parasitic copper and shunt

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 \cdot (R_{trace} + R_{shunt}) \cdot k_{path}$$

Using 0.25 mΩ for the phase path and 0.25 mΩ for a shared shunt (k ≈ 1 for the phase path):

P_Cu ≈ 6400 × 0.5 mΩ = **3.2 W per channel**

This is significant and frequently forgotten.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (f_sw = 48 kHz)

Only the PWM leg hard-switches, which means one hard-switched FET plus one sync rectifier per channel at any instant.

| Term | Equation | Value @ 80 A, 25.2 V |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 20 ns × 48 k = **0.97 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.05 W** |
| Output capacitance | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw × 2 FETs (Q_oss ≈ 30 nC) | **0.04 W** |
| Body diode (dead time) | V_SD·I·2t_dt·f_sw | 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive | Q_g·V_GS·f_sw × 2 | 50 nC × 10 × 48 k × 2 = **0.05 W** (in the driver, not the FETs) |
