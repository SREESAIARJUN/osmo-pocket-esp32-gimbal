# STM32H743 AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC): 80 A Burst Electrothermal Model, 48 kHz Switching, and Bidirectional DShot600 DMA Plan

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Feasibility Check

Several numbers below depend on parts and board details not yet fixed. Every assumed value is tagged **[A]**. Replace these with datasheet values, then re-run the equations.

**Architecture.** The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot600. It does not commutate the motors. Each ESC channel has its own small MCU (e.g., AT32F421 running AM32), which does the 48 kHz PWM, commutation, and returns eRPM telemetry. This is standard practice, and the models below assume it.

| Parameter | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | 6S, V_bus,max = 25.2 V | [A] |
| "80 A burst" | Per-channel **phase** current during the active commutation interval | [A]. Battery current ≈ D·I_phase |
| MOSFET | 40 V, R_ds,on(25 °C) = 0.9 mΩ, Q_g = 60 nC, Q_rr = 40 nC, C_oss ≈ 1.5 nF, V_SD = 0.8 V, R_θJC = 1.5 K/W | [A], representative 3.3×3.3 or 5×6 part |
| Gate driver | 3-phase bootstrap driver (FD6288Q class), V_CC = 10 V, t_r + t_f ≈ 40 ns effective, t_dead = 100 ns | [A] |
| PCB | 26×26 mm, 8 layers, 2 oz on all layers, ~70 % copper fill | [A] |

> **Feasibility check (read this first).** At 80 A × 4 channels, the board dissipates about 80 W. A nano-format board can absorb that only **adiabatically, for roughly 2 seconds**. That makes "80 A burst" a thermal-mass rating, not a continuous one. Section 3 quantifies this. The datasheet should state the burst rating with its duration and starting temperature, e.g., "80 A / 1 s @ 40 °C start".

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-step commutation with synchronous rectification

During any commutation sector, current flows through two paths at once:
- The PWM leg: the high-side FET for D·T_sw, then the complementary low-side FET for (1−D)·T_sw.
- The return leg: the low-side FET, on for the whole sector.

At every instant, then, exactly two R_ds,on are in series with the phase current:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)\,[\,D + (1-D) + 1\,] = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

With synchronous rectification, conduction loss does **not depend on duty cycle**.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence of R_ds,on

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}\ \text{[A]}$$

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per channel at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 0.90 mΩ | 11.5 W |
| 100 °C | 1.20 mΩ | 15.4 W |
| 125 °C | 1.31 mΩ | **16.7 W** |

### 1.3 PCB copper conduction

The sheet resistance of 2 oz copper is:

$$R_\square = \frac{\rho}{t} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}}{70\times10^{-6}} = 0.246\ \text{m}\Omega/\square$$

Assume the phase and return path is 5 squares, paralleled over 4 layers **[A]**:

- R_path ≈ 0.31 mΩ
- P_Cu = 80² × 0.31 mΩ ≈ **2.0 W per channel**

Copper also heats up (TCR 0.0039 /K), adding about 25 % at 90 °C.

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

The electrical frequency at high RPM is several kHz, far faster than the millisecond-scale thermal time constants. Loss therefore averages evenly over all six FETs:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{2 I^2 R_{ds}}{6} = 2.78\ \text{W at } 125\,°\text{C}$$
