## 1. Field-Oriented Control

### 1.1 Transforms

Use the amplitude-invariant Clarke transform (with i_a + i_b + i_c = 0):

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}(i_a + 2i_b)
$$

Park transform:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix}=
\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}
$$

Electrical angle with latency compensation:

$$
\theta_e = p\,(\theta_m - \theta_{\text{off}}) + \omega_e \cdot 1.5\,T_s
$$

The 1.5·T_s term covers one sample of computation delay plus half a sample of PWM zero-order hold.

### 1.2 Plant and current PI with decoupling

The d-q voltage equations are:

$$
v_d = R i_d + L\dot i_d - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad
v_q = R i_q + L\dot i_q + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e\lambda_m
$$

The current PI uses pole-zero cancellation for a target bandwidth ω_c, then discretizes with backward Euler:

$$
K_p = L\,\omega_c,\qquad K_i = R\,\omega_c,\qquad \omega_c \le \frac{2\pi f_{pwm}}{10}
$$

Control law with cross-coupling and back-EMF feedforward:

$$
v_d^* = PI_d(i_d^*-i_d) - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad
v_q^* = PI_q(i_q^*-i_q) + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e\lambda_m
$$

The voltage vector is limited to the inscribed SVPWM circle:

$$
\sqrt{v_d^2+v_q^2} \le \frac{V_{bus}}{\sqrt3}
$$

The limit is applied with d-axis priority, and the PI integrators use back-calculation anti-windup.

> Gimbal motors have high R and low speed, so voltage-mode FOC (v_q = R·i_q* + ω_e·λ_m) is a valid fallback if you don't have current sensing. The code supports both modes via `kVoltageMode`.

### 1.3 SVPWM (min-max zero-sequence injection)

This method is mathematically equivalent to 7-segment SVPWM:

$$
v_{a,b,c} = \text{invClarke}(v_\alpha,v_\beta),\quad
v_0 = -\tfrac12(\max + \min),\quad
d_x = \tfrac12 + \frac{v_x + v_0}{V_{bus}}
$$
