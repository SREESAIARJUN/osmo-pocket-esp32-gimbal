# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #3): Thermal, Switching, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Design

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

Two architectural points need correcting before the math.

1. **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz motor PWM.** In an ArduPilot stack, the H743 runs the flight controller and emits DShot600. Each ESC channel's MOSFET PWM comes from a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071. The 48 kHz switching analysis therefore applies to the ESC power stage. The DMA/timer analysis applies to the H743.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) 4-in-1 is not physically credible as 4×80 A.** I model 80 A per motor as a short burst on a 30.5×30.5 mm-class ESC with a 20×20 FC stacked on top. Section 2.5 derives how long the board can survive this burst. That duration is the real specification.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.4 × 1.6 = **2.24 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-step (trapezoidal) conduction with complementary PWM

At any instant, current flows through two phase legs. The current path is:

- Phase A: HS_A conducts for duty D, then LS_A (synchronous rectification) conducts for 1−D.
- Phase B: LS_B conducts for 100% of the 60° sector.

Two channels always carry I, regardless of D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Each of the 6 FETs is active in 2 of every 6 sectors. The average loss per FET is therefore:

$$P_{cond,FET}\approx \frac{2I^2R_{ds}}{6}$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per motor | Per FET (average) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.40 mΩ | 17.9 W | 3.0 W |
| 125 °C | 2.24 mΩ | **28.7 W** | **4.8 W** |

PCB copper adds loss. With 2 oz copper and ~0.5 mΩ of effective trace/via path per phase: P_Cu ≈ I²R = 6400 × 0.0005 ≈ **3.2 W per motor**.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ed leg switches hard: HS_A hard-switches, and LS_A commutates through the body diode during dead time.

### 2.1 Hard-switching (V–I overlap) loss

$$P_{sw}=\tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(40\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz}) = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery loss

$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}=40\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k}=0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance loss

$$P_{oss}=\tfrac12 Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}=0.030\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction loss

$$P_{dt}=V_f I (2t_{dead}) f_{sw}=0.8\times80\times200\text{n}\times48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive loss (two FETs per switching leg)

$$P_g=2Q_gV_{GS}f_{sw}=2\times40\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k}=0.038\ \text{W}$$

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FETs.

**Switching subtotal: ≈ 2.67 W per motor.**

**Design insight:** At 48 kHz, switching loss is about 8% of conduction loss. Raising the frequency from 24 kHz to 48 kHz (for quieter motors and lower current ripple) costs only about 1.3 W per motor. Dead time is the dominant switching-related term after the overlap loss. Each 50 ns of dead time removed saves ~0.3 W per motor, provided the shoot-through margin allows it.

## 3. Total Loss Budget at 80 A Burst, T_j = 125 °C

| Term | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 28.7 W | 114.8 W |
| Copper (PCB) | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| Switching + dead time + Q_rr + C_oss | 2.6 W | 10.5 W |
| Gate drive | 0.04 W | 0.15 W |
| **Total** | **34.5 W** | **≈138 W** |

The input power is about 8 kW, so efficiency is about 98.3%. That sounds good, but 138 W concentrated in roughly 9 cm² is not survivable continuously.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Steady-state check with electro-thermal feedback

$$T_j = T_a + R_{\theta JA}\,P(T_j),\qquad P(T_j)=P_0[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Solving for T_j:

$$T_j=\frac{T_a + R_{\theta JA}P_0(1-25\alpha)}{1-\alpha R_{\theta JA}P_0}$$

**Thermal runaway criterion:** α · R_θJA · P_0 ≥ 1.
