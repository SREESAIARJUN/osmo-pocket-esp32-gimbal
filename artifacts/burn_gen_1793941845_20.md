# STM32H743 AIO 4-in-1 ESC Flight Controller (Job #17): Engineering Model

## 0. Scope Corrections

These points affect the validity of every number that follows.

1. **The H743 does not switch the MOSFETs.** In a 4-in-1 design, each bridge is driven by its own ESC MCU (e.g., AT32F421 running AM32, or STM32G071 running BLHeli_32). The 48 kHz PWM is an ESC firmware setting. The H743 runs ArduPilot and only generates bidirectional DShot600 commands.
2. **"80 A burst per channel" on a 20×20 mm nano board is not physically credible.**
   - Twenty-four PDFN 3.3×3.3 FETs occupy about 260 mm², against 400 mm² of single-side area.
   - Four channels at 80 A means 320 A total through the battery pads.
   - Section 3 shows the board cannot survive this beyond a few seconds.
   - This model therefore assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm stack**: an ESC board plus an FC board. The 80 A figure is treated as a short burst rating, not a continuous one.
3. **All device parameters below are representative assumptions.** Replace them with datasheet values once the BOM is frozen. The structure of the model is what matters.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Who Conducts When

With 6-step trapezoidal commutation and synchronous (complementary) PWM:

- Two phases are active at any instant.
- In the PWM phase, current flows through the high-side FET during D and through the low-side FET during (1−D).
- In the sink phase, the low-side FET conducts continuously.

### 1.2 Per-Channel Conduction Loss

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{DS,hot}\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2 I^2 R_{DS,hot}$$

With R_DS,hot = 1.2 mΩ × 1.6 = 1.92 mΩ:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2(80^2)(1.92\,\text{m}\Omega) = \mathbf{24.6\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET Distribution

Each phase spends 1/3 of the electrical cycle as the PWM phase and 1/3 as the sink phase.

| FET | Loss expression | Value |
|---|---|---|
| High-side | I²R · D/3 | 3.69 W |
| Low-side | I²R · [1/3 + (1−D)/3] | 4.51 W |

Check: 3 × (3.69 + 4.51) = 24.6 W ✓

### 1.4 Board and Shunt Copper

- Shunt at 0.25 mΩ: 80² × 0.25 mΩ = **1.6 W per channel**
- Phase and bus copper: 2 oz, roughly 0.3 mΩ effective path, giving **~1.9 W per channel**

### 1.5 Thermal Runaway Coupling

R_DS(on) rises with junction temperature, which raises loss, which raises temperature:

$$R(T_j)=R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006/\text{K}$$

The loop is stable only if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\,Z_{th} < 1 \;\Rightarrow\; 2I^2R_{25}\,\alpha\,Z_{th} < 1$$

This condition is checked numerically in Section 3.
