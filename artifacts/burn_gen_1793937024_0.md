## 0. Architectural Clarification and Baseline Assumptions

On any practical 4-in-1 ESC design, the **STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs**. The H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bi-directional DShot600. Each ESC channel is driven by a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, which generates the 48 kHz PWM and commutation. The partitioning is:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) --BDShot600--> 4x ESC MCU (AM32) --> 3-phase gate driver --> 6x N-FET --> motor
```

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 0.90 × 1.45 = **1.31 mΩ**

### 1.2 Phase-current path in 6-step complementary PWM

At any instant, two phases are active:

- **On-state (duty D):** current flows through the high-side FET of phase A and the low-side FET of phase B.
- **Freewheel (1−D):** current flows through the synchronous low-side FET of phase A and the low-side FET of phase B.

In both states the current sees exactly two R_ds in series. This gives:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)\quad(\text{independent of }D)$$

For the trapezoidal phase current at burst, take I_rms ≈ I_pk = 80 A. This is conservative because the flat-top current is near DC.

$$P_{cond,ch} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.31\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{16.8\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each FET carries current for 120° of every 360° electrical cycle.

- High-side FETs conduct only during D.
- Low-side FETs conduct as the static return leg (120°) plus synchronous freewheel during (1−D).

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}I^2R_{ds}D \qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}I^2R_{ds}(1 + (1-D))$$

Worked example at D = 0.8:

- P_HS = 2.24 W
- P_LS = 3.35 W

This confirms that **low-side FETs are the thermal hot spot**. Place them on the best copper.

### 1.4 Copper and interconnect conduction

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{trace},\quad R_{trace}=\rho_{Cu}(T)\frac{\ell}{w\,t}$$

Assuming R_trace ≈ 0.25 mΩ per channel (phase pad, bus planes, vias, at 100 °C):

$$P_{Cu} \approx 80^2 \times 0.25\text{ mΩ} = \mathbf{1.6\ W}$$

### 1.5 Battery bus shunt (shared)

At 4 × 80 A × D ≈ 256 A average burst, a 0.25 mΩ shunt dissipates:

$$P_{shunt} = 256^2 \times 0.25\text{ mΩ} = \mathbf{16.4\ W}$$

> **Design flag:** Use 0.1 mΩ or Hall/TMR sensing. At 0.25 mΩ the shunt alone rivals one channel's losses.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Transition times (Miller-plateau approximation)

$$t_r \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g} = \frac{9\text{ nC}}{0.9\text{ A}} = 10\text{ ns}$$

### 2.2 Hard-switching V·I overlap loss

Only the high-side FET of the PWM'd phase hard-switches. The synchronous low-side FET switches near zero voltage (ZVS) after dead time.

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2}V_{bus}I(t_r+t_f)f_{sw}$$

$$P_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.97\ W/ch}$$

### 2.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.61\ W/ch}$$

### 2.4 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = V_{bus}Q_{rr}f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 50\text{ nC} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.06\ W/ch}$$

The body diode was conducting at turn-on, so the HS FET also absorbs I_rrm overlap. Add a 1.5× margin, giving ≈ 0.09 W.

### 2.5 Output-capacitance loss

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2}C_{oss}V_{bus}^2f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{ nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.02\ W/ch}$$

### 2.6 Gate-drive loss (dissipated in the driver and R_g, not the FETs)

Only the PWM'd HS/LS pair toggles at f_sw:

$$P_{gate} = 2\,Q_gV_{GS}f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{ nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.048\ W/ch}$$
