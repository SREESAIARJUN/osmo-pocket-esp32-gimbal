# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #16): Thermal, Switching and DShot Design Model

## 0. Scope, Architecture and Assumptions

### What runs where

- **STM32H743 (flight controller).** Runs ArduPilot. It generates **bi-directional DShot600** frames to the four ESC MCUs and decodes the eRPM telemetry they send back.
- **ESC MCUs (×4).** These are the AM32 or BLHeli_32-class controllers, for example an AT32F421 or STM32G071. They generate the **48 kHz** complementary PWM and run commutation.
- **Consequence for the design.** The H743 never touches the 48 kHz PWM. The switching-loss model below applies to the ESC power stage, and 48 kHz is an ESC firmware setting (AM32 supports it).

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step (trapezoidal) drive with synchronous rectification

In every commutation step, one phase is PWM-switched and one phase is held low.

- **During the on-time (duty D):** current flows through the high-side FET of phase A, the winding, then the low-side FET of phase B.
- **During the off-time (1 − D):** the low-side FET of phase A (synchronous rectification) carries the current, again returning through the low-side FET of phase B.

So at every instant **exactly two FETs carry I**, regardless of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence and ripple

On-resistance rises with junction temperature:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

The RMS current includes the PWM ripple:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{LL}\, f_{sw}}$$

- L_LL is the line-to-line inductance.
- **Example:** a 5" class motor with L_LL ≈ 20 µH, at D = 0.9 and V_bemf ≈ 21 V, gives ΔI ≈ 3.9 A. The ripple term adds only ≈0.2% to I², so it is negligible at 48 kHz. This is one argument for running 48 kHz rather than 24 kHz.

### 1.3 Worked numbers at 80 A

At T_j = 125 °C:

- R_DS = 1.2 × 1.5 = **1.8 mΩ**
- P_cond = 2 × 80² × 1.8 mΩ = **23.0 W per ESC**

Each FET conducts for about one third of the electrical cycle. The electrical frequency is in the kHz range, which is far faster than the thermal time constants, so the losses average out:

- **Average per FET ≈ 23.0 / 6 ≈ 3.8 W**
- **Instantaneous per conducting FET = 11.5 W**

### 1.4 Thermal stability (runaway criterion)

Conduction loss rises with temperature, which raises temperature further. The loop gain is:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\, R_{th} = 2I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\, R_{th,ja} < 1$$

- At 80 A this gives 2 × 6400 × 1.2e-3 × 0.005 × R_th < 1, so **R_th,ja must be below 13 K/W per ESC**.
- The board easily meets this in transient operation. Steady-state margin is governed by the system R_th in Section 3.

### 1.5 Other conduction losses (not per-FET)

| Element | Value | Loss at burst |
|---|---|---|
| Battery current at full load (4 ESCs × 80 A × D ≈ 0.9) | ≈ 290 A | — |
| Shunt, if a single 0.1 mΩ shunt carries total current | 0.1 mΩ | 290² × 0.1 mΩ ≈ **8.4 W** |
| Copper (2 oz, ≈0.3 mΩ effective path per ESC) | 0.3 mΩ | 80² × 0.3 mΩ ≈ **1.9 W per ESC** |

**Recommendation:** use one 0.25 mΩ shunt per ESC (≈1.3 W each at 80 A × D) rather than a single total-current shunt. Better still, size the current measurement to the battery-side average current, not the phase current.
