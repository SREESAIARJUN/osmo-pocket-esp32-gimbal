## 0. Architecture and Scope

Three points shape the rest of this document.

1. **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz PWM.** In a 4-in-1 ESC, each bridge (or the whole board) is driven by its own ESC MCU, typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 commands. The 48 kHz figure is the ESC firmware's PWM setting. The two timing domains are analyzed separately below.
2. **"Nano" vs. 80 A.** No 20×20 mm ESC can carry 4×80 A, even briefly, with acceptable junction temperatures. This design assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz ESC board** stacked with a 20×20 or 30.5×30.5 H743 FC. The thermal model in Section 3 shows that even this board supports only a **seconds-scale** 80 A burst on all four channels.
3. **Component values are representative.** MOSFET, driver, and shunt parameters are stated assumptions. Substitute datasheet values for your BOM and rerun the equations. The netlists are design-intent netlists, not fabrication-ready CAD output.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A burst)

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation, two phases conduct at any instant. During the PWM ON-time, current flows through the HS FET of phase A and the LS FET of phase B. During the OFF-time with synchronous rectification, current flows through the LS FET of phase A and the LS FET of phase B.

Either way, current always passes through **two channel resistances**, so the conduction loss is nearly independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \left[ D\cdot R_{HS} + (1-D)\cdot R_{LS} + R_{LS,return} \right] \approx 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond per channel |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.0 mΩ | 2 × 6400 × 1.0 m = **12.8 W** |
| 125 °C | 1.6 mΩ | 2 × 6400 × 1.6 m = **20.5 W** |

### 1.4 Copper and shunt losses

| Element | Resistance | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase pad + pour path (2 oz, 8 L, short) | ~0.3 mΩ | 1.9 W |
| Bus shunt (if 0.2 mΩ, carries ~D·I) | 0.2 mΩ | ≤ 1.3 W |

The design uses per-board current sensing (one shunt), not per channel. Copper loss per channel is therefore **≈ 2 W**.

### 1.5 Thermal-electrical coupling

Conduction loss raises T_j, which raises R_DS(on). Iterate until the two agree:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_a + P_{tot}(T_j^{(k)})\cdot Z_{th}(t)$$

The model converges in about 3 iterations for the cases below. It diverges, which indicates thermal runaway, when $\partial P/\partial T_j \cdot Z_{th} \geq 1$.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per channel, only one half-bridge is hard-switching at a time (the PWM leg). Per PWM cycle, that leg contributes the following losses.

**(a) V–I overlap loss**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

$$= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**(b) Output capacitance loss**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} \times 2$$

$$= 0.5 \times 1.5\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

**(c) Reverse recovery** (LS body diode conducts during dead time, then HS turns on)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

The Q_rr-induced extra turn-on current also adds roughly 0.1 W of overlap loss. Treat this term as **≈ 0.15 W**.

**(d) Dead-time diode conduction**

$$P_{dt} = V_f I_{ph} \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive** (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors)

$$P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

**Switching subtotal per channel ≈ 2.8 W.**
