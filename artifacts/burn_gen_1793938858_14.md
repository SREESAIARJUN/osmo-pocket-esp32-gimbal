## 0. Architecture Constraints

These constraints shape the model, so they come first.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot is a flight-control stack. It does not perform 6-step or FOC commutation. The 48 kHz PWM, dead-time and BEMF sensing are performed by **one ESC MCU per channel**, for example AM32 or BLHeli_32 class firmware on an AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 only emits bi-directional DShot600 and decodes the eRPM telemetry. The model below treats the system as two domains linked by four DShot signal lines.
2. **80 A per channel on a nano board is a burst rating only, measured in seconds.** Section 3 shows that the limit is the board's heat capacity, not the MOSFETs. Any "80 A" claim must state the burst duration and the starting temperature.
3. **Component values are representative assumptions.** They describe a modern 40 V MOSFET in a 3.3×3.3 or 5×6 package. Substitute your selected part's datasheet values into the equations before sign-off.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Hot R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS,hot} = R_{25}\,\big[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\big] \approx k_T R_{25} = 1.6 \times 0.70 = 1.12\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Channel Conduction Loss

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous (complementary) PWM. At every instant, the current flows through exactly two MOSFET positions:

- the PWM leg, where either the high-side (HS) or the low-side (LS) is on, and
- the static return leg, where the LS is on.

The duty cycle therefore cancels out of the channel total:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS,hot} = 80^2 \times 2.24\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{14.3\ W}$$
