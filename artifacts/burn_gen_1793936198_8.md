# STM32H743 + 4-in-1 ESC Nano Stack (Job #10): Loss, Thermal and DShot Architecture Model

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

I don't have Job #10's BOM, stackup or layout files. Everything below is a parameterized model built on stated assumptions. Substitute your actual datasheet values into the equations. The numeric results are worked examples, not sign-off values.

**Architecture clarification.** In a 4-in-1 ESC, the H743 does **not** switch the MOSFETs. Each ESC channel, or a shared ESC MCU in some designs, runs AM32 or BLHeli_32 on an AT32F421 or STM32G071 class part, which generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks bidirectional DShot600 to those MCUs. The model therefore splits into:
- **ESC power stage** (Sections 1–3)
- **FC timer/DMA** (Section 4)

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

In 6-step commutation, two phase legs conduct at any instant: one high side (PWM) and one low side. Each phase leg is active for 120° of the 360° electrical cycle as high side and 120° as low side.

**Per-MOSFET RMS current at duty D:**

$$I_{HS,rms} = I_m\sqrt{\tfrac{D}{3}}, \qquad I_{LS(sync),rms} = I_m\sqrt{\tfrac{1-D}{3}}, \qquad I_{LS(on),rms} = I_m\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{3}}$$

**Total conduction loss per channel:**

$$P_{cond} = I_m^2 \cdot R_{ds(on)}(T_j)\cdot\left[\underbrace{1}_{\text{static LS}} + \underbrace{D + (1-D)}_{\text{PWM leg}}\right] = 2\, I_m^2 R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right] \approx R_{25}\, k_T$$

Note that conduction loss is roughly **independent of D** with synchronous rectification.

**Worked example at 80 A:**
- P_cond = 2 × 80² × 1.6 mΩ = **20.5 W per channel**
- Hottest single FET (a low side held on for 1/3 of the cycle): 80² / 3 × 1.6 mΩ = **3.4 W**

**Positive feedback.** Because R_ds(on) rises with T_j, the loss rises with temperature. Iterate the solution:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + P(T_j)\, R_{th}$$

The iteration converges only if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\cdot R_{th} < 1$$

Thermal runaway risk appears when α·P·R_th approaches 1.

**Copper and shunt losses.** These are often forgotten, and on a nano board they are not negligible. Total bus current is 4 × 80 = **320 A**. Through 0.5 mΩ of board copper plus shunt, that adds:
- I²R = 320² × 0.5 mΩ = **51 W**

Measure your actual DC-path resistance (4-wire) and include it.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Hard switching occurs only in the PWM leg, so there is one hard-switched commutation pair per channel.

| Mechanism | Equation | Value at 25.2 V, 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 20 ns × 48 k = **0.97 W** |
| Output charge | Q_oss·V·f_sw | 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.048 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 30 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.036 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2t_d·f_sw | 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate charge (mostly in driver and R_g) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw | 2 × 60 nC × 10 × 48 k = **0.058 W** |
| **Total switching-related** | — | **≈ 1.72 W per channel** |

**Observations:**
- At 48 kHz, dead-time diode loss is about 2/3 of the hard-switching loss. Cutting t_d from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3
