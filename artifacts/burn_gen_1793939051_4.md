## 0. Scope, Assumptions and a Feasibility Warning

No component BOM was supplied for Job #46. Every numeric result below is therefore derived from **stated, typical-class parameters**. The equations are general. Substitute your actual datasheet values before release. Assumptions are tagged **[A#]** so they can be traced.

| Tag | Assumption |
|---|---|
| A1 | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V (full charge, worst case for switching loss) |
| A2 | 80 A **per motor channel**, burst duration target 5 s, T_amb = 40 °C |
| A3 | ESC MCU per channel: AT32F421 / STM32G071 running AM32. The H743 is the flight controller. It does not commutate motors. |
| A4 | Gate driver: FD6288Q class, 3-phase, bootstrap, ~1.5 A source / 1.8 A sink, V_drv ≈ 10 V |
| A5 | MOSFET: 40 V N-ch, PQFN 3.3×3.3 / 5×6 class. R_DS(on),max(25 °C) = 1.2 mΩ @ 10 V. Q_g = 50 nC, Q_gd = 8 nC, Q_gs2 = 5 nC, Q_rr = 40 nC, Q_oss = 60 nC, V_plateau ≈ 3.5 V at 80 A, R_g,int = 1 Ω, V_SD = 0.8 V, R_θJC = 1.2 K/W |
| A6 | Complementary (synchronous-rectified) 6-step trapezoidal drive, f_sw = 48 kHz, dead-time 100 ns |
| A7 | Board is 8-layer, 2 oz (70 µm) on all layers. Thermal analysis uses 30×30 mm because a 20×20 "nano" footprint is shown below to be infeasible. |

> **Feasibility flag (read first):** 4 × 80 A is 320 A of battery current at full throttle. A 20×20 mm board cannot carry or dissipate this. The math in §3 shows that even a 30×30 mm board reaches junction limits in roughly 3 s. The realistic options are:
> - (a) "80 A" means a short (<1–2 s) per-channel transient with a much lower aggregate, **or**
> - (b) the form factor must grow.
>
> Please confirm the definition of the burst rating with the customer.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.0048\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

This value of α corresponds to the typical datasheet normalization curve of ≈1.6× at 150 °C.

At the design junction temperature T_j = 125 °C:

$$
R_{DS}(125) = 1.2\times(1+0.48) = 1.78\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

### 1.2 Current path in complementary 6-step drive

In any 60° commutation sector, phase X is PWM'd and phase Y is held low:

- **PWM ON (duty D):** current flows through HS_X → motor → LS_Y.
- **PWM OFF (1−D):** current flows through LS_X (synchronous rectification) → motor → LS_Y.
