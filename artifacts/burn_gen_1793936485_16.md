## 0. Architectural Ground Truth

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot runs flight control and emits DShot. Each motor channel needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical choices are the AT32F421 or STM32G071, which generate the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The 48 kHz switching analysis therefore applies to the ESC MCUs, and the DShot/DMA analysis applies to the H743.
2. **"80 A" in a nano (20×20 or 30×30 mm) form factor can only be a burst rating.** Section 3 shows the result: about 3–4 s of 4-motor burst and about 24 A per channel continuous with airflow. Any marketing number above that is not physically defensible.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

AM32/BLHeli_32 use 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. At any instant, phase current I flows through:

- the **PWM leg**: either the high-side (HS) FET during D, or the low-side (LS) FET during 1−D, since synchronous rectification keeps the current in a channel rather than the diode;
- the **return leg**: the LS FET, held fully on for the 60° sector.

Exactly two channels are conducting at all times, regardless of D. The instantaneous conduction loss per motor channel is:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j) \;+\; I^2 R_{pcb,phase} \;+\; I_{dc,rms}^2\,R_{pcb,bus}$$

with:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

**At 80 A, T_j = 100 °C (k = 1.3, R_ds = 1.3 mΩ):**

$$P_{FET} = 2(80)^2(1.3\times10^{-3}) = 16.6\ \text{W}$$

**PCB copper.** Assume a 2 oz equivalent stack at 0.25 mΩ/□ per oz-layer, with about 0.3 mΩ effective phase path (pad → FET → motor pad):

$$P_{pcb} \approx 2(80)^2(0.3\times10^{-3}) = 3.8\ \text{W}$$

**Per-FET distribution.** Over one electrical revolution, each FET conducts about 1/3 of the time on average. HS and LS share the PWM-leg time by D.

$$\bar P_{FET,each} \approx \frac{16.6}{6}\approx 2.8\ \text{W}$$

The worst individual FET is the LS at low duty, which approaches 2/3 conduction. Use 1.5× for hotspot margin, giving about 4.2 W.
