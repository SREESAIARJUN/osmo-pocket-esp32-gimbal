# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #55): Architecture, Loss Model, and Timer/DMA Plan

## 0. Scope Corrections and Assumptions

Three framing points shape everything below.

1. **The 48 kHz switching happens on the ESC MCU, not the H743.** A 4-in-1 ESC runs its own firmware (AM32 or BLHeli_32) on per-motor or shared MCUs (e.g. AT32F421 or STM32G071). The H743 only produces DShot600 frames and decodes the bidirectional eRPM telemetry. The two problems are coupled only through the DShot line and the thermal budget.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is a seconds-scale rating, not a continuous one.** The thermal model in §3 shows why. I treat it as **80 A per motor for a bounded burst**.
3. **No MOSFET part number was specified.** The model is parametric. Example numbers use a representative 40 V, ~1 mΩ-class PowerPAK/DFN 3.3×3.3 part. **Replace them with your selected FET's datasheet values before signing off.** Do not treat my example values as datasheet data.

| Symbol | Meaning | Example value |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 6S full charge | 25.2 V |
| I_ph | Phase current (burst) | 80 A |
| R_ds(25) | On-resistance at 25 °C, V_gs = 10 V | 1.0 mΩ |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

R_ds(T_j) = R_ds(25) · [1 + α(T_j − 25)]

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.0 mΩ × 1.5 = **1.5 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Per-motor conduction (6-step trapezoidal, complementary PWM)

At any instant, current flows through one high-side path and one low-side path.

- With synchronous rectification, the PWM leg's current alternates between its HS FET (duty D) and its LS FET (1−D).
- The resulting conduction loss is independent of D:

P_cond = I_ph² · [D·R_HS + (1−D)·R_LS + R_LS,static] ≈ **2 · I_ph² · R_ds(T_j)**

At 80 A and 125 °C:

P_cond = 2 × 6400 × 1.5 mΩ = **19.2 W per motor**

### 1.3 Parasitic copper and sense resistance (often forgotten)

Add a copper path term, P_Cu = I_ph² · R_trace.

- On a nano board, 2 oz copper in 4 parallel layers, ~15 mm path, 3 mm wide gives ~0.15–0.3 mΩ.
- That adds **1–2 W per motor**.
- Add the shared current shunt at the battery input: I_bat² · R_shunt. For example, (4 × 20 A avg)² × 0.25 mΩ = 1.6 W.

### 1.4 RMS correction for 6-step commutation

Each FET conducts 1/3 of the electrical cycle, so per-FET I_rms = I_ph/√3. Average loss per individual FET is therefore about P_cond/6 ≈ **3.2 W/FET**. The total per motor is unchanged. This matters for the junction-to-board ΔT in §3.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches hard in each 60° sector, so there is one hard-switched leg per motor at any time.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap

Switching times from gate charge:

t_r ≈ t_f ≈ (Q_gs2 + Q_gd)/I_g = 15 nC / 1.5 A = 10 ns

P_sw = ½ · V_bus · I_ph · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw
 = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 20 ns × 48 kHz = **0.97 W**

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

P_dt = V_f · I_ph · 2·t_dt · f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 kHz = **0.61 W**

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

P_rr = V_bus · Q_rr · f_sw = 25.2 × 50 nC × 48 kHz = **0.06 W**

Real Q_rr rises with di/dt, so add 2–3× margin.

### 2.4 Output capacitance

P_oss = ½ · C_oss,eq · V_bus² · f_sw ≈ 0.5 × 2 nF × 635 × 48k = **0.03 W**

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs)

P_g = Q_g · V_gs · f_sw · N_switching = 60 nC × 10 V × 48k × 2 = **0.06 W per motor**

### 2.6 Per-motor budget at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.2 |
| Copper | 1.5 |
| Hard switching | 0.97 |
| Dead time | 0.61 |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.10 |
| **Total** | **≈ 22.4 W** |

**Key insight: at 80 A, conduction dominates (~86%).**

- Dropping f_sw from 48 to 24 kHz saves only ~0.8 W per motor.
- Halving R_ds saves ~10 W.
- Spend design effort on R_ds and copper, not switching speed.
- 48 kHz is justified for low-inductance motors (current ripple, acoustic, sync), not for efficiency.

### 2.7 Frequency trade-off equation

P_total(f) = 2·I²·R_ds(T_j) + f · [½·V·I·(t_r+t_f) + 2·V_f·I·t_dt + V·Q_rr + ½·C_oss·V²]

At 80 A the bracketed term is ~33 µJ/cycle, i.e. **~1.6 W per 48 kHz**.
