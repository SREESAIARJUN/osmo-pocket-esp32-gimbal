# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC: 80 A Burst Thermal, 48 kHz Switching, and Bidirectional DShot600 Allocation

**Job #5 Engineering Analysis, Rev A**

---

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Feasibility Flags

**1. The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In a standard 4-in-1 architecture:

- The H743 is the flight controller. It runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600.
- Each ESC channel has its own MCU (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071) running AM32 or BLHeli_32.
- That ESC MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM through a 3-phase gate driver.

The DShot section therefore concerns H743 timers. The switching and conduction sections concern the ESC power stage.

**2. 80 A per channel on a "nano" (20×20 mm) form factor is not achievable continuously.** The math below shows it is survivable only as a short burst of roughly 1 s. I recommend either a 30×30 board or a burst spec of 80 A for ≤1 s on one or two channels, with 35 to 45 A continuous.

**3. Unknowns.** All MOSFET and driver values below are representative parameters, not verified datasheet values. Re-run the model with your chosen parts' datasheet figures before layout freeze.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

R_ds(on)(T_j) = R_25 · [1 + α(T_j − 25)]

R_ds(on)(110 °C) = 1.5 mΩ × (1 + 0.005 × 85) = **2.14 mΩ**

### 2.2 Current path

In each 6-step sector, current flows through one high-side leg and one low-side leg. With synchronous rectification, the PWM leg's current alternates between its high-side FET and its low-side FET. Either way, two channels are always in series with the motor current.

P_cond = 2 · I² · R_ds(on)(T_j) + I² · R_cu

P_cond = 2 × 6400 × 2.14 mΩ + 6400 × 0.5 mΩ = 27.4 W + 3.2 W = **30.6 W per ESC channel**

### 2.3 Distribution

- In the PWM leg, the high-side FET carries duty D and the low-side FET carries (1 − D).
- The static leg's low-side FET carries 100% of the current.
- Worst-case single-FET loss is I²·R ≈ **13.7 W**. This occurs in the static low-side FET at that instant, but it rotates every 60° electrical.

### 2.4 Averaging over rotation

Each FET conducts about 1/3 of the electrical period (static, 120°) plus its PWM share. The time-averaged loss per FET is about P_cond/6 + PWM share, which is approximately **5–7 W per FET** at 80 A.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In 6-step operation, only one leg hard-switches per sector.

**(a) Hard-switching V·I overlap loss**

P_sw = ½ · V_bus · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 20 ns × 48 kHz = **0.968 W**

**(b) Body-diode reverse recovery**

P_rr = Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw = 30 nC × 25.2 × 48 kHz = **0.036 W**

**(c) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (2 transitions per period)

P_dt = 2 · V_f · I · t_dt · f_sw = 2 × 0.8 × 80 × 100 ns × 48 kHz = **0.614 W**

**(d) C_oss charge/discharge**

P_oss = ½ · C_oss · V² · f_sw = 0.5 × 1 nF × 635 × 48 kHz = **0.015 W**

**(e) Gate drive** (two switching FETs, dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g, not the FET)

P_g = 2 · Q_g · V_gs · f_sw = 2 × 40 nC × 10 V × 48 kHz = **0.038 W**

**Total switching-related loss ≈ 1.67 W per channel.**

**Observations:**

- At 80 A, conduction loss is about 95% of the total. Moving from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves only about 0.8 W per channel.
- 48 kHz is justified for low-inductance motors (current ripple and acoustic noise), not for efficiency.
- Dead time is the second-largest switching term. Reducing t_dt from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel.

## 5. Thermal Model

### 5.1 Network (Cauer/Foster approximation)

```
Pj ─┬─[Rθjc 1.2 K/W]─┬─[Rθcb 0.5 K/W]─┬─[Rθba]─── Tamb
    │                │                │
   Cj≈0.02 J/K      Cc≈0.1 J/K       Cboard
```

### 5.2 Board-level parameters (20×20 mm, 6L, 2 oz)

**Thermal mass:**

- FR4: about 1.6 g × 1.1 J/g·K ≈ 1.8 J/K
- Copper: about 1.4 g × 0.385 J/g·K ≈ 0.55 J/K
- FETs and other parts: about 0.4 J/K
- **C_board ≈ 2.75 J/K**

**Board-to-ambient resistance:**

- Still air: R_θba ≈ 25 K/W
- Prop wash at 8–15 m/s: R_θba ≈ 8 K/W

### 5.3 Steady-state check (all 4 channels at 80 A, prop wash)

ΔT_board = 135.5 W × 8 K/W = **1084 K → physically impossible.**

Maximum continuous dissipation for T_board ≤ 90 °C at 40 °C ambient is ΔT_max / R_θba = 50 K / 8 K/W = **6.25 W total**.

4 · (2·R_ds + R_cu + R_sh) · I² = 6.25
