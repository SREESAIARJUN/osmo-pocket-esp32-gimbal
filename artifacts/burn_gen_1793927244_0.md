### 0.2 Red Flags to Resolve in Design Review

1. **80 A/motor on a 20×20 board is not thermally credible as a sustained rating.** The math below shows roughly 115 W of loss across the four ESCs at burst. That figure is only survivable for about 2–4 s, and only with prop-wash airflow. Market "80 A" nano ESCs are marketing burst figures. Specify the burst as **"80 A for ≤ X s from T_board ≤ Y °C"** and enforce it in firmware.
2. **6 FETs × 4 ESCs = 24 DFN3.3 FETs** on a ~25×25 mm board leaves almost no area for copper spreading. Consider 30×30 if the 80 A requirement is firm.
3. **Ripple current on bulk capacitance (~40 A_rms worst case, §4)** cannot be handled by onboard ceramics alone. A low-ESR electrolytic on the battery leads is mandatory.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-Dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.0055\text{–}0.0065\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

At T_j = 100 °C with α = 0.006:

$$
R_{DS}(100) = 1.5 \times (1 + 0.006 \times 75) = 1.5 \times 1.45 = 2.18\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

Datasheet curves often show 1.5–1.7× at 125 °C. Use the curve if available.

### 1.2 Current Path, 6-Step Trapezoidal with Synchronous Rectification

At any instant, two phases conduct. In each conducting phase, either the high-side or the low-side FET carries the current. Summed over a PWM period, one phase contributes D·I²R + (1−D)·I²R = I²R regardless of duty.

**Per ESC:**

$$
P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

Add the board copper path (traces, vias, and pads, typically 0.3–0.6 mΩ per phase loop):

$$
P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,\big(R_{DS}(T_j) + R_{Cu,ph}\big)
$$

### 1.3 At the 80 A Burst

| Case | R per phase path | P per ESC | P for 4 ESCs |
|---|---|---|---|
| FETs only, 25 °C | 1.5 mΩ | 2 × 6400 × 0.0015 = **19.2 W** | 76.8 W |
| FETs only, 100 °C | 2.18 mΩ | **27.9 W** | 111.6 W |
| FETs + 0.4 mΩ copper, 100 °C | 2.58 mΩ | **33.0 W** | 132 W |
| 0.7 mΩ FETs (5×6, won't fit nano), 100 °C | 1.02 mΩ | 13.0 W | 52 W |

**Per-FET dissipation** (worst-case FET, single phase at D ≈ 0.5):

$$
P_{FET} \approx \tfrac{1}{2}\,I^2 R_{DS} \cdot \tfrac{2}{3}
$$

The 2/3 factor accounts for each phase conducting 2/3 of the electrical cycle. At 100 °C this gives ≈ 4.6 W, plus switching loss (§2) for the PWM'd FET.

### 1.4 Note on FOC/Sinusoidal Drive

If the ESC firmware uses sinusoidal (FOC) drive instead of 6-step, replace I_ph with the phase RMS current and use three conducting phases:

$$
P_{cond} = 3\,I_{rms}^2\,R
$$

For equal torque this result is comparable.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Conditions: V_bus = 25.2 V, I = 80 A, one PWM'd half-bridge per commutation step (complementary).

### 2.1 Hard-Switching V·I Overlap (Active FET)

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}
$$

With an FD6288-class driver (~1.5 A sink/source) and Q_gd ≈ 10 nC, t_r + t_f ≈ 20 ns:

$$
P_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\times10^{-9} \times 48\,000 = 0.97\ \text{W}
$$

The rise/fall time comes from the Miller plateau:

$$
t_{r,f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,R_{G,total}}{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}
$$

For example, 10 nC × 5 Ω / (10 − 3.5) V ≈ 7.7 ns, plus the Q_gs2 interval.

### 2.2 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD}\,I\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\times10^{-9} \times 48\,000 = 0.61\ \text{W}
$$

Dead time is the second-largest switching term. Trim it to 50–70 ns once you have measured driver propagation skew.

### 2.3 Reverse Recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 40\times10^{-9} \times 25.2 \times 48\,000 = 0.048\ \text{W}
$$

The Q_rr at 80 A di/dt may be 2–3× the datasheet value; budget 0.15 W.

### 2.4 Output Capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,C_{oss,eq}\,V^2\,f_{sw}\times 2 = 1.2\times10^{-9} \times 635 \times 48\,000 = 0.037\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.5 Gate Drive (Dissipated in Driver + R_G, Not in the FET)

$$
P_{gate} = Q_g\,V_{drv}\,f_{sw}\times 2 = 45\times10^{-9} \times 10 \times 48\,000 \times 2 = 0.043\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.6 Switching Totals per ESC at 80 A

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| V·I overlap | 0.97 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Q_rr (derated) | 0.15 |
| C_oss | 0.04 |
| Gate (driver) | 0.04 |
| **Total** | **≈ 1.8 W** |

At 48 kHz, switching is about 6 % of the conduction loss at burst. **Conduction dominates, so prioritize R_DS(on) and copper over switching speed.** Dropping to 24 kHz would save only ~0.9 W per ESC but would increase current ripple and acoustic noise.

### 2.7 Total Power-Stage Loss at Burst (T_j ≈ 100 °C, with Copper)

$$
P_{ESC} \approx 33.0 + 1.8 = 34.8\ \text{W}, \qquad P_{4\text{-in-}1} \approx 139\ \text{W}
$$

Add the remaining board losses:

- Shunt resistors (if per-ESC current sense, 0.5 mΩ): 80² × 0.0005 ≈ 3.2 W each
- Bulk capacitor ESR (§4): ~1–3 W
- BEC/LDO: ~1 W

**Planning number: ≈ 150 W total at full 4×80 A burst.**
