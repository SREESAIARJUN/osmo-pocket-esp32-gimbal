# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #61): Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Budget, and Bi-Directional DShot600 DMA Allocation

## 0. Scope, Architecture, and Assumptions

**Architecture.** On every 4-in-1 design I am aware of, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot only. It does not commutate the motors. Each ESC channel, or the stack as a whole, has its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. This document uses that split:

- **H743 (FC):** generates bi-directional DShot600 and decodes eRPM telemetry.
- **4× AT32F421 (ESC MCUs, AM32):** 6-step commutation with complementary 48 kHz PWM.

**Assumed design parameters.** Replace these with your BOM values. Every equation below is parametric.

| Symbol | Meaning | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | Battery voltage (6S, full charge, worst case) | 25.2 V |
| I_ph | Burst phase current per motor | 80 A |
| R_ds(on),25 | MOSFET on-resistance at 25 °C (40 V, PDFN 3.3×3.3 class) | 1.0 mΩ |
| k_T | R_ds(on) multiplier at T_j ≈ 125 °C | 1.5, so R_ds(on),hot = 1.5 mΩ |
| t_r, t_f | Switching rise / fall time (≈1 A gate drive, Q_gd ≈ 10 nC) | 15 ns each |
| t_dt | Dead time per edge | 100 ns |
| V_SD | Body-diode forward voltage at 80 A | 0.8 V |
| Q_rr | Reverse-recovery charge | 40 nC |
| C_oss(er) | Energy-related output capacitance | 1.5 nF |
| Q_g | Total gate charge at V_gs = 10 V | 60 nC |
| f_sw | PWM frequency | 48 kHz |
| — | FET configuration | 1 FET per switch position (6 per ESC, 24 total) |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous commutation

In each 60° sector, two legs are active:

- **PWM leg:** current flows through the high-side (HS) FET for a fraction D of each PWM period. For the remaining (1−D), it flows through the complementary low-side (LS) FET in synchronous rectification. A small part of that interval is spent in the body diode during dead time.
- **Return leg:** the LS FET is held fully on.

So, outside dead time, exactly two R_ds(on) are in series with the phase current at all times:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds(on)}(T_j)\cdot\left(1 - \frac{2t_{dt}}{T_{sw}}\right)
$$

The dead-time fraction is 2·100 ns·48 kHz = 0.96%, which is negligible here.

### 1.2 Per-FET distribution

Each FET conducts for 120° electrical, so its average share is about 1/3 of its instantaneous loss. The split between HS and LS depends on D:

$$
P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{ds}\, D, \qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{ds}\,(2 - D)\;\big/\;\text{(normalised over 2 legs)}
$$

At burst current D → 1, so the losses are nearly symmetric.

**Why averaging is valid.** At 30 000 rpm with a 14-pole (7 pole-pair) motor, f_e = 3.5 kHz and T_e ≈ 286 µs. That is far shorter than the die thermal time constant (several ms), so averaging over the electrical cycle is accurate.

**Battery vs. phase current.** I_batt ≈ D·I_ph. Any "80 A burst" spec must state which current it refers to. Below I treat 80 A as the phase current at near-full throttle, where the two are approximately equal.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

- Instantaneous loss per conducting FET: 80² × 1.5 mΩ = **9.6 W**
- Per channel: 6400 × 2 × 1.5 mΩ = **19.2 W**
- Per FET (average): 19.2 / 6 = **3.2 W**
- Copper loss (see below): 6400 × 0.6 mΩ ≈ **3.8 W per channel**

The copper loss is significant. It assumes about 0.3 mΩ of phase-pad trace plus 0.3 mΩ of bus/ground path per channel, which is realistic for 2 oz copper on a 20×20 board. **It must be included in the thermal budget.**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. Its loss terms are:

$$
P_{sw} = \underbrace{\tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw}}_{\text{V–I overlap}}
+ \underbrace{Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}}_{\text{reverse recovery}}
+ \underbrace{2\cdot\tfrac12 C_{oss(er)}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw}}_{\text{C}_{oss}\text{ (both FETs)}}
+ \underbrace{V_{SD} I_{ph}\,2t_{dt} f_{sw}}_{\text{dead-time diode}}
$$

The gate-drive term, P_g = 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw, is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET.

| Term | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 30 ns × 48 k | **1.452 W** |
| Reverse recovery | 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.048 W |
| C_oss | 1.5 nF × 25.2² × 48 k | 0.046 W |
| Dead-time body diode | 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k | **0.614 W** |
| **Subtotal (FETs)** | | **≈ 2.16 W per channel** |
| Gate drive (driver IC/R_g) | 2 × 60 nC × 10 × 48 k | 0.058 W |

**Effect of the 48 kHz choice.** Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. Compared with AM32's common 24 kHz default, 48 kHz adds about 1.1 W per channel. In exchange, you get lower current ripple and quieter motors. Keep 48 kHz only if the ripple or acoustic benefit is measured on your motors.
