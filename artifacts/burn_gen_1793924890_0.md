# Job #62: STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC
## Design Model: Losses, Thermal Budget, Bi-directional DShot600 Timer/DMA Allocation, and Netlists

## 0. Architecture Assumptions

The brief leaves several points open. These assumptions apply throughout. Change any of them and the numbers follow the equations below.

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Topology | Stacked pair: **FC (STM32H743)** sends DShot to a **4-in-1 ESC** that has 4 independent ESC MCUs. The H743 does not drive gates directly. Bi-directional DShot only makes sense with this split. |
| ESC MCU / firmware | 4 × AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32. AM32 supports bi-directional DShot and a configurable PWM frequency (48 kHz is in range). |
| Gate driver | 1 × FD6288Q-class 3-phase half-bridge driver per motor (VCC 10 V from an LDO) |
| MOSFETs | 40 V N-ch, PDFN 3.3×3.3 or 5×6, 12 per board. Representative parameters: R_DS(on)(25 °C) = 1.5 mΩ max, t_r = 12 ns, t_f = 10 ns, Q_g = 30 nC, Q_rr = 40 nC, Q_oss = 30 nC, V_SD = 0.8 V, R_θJC = 1.5 K/W |
| Bus | 6S, V_bus = 25.2 V (full charge, worst case for switching loss) |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal, complementary (synchronous) PWM on the high side, f_sw = 48 kHz, dead time t_dt = 50 ns per edge |
| Burst definition | **80 A per motor, all 4 motors simultaneously**, for the worst case. A single-motor case is shown separately. |
| Form factor | "Nano" = 20×20 mm mounting. A 30×30 comparison is given for reference. |

> **Headline finding:** 80 A per motor × 4 on a 20×20 board is physically achievable only for about **1 s**. That is a burst number, not a continuous rating. The detailed derivation is in §3.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \mathrm{K^{-1}}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS(on) = 1.5 mΩ × 1.5 = **2.25 mΩ**. Use this value for all burst calculations.

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

In each 60° sector, one phase is the "PWM high side" (Q_H, duty D), its complementary low side is Q_L' (duty 1−D), and one low side Q_L is fully on in the return phase. Ignoring ripple (valid when L_phase·f_sw is much larger than R), the losses are:

$$P_{cond,H} = I^2 R\,D,\qquad P_{cond,L'} = I^2 R\,(1-D),\qquad P_{cond,L}=I^2R$$

$$\boxed{P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)} \quad\text{(independent of } D\text{)}$$

Including ripple: replace I² with I_rms² = I² + ΔI²/12, where ΔI = V_bus·D(1−D)/(L·f_sw). For a typical 5–20 µH winding at 48 kHz, this adds less than 2%. It is neglected below.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.25\,\mathrm{m\Omega} = \mathbf{28.8\ W\ per\ motor}$$

Worst-case single device (Q_L, 100% on within a sector):

$$P_{FET,max}=80^2\times 2.25\,\mathrm{m\Omega}=14.4\ \mathrm{W}$$

Commutation rotates which FET is stressed, so averaged over an electrical revolution each FET carries 1/3 duty as Q_L. Each FET therefore averages 28.8/6 ≈ 4.8 W. At low eRPM (startup, stall) the instantaneous 14.4 W figure applies.

### 1.4 Copper and trace conduction (often overlooked)

Phase pad plus bus copper on a 20×20 board with 2 oz × 2 power layers gives roughly 0.3–0.5 mΩ per current path:

$$P_{Cu}\approx 80^2\times 0.4\,\mathrm{m\Omega}\approx 2.6\ \mathrm{W\ per\ motor}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM high side hard-switches. The low side switches at near-zero V_DS (ZVS via body diode).

### 2.1 Overlap (V–I crossover)

$$P_{ov}=\tfrac12 V_{bus} I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw}=\tfrac12(25.2)(80)(22\,\mathrm{ns})(48\,\mathrm{kHz})=\mathbf{1.064\ W}$$

Better practice: compute t_r and t_f from the gate drive instead of reading them off the datasheet.

$$t_{r}\approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,R_{g,tot}}{V_{DRV}-V_{plateau}},\qquad t_f\approx\frac{Q_{gd}\,R_{g,tot}}{V_{plateau}}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery of the complementary body diode

$$P_{rr}=V_{bus}\,Q_{rr}\,f_{sw}=25.2\times40\,\mathrm{nC}\times48\,\mathrm{k}=0.048\ \mathrm W$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss}=V_{bus}\,Q_{oss}\,f_{sw}=25.2\times30\,\mathrm{nC}\times48\,\mathrm k=0.036\ \mathrm W$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM period.

$$P_{dt}=V_{SD}\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw}=0.8\times80\times100\,\mathrm{ns}\times48\,\mathrm k=0.307\ \mathrm W$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

Two FETs are switched per PWM cycle. This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g, not in the FET.

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{DRV} f_{sw}=2\times30\,\mathrm{nC}\times10\times48\,\mathrm k=0.029\ \mathrm W$$

### 2.6 Switching summary (per motor, 80 A)

| Component | W |
|---|---|
| Overlap | 1.064 |
| Q_rr | 0.048 |
| Q_oss | 0.036 |
| Dead time | 0.307 |
| Gate | 0.029 |
| **Total P_sw** | **1.48 W** |

Conduction dominates by about 20×. Raising f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz costs about 0.74 W per motor. That is a reasonable price for lower acoustic noise and lower current ripple. The design lever is R_DS(on), not switching speed.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Total burst dissipation

| Source | Per motor | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 28.8 W | 115.2 W |
| FET switching | 1.5 W | 6.0 W |
| Copper | 2.6 W | 10.4 W |
| Bulk cap ESR (see §3.5) | ~0.3 W | ~1.2 W |
| **Total** | **~33 W** | **~133 W** |

### 3.2 Thermal network (Cauer ladder)

```
P_FET ──[R_θJC 1.5]──●──[R_spread ~4]──●──[R_board→air]── T_amb
                     │                  │
                  C_pkg ~0.05 J/K    C_board ~2 J/K
```

Board-to-air resistance in prop-wash at about 10 m/s with h ≈ 80 W/m²K over 2 × 4 cm² gives:

$$R_{ba}=\frac{1}{h\,A}=\frac{1}{80\times8\times10^{-4}}\approx 15.6\ \mathrm{K/W}$$

The **steady-state capacity of a 20×20 board is about 5–6 W per 80 K rise**. Any 133 W event is therefore governed entirely by thermal capacitance.
