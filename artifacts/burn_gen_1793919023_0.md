# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #53): Power, Thermal and DShot Design Model

## 0. Architectural Corrections

Three issues change the design and need to be settled first.

1. **The H743 cannot commutate the motors under ArduPilot.** ArduPilot has no on-FC BLDC commutation. A 4-in-1 design therefore needs **four ESC MCUs** running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. The 48 kHz PWM, dead-time and back-EMF zero-crossing all live on those MCUs. The H743 only produces bidirectional DShot600 and consumes the eRPM telemetry.
   - Assumed ESC MCU: AT32F421K8U7 (QFN32), supported by AM32.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano board is a transient, not a rating.** The thermal model in §3 shows this is roughly a **1–2 s** capability before the junction limit. The steady-state capability is about 25–30 A per channel, and only with airflow.
3. **Form factor.** A 20×20 board cannot carry an H743 (LQFP100 is 14×14 mm), 24 MOSFETs and current-carrying copper.
   - Recommendation: a **two-board 25.5×25.5 mm stack** (FC on top, ESC below).
   - Or a single 30.5×30.5 mm "AIO" board.
   - The models below assume the stack.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction loss

In 6-step complementary PWM, phase current always flows through **exactly two conducting FETs**:
- **On-time:** the high-side of phase x and the low-side of phase y.
- **Off-time:** the low-side of phase x (synchronous freewheel) and the low-side of phase y.

This means the channel conduction loss does not depend on duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS}(T_j)=R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

Evaluated at T_j = 100 °C:
- R_DS = 1.45 mΩ
- P_cond = 2 · 6400 · 1.45 mΩ = **18.56 W**

Temperature makes this an electrothermal positive-feedback loop. Solving self-consistently:

$$P_{cond}(T_j)= \frac{2I^2R_{25}(1-25\alpha)+ \dots}{}$$

In practice, iterate T_j from the result of §3.

### 2.2 Switching loss (one leg hard-switches per PWM period)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = \tfrac12(25.2)(80)(40\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz}) = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

Sanity check on the switching time (Miller plateau):
- Plateau gate current: I_G ≈ (V_DRV − V_pl)/(R_G,ext + R_G,int) = (10 − 3.5)/(3.3 + 1.2) ≈ 1.4 A.
- Miller transition: t ≈ Q_gd / I_G ≈ 5.7 ns per transition, intrinsic.
- The 20 ns per edge allowance covers driver current limits and source inductance (L_s ≈ 1 nH, di/dt feedback). Measure this on the prototype.

### 2.3 Secondary loss terms

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_F · I · 2t_dt · f | 0.8 · 80 · 400 ns · 48 k = **1.23 W** |
| Reverse recovery | V_bus · Q_rr · f | **0.05 W** |
| C_oss | ½ C_oss V² f | **0.015 W** |
| Gate drive (two switching FETs) | 2 Q_g V_DRV f | **0.04 W** (dissipated in driver and R_G) |

Dead-time diode loss is significant. Reducing dead-time to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per channel, but only if shoot-through margin allows it.

### 2.4 Totals at the 80 A burst

| | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 18.56 W | 74.2 W |
| Switching | 1.94 W | 7.8 W |
| Dead-time / Q_rr / C_oss | 1.30 W | 5.2 W |
| **Total** | **≈21.8 W** | **≈87 W** |

Additional loss sources:
- **Shunt:** 0.5 mΩ, 80 A on the total bus → about 3.2 W at 80 A.
- **Copper:** 2 oz, about 0.3 mΩ effective → about 2 W per channel.
- **Board total:** about **95–100 W**.
