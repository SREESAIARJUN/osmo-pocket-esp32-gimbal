# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage and DShot Architecture Model

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Corrections

These choices determine every number that follows, so they come first.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot cannot run four sensorless BLDC controllers. The correct partitioning is:
   - **H743 (ArduPilot):** sends bi-directional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs.
   - **ESC MCUs (AM32 on AT32F421 or STM32G071, or Bluejay-class):** handle commutation.
   - **Power stage:** 3-phase gate driver → 6 N-MOSFETs per motor, 24 FETs total.
2. **"80A burst" is treated as an 80A RMS phase-current pulse per channel, with a stated duration.** As shown in Section 3, a nano-form-factor board (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) cannot sustain this. The thermal model below quantifies how long it survives.
3. **Component parameters are representative placeholders, not a sourced BOM.** Substitute your actual datasheet values into the equations.

| Parameter | Symbol | Assumed value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S, full charge) | V_bus | 25.2 V |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (6-step trapezoidal, synchronous rectification)

In any commutation sector, one high-side and one low-side FET carry the phase current. With complementary PWM and synchronous rectification, the current always flows through exactly two channels. During PWM-on, the path is the high-side FET of phase A and the low-side FET of phase B. During PWM-off, it is the low-side FETs of both phases. Therefore:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot \left(2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + R_{cu}\right), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right] \approx k_T R_{25}$$

At 80 A with T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{hot} = 1.5 \times 0.8 = 1.2\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{FET,cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.2\text{m} = \mathbf{15.36\ W}$$

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.6\text{m} = \mathbf{3.84\ W}$$

**Electrothermal feedback.** R_DS(on) rises with T_j, which raises the loss, which raises T_j further. Solve iteratively:

$$T_j = T_a + P(T_j)\cdot R_{\theta JA}$$

This loop is unstable (thermal runaway) when:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\cdot R_{\theta JA} \ge 1$$

where ∂P/∂T_j ≈ I²·2·R₂₅·α ≈ 6400 × 2 × 0.8 m × 0.005 ≈ 51 mW/K per ESC. Section 3 shows this matters on a nano board.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switched FET (hard-switched high side) incurs V·I overlap loss.

| Loss term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 20 ns × 48 k | **0.968 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2·t_dt·f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 2 × 100 ns × 48 k | **0.614 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 50 n × 25.2 × 48 k | **0.060 W** |
| C_oss charge | Q_oss·V·f_sw = 40 n × 25.2 × 48 k | **0.048 W** |
| Gate drive (driver dissipation) | Q_g·V_drv·f_sw × 2 FETs = 60 n × 10 × 48 k × 2 | **0.058 W** |
| **Total switching-related** | | **≈ 1.75 W** |

### Per-ESC and board totals at 80 A

| | Per ESC | Board (4 ESCs) |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 15.36 W | 61.4 W |
| Switching | 1.75 W | 7.0 W |
| Copper | 3.84 W | 15.4 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 21 W** | **≈ 84 W** |

**Design insight.** At 48 kHz, switching loss is only about 8% of the total, so this design is conduction-dominated. Dropping f_sw to 24 kHz saves about 0.8 W per ESC. Paralleling FETs halves the conduction term, which is the far more effective lever.

## 3. Thermal Model

### Equivalent network (per FET, Foster/Cauer simplified)

```
P_FET ──[Rθjc ≈ 1.5 K/W]── case ──[Rθcb ≈ 2 K/W (pad+vias)]── PCB ──[Rθba]── ambient
                                                                 │
                                                           C_board (shared)
```

**Board thermal capacitance** (25.5×25.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz power layers):
- Copper ≈ 4 g × 0.385 J/g·K ≈ 1.5 J/K
- FR4 ≈ 3 g × 1.1 J/g·K ≈ 3.3 J/K
- **C_board ≈ 5 J/K** (optimistic)

**Board-to-ambient resistance**, R_θba:
- About 15 K/W in still air
- About 5 K/W in strong prop-wash

### Steady state at 80 A on all 4 channels

$$\Delta T = 84\ \text{W} \times 5\ \text{K/W} = 420\ \text{K} \;\Rightarrow\; \text{impossible (destruction).}$$

### Burst survival time

Use a first-order model with τ = R_θba·C = 5 × 5 = 25 s. Allow T_board,max = 110 °C from T_a = 40 °C, so ΔT_allow = 70 K:

$$t_{burst} = -\tau \ln\left(1 - \frac{\Delta T_{allow}}{P R_{\theta ba}}\right) = -25\ln\left(1 - \frac{70}{420}\right) \approx \mathbf{4.6\ s}$$

This is a best case. It assumes all four channels start cold, there is strong airflow, and the thermal capacitance estimate holds.

### Junction-to-board offset

Each FET dissipates roughly 15.36/2 + 1.75 ≈ 9.4 W on the switched high-side device:

$$\Delta T_{j-b} = 9.4 \times (1.5 + 2) \approx 33\ \text{K}$$

So T_j reaches about 143 °C at the moment the board hits 110 °C. This leaves margin to T_j,max = 175 °C only if the electrothermal loop is stable.

**Stability check** (per FET):

$$0.026\ \text{W/K} \times 3.5\ \text{K/W} \approx 0.09 \ll 1$$

The loop is stable locally. The board-level loop is still the binding constraint.

### Realistic continuous rating

Solve P(I) × R_θba ≤ 70 K with R_θba = 5 K/W, so P_board ≤ 14 W:

$$4 \times I^2 \times (2 \times 1.2\text{m} + 0.6\text{m}) + 7 \times \frac{I}{80} \le 14 \;\Rightarrow\; I \approx \mathbf{32\ A\ continuous\ per\ channel}$$

**Recommendation: spec the board as 30 A continuous / 80 A for ≤ 2–3 s.** That duration requires:
- ESC firmware current limiting (AM32 current-limit set from the shunt)
- Temperature telemetry enforced in ArduPilot via EDT

Do not market 80 A without a duration attached.

**Also note battery current versus phase current.**

$$I_{bat} \approx D \cdot I_{ph}$$

At full duty, four channels at 80 A means about 320 A through the XT60 and battery pads. That alone dissipates I²R ≈ 320² × 0.2 mΩ ≈ 20 W in the input path, before any of the losses above.
