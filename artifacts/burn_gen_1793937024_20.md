# Job #44: STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: System Design Package

## 0. Architecture Framing and Assumptions

### 0.1 Architecture

The STM32H743 is the **flight controller** MCU. It does not commutate motors. A 4-in-1 ESC needs dedicated commutation MCUs: one per channel, or a shared multi-channel part. The standard partition is:

```
[STM32H743 FC] --DShot600 bidir (×4)--> [4× AT32F421 running AM32] --> [4× FD6288Q gate driver] --> [24× N-MOSFET] --> motors
```

### 0.2 Feasibility Flag

80 A burst per channel on a nano form factor (20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm) is thermally marginal. Section 2 shows that four channels at 80 A simultaneously dissipate more than 100 W into a board with roughly 3–6 J/K of heat capacity. That supports a burst of about 1–2 s, not continuous operation. The math below is general. Substitute your actual MOSFET datasheet values where marked **[DS]**.

## 1. Loss Model (per ESC channel, 6-step trapezoidal, synchronous rectification)

### 1.1 Conduction Losses

In any 60° commutation sector, one leg PWMs and one low-side FET carries the return current continuously. The PWM leg alternates as follows:

- High-side on for fraction D of each period.
- Complementary low-side on for fraction (1−D), minus dead time.

The current therefore always flows through exactly **two** channels in series, independent of D:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right]$$

This is self-consistent: R_ds depends on T_j, which depends on P. Solve by fixed-point iteration:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_{ref} + R_{\theta,eff}\cdot P\!\left(T_j^{(k)}\right)$$

**Evaluated at T_j = 125 °C:**

- R_ds = 1.5 mΩ × 1.40 = **2.10 mΩ**
- P_cond = 2 × 80² × 2.10 m = **26.9 W per channel**

### 1.2 Switching Losses (hard-switched edge, PWM leg only)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.968\ W}$$

### 1.3 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction (two dead-time intervals per period)

$$P_{dt} = 2\,V_f\,I\,t_{dt}\,f_{sw} = 2 \times 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.614\ W}$$

### 1.4 Reverse Recovery, C_oss, and Gate Drive

| Loss term | Formula | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw = 40n × 25.2 × 48k | 0.048 W |
| C_oss | ½ · C_oss · V² · f_sw = 0.5 × 1n × 635 × 48k | 0.015 W |
| Gate drive (two switching FETs, dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g) | 2 · Q_g · V_drv · f_sw = 2 × 30n × 10 × 48k | 0.029 W |

### 1.5 Totals

| Term | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 26.9 W | 107.6 W |
| Switching | 0.97 W | 3.9 W |
| Dead time | 0.61 W | 2.5 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.09 W | 0.4 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, if used: 80² × 0.25 m) | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| **Total** | **≈30.2 W** | **≈121 W** |

At 80 A the design is **~89 % conduction-dominated**. Two consequences follow:

- 48 kHz is affordable. Dropping to 24 kHz saves only about 0.8 W per channel.
- The lever that matters is R_ds(on). Paralleling two FETs per switch halves P_cond, giving about 13.5 W per channel.

Copper losses in 2 oz internal planes add a further ~1–3 W at 320 A battery current and should not be ignored.

## 2. Thermal Model

### 2.1 Per-FET Steady Rise Over Board

Each FET conducts for about 1/3 of the electrical cycle. Thermal time constants are on the order of milliseconds, comparable to or longer than the commutation period, so average power is used:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx P_{chan}/6 \approx 30.2/6 \approx 5.0\ \text{W}$$

$$\Delta T_{j-board} = \bar P_{FET}(R_{\theta JC}+R_{\theta CB}) = 5.0 \times 9.5 \approx \mathbf{48\ K}$$
