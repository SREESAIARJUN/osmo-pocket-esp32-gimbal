## 0. Architecture Decisions and Feasibility Flags

| Item | Decision |
|---|---|
| FC MCU | STM32H743VIT6, 480 MHz core. APB1 and APB2 timer clocks are 240 MHz. |
| ESC topology | 4 independent ESC MCUs (AT32F421 / AM32). The H743 does **not** commutate motors. It sends bidirectional DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry. |
| Bus voltage | 6S LiPo: 22.2 V nominal, 25.2 V maximum. Absorb 30 V regen spikes. |
| MOSFETs | 40 V, Rds(on) = 1.0 mΩ typ at 25 °C, PDFN5×6 class. 24 devices total. |
| PWM | 48 kHz center-aligned complementary PWM, 100 ns dead time. |
| Rating | 80 A burst for ≤ 5 s per motor. 45 A continuous with airflow. |

**⚠ Feasibility flag: form factor.** A "nano" stack (20×20 mm) cannot physically host 24 PDFN5×6 devices or dissipate the ~100 W burst computed below. This design therefore assumes:

- The FC is a 20×20 nano board.
- The ESC is a **30.5×30.5 mm** board with 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner copper, 6-layer.

If 20×20 is mandatory for the ESC as well, derate to approximately 35–40 A burst.

## 1. Electrical Operating Model (6-Step Trapezoidal, Complementary PWM)

During any 60° commutation sector:

- **Phase A (sourcing):** the high-side (HS) switch runs PWM at duty D, and the low-side (LS) switch acts as the complementary synchronous rectifier.
- **Phase B (sinking):** the LS switch is held fully on.
- **Phase C:** floating, used for BEMF zero-cross detection.

Each phase spends 1/3 of the electrical cycle sourcing, 1/3 sinking, and 1/3 floating.

Define the following symbols:

- I = phase current, assumed flat-top. Burst I = 80 A.
- R_on(T_j) = R_on,25 · [1 + α(T_j − 25)], with α ≈ 0.0065 /K.
  - At T_j = 125 °C: R_on ≈ 1.65 mΩ.
- V_bus = 25.2 V (worst case).
- f_sw = 48 kHz.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Per-switch conduction loss

**High-side switch:**

$$P_{cond,HS} = \frac{1}{3} I^2 R_{on}(T_j)\, D$$

**Low-side switch.** It acts as the synchronous rectifier while its phase sources, and is fully on while its phase sinks:

$$P_{cond,LS} = \frac{1}{3} I^2 R_{on}(T_j)(1-D) + \frac{1}{3} I^2 R_{on}(T_j)$$

### 2.2 Per-channel total

Summing over 3 phases:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 3(P_{HS}+P_{LS}) = I^2 R_{on}(D + (1-D) + 1) = 2 I^2 R_{on}$$

This result is independent of D. Two switches are always in the current path.

### 2.3 80 A burst evaluation

The calculation iterates T_j to convergence:

| Iteration | Assumed T_j | R_on | 2·I²·R_on |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 100 °C | 1.49 mΩ | 19.1 W |
| 2 | 125 °C | 1.65 mΩ | 21.1 W |
| 3 (converged, see §4) | 128 °C | 1.67 mΩ | **21.4 W** |

**Copper and shunt path loss:**

- Bus plane + phase pad + solder ≈ 0.35 mΩ effective → 80² × 0.35 mΩ = **2.24 W**
- Shunt resistor (0.25 mΩ, one per channel, carrying DC-link current I·D) at D = 1 → **1.6 W**

**Per-channel conduction total at burst: ≈ 25.2 W**

**Worst single switch.** This is the LS device during its sink interval plus the synchronous-rectifier interval. At D = 0.8:

$$P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(6400)(1.67\text{m})(0.2+1) = 4.28\text{ W}$$

At D = 1, the HS and LS devices each dissipate 3.56 W.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing phase leg switches. There is one switching leg per channel at any instant.

### 3.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

With t_r = t_f = 15 ns (2 A driver, R_g = 2.2 Ω):

$$P_{VI} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(30\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz}) = 1.45\text{ W}$$

### 3.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM cycle:

$$P_{dt} = V_F I (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(200\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz}) = 0.61\text{ W}$$

### 3.3 Reverse recovery (Q_rr ≈ 40 nC at 80 A, di/dt-dependent)

$$P_{rr} = V_{bus} Q_{rr} f_{sw} = 25.2(40\text{ nC})(48\text{ kHz}) = 0.048\text{ W}$$

### 3.4 Output capacitance (Q_oss ≈ 55 nC)

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.067\text{ W}$$

### 3.5 Gate drive (Q_g = 60 nC, V_GS = 10 V)

This loss is mostly dissipated in the driver and R_g, not in the MOSFET:

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 0.058\text{ W per leg}$$

### 3.6 Switching subtotal

| Term | W/channel |
|---|---|
| V–I overlap | 1.45 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Q_rr | 0.05 |
| Q_oss | 0.07 |
| Gate | 0.06 |
| **Total** | **2.24 W** |

**Sensitivity to switching frequency:**

$$\partial P_{sw}/\partial f = 46.7\ \mu\text{J}$$

Dropping from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves about 1.1 W per channel, at the cost of current ripple and audible noise.

**At D = 1 (100 % throttle):** if the firmware allows 100 % duty, switching loss collapses to approximately 0. **Design to the worst case of D ≈ 0.9 at 80 A.**
