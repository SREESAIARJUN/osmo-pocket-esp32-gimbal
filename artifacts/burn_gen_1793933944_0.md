# STM32H743 AIO + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and Bidirectional DShot600 Allocation

## 0. Assumptions and Scope

I don't have access to a "Job #62" spec, so every input parameter is stated explicitly. Replace the assumed values with your BOM's datasheet values and the equations still hold.

**Architecture assumption.** The STM32H743 is the *flight controller* MCU. Each ESC channel uses its own small MCU (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32), driven by the H743 over bidirectional DShot600. This is the standard AIO (all-in-one) topology. The H743 cannot practically commutate four BLDC motors in sensorless mode while also running ArduPilot.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation, current flows through the high-side FET of one phase and the low-side FET of another.

With synchronous rectification:
- During PWM on-time, current flows through HS_A and LS_B.
- During off-time, current freewheels through LS_A (actively enhanced) and LS_B.

So at every instant, two FET channels are in series with the motor current, independent of duty cycle D.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1} \Rightarrow R_{125} \approx 1.8\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Per-channel conduction loss

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j) + I^2 R_{Cu}$$

Assume R_Cu ≈ 0.5 mΩ, covering the phase copper, vias, and solder pad path on 2 oz copper.

| Case | I | R_DS | P_FET,cond (2 FETs) | P_Cu | **P_cond,ch** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Burst, hot | 80 A | 1.8 mΩ | 23.0 W | 3.2 W | **26.2 W** |
| Burst, cold | 80 A | 1.2 mΩ | 15.4 W | 3.2 W | 18.6 W |
| Continuous, hot | 45 A | 1.8 mΩ | 7.3 W | 1.0 W | 8.3 W |

### 1.4 Distribution across FETs

The loss is not evenly split. Over a commutation cycle, each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical period, as either the PWM switch, the freewheel path, or the static low side. Averaged:

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} = \frac{2 I^2 R_{DS}}{6} = \frac{I^2 R_{DS}}{3} \approx 3.84\ \text{W at 80 A, hot}$$

The instantaneous peak per FET is I²R = 11.5 W. That figure matters for transient junction rise at low eRPM, for example when a motor stalls or during a slow desync.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing FET pair (HS and its complementary LS) of the active phase switches per period.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap (high side)

$$E_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 24\text{ ns} = 24.2\ \mu\text{J}$$

$$P_{sw} = E_{sw} f_{sw} = 24.2\ \mu\text{J} \times 48\ \text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.16\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction (low side)

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD} \cdot I \cdot 2t_d \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{ ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

The loss is dissipated mostly in the HS FET at turn-on:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

Note that Q_rr scales with di/dt and current, so expect 2–3× this at 80 A. Budget 0.15 W.

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} \times 2 = 0.5 \times 1\text{nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = \mathbf{0.03\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET:

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2 = 50\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = \mathbf{0.048\ W/ch}$$

### 2.6 Switching summary per channel (80 A burst)

| Term | Loss |
|---|---|
| V·I overlap | 1.16 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.31 W |
| Q_rr (derated) | 0.15 W |
| C_oss | 0.03 W |
| **P_sw,ch** | **1.65 W** |

**Key insight.** At 48 kHz and 80 A, conduction loss (26 W) dominates switching loss (1.65 W) by roughly 16:1. Raising PWM to 96 kHz for smoother motor current costs only about 1.7 W more per channel. Lowering R_DS(on), by paralleling FETs or improving copper, is where the thermal margin lives.

## 3. Total Board Loss

$$P_{ch} = P_{cond} + P_{sw} + P_g \approx 26.2 + 1.65 + 0.05 = 27.9\ \text{W}$$

| Scenario | Per channel | ×4 channels | + H743, BEC, ESC MCUs (~1.5 W) | **Total** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 80 A burst, all motors, hot | 27.9 W | 111.6 W | 1.5 W | **≈113 W** |
| 45 A continuous, hot | 9.4 W | 37.6 W | 1.5 W | **≈39 W** |

Efficiency at burst: P_out ≈ 4 × 80 A × 22.2 V × D_eff. At full throttle the bus-side power approaches about 7 kW, so ESC efficiency is about 98.4%. That sounds good, but 113 W concentrated in roughly 30×30 mm is the actual problem.
