## 0. Architecture Clarification

The STM32H743 is the **flight controller** MCU. It does **not** commutate the motors. In ArduPilot, the H743 generates bidirectional DShot600 frames and decodes eRPM telemetry. Each ESC channel runs its own commutation MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071) with AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, plus a 3-phase gate driver.

```
LiPo 6S ─┬─► 4× [ESC MCU → 3-ph gate driver → 6× N-MOSFET] → Motors 1-4
         └─► Buck 5V/3V3 → STM32H743 FC ──DShot600 bidir──► ESC MCUs
```

**On "80 A burst":** I interpret this as **80 A per motor phase current for ≤ 5 s**. If 80 A is the *total battery* current (about 20 A per motor), the conduction losses below drop by roughly 16×. The derivation shows that 80 A per motor on a nano footprint is only adiabatically survivable for about 2 to 3 s. This is the key design risk.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current path in 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification

In any commutation sector, two phases carry I_ph and one floats.

- The PWM leg alternates between the high-side FET (fraction D) and the synchronous low-side FET (1 − D).
- The return leg's low-side FET conducts 100 %.

So at every instant, exactly **two FETs** are in series with the motor current, regardless of duty cycle:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_J) + I_{ph}^2\,R_{cu}
$$

### 2.2 Temperature dependence (self-consistent form)

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_J) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_J-25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0065\ \mathrm{K^{-1}}
$$

Since T_J itself depends on P, the equation is solved iteratively. Convergence is reached at T_J ≈ 110 °C, which gives R_hot = 1.55 mΩ.

### 2.3 Numbers at 80 A

$$
I^2 = 6400\ \mathrm{A^2}
$$

| Term | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (2 in series) | 2 × 6400 × 1.55 mΩ | **19.84 W** |
| PCB copper | 6400 × 0.4 mΩ | **2.56 W** |
| **Conduction subtotal per motor** | | **22.4 W** |

### 2.4 Per-FET loss distribution

Each of the 6 FETs acts as the "100 % return" device for 1/3 of the electrical cycle. It also shares the PWM role for another 1/3.

- Average loss per FET ≈ 19.84 / 6 × 2 ≈ **6.6 W** (time-averaged over the electrical period).
- **Peak instantaneous loss** while conducting = 6400 × 1.55 mΩ = **9.9 W**.

At high eRPM, the electrical period (about 0.3 to 2 ms) is shorter than the die thermal time constant (about 5 ms). The junction therefore sees close to the average loss, with a ripple of a few kelvin.

## 3. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

### 3.1 V-I overlap (hard switching, inductive load)

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 20\text{ ns} × 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.968\ W}
$$

### 3.2 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,er} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 × 1.5\text{ nF} × 635 × 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.023\ W}
$$

### 3.3 Body-diode reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 40\text{ nC} × 25.2 × 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

### 3.4 Dead-time diode conduction (2 edges per period)

$$
P_{dt} = 2\,V_F I_{ph} t_{dt} f_{sw} = 2 × 0.8 × 80 × 200\text{ ns} × 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{1.229\ W}
$$

**Design note:** dead-time loss exceeds V-I overlap loss. Reducing t_dt from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per motor. This is the cheapest efficiency win available, subject to the gate driver's shoot-through margin.

### 3.5 Gate drive (dissipated in driver and gate resistors, not in the FET)

$$
P_{gate} = Q_g V_{DRV} f_{sw} × 2 = 60\text{ nC} × 10 × 48\text{ k} × 2 = 0.058\ \mathrm{W}
$$

### 3.6 Switching subtotal per motor

| Loss | W |
|---|---|
| V-I overlap | 0.968 |
| C_oss | 0.023 |
| Q_rr | 0.048 |
| Dead time | 1.229 |
| **FET switching total** | **2.27 W** |
| Gate drive (driver IC) | 0.058 |

### 3.7 Total power-stage loss

| | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 19.84 W | 79.4 W |
| Copper | 2.56 W | 10.2 W |
| Switching | 2.27 W | 9.1 W |
| Gate drive | 0.06 W | 0.2 W |
| **Total** | **24.7 W** | **≈ 99 W** |

Output power at 80 A × 4 × (about 0.75 × 25.2 V effective) ≈ 6 kW, so the power stage is about 98.4 % efficient. The difficulty is not efficiency. It is that **99 W is concentrated on a 6.5 cm² board**.

### 3.8 Frequency sensitivity (scaling law)

$$
P_{total}(f) = P_{cond} + f\,(E_{VI}+E_{oss}+E_{rr}+E_{dt}),\qquad \sum E \approx 47.3\ \mu\mathrm{J}
$$

| f_sw | Switching loss per motor |
|---|---|
| 24 kHz | 1.14 W |
| 48 kHz | 2.27 W |
| 96 kHz | 4.54 W |

At 80 A, conduction loss dominates (about 90 %). At 48 kHz, frequency is a secondary lever.
