## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

**Who switches the MOSFETs.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and generates bi-directional DShot600. It does not switch the MOSFETs. Each ESC channel has its own MCU (here an AT32F421, running AM32), which generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM through a triple half-bridge gate driver. The model below follows that split.

**Assessment of "80A burst" on a nano form factor.** The numbers below show that 80 A per channel is a transient rating of a few seconds, set by the board's thermal mass. It cannot be a thermal steady state. This has to be designed into firmware limits, not hidden in a spec sheet.

## 1. Electrical Loss Model, Per ESC Channel

### 1.1 Commutation topology

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal (AM32-style) commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM on the active high-side leg:

- Each 60° electrical sector has two legs conducting: one PWM leg and one static low-side leg.
- Each MOSFET conducts for 120° of each electrical cycle, i.e. 1/3 of the time.
- Within the PWM leg, the high-side (HS) conducts for fraction D and the low-side (LS) synchronous rectifier conducts for (1−D).

### 1.2 Conduction losses

With R_hot = k_T · R_ds(on),25 = 1.5 mΩ:

**Per-device average (over the electrical cycle):**

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{hot} D \qquad P_{LS,sync} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{hot}(1-D) \qquad P_{LS,static} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{hot}$$

**Per channel.** Two devices always carry the current, whatever D is:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2(2R_{hot} + R_{cu}) = 6400\,(3.0 + 0.25)\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{20.8\ W}$$

The single hottest device is the low-side FET, which carries static plus synchronous conduction. Its worst-case share is:

$$P_{FET,max} \approx \tfrac{2}{3}\,I^2 R_{hot} \approx 6.4\ \text{W (at low D)}$$

This is an upper bound when sync and static intervals overlap on the same leg across sectors. Averaged over all six FETs it is about 3.2 W each.

**Duty-cycle note.** At 80 A the motor is near full throttle, so D → 0.9–1.0. The HS FETs then carry most of the PWM-leg loss, and the LS sync loss collapses.

### 1.3 Switching losses (one leg switches per PWM period)

**Hard-switching overlap (V–I crossover):**
$$E_{sw} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r + t_f) = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\,\text{ns} = 30.2\ \mu\text{J}$$
$$P_{sw} = E_{sw} f_{sw} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery** (LS body diode recovers when HS turns on):
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f = 50\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

**Output capacitance:**
$$P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss} V f = 0.07\ \text{W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period):**
$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2 t_{dead}) f = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**Gate drive** (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET):
$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{GS} f = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

### 1.4 Frequency sensitivity

Switching-related loss is about **2.2 W at 48 kHz** versus **1.1 W at 24 kHz**. At 80 A, 48 kHz costs roughly 1.1 W/channel, about 5% of channel loss.

The benefit of 48 kHz is lower current ripple and acoustic noise on low-inductance motors. Make it a parameter, not a fixed choice.
