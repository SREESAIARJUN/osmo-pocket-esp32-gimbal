## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step complementary PWM

In each 60° commutation sector, two phases conduct:

- **Phase A (PWM leg):** the high side conducts for duty D, then the low side conducts for 1−D (synchronous rectification).
- **Phase B (return leg):** the low side conducts for 100% of the sector.

The conduction loss per channel is therefore:

$$P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\left[D + (1-D) + 1\right] = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

The result is independent of D. This is the key insight: conduction loss at a given phase current does not depend on throttle.

### 1.2 Ripple and temperature terms

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{2L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

Take a typical 2207 motor with L_ph ≈ 15 µH, and a burst at D ≈ 0.9 with V_bemf ≈ 20 V:

- ΔI_pp ≈ (5.2 × 0.9) / (2 × 15e-6 × 48e3) ≈ 3.3 A
- The ripple term is ≈ 0.9 A², which is negligible against 6400 A².

The temperature dependence of R_ds(on) is:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right]$$
