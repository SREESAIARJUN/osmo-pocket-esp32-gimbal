## 0. Architecture Clarification

A real "H743 4-in-1 ESC" is split into two compute domains:

| Domain | Device | Firmware | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 @ 400 MHz (480 MHz on rev V) | ArduPilot | Generates bidirectional DShot600 ×4 and decodes eRPM telemetry |
| ESC (×4) | AT32F421 / STM32G071 class, one per motor | AM32 or BLHeli_32 | Six-step commutation and 48 kHz complementary PWM |

The H743 does **not** drive MOSFET gates directly. Its six advanced-timer outputs could not produce 4 motors × 3 phases. Even if they could, ArduPilot has no sensorless commutation loop. The 48 kHz switching is produced by the ESC MCUs. The H743 sends one DShot frame per motor per control loop.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Conduction topology

In 120° six-step, exactly two phase legs carry current at any instant: one sourcing and one sinking. With complementary PWM at duty D, the conduction path works as follows:

- **PWM leg:** the high side conducts for fraction D; the synchronous low side conducts for (1 − D).
- **Return leg:** the low side conducts continuously during its 60° sector.

So **exactly two channels are always in the current path**, and the per-motor conduction loss is independent of D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) + I^2 R_{pcb} + I^2 R_{shunt}$$

with the temperature dependence

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

**Per-device average.** Each FET position is active for 1/3 of the electrical cycle. Within that window, the PWM-side FET carries the current for fraction D or (1 − D). For the worst-case device (return-leg low side, or high side at D → 1):

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{1}{3}\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 2.2 Ripple correction

Phase-current ripple adds to the RMS current:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp} \approx \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{2L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

For a typical L_ph = 15 µH at D = 0.5:

- ΔI_pp ≈ 25.2·0.25 / (2·15 µH·48 kHz) ≈ 4.4 A.
- The ripple term is ΔI_pp²/12 ≈ 1.6 A², which is negligible against 6400 A².
- At 48 kHz, ripple loss is **not** a design driver.
