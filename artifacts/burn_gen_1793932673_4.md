# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #56): Architecture, Loss Model, DShot/DMA Plan

## 0. Architecture Corrections Before Modeling

Two problems in the brief would sink the design if not corrected first.

**A. The H743 should not commutate the motors itself.** Four motors need 4 × 3 = 12 complementary half-bridge pairs, which is 24 gate signals with hardware dead-time. The H743 can only provide part of that:

| Resource | Complementary pairs with HW dead-time |
|---|---|
| TIM1 | 3 (+CH4, no complement) |
| TIM8 | 3 (+CH4, no complement) |
| HRTIM (5 units × 2 outputs) | 5 |
| **Total** | **11, fewer than the 12 required, with no FOC/BEMF timing headroom** |

On top of that, sharing the flight-control loop with 4× commutation ISRs at 48 kHz costs determinism.

**Recommended architecture:** H743 as the flight controller, plus 4× dedicated ESC MCUs (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32). These are linked on-board by bidirectional DShot600. Everything below assumes this.

**B. 80 A burst × 4 on a "nano" (20 × 20 mm) board is not thermally achievable.** Section 2.4 proves this numerically. Two options:
- Treat 80 A as a **per-motor, ≤1 s** burst on a **30.5 × 30.5 mm** board.
- Keep 20 × 20 mm and re-spec to roughly 35–40 A burst.

## 2. Thermal / Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction loss

In 6-step operation with synchronous rectification, two FETs are always in series with the motor current:
- **On-interval:** HS_A + LS_B
- **Off-interval:** LS_A (synchronous) + LS_B

Conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle D, to first order:

$$
P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS,hot}, \qquad R_{DS,hot} = k_T R_{25} = 2.25\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.00225 = \mathbf{28.8\ W}
$$

Per-FET split: this is spread over 6 FETs per electrical cycle, about 4.8 W average per FET. However, the instantaneous FET carries $I^2 R$ = **14.4 W** while conducting, and the fundamental electrical period is short relative to junction τ, so use the average for the board and the instantaneous value for T_j ripple checks.

### 2.2 Switching losses (hard-switched leg, at 48 kHz)

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = 0.5·25.2·80·30 ns·48 k | 1.45 W |
| Coss charge | Q_oss·V·f = 40 n·25.2·48 k | 0.05 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f = 50 n·25.2·48 k | 0.06 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_F·I·2t_dt·f = 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k | 0.61 W |
| Gate drive (in driver, 2 FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_drv·f | 0.06 W |
| **P_sw total** | | **≈ 2.2 W** |

Add a 30–50 % margin for layout-induced ringing. Derate t_r/t_f using the measured V_DS waveform.

### 2.3 Per-channel and board totals at 80 A

| | Per ESC | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| P_cond | 28.8 W | 115 W |
| P_sw | 2.2 W | 9 W |
| Bus copper (2 oz, ~0.3 mΩ path) | ~1.9 W | ~8 W |
| **Total** | **~33 W** | **~132 W** |

Note also that the battery input sees **4 × 80 A = 320 A** of burst current. Neither an XT60 nor a nano-class pad can carry this.

### 2.4 Transient (adiabatic) thermal response

For a short burst, the board behaves as a lumped heat capacity:

$$
\frac{dT}{dt} \approx \frac{P_{total}}{C_{th}}, \qquad C_{th} = \sum m_i c_{p,i}
$$

**20 × 20 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz Cu, 1.6 mm FR4:**
- Cu: 0.02 × 0.02 × (6 × 70 µm) × 8960 kg/m³ ≈ 1.5 g → 0.58 J/K
- FR4: 0.02 × 0.02 × 1.6 mm × 1850 kg/m³ ≈ 1.2 g → 1.3 J/K
- FETs/components: ≈ 0.3
