# STM32H743 AIO with 4-in-1 80 A ESC: System Architecture, Loss Model, DShot DMA Plan

**Job #14: Rev A Architecture Package**

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Reality Checks

The analysis below rests on three architectural points and a set of assumed component values.

**The H743 does not run the ESC.**
- In a "4-in-1 ESC + FC" AIO, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bi-directional DShot600.
- Commutation and the 48 kHz PWM run on separate ESC MCUs. This design assumes 4× AT32F421 running AM32. A single-MCU 4-motor design such as an STM32G4 is possible but is not assumed.
- The 48 kHz switching frequency is therefore an ESC firmware setting, not an ArduPilot one.

**"80 A" means per-motor burst current, not continuous.**
- On a 20×20 mm "nano" board, Section 3 shows the realistic continuous rating is about 25–30 A per motor.
- The 80 A figure is a time-limited burst of about 1.5–2 s.
- Any datasheet or marketing claim should state both numbers.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-step (trapezoidal) with complementary PWM

These assumptions apply:
- AM32 runs synchronous rectification.
- Phase current always flows through exactly two conducting devices: one high-side (HS) and one low-side (LS).
- During PWM off-time, the HS current transfers to the complementary LS (synchronous rectification).
- The series path therefore stays at roughly 2·R_DS(on) regardless of duty cycle D.

**Per-motor conduction loss:**

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\right]$$

With α ≈ 0.005 /K, the multiplier at 125 °C is ≈ 1.5:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 2.4\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{30.7\ W}$$

**Per-FET average loss.** Each FET carries current for 120° of the electrical period. At typical eRPM, the electrical period is ≪ the thermal time constant of the die:

$$P_{FET,avg} = \frac{30.7}{6} \approx \mathbf{5.1\ W}, \qquad P_{FET,inst} = I^2 R_{hot} = 15.4\ \text{W}$$

**Board total (all 4 motors at burst):** 4 × 30.7 ≈ **123 W**.

### 1.2 Thermal-runaway check

Because R_DS(on) rises with T_j, the fixed point must be solved by iteration:

$$T_j = T_{board} + P(T_j)\cdot R_{\theta jb}$$

With R_θjb ≈ 1.5 K/W, the die runs about 8 K above board. The loop is stable whenever:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\,R_{\theta} < 1$$

Here ∂P/∂T ≈ 5.1 W × 0.005/1.5 ≈ 0.017 W/K, so the loop gain is about 0.03. This is stable. Board temperature is the limiter, not the junction.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

In six-step operation, one leg per motor is PWM-switching at any instant.

| Loss term | Equation | Per motor |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·20 ns·48 k | **0.97 W** |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_SD·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k | **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 30 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.036 W |
| C_oss charge/discharge | C_oss·V²·f_sw (both edges) | 0.03 W |
| Gate drive (in the driver, not the FET) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw | 0.038 W |
| **Switching total** | | **≈ 1.65 W** |

**Total per motor at 80 A:** 30.7 + 1.65 ≈ **32.4 W**. **Board total:** ≈ **130 W**.

**Design implications:**
- Conduction loss is about 95% of the total. Spend budget on lower R_DS(on) or paralleled FETs, not on faster edges.
- Going from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves only about 0.8 W per motor.
- 48 kHz is justified for low-inductance motors, where it reduces current ripple: ΔI = V·D(1−D)/(L·f_sw).
- It is also justified for acoustic reasons, since it keeps switching noise above audible range.
- Dead-time loss is the second-largest term. Do not set AM32 dead time higher than needed for shoot-through margin; verify with a scope on the switch node.
