# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #5)
## Architecture, Loss Model, Thermal Budget, Bi-directional DShot600 DMA Plan, and Reference Netlists

---

## 0. Key Findings

These issues determine whether the design is viable, so they come first.

1. **The H743 should not commutate the motors.** A practical 4-in-1 AIO uses the H743 as the flight controller running ArduPilot. It sends bi-directional DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32). Running ArduPilot's scheduler and four sensorless FOC/6-step loops on one H743 is not a supported configuration. The DMA analysis in §4 assumes this split.
2. **80 A per motor is a burst rating only, about 2 s.** On a nano or 30.5 mm board, the model in §3 gives about 99 W of total bridge loss at 4×80 A. The board can shed only about 13 W continuously. Sustainable continuous current is about 24 A per motor with all four loaded. The board must be marketed and configured as **"24 A continuous / 80 A ≤2 s burst"**, and an ESC-side current or thermal limiter must enforce it.
3. **"Nano" means 20×20 mm, which cannot hold 24 MOSFETs rated for 80 A plus an H743.** The calculations below assume a **30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz outer / 1 oz inner** board. On a true 20×20 board, divide the burst duration by about 2.3, the area ratio.
4. All component values are representative choices for the model. They must be validated against the actual datasheets and by bench thermal imaging before release.

## 3. Loss Model

### 3.1 Conduction losses (6-step trapezoidal, complementary PWM)

At any instant, two phases carry current. The PWM leg alternates HS↔LS synchronously, and the opposing leg's LS FET is held fully on. The current path therefore always passes through two channels in series:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j)=k_T R_{ds,25}=1.6\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.6\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{20.5\ W\ per\ motor\ channel}$$

Averaged over the six FETs, each FET is one of the two conductors one-third of the time:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \tfrac{1}{3}I^2R_{ds} = \mathbf{3.41\ W}$$

**Ripple correction.** The phase current ripple is

$$\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}-E)\,D}{L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 2–3 mm-class motor (L ≈ 10 µH), ΔI_pp is about 10–15 A. Then

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \Delta I^2/12$$

This adds less than 0.3 %, so it is neglected.

### 3.2 Switching losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The HS FET takes the V-I overlap, and the LS FET takes the dead-time diode loss.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·60 ns·48 k | 2.90 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 50 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.06 W |
| Coss | Q_oss·V·f_sw = 60 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.07 W |
| Dead-time diode | V_SD·I·2t_d·f_sw = 0.8·80·400 ns·48 k | 1.23 W |
| Gate drive (in driver, not FET) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw |
