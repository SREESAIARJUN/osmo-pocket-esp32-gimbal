# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #81): Architecture, Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, DShot Allocation, Netlists

## 0. Feasibility Findings

Three problems with the brief need resolving before the math is useful.

1. **The STM32H743 cannot be the ESC controller under ArduPilot.** ArduPilot runs the flight controller. It does not commutate motors. The 4-in-1 ESC needs its own MCUs. The reference design here uses four AT32F421 (or STM32G071) chips running AM32. The H743 talks to them over bidirectional DShot600. Everything below assumes this split.
2. **"80A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is thermally impossible beyond a few hundred milliseconds.** Section 3 shows why. The math gives a defensible rating and the layout changes needed to reach 80A on a 30.5×30.5 board.
3. **Real part numbers must be substituted.** I use representative datasheet values and label them. Replace them with your chosen MOSFET's numbers before release.

## 2. Power-Stage Loss Model

### 2.1 Assumed parameters (replace with your datasheet)

| Symbol | Meaning | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 6S, full charge | 25.2 V |
| I_ph | Burst phase current | 80 A |
| R_ds,on(25°C) | 40 V, PDFN 5×6 class FET | 0.75 mΩ typ |
| k_T | R_ds,on multiplier at T_j = 125 °C | 1.6 |
| t_r, t_f | Switching edges at the driver's R_g | 18 ns, 15 ns |
| Q_g | Total gate charge at 10 V | 65 nC |
| Q_rr | Body-diode reverse recovery charge | 45 nC |
| C_oss,eq | Energy-equivalent output capacitance | 1.8 nF |
| V_SD | Body-diode forward voltage | 0.8 V |
| t_dt | Dead time per edge | 100 ns |
| f_sw | PWM frequency | 48 kHz |
| R_θJC | Junction to case | 1.0 K/W |

### 2.2 Commutation model

The drive is 6-step trapezoidal with complementary (synchronous) PWM on the high side. At any instant, one phase's high-side/low-side pair is PWM-switching at duty D, and one low-side FET of another phase conducts continuously.

**Conduction loss** (worst case at 125 °C):

$$R_{hot}=k_T R_{ds,on}=1.6\times0.75=1.2\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond}=I^2R_{hot}\big[D+(1-D)\big]+I^2R_{hot}=2I^2R_{hot}$$

Synchronous rectification makes the PWM leg's loss independent of D.

$$P_{cond}=2\times80^2\times1.2\times10^{-3}=\mathbf{15.36\ W\ per\ channel}$$

**Switching (V–I overlap) loss**, hard-switched leg only:

$$P_{sw}=\tfrac12 V_{bus}I(t_r+t_f)f_{sw}=0.5\times25.2\times80\times33\text{ns}\times48\text{k}=\mathbf{1.60\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction:**

$$P_{dt}=V_{SD}\,I\,(2t_{dt})f_{sw}=0.8\times80\times200\text{ns}\times48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}=45\text{nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.054\ W}$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss}=\tfrac12 C_{oss}V_{bus}^2f_{sw}\times2=1.8\text{nF}\times635\times48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.055\ W}$$

**Gate drive** (two FETs switching):

$$P_g=2Q_gV_{GS}f_{sw}=2\times65\text{nC}\times10\times48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.062\ W}$$

This loss is dissipated in the driver and R_g, not in the FET.

**Copper losses.** Assume a 2 oz/4-layer phase path of 0.4 mΩ, plus 0.3 mΩ of the bus share.

$$P_{Cu}\approx80^2\times0.7\text{m}\Omega=\mathbf{4.5\ W}$$

### 2.3 Totals

| Loss | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 15.36 W | 61.4 W |
| Switching | 1.60 W | 6.4 W |
| Dead-time | 0.61 W | 2.4 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.11 W | 0.4 W |
| Copper | 4.5 W | 18.0 W |
| **Total** | **≈22.2 W** | **≈88.7 W** |

Conduction dominates at about 70%. Raising f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz therefore costs only about 1.1 W per channel. Lowering R_ds,on is the effective lever.

Paralleling two FETs per position halves conduction loss. Switching loss stays roughly the same because Q_g doubles, which slows the edges. With paralleling, the per-channel total falls to about 14.5 W.

### 2.4 Bus-capacitor ripple at 48 kHz

$$I_{C,rms}=I\sqrt{D(1-D)}\;\Rightarrow\;40\text{ A rms at }D=0.5$$

With four channels interleaved (random phase), the combined ripple is approximately 40·√4 = 80 A rms worst-case correlated, or about 40–55 A in practice.

- Allocate about 3 A rms per 1206 X7R 10 µF/50 V.
- Use at least 12 MLCCs per channel, placed within 3 mm of each half-bridge.
- Add an external 1000 µF/35 V low-ESR electrolytic on the pigtail.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Junction rise for one FET (steady state)

During a burst, the worst FET is the low side that conducts continuously for its 1/3 commutation window. Averaged over the electrical cycle, per-FET dissipation is about P_ch/4 ≈ 4.3 W. The instantaneous peak while conducting is I²R = 7.7 W.

The thermal network:

$$T_j=T_a+P\,(R_{\theta JC}+R_{\theta CB}+R_{\theta BA,share})$$

For a 20×20 board, 4-layer 2 oz copper, with 15 m/s prop-wash:

- R_θCB (thermal vias, 25× 0.3 mm vias) ≈ 2.5 K/W
- Board to ambient, whole board: about 4 K/W. Shared by 24 FETs, this behaves as a common node.

The board node temperature is:

$$T_{board}=T_a+P_{total}\cdot R_{\theta BA}=40+88.7\times4\approx\mathbf{395\ °C}$$

This is impossible. The board cannot sustain 80A on all four channels. Steady state on this board is limited to about 15–20 W total, which corresponds to roughly 25–30 A continuous per channel.

### 3.2 Transient (burst) model, two-node Foster network

| Node | Capacitance |
|---|---|
| Board + Cu + FETs, 20×20 | C_b ≈ 2.5 J/K (≈2.8 g effective, c ≈ 0.9 J/gK) |
| Junction | C_j ≈ 0.02 J/K, τ_j = R_θJC·C_j ≈ 20 ms |

Board heating rate at full 4×80 A:

$$\frac{dT_b}{dt}\approx\frac{P_{total}}{C_b}=\frac{88.7}{2.5}=35\ \text{K/s}$$

With T_a = 40 °C, a board limit of T_b,max = 110 °C, and T_j,max = 150 °C (leaving 40 K of headroom for the junction-to-board rise of 7.7 W × 3.5 K/W ≈ 27 K):

$$t_{burst}\approx\frac{110-40}{35}\approx\mathbf{2.0\ s}\ \text{(all 4 channels, from cold)}$$

From a warm board at 80 °C, the burst window is about 0.85 s.

### 3.3 Defensible rating

| Board | Continuous / channel | Burst |
|---|---|---|
| 20×20 nano, 6 FETs/channel | 30 A | 60 A for 1 s |
| 30.5×30.5, 12 FETs/channel, 3 oz | 50 A | 80 A for 3 s (C_b ≈ 6 J/K, R_θBA ≈ 2 K/W) |

**Recommendation:** If marketing requires an 80 A burst, use the 30.5×30.5 board with paralleled FETs. The nano board ships at "60 A burst".

### 3.4 Firmware thermal protection

The ESC MCU runs the following model at 1 kHz:

```c
T_b += dt*(P_est - (T_b - T_ntc_ambient)/R_ba)/C_b;   // P_est = 2*I^2*R(T) + k_sw*I*V*f
if (T_b > 105) current_limit = derate(T_b);            // linear to 0 at 125 C
```

Hardware thermal sensing uses one NTC 10 kΩ (B = 3435) per channel, placed between the low-side FETs.
