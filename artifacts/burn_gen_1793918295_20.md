## 0. Architecture Clarification (Read First)

Three corrections to the premise:

1. **The STM32H743 cannot be the ESC commutation controller.** In every production "FC + 4-in-1" stack, the H743 is the flight controller running ArduPilot. Commutation runs on four dedicated ESC MCUs, such as STM32G071 or AT32F421 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The 48 kHz PWM is an ESC-side parameter. DShot600 is the FC-to-ESC link. This document models both sides, with that partition.

2. **"Nano" (20×20 mm) and "80 A burst" are thermally incompatible except for very short bursts.** Section 3 shows the board heats at roughly 25 to 30 K/s at full 4-motor burst. Section 3.5 recommends changes.

3. **All component values are representative.** Before release, substitute the datasheet values for your actual MOSFET, gate driver, and capacitors. The equations are exact. The numbers are illustrative.

**MOSFET voltage rating.** A 40 V part on 6S leaves only about 15 V of margin for ringing. That is acceptable only with less than 2 nH of loop inductance and local MLCC snubbing. Use 60 V parts if bus ringing exceeds about 8 V peak on prototype measurements.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per motor, six-step trapezoidal, synchronous complementary PWM)

### 2.1 Conduction topology

In six-step commutation, two legs are active at any instant. With synchronous rectification, the current path always contains exactly one FET in the PWM leg (high-side during t_on, low-side during t_off) plus one FET in the static low leg. Therefore:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{ph,rms}^2 \, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At I = 80 A (flat-top approximation, I_rms ≈ I_dc during the 120° conduction window, which is applied to every instant):

| T_j (°C) | R_ds (mΩ) | P_cond per motor (W) |
|---|---|---|
| 25 | 1.00 | 12.8 |
| 75 | 1.30 | 16.6 |
| 110 | 1.51 | 19.3 |
| 150 | 1.75 | 22.4 |

**Electro-thermal feedback.** Because P depends on T_j, solve the fixed point:

$$T_j = T_a + R_{\theta,eff}\cdot 2I^2 R_{ds,25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

$$T_j = \frac{T_a + R_{\theta,eff}\, 2I^2R_{ds,25}(1-25\alpha)}{1 - R_{\theta,eff}\, 2I^2R_{ds,25}\,\alpha}$$

Thermal runaway occurs when the denominator reaches 0:

$$R_{\theta,eff} \ge \frac{1}{2I^2R_{ds,25}\alpha} = \frac{1}{2\cdot 6400\cdot 10^{-3}\cdot 0.006} = 13.0\ \text{K/W}$$

The per-motor effective thermal resistance to ambient must therefore be well below 13 K/W for any steady 80 A operation. Section 3 shows a 20×20 board cannot achieve this.
