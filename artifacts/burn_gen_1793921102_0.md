## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

Two issues in the brief need correcting before any math is valid.

**1. The STM32H743 cannot be the ESC controller.** ArduPilot runs on the H743 as the flight controller. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, because commutation and back-EMF zero-cross detection must run in hard real time. A correct architecture is:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──Bidir DShot600 ×4──► 4× AT32F421 (AM32) ──► 4× 3-phase bridges
```

**2. "80A" on a nano board must be a burst rating, not continuous.** The math below proves this.

**Design basis (all values representative; verify against the actual datasheets):**

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 100 °C:

$$R_{ds} = 1.2\,[1 + 0.0055 \times 75] = 1.695 \approx 1.7\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Bridge current path

With 6-step trapezoidal commutation and synchronous (complementary) PWM, phase current always flows through exactly two conducting devices:

- During the ON interval: high-side of phase A and low-side of phase B.
- During the OFF interval: the complementary low-side of phase A (synchronous freewheel) and low-side of phase B.

The two-device path therefore holds for the whole PWM cycle, independent of duty D.

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.7\times10^{-3} = 21.8\ \text{W per ESC}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Over one electrical cycle each FET is active for 1/3 of the time:

- **High-side:** carries current for fraction D of its active third.
- **Low-side:** carries current for the whole active third plus the (1−D) freewheel fraction of the adjacent phase.

Worst case is the low-side at low duty (D → 0), which approaches 2/3 conduction.

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I^2 R_{ds}, \qquad P_{LS,max} \approx \tfrac{2}{3}\,I^2 R_{ds}$$

At 80 A with D ≈ 0.85 (bursts occur at high throttle):

| Device | Power |
|---|---|
| High-side FET | 3.1 W |
| Low-side FET | 4.2 W |

Peak instantaneous dissipation per FET is I²R = 10.9 W, which drives the transient Z_th check in §4.3.

### 1.4 Copper and shunt losses

| Element | Value | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase trace + pad (2 oz, approx.) | 0.25 mΩ | 2 × 80² × 0.25 m = 3.2 W |
| Bus shunt (if used) | 0.25 mΩ | (D·I)² R = 1.2 W |
| **Copper subtotal** | — | **≈ 4.4 W** |

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In 6-step PWM, one leg hard-switches per PWM period. Each loss term is written per ESC.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard switching)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

$$= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 1.94\ \text{W}$$

The transition time follows from the gate-driver current and the switching charge (Q_gs2 + Q_gd ≈ 8 nC):

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{drv}}, \qquad I_{drv} \approx \frac{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}{R_g+R_{drv}}$$

With a FD6288Q-class driver (~1.2 A source / 1.5 A sink) and R_g = 4.7 Ω, each edge takes about 15–25 ns. That makes the 40 ns total a realistic figure.

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 40\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

Q_rr rises with di/dt and temperature. Budget 2–3× this value, i.e. about 0.15 W.

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = 2 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss,er} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1.2\text{n} \times 25.2^2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.037\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in the driver and R_g, not in the FET channel)

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g\,V_{gs}\,f_{sw} = 2 \times 35\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.034\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Switching total

$$P_{sw,ESC} \approx 1.94 + 0.61 + 0.15 + 0.04 + 0.03 \approx \mathbf{2.8\ W}$$

**Frequency sensitivity:** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw.

| f_sw | P_sw per ESC |
|---|---|
| 24 kHz (AM32 default region) | 1.4 W |
| 48 kHz | 2.8 W |

At 80 A, 48 kHz costs about 1.4 W per ESC
