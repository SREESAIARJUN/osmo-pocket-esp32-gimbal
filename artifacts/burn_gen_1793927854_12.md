# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC: Electrical, Thermal and DShot Design Model

**Job #10, Rev A (engineering baseline)**

I don't have prior context for Job #10, so the assumptions are stated explicitly below. Every number is parameterized so you can substitute your actual BOM.

## 0. Architecture Corrections

Two issues affect everything downstream.

**1. The H743 cannot be the ESC controller.**
- The H743 has only two advanced timers with complementary outputs (TIM1, TIM8), and ArduPilot does not run BLDC commutation firmware.
- Bidirectional DShot also implies a separate ESC MCU that answers with eRPM telemetry.
- **Correct architecture:** H743 running ArduPilot, sending DShot600 to 4× ESC MCUs (e.g., AT32F421 running AM32). AM32 natively supports 48 kHz PWM, which matches your spec.

**2. "Nano" vs. 80A burst.**
- On a 20×20 mm board, 80A/channel burst is not thermally credible beyond about 1 s (see §3).
- On a 30.5×30.5 mm board with 5×6 mm PQFN FETs, it is credible for roughly 2–5 s.
- **This document assumes the 30.5×30.5 form factor.** The 20×20 numbers are shown for comparison.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

With 6-step commutation and synchronous rectification, at any instant:
- One leg is statically ON (low side).
- One leg is PWM'd. Its high side conducts for D, its low side for (1−D).
- One leg floats.

The PWM'd leg therefore always contributes exactly one conducting FET, plus the static FET. The result is independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot \big(R_{ds,HS}\,D + R_{ds,LS}(1-D)\big) + I_{rms}^2 R_{ds,LS} \;\approx\; 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\big], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds ≈ 1.2 mΩ × 1.5 = **1.8 mΩ** (worst-case max part).

Because R_ds rises with temperature, loss rises with temperature. This creates a positive-feedback loop, which is solved in §3.3.

### 1.3 Ripple correction

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{avg}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I \approx \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5" motor (L_ph-ph ≈ 20 µH, so per-path L ≈ 20 µH) at 48 kHz with D = 0.9 and V_bus − V_bemf ≈ 4 V:

ΔI ≈ 4 × 0.9 / (20e-6 × 48e3) ≈ 3.75 A

The ripple contribution is negligible (≈ 1.2 A² against 6400 A²). This is a key benefit of 48 kHz over 24 kHz.
