## 0. Executive Summary and Architectural Corrections

Three facts shape everything below:

1. **The STM32H743 does not generate the 48 kHz bridge PWM.** In any ArduPilot-compatible 4-in-1 design, the H743 sends DShot600 to four (or one quad) ESC MCUs. These are typically AT32F421, STM32G071 or EFM8BB51 parts running AM32 or BLHeli. The ESC MCUs generate the 48 kHz PWM and return eRPM telemetry. So the model is split into an ESC power domain (Sections 1–3) and an FC timing domain (Section 4).
2. **80 A per motor on a nano-class board is a burst rating measured in seconds, not a continuous rating.** The model predicts about **145 W of total dissipation** at 80 A on all four motors. An adiabatic temperature ramp of about **24 K/s** limits a safe burst to roughly **2–3 s**. Realistic continuous capability is about **20–25 A per motor**. This matches what commercial 20×20 / 25.5×25.5 4-in-1 boards actually sustain.
3. **Conduction loss dominates switching loss by about 15:1 at 48 kHz.** Raising the PWM frequency is not the thermal problem; Rds(on) and copper are.

## 2. Loss Model (per motor)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss: 6-Step Trapezoidal with Synchronous Rectification

During each 60° commutation sector, two phases carry current I:

- **Phase A (PWM leg):** the high-side conducts for D·T_sw and its complementary low-side for (1 − D)·T_sw.
- **Phase B:** the low-side is held on continuously.

The instantaneous bridge conduction loss is therefore:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

The result is **independent of duty cycle**. This is the key insight: throttle position does not reduce FET conduction loss at a given phase current.

The temperature-corrected resistance is:

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.5 × 1.45 = **2.18 mΩ**.

$$
P_{cond,FET} = 2(80)^2(2.18\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{27.9\ W}
$$

Copper loss follows the same two-leg path:

$$
P_{cu} = 2 I^2 R_{cu} = 2(6400)(0.5\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{6.4\ W}
$$

**Ripple correction.** Phase current ripple adds I_rms² = I_dc² + ΔI²/12. A low-inductance quad motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH) at D = 0.5 gives:

$$
\Delta I = \frac{V(1-D)D}{L f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2(0.25)}{15\mu H \cdot 48k} \approx 8.75\ A
$$

This adds only about 0.1% to the result and is negligible.
