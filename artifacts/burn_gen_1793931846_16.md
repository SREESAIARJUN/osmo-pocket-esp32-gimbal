# STM32H743 Nano AIO (20×20) with 4-in-1 ESC: Job #39 Systems Architecture

## 0. Architectural Assumptions

Your brief combines two subsystems, so I fixed the architecture before deriving anything:

| Item | Assumption | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller MCU | STM32H743VIH6 (TFBGA100, 7×7 mm) running ArduPilot | LQFP100 (14×14) does not fit a 20×20 board alongside the IMU and baro. |
| ESC MCUs | 4× AT32F421K8U7 (QFN32) running AM32, one per channel | ArduPilot is not ESC firmware. A 48 kHz PWM frequency is an AM32 parameter. A single-H743 direct-drive design is a different architecture. |
| Link | Bi-directional DShot600, H743 → AT32 ×4 | |
| Battery | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V full charge (V<sub>bus</sub>) | |
| MOSFET | 40 V N-ch, PDFN3.3×3.3 class. R<sub>DS(on)</sub> = 1.5 mΩ typ at V<sub>GS</sub> = 10 V, 25 °C. Q<sub>g</sub> ≈ 50 nC. Q<sub>rr</sub> ≈ 40 nC | Largest package that fits 6 FETs per channel at 20×20 pitch. |
| Gate driver | 3-phase half-bridge driver (FD6288Q class), with t<sub>r</sub> + t<sub>f</sub> ≈ 40 ns and dead time 200 ns | |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal, synchronous (complementary) rectification | AM32 default. |

**The math below shows that 80 A on a 20×20 board is only survivable as a burst lasting a few seconds.** The model quantifies exactly how long.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

This gives about 1.6× at 125 °C, consistent with typical datasheet normalization curves.

| T<sub>j</sub> | R<sub>DS</sub> |
|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.50 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 2.18 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 2.40 mΩ |

### 1.2 Six-step conduction topology

In any 60° sector, current flows through one high-side leg and one low-side leg:

- During PWM-on, the path is HS(x) → motor → LS(y).
- During PWM-off with synchronous rectification, the path is LS(x) → motor → LS(y).

In either state, exactly two channels carry the full phase current I. Per ESC channel:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) + I^2 R_{Cu}$$

Here R<sub>Cu</sub> is the PCB copper, pad, and shunt path. For 2 oz inner/outer copper, short pours, and a 0.5 mΩ shunt (current-sense resistor), I take R<sub>Cu</sub> ≈ 0.5 mΩ, with the shunt itself included.

Per-FET RMS split, for current I and duty D:

- **Switching-leg high side:** I²·R·D·(1/3)
- **Switching-leg low side:** I²·R·(1−D)·(1/3)
- **Sink low side:** I²·R·(1/3)

Averaged over an electrical revolution, the hottest FETs are the low sides at low duty and the high sides at high duty. The **single-FET worst case** is therefore ≈ I²R·(2/3) at D → 1 for high-side conduction plus its own low-side sink third. Bounding it: **P<sub>FET,max</sub> ≈ (2/3)·I²·R<sub>DS</sub>**.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A burst (T<sub>j</sub> = 125 °C)

$$P_{cond,FET} = 2(80)^2(2.4\text{ m}\Omega) = 30.7\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{Cu} = (80)^2(0.5\text{ m}\Omega) = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{FET,max} \approx \tfrac{2}{3}(6400)(0.0024) = 10.2\ \text{W per FET}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg is PWM-switched at a time in 6-step operation.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus}\, I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw} = \tfrac12(25.2)(80)(40\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz}) = 1.94\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two transitions per PWM period:

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\,(2t_d)\, f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(400\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz}) = 1.23\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{ nC}(25.2)(48\text{ kHz}) = 0.05\ \text{W}$$

For hard reverse recovery, add the Q<sub>rr</sub>-induced HS turn-on loss, roughly another 0.1 W.
