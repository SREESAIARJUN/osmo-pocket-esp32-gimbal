# STM32H743 AIO "Nano" FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model (Job #60)

**Scope:** 80 A burst conduction losses, 48 kHz switching losses, thermal model, ArduPilot bi-directional DShot600 timer/DMA allocation, and reference netlists.

---

## 0. Findings That Change the Design

These results come out of the math below and should be resolved before layout.

1. **The H743 cannot run the motors.** In every viable 4-in-1 design, each ESC channel (or pair of channels) has its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. This document uses an AT32F421 per channel. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot to those MCUs. The architecture below reflects this.
2. **"80 A burst" on a 20×20 mm board lasts about 1–2 seconds, not 10.** At 80 A per channel the stack dissipates about 80 W into roughly 2 J/K of thermal mass. That is a temperature rise of about 40 K/s. Continuous capability on 20×20 with prop-wash cooling is about 12–18 A per channel.
3. **Recommendation:** Either specify the rating as "80 A, ≤1 s, per channel, single channel" (marketing-burst style), or move to 30.5×30.5 mm with 5×6 mm FETs and a heatsink pad. Section 3.6 gives the derating table.
4. **Bi-directional DShot pins must sit on full timer channels (CH1–CH4), not complementary CHxN outputs.** Put all four motors on one advanced timer to minimise DMA streams.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current path

The motor is driven with 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation and complementary PWM. In any sector, current flows through two switch positions:

- **The PWM leg:** the high-side FET carries current for a fraction D of each period. Its synchronous low-side partner carries it for (1 − D).
- **The return leg:** the low-side FET is held on for the whole sector.

Neglecting dead time, the current I therefore always flows through exactly two FET channels in series.

### 2.2 Per-channel conduction power

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2 R_{ds}(T_j) \quad\text{where}\quad R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right],\ \ \alpha \approx 0.0053\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

Over an electrical revolution, each phase conducts 2/3 of the time. Six FETs share the loss, so the average loss per FET is P_cond / 3. Within a single PWM period, however, one FET can carry the full I²R_ds. Use that worst case for junction-temperature checks.

### 2.3 Numbers at 80 A

| Quantity | 25 °C | 110 °C |
|---|---|---|
| R_ds | 1.00 mΩ | 1.45 mΩ |
| P_cond per channel | 12.8 W | **18.6 W** |
| Plus PCB copper (≈0.3 mΩ path) | +1.9 W | +2.4 W |
| Plus 0.25 mΩ shunt share (total ÷ 4) | — | +0.4 W |
| **Channel conduction total** | 14.7 W | **≈21.4 W** |

Rds rises with temperature, which raises loss, which raises temperature. This positive feedback is closed by iteration in Section 3.4.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches hard. Its synchronous partner switches with near-zero voltage (ZVS) after the dead time.

### 3.1 Overlap (V–I crossover) loss

$$P_{ov} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 20\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 3.2 Output-capacitance loss

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

### 3.3 Dead-time diode conduction and reverse recovery

$$P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 100\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.31\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 3.4 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated in the FD6288 and gate resistors, not in the FETs.

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 40\text{n} \cdot 10 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.038\ \text{W}$$
