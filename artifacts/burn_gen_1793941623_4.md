## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in one commutation step

During any 60° sector of 6-step commutation:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side conducts for duty D. The low-side synchronously rectifies for (1−D).
- **Static leg:** the low-side is on 100%.
- **Floating leg:** off. Back-EMF zero-cross is sensed here.

The phase current I flows through exactly one PWM-leg device and the static-leg low side at every instant. This gives the conduction loss per motor channel:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{hot}\left[D + (1-D)\right] + I^2 R_{hot} = 2\,I^2 R_{hot}
$$

The result is **duty-independent**, which is a useful property of synchronous rectification. Body-diode conduction occurs only during dead time and is handled in §1.3.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence of R_DS(on)

$$
R_{hot} = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_J - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{/K} \text{ [P]}
$$

At T_J = 125 °C, R_hot = 1.5 mΩ.

You must also add the PCB copper path. For 2 oz copper, ρ = 1.72×10⁻⁸ Ω·m at 20 °C and the thickness is 70 µm:

$$
R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho\,L}{w\,t}\left[1+0.0039(T-20)\right]
$$

With L = 15 mm, w = 6 mm, and 2 layers in parallel, R_Cu ≈ 0.31 mΩ at 20 °C and about 0.40 mΩ at 100 °C. This applies per leg and is counted twice in the path.
