# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #32): System Architecture Model

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Up-Front Findings

**Architecture assumption.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller. Each ESC channel runs on its own MCU (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). The H743 does not commutate the motors. It sends bidirectional DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs.

**Electrical and package assumptions** (replace these with your actual BOM datasheet values):

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S, full charge) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| MOSFET (40 V, PDFN 3.3×3.3) R_DS(on) at 25 °C | R₂₅ | 1.1 mΩ typ |
| R_DS(on) at T_j ≈ 100 °C | R_hot | 1.54 mΩ (×1.4) |
| Total switching transition time | t_r + t_f | 40 ns |
| Gate charge / Miller charge | Q_g / Q_gd | 60 nC / 10 nC |
| Body diode reverse recovery / forward voltage | Q_rr / V_f | 50 nC / 0.8 V |
| Dead time (each edge) | t_dt | 50 ns |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| Junction-to-case thermal resistance | R_θJC | 1.5 K/W |

> ⚠️ **Key engineering findings. Read these before layout.**
> 1. **4 × 80 A simultaneous is 320 A from the pack.** No XT60, nano pad, or 20×20 / 30×30 copper stack supports this continuously. Treat "80 A burst" as a **per-channel, sub-2-second rating**.
> 2. The model below shows that **board thermal mass, not junction resistance, is the limiting factor.** Sustained output converges to roughly **20 A per channel continuous**, which matches real nano ESCs on the market.
> 3. The netlists in §5 are **reference topology**. They are not fab-ready. They must be validated in ECAD (ERC/DRC) against the real part numbers.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0053\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

$$R_{DS}(100^\circ C) \approx 1.1 \times 1.4 = 1.54\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Current path in six-step commutation with synchronous rectification

At any instant, the phase current flows through **two FETs**:
- During the PWM on-time, the path is the high-side FET of phase A plus the low-side FET of phase B.
- During the off-time, the path is the low-side FET of phase A (synchronous freewheel) plus the low-side FET of phase B.

Either way, there are always two R_DS(on) drops in series:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j) \cdot k_{ripple}$$

The ripple RMS correction is

$$k_{ripple} = 1 + \frac{1}{12}\left(\frac{\Delta I}{I}\right)^2$$

With motor inductance of roughly 10 µH, ΔI/I is about 0.15. This gives k ≈ 1.002, which is negligible.

$$P_{cond,ch} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.54\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.7\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each FET conducts for 120° electrical. The die thermal time constant (around 1–5 ms) is longer than the electrical period. For example, at 30 k RPM with 14 poles, f_e = 3.5 kHz, so the die sees the average:

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{19.7}{6} \cdot \text{(LS FETs carry more due to freewheel share)} \approx 3.0\text{–}4.0\ \text{W}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the high-side FET in the PWM leg hard-switches. The low-side FET switches with near-zero voltage after dead time.

### 2.1 V-I overlap loss

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

The transition-time estimate is checked against the gate drive.
