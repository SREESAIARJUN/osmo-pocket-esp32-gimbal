## 0. Architectural Corrections

These points change the design, so they come first.

1. **The STM32H743 should not commutate the motors.** Running ArduPilot and driving 12 half-bridges at 48 kHz with sensorless BEMF on one core is not a viable design. The standard architecture, assumed here, is:
   - **STM32H743VIT6** runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 commands.
   - **4× AT32F421K8U7** (or STM32G071) run AM32 firmware, one per motor. Each generates 48 kHz complementary PWM and replies with eRPM telemetry.

2. **The 80 A rating is a per-motor, single-motor burst figure.** As shown in §3, four motors at 80 A simultaneously dissipate about 90 W. On a nano-class board, that saturates thermally in about 2–3 s, and a 3.3×3.3 mm package is usually limited by bond wires and clip current, not by R_DS(on). Specify the product as **"80 A burst (≤1 s, single channel), 45–55 A continuous per channel with airflow."**

3. **Total battery current must be bounded separately.** 4 × 80 A = 320 A exceeds any XT60 or XT90 connector and the nano-board power pour. Size the input path to a defined aggregate, for example 180 A burst.

## 2. Loss Model per Motor Channel

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

With complementary PWM, current always flows through exactly two FETs:
- During the on-time: the high-side FET of phase A and the low-side FET of phase B.
- During the off-time: the low-side FET of phase A (synchronous freewheel) and the low-side FET of phase B.

The sum is therefore independent of duty cycle D:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I^2\, R_{DS}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

Per-FET distribution:
- High side: D·I²R
- Freewheel low side: (1−D)·I²R
- Opposite low side: I²R, and this FET dominates

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond (2 FETs, 80 A) | Worst single FET (opposite LS) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W | 6.4 W |
| 100 °C | 1.34 mΩ | 17.1 W | 8.6 W |
| 125 °C | 1.45 mΩ | 18.6 W | 9.3 W |

Commutation rotates the "opposite low side" role across all three low-side FETs over 120° electrical intervals. The time-averaged dissipation is therefore about one third of the instantaneous worst case. However, at low eRPM the instantaneous value governs the junction temperature, because an electrical period is comparable to the FET die thermal time constant (~1–10 ms).

### 2.2 Electrothermal Fixed Point and Runaway Criterion

For one FET with junction-to-reference thermal impedance R_th:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + R_{th}\, I^2 R_{DS25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Solving for T_j:

$$T_j - 25 = \frac{(T_{ref}-25) + R_{th} I^2 R_{DS25}}{1-\alpha R_{th} I^2 R_{DS25}}$$

**Stability requires** α · R_th · I² · R_DS25 < 1.

At 80 A, with R_DS25 = 1 mΩ and α = 0.0045, this gives R_th < 34.7 K/W. That is easily met at the FET level, so there is no single-FET runaway. The binding constraint is the board's heat capacity (§3).

### 2.3 Switching Loss at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing high-side FET hard-switches. The synchronous low-side FET switches at zero voltage, apart from the dead-time diode interval.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw | ½ · 25.2 · 80 · 20 ns · 48 k = **0.97 W** |
| C_oss | ½ · C_oss · V² · f_sw (per switching node) | ½ · 1.5 nF · 635 · 48 k = **0.023 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw | 50 nC · 25.2 · 48 k = **0.060 W** |
| Dead-time body diode | V_SD · I · 2t_dt · f_sw | 0.85 · 80 · 400 ns · 48 k = **1.31 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs, mostly in driver IC) | 2 · Q_g · V_drv · f_sw | 2 · 60 n · 10 · 48 k = **0.058 W** |
| **Total switching-related** | | **≈ 2.42 W** |

Two design notes follow from this table:
- **Dead time is the largest switching term.** Cutting t_dt from 200 ns to 100 ns saves 0.65 W per channel. Verify shoot-through margin against the FD6288Q propagation mismatch, which is about 50 ns maximum.
- **Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw.** At 24 kHz, the total drops to about 1.2 W, but current ripple doubles and motor copper loss rises. 48 kHz is a reasonable choice for low-inductance FPV motors.

### 2.4 Copper, Sense, and Capacitor Losses

**PCB phase path.** Each phase has about 0.4 mΩ effective resistance (8 layers of 2 oz in parallel, ~10 mm path with via transitions):

P = 2 · 80² · 0.4 mΩ = **5.1 W**

This is significant, so copper is a loss component, not just a heat spreader.

**Shunt (if present).** A 0.25 mΩ shared shunt on the battery return gives:

P = I_bat² · R

This is dominated by the aggregate current and is accounted for in §3.

**Bulk capacitor ripple.** Ripple current per ESC is:

I_C,rms ≈ I · √(D(1−D)) = 40 A at D = 0.5
