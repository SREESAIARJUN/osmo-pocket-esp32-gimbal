## 0. Architectural Correction Up Front

The H743 should **not** drive the bridges directly. The motor-control loop (6-step commutation, BEMF zero-crossing at up to ~200 k eRPM) runs on dedicated ESC MCUs. DShot is the link *between* the FC and those MCUs.

**Partitioning**

| Block | Device (reference) | Role |
|---|---|---|
| FC MCU | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100) | ArduPilot, bi-directional DShot600 master |
| ESC MCU ×4 | AT32F421K8U7 (QFN32) or STM32G071 | AM32 firmware, 48 kHz PWM, BEMF sensing, eRPM telemetry |
| Gate driver ×4 | Fortior FD6288Q (3-phase, bootstrap) | Half-bridge drive, 4.5–20 V VCC |
| MOSFETs ×24 | 60 V, ~1.4 mΩ, PDFN 5×6 (6S-capable) | Power stage |

**Feasibility flags. Read these before committing to "nano" + "80 A".**

1. **Total current.** 4 × 80 A = **320 A** of simultaneous burst. That exceeds XT60 (≈60 A continuous) and XT90 (≈90 A continuous) ratings. It also needs a pack rated ≥ 320 A. Industry "80 A burst" ratings are per-channel and assume *non-simultaneous*, short (≤10 s) bursts with propwash airflow.
2. **Form factor.** A 20×20 mm "nano" footprint cannot physically hold 24 × 5×6 mm FETs (24 × 30 mm² = 720 mm² of a 400 mm² board). The minimum realistic footprint is **30.5×30.5 mm**, or a stacked FC + ESC. §3 shows that even 30.5 mm is thermally burst-only at 80 A.

- the PWM leg (HS during on-time, LS during off-time), and
