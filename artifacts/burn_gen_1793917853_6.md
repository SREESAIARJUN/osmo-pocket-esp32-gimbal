# 3-Axis Gimbal: FOC Control Loop and Dual-IMU Sensor Fusion Design

## 0. Assumptions

I don't have the Job #18 specification, so this design uses typical brushless-gimbal values. Every value below is a named constant in the code. Replace them with your hardware's figures.

| Item | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| MCU | STM32G4 / H7 class, single-precision FPU, about 170 MHz |
| Motor | 12N14P gimbal BLDC (pole pairs p = 7), R ≈ 5–15 Ω, L ≈ 2–5 mH |
| Rotor sensor | 14-bit absolute magnetic encoder (MT6701 / AS5047P) |
| Current sensing | 2-shunt, center-aligned PWM sampling |
| IMUs | 2× ICM-42688-P on the camera plate, SPI, ODR 8 kHz, gyro ±2000 dps |
| Loop rates | Current 20 kHz → Velocity 4 kHz → Angle 1 kHz |
| Bandwidth targets | Current ≈ 1.5 kHz, Velocity ≈ 150 Hz, Angle ≈ 20–30 Hz |

**Key architectural point.** For stabilization, the inner velocity loop is closed on the **camera-frame gyro rate** projected onto each joint axis, not on the encoder. The outer angle loop is closed on the fused **camera attitude**. The encoder is used for:
- electrical commutation angle θₑ,
- the cogging-compensation phase,
- joint-limit handling,
- velocity damping and backup feedback.

## 1. FOC Current Loop (20 kHz)

### 1.1 Transforms (amplitude-invariant)

**Clarke transform.** With 2-shunt sensing, i_c = −i_a − i_b:

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \frac{1}{\sqrt3}(i_a + 2 i_b)
$$

**Park transform.** θₑ = p·θₘ − θ_offset:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d \\ i_q \end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e \\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha \\ i_\beta \end{bmatrix}
$$

**Inverse Park transform:**

$$
v_\alpha = v_d\cos\theta_e - v_q\sin\theta_e,\qquad v_\beta = v_d\sin\theta_e + v_q\cos\theta_e
$$

### 1.2 Plant model (surface PMSM, L_d = L_q = L)

$$
v_d = R i_d + L\dot i_d - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad
v_q = R i_q + L\dot i_q + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e\lambda_m
$$

The electromagnetic torque is:

$$
\tau_e = \tfrac32 p\lambda_m i_q = K_t i_q
$$

### 1.3 Current PI controller with pole–zero cancellation

After feedforward decoupling, each axis reduces to G(s) = 1/(Ls + R). Choose the PI controller

$$
C(s) = K_p + \frac{K_i}{s},\qquad K_p = L\,\omega_{c},\quad K_i = R\,\omega_{c}
$$

With these gains the PI zero cancels the electrical pole, giving L(s) = ω_c/s and a closed loop of ω_c/(s+ω_c).

**Rule of thumb for the bandwidth:** ω_c ≤ 2π·f_pwm/10 to 2π·f_pwm/20, because the loop has about 1.5 sample periods of delay (computation plus ZOH).

The decoupled voltage commands are:

$$
v_d^* = \text{PI}_d(i_d^* - i_d) - \hat\omega_e \hat L i_q,\qquad
v_q^* = \text{PI}_q(i_q^* - i_q) + \hat\omega_e(\hat L i_d + \hat\lambda_m)
$$

**Delay compensation.** Rotate the inverse-Park angle forward by θₑ + 1.5·ωₑ·T_s.

**Voltage limit.** Apply a circular limit |v_dq| ≤ V_dc/√3, with d-axis priority:

$$
v_d^{sat} = \text{clamp}(v_d^*, \pm V_{max}),\qquad
v_q^{sat} = \text{clamp}\left(v_q^*, \pm\sqrt{V_{max}^2 - (v_d^{sat})^2}\right)
$$

### 1.4 SVPWM (min–max zero-sequence injection)

$$
v_{a,b,c} = \text{invClarke}(v_\alpha, v_\beta),\quad
v_0 = -\tfrac12\left(\max(v_{abc}) + \min(v_{abc})\right),\quad
d_x = \tfrac12 + \frac{v_x + v_0}{V_{dc}}
$$
