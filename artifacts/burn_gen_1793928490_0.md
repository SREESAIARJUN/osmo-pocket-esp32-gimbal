**Architecture note.** The H743 does not drive the power stage directly. It sends bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs (for example AT32F421 running AM32), and those MCUs generate the 48 kHz PWM. The thermal and switching model therefore applies to the ESC board, and the DMA model applies to the FC.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

| T_j | R_DS(on) |
|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.20 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 1.74 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 1.92 mΩ |

### 1.2 Six-step synchronous conduction

During any commutation step, current flows through two legs:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for D·T. The synchronous low-side FET conducts for (1−D)·T, minus dead time.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET conducts 100% of the step.

Because one of the two PWM-leg FETs is always on, the conduction loss is independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

At 80 A:

| Case | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Cold (25 °C) | 2 · 6400 · 1.20 mΩ | 15.4 W |
| Hot (125 °C) | 2 · 6400 · 1.92 mΩ | **24.6 W per channel** |

**Per-FET average.** Each of the 6 FETs carries current for about 1/3 of the electrical cycle, so the average is 24.6 / 6 ≈ 4.1 W per FET. The electrical period at typical eRPM (2–10 ms) is short compared with the package thermal time constant, so the average is valid. Add about 10% for current ripple, using I_rms² = I² + ΔI²/12.

### 1.3 Parasitic copper and shunt losses

| Element | Assumed resistance | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copper path (pads, planes, vias) | 0.3 mΩ | 80² · 0.3 mΩ | 1.9 W |
| Current shunt (if used per channel) | 0.2 mΩ | 80² · 0.2 mΩ | 1.3 W |

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap (high side)

$$t_{r} \approx t_{f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g}} \approx \frac{10\text{ nC}}{0.8\text{ A}} \approx 12\text{ ns}$$

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 24\text{ ns} \cdot 48\text{ kHz} = 1.16\text{ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction (low side, two edges)

$$P_{dt} = V_F\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\text{ ns} \cdot 48\text{ kHz} = 0.61\text{ W}$$

This term is significant. Trimming dead time to about 50 ns halves it, but only if the driver propagation delay is matched.

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{ nC} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{ kHz} = 0.048\text{ W}$$

Q_rr also adds turn-on current overshoot, which effectively raises P_VI by about 10–20%.

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.03\text{ W}$$
