# STM32H743 Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Nano Format): Design Package, Job #53

## 0. Architecture and Feasibility Constraints

Two constraints shape the rest of this package.

**1. The H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC commutation MCU.** A 4-in-1 ESC runs commutation on dedicated MCUs, typically one per channel with AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware on a Cortex-M0+/M4 (AT32F421 or STM32G071 class). The H743 talks to them over DShot. Running four sensorless FOC/6-step loops at 48 kHz on the H743 alongside ArduPilot is possible in principle, but no maintained firmware supports it. This package therefore treats the system as:

```
[H743 FC] --bidir DShot600 x4--> [4x ESC MCU] --> [4x 3-phase gate driver] --> [4x 6-FET bridge]
```

**2. 80 A per channel on a nano (20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm) board is a transient rating, not a continuous one.** The model below quantifies this. The conclusion, stated up front:
- 80 A is survivable for roughly 1–3 s bursts with airflow.
- Continuous capability on this footprint is about 25–35 A per channel.
- Any marketing figure above that needs the thermal derivation below to back it.

All component values are representative engineering assumptions. Replace them with your selected parts' datasheet values (marked ⚙) before sign-off.

  R_DS(T_j) = R_25 · [1 + α(T_j − 25)], with α ≈ 0.006 /K, giving 1.6× at 125 °C.

This feedback term is what turns a long burst into thermal runaway. It is closed in §4.4.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

**Commutation scheme.** The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous (complementary) PWM, which is AM32's default:
- During any 60° sector, one leg is PWM'd: high-side on for duty D, low-side synchronously rectifying for 1−D.
- A second leg's low-side is held fully on.
- The third leg floats.

Phase current flows through exactly two conducting FET channels at all times (ignoring dead time):

  P_cond = I_ph² · [D·R + (1−D)·R] + I_ph² · R = 2 · I_ph² · R_DS(T_j)

Duty cancels out. Under synchronous rectification, conduction loss is independent of throttle at a fixed phase current.

At 80 A and T_j = 125 °C:

  P_cond = 2 × 80² × 1.6 mΩ = **20.5 W per channel**

Per-FET distribution, averaged over 6 sectors:
- Each FET position carries current for 2/6 of the electrical cycle on average.
- Average per FET ≈ 20.5 / 6 × 2 ≈ **6.8 W**.
- Peak instantaneous per FET is about 10.2 W while it is the static low-side conductor.

**Copper / PCB loss.** This term is frequently ignored and should not be. For a 2 oz (70 µm) outer layer plus 2 oz inner layer, a 4 mm wide, 15 mm long phase path:

  R_Cu = ρL / (wt) = (1.72×10⁻⁸ × 0.015) / (0.004 × 140×10⁻⁶) ≈ 0.46 mΩ (before the +39% rise at 125 °C) → ≈ 0.64 mΩ hot

  P_Cu ≈ 2 × 80² × 0.64 mΩ ≈ **8.2 W per channel**

This term rivals the FET loss. Mitigations:
- Use 3 oz copper on 4–6 layers with stitched parallel planes.
- Solder motor pads directly at the bridge.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Hard-switched half bridge with inductive load. Per channel, only the PWM'd leg switches.

**3.1 V–I overlap loss**

  P_VI = ½ · V_bus · I_ph · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 20 ns × 48 kHz = **0.968 W**

**3.2 Output capacitance (C_oss)**

  P_oss = Q_oss · V_bus · f_sw = 40 nC × 25.2 × 48k = **0.048 W**

**3.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction**

  P_dt = V_F · I_ph · 2t_dt · f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48k = **0.614 W**

Tightening dead time to 50 ns halves this. The lower limit is the driver's propagation-delay mismatch plus margin.

**3.4 Reverse recovery**

  P_rr = Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw = 50 nC × 25.2 × 48k = **0.060 W**

Q_rr scales with dI/dt and temperature. Apply a 2× derate when hot, giving 0.12 W.

**3.5 Gate drive (dissipated in driver and gate resistors, not in the FET die)**

  P_g = 2 · Q_g · V_GS · f_sw = 2 × 50 nC × 10 × 48k = **0.048 W**

**3.6 Switching total per channel**

  P_sw ≈ 0.968 + 0.048 + 0.614 + 0.12 + 0.048 ≈ **1.80 W**

**Observation:** at 48 kHz with fast FETs, switching loss is under 10% of conduction loss. Dropping to 24 kHz saves only about 0.9 W per channel but increases current ripple and acoustic noise. 48 kHz is justified; R_DS(on) and copper dominate.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Total burst dissipation

| Term | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (hot) | 20.5 W | 82.0 W |
| PCB copper (hot) | 8.2 W | 32.8 W |
| Switching | 1.8 W | 7.2 W |
| Bus caps ESR (see §4.5) | ~0.5 W | 2.0 W |
| **Total** | **31.0 W** | **124 W** |

This assumes all four motors at 80 A simultaneously. Battery current is then I_bat ≈ Σ D·I_ph, up to roughly 300 A at full duty. That is also a constraint on the XT60/XT90, the bus wiring, and the pack C-rating.

### 4.2 Junction-level steady-state model

Network: junction → case → PCB → ambient (airflow). Representative values for a 3.3×3.3 mm PQFN:

| Element | Value |
|---|---|
| R_θJC (bottom pad) ⚙ | 1.5 K/W |
| R_θ(case→board), solder + via array (≥ 9 vias, 0.3 mm, filled) | 3 K/W |
| R_θ(board→air) for the whole board, prop wash ~5 m/s, 25×25 mm, both sides | ≈ 4 K/W |
| R_θ(board→air), still air | ≈ 20 K/W |

Junction temperature rise:

  ΔT_j = P_FET · (R_θJC + R_θCB) + P_board_total · R_θBA

  ΔT_j = 6.8 × 4.5 + 124 × 4 ≈ 31 + 496 ≈ **527 K**

At 80 A, steady state is impossible by a factor of roughly 5. The only question is how long the burst can last.
