# Job #31: STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (80A Burst) Engineering Model

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Corrections

The STM32H743 cannot run the ESC commutation loops itself. In a working "4-in-1 ESC nano flight controller", the H743 is the flight controller, and four dedicated ESC MCUs run AM32 or BLHeli_32 on the same PCB stack. This model assumes that split.

| Block | Assumed part | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| FC MCU | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100), ArduPilot | Sufficient timers and DMAMUX |
| ESC MCU (×4) | AT32F421K8U7 (QFN32), AM32 firmware | Supports bidirectional DShot and 48 kHz PWM |
| Gate driver (×4) | FD6288Q (3-phase, 250 V, bootstrap) | Common in 4-in-1 designs |
| MOSFETs (×24) | 40 V, R_DS(on),25 = 1.0 mΩ @ V_GS = 10 V, PDFN 5×6 | 3×3 parts cannot survive 80A (see §3) |
| Bus | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max | Worst case |
| Form factor | 30.5×30.5 mm mount, 6-layer, 2 oz outer / 2 oz inner | **A 20×20 "nano" layout cannot dissipate this power; see §3.4** |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step (trapezoidal) drive with synchronous rectification

At any instant, two of three phase legs are active:

- **PWM leg:** HS conducts for duty D. The LS (synchronous rectification) conducts for 1−D, minus dead time.
- **Return leg:** LS conducts continuously during that 60° sector.

At every instant, therefore, exactly **two MOSFETs** carry the phase current I.

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{rms}^2 \, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \mathrm{K^{-1}}
$$

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond per channel @ 80A |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W |
| 100 °C | 1.45 mΩ | 18.6 W |
| 125 °C | 1.60 mΩ | 20.5 W |

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each FET conducts during roughly one-third of the electrical cycle, either as HS or as LS. Averaged over a revolution, per FET:

$$
P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6}\cdot 2 \;\;\text{(peak-sector weighting)} \Rightarrow \approx 3.4\text{–}6.8\ \mathrm{W}
$$

Use **6.8 W** for the junction calculation. It is the worst-sector power, because at low eRPM the sector dwell time is comparable to the FET's thermal time constant.

### 1.4 Parasitic copper and shunt

- **Shunt:** 0.2 mΩ. P = 80² × 0.2 mΩ = **1.28 W per channel**.
- **PCB copper:** Phase trace plus pours, about 0.3 mΩ round trip. P ≈ **1.9 W per channel**.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches, so 1 HS/LS pair switches per channel.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard switching)

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}
= 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 20\,\mathrm{ns} \cdot 48\,\mathrm{kHz} = 0.97\ \mathrm{W}
$$

This assumes t_r + t_f = 20 ns with a gate resistor R_g of about 10 Ω and FD6288 drive (+0.3 A / −0.6 A).

### 2.2 Body-diode dead-time conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 2(50\,\mathrm{ns}) \cdot 48\,\mathrm{k} = 0.31\ \mathrm{W}
$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\,\mathrm{nC}\cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\,\mathrm{k} = 0.06\ \mathrm{W}
$$

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,eq} V^2 f = 0.5 \cdot 2\,\mathrm{nF}\cdot 25.2^2 \cdot 48\,\mathrm{k} = 0.03\ \mathrm{W}
$$

### 2.5 Gate charge

This loss is dissipated in the driver and R_g, not in the junction.

$$
P_g = Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} \cdot N = 50\,\mathrm{nC}\cdot 10 \cdot 48\,\mathrm{k} \cdot 2 = 0.048\ \mathrm{W}
$$

This is per switching pair. With commutation-driven switching of the other legs included, the total is about 0.14 W per driver.

### 2.6 Loss budget per channel @ 80A, T_j = 125 °C

| Component | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (2 FETs) | 20.5 |
| Switching overlap | 0.97 |
| Dead time | 0.31 |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.09 |
| Shunt | 1.28 |
| Copper | 1.9 |
| **Total / channel** | **≈ 25.0 W** |
| **4 channels simultaneous** | **≈ 100 W** |

**Key conclusion:** At 48 kHz, losses are **>80% conduction-dominated**. Raising f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz costs only about 0.6 W per channel. Choosing lower R_DS(on) parts matters far more than optimising the switching transitions.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Junction network (Foster, per FET)

| Layer | R_th | τ |
|---|---|---|
| Junction → case (PDFN 5×6) | 0.9 K/W | ~5 ms |
| Case → PCB copper (solder + vias, 25× 0.3 mm vias) | 2.5 K/W | ~50 ms |
| PCB → ambient (with prop wash, ~3 m/s) | board level, see §3.3 | — |

### 3.2 Short-burst junction rise (t < 100 ms)

$$
\Delta T_{j-pcb} = P_{FET}(R_{jc} + R_{c-pcb}) = 6.8 \cdot 3.4 = 23\ \mathrm{K}
$$

Junction-to-board gradient is not the limiter.

### 3.3 Board-level adiabatic heating (the real limiter)

**Heat capacity, 30.5×30.5 board with FETs, caps and connectors:**

- Copper: about 4.5 g × 0.385 J/g·K = 1.7 J/K
- FR4: about 6 g × 1.1 J/g·K = 6.6 J/K
- Components: about 2 J/K
- **Total C_th ≈ 10 J/K**

**Convection to ambient:** h ≈ 40 W/m²K under prop wash, over about 2×9.3 cm² of area, gives:

$$
R_{th,b-a} \approx 13\ \mathrm{K/W}, \qquad \tau_{board} = R C \approx 130\ \mathrm{s}
$$

For bursts much shorter than τ, heating is essentially adiabatic:

$$
\Delta T_{board}(t) \approx \frac{P\,t}{C_{th}} = \frac{100\,t}{10} = 10\ \mathrm{K/s}
$$
