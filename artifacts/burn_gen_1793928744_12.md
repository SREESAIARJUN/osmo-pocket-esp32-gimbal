## 0. Architecture Clarification and Design Review Flags

**0.1 The STM32H743 cannot be the commutation controller.** ArduPilot does not run ESC firmware. The correct partitioning is:

| Domain | Device | Firmware |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIH6 (TFBGA100) or VIT6 (LQFP100), 400 MHz | ArduPilot (ChibiOS) |
| ESC ×4 | AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071 (one per motor) | AM32 or BLHeli_32, bi-directional DShot600 enabled |
| Gate drive ×4 | 3-phase half-bridge driver (e.g., FD6288Q class) | — |
| Power stage ×4 | 6× N-channel MOSFET, 40 V class | — |

**0.2 The 80 A burst rating is extremely aggressive for a nano (20×20 / 25.5×25.5 mm) form factor.** Section 2.4 shows the board is limited by thermal capacitance to a few seconds at the full 4×80 A burst. Treat 80 A as a ≤2–5 s, single-motor-dominant rating, not a 4-motor simultaneous rating.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Bridge topology during 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation

In any 60° electrical sector, two phases carry current. With complementary PWM (synchronous rectification), as AM32 and BLHeli_32 do:

- **PWM leg:** the high side (HS) conducts for fraction D. The low side (LS) conducts for (1−D) as the synchronous rectifier.
- **Return leg:** the low side conducts for 100% of the sector.
- **Floating leg:** no current. This is the leg used for back-EMF zero-crossing sensing.

Instantaneous conduction loss in a sector:

$$P_{sector} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

The result is independent of duty cycle. Synchronous rectification removes the D-dependence, which is why burst loss at low throttle and high current (e.g., during prop-wash recovery) is as bad as at full throttle.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 115 °C: R_ds = 0.9 × (1 + 0.0045 × 90) = **1.265 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Copper and shunt contributions

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{trace} \qquad R_{trace} = \frac{\rho_{Cu}(T)\,\ell}{w\,t\,n_{layers}}$$

For example, a 6 mm phase path, 5 mm wide, 2 oz copper (70 µm) on 4 parallel layers gives:

R ≈ 1.72×10⁻⁸ × 1.3 × 0.006 / (0.005 × 70×10⁻⁶ × 4) ≈ **96 µΩ**, so P_Cu ≈ 0.61 W per motor.
