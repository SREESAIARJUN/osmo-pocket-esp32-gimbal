# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #37): System Architecture and Mathematical Model

## 0. Architecture Decisions and Feasibility

The design is only coherent once these points are settled.

| Issue | Finding | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Who switches at 48 kHz? | The H743 has only two advanced timers (TIM1/TIM8) with complementary outputs and dead-time. That covers 2 motors at most, with no BEMF zero-cross resources left over. | **Four dedicated ESC MCUs** (AT32F421 or STM32G071, running AM32) do the 48 kHz commutation. The H743 is the flight controller and talks to them over bidirectional DShot600. |
| "80 A burst" scope | 4 × 80 A = 320 A of simultaneous battery current. That is not physically deliverable through XT60/XT90 leads or a nano-class PCB. | Spec it as **80 A per channel, single-channel transient, ≤ 2–3 s**. Rate the bus for 160–180 A aggregate peak. |
| "Nano" form factor | A 20×20 mm board has about 2 J/K of thermal mass. As shown in §2.6, it cannot sustain an 80 A burst for more than about 1 s. | **30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz outer / 2 oz inner power planes.** If 20×20 is mandatory, derate to 45–50 A burst. |
| Voltage | The burst spec only makes sense at 6S. | 6S (25.2 V max) → **40 V MOSFETs** (60 V gives more margin against ringing, at higher RDS(on)). |

## 2. Power-Stage Loss Model (per ESC channel, 6 FETs)

### 2.1 Commutation and Current Path

The ESC uses trapezoidal 6-step commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. In each 60° sector, phase A's high-side FET is PWM-switched and phase B's low-side FET is held on.

- **During t_on (D·T):** the current flows HS_A → motor → LS_B. Two FETs are in series.
