## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Assumptions

Three issues affect the whole design and should be resolved first.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot runs on the H743 as the flight controller. It generates bi-directional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs, which run AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. The baseline here uses AT32F421, one per motor, each driving an FD6288Q 3-phase gate driver.
2. **80 A per channel on a "nano" board is a burst rating, not a continuous one.** Section 3 shows the thermal limit is about 1–2 s at 80 A per motor with all four motors loaded. A 20×20 mm board cannot physically host the FETs and copper needed. The assumed form factor is 30.5×30.5 mm with a 6-layer 2 oz/2 oz/1 oz… stack-up.
3. **Battery-side current is the real system constraint.** Four motors at 80 A gives 320 A. No XT60/XT90 lead, battery or bulk capacitor bank on a board this size sustains that. The 80 A figure must be specified as per-channel, short-duration and non-simultaneous unless the requirement owner states otherwise.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation Topology

The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM. In every 60° sector, the current path is the high-side FET of one leg plus the low-side FET of another. Synchronous rectification puts the freewheel current in a FET channel rather than the diode, so exactly two FET channels carry I_phase at all times:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{ds}(T_j) + I_{rms}^2 R_{Cu}$$

### 1.2 Temperature-Dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.5 mΩ × 1.5 = **2.25 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Ripple Correction

The current ripple comes from phase inductance L_ph. For a typical 2207 motor, L_ph ≈ 15 µH line-to-line. Worst-case ripple occurs at D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L\,f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{15\mu \times 48k} = 8.75\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{rms} = \sqrt{I^2 + \Delta I^2/12} = \sqrt{6400 + 6.4} = 80.04\ \text{A}$$

The ripple contribution is negligible at 48 kHz. That is one benefit of the higher PWM frequency.

### 1.4 Copper Path Resistance

Each phase path is about 10 mm of 2 oz copper, 4 mm wide, paralleled on 2 layers. Sheet resistance is 0.25 mΩ/□ at 2 oz.

$$R_{Cu} \approx \frac{0.25\,\text{m}\Omega \times (10/4)}{2} \approx 0.31\ \text{m}\Omega$$

Doubling for the return path gives about 0.6 mΩ.

### 1.5 Result at 80 A

$$P_{cond,ch} = 6400 \times (2 \times 2.25\text{m} + 0.6\text{m}) = 6400 \times 5.1\text{m} = \mathbf{32.6\ W}$$

Of this, 28.8 W is in the FETs, about 14.4 W in each conducting device.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg hard-switches per sector: the PWM leg. The model below covers that leg.

### 2.1 Transition Times

The Miller plateau is about 3.5 V. The driver's internal resistance is about 5 Ω.

- Turn-on: I_G,on = (12 − 3.5)/(5.7 + 5) = 0.79 A
- Turn-off: I_G,off = 3.5/10.7 = 0.33 A

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{G,on}} = \frac{12\,\text{nC}}{0.79} = 15\ \text{ns}$$

$$t_f \approx \frac{12\,\text{nC}}{0.33} = 36\ \text{ns}$$
