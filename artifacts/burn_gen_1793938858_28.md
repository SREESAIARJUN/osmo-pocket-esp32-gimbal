## 0. Scope and Assumptions

This is a **reference derivation built on stated assumptions**, not a validated design. You must replace every bracketed parameter with your actual MOSFET datasheet values, stackup, and measured airflow before the numbers are binding.

**Architectural clarification.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does not commutate the motors. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU running AM32, BLHeli_32, or Bluejay-class firmware with EDT/bidirectional DShot support. The H743 talks to those MCUs over DShot600. Designs that try to make the H743 do both jobs fail on timer and DMA budget and on real-time determinism.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)] \approx R_{ds,25}\cdot k_T$$

With k_T = 1.5 at T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{ds,hot} = 1.3 \times 1.5 = 1.95\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

At any instant, current flows through one high-side leg and one low-side leg.

- During the PWM on-time D, the current path is HS(A) → motor → LS(B).
- During the off-time (1−D), the current path is LS(A) (sync-rectified) → motor → LS(B).

The motor phase current I_ph therefore always flows through **exactly two R_ds(on) channels**:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds,hot}\quad(\text{independent of } D)$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.95\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{24.96\ W}$$

Each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical cycle, so the average per FET is ≈ 4.2 W. Its instantaneous dissipation while conducting is 80² × 1.95 mΩ = **12.5 W**.

### 1.3 PCB copper loss (frequently ignored, not negligible)

$$R_{Cu} = \rho_{Cu}(T)\frac{L}{W\cdot t\cdot n_{layers}},\qquad \rho_{Cu}(100°C) \approx 2.2\times10^{-8}\ \Omega\cdot m$$
