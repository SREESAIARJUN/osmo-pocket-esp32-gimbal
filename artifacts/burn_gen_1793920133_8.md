## 0. Architectural Corrections and Feasibility Flags

These issues affect every calculation that follows, so they come first.

| # | Issue | Resolution adopted in this document |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | The STM32H743 should not commutate the motors directly. It runs ArduPilot, which is not an ESC firmware. Production 4-in-1 boards use dedicated ESC MCUs. | The H743 is the **flight controller**. Each channel has its own **AT32F421 (or STM32G071) running AM32**, which generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 talks to them over bidirectional DShot600. |
| F2 | "Nano" (20×20 mm, M2) with **80 A × 4** is not thermally viable for sustained operation. 24 power FETs in 3.3×3.3 mm packages use about 260 mm² of a 400 mm² board before drivers, MCUs, shunts and pads. | The model below uses a **30.5×30.5 mm** board. The 20×20 case is included in §3.5 to quantify the shortfall. Treat 80 A as a **≤1 s per-motor burst**, not a continuous rating. |
| F3 | Four motors bursting simultaneously draws 320 A from the battery, which is beyond any XT60 or XT90 pad and 12 AWG lead. | Specify **80 A burst per channel, non-coincident**, with a pack-side total of about 160 A peak. Enforce with ArduPilot `BATT_*` current limiting and AM32 current limit. |

| Body-diode reverse recovery | Q_rr | 30 nC | — |

| Total gate charge | Q_g | 50 nC | — |

**Topology:** six-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM. In each 60° sector, one leg is PWM-switched (high-side or low-side alternately via complementary drive) and one low-side FET is held on.

## 2. Loss Model

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

With synchronous rectification, phase current always flows through exactly two channels in series:

- the switching leg (HS during t_on, LS during t_off), and
- the static low-side FET.

This makes conduction loss independent of duty cycle:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = k_T\, R_{25}
$$

At the burst point:

$$
R_{DS(on)} = 1.5 \times 1.0\ \text{m}\Omega = 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega
$$
$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W}
$$

**PCB copper loss.** Each phase path has about 0.25 mΩ of copper (2 oz, 4 parallel layers, about 6 mm of run). Over the two series paths:

$$
P_{Cu} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.25\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}
$$

**Current shunt loss.** A 0.5 mΩ shunt on the bus return carries I during the conduction interval:

$$
P_{sh} \approx 80^2 \times 0.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}
$$

This is large. Either use a **0.25 mΩ shunt (1.6 W)** or switch to phase-current or Hall sensing.

**Per-FET average.** Thermal time constants of milliseconds or more are much longer than the electrical period. For example, at 30 000 rpm with 14 poles the electrical frequency is 3.5 kHz (T ≈ 286 µs). So each FET can be treated by its average. Each of the 6 FETs carries current 1/3 of the time, which spreads 2·I²R evenly:

$$
P_{cond,FET} = \frac{2 I^2 R}{6} = 3.2\ \text{W}
$$

The instantaneous peak is I²R = 9.6 W, but it is thermally filtered at commutation rate.

### 2.2 Switching Loss at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The terms below are per motor.

**(a) V–I overlap, hard switching (linear approximation):**

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus}\, I\, (t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}
= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\times10^{-9} \times 48\,000 = \mathbf{1.45\ W}
$$

**(b) Output capacitance:**

$$
P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

**(c) Body-diode reverse recovery:**

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}
$$

**(d) Dead-time diode conduction (two edges per period):**

$$
P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\times10^{-9} \times 48\,000 = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

**(e) Gate drive** (mostly dissipated in the driver and R_g, two FETs per switching leg):

$$
P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

**Switching total:**

$$
P_{sw,motor} = 1.45 + 0.048 + 0.036 + 0.61 + 0.048 \approx \mathbf{2.19\ W}
$$

**Sensitivities:**
- Per kHz: ∂P_sw/∂f_sw ≈ 45.6 mW/kHz. Going to 96 kHz adds about 2.2 W per motor.
- Per ns: ∂P_sw/∂(t_r+t_f) ≈ 48 mW/ns.
- Per 10 ns: cutting dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W. It is the cheapest optimization, but verify shoot-through margin first
