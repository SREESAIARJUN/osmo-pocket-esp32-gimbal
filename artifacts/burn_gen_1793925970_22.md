# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage and DShot Design Model (Job #30)

## 0. Feasibility Summary

An **80 A burst per motor on a nano-format (20×20 mm mount) 4-in-1 board is thermally feasible only for about 1 s**, and only with aggressive layout. Sections 1–3 derive this. Sustained or multi-second bursts at 80 A need one of the following:
- a 30.5×30.5 mm board with dual FETs per switch position,
- a heatsink, or
- a re-specification to **80 A burst total** (about 20 A per motor), which is what most nano stacks actually mean.

A note on architecture: the H743 is the flight controller MCU. The 48 kHz PWM, gate drive and commutation run on **four separate ESC MCUs** (typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). The H743 only sources bidirectional DShot600 and consumes the eRPM telemetry. The model below follows that partitioning.

## 2. Loss Model (per motor, 6-step trapezoidal, complementary PWM)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

In any 60° sector, current flows through exactly **two switch positions**:
- the PWM'd leg's HS (during D) or its LS (during 1−D, via synchronous rectification), and
- the opposite leg's LS, which is on continuously.

Because synchronous rectification puts a FET in the path at all times, the bridge conduction loss is independent of D:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds,hot} = 2(80)^2(2.1\,\text{m}\Omega) = \mathbf{26.9\ W}$$

Each FET is active for 120° of every 360° electrical cycle, so the average per FET is:

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{I^2 R_{ds,hot}}{3} \cdot (\text{fraction}) \;\le\; \frac{13.4}{3} \approx \mathbf{4.5\ W}$$

The worst case is the HS at D → 1.

**Electro-thermal coupling.** R_ds depends on T_j, so iterate:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0046/\text{K}$$

The 2.1 mΩ figure is the converged value for T_j ≈ 125 °C.
