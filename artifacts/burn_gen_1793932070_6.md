# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #64): Power, Thermal and DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope and Feasibility Assumptions

**Architecture.** The H743 does not drive the gates. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU (AT32F421 running AM32, or BLHeli_32-class firmware). That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 commands to the four ESC MCUs.

**"Nano" vs. 80 A.** An 80 A-per-motor burst is not achievable on a 20×20 mm board. The model below assumes:

- **Board:** 30.5×30.5 mm mounting pattern, ~38×38 mm outline.
- **MOSFETs:** 5×6 mm DFN.
- **Copper:** 2 oz outer layers and 2 oz inner planes.
- **Burst duration:** ≤ 5 s.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (Six-Step, Synchronous Rectification)

In any commutation sector, two phases conduct:

- **Phase A (PWM phase):** high-side conducts for duty D. The complementary low-side conducts for (1−D).
- **Phase B (return phase):** low-side conducts continuously.

The per-motor conduction loss is:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{DS,hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS,hot} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS,hot}$$

The result is independent of duty cycle, because synchronous rectification removes the duty dependence.

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.35\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{17.28\ W/motor}$$

**Copper and trace loss.** Assume 0.5 mΩ of effective path resistance per phase (pad, plane and solder pad):

$$P_{Cu} = 2 I^2 R_{trace} = 6.4\ \text{W/motor}$$

This term is often ignored, but it matters at 80 A.

**Per-FET distribution.** Each FET carries current for about 1/3 of the electrical cycle. Its average loss is therefore about P_cond / 6 ≈ 2.9 W. Its instantaneous loss while conducting is I²R = 8.64 W.

## 2. Switching Loss Model @ 48 kHz

Only the PWM half-bridge switches.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = 0.5 · 25.2 · 80 · 30 ns · 48 k | **1.452 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw = 40 nC · 25.2 · 48 k | 0.048 W |
| C_oss (2 FETs) | 2 · ½ · C_oss · V² · f_sw | 0.046 W |
| Dead-time diode | V_F · I · 2t_d · f_sw = 0.8 · 80 · 200 ns · 48 k | **0.614 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs, mostly in driver) | 2 · Q_g · V_GS · f_sw | 0.058 W |
| **P_sw total** | | **≈ 2.22 W/motor** |

**Sensitivities:**

- P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. At 96 kHz it rises to about 4.4 W.
- P_cond does not depend on f_sw. At 80 A, conduction dominates (~89%), so 48 kHz is a reasonable choice.
- Reducing dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per motor. Verify shoot-through margin on a scope (V_GS of HS and LS overlaid) before doing this.

**Efficiency check:** P_out ≈ 4 × 80 A × ~22 V (effective) ≈ 7 kW, so η ≈ 98.5%. Losses are modest relative to throughput, but very large relative to the thermal mass of a small board.
