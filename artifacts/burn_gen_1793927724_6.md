# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC Nano Stack: Loss, Thermal, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Design Package

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Red Flags

I have no record of "Job #72," so this package is built from first principles. Every component-specific number is a **parameterized assumption** for you to replace with values from your BOM datasheets. The equations stay valid when you do.

### Architecture clarification
An STM32H743 is a flight-controller-class MCU. Production 4-in-1 ESCs run a dedicated MCU per motor (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32). This package assumes:

- **FC:** STM32H743 running ArduPilot. It generates DShot600 frames and decodes bi-directional (BDShot) telemetry.
- **ESC:** 4 × (commutation MCU + 3-phase gate driver + 6 N-MOSFETs), stacked on the FC.

If you actually intend the H743 to commutate all four motors directly, tell me. That changes Section 3 entirely: it would need 4 × 6 complementary PWM channels with dead time, which only TIM1/TIM8 (and HRTIM) provide natively.

### Red flags to resolve before layout

| Issue | Magnitude | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| 80 A phase burst × 4 on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5) board | ~85–95 W total dissipation (Section 2) | Adiabatic heating of roughly 15–20 K/s. Bursts must be limited to about 2–4 s. |
| Battery current at full duty | Up to ~320 A | Exceeds XT60 (60 A continuous) and XT90 ratings and typical nano board copper. 80 A is realistic as a *phase-current* burst, not a simultaneous 4-motor battery draw. |
| 6S on 30 V MOSFETs | 25.2 V + ringing | Insufficient margin. Use 40 V parts for 6S, or restrict to ≤4S. |

## 2. Mathematical Loss Model

### 2.1 Conduction losses

In 6-step commutation, exactly two phase legs carry I_ph at any instant (one sourcing, one sinking). With complementary PWM on the switching leg:

- The high-side device conducts during D.
- The low-side device conducts during (1 − D).

Either way, one device per active leg is always on. The instantaneous bridge conduction loss is therefore independent of duty (dead time neglected here; added in 2.2):

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

Each device conducts for 120° per electrical cycle, so the per-device average is:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \tfrac{1}{3}\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

Evaluated at T_j = 100 °C (k_T = 1.34, giving R_ds = 1.47 mΩ):

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.47\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{18.8\ W}$$

$$P_{cond,FET} = 6.27\ \text{W during its 120° sector} \;\rightarrow\; \mathbf{3.1\ W\ average}$$

**Copper and shunt terms.** Use 0.25 mΩ for the bridge-to-pad and plane path and 0.5 mΩ for a low-side shunt (if fitted):

$$P_{Cu} \approx 80^2 \times 0.25\text{ m}\Omega = 1.6\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{shunt} \approx 80^2 \times 0.5\text{ m}\Omega \times \tfrac{2}{3} \approx 2.1\ \text{W}$$

Many nano ESCs omit per-phase shunts. Delete the shunt term if yours does.

### 2.2 Switching losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. The non-PWM leg toggles only at commutation (≪ f_sw, negligible).

**(a) V–I overlap (hard switching):**
$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I_{ph}\,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 35\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.69\ W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery** (the low-side body diode recovers at each high-side turn-on):
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

**(c) Output capacitance:**
$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.018\ W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead-time intervals per period):
$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I_{ph}\,(2\,t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$
