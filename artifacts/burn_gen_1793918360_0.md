## 0. Feasibility Findings

These results come out of the math below and should be resolved at the requirements level before layout starts.

| # | Finding | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | 80 A × 4 motors at 6S is roughly **8 kW** of electrical input. A 20×20 mm "nano" stack cannot reject the resulting ~120 W of power-stage loss continuously. | "80 A" must be specified as a **per-motor burst of bounded duration** (≤1–2 s), not a sustained or all-motor rating. |
| F2 | The H743 is the **flight controller** MCU. The 4-in-1 ESC needs four dedicated ESC MCUs (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32). The H743 generates DShot; it does not commutate the motors. | This drives the architecture split between FC and ESC. |
| F3 | Conduction loss is about 95% of total loss at 80 A. Switching loss at 48 kHz is secondary. | Optimize R<sub>DS(on)</sub> and copper first, and switching speed second. |
| F4 | At 20×20 mm, 6S operation requires 40 V FETs in 3.3×3.3 mm packages. 80 A burst means you must check SOA and package current limits, not just R<sub>DS(on)</sub>. | This sets the FET selection gate. |

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current Path in 6-Step / Synchronous Rectification

During any commutation sector, two phases carry the full phase current *I*:

- **PWM leg:** HS conducts for fraction *D*. LS (synchronous rectification) conducts for *(1−D)*, ignoring dead time.
- **Return leg:** LS conducts for the full sector.

The I²R-weighted conduction is therefore:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

This result is independent of duty cycle, as long as synchronous rectification is active.

### 2.2 Temperature Dependence

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big], \quad \alpha \approx 0.004\text{–}0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

**Assumed FET** (replace with your datasheet values): 40 V, 3.3×3.3 DFN, R<sub>DS,25</sub> = 1.5 mΩ at V<sub>GS</sub> = 10 V, α = 0.005.

| T<sub>j</sub> | R<sub>DS</sub> | P<sub>cond</sub> per motor @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.50 mΩ | 2 × 6400 × 1.50 m = **19.2 W** |
| 100 °C | 2.06 mΩ | **26.4 W** |
| 125 °C | 2.25 mΩ | **28.8 W** |

### 2.3 Copper and Shunt Loss

Assume a 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner stack with an effective phase-path plus bus resistance of R<sub>Cu</sub> ≈ 0.4 mΩ per motor. Copper's temperature coefficient is 0.0039 K⁻¹.

$$
P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.4\,\text{m} = 2.56\ \text{W per motor at } 25\,°\text{C}
$$

### 2.4 Thermal Runaway Check

Conduction loss rises with T<sub>j</sub>, which raises T<sub>j</sub> further. The loop gain is:

$$
G = \frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\,R_{th} = 2I^2 R_{DS,25}\,\alpha\,R_{th,ja}
$$

With R<sub>th,ja</sub> ≈ 40 K/W per FET pair on a nano board: G = 2 × 6400 × 1.5 m × 0.005 × 40 ≈ **3.8**.

**G > 1 means the operating point is thermally unstable in steady state.** This is the formal proof of F1. 80 A is only valid as a transient bounded by thermal capacitance (§5).

## 3. Switching Loss Model at f<sub>sw</sub> = 48 kHz

Parameters: V<sub>bus</sub> = 25.2 V, I = 80 A, f<sub>sw</sub> = 48 kHz.

**Assumptions:** t<sub>r</sub> = t<sub>f</sub> = 15 ns at ~1.5 A gate drive; Q<sub>oss</sub> = 30 nC; Q<sub>rr</sub> = 40 nC; Q<sub>g</sub> = 50 nC; V<sub>F</sub> = 0.8 V; t<sub>dt</sub> = 50 ns.

### 3.1 V–I Overlap (Hard-Switched HS Only)

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}
$$

### 3.2 Output Capacitance

$$
P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}
$$

### 3.3 Body-Diode Reverse Recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

Reverse recovery also adds roughly Q<sub>rr</sub>/I worth of extra overlap at turn-on. This is included as a +10% margin on P<sub>VI</sub>.

### 3.4 Dead-Time Diode Conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}
$$

### 3.5 Gate Drive (Dissipated in Driver and R<sub>g</sub>, Not the FET)

$$
P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

### 3.6 Frequency Trade-Off

All switching terms scale linearly with f<sub>sw</sub>:

| f<sub>sw</sub> | P<sub>sw,total</sub> per motor |
|---|---|
| 24 kHz | ~0.95 W |
| 48 kHz | ~1.9 W |
| 96 kHz | ~3.8 W |

48 kHz costs about 1 W per motor over 24 kHz. In exchange, it lowers current ripple, reduces audible noise, and smooths low-throttle behaviour on low-inductance motors. **Keep 48 kHz.** Conduction loss dominates either way.

## 4. Total Loss Budget at 80 A Burst (T<sub>j</sub> = 100 °C Operating Point)

| Term | Per motor | 4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (2I²R<sub>DS</sub>) | 26.4 W | 105.6 W |
| Copper | 2.9 W | 11.6 W |
| V–I overlap (+10% Q<sub>rr</sub> margin) | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| Dead time | 0.31 W | 1.24 W |
| C<sub>oss</sub> + Q<sub>rr</sub> | 0.08 W | 0.33 W |
| Gate drive | 0.05 W | 0.19 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 31.3 W** | **≈ 125 W** |

At 80 A per motor and 6S, the power stage is about **98.5% efficient**. The problem is not efficiency; it is power density, at about 31 W/cm² across a 4 cm² board.
