**Engineering flag up front:** 80 A on a nano-format 4-in-1 is at the edge of physical plausibility. The model below shows it is survivable only as a short, airflow-assisted burst. It cannot be a continuous rating.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

This gives about 1.6× at 150 °C, consistent with typical datasheet normalisation curves. Fit α to your chosen part's curve.

### 1.2 Bridge conduction in 6-step synchronous PWM

In each 60° sector, two phases conduct:
- **PWM leg:** the high-side (HS) FET conducts for a fraction D. The synchronous low-side (LS) FET conducts for (1−D) of each period, minus dead time.
- **Return leg:** the LS FET conducts for 100% of the sector.

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,\big[D + (1-D) + 1\big] = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

This is independent of D, provided synchronous rectification is used.

Phase current versus battery current (ignoring ripple):

$$I_{batt} \approx D \cdot I_{ph}$$

Add the trace, shunt, and solder-joint resistance in the current path:

$$P_{Cu} = I_{ph}^2\,(2R_{trace} + R_{shunt}) \quad (\text{shunt only if in the phase path; low-side bus shunt sees } I_{batt}\text{-like current})$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

Assume T_j = 110 °C. This is checked iteratively in §3.4.

- R_DS(110 °C) = 1.2 × (1 + 0.005·85) = **1.71 mΩ**
- P_cond,ch = 2 × 80² × 1.71×10⁻³ = **21.9 W per channel**

Per FET, time-averaged over an electrical cycle (6 FETs share the load): ≈ 3.65 W.

Instantaneous worst case: the return-leg LS FET carries 80² × 1.71 mΩ = **10.9 W** for 1/3 of the electrical cycle. Die thermal time constants are on the order of ms. At low eRPM (sectors longer than ~1 ms), the die therefore follows the instantaneous figure, not the average. This is the worst case for punch-outs and stall starts.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM-leg HS FET hard-switches. The sync LS FET turns on after its body diode is already conducting, so it switches at near-zero voltage.
