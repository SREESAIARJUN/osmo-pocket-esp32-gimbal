# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Systems Architecture Package (Job #30)

This package covers the electrothermal model, the bi-directional DShot600 timer/DMA plan for ArduPilot, a reference netlist and the thermal budget. All device parameters are representative assumptions; substitute your selected parts' datasheet values before release.

---

## 0. Architecture Decisions and Feasibility Flags

| # | Issue | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | The H743 cannot run ArduPilot and 4× sensorless 48 kHz commutation as a production design. | Use the **H743 as FC (ArduPilot)** and **4× AT32F421 ESC MCUs (AM32 firmware)**. The H743 talks to them via bi-directional DShot600. |
| A2 | 80 A per channel × 4 = **320 A battery current**. XT60 (60 A continuous) is inadequate. | Use an XT90 or soldered 10 AWG leads, and add an external low-ESR bulk capacitor. |
| A3 | "Nano" (20×20 mm) has very little thermal mass. | 80 A is a **≤1–2 s burst rating** on 20×20 (Section 4). The recommended alternative is a split stack at 30.5×30.5 mm. |
| A4 | A 6S nominal bus (25.2 V max) with 40 V MOSFETs gives about 37% margin over the expected ringing. | Acceptable only with tight layout plus a TVS. |

**Assumed MOSFET** (40 V class, PDFN5×6 / DFN3.3): R_DS(on),25 = 0.70 mΩ @ V_GS = 10 V, α_T ≈ 1.6 at T_j = 125 °C, Q_g = 60 nC, Q_rr = 50 nC, C_oss(er) = 1.5 nF, t_r + t_f = 20 ns at the driver's ~1.5 A source/sink, V_SD = 0.8 V, R_θJC = 0.8 K/W.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths
- The motor is driven 6-step trapezoidal with synchronous rectification.
- In any commutation sector, two phase legs are active: the PWM leg (HS/LS complementary) and the commutated leg (LS held on).
- At any instant, the motor current therefore flows through exactly **one switch on the PWM leg plus one LS switch**.

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2\left[D\,R_{HS}+(1-D)\,R_{LS}+R_{LS}\right] + I_{ph}^2 R_{Cu} + I_{ph}^2 R_{sh}
$$

With identical HS/LS devices, $R_{HS}=R_{LS}=R_{DS}$:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I^2\left(2R_{DS}(T_j)+R_{Cu}+R_{sh}\right)
$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence
$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}\Rightarrow R_{DS}(125^\circ C) = 1.12\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

### 1.3 Numbers at I = 80 A, T_j = 125 °C
| Term | Value | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| 2 × R_DS | 2.24 mΩ | 2.24e-3 × 6400 = **14.34 W** |
| R_Cu (planes + vias, 2 oz × 4 layers paralleled) | 0.30 mΩ | **1.92 W** |
| R_shunt (per-channel current sense, optional) | 0.25 mΩ | **1.60 W** |
| **Subtotal** | | **17.86 W/ch** |

Per-device split: a worst-case LS that is always conducting in its sector sees $I^2 R_{DS}$ = 7.17 W while on. Averaged over the 1/3 sector duty for trapezoidal drive, this falls to about 2.4–4.8 W per FET, depending on how the commutation pattern shares sectors.

> **Recommendation:** delete the per-channel shunts and use one common bus shunt for total current. This saves 4.8 W at 320 A.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Switching losses occur only on the PWM leg. V_bus = 25.2 V (6S full charge), I = 80 A.

| Mechanism | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | $\tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f$ | 0.5·25.2·80·20 ns·48 k = **0.968 W** |
| Output capacitance | $C_{oss(er)} V^2 f$ | 1.5 nF·635·48 k = **0.046 W** |
| Reverse recovery | $Q_{rr} V f$ | 50 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.060 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | $V_{SD} I \cdot 2t_{dt} f$, t_dt = 100 ns | 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k = **0.614 W** |
| Gate drive (×2 FETs, at the driver) | $Q_g V_{GS} f$ ×2 | 60 nC·10·48 k·2 = **0.058 W** |
| **Subtotal** | | **≈1.75 W/ch** |

Design levers:
- Cutting dead time to 50 ns saves 0.31 W/ch. Verify shoot-through margin with a scope at T_j(max).
- 48 kHz roughly doubles switching loss compared with 24 kHz. Keep 48 kHz only for low-inductance motors, i.e. where ripple $\Delta I = \frac{V(1-D)D}{L f}$ needs it.

### Total per channel and per board
$$
P_{ch} = 17.86 + 1.75 \approx 19.6\ \text{W}\quad\Rightarrow\quad P_{4ch} \approx 78\ \text{W}\ (\text{with shunts}),\ \approx 72\ \text{W}\ (\text{common shunt})
$$

Efficiency at the 80 A burst: P_out ≈ 25.2·80·4·D. At D = 0.9 this is ≈7.3 kW, so η ≈ 99%. That is electrically fine; **the problem is purely thermal density**.

### Bus capacitor ripple
$I_{C,rms} = I\sqrt{D(1-D)}$, which peaks at 40 A_rms per channel when D = 0.5.
- Use about 4× 22 µF X7R 50 V MLCC per channel near the bridge, plus an external 1000 µF/35 V low-ESR bulk capacitor on the leads.
- MLCC DC-bias derating at 25 V leaves about 30–40% of nominal capacitance.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Steady state: not achievable on a nano board
- The board-to-ambient resistance under prop wash is h ≈ 50 W/m²K, with A = 2 × 20×20 mm = 8×10⁻⁴ m², giving R_θBA ≈ 25 K/W.
- The resulting steady-state rise is 72 W × 25 K/W = 1800 K. **The design is therefore burst-only.**
- Continuous rating, with ΔT_board = 60 K:
  - 60 / 25 = 2.4 W total.
  - Solving $4\cdot I^2 \cdot 2.54\text{ mΩ} \approx 2.4$ gives **I_cont ≈ 15 A/ch** on 20×20, before airflow improvements.
  - On 30.5×30.5 (R_θBA ≈ 10.7 K/W), I_cont ≈ 23 A/ch.
