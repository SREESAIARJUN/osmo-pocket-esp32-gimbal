## 1. FOC Current Loop

### 1.1 Transforms

The **Clarke transform** (amplitude-invariant, using the balanced-phase condition i_a + i_b + i_c = 0):

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \frac{1}{\sqrt3}\,(i_a + 2 i_b)
$$

The **Park transform**, with electrical angle θ_e = p·θ_m − θ_offset:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d \\ i_q\end{bmatrix} =
\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e \\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha \\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}
$$

The **inverse Park transform** returns the voltage commands to the stationary frame:

$$
v_\alpha = v_d\cos\theta_e - v_q\sin\theta_e,\qquad
v_\beta = v_d\sin\theta_e + v_q\cos\theta_e
$$

### 1.2 Plant Model

$$
v_d = R i_d + L\dot i_d - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad
v_q = R i_q + L\dot i_q + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e\lambda_m
$$

### 1.3 PI Controller with Decoupling and Back-EMF Feedforward

The controller uses pole-zero cancellation. Setting K_i/K_p = R/L cancels the electrical pole, which leaves a first-order closed loop with bandwidth ω_c:

$$
K_p = L\,\omega_c,\qquad K_i = R\,\omega_c
$$

$$
v_d^* = \text{PI}_d(i_d^* - i_d) - \omega_e L i_q
$$

$$
v_q^* = \text{PI}_q(i_q^* - i_q) + \omega_e (L i_d + \lambda_m)
$$

The current references are i_d* = 0 and i_q* = τ*/K_t, where K_t = (3/2)·p·λ_m.

**Discretization.** The PI uses backward Euler with integrator clamping:

$$
x_{k} = x_{k-1} + K_i T_s e_k
$$

**Voltage limiting.** The voltage vector is limited circularly to |v| ≤ V_bus/√3, which is the linear SVPWM region. Integration is frozen whenever the vector is saturated. Choose ω_c ≤ 2π·f_pwm/10 so that the one-sample PWM delay (≈1.5·T_s) costs less than about 20° of phase margin.

### 1.4 SVPWM by Min-Max Zero-Sequence Injection

This method is equivalent to classic SVPWM and does not require sector logic:

$$
v_a = v_\alpha,\quad v_b = -\tfrac12 v_\alpha + \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}v_\beta,\quad v_c = -\tfrac12 v_\alpha - \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}v_\beta
$$

$$
v_0 = -\tfrac12\big(\max(v_{abc}) + \min(v_{abc})\big),\qquad
D_x = \tfrac12 + \frac{v_x + v_0}{V_{bus}}
$$

## 2. Cascaded Angle/Velocity Control

The plant for each axis, seen from i_q, is:

$$
J\dot\omega = K_t i_q - b\,\omega - \tau_{cog}(\theta) - \tau_{dist}
$$

### 2.1 Velocity Loop (PI)

The feedback signal is the gyro rate rotated into the motor axis. This rejects base motion directly; the encoder derivative would not.

$$
i_q^* = K_{pv}\,e_\omega + K_{iv}\!\int e_\omega\,dt + i_{q,ff},\qquad e_\omega = \omega^* - \omega_{gyro}
$$

**Gain selection.** Treat the current loop as approximately 1 at velocity bandwidth. The open loop is then L(s) = K_t·K_pv·(1 + ω_i/s)/(J·s). Choose:

$$
K_{pv} = \frac{J\,\omega_v}{K_t},\qquad K_{iv} = K_{pv}\,\frac{\omega_v}{4}
$$

This places the PI zero at ω_v/4, which gives about 76° of phase margin before delays.

**Anti-windup.** Use back-calculation with T_t ≈ √(T_i·T_d), or T_t = T_i/2 for PI:

$$
x_k = x_{k-1} + T_s\Big(K_{iv}e_k + \tfrac{1}{T_t}(u_{sat}-u)\Big)
$$
