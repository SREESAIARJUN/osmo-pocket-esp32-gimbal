# STM32H743 + 4-in-1 ESC "Nano" Stack (Job #80): System Architecture & Loss Model

## 0. Feasibility Findings

These findings shape everything below.

1. **The H743 cannot be the ESC controller.** It is the flight controller. Each ESC channel needs its own MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071, plus a 3-phase gate driver. The H743 talks to the ESCs over bidirectional DShot600. One H743 cannot run four closed-loop sensorless 6-step commutators at 48 kHz while also running ArduPilot.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano footprint is limited by thermal energy storage, not by the silicon.** The FETs can carry 80 A. The board cannot dump about 85 W, so it heats roughly adiabatically. Section 3 shows that 80 A on all four channels is only safe for about 2–3 s.
3. **Twenty-four 5×6 mm FETs do not fit on a 20×20 mm board.** The packages alone need 24 × 30 mm² = 720 mm², versus 400 mm² per side.
   - A 30.5×30.5 mm board, or 3.3×3.3 mm FETs at roughly 2× the Rds(on), is required.
   - All numbers below assume **30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz outer / 1 oz inner copper, 40 V FETs, 6S**.

All component values are representative engineering assumptions. Substitute datasheet values before release.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

In 6-step operation with synchronous rectification, exactly **two FETs** carry the phase current at every instant: one in the PWM'd leg (high or low side, depending on PWM state) and one in the fixed leg.

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

**Ripple contribution.** Peak-to-peak ripple at D = 0.5 (the worst case):

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L\,f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\,\mu H \times 48\,kHz} = 6.56\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.6 = 6403.6\ \text{A}^2$$

The ripple term adds less than 0.06%, so it is negligible. The 48 kHz choice is justified on acoustics and current ripple, not conduction loss.

**Temperature dependence of Rds(on):**

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

**Self-consistent junction temperature.** Iterating between loss and temperature with the board model in §3 converges near T_j ≈ 110 °C at the end of a 2.5 s burst. At that point R_ds ≈ 1.38 mΩ.

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 6404 \times 1.38\ m\Omega = \mathbf{17.7\ W}\ \ (\text{cold start: } 12.8\ W)$$

**Per-FET average.** Each leg conducts 2/3 of the electrical cycle, and that time is split between the high and low side by D. The average per FET is:

$$\frac{17.7}{6} \approx 2.95\ W$$

The instantaneous peak per FET is 8.85 W.

**Copper loss** must be added: bus planes plus phase pads, about 0.25 mΩ effective round-trip on 2 oz copper.

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.25\ m\Omega = \mathbf{1.6\ W}$$

### 2.2 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches. Each PWM period has one turn-on and one turn-off hard-switching transition.

| Mechanism | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = ½ · 25.2 · 80 · 20 ns · 48 k | **0.968 W** |
| Coss charge | Q_oss · V · f_sw = 40 n · 25.2 · 48 k | 0.048 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw = 50 n · 25.2 · 48 k | 0.060 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f · I · 2t_dt · f_sw = 0.8 · 80 · 200 n · 48 k | **0.614 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs/leg) | 2 · Q_g · V_gs · f_sw | 0.058 W (mostly in driver/Rg) |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 1.75 W** |

**Design lever:** dead-time loss is 35% of the switching total. Cutting t_dt from 100 ns to 50 ns (AM32 `DEAD_TIME` setting, checked against driver propagation skew) saves about 0.3 W per channel.

### 2.3 DC-Link Capacitor Ripple Loss

The input RMS ripple for 6-step operation is:

$$I_{C,rms} \approx I\sqrt{D(1-D)} = 80 \times 0.5 = 40\ \text{A}\ \ (\text{per channel, D = 0.5})$$

Interleaving across 4 channels is not guaranteed because the motors are asynchronous. The worst case is the uncorrelated sum:

$$\sqrt{4} \times 40 = 80\ \text{A}_{rms}$$

This total ripple is shared by:
- the board ceramics: 8 × 10 µF 50 V X7R 1210, ESR about 3 mΩ each, roughly 0.4 mΩ in parallel at 48 kHz;
- an external low-ESR electrolytic on the pigtail, for example 1000 µF 35 V, ESR about 20 mΩ.

The current split follows the impedance ratio, with ceramics dominating at 48 kHz and harmonics. Estimated ceramic dissipation is about 2–3 W, spread across the caps.

**Mandatory:** the external electrolytic plus a TVS. Without it, the ripple current flows through the battery lead inductance, causing voltage spikes that kill 40 V FETs on 6S.
