## 0. Executive Summary

1. **The 80 A burst target is achievable at the MOSFET level but not at the system level on a nano form factor without strict time limits.**
   - Per-channel dissipation at 80 A is about **21 W**, or **~84 W** for all four channels.
   - A 30×30 mm 6-layer board has roughly 4.5 J/K of thermal mass. That gives about **3–4 s** of all-motor burst before the PCB reaches 110 °C.
   - Continuous capability with propwash cooling is roughly **25–30 A per channel**.
2. **System current is the real limiter, not the FETs.**
   - 4 × 80 A = 320 A from the battery.
   - A shared current-sense shunt dissipates 10–20 W at that current.
   - The power entry needs solder pads, not XT60/XT30 connectors.
3. **The bidirectional DShot600 plan uses TIM3 + TIM5 with 6 DMA streams.** This mirrors a pin/timer pattern known to work in ArduPilot on H743 (MatekH743-style).

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j)=R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha_R (T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0045·100) = **1.45 mΩ**.

### 2.2 Current path in 6-step drive

At any instant two phases conduct. Current flows through:
- one high-side FET (during the PWM on-time), or its complementary low-side FET (during off-time, synchronous rectification), **plus**
- one low-side FET in the return leg (on for the full 60° sector).

Because of synchronous rectification, the PWM leg always has exactly one FET conducting. Duty cycle therefore cancels for the leg total:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2\,(R_{DS,HS/LS} + R_{DS,LS,ret}) + I^2 R_{Cu}$$

Include ripple:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{DC}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12},\qquad \Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}-E_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

Typical 6S motor at high load: L_ph ≈ 15 µH, D ≈ 0.9, E ≈ 21 V. This gives ΔI ≈ (4.2·0.9)/(15e-6·48e3) ≈ 5.3 A, so ΔI²/12 ≈ 2.3 A². That is negligible next to 6400 A².
