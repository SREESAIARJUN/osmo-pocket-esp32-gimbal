# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage, Thermal, and Bidirectional DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope Corrections

Three things in the brief would cause this design to fail if taken literally. The rest of this document addresses each one.

| Issue | Problem | Resolution used here |
|---|---|---|
| **H743 as the ESC controller** | ArduPilot cannot also commutate 4 motors at 48 kHz with sensorless back-EMF zero-cross detection. Its scheduler, DMA, and ADC budget are already committed. | The H743 is the **flight controller**. The ESC uses **4× AT32F421K8U7** (or STM32G071) running **AM32** at 48 kHz PWM. The H743 talks to them over bidirectional DShot600. |
| **"80A burst" in a nano (20×20 mm) form factor** | 4 × 80 A × 25.2 V ≈ **8 kW**. A 20×20 board cannot take this current through its pads, leads, or connector. Commercial 20×20 AIOs are rated 20–35 A/motor. | The model is derived generally. Numbers are evaluated at 80 A/motor so you can see why it fails. A **30.5×30.5 mm** board is the minimum credible platform for 80 A bursts (§4.5). |
| **Component parameters** | No MOSFET was specified. | A representative 40 V, ~1 mΩ PQFN 3.3×3.3 class device is used (§1). **Replace every parameter with your chosen part's datasheet values before release.** |

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Commutation scheme

AM32 uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary PWM (synchronous rectification) on the PWM'd leg. In each 60° sector:

- **Leg A (PWM'd):** the high-side FET conducts for duty D. The low-side FET conducts for 1 − D (synchronous rectification).
- **Leg B (return):** the low-side FET conducts continuously.

Over one electrical cycle, each leg spends 120° as PWM leg, 120° as return leg, and 120° floating. The per-FET RMS-equivalent conduction is:

$$P_{HS} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,\frac{D}{3}, \qquad P_{LS} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,\frac{(1-D)+1}{3}$$

Summed over the bridge, two FETs are always in the current path:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\quad\text{(independent of } D\text{)}$$
