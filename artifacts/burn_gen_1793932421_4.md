# 3-Axis Gimbal: FOC Control, Anti-Cogging Feedforward, and Dual ICM-42688-P Fusion

I don't have the Job #16 spec sheet, so I've built everything around stated, parameterized assumptions. Replace the constants in `GimbalConfig` with your hardware values. Every equation maps directly to a function in the code.

## 0. Assumptions and Loop Timing

| Item | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Motor | Gimbal BLDC, 12N14P (p = 7 pole pairs), R ≈ 5–15 Ω, L ≈ 1–3 mH |
| Rotor sensor | 14-bit absolute magnetic encoder (MT6701 / AS5048A) on each axis |
| MCU | STM32G4 / H7 class, single-precision FPU |
| Inverter | 3-phase, center-aligned PWM at 20 kHz, 2-shunt or 3-shunt sensing |
| IMUs | 2 × ICM-42688-P on the camera plate, SPI at 24 MHz, ODR 8 kHz, FIFO + DRDY |

**Loop rates:**

```
20 kHz  : ADC → Clarke/Park → current PI (id, iq) → SVPWM            [ISR, PWM-synchronous]
 8 kHz  : IMU FIFO read → per-sensor AAF/notch → dual fusion          [SPI DMA complete ISR]
 4 kHz  : attitude update, velocity PI + cogging FF + accel FF        [decimated by 2]
 1 kHz  : angle (attitude) loop, setpoint shaping                     [decimated by 4]
```

Each cascade layer runs at ≥4× the rate of the layer outside it, and its closed-loop bandwidth is ≤¼ of the inner layer's. This bandwidth separation is what makes the cascade design rigorous: each outer loop can treat its inner loop as approximately unity gain.

## 1. FOC Current Loop (Innermost)

### 1.1 Electrical model (PMSM, dq-frame, surface magnets, L_d ≈ L_q = L)

$$
v_d = R\,i_d + L\frac{di_d}{dt} - \omega_e L\,i_q
$$
$$
v_q = R\,i_q + L\frac{di_q}{dt} + \omega_e L\,i_d + \omega_e \lambda_m
$$
$$
\tau_e = \tfrac{3}{2}\,p\,\lambda_m\,i_q = K_t\,i_q,\qquad \omega_e = p\,\omega_m,\quad \theta_e = p\,\theta_m - \theta_{\text{offset}}
$$

### 1.2 Transforms

**Clarke (amplitude-invariant, with i_a + i_b + i_c = 0):**

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \frac{i_a + 2 i_b}{\sqrt{3}}
$$

**Park:**

$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix}=
\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}
$$

**Inverse Park** is the transpose of the Park matrix.

**Angle latency compensation.** The voltage computed in PWM period k is applied about 1.5 T_s later. Use this angle for the inverse Park transform:

$$
\theta_e^{\text{inv}} = \theta_e + 1.5\,\omega_e T_s
$$

### 1.3 Current PI with decoupling and back-EMF feedforward

$$
v_d^* = PI_d(i_d^* - i_d) - \omega_e L\, i_q
$$
$$
v_q^* = PI_q(i_q^* - i_q) + \omega_e L\, i_d + \omega_e \lambda_m
$$

**Gain design by pole-zero cancellation.** Place the PI zero on the electrical pole R/L. The closed loop then becomes first-order with bandwidth ω_c:

$$
K_{p,i} = L\,\omega_c,\qquad K_{i,i} = R\,\omega_c,\qquad \omega_c \le \frac{2\pi f_{\text{PWM}}}{10}
$$

With f_PWM = 20 kHz, this gives ω_c ≈ 2π·1.5 kHz.

**Voltage limit (linear SVPWM region).** V_max = V_bus/√3. The d-axis has priority:

$$
v_d \leftarrow \text{clamp}(v_d, \pm V_{\max}),\qquad
v_q \leftarrow \text{clamp}\!\left(v_q, \pm\sqrt{V_{\max}^2 - v_d^2}\right)
$$
