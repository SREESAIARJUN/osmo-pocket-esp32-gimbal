# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: System Architecture Package (Job #35)

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Check

**Partitioning.** The STM32H743 is the flight-controller MCU. It runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600. It does not commutate the motors. Each ESC channel uses a dedicated low-cost MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32), which generates the 48 kHz PWM. Putting 12 half-bridges of 48 kHz six-step control plus flight control on one H743 is possible in principle, but it is not supported by ArduPilot and is not done in practice.

**Feasibility warning.** "80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board is thermally marginal. The model below shows it is achievable only as a **burst of about 1–2 s per channel**, not continuous. The deliverable treats 80 A as a time-limited rating and derives that limit explicitly.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in six-step with complementary (synchronous) PWM

At any instant, exactly two phases conduct:
- One phase is PWM-ing: its high-side FET is on for duty D, and its low-side FET is on for (1 − D) as the synchronous rectifier.
- One phase is held low: its low-side FET is on continuously.

The motor current therefore always flows through **exactly two FETs in series**, independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

### 1.2 Per-FET RMS current

Each phase leg conducts for 2/3 of the electrical cycle.

- **Held-low FET** (conducts for 1/3 of the cycle):
$$I_{rms,LS} = I_{ph}\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{3}}$$
- **Switching-phase FETs** (1/3 of the cycle, split by D):
$$I_{rms,HS} = I_{ph}\sqrt{\tfrac{D}{3}}, \qquad I_{rms,LS,sync} = I_{ph}\sqrt{\tfrac{1-D}{3}}$$

Summed across 6 FETs, this reproduces the 2·I²·R result. The worst single FET is the low-side, so it sets the hotspot.
