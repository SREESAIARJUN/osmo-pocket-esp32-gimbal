# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal, and Bi-Directional DShot Design Package (Job #51)

## 0. Assumptions and Corrections

I have no prior project context for "Job #51", so all design parameters are stated below. Replace them with your actual BOM values before release.

**Architectural correction.** On a 4-in-1 ESC, the STM32H743 does **not** perform 48 kHz motor switching. The FC sends DShot600 to a separate ESC MCU (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32), and that MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The model is therefore split into two domains:

| Domain | Device | Function |
|---|---|---|
| FC | STM32H743VIT6 @ 480 MHz | ArduPilot, bi-directional DShot600, DMA |
| ESC (×4 or ×1 quad-core) | AT32F421 @ 120 MHz (AM32) | 48 kHz PWM, commutation, eRPM telemetry |
| Power stage | 6 N-MOSFETs per motor, 3-phase gate driver (FD6288Q class) | — |

**Design basis:**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo: V_bus = 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal |
| Burst current | 80 A per motor, ≤10 s rated |
| MOSFET (typical 40 V, 5×6 or 3.3×3.3 DFN class) | R_ds(on) = 1.5 mΩ @ 25 °C, temp coefficient α ≈ 0.0055 /K |
| Switching edges | Q_g = 50 nC, Q_rr = 40 nC, C_oss,eff = 2 nF, t_r = t_f = 20 ns |
| Thermal resistance | R_θJC = 1.0 K/W, V_f(body diode) = 0.8 V |
| Dead time | t_dt = 100 ns per edge |
| PWM | f_sw = 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous rectification), 6-step trapezoidal drive |

**Realism warning.** "80 A burst" on a nano-format (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) 4-in-1 board is at the edge of physical feasibility. The math in §3 shows that 80 A on all four motors simultaneously is only survivable for a few seconds.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step drive

During any commutation sector, two phases carry current I:

- **Switching leg (complementary PWM):**
  - High-side FET conducts for fraction D.
  - Low-side FET (synchronous rectifier) conducts for fraction (1−D).
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET is on 100% of the sector.

The instantaneous conduction loss is therefore:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

This result is **independent of duty cycle**, which is the key benefit of synchronous rectification.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond @ 80 A (2·I²·R) |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.50 mΩ | 19.2 W |
| 100 °C | 2.12 mΩ | 27.2 W |
| 125 °C | 2.33 mΩ | 29.8 W |

### 1.3 PCB copper loss

Assume the phase and bus path is 2 oz copper with about 0.5 mΩ effective resistance per motor channel:

$$
P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.0005 = 3.2\ \text{W per motor}
$$

### 1.4 Per-FET average loss

Each FET is active for 1/3 of an electrical revolution (120° conduction), so the per-FET average is:

$$
\bar P_{FET} \approx \frac{P_{cond}}{6}\cdot 2 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} \approx \frac{P_{cond}+P_{sw}}{6}\ \text{(per phase pair, averaged)}
$$

The electrical period at typical eRPM (≥10 kHz electrical) is far shorter than the die thermal time constant (~ms), so the average is valid for junction temperature. Use the **peak sector loss** only for SOA checks.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg switches per sector. Each term below is evaluated at V = 25.2 V and I = 80 A.

### 2.1 V–I overlap

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = 1.94\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

The low-side body diode conducts during dead time and recovers when the high side turns on:

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = 40\text{nC}\cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

This loss applies to both FETs in the leg:

$$
P_{oss} = 2 \cdot \tfrac12 C_{oss,eff} V^2 f_{sw} = 2\text{nF}\cdot 635 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.061\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM period:

$$
P_{dt} = 2 V_f I\, t_{dt} f_{sw} = 2\cdot 0.8\cdot 80\cdot 100\text{ns}\cdot 48\text{k} = 0.614\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET:

$$
P_g = 2 Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\cdot 50\text{nC}\cdot 10\cdot 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.6 Switching total and frequency sensitivity

$$
P_{sw,total} \approx 2.67\ \text{W per motor @ 80 A}
$$

The 48 kHz choice costs about 2.67 W per motor. At 24 kHz this would be about 1.33 W.

The tradeoff favors 48 kHz anyway:
- smoother current ripple,
- lower motor iron/copper ripple loss,
- inaudible operation.

### 2.7 PWM resolution on the ESC MCU

- Edge-aligned: 120 MHz / 48 kHz = **2500 counts**, about 11.3 bits.
- Center-aligned: 1250 counts. This is still adequate, at about 0.08% duty resolution.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Per-motor power budget @ 80 A, T_j ≈ 110 °C

| Loss | Value |
|---|---|
| Conduction (2·I²·R_ds, R = 2.2 mΩ) | 28.2 W |
| Switching (total) | 2.7 W |
| Copper | 3.2 W |
| **Per motor** | **≈ 34 W** |
| **4 motors at 80 A** | **≈ 136 W** |

### 3.2 Electro-thermal feedback (closed form)

Write the temperature-dependent loss as:

$$
P(T_j) = P_0[1+\alpha(T_j-25)] + P_{fixed}
$$

and the junction temperature as T_j = T_ref + P·R_θ. Solving the two together gives:

$$
T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + R_\theta\left[P_0(1-25\alpha) + P_{fixed}\right]}{1 - \alpha P_0 R_\theta}
$$

**Thermal runaway condition:** the design is unstable if α·P₀·R_θ ≥ 1.

For a single FET, using per-FET conduction P₀ ≈ 19.2/3 W ≈ 6.4 W and R_θJA,eff ≈ 15 K/W (board-mounted, no airflow):

$$
\alpha P_0 R_\theta = 0.0055 \cdot 6.4 \cdot 15 = 0.53
$$

This is stable, but the steady-state result is:

$$
T_j \approx \frac{40 + 15(6.4\cdot0.8625 + 0.9)}{0.47} \approx 290\ ^\circ C
$$

That is **not survivable in steady state**. Continuous 80 A is impossible on this footprint, which is why the rating must be a *burst* rating.
