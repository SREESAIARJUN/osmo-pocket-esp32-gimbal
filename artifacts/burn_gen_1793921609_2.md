# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #31): Power-Stage Thermal Model, Switching Analysis, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Timer/DMA Allocation

## 0. Architecture and Key Assumptions

**Architecture clarification:** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does not commutate the motors. The 4-in-1 ESC section uses four dedicated ESC MCUs running AM32 or BLHeli_32, typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 class. The H743 talks to them over bi-directional DShot600. Any design that tries to commutate four motors directly from the H743 while also running ArduPilot is not recommended.

**Assumptions.** All numbers below are design inputs. Replace them with your actual BOM datasheet values before sign-off.

**Feasibility flag:** 80 A per channel on a 20×20 board is a **burst-only** rating. The numbers below show it is sustainable for single-digit seconds at most. Section 2 quantifies this.

## 1. Loss Model, per Channel (Three-Phase Bridge, 6-Step, Synchronous Rectification)

### 1.1 Conduction Loss

With complementary PWM and synchronous rectification, at every instant the phase current flows through exactly **two** FETs: one in the PWM'd leg (HS or LS, complementary) and one in the static leg. The loss is therefore duty-independent to first order:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot (1.0\text{ mΩ}\cdot 1.5) = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

Averaged over an electrical cycle across 6 FETs:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{I^2 R}{3} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

The instantaneous peak per FET is 9.6 W while it is in conduction.

### 1.2 Switching Loss (Only the PWM'd Leg Switches)

**V–I overlap (hard switching):**
$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot40\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**Output capacitance:**
$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss}V^2 f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot1.5\text{n}\cdot635\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.023\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period):**
$$P_{dt} = V_f I\,(2t_{dead}) f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot400\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

**Gate drive (mostly dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET):**
$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2\cdot60\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.058\ W}$$

### 1.3 Totals

| Term | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.20 W | 76.8 W |
| V–I switching | 1.94 W | 7.7 W |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 W | 4.9 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.08 W | 0.3 W |
| Gate | 0.06 W | 0.2 W |
| **Total** | **≈22.5 W** | **≈90 W** |

**Shunt and copper add-on:** a 0.25 mΩ shunt plus about 0.5 mΩ of trace/via resistance adds $80^2 \cdot 0.75\text{ mΩ} = 4.8$ W per channel. Fold this in if the shunt sits in the bus path. Including it, the worst-case total is **≈110 W**.

### 1.4 Generalized Model (for Plotting vs. Current)

$$P_{ch}(I) = 2I^2R_{ds}(T_j) + I^2R_{path} + I\!\left[\tfrac12 V(t_r+t_f) + 2V_f t_{dead}\right]\!f_{sw} + (Q_{rr}V + \tfrac12 C_{oss}V^2 + 2Q_gV_{drv})f_{sw}$$

$R_{ds}$ depends on temperature: $R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$ with α ≈ 0.005/K. This creates positive feedback. Iterate $T_j \leftrightarrow P$ until convergence, or until divergence, which is thermal runaway.

**Design lessons:**
- At 48 kHz, dead-time loss is about 63 % of the V–I switching loss.
- Trimming dead time from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per channel.
- Conduction loss dominates at 80 A, so the most effective lever is lower R_ds(on) or paralleled FETs. Halving R halves 85 % of the loss.
