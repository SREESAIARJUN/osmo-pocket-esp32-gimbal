# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Design Package for Job #8

**Scope:** Power-stage loss model (80 A burst, 48 kHz PWM), transient thermal model, ArduPilot bi-directional DShot600 timer and DMA allocation, and a representative netlist.

**Bottom line:** The numbers show that **80 A per motor cannot be sustained on a nano-format board, and even as a burst it is limited to about 2–3 s.** The architecture and burst rating below are set accordingly.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Six-step trapezoidal with complementary (synchronous) PWM

At any instant, two phases carry current:
- During the on-time, the current path is HS(A) → motor → LS(B).
- During the off-time, current freewheels through LS(A), which is actively on because of synchronous rectification, and LS(B).

Either way, current always flows through **two conducting channels in series**. The per-motor conduction loss is therefore essentially independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,motor}=I_{ph}^2\,(2R_{ds}(T_j)+R_{cu})$$

$$P_{cond,motor}=80^2\,(2\cdot1.55+0.4)\,\text{mΩ}=6400\cdot3.5\times10^{-3}=\mathbf{22.4\ W}$$

- MOSFET share: 6400 × 3.1 mΩ = **19.84 W**
- Copper share: 2.56 W

### 2.2 Per-FET distribution

Each FET conducts for 120° electrical (1/3 of the period):

$$P_{cond,FET}=\frac{I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)}{3}\cdot 2\cdot\frac{6}{6}\Big/2 = \frac{6400\cdot1.55\text{m}}{3}\approx\mathbf{3.31\ W}$$

Check: 6 FETs × 3.31 W = 19.84 W ✓

### 2.3 Phase current vs. battery current

At duty D, the battery current is I_bat ≈ D·I_ph. An "80 A burst" specified as **battery** current at D < 1 implies **higher** phase current, since I_ph = I_bat/D. Conduction loss scales with I_ph².

Rated burst must therefore be stated as phase current. All figures here assume I_ph = 80 A, which is the worst-case interpretation.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches during each commutation sector. That is one half-bridge with two hard edges per PWM period.

### 3.1 V–I overlap (hard switching)

$$P_{VI}=\tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f)f_{sw}=\tfrac12(25.2)(80)(40\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz})=\mathbf{1.935\ W}$$

### 3.2 Body-diode conduction during dead time

$$P_{DT}=V_{SD}I_{ph}(2t_{dead})f_{sw}=0.85\cdot80\cdot200\,\text{ns}\cdot48\,\text{kHz}=\mathbf{0.653\ W}$$

### 3.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}=40\,\text{nC}\cdot25.2\cdot48\,\text{kHz}=\mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 3.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss}=\tfrac12 C_{oss,er}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw}=0.5\cdot1.5\,\text{nF}\cdot635\cdot48\,\text{kHz}=\mathbf{0.023\ W}$$

### 3.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs:

$$P_{g}=2\,Q_gV_{gs}f_{sw}=2\cdot60\,\text{nC}\cdot10\cdot48\,\text{kHz}=\mathbf{0.058\ W}$$

### 3.6 Switching total per motor

$$P_{sw,motor}=1.935+0.653+0.048+0.023=\mathbf{2.66\ W}\quad(+0.058\text{ W in driver})$$
