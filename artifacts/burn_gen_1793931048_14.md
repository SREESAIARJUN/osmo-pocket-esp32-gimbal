# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #21): Architecture, Loss Model, Thermal Limits, DShot DMA Plan

## 0. Feasibility Findings

The numbers below show that **80 A per motor cannot be sustained on a nano (20×20 mm) 4-in-1.** The board's thermal mass limits an 80 A event to about **2–3 s**, starting warm and with no forced convection. Its continuous dissipation capacity supports roughly **20–25 A per motor**. Marketing an "80 A burst" rating requires a defined pulse duration (≤2 s) and ambient temperature. Otherwise you need to move to a 30.5×30.5 mm layout.

**Architecture assumption.** The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. It runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600. Each motor has its own ESC MCU (AT32F421 running AM32) and a 3-phase gate driver. This matches standard 4-in-1 practice. Running four sensorless FOC/6-step loops at 48 kHz on the H743 while it also runs EKF3 is not a sound design.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (6-Step, Synchronous)

### 1.1 Current path
In any commutation sector, one phase is the PWM leg and one is the low-side return.
- During **D**: HS(x) → motor → LS(y).
- During **1−D**: LS(x) (synchronous rectification) → motor → LS(y).

So there are **always exactly two FETs in series** with the phase current:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph,rms}^2\, R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

With trapezoidal drive and low ripple, I_ph,rms ≈ I_ph (DC-like during the sector).

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R = 1.6 × (1 + 0.0045·85) = **2.21 mΩ**

### 1.3 Ripple correction
Phase-current ripple is set by motor inductance L_ph (two phases in series):

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{2L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}, \qquad I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}$$

Example: 2207-class motor, L_ph ≈ 15 µH, D = 0.9, V_bus − V_bemf ≈ 4 V. This gives ΔI ≈ 2.5 A, so the ripple term is about 0.5 A², which is negligible at 80 A.

### 1.4 PCB copper loss
Assume 2 oz inner/outer, effective path ~25 mm long × 6 mm wide × 4 layers:

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{A} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8} \times 0.025}{4 \times 6\times10^{-3} \times 70\times10^{-6}} \approx 0.26\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.5 Totals at 80 A, T_j = 110 °C

| Term | Equation | Per channel |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 2 × 80² × 2.21 mΩ | **28.3 W** |
| PCB copper | 80² × 0.26 mΩ | 1.7 W |
| **Sum** | | **≈ 30.0 W** |

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Hard switching occurs only on the PWM leg. The synchronous LS commutates through its body diode (ZVS-like turn-on).

| Mechanism | Equation | Value at 25.2 V, 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap (HS) | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·30 ns·48 k | **1.45 W** |
| Reverse recovery (LS diode → HS turn-on) | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 30 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.036 W |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss·V·f_sw = 25 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.030 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_SD·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k | **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (per channel) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw = 2·40 nC·10·48 k | 0.04 W (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g) |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.17 W** |

**Per-channel total at 80 A ≈ 32.2 W.** That is about 93 % conduction loss.

**Implications:**
- Raising f_sw to 96 kHz adds only about 2 W per channel.
- Halving R_ds(on) saves about 14 W per channel.
- **Use the lowest R_ds(on) part that fits; do not optimize switching speed.**
- Dead time should be ~50–100 ns, and the switching node should be cleaned up for EMI and ringing, not for loss.

### 2.1 Bus capacitor ripple (non-negligible at 80 A)

$$I_{C,rms} = I_{ph}\sqrt{D(1-D)}$$

- Worst case at D = 0.5: **40 A_rms per channel**.
- Four channels interleave only loosely, so plan for ≥ 25 A_rms total.
