# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Loss, Thermal, and BDShot DMA Design

**Scope and assumptions.** Values marked **[A]** are design assumptions. Values marked **[V]** must be verified against the datasheet or reference manual of the exact part revision you buy. The numbers are worked engineering estimates, not a substitute for bench validation with thermocouples or an IR camera.

## 0. Feasibility Check

Read this before committing the layout. The physics below shows that **"80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is only achievable as a short single-motor transient**. It cannot be a sustained four-motor condition. With all four motors at 80 A and full duty:

- The battery current reaches about 320 A, which is about 5× the XT60 rating.
- Power-stage dissipation is about 95 W into roughly 2.5 J/K of board.

The model therefore defines **burst as a time-limited quantity** (Section 3.4). The firmware current limit and the datasheet rating should reflect that.

The architecture also has to be split correctly between two processors:

| Function | Processor |
|---|---|
| 48 kHz power-stage PWM, commutation, current sensing | ESC MCU(s), e.g. AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32 **[A]** |
| ArduPilot, DShot600 generation, bidirectional eRPM capture | STM32H743 |

The H743 never generates the 48 kHz PWM.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C this gives R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.7) = **1.7 mΩ**.

### 2.2 Instantaneous conduction per motor

In each 60° commutation sector two legs carry current:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for a fraction D of each period and the low-side FET (synchronous rectification) for 1 − D.
- **Fixed leg:** the low-side FET is held on continuously.

$$P_{cond,motor}= I^2R_{ds}\,[D+(1-D)] + I^2R_{ds} = 2\,I^2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This result is independent of duty cycle, which is a property of synchronous 6-step drive.

At burst conditions:

- 2 × 80² × 1.7 mΩ = **21.8 W per motor**
- Over four motors: **87 W**

Add the copper path (shunt plus traces):

- Assume a 0.25 mΩ shunt **[A]** plus 0.3 mΩ of 2 oz copper path: 6400 × 0.55 mΩ ≈ **3.5 W per motor**.

### 2.3 Per-FET average over one electrical cycle

Each leg spends one third of the cycle as the PWM leg, one third as the fixed-low leg, and one third floating.

$$\bar P_{HS}=\tfrac{D}{3}I^2R_{ds},\qquad \bar P_{LS}=\tfrac{(1-D)+1}{3}I^2R_{ds}$$

At D → 1 (burst):

- High-side FET: P_HS ≈ 10.9 / 3 ≈ **3.6 W**
- Low-side FET: P_LS ≈ **3.6 W**

The low-side FET is the hot device at partial throttle. At D = 0.5 it carries 2.5 / 3 of I²R.
