## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

The brief has three problems that change the analysis.

1. **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz PWM.** In any practical 4-in-1 design, each motor (or the whole ESC) has a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. The ESC MCU might be an AT32F421 or STM32G071. That MCU does commutation, the 48 kHz complementary PWM, and dead-time. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to the ESCs only over bidirectional DShot600. So the switching-loss model belongs to the ESC power stage, and the DMA model belongs to the H743.
2. **80 A burst on a "nano" board is thermally marginal.** Section 3 shows this quantitatively: it holds for about 1–2 s with all four motors at 80 A. It is not a continuous rating.
3. **Assumed operating point:** 6S battery at worst case (25.2 V). The MOSFET is a 40 V, ~0.7–1.0 mΩ, 5×6 mm DFN part (e.g., TPHR7404PU class). Parameters are listed below. Substitute your actual datasheet values. The equations are general.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value used |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Burst phase current (per motor) | I | 80 A |
| R_DS(on) max @ 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | R_ds25 | 0.90 mΩ |
| R_DS(on) temperature factor @ T_j ≈ 110 °C | k_T | 1.45 |
| PCB copper resistance per conduction path | R_pcb | 0.25 mΩ |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (per motor, 6 FETs)

### 1.1 Current path
With six-step (trapezoidal) commutation and synchronous rectification (complementary PWM), there are always **exactly two FETs in series** with the motor current:

- During the PWM ON time: high-side (HS) of phase A and low-side (LS) of phase B.
- During the PWM OFF time: LS of phase A (synchronous rectification) and LS of phase B.

The conduction path therefore always contains two R_DS(on) plus copper, independent of duty cycle.

### 1.2 Hot R_DS(on)

$$R_{ds,hot} = k_T \cdot R_{ds25} = 1.45 \times 0.90 = 1.305\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Total conduction loss per motor

$$P_{cond,FET} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds,hot} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.305\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{16.70\ W}$$

$$P_{cond,PCB} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{pcb} = 6400 \times 0.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{3.20\ W}$$

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution
Each phase leg conducts for 2/3 of the electrical cycle. Averaged over the commutation cycle, the 16.7 W spreads over 6 FETs:

$$\bar{P}_{cond/FET} \approx 16.70 / 6 = 2.78\ \text{W}$$

The instantaneous peak in a single FET is higher, and this matters for junction transient analysis:

$$P_{FET,peak} = I^2 R_{ds,hot} = 8.35\ \text{W}$$

### 1.5 Thermal runaway check
Because R_DS(on) rises with T_j, iterate:

$$T_j = T_c + P(T_j)\,R_{\theta jc}, \quad P(T_j) = I^2 R_{ds25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

Take α ≈ 0.0053 /K, which matches k_T = 1.45 at 110 °C, and R_θjc ≈ 1.2 K/W. The loop gain is

$$G = I^2 R_{ds25}\,\alpha\,R_{\theta jc} = 6400 \times 0.9\text{m} \times 0.0053 \times 1.2 = 0.037 \ll 1$$

The loop is stable at the junction level. Runaway risk exists only at the board level, through the board-to-air thermal resistance (see §3).

## 2. Switching Loss Model @ 48 kHz (per motor)

In six-step operation, only one leg is PWM-switching at any instant. There is one hard-switched HS/LS pair per motor.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 35\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.69\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two dead-times per PWM period)

$$P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$
