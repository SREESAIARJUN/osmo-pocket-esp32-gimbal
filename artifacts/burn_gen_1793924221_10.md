# STM32H743 AIO / 4-in-1 ESC: Loss, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

## 0. Architectural Clarifications

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In both ArduPilot and Betaflight AIO designs:
- The **STM32H743** is the flight controller. It generates bidirectional DShot600 and runs ArduPilot.
- A **separate ESC MCU per motor or per board** handles commutation and the 48 kHz PWM. Typical choices are AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32.

The model below is partitioned that way. If you intend the H743 to commutate motors directly, ArduPilot does not support it, and the architecture changes entirely.

**2. "80 A burst" on a nano board is a transient rating, not a thermal steady state.** Section 3 shows that a 20×20 mm board at 4×80 A survives only about 1–2 s. Treat the published burst number as time-limited, and define the time limit explicitly in the spec.

**3. The netlists in §5 are reference topology.** They are not production-released. They require layout-specific SI/PI and thermal validation.

| Symbol | Value | Notes |

| I_burst | 80 A per channel | Phase current, trapezoidal |

| α_R | 0.0055 /K | R_ds tempco (≈1.5× at 115 °C) |

| Q_rr | 50 nC | Body diode |
| C_oss,eff | 2 nF | Energy-equivalent |
| V_f (body diode) | 0.8 V | At 80 A, hot |
| t_dead | 100 ns | Per edge |
| V_drv / R_g,total | 10 V / 3 Ω | Driver + gate resistor + internal R_g |
| V_plateau | 3.0 V | |
| R_Cu,phase | 0.25 mΩ | Bus + phase copper per conducting path |

**Topology.** Each channel is a 3-phase bridge using 120° trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM on the active leg. At any instant, two bridge positions carry the full current:
- One static low-side FET.
- The PWM leg, where the high-side and its synchronous low-side complement share duty D and (1−D).

## 2. Loss Model (Per Channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

Temperature-dependent on-resistance:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha_R(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 115 °C: R_ds = 0.8 × (1 + 0.0055 × 90) = **1.20 mΩ**.

Because the PWM leg is always conducting through either the high-side or its synchronous complement, the current path always contains two FETs:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2(80^2)(1.20\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{15.4\ W}$$

Each FET averages one third of this over an electrical cycle (120° conduction), which is about 5.1 W/FET averaged. The instantaneous dissipation while conducting is 7.7 W.

Copper loss: P_Cu = I²·R_Cu = 6400 × 0.25 mΩ = **1.6 W**.

### 2.2 Switching Loss (Hard-Switched Edges on the PWM Leg)

Gate current during the Miller interval:

$$I_g = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_g} = \frac{10-3}{3} = 2.33\ A$$

Ideal transition time:

$$t_{tr} = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_g} = \frac{20\text{ nC}}{2.33\text{ A}} \approx 8.6\text{ ns}$$

Common-source inductance and driver sag stretch this. Use **t_r = t_f = 15 ns** as a layout-realistic value.

| Mechanism | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 30 ns × 48 k | **1.45 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 50 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.06 W |
| C_oss (2 FETs in the leg) | 2 × ½·C_oss·V²·f_sw | 0.06 W |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_f·I·2t_dead·f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k | **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (driver/R_g) | 2·Q_g·V_drv·f_sw | 0.09 W |
| **Total switching-related** | | **≈ 2.27 W** |

### 2.3 Totals

$$P_{ch} = P_{cond}+P_{Cu}+P_{sw} \approx 15.4+1.6+2.3 = \mathbf{19.3\ W}$$

$$P_{4ch} \approx \mathbf{77\ W}\ \text{(at 4×80 A burst)}, \quad \eta \approx 1 - \frac{77}{4\cdot 80\cdot 25.2\cdot D}$$

For reference, the same math at 35 A continuous and T_j = 90 °C gives 2 × 35² × 1.09 mΩ + 0.31 + ~0.9 ≈ **3.9 W/channel**, or 15.6 W total.

**Design implications:**
- Conduction losses dominate (~80%). Lowering R_ds (paralleling FETs, or using a 0.5 mΩ class part) pays off far more than faster edges.
- At 48 kHz, dead time costs about 0.6 W/channel. AM32 dead-time tuning and Schottky-like body-diode FETs are worth doing.
- Going from 48 to 96 kHz roughly doubles P_sw, adding about 2.3 W/channel. That is acceptable for motor smoothness only if the thermal budget allows it.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network (Per FET to Ambient)

```
Tj ──[Rth_jc 0.6 K/W]── Tc ──[Rth_cb 1.5 K/W: solder + vias]── T_board ──[Rth_ba]── T_amb
                                                               │
                                                        C_board (thermal mass)
```

### 3.2 Board Steady State: Impossible at Burst

| Board | Area (2 sides) | Rth_ba, prop-wash ≈ 3 m/s | ΔT at 77 W |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20×20 mm | 8 cm² | ≈ 4.5 K/W | 346 K ✗ |
| 30.5×30.5 mm | 18.6 cm² | ≈ 2.2 K/W | 170 K ✗ |

Neither board reaches an acceptable steady state, so burst current must be time-limited.

### 3.3 Transient (Lumped Capacitance)

Thermal mass:
- 20×20 board, 8-layer, 2 oz outer copper: ≈ 2.0 J/K, including FETs and connectors.
- 30.5×30.5 board: ≈ 4.5 J/K.

Board temperature over time:

$$T_b(t) = T_{b0} + P\,R_{ba}\left(1-e^{-t/\tau}\right),\qquad \tau = R_{ba}C_b$$

**20×20 board:** τ = 4.5 × 2.0 = 9 s.
