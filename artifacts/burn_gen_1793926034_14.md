## 0. Architecture Assumptions

The model rests on one key architectural fact. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** A 4-in-1 ESC needs four dedicated ESC MCUs, one per motor, running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to those four MCUs. The 48 kHz switching therefore happens in the ESC MCUs and gate drivers, not in the H743.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step with complementary PWM

In any 60° sector, one phase is high (HS PWM at duty D, LS complementary at 1−D) and one phase is low (LS fully on). The phase current I passes through two channels at every instant:

- During D: HS_A → motor → LS_B.
- During 1−D: LS_A (synchronous rectification) → motor → LS_B.

Duty D therefore drops out of the bridge total:

$$
P_{cond,bridge} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0059/\text{K} \;\Rightarrow\; 2.25\ \text{m}\Omega \text{ at } 110\,^\circ\text{C}
$$

### 1.2 Numbers at 80 A burst

$$
P_{cond,bridge} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.25\times10^{-3} = 28.8\ \text{W}
$$

Within its 120° conduction window, the worst single FET (the HS during a high-duty burst) carries about I²R = **14.4 W**. Averaged over an electrical cycle, each conducting device sees roughly ⅓ of that, about 4.8 W. However, at low eRPM the window is long relative to the FET thermal time constant, so design to the **instantaneous 14.4 W**.

### 1.3 Copper and shunt losses

| Element | Resistance | Loss @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase/bus copper per channel (2 oz, short pours, via stitched) | ~0.4 mΩ effective | 2.6 W |
| Current-sense shunt on shared bus (320 A total) | 0.1 mΩ | 10.2 W, shared → design-limiting |

**Recommendation:** use per-channel 0.25 mΩ shunts (1.6 W each at 80 A), or a Hall sensor (e.g. ACS-class or TMCS1100-class with an external field path) for the battery current measurement. A single shared shunt at 320 A is not survivable on this board.
