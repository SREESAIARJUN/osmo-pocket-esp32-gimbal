## 0. Architecture Corrections (Read First)

Three framing points affect everything downstream.

1. **The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In a production AIO board, the H743 is the flight controller running ArduPilot. Each of the four ESC channels has its own small motor-control MCU, for example an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or an EFM8 running BLHeli_S. That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 talks to the ESC MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. Running FOC/6-step for four motors plus ArduPilot on one H743 is not supported by ArduPilot.
2. **The 48 kHz switching frequency and the DShot600 rate are unrelated domains.**
   - 48 kHz is the ESC bridge switching frequency (Section 3).
   - 600 kbit/s is the FC→ESC command link (Section 5).
3. **80 A burst × 4 on a "nano" board is a burst-only rating.**
   - 4 × 80 A from a 6S pack is about 320 A and about 7 kW, beyond any XT60 or nano-class pack.
   - Section 4 shows the board survives roughly 1–4 s at full burst, not continuously.
   - Specify: **80 A per channel, ≤ 1 s burst, ≤ 25 % duty-cycle repetition, 35–45 A continuous with prop-wash airflow.**

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

R_ds(Tj) = R_ds25 · [1 + α(Tj − 25)]

At a design-point Tj = 110 °C:

R_ds = 1.0 mΩ × (1 + 0.006 × 85) = **1.51 mΩ**

### 2.2 Current paths in 6-step synchronous PWM

In any commutation sector, current flows through two series switch positions:

- the PWM leg, which alternates between its HS FET (duty D) and its LS FET (1 − D), and
- the static LS FET of the return phase.

Under synchronous rectification, a FET carries the current at every instant, so:

P_cond,ch = 2 · I_ph² · R_ds(Tj), independent of D

P_cond,ch = 2 × 80² × 1.51 mΩ = **19.3 W per channel**

Per-FET average: each FET is active about 1/3 of the electrical period across the two roles, so 19.3 W / 6 ≈ **3.2 W average**. The instantaneous peak in the active sector is 80² × 1.51 mΩ ≈ 9.7 W.

### 2.3 Copper interconnect loss

R_cu = ρ·L / (w·t)

- ρ = 1.72e-8 Ω·m
- 2 oz copper: t = 70 µm
- Effective path L ≈ 15 mm, w ≈ 6 mm, with 2 paralleled layers

R_cu ≈ 1.72e-8 × 0.015 / (0.006 × 70e-6 × 2) ≈ 0.31 mΩ, which is about 0.45 mΩ hot.

P_cu = I² · R_cu ≈ 6400 × 0.45 mΩ ≈ **2.9 W per channel**

This term is not negligible. Copper loss reaches about 30 % of FET conduction loss.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Both of its FETs commutate once per period.

**(a) V–I overlap, hard-switched edge**

P_VI = ½ · V_bus · I_ph · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw
= 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 kHz = **1.94 W**

**(b) Output capacitance**

P_oss ≈ Q_oss · V_bus · f_sw = 40 nC × 25.2 × 48k = **0.048 W**

**(c) Body-diode reverse recovery**

P_rr = Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw = 30 nC × 25.2 × 48k = **0.036 W**

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction**

P_dt = V_SD · I_ph · 2·t_dt · f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48k = **0.61 W**

**(e) Gate drive** (dissipated mainly in the driver and gate resistors)

P_g = 2 · Q_g · V_drv · f_sw = 2 × 60 nC × 10 × 48k = **0.058 W**

**Switching subtotal per channel: P_sw ≈ 2.65 W**

### Frequency sensitivity

dP_sw/df_sw ≈ 55 µW/Hz.

Moving from 24 kHz to 96 kHz adds about 2.6 W per channel at 80 A. At 48 kHz, conduction dominates by roughly 7:1, so **R_DS(on) and copper are the levers, not f_sw.** Going above 48 kHz is justified only for low-inductance motors that need it to keep current ripple down.
