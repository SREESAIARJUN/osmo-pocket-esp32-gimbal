# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Systems Architecture Model (Job #2)

## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility Flag

I don't have a prior spec for Job #2, so every model below is parameterized. The worked numbers use the assumptions in the table; substitute your actual datasheet values.

| Parameter | Symbol | Assumed value |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | V_bus | 6S, 25.2 V max (22.2 V nominal) |
| Burst phase current, per motor | I_pk | 80 A |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| MOSFET | — | 40 V, R_ds(on) = 1.2 mΩ @ 25 °C, 3.3×3.3 mm package |
| R_ds(on) temperature factor | k_T | 1.6 at T_j ≈ 125 °C |
| Rise / fall time | t_r, t_f | 20 ns / 20 ns |
| Reverse recovery charge | Q_rr | 50 nC |
| Output capacitance | C_oss | 1.5 nF |
| Total gate charge | Q_g | 50 nC |
| Dead time | t_dt | 100 ns |
| Body-diode forward voltage | V_f | 0.8 V |
| Commutation | — | Trapezoidal six-step with synchronous complementary PWM (AM32/BLHeli_32 style) |
| Architecture | — | H743 runs ArduPilot. Each ESC channel is driven by its own ESC MCU (e.g. STM32G071 or AT32F421 running AM32). |

The H743 cannot commutate the motors itself; ArduPilot does not contain ESC firmware.

> **⚠️ Feasibility flag: 80A on a "nano" (20×20 mm) board.**
> The math in §1–§3 shows roughly 110 W of dissipation with all four motors at 80 A. A 20×20 board can sustain about 1 second of that before hitting thermal limits. Commercial 20×20 4-in-1 boards are typically rated 20–35 A continuous for exactly this reason.
> Treat "80 A" strictly as a single-motor, short-duration burst rating, or move to 30.5×30.5 mm or larger with a heatsink. 40 V FETs on 6S also leave limited margin for regenerative and ringing spikes.
