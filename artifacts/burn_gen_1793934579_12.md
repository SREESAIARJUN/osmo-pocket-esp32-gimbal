# STM32H743 4-in-1 ESC Nano Flight Controller (Job #57): Mathematical Model and Reference Design

## 0. Architectural Corrections

These items change the design before any math is done.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot is flight-control firmware. It produces DShot frames and consumes eRPM telemetry. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, for example 4× AT32F421 or STM32G071. The 48 kHz figure is the ESC-MCU PWM frequency, not something ArduPilot generates. The H743's job is DShot600 generation and bidirectional capture only.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano board (20×20 or 30×30 mm) is a transient rating, not a thermal steady state.** The model in §3 shows that four channels at 80 A dissipate about 90 W. Board temperature then rises at about 23 K/s, so the honest rating is roughly 2–3 s for all motors together.
3. **Use two separate boards** (FC + ESC stack) or a deliberately partitioned AIO. Gate-drive return currents above 40 A and 48 kHz switching edges must not share ground copper with the IMU or the H743 analog section.

All component values below are parametric assumptions. Substitute datasheet values for your chosen FET, then re-run the equations.

## 2. Loss Model per ESC Channel

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

In 6-step operation with synchronous rectification, phase current always flows through exactly **two** conducting channels:
- either the high-side FET or its complementary low-side FET in the PWM leg, and
- the low-side FET of the return leg.

$$
P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,k(T_j)
$$

At T_j ≈ 100 °C:

$$
P_{cond} = 2(80)^2(1.4\,\text{m}\Omega) = \mathbf{17.9\ W}
$$
