**Recommendation:** A "nano" (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm mounting) AIO cannot honestly be rated at 80 A for a typical 10 s burst. Choose one of three options:

- (a) Rate it at **35 A continuous / 80 A for ≤ 2 s**.
- (b) Move to a **30.5×30.5 mm** board with a bonded heatsink.
- (c) Keep 80 A but add a **≥ 6 g aluminium spreader** (see §3.5).

The rest of this document shows the derivation so the rating can be defended or adjusted.

## 2. Loss Model

### 2.1 Conduction Loss With Electrothermal Feedback

In 6-step synchronous operation, two phase legs carry the phase current at every instant:

- **PWM leg:** current flows through the high-side FET for duty D and its synchronous low-side FET for 1−D. Exactly one of the two conducts at any time (dead time ignored here).
- **Return leg:** current flows through the low-side FET of the sinking phase, which stays fully on.

The current therefore always passes through two R_DS(on) in series, independent of D:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS}(T_j)=R_{25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_DS = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0055 × 85) = **1.47 mΩ ≈ 1.5 mΩ**

$$P_{cond}=2\times 80^2\times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ channel}$$

**Distribution across the six FETs.** Over one electrical revolution, each phase sources for 120° and sinks for 120°. The 19.2 W therefore averages to about **3.2 W per FET**. The instantaneous worst-case FET dissipates I²·R = 80² × 1.5 mΩ = **9.6 W** while it is conducting.
