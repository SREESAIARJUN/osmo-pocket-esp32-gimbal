## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step with synchronous rectification

During any commutation sector, phase X is PWM'd and phase Y is held low. With duty cycle D:

- **On-time (D):** current flows through HS_X → motor → LS_Y.
  P = I²·R_HS + I²·R_LS = 2I²R
- **Off-time (1−D), synchronous rectification:** current flows through LS_X → motor → LS_Y.
  P = 2I²R

Therefore, independent of D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph,rms}^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j) \;+\; I_{ph,rms}^2\,R_{Cu}$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence of R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\text{–}0.008\ /\text{K}$$

At T_j = 100 °C with α = 0.0067: R ≈ 1.5 × R_25 = **1.5 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| Term | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 2 × 80² × 1.5 mΩ | **19.2 W / motor** |
| PCB copper and pads (R_Cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ loop) | 80² × 0.3 mΩ | 1.9 W / motor |
| Per-FET average (6 FETs/motor) | 19.2 / 6 | **3.2 W / FET** |

**Worst-case per-FET loss:** at low D, the PWM-leg low-side carries current for (1−D) of its sector. Size the worst-case LS FET at about 1.3–1.5× the per-FET average, i.e. **≈4.5 W**.

**Coupled equation (requires iteration).** R depends on T_j, and T_j depends on the losses:

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}\,Z_{th,jc}(t), \qquad P_{FET} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Solve by fixed-point iteration. It converges in 3–4 steps.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. That is one turn-on and one turn-off per PWM period, per motor.

### 2.1 Loss components

**(a) V–I overlap:**
$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw}$$

The transition time is dominated by the Miller plateau:
$$t_{r,f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g}}, \qquad I_g \approx \frac{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}{R_g + R_{drv}}$$

With Q_gd = 10 nC and an effective I_g ≈ 0.7 A: t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 15 ns.

**(b) Reverse recovery (body diode of the complementary FET):**
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw}$$

**(c) Output capacitance:**
$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw}$$

**(d) Dead-time diode conduction (two edges per cycle):**
$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\,I_{ph}\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw}$$

**(e) Gate charge (two FETs per leg; this power dissipates mostly in the driver and R_g):**
$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw}$$

### 2.2 Numbers at 25.2 V, 80 A, 48 kHz

| Term | Value |
|---|---|
| P_sw = ½·25.2·80·30 ns·48 k | **1.45 W** |
| P_rr = 50 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.06 W |
| P_oss = ½·40 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.02 W |
| P_dt = 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k | **0.61 W** |
| P_g = 2·50 nC·10·48 k | 0.05 W |
| **Switching subtotal** | **≈2.2 W / motor** |

**Design insight.** At 48 kHz, conduction loss dominates (~19 W vs ~2 W). Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw:

- Going to 24 kHz saves about 1.1 W per motor but increases current ripple and acoustic noise.
- Reducing dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per motor.
