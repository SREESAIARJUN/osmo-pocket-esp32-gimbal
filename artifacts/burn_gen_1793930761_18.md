## 0. Architecture Corrections (Read First)

**1. The H743 cannot also be the ESC.** ArduPilot on the H743 cannot run four channels of sensorless commutation (ADC/comparator zero-cross detection at 48 kHz PWM) alongside the flight stack. The correct architecture is:

- **FC MCU:** STM32H743, running ArduPilot.
- **ESC MCUs:** 4× AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071GBU6, running AM32, one per motor.
- **Link:** bi-directional DShot600 from the H743 to each ESC MCU.

**2. "80 A burst" on a nano board is thermally marginal.** At 80 A per motor, the board dissipates roughly 130 W. Without heavy airflow, a 30.5 mm board has a thermal mass of only about 7 J/K. The analysis below shows a realistic burst of **about 1 s**, not 10 s. The numbers are derived rather than assumed, so you can re-run them with your actual MOSFET datasheet.

**Baseline assumptions** (replace with your datasheet values):

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Bus | 6S, V_bus max = 25.2 V |

| R_ds(on) temperature coefficient | ×1.5 at T_j = 125 °C, so R_hot = 2.4 mΩ |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths

In six-step drive, at any instant two phases carry the motor current I_m and one floats.

- **PWM leg:** the high-side (HS) FET conducts for duty fraction D. The low-side (LS) FET conducts synchronously for 1 − D.
- **Return leg:** the LS FET conducts continuously.

There is always exactly one FET in series in each active leg, so:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Including inductor current ripple ΔI:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_m^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

The ripple term is small and is neglected here.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha(T_j - 25)\right], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

This gives R_ds(125 °C) = 1.6 × 1.5 = **2.4 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Results at 80 A burst

$$P_{cond,ch} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 2.4\,\text{m}\Omega = 6400 \times 0.0048 = \mathbf{30.7\ W/channel}$$

Each phase is active for 2/3 of the electrical period, so the 30.7 W is distributed over 6 FETs:

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx 30.7/6 \approx \mathbf{5.1\ W/FET}$$

The peak instantaneous value is higher, but over a commutation cycle (well under 1 ms) it averages out thermally.

### 1.4 Additional series losses (same current path)

| Element | R | P @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase pad / trace (2 oz, ~4 mΩ round trip) | 0.4 mΩ effective | 2.6 W |
| Current shunt (shared, 0.25 mΩ; carries total I_bus, not I_m) | — | 0.25 mΩ × I_bus² (see §3) |

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The HS turn-on and turn-off edges are hard-switched, and the LS FET commutates softly through its body diode during dead time.

### 2.1 Loss components

**(a) V–I overlap, hard switching:**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_m (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**(b) Body-diode reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

**(c) Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = 0.015\ \text{W}$$

**(d) Dead-time diode conduction (two edges per period):**

$$P_{dt} = V_f I_m (2t_d) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive** (dissipated in the driver and R_g, not in the FET):

$$P_g = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} \times 2 = 40\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = 0.038\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Totals per channel

$$P_{sw,ch} \approx 1.94 + 0.05 + 0.02 + 0.61 = \mathbf{2.6\ W}$$

### 2.3 Sensitivity of P_sw to design choices

| Change | Effect on P_sw |
|---|---|
| 24 kHz PWM | halves P_sw (−1.3 W). More current ripple and audible noise. |
| t_r, t_f → 10 ns | (a) drops to 0.97 W. Ringing and EMI increase; needs a tighter layout. |
| Dead time → 50 ns | (d) drops to 0.31 W. Requires verified driver propagation-delay matching. |

**Conclusion:** at 80 A, conduction dominates (about 92 %). Switching optimisation is secondary. Spend design effort on R_ds(on) and copper.

| **Total** | | **≈ 161 W** |

**Efficiency check.** Motor input power is 25.2 × 256 ≈ 6.45 kW, so η_ESC ≈ 97.5 %. This is plausible.

**Battery side.** 256 A from a 6S pack is extreme. Verify that the pack, the XT60/XT90 connector, and the 12 AWG leads can sustain it. Most nano-class "80 A" ratings mean *per motor, momentary, one motor at a time*.
