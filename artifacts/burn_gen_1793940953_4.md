# STM32H743 Nano AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC): Design Package

## 0. Architectural Ground Rules and Corrections

Three facts shape the rest of this package:

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** A 4-in-1 ESC needs four dedicated ESC MCUs, typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 (or BLHeli_32-class firmware). They generate the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot600 to them. The switching-loss model below therefore applies to the ESC stage. The DMA model applies to the H743.

2. **"80A burst" on a nano board is a transient rating, not a thermal steady state.** The math in §3 shows that four motors at 80 A on a 20×20 mm board saturate in about a second. I quantify this rather than assume it away. If 80 A must be a real per-motor figure, the board must grow to 30.5×30.5 mm with 5×6 mm FETs.

3. **All component parameters below are representative.** Before release, replace them with values from the specific FET and driver datasheets you select. The equations are exact. The numbers are only as good as their inputs.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

**Temperature-dependent on-resistance**

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 2.5 mΩ × 1.45 ≈ **3.6 mΩ**.

**Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification**

- At any instant, two legs are active.
- The PWM'd leg alternates between its high-side FET (for duty D) and its low-side FET (for 1−D). Either way, one FET in that leg conducts the full current I.
- The other active leg holds one FET fully on.
- So exactly **two FETs always carry I**, independent of duty cycle.

**Per-motor conduction loss**

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 3.6\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{46.1\ W}$$

**Per-FET loss**

- Instantaneous, while conducting: I²R = 23 W.
- Averaged: each FET carries current for 1/3 of the electrical cycle, split between D and 1−D. Its time-average is roughly 23 W × (1/3) × D_eff ≈ **4–8 W**.

**Electrothermal coupling**

R_ds depends on T_j, and T_j depends on loss, so the system must be solved iteratively:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_{ref} + P(T_j^{(n)})\,Z_{th}$$

Iterate until the values converge. If

$$2 I^2 R_{ds,25}\,\alpha\,Z_{th} \ge 1$$

the loop has no stable solution and the FET runs away thermally.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches hard, so there is one hard-switching node per motor.

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = ½ · 25.2 · 80 · 40 ns · 48 kHz | 1.94 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f · I · 2t_dt · f_sw = 0.8 · 80 · 200 ns · 48 kHz | 0.61 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw = 30 nC · 25.2 · 48 kHz | 0.036 W |
| Coss | ½ · Q_oss · V · f_sw | 0.015 W |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver/R_g) | 2 · Q_g · V_gs · f_sw | 0.029 W |
| **Total switching, per motor** | | **≈ 2.6 W** |

**Total loss per motor**

$$P_{motor} = 46.1 + 2.6 \approx \mathbf{48.7\ W}$$

**Four motors simultaneously**

$$P_{total} \approx \mathbf{195\ W}$$

Add roughly 2–4 W for copper and shunt I²R losses.

**Design insight:** conduction loss dominates about 18:1. Going from 24 kHz to 48 kHz costs only about 1.3 W per motor. Lowering R_ds or improving the copper pays off far more than adjusting frequency.

### 4.2 Adiabatic board heating

Burst durations are short, so convection is negligible and the board heats adiabatically:

$$\frac{dT}{dt} = \frac{P_{total}}{C_{th}} = \frac{195}{3.1} \approx 63\ \text{K/s}$$
