## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

With 6-step commutation, current flows in series through one high-side and one low-side path at any instant. With synchronous PWM, the PWM'd leg alternates between its HS FET (on-time, fraction D) and LS FET (off-time, fraction 1−D). The current therefore always sees **two** channel resistances, independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = I^2 \cdot 2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

Temperature dependence is applied self-consistently:

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha = 0.0048\ \mathrm{K^{-1}}$$

### 1.2 Evaluated at 80 A burst

At the design-point T_j = 125 °C, R = 0.90 × 1.48 = **1.33 mΩ**.

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.33\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{17.0\ W\ per\ ESC}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution (hot-spot driver)

- The worst-case FET is the one conducting continuously across its 120° conduction window.
- Its average share is roughly I²·R·(1/3) over a full electrical cycle.
- Thermal time constants of a PDFN die (~ms) are comparable to the electrical period at low eRPM, so the peak instantaneous dissipation matters:

$$P_{FET,peak} = I^2 R = 80^2 \times 1.33\,\text{m} = 8.5\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}(8.5) + \text{switching share} \approx 3.3\ \text{W}$$

### 1.4 Copper & shunt losses

Shunt loss:

$$P_{shunt} = 80^2 \times 0.25\,\text{m}\Omega = 1.6\ \text{W}$$

Trace loss: with 2 oz outer copper (R_□ ≈ 0.25 mΩ/□ at 25 °C, ×1.4 hot ≈ 0.35 mΩ/□) and roughly 1.5 squares of effective path per phase pair, inner layers stitched in parallel (≈3 layers effective):

$$P_{trace} \approx 80^2 \times \frac{1.5 \times 0.35\,\text{m}}{3} \approx 1.1\ \text{W}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches: one HS/LS pair per sector.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap

Transition time is limited by Miller charge:

$$t_r = \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,src}} = \frac{9\,\text{nC}}{0.6\,\text{A}} = 15\ \text{ns},\qquad t_f = \frac{9\,\text{nC}}{1.0\,\text{A}} = 9\ \text{ns}$$

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 22.2 \times 80 \times 24\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{1.02\ W}$$

At V_max (25.2 V): **1.16 W**.

> Add ~30% for di/dt-induced voltage overshoot from loop inductance (≈1.5 nH layout) → **1.3–1.5 W** design value.

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2 t_d \cdot f_{sw} = 0.85 \times 80 \times 300\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.98\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 45\,\text{n} \times 22.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

Q_rr scales with di/dt and I_F. Derate ×2 at 80 A → **0.10 W**.

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss} V^2 f_{sw} \times 2 = 900\,\text{p} \times 22.2^2 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.021\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in driver + R_g, not in the FET)

$$P_{gate} = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2 = 55\,\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\,\text{k} \times 2 = 0.053\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Frequency sensitivity

$$\frac{\partial P_{sw,total}}{\partial f_{sw}} \approx \frac{1.5 + 0.98 + 0.10}{48\,\text{kHz}} \approx 54\ \text{mW/kHz}$$

- Dropping to 24 kHz saves ~1.3 W per ESC at 80 A.
- The cost is higher current ripple and audible noise.
- 48 kHz is justified for low-inductance 2–3" motors.
