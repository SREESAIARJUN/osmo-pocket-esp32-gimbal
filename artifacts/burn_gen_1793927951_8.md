## 1. Control Architecture

```
               ┌──────────── per joint j ∈ {yaw, roll, pitch} ─────────────────────────────┐
θ_ref(1 kHz) → │ Angle PID → ω_ref ──(+ω_ff)→ Velocity PID → i_q,fb ─(+i_q,cog +i_q,J)→ i_q*  │
               │     ↑ θ̂ (IMU attitude → joint frame)  ↑ ω̂ (fused gyro → joint axis)        │
               │                                                       Current PI (d,q) 20kHz│
               │   encoder θ_m → θ_e (commutation)      ← Clarke/Park ← i_a,i_b ← SVPWM     │
               └───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Feedback sources are separated by purpose:

- **Commutation and cogging phase** use the joint encoder, because both depend on rotor angle.
- **Velocity feedback** uses the fused inertial gyro rate projected onto each joint axis. This rejects base disturbances (aircraft or vehicle motion) directly.
- **Angle feedback** uses the IMU attitude estimate converted to joint-space error.

## 2. FOC Equations

### 2.1 Clarke and Park Transforms

With amplitude-invariant scaling and the constraint i_a + i_b + i_c = 0:

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \frac{1}{\sqrt3}(i_a + 2 i_b)
$$

$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d \\ i_q\end{bmatrix}=
\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha \\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}
$$

### 2.2 Electrical Angle with Delay Compensation

The encoder sample, computation and PWM update together introduce about 1.5 T_s of latency. Compensate by predicting the angle forward:

$$
\theta_e = p\,(\theta_m - \theta_{\text{off}}) + \hat\omega_e \cdot 1.5\,T_s
$$
