# Job #58: STM32H743 Nano AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC
## Electrical, Thermal and Firmware Integration Model (Rev A)

---

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Up-Front Risk Flags

The 4-in-1 ESC does **not** run on the STM32H743. The H743 is the flight controller. It sends bi-directional DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs, and each ESC MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM for its own motor.

| Parameter | Baseline value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal | 4S results are given in §2.6 |
| Burst current | 80 A **per motor**, 5 s burst | Worst case. The 80 A-total case (20 A/motor) is in §2.6 |
| PWM | 48 kHz complementary (synchronous rectification) | Typical AM32 / BLHeli_32 setting |
| Commutation | Trapezoidal, 6-step | FOC changes the conduction expressions (see §2.2) |
| MOSFETs | 40 V N-ch, PDFN 3.3×3.3, R_ds(on) = 1.0 mΩ at 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | Generic class. Verify against the chosen datasheet |
| ESC MCU | AT32F421 class running AM32 | Alternatives: STM32G071, EFM8 (BLHeli_S lacks 48 kHz plus bidir eRPM on all builds) |
| Gate driver | 3-phase bootstrap driver, FD6288Q class | Its V_CC must accept your 10–12 V gate rail |
| Ambient | 35 °C | Inside the frame, no prop wash during a punch-out |

> **⚠ Risk flag 1 (thermal):** 80 A per motor on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board **cannot be sustained**. The model below shows it is survivable only as a short burst of about 3–5 s, with active copper spreading and prop-wash convection. Plan the product rating as "80 A burst / 35–40 A continuous" only after thermal validation (see §6).
>
> **⚠ Risk flag 2 (voltage):** For 6S, use 40 V FETs. 30 V parts leave too little margin for inductive ringing during regenerative braking.

## 2. Power-Stage Loss Model

### 2.1 Notation

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| I | Motor (phase) current during a commutation interval, ≈ the DC-link current divided by D |
| D | PWM duty |
| f_sw | 48 kHz |
| R_ds(T) | R_ds,25 · [1 + α(T_j − 25)], with α ≈ 0.005 /K (gives ×1.5 at 125 °C) |
| t_r, t_f | Current rise and fall times at the switch node |
| t_dt | Dead time |
| Q_rr, Q_oss, Q_g | Reverse-recovery, output and gate charges |

### 2.2 Conduction Losses (Trapezoidal, Complementary PWM)

In each 60° commutation step:

- **Leg A (PWM leg):** the high side conducts for D·T_s and the low side, as synchronous rectifier, for (1−D)·T_s.
- **Leg B (return leg):** the low side conducts 100%.
- **Leg C:** floating.

The PWM leg always carries I through exactly one FET, so the per-motor conduction loss is independent of D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Over a full electrical cycle, each FET carries current for about 1/3 of the time. The electrical frequency at 80 A is roughly 1–3 kHz, which is much faster than the board thermal constant. Average the loss over the cycle for case and board temperature, but not for die ripple.

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} \approx \frac{2 I^2 R_{ds}}{6} = \frac{I^2 R_{ds}}{3}$$

**Numbers (I = 80 A, T_j = 125 °C, R_ds = 1.5 mΩ):**

- P_cond,motor = 2 · 6400 · 0.0015 = **19.2 W**
- P_cond,FET,avg = **3.2 W**
- Instantaneous loss in one conducting FET = I²R = **9.6 W** (relevant for die-level ripple)

Add the copper and shunt losses:

- **Shunt:** 0.5 mΩ low-side shunt, conducting about 2/3 of the time: P ≈ (2/3) · 80² · 0.0005 = **2.1 W**
- **Copper:** 2 oz inner/outer planes, phase path ≈ 0.3 mΩ: P ≈ 2 · 80² · 0.0003 = **3.8 W**

(*FOC note: with sinusoidal currents, P_cond = 3·I_rms²·R_ds, using the phase RMS current.*)

### 2.3 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-leg high side hard-switches. The low side commutates softly, through its body diode, during dead time.

**(a) V·I overlap (hard switching):**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

With t_r + t_f = 20 ns: 0.5 · 25.2 · 80 · 20e-9 · 48e3 = **0.97 W**

**(b) Body-diode reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw}$$

With Q_rr = 40 nC at 80 A: 40e-9 · 25.2 · 48e3 = **0.05 W**. Snubber and ringing losses scale with this term.

**(c) Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw}$$

With Q_oss = 30 nC (counting both FETs' C_oss charging in the leg): **0.036 W**

**(d) Dead-time diode conduction:**

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I \,(2 t_{dt}) f_{sw}$$

With V_F = 0.8 V and t_dt = 100 ns: 0.8 · 80 · 200e-9 · 48e3 = **0.61 W**

**(e) Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors):**

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw}\ \text{per switching FET pair}$$

With Q_g = 50 nC at 10 V: 2 · 50e-9 · 10 · 48e3 = **0.048 W**

**Switching total per motor: ≈ 1.7 W.**

**Frequency sensitivity:** about 1.7 W is proportional to f_sw. At 96 kHz this becomes ≈ 3.4 W per motor; at 24 kHz, ≈ 0.85 W. At 80 A, 48 kHz is a reasonable compromise between switching loss and current ripple.
