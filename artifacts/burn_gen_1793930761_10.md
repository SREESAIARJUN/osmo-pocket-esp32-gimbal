# STM32H743 AIO "Nano" FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal, and DShot Architecture Model (Job #37)

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Red Flags

I'm flagging these first because they change the design more than any equation below.

| # | Issue | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | The H743 should not commutate the motors. Production 4-in-1 boards use a dedicated ESC MCU per motor running AM32 or BLHeli_32, such as the AT32F421 or STM32G071. | The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to 4 ESC MCUs and receives eRPM telemetry. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs. |
| A2 | 80 A per motor on a 20×20 mm "nano" board is not thermally sustainable (see §3). | 80 A is treated as a burst rating of ≤1–2 s. Continuous ratings will be about 35–45 A with airflow. |
| A3 | 4 × 80 A means a battery current of up to about 300 A at full throttle. | An XT60 or XT90 connector and a 12 AWG pigtail cannot carry this. The real system burst will be limited by battery and wiring, not by the FETs. |
| A4 | Bus voltage | 6S, V_bus = 25.2 V maximum. FETs rated 40 V. |
| A5 | Reference FET | 40 V, 5×6 mm PDFN. R_DS(on) = 1.0 mΩ at 25 °C, Q_g = 60 nC, Q_rr = 50 nC, C_oss ≈ 1.5 nF, R_θJC = 0.8 °C/W. Substitute your actual part's datasheet values. |

Every numeric result below derives from these assumptions. Re-run them with your selected BOM.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)
$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ /^\circ\mathrm{C}
$$
At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS ≈ 1.0 × 1.6 = **1.6 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current path in trapezoidal 6-step with synchronous rectification
At any instant, current flows through exactly two FETs in series:
- one high-side (HS) FET, which is either PWM-on or replaced by its complementary low-side during the off-time, and
- one low-side (LS) FET.

Per channel:
$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)
$$
Averaged over 6 FETs per bridge (each carries current about 1/3 of the electrical period):
$$
P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}}{3}
$$

### 1.3 Numbers at I_ph = 80 A, T_j = 125 °C
- I² = 6400 A²
- P_cond,ch = 2 × 6400 × 1.6 mΩ = **20.5 W per motor channel**
- P_cond,FET ≈ **3.4 W**
- Four channels: **≈ 82 W**

### 1.4 Copper and interconnect (often forgotten)
$$
P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{trace},\qquad R_{trace} = \frac{\rho\,L}{w\,t}
$$
For 2 oz copper (70 µm), a 10 mm × 3 mm phase pour gives R ≈ 0.8 mΩ, so P ≈ 5 W per phase path at 80 A. Use 3–4 oz internal planes, stitched vias, and solder-filled phase pads.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches in each channel.

### 2.1 V–I overlap
$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw}
$$
With t_r + t_f = 40 ns: 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 kHz = **1.94 W**

### 2.2 Output capacitance
$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 × 1.5\text{ nF} × 635 × 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.023\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Body-diode reverse recovery
$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 50\text{ nC} × 25.2 × 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}
$$

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction
There are two dead-time intervals per PWM period:
$$
P_{dt} = V_F\,I_{ph}\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 × 80 × 200\text{ ns} × 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in the driver and gate resistors)
$$
P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 × 60\text{ nC} × 10 × 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.058\ W}
$$

### 2.6 Loss budget per channel at 80 A, 125 °C

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction | 20.5 |
| V–I switching | 1.94 |
| Dead-time | 0.61 |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.14 |
| **Total (FETs)** | **≈ 23.2 W** |
| **× 4 channels** | **≈ 93 W** (+ about 20 W copper) |

**Design levers:**
- Conduction dominates at about 88%, so a lower R_DS(on) beats a faster switching edge.
- Dropping to 24 kHz saves only about 1.3 W per channel. It also raises current ripple and acoustic noise, so 48 kHz is justified.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Steady state (Cauer chain)
$$
T_j = T_a + P_{FET}\,R_{\theta JC} + P_{board}\,R_{\theta BA}
$$
- **Junction-to-case:** 3.9 W (worst HS FET: conduction plus switching) × 0.8 = 3.1 K. This is negligible; the die is not the problem.
- **Board-to-ambient:** a 20×20 mm, 6-layer board in prop wash has R_θBA ≈ 8–12 K/W.
- **Result:** 113 W × 10 K/W ≈ **1100 K rise**. Steady-state 80 A on all four channels is physically impossible.

**Continuous rating.** Solve for T_j ≤ 110 °C at T_a = 40 °C with R_θBA = 10 K/W. This allows about 7 W total. Using P ∝ I², a ≈ 10 W budget gives I_cont ≈ 80·√(10/113) ≈ **24 A per channel** without a heatsink. With an aluminium heat spreader and direct prop wash (R_θBA ≈ 3 K/W), the continuous rating is about 40 A.
