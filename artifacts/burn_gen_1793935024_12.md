## 0. Architecture Corrections and Scope

Before the math, three points change the design materially.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot runs on the H743 and sends bidirectional DShot600 commands. Each 4-in-1 ESC still needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. This design uses 4 × AT32F421 running AM32, one per motor. Putting FOC/6-step for four motors plus ArduPilot on one H743 is not supported by ArduPilot and is not proposed here.
2. **"80 A burst" must be bounded.** This document defines it as **80 A per motor, 4 motors simultaneously, at 6S, for a duration *t_b***. Section 3 shows that a 20 × 20 mm "nano" board **cannot** sustain this for a meaningful time. The thermal model is used to derive the allowable burst duration rather than assume one.
3. **The numeric values are worked examples.** Device parameters are representative of a 40 V, ~1 mΩ PQFN 5×6 MOSFET class. Replace them with your selected part's datasheet values; all equations stay valid.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation Topology

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM. At any instant two phases carry current:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for fraction *D* and the sync low-side for *(1 − D)*. Exactly one FET of this leg carries *I* at all times (dead time excepted).
- **Static leg:** the low-side FET is on for the whole 60° sector.

Therefore exactly **two FETs are in the current path at all times**.

### 1.2 Temperature-Dependent R_ds(on)

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1} \Rightarrow k_T(125^\circ\text{C}) = 1.6
$$

### 1.3 Per-Motor Conduction Loss

$$
P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 \left(2\,R_{ds}(T_j) + R_{Cu,ph}\right)
$$

- FET term: 80² × 2 × 1.6 mΩ = **20.5 W**
- Copper term (phase plus bus path), with R_Cu,ph ≈ 0.5 mΩ on 6-layer 2 oz
