# STM32H743 AIO Nano: 4-in-1 ESC Power Stage and DShot Architecture

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

**The H743 does not commutate the motors.** A 4-in-1 ESC needs four dedicated ESC MCUs (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32), one per motor. Each needs its own 3-phase sensorless field-oriented or 6-step loop. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to those four MCUs.

The numbers below rest on these assumptions. Substitute your actual BOM values.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Battery (6S, full) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Burst phase current per motor | I_ph | 80 A |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| MOSFET (40 V, 3×3 / 5×6 DFN class) | R_DS(on),25 | 1.0 mΩ |
| R_DS(on) temperature coefficient (25→125 °C) | k_T | 1.5 |
| Total gate charge | Q_g | 30 nC |
| Output charge | Q_oss | 25 nC |
| Reverse-recovery charge (body diode) | Q_rr | 40 nC |
| Rise + fall (driver ~1 A) | t_r + t_f | 20 ns |
| Dead time (each edge) | t_dt | 200 ns |
| Body-diode forward voltage | V_F | 0.8 V |
| Gate drive voltage | V_GS | 10 V |
| Junction–case thermal resistance | R_θJC | 1.5 K/W |
| Max junction temperature | T_J,max | 150 °C (175 °C rated) |

Topology: three half-bridges per motor, 24 MOSFETs total. Complementary synchronous PWM is used, with 6-step-equivalent current flow (two phases conducting at any instant).

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_J) = R_{DS(on),25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_J - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_J = 125 °C: R = 1.0 × 1.5 = **1.5 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Per-motor conduction loss

Phase current always flows through one high-side and one low-side path in series. With synchronous rectification, the freewheel path is also a MOSFET channel, not a diode. Therefore:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_J)$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each FET conducts for 120° of each 360° electrical cycle, a duty of 1/3. Averaged over the cycle:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \tfrac{1}{3} I_{ph}^2 R_{DS(on)} = \tfrac{1}{3}(6400)(1.5\text{ m}\Omega) = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

Check: 6 FETs × 3.2 W = 19.2 W ✔

### 1.4 Copper and interconnect losses

These are often larger than the FET losses on a nano board. Assume 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner planes on the phase and bus paths, with an effective path resistance of about 0.5 mΩ per motor at temperature:

$$P_{Cu,motor} = I^2 R_{path} = 6400 \times 0.5\text{ m}\Omega = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

Battery-lead and pad losses for the shared bus come later, in Section 3.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switching FET in the active leg incurs hard-switching loss. The complementary FET switches with near-zero voltage after dead time.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard switching)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I_{ph}\,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}$$

$$P_{VI} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{e-}9 \times 48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{0.968\ W}$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 25\text{e-}9 \times 25.2 \times 48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{0.030\ W}$$

### 2.3 Body-diode reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 40\text{e-}9 \times 25.2 \times 48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction (two edges per period)

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I_{ph}\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{e-}9 \times 48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{1.229\ W}$$

Dead time costs more than switching. Tuning it from 200 ns down to 80–100 ns with a fast driver roughly halves this term.

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET. Two FETs are switching in the PWM leg:

$$P_{gate} = 2\, Q_g\, V_{GS}\, f_{sw} = 2 \times 30\text{e-}9 \times 10 \times 48\text{e}3 = 0.029\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Switching total per motor

$$P_{sw,motor} = 0.968 + 0.030 + 0.048 + 1.229 + 0.029 \approx \mathbf{2.30\ W}$$

### 2.7 Sensitivity to frequency

All switching terms scale linearly with f_sw:

$$P_{sw}(f) = 2.30 \times \frac{f}{48\text{ kHz}}\ \text{W}$$

| f_sw | P_sw per motor | Total for 4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| 24 kHz | 1.15 W | 4.6 W |
| 48 kHz | 2.30 W | 9.2 W |
| 96 kHz | 4.60 W | 18.4 W |

At 80 A, conduction dominates switching by about 8:1. Raising PWM frequency for smoother low-throttle behaviour is cheap; cutting R_DS(on) is what matters at burst.

## 3. Total Burst Power Budget

| Loss term | Per motor | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| MOSFET conduction (125 °C) | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching (48 kHz) | 2.3 W | 9.2 W |
| Phase copper | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| Bus copper + pads (320 A, ~0.15 mΩ) | — | 15.4 W |
| Gate drivers + ESC MCUs + LDOs | — | ~1.5 W |
| **Total** | | **≈ 116 W** |

Efficiency at burst:

- Electrical input ≈ 25.2 V × 320 A × duty. At full throttle with motor back-EMF, input power is roughly 6–7 kW.
- η ≈ 98% electrically. That is fine.
- The problem is not efficiency. The problem is dumping 116 W into a 20×20 mm or 30×30 mm board.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Steady-state junction temperature (per FET)

$$T_J = T_{board} + P_{FET}\, R_{\theta JC} + P_{FET}\, R_{\theta CB}$$

Worst-case FET (the PWM-switching high side):

$$P_{FET} = 3.2 + 0.97 + 1.23/2 \approx 4.8\ \text{W}$$

With R_θCB ≈ 2 K/W (solder plus local copper):

$$\Delta T_{J-board} = 4.8 \times (1.5 + 2.0) = \mathbf{16.8\ K}$$

The junction-to-board rise is benign. **The board temperature is the limiting factor.**

### 4.2 Board steady state: lumped convection

For a 30×30 mm board in prop wash at about 10 m/s, with h ≈ 60 W/m²K and two-sided area A ≈ 1.8×10⁻³ m²:

$$R_{\theta,board-air} = \frac{1}{hA} = \frac{1}{60 \times 1.8\text{e-}3} \approx 9.3\ \text{K/W}$$

$$\Delta T_{board,ss} = 116 \times 9.3 \approx 1080\ \text{K}$$

**80 A continuous on all four motors is physically impossible on a nano board.** Treat 80 A strictly as a transient rating.

### 4.3 Transient (burst) model: first-order RC

Board thermal capacitance, assuming an 8 g assembly (FR4 + copper + FETs, c_eff ≈ 0.75 J/gK):

$$C_{th} \approx 8 \times 0.75 = 6\ \text{J/K}$$

$$\tau = R_{\theta} C_{th} = 9.3 \times 6 \approx 56\ \text{s}$$

$$T_{board}(t) = T_0 + P\,R_\theta\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right) \approx T_0 + \frac{P}{C_{th}}\,t \quad (t \ll \tau)$$

The heating rate is 116 / 6 ≈ **19.3 K/s**.

Allowable burst duration for T_board,max = 110 °C, leaving about 17 K of headroom to the junction (T_J ≈ 127 °C, consistent with the 125 °C R_DS(on) assumption):

| Starting board temp | Allowed burst |
|---|---|
| 40 °C (cruise-heated) | (110−40)/19.3 ≈ **3.6 s** |
| 60 °C (hot day / after aggressive flying) | ≈ **2.6 s** |
| 80 °C | ≈ **1.6 s** |
