## 1. Conduction Loss Model

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, at any instant exactly two FETs carry the phase current in series:
- During the on-time: high side of phase A plus low side of phase B.
- During the off-time: the low side of phase A freewheels in place of the high side.

**Path loss per ESC channel:**

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot 2 R_{ds}(T_j) + I^2 R_{Cu}$$

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\cdot k_T = 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.5 = 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{FET,cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = 6400 \times 3.0\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

**Copper loss.** Assume a phase plus bus trace and via resistance of R_Cu ≈ 0.5 mΩ per channel path:

$$P_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

**Per-FET distribution.** Each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical period. The 2·I²R term spread across 6 FETs gives:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{19.2}{6} = 3.2\ \text{W average per FET}$$

The instantaneous loss while conducting is I²R = 9.6 W per FET. The FET's thermal capacitance smooths this at commutation rates above about 100 Hz electrical.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-chopped leg switches hard. With complementary PWM, one hard-switched transition pair occurs per PWM period.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\times25.2\times80\times40\text{ns}\times48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{ns}\times48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{nC}\times25.2\times48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

This also causes ringing and EMI that can exceed its loss contribution. Snubbers and layout matter more than the watts.

### 2.4 Output-capacitance loss

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5\times1.5\text{nF}\times635\times48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.02\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in the driver and gate resistor, not the FET)

$$P_g = 2 \cdot Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2\times50\text{nC}\times10\times48\text{kHz} = 0.048\ \text{W per channel}$$

### 2.6 Switching total and sensitivity

$$P_{sw,total} \approx 1.94 + 0.61 + 0.06 + 0.02 = \mathbf{2.63\ W per channel}$$

Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. Changing f_sw from 48 to 24 kHz saves about 1.3 W per channel at 80 A. Conduction dominates by roughly 7:1, so **Rds(on) and copper are the levers, not frequency.**

## 3. Total Power Budget at 80 A Burst

| Loss term | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Copper (PCB) | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| Switching (all terms) | 2.6 W | 10.5 W |
| Driver and gate | 0.05 W | 0.2 W |
| Bus capacitor ESR (see §5) | ~0.5 W | ~2 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 25.5 W** | **≈ 102 W** |

Output power is about 4 × 80 A × 25.2 V × D. At D ≈ 0.85 this is roughly 6.9 kW, so efficiency is about 98.5%. The absolute dissipation is nonetheless huge for a 30 mm board.

**Hottest-FET loss.** The chopped high side carries conduction plus most of the switching loss:

$$P_{FET,max} \approx 3.2 + \frac{2.63}{3} \approx \mathbf{4.1\ W}$$
