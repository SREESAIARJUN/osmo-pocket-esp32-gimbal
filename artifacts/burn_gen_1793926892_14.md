## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility

Neither a MOSFET part number nor a board size was specified, so every number below uses an explicit assumed parameter. When the real BOM is fixed, substitute datasheet values into the same equations.

### 0.1 Architecture decision

The H743 runs ArduPilot and **does not commutate motors**. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 (assumed AT32F421, ×4), driven by bidirectional DShot600 from the H743. This is the only architecture in which ArduPilot's BDShot stack applies.

### 0.2 Feasibility warning: "nano" vs. 80 A

- **4 × 80 A burst means up to 320 A from the battery** at full simultaneous throttle.
- A 20×20 mm nano footprint **cannot** carry that. Battery pads, copper cross-section and bulk capacitance physically do not fit.
- This design therefore assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm, 8‑layer** board. On 20×20 mm, derate to roughly 35–45 A burst per channel.
- **80 A is treated as a burst (≤5 s)**, not a continuous rating. The thermal section quantifies this.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Topology current paths

The model assumes 6‑step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous (complementary) PWM. In any commutation sector (A+ → B−):

- **PWM ON (duty D):** Q_AH + Q_BL conduct.
- **PWM OFF (1 − D):** Q_AL (synchronous rectifier) + Q_BL conduct.

So **exactly two FETs carry I at every instant**, independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS,hot} \;(1+\tfrac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12 I^2})$$
