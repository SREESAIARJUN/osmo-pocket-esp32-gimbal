# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #16): Thermal, Switching and DShot Design Package

## 0. Architecture Decision and Feasibility Flags

Three issues must be resolved before layout begins. They drive everything that follows.

| # | Issue | Resolution |
|---|-------|------------|
| 1 | **The H743 should not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot is flight-control firmware. It does not run ESC commutation, and the H743 has no qualified 6-step or FOC ESC firmware for 4 × 48 kHz bridges. | Use the H743 as the flight controller. Each motor gets a dedicated ESC MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071) running **AM32**, which supports bidirectional DShot and 48 kHz PWM. The H743 talks to them over bidirectional DShot600. |
| 2 | **80 A on a nano footprint (20×20 to 30×30 mm) is transient only.** | The model below shows about 115 W of total dissipation with all four motors at 80 A. That is only survivable for about 1–2 s. Specify **80 A as a ≤1 s burst** and 30–35 A continuous, with firmware current and temperature limiting. |
| 3 | **Bus voltage is not specified.** | Assumed 6S: V_bus = 25.2 V maximum. Use 40 V MOSFETs, never 30 V, because of inductive overshoot. |

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Conduction topology

The scheme is 6-step trapezoidal with synchronous (complementary) PWM, as AM32 does by default. During each 60° commutation sector, one phase is PWM'd high-side, one is held low-side, and one floats.

| Device | Conduction fraction |
|---|---|
| PWM high-side FET | D |
| Its complementary low-side FET | 1 − D |
| Static low-side FET (other phase) | 1 |

Current *I* always flows through exactly two R_DS(on) in series. The bridge conduction loss is therefore independent of duty:

$$
P_{cond,bridge} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

### 2.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)]
$$

This makes the model self-consistent: a higher T_j raises R_DS, which raises loss, which raises T_j. Iterate to convergence.

| Iteration | T_j assumed | R_DS (mΩ) | P_cond, bridge (W) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 25 °C | 1.50 | 19.2 |
| 1 | 90 °C | 2.04 | 26.1 |
| 2 | 105 °C | 2.16 | 27.6 |
| 3 (converged) | 108 °C | 2.18 | **27.9** |

### 2.3 Per-FET average

Each FET carries current for 2 of 6 sectors (static low-side or PWM pair). Averaged over the electrical cycle, the loss distributes across the 6 FETs:

$$
P_{cond,FET,avg} = \frac{27.9}{6} \approx 4.65\ \text{W}
$$

**Worst-case instantaneous: static low-side during its sector.**
- P = I²·R = 6400 × 2.18 mΩ = **13.95 W** for that sector.
- At 20 000 eRPM, a sector lasts about 0.5 ms.
- The die thermal time constant is about 1–5 ms, so the die partially sees this peak.
- Add a ripple term of **+8–12 K** to T_j.

### 2.4 Copper and interconnect conduction

| Path | Approximate R |
|---|---|
| Battery pad → bus plane (2 oz, short) | 0.15 mΩ |
| Phase pours, FET → motor pad | 0.25 mΩ × 2 conducting phases |
| Current shunt (if used) | 0.25 mΩ |
| **Total** | ≈0.9 mΩ |

$$
P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.9\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 5.8\ \text{W per motor}
$$

Most of this heat goes into the same board mass. Do not ignore it.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd phase leg switches. Losses are per motor, at V = 25.2 V and I = 80 A.

### 3.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = \tfrac12 (25.2)(80)(45\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = 2.18\ \text{W}
$$

### 3.2 Body-diode reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V\, f_{sw} = 40\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}
$$

This is small because the dead time is short. Q_rr grows with di/dt, so recheck it on the bench.
