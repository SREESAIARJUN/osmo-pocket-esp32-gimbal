## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

Three points in the brief need correcting before the math, because they change the model.

1. **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz PWM.** On a 4-in-1 ESC, each channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, for example the AT32F421 or STM32G071. That MCU runs six-step commutation at 48 kHz. The H743 runs ArduPilot and only emits DShot600 frames and captures the bidirectional eRPM replies. Switching loss is therefore an ESC-side quantity. The DMA budget is an FC-side quantity.
2. **"80 A" is defined here as peak motor phase current per channel, held for a burst of ≤ 2 s.** Battery current is lower: I_bat ≈ D·I_ph.
3. **"Nano" with 80 A cannot be a continuous rating.** The math below shows the board's continuous capability is about 25–30 A per channel. Treat 80 A as a transient rating only.

| Parameter | Value | Note |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in six-step with synchronous rectification

In each 60° sector exactly two phases conduct. Take one phase leg over a full electrical cycle:

- **High-side FET:** on for its 120° window × D. Conduction fraction = D/3.
- **Low-side FET:** on for its own 120° sink window (1/3), plus freewheeling during the off-time of its own high-side window ((1−D)/3).

The conduction loss per leg is therefore:

$$
P_{\text{leg}} = I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\left[\frac{D}{3} + \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1-D}{3}\right] = \frac{2}{3} I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

For a full channel (three legs, six FETs):

$$
\boxed{P_{\text{cond,ch}} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)}, \qquad R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

This result is independent of D, which is correct: at every instant there are two FETs in series in the current path. The split between FETs does depend on D, though:

$$
P_{HS} = \frac{D}{3} I^2 R, \qquad P_{LS} = \frac{2-D}{3} I^2 R
$$

The low-side FET is the hottest device whenever D < 1, for example at low-duty punch-out or in a stalled motor.

### 1.2 Numbers at 80 A

Assume T_j = 100 °C, so R_ds = 1.2 mΩ × 1.3375 = **1.605 mΩ**.

| Quantity | D = 1.0 | D = 0.5 | D = 0.2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| P_cond per channel | 20.5 W | 20.5 W | 20.5 W |
| Per-leg loss | 6.85 W | 6.85 W | 6.85 W |
| P_HS (one FET) | 3.42 W | 1.71 W | 0.68 W |
| P_LS (one FET) | 3.42 W | 5.14 W | 6.16 W |

### 1.3 Electro-thermal fixed point

Because R_ds rises with T_j, solve for T_j iteratively:

$$
T_j = T_{pcb} + R_{\theta,j\text{-}pcb}\cdot P_{FET}(T_j)
$$

This converges only if R_θ · ∂P/∂T < 1. For the worst-case LS FET at D = 0.2 with R_θ,j-pcb = 3 K/W (package plus local copper spreading):

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial T} = 6.16 \times \frac{0.0045}{1.3375} = 0.0207\ \text{W/K}
$$

The loop gain is 3 × 0.0207 = 0.062, so the iteration is stable (no thermal runaway at the FET level).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg hard-switches. Its high-side FET sees V–I overlap, the reverse recovery of the opposite body diode, and C_oss discharge. Its low-side FET sees dead-time body-diode conduction.

$$
P_{sw} = \underbrace{\tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f}_{\text{V–I overlap}} + \underbrace{Q_{rr} V f}_{\text{recovery}} + \underbrace{\tfrac12 Q_{oss} V f}_{\text{C}_{oss}} + \underbrace{V_f I\,(2t_{dt}) f}_{\text{dead time}}
