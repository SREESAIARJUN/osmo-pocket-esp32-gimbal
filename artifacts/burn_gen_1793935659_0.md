# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #65): Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, DShot DMA Allocation, and Netlists

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Design Flags

### 0.1 System architecture

Commercial 4-in-1 ESCs do not drive motors from the flight-controller MCU. The usual split is:

- **Flight controller:** STM32H743 running ArduPilot. It generates bidirectional DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry.
- **ESC:** four local ESC MCUs (AT32F421 or STM32G071) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Each MCU commutates its own three half-bridges at 48 kHz.

This document models that architecture. The H743 never sees the 48 kHz PWM.

### 0.2 Baseline parameters

The MOSFET values below are representative of the 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN parts used in 4-in-1 ESCs. Replace them with your actual BOM datasheet values.

### 0.3 Design flags

- **Board size vs. current:** "80 A × 4" on a 20×20 or 30×30 nano board is a **burst** rating. It is thermally impossible as a continuous rating (shown in §2).
- **Validation:** treat every number here as a model. Validate it with thermal-camera and thermocouple testing (§2.5).

## 1. Electrical Loss Model (Per ESC Channel)

### 1.1 Commutation topology

In 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM, two phases carry current at any instant:

- **Leg A (PWM leg):** the high-side FET conducts for D·T. The low-side FET conducts for (1−D)·T as a synchronous rectifier.
- **Leg B (return leg):** the low-side FET conducts for 100% of T.

At any instant, exactly two channels conduct the full phase current. This holds regardless of D.

### 1.2 Conduction loss with self-heating

R_ds(on) at junction temperature:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha_R (T_j - 25)]$$

Channel conduction loss:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Per-FET worst case is the return-leg low side, which conducts 100% of the time:

$$P_{cond,FET,max} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

These form a fixed-point problem, because T_j depends on P, which depends on T_j:

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}(T_j)\,(R_{th,jc}+R_{th,c\text{-}pcb})$$

**Closed form.** Substitute P = I²R₂₅[1+α(T_j−25)] and let R_θ = 2.2 K/W:

$$T_j = \frac{T_{pcb} + I^2 R_{25} R_\theta (1 - 25\alpha)}{1 - \alpha I^2 R_{25} R_\theta}$$

**Stability condition (thermal runaway):**

$$\alpha I^2 R_{25} R_\theta < 1$$

Evaluating with the baseline values:

$$0.005 \times 6400 \times 1.2\times10^{-3} \times 2.2 = 0.0845 \;\ll\; 1 \quad \text{(stable)}$$

**Worked example at T_pcb = 90 °C, I = 80 A:**

- I²R₂₅R_θ = 6400 × 1.2e-3 × 2.2 = 16.9 K
- T_j = (90 + 16.9 × 0.875) / (1 − 0.0845) = (90 + 14.8) / 0.9155 = **114.5 °C**
- R_ds(114.5 °C) = 1.2 × (1 + 0.005 × 89.5) = **1.74 mΩ**
- P_FET,max = 6400 × 1.74e-3 = **11.1 W**
- P_cond,channel = 2 × 11.1 = **22.3 W**

**Additional copper loss.** Include phase and bus copper. With roughly 0.3 mΩ of effective trace and via resistance in the current path:

$$P_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.3\times10^{-3} \approx 1.9\ \text{W per channel}$$

### 1.3 Switching losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches hard: one hard turn-on and one hard turn-off per period.

**V–I overlap:**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{e-9} \times 48\text{e3} = 0.97\ \text{W}$$

**Reverse recovery.** The synchronous FET's body diode conducts during dead time and then recovers:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = 40\text{e-9} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{e3} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

**Output capacitance (hard turn-on discharges C_oss):**

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 50\text{e-9} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{e3} = 0.030\ \text{W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period):**

$$P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2 t_{dead} f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{e-9} \times 48\text{e3} = 1.23\ \text{W}$$

> Dead-time loss exceeds V–I overlap loss here. Reducing t_dead to 100 ns, if the driver and FET propagation allow, saves about 0.6 W per channel. This is the cheapest efficiency gain in the design.

**Gate drive.** This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs. Only one leg is PWMing at a time:

$$P_g = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} \times 2 = 50\text{e-9} \times 10 \times 48\text{e3} \times 2 = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

**Switching subtotal:** P_sw ≈ 0.97 + 0.05 + 0.03 + 1.23 = **2.28 W per channel**

**Frequency scaling.** All switching terms scale linearly with f_sw:

$$P_{sw}(f) = 2.28 \times \frac{f}{48\text{ kHz}}\ \text{W}$$

At 96 kHz, switching loss rises to about 4.6 W per channel. Conduction loss still dominates.

### 1.4 Total loss at 80 A burst

| Term | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (T_j ≈ 115 °C) | 22.3 W | 89.2 W |
| Copper | 1.9 W | 7.6 W |
| Switching + dead time | 2.3 W | 9.1 W |
| Gate drive + ESC MCU + LDO | ~0.3 W | 1.2 W |
| **Total** | **~26.8 W** | **~107 W** |

**Efficiency check.** Input power at 80 A is about 25.2 × 80 × D_eff. At full throttle (P_in ≈ 2 kW per channel), η ≈ 98.7%. The problem is not efficiency. The problem is that roughly 107 W is dumped into a few grams of PCB.

**General per-channel model for any operating point:**

$$P_{ch}(I,T_j,f) = 2I^2R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)] + I^2 R_{Cu} + \tfrac12VI(t_r+t_f)f + (Q_{rr}+\tfrac12Q_{oss})Vf + 2V_fIt_{dt}f + 2Q_gV_{gs}f$$

## 2. Thermal Model

### 2.1 Network (Cauer approximation, per FET to board to ambient)

```
T_j --[R_jc 1.2]--+--[R_c-pcb 1.0]--+--[R_pcb-amb]-- T_amb
                  |                 |
               C_j (~0.05 J/K)   C_board (lumped)
```

### 2.2 Board heat capacity

For a 30.5×30.5 mm ESC, 8 layers of 2 oz copper, 1.6 mm thick:

- **Copper:** 8 × 30.5² mm² × 70 µm ≈ 0.52 cm³ → 4.7 g × 0.385 J/g·K = **1.8 J/K**
- **FR4:** about 1.2 cm³ → 2.2 g × 1.1 J/g·K = **2.4 J/K**
- **Components** (24 FETs, caps, connectors): about **1.3 J/K**

**Total C_board ≈ 5.5 J/K**

A 20×20 board has roughly 2.5 J/K.
