## 1. Commutation Model (Basis for Per-FET Loss Allocation)

The bridge uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM.

- In each 60° sector, one leg is PWM'd and one leg is held low.
- **The PWM'd leg:** the high-side (HS) FET conducts for a fraction D of each period. The low-side (LS) FET conducts for 1 − D, after dead time.
- **The held leg:** its LS FET conducts 100% of the sector.
- At every instant the phase current flows through exactly **two FET channels in series**. The dead-time intervals are the exception, when current flows through one FET plus a body diode.

Each FET is active for 120° of every 360° electrical cycle.

Thermal averaging over the electrical period is valid. For example, a 7-pole-pair motor at 40 krpm has an electrical frequency of 4.7 kHz. That gives a 213 µs period, which is far shorter than the roughly 1 ms junction thermal time constant.

## 2. Conduction Losses at 80 A

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds(on),25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At the design point T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{ds(on)} = 1.4\,\text{mΩ}\,(1 + 0.006\cdot 85) = 2.11\ \text{mΩ}$$

### 2.2 Current form factor

With trapezoidal commutation and adequate phase inductance, the phase current is approximately flat at I_pk during conduction.

I_rms per FET over its own conduction interval equals I_pk. Duty weighting is handled by the conduction fractions in Section 2.3.

### 2.3 Per-channel conduction loss

Two series FETs carry the full current at all times:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{pk}^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 2.11\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{27.0\ W}$$
