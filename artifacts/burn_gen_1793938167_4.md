# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80A-Burst 4-in-1 ESC: Systems Architecture Package (Job #3)

## 0. Feasibility Findings and Assumptions

Two issues change the design, so they come first.

**1. The STM32H743 is not the ESC motor controller.** In this architecture the H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller and generates bi-directional DShot600. Each motor channel on the 4-in-1 ESC has its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, which generates the 48 kHz PWM. The model below is partitioned that way.

**2. 80 A per motor burst is not credible on a 20×20 mm "nano" stack.** Section 2.6 shows the board would heat at about 28 K/s. I model both readings of the requirement:
- **Case A:** 80 A per motor burst.
- **Case B:** 80 A total pack burst, i.e. 20 A per motor.

## 1. System Partitioning

```
6S Pack ──► XT60 ──► 4-in-1 ESC (20×20 or 30×30)
                     ├─ 4× ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32)  ← 48 kHz PWM
                     ├─ 4× 3-phase gate drivers (or 12× half-bridge)
                     ├─ 24× MOSFETs
                     ├─ Current sense → CURR pad (aggregate)
                     └─ 8-pin JST-SH ──► FC
FC: STM32H743 (480 MHz core, 240 MHz APB timers @ 480 MHz core*)
     ├─ M1..M4 DShot600 bidirectional (single wire per motor)
     ├─ VBAT / CURR ADC
     └─ ESC telemetry UART (optional; bidir DShot makes it mostly redundant)
```

\*Timer kernel clock depends on the ArduPilot board clock tree. Many H743 hwdefs run at 400 MHz core with 200 MHz timer clock. I use **200 MHz** below.

## 2. Thermal and Loss Model, Per ESC Channel

### 2.1 Conduction Topology

In 6-step commutation, two phases conduct at any instant. With synchronous (complementary) PWM, the current in each active phase flows through exactly one FET of that leg at any moment: either the high-side FET or the low-side FET in synchronous freewheel.

Each FET belongs to an active leg for 1/3 of the electrical cycle. Per-FET conduction loss:

$$P_{cond,FET} = I^2 \cdot R_{DS(on)}(T_j)\cdot \tfrac{1}{3}\cdot\delta_{share}$$

Within its 1/3 window, the high-side and low-side split time by duty D. Summed over the leg, the share is 1. The simplest invariant is therefore the per-channel total:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\cdot I^2\cdot R_{DS(on)}(T_j) \quad\text{(two FETs carry } I \text{ at all times)}$$

**Case A (80 A):**

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.5\times10^{-3} = 19.2\ \text{W}$$

**Case B (20 A):**

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \cdot 20^2 \cdot 1.5\times10^{-3} = 1.2\ \text{W}$$

**Worst-case single FET** (low-side, low duty, synchronous rectification):

$$P_{FET,max} \approx I^2 R\cdot\tfrac{1}{3} = 3.2\ \text{W (A)},\quad 0.2\ \text{W (B)}$$

### 2.2 Shunt and Copper

$$P_{shunt}=I^2 R_{sh}=80^2\cdot0.5\,\text{m}\Omega=3.2\ \text{W (A)};\quad 0.2\ \text{W (B)}$$

For copper, assume 2 oz copper with an effective 0.4 mΩ path per channel, plus about 0.3 mΩ shared bus:

$$P_{Cu,ch}\approx I^2\cdot0.4\,\text{m}\Omega = 2.56\ \text{W (A)};\ 0.16\ \text{W (B)}$$

### 2.3 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only one leg is hard-switched at a time.

**(a) V–I overlap:**

$$P_{VI}=\tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}=\tfrac12\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot20\text{n}\cdot48\text{k}=0.968\ \text{W (A)};\ 0.242\ \text{W (B)}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery.** The body diode conducts during dead time and then recovers:

$$P_{rr}=V_{bus}Q_{rr}f_{sw}=25.2\cdot50\text{n}\cdot48\text{k}=0.060\ \text{W}$$

**(c) Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss}=\tfrac12 V_{bus}Q_{oss}f_{sw}\cdot 2=25.2\cdot40\text{n}\cdot48\text{k}=0.048\ \text{W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two edges per period):

$$P_{dt}=V_{SD}\,I\cdot2t_{dt}f_{sw}=0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{n}\cdot48\text{k}=0.614\ \text{W (A)};\ 0.154\ \text{W (B)}$$

**(e) Gate drive.** Two FETs are switched at 48 kHz per channel. The commutation-only FETs are negligible.

$$P_{g}=2\cdot Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw}=2\cdot60\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k}=0.058\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Per-Channel and Board Totals

| Loss term | Case A (80 A/motor) | Case B (20 A/motor) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 19.20 W | 1.20 W |
| Shunt | 3.20 W | 0.20 W |
| Copper | 2.56 W | 0.16 W |
| V–I switching | 0.97 W | 0.24 W |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.11 W | 0.11 W |
| Dead time | 0.61 W | 0.15 W |
| Gate drive | 0.06 W | 0.06 W |
| **Per channel** | **26.7 W** | **2.12 W** |
| **Board (×4)** | **≈107 W** | **≈8.5 W** |
| Efficiency at P_out ≈ 25.2 V × I_avg (D≈1) | ≈95 % | ≈99 % |

At 80 A, conduction dominates. Switching frequency matters little: dropping to 24 kHz saves only about 0.8 W per channel.

### 2.5 Junction Temperature (Hot FET)

Use a Foster thermal network: Z_th,jc(t) from the datasheet, plus PCB spreading.

For a DFN3.3, take R_th,jc ≈ 1.5 K/W and Z_th,jc(10 ms) ≈ 0.6 K/W. On a nano board, R_th,c-board ≈ 25 K/W per FET effective. This assumes thermal vias and shared copper, with neighbours also hot.

**Steady state:**

$$T_j = T_{board}+P_{FET}(R_{jc}+R_{via}),\qquad R_{via}\approx 3\ \text{K/W (8 vias, 0.3 mm)}$$

$$T_j = T_{board}+3.2\cdot4.5 = T_{board}+14.4\ \text{K}$$

The junction-to-board rise is not the problem. **The board temperature itself is the limit.**

### 2.6 Board Lumped Thermal Capacitance (Burst Limit)

For the nano board: mass m ≈ 6 g, mixed c ≈ 0.55 J/(g·K), giving C_th ≈ 3.3 J/K. With R_th,board-air at prop-wash airflow ≈ 6 K/W (20×20 mm, both sides):

$$T_{board}(t)=T_a+P R_{th}\left(1-e^{-t/\tau}\right),\quad \tau=R_{th}C_{th}\approx 20\ \text{s}$$

**Case A (P = 107 W):**
- Initial slope is about 32 K/s.
