# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC: Reference Architecture and Loss Models

This document covers the electro-thermal loss model, the bi-directional DShot600 timer/DMA plan for ArduPilot, a reference netlist, and a thermal verdict. It does not include production Gerbers.

## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility Warning

The datasheet values below are representative of 40 V PDFN3333/5×6 MOSFETs used in high-current 4-in-1 ESCs. Replace them with your actual part's datasheet values before signing anything off.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S full) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Burst phase current (per ESC channel) | I | 80 A |
| R_DS(on) @ 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | R₂₅ | 0.90 mΩ |
| R_DS(on) temp coefficient | α | 0.006 /K |
| Rise + fall (driver-limited) | t_r + t_f | 30 ns |
| Dead time (per transition) | t_dt | 100 ns |
| Body-diode forward voltage @ 80 A | V_F | 0.8 V |
| Reverse recovery charge | Q_rr | 40 nC |
| Output-capacitance energy at V_bus | E_oss | ~0.6 µJ |
| Total gate charge | Q_g | 60 nC @ 10 V |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| Bus shunt (per channel) | R_sh | 0.25 mΩ |
| Commutation | — | 6-step trapezoidal with synchronous rectification (AM32 / BLHeli_32 style) |

> **Architectural flag:** "Nano" FC stacks (20×20 mm) do not physically support 4 × 80 A. That would mean 24 MOSFETs plus 320 A of copper on about 4 cm². Section 4 shows that even a 30×30 mm board survives 80 A per channel only for a few seconds. I've modelled a **30.5×30.5 mm ESC** with an **H743 FC on a separate stacked board**. The H743 cannot sit on the ESC's 80 °C+ copper and stay within its 85 °C (–6 grade) ambient rating.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step drive
In any 60° commutation sector, one phase is driven high (PWM), one is held low, and one floats. During PWM-on, current flows through the HS FET of the driven leg and the LS FET of the low leg. During PWM-off, the same leg's LS FET synchronously freewheels the current.

**Two FETs therefore carry I at every instant (outside dead time):**

$$P_{cond,total} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

Per-device distribution over one electrical cycle, for each leg:

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,D\,I^2 R(T_j) \qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(2-D)\,I^2 R(T_j)$$

Low-side FETs run hotter. At full throttle (D → 1) the HS and LS losses are equal. At mid-throttle the LS loss is up to 2× the HS loss.

### 1.2 Temperature coupling
$$R(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 100 °C: R = 0.90 × 1.45 = **1.31 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Numbers @ 80 A, T_j = 100 °C

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 2·80²·1.31 mΩ | **16.8 W** |
| Shunt | 80²·0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| PCB copper (≈0.15 mΩ effective path) | 80²·0.15 mΩ | 1.0 W |
| **Conduction subtotal / channel** | | **19.4 W** |

Peak per-FET loss: a LS FET of a held-low leg dissipates I²R = **8.4 W** while conducting, averaged ≈ 3.6–5.6 W depending on D.

### 1.4 Thermal-runaway stability criterion
Conduction loss rises with temperature, so a positive feedback loop exists. It is stable if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\,R_{th,ja} = I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\,k\,R_{th,ja} < 1$$

Here k is the per-device duty share (≤ 2/3). With R_th,ja ≈ 25 K/W (device on shared copper, no airflow), the loop gain is:

6400 × 0.9e-3 × 0.006 × 0.67 × 25 ≈ **0.58**

This is stable, but the temperature-rise amplification factor is 1/(1−0.58) ≈ **2.4×**. With poor airflow (R_th,ja > 43 K/W), the loop gain exceeds 1 and the FET runs away thermally.

## 2. Switching Loss Model @ 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches: one HS/LS pair per channel at any instant.

| Mechanism | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap (hard-switched HS) | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·30n·48k | **1.45 W** |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_F·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·200n·48k | **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 40n·25.2·48k | 0.05 W |
| Coss discharge | E_oss·f_sw | 0.03 W |
| Gate drive (2 FETs, mostly in driver) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw | 0.06 W |
| **Switching subtotal / channel** | | **≈2.2 W** |

Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it drops to ~1.1 W, but 48 kHz keeps ripple current and audible noise down for low-inductance racing motors.

The dead-time term is 28% of switching loss. Cutting dead time from 100 ns to 60 ns (once you have verified there is no shoot-through on your gate-driver/FET pair) saves ~0.25 W per channel.

### Per-channel and board totals @ 80 A burst

$$P_{ch} \approx 19.4 + 2.2 \approx 21.6\ \text{W}, \qquad P_{board} = 4 \times 21.6 \approx \mathbf{86\ W}$$

Add ~1.5 W for the ESC MCUs, LDOs, and gate drivers.

## 3. Thermal Calculations

### 3.1 Steady-state (why 80 A is burst-only)
A 30×30 mm 6-layer 2 oz board with no heatsink and prop-wash airflow has R_th,board-amb ≈ 3–5 K/W:

ΔT_ss = 86 W × 4 K/W ≈ **344 K**

Continuous 80 A is therefore impossible. Continuous capability is roughly:

I_cont ≈ 80·√(ΔT_allow / ΔT_ss)

With ΔT_allow = 60 K, that gives about **35 A per channel**. This is consistent with real 30×30 "55–65 A burst" products.

### 3.2 Transient (lumped heat capacity)

| Mass | Estimate | C_th |
|---|---|---|
| Copper (9 cm² × 6 L × 70 µm ≈ 3.4 g) | ×0.385 J/g·K | 1.3 J/K |
| FR-4 (≈2.6 g) | ×1.1 J/g·K | 2.9 J/K |
| 24 MOSFETs, caps, solder | — | ~1.3 J/K |
| **Total** | | **≈5.5 J/K** |

Thermal time constant: τ = R_th·C_th ≈ 4 × 5.5 ≈ 22 s.

$$\Delta T(t) = P\,R_{th}\left(1-e^{-t/\tau}\right)$$

| Burst duration | ΔT_board | T_board from 40 °C |
|---|---|---|
| 1 s | 15 K | 55 °C
