# STM32H743 Nano AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC): Burst Loss Model, DShot Timer Allocation, and Netlists

## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility

These findings change the design and should be resolved before layout.

1. **The H743 does not drive the gates.** A 4-in-1 ESC needs a local ESC MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32) per motor. That MCU generates the 48 kHz commutation PWM, dead time, and BEMF sensing. The H743 sends only DShot600 frames and receives eRPM telemetry. The architecture below reflects this.
2. **4× 80 A burst on a 20×20 mm board is thermally possible only for about 1–2 s.** The math is in §3. Treat 80 A as a ≤1 s transient, or move to a 30.5×30.5 board or a separate ESC stack.
3. **48 kHz PWM roughly doubles switching loss compared with 24 kHz** (the AM32 default) for a modest smoothness gain. I model both.
4. **Component parameters are representative, not vendor-verified.** Substitute datasheet values for your chosen MOSFET and gate driver.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value used |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S, full) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Burst phase current | I | 80 A |
| MOSFET R_DS(on) @25 °C / @T_j≈110 °C | R_25 / R_hot | 1.5 mΩ / 2.4 mΩ (×1.6) |
| Switching rise + fall time | t_r + t_f | 20 + 20 ns |
| Dead time (per edge) | t_dt | 100 ns |
| Body diode forward drop | V_f | 0.8 V |
| Reverse-recovery charge / output charge / gate charge | Q_rr / Q_oss / Q_g | 40 / 30 / 30 nC |
| Gate drive voltage | V_gs | 10 V |
| Motor phase-to-phase inductance | L | 20 µH |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (trapezoidal commutation, complementary PWM)

In each of the 6 commutation steps, two legs are active:
- **Leg A (PWM leg):** the high-side (HS) conducts for fraction D; the low-side (LS) synchronously rectifies for 1−D.
- **Leg B:** the LS is on for 100% of the step.

**Per-motor conduction loss:**

$$P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 R_{hot}\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I_{rms}^2 R_{hot}$$

This is independent of duty cycle.

**Ripple correction:**

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \cdot 0.25}{20\,\mu\text{H}\cdot 48\,\text{kHz}} = 6.56\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.6$$

The ripple term is under 0.1% and can be neglected.

**Result:**

$$P_{cond} = 2 \cdot 6400 \cdot 0.0024 = \mathbf{30.7\ W\ per\ motor}$$

The total across four motors is **122.9 W**.

**Per-FET distribution.** Each leg is HS-active for 1/3 of the electrical cycle, LS-active for 1/3, and floating for 1/3:

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R D, \qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R\,(2-D)$$

At D → 1, each FET dissipates ≈ 5.1 W of conduction loss. The LS FET is the worst case at partial throttle (up to 2× the HS loss at D → 0, though phase current is lower there).
