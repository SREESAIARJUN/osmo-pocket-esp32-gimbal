# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #42): Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Budget, and Bi-Directional DShot600 DMA Allocation

**Status:** Engineering reference, revision A
**Scope:** Analytical models with worked numbers, ArduPilot `hwdef` timer and DMA plan, and representative netlists.

---

## 0. Up-Front Caveats

1. **No component-level data for Job #42 was supplied.** All numbers below rest on the baseline assumptions in §1. Every equation is parameterized, so you can substitute real datasheet values. Treat the worked results as order-of-magnitude design guidance, not sign-off values.
2. **Architecture:**
   - The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and generates DShot600.
   - The 48 kHz PWM and commutation run on a separate ESC MCU per motor. AM32 on AT32F421 is assumed, so the loss model applies to the ESC power stage, not the H743.
3. **Headline finding:** 80 A per channel on a nano-format board (about 26 × 26 mm usable area) is only viable as a **sub-second to ~2 s burst** with firmware current limiting. It is not a continuous rating. This is quantified in §4.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.0 × 1.55 = **1.55 mΩ**.

### 2.2 Current path in six-step synchronous drive

In each 60° commutation sector, two legs carry current:
- the PWM leg, where the high-side (HS) and low-side (LS) FETs alternate complementarily, and
- the static leg, which is held on.

Outside the dead-time intervals, the motor current therefore always flows through **exactly two channels in series**.

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.55\,\text{m}\Omega = 6400 \times 3.1\,\text{m} = \mathbf{19.8\ W}$$

### 2.3 Per-FET distribution

Each FET is on for roughly a 120° electrical window, i.e. about ⅓ of the period. It also shares duty with its complementary partner while PWM-ing.

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} \approx \frac{P_{cond,motor}}{6} \approx 3.3\ \text{W}, \qquad P_{cond,FET,inst} = I^2 R_{DS} = 9.9\ \text{W}$$

The instantaneous 9.9 W matters for junction ripple (§4.3), because the electrical period at high eRPM, about 0.3–1 ms, is comparable to the die time constant.

### 2.4 Self-heating convergence

R_DS depends on T_j, and T_j depends on loss. Iterate:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_{pcb} + P(T_j^{(n)})\cdot R\theta_{j\text{-}pcb}$$

This converges in 2–3 iterations for these values. **Thermal runaway condition:** the loop diverges if

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\cdot R\theta \ge 1 \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\, R\theta \ge 1$$

At 80 A and 3.5 K/W junction-to-PCB: 6400 × 1e-3 × 0.0055 × 3.5 ≈ **0.12**. That is stable with margin. Runaway is not the failure mode; bulk PCB heating is.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

These losses apply to the PWM leg only (one HS/LS pair per motor at any time).

| Mechanism | Equation | Per motor @ 80 A, 25.2 V |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | ½·25.2·80·40 ns·48 k = **1.94 W** |
| Body-diode conduction in dead time | V_F·I·2t_dt·f_sw | 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 50 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.06 W** |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss·V·f_sw | 30 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.04 W** |
| Gate drive (two FETs, mostly in driver and R_g) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw | 2·50 nC·10·48 k = **0.05 W** |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.7 W** |

**Interpretation:**
- At 48 kHz, switching is about 12% of total loss. Conduction dominates at 80 A.
- Reducing f_sw to 24 kHz would save only about 1.3 W per motor, at the cost of more current ripple and audible noise.
