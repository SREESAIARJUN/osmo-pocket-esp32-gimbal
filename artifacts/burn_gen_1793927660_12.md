## 0. Architectural Clarification and Feasibility Flags

Two points need to be settled before the analysis.

**1. The STM32H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC.**
- A 4-in-1 ESC has four independent motor-control MCUs running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical parts are the AT32F421 and STM32G071.
- The H743 runs ArduPilot and emits four DShot600 streams to those MCUs.
- The "48 kHz switching" is the ESC PWM frequency, a firmware setting on the ESC MCUs. It is unrelated to H743 timers.
- The models below treat the two domains separately.

**2. "80 A burst" on a nano form factor is a transient rating, not a thermal design point.**
- The math below shows roughly 22 W of dissipation per motor at 80 A, or about 88 W board-wide.
- A 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm board has only about 3 J/K of heat capacity.
- An 80 A rating is therefore defensible only as a burst of about 1 s or less, at a defined duty cycle.
- I specify that limit explicitly in §3.4 and recommend the specification sheet state it.

**All device parameters are placeholders.** Values marked *(A)* are assumptions representative of 30–40 V, sub-1 mΩ, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN MOSFETs. Replace them with datasheet values for the selected part before sign-off. The equations are the deliverable; the numbers are a worked example.

## 2. Loss Model per Motor

### 2.1 Commutation Model

The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal drive (AM32/BLHeli default) with complementary synchronous rectification.

- At any instant, exactly one high-side leg and one low-side leg carry motor current I.
- The PWM leg alternates between its high-side FET (for a fraction D of each period) and its low-side FET (for 1−D).
- The current path therefore always contains two channel resistances in series.

### 2.2 Conduction Loss

The hot on-resistance is:

$$
R_{hot} = k_T \cdot R_{ds(on),25} = 1.6 \times 0.75 = 1.2\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

The motor conduction loss, independent of D because the current path always contains two FETs, is:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{hot} = 80^2 \times 2.4\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{15.4\ W}
$$

Copper loss in the path is:

$$
P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{trace} = 6400 \times 0.4\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{2.56\ W}
$$

Current ripple adds a correction term:

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus}-E)\,D}{L_{ph} f_{sw}}
$$

For a typical 5" motor with L_ph ≈ 20 µH, the ripple adds less than 1 %, so it is neglected here.

**Per-FET RMS current (for selecting the device).** Each FET is in the active pair for 1/3 of the electrical cycle. In the PWM leg, the high and low sides share that time as D and 1−D. In the worst case, a non-PWM low-side holds the full current for 1/3 of the cycle:

$$
I_{rms,FET,max} = I\sqrt{1/3} = 46.2\ \text{A}
$$

### 2.3 Switching Loss at 48 kHz

The model assumes one hard-switched transition pair per PWM period. The high-side FET of the PWM leg turns on into the conducting low-side body diode and turns off with the current transferring to the diode.

**(a) V–I overlap loss**

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

**(b) Output capacitance loss**

$$
P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 45\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.054\ W}
$$

This term is mostly dissipated in the hard-switched FET.

**(c) Reverse recovery loss**

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead-time windows per period)

$$
P_{dt} = V_F I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.85 \times 80 \times 200\text{ ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.65\ W}
$$

**(e) Gate drive** (two switching FETs; this loss lands in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET)

$$
P_{gate} = 2 Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.058\ \text{W}
$$

**Switching subtotal (in FETs):** 1.94 + 0.054 + 0.048 + 0.65 = **2.69 W**

**Frequency sensitivity.** The frequency-dependent part scales as:

$$
\frac{\partial P_{sw}}{\partial f_{sw}} \approx \frac{2.69\text{ W}}{48\text{ kHz}} = 56\ \mu\text{W/Hz}
$$

| f_sw | P_sw |
|---|---|
| 24 kHz | 1.35 W |
| 48 kHz | 2.69 W |
| 96 kHz | 5.4 W |

At 80 A, 48 kHz is acceptable. The penalty for higher frequencies is moderate because conduction loss dominates at 85 %.
