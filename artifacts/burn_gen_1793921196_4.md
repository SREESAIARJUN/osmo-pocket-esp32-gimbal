## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and a Critical Finding

**Partitioning.** The STM32H743 does **not** drive the MOSFETs. It runs ArduPilot and emits bi-directional DShot600 to four ESC microcontrollers (one per channel, e.g. AT32F421 running AM32). Those ESC MCUs generate the 48 kHz PWM and perform BEMF commutation. Merging the two would require 24 complementary PWM outputs with dead-time insertion and four concurrent sensorless commutation loops on the flight MCU, which is not a reasonable architecture.

> ⚠️ **Critical finding: "80 A" cannot be a continuous rating on a nano board.** The math in §3 shows that four channels at 80 A dissipate about **130 W**. With no heatsink, a 30×30 mm board heats at about **36 °C/s**, which gives roughly **2 s** before the FETs reach their thermal limit. The realistic continuous rating is about **20–25 A per channel**. The 80 A figure must be specified as a time-limited burst, and the firmware must enforce it (see §3.5).

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in synchronous six-step commutation

In each 60° commutation sector, one phase's high side is PWM'd at duty D, and another phase's low side is held fully on. With complementary (synchronous) PWM, the off-time current freewheels through the low-side FET of the PWM'd leg rather than its body diode.

As a result, the motor current always passes through exactly **two channel resistances in series**, regardless of D (dead time is treated separately in §2):

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS,hot} \qquad\text{(valid for any } D\text{, synchronous mode)}$$
