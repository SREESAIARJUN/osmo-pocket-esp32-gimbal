### Architectural correction (important)

The H743 should **not** commutate the motors. Running 12 complementary PWM pairs plus 4 sensorless back-EMF zero-cross loops at 48 kHz while also running ArduPilot's EKF3 is not viable.

The correct topology is:

- **H743 (ArduPilot):** sends bidirectional DShot600 on 4 lines.
- **4× ESC MCUs** (AT32F421 or STM32G071, running AM32/BLHeli_32): one per motor.
- **3-phase gate driver** per ESC MCU.
- **6× MOSFETs** per motor.

Everything below assumes this topology.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Six-step synchronous commutation

At any instant, two phases carry current. With complementary (synchronous) PWM:

- **PWM-on interval (D):** the current path is HS(A) → motor → LS(B), so two FETs are in series.
- **PWM-off interval (1−D):** the current freewheels through LS(A) and LS(B), again two FETs in series.

The total conduction loss per motor is therefore **independent of duty cycle**:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_B^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1} \Rightarrow 1.6\ \text{m}\Omega\ @\ 125\,^\circ\text{C}$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.6\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{20.5\ W}$$

### 1.2 Per-device distribution

Each phase conducts for 2/3 of the electrical cycle: 1/3 as the PWM-switched "high" phase and 1/3 as the "low" return phase.

$$P_{HS} = \frac{I^2 R\,D}{3}$$

$$P_{LS} = \frac{I^2 R\,(1-D)}{3} + \frac{I^2 R}{3}$$

At burst, D ≈ 0.9 and I²R = 10.24 W, which gives:

- **P_HS,cond = 3.07 W**
- **P_LS,cond = 3.75 W**

The worst case for the low side is D → 0 (low-throttle, high-current braking), where P_LS = 6.8 W.

### 1.3 Copper loss

Assume 2 oz outer + 2 oz inner bus with ≈ 0.3 mΩ effective path resistance per motor:

$$P_{Cu} \approx I_B^2 \times 0.3\ \text{m}\Omega = 1.9\ \text{W per motor}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd high-side FET hard-switches. The low-side FET switches at near-zero V_ds because the body diode is already conducting during dead time.

**(a) V–I overlap:**
$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 0.97\ \text{W}$$

**(b) Output capacitance:**
$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.024\ \text{W}$$

**(c) Reverse recovery** (dissipated in the high side):
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (dissipated in the low side):
$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2\,t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 1.23\ \text{W}$$

**(e) Gate drive** (dissipated mainly in the driver and gate resistors):
$$P_g = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} \times 2 = 50\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

**Key result:** dead time costs more than switching. Tuning the AM32 dead-time down from 200 ns to 80 ns saves about 0.75 W per motor.
