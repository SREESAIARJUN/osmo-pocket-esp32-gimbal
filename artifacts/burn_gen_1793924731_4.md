### Architectural Clarification

The STM32H743 does **not** drive the gates directly. A correct 4-in-1 architecture uses:

```
H743 (ArduPilot) --DShot600 bidir (1 wire/motor)--> 4x ESC MCU (e.g., AT32F421, AM32)
    --> 4x triple half-bridge gate driver --> 24 MOSFETs (6 per motor)
```

This keeps 48 kHz PWM generation, BEMF zero-crossing detection and commutation local to each ESC MCU. The H743 handles only DShot and telemetry.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current Paths in 6-Step with Synchronous Rectification

In any 60° sector, two phases conduct:
- **Phase A (PWM-ing):** the high-side (HS) conducts for duty D. The low-side (LS) conducts for (1 − D) as a synchronous rectifier, minus dead time.
- **Phase B (return):** the LS is held fully on (D = 1).
- **Phase C:** floating (BEMF sense).

Instantaneous conduction loss per motor:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\cdot 1 = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

To first order, this is **independent of duty cycle**. Synchronous rectification makes the sum of the PWM leg constant.

### 1.2 Ripple Correction

Peak-to-peak current ripple in the PWM leg:

$$
\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}} \le \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\times10^{-6}\times 48\,000} = 6.56\ \text{A}
$$

RMS correction:

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I_{DC}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.6 = 6403.6\ \text{A}^2
$$

The ripple contributes 0.06%, which is negligible. With a low-inductance motor (≤ 5 µH), recompute: ΔI rises above 25 A.

### 1.3 Temperature-Dependent R_DS(on): Self-Consistent Solution

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha = 0.0055\ /\text{K}
$$

T_j depends on P_cond, so iterate (shown in §3.4). At a converged T_j ≈ 110 °C:

$$
R_{DS} = 1.0 \times (1 + 0.0055 \times 85) = 1.47\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 6403.6 \times 1.47\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{18.8\ W}
$$

### 1.4 Per-FET Distribution

Over one electrical revolution, each phase is:
- HS-PWM for 120°
- LS-on for 120°
- Floating for 120°

Averaged per FET (six per motor):

$$
\bar P_{FET,cond} \approx \frac{18.8}{6} \approx 3.1\ \text{W}
$$

At low eRPM, a single FET can be stuck in one state for milliseconds. The worst-case instantaneous figure, 80² × 1.47 mΩ = **9.4 W in one FET**, must be checked against Z_θJC(t) (see §3.3).

### 1.5 Copper and Shunt Losses

| Element | Resistance | Loss @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase trace + pad, 2 oz, ~8 mm, ~3 mm wide (×2 phases active) | ≈0.35 mΩ each | 2 × 2.24 = 4.5 W |
| Motor wire solder pad / via field | ≈0.1 mΩ | 0.64 W ×2 = 1.3 W |
| Per-motor bus feed (shared plane) | ≈0.1 mΩ | 0.64 W |
| **Copper subtotal per motor** | | **≈6.4 W** |

**Current sensing:** a single bus shunt sized for 4 × 80 A = 320 A is not physically realistic on a nano board. Use one of:
- (a) a 0.1 mΩ shunt rated for ~150 A average with firmware current limiting, or
- (b) per-ESC low-side shunt-less estimation.

For a 0.1 mΩ shunt at a realistic 4-motor peak of 200 A: P = 200² × 0.1 mΩ = **4.0 W**.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing leg switches hard. The HS switches hard against the LS body diode, then the dead-time diode conducts.

### 2.1 V–I Overlap (HS Turn-On and Turn-Off)

$$
P_{sw,VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I\,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}
= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.968\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction (LS Diode, Two Edges per Cycle)

$$
P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.614\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Reverse Recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}
$$
