**Architectural warnings (read before layout):**

1. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In every production 4-in-1 stack, each ESC has its own MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 flight controller only emits DShot600 and decodes bi-directional telemetry. This document models both sides.
2. **"Nano" vs 80 A is a physical conflict.** A 20×20 mm board fits 24 FETs only in 3×3 mm packages, with R_DS(on) ≈ 2–3 mΩ and R_θJC ≈ 3–5 K/W. At 80 A that roughly doubles the conduction loss computed below. 80 A burst is realistic on a 30.5×30.5 mm board with 5×6 FETs and a heatsink. On a 20×20 board, de-rate to about 45–55 A burst.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification

At any instant, two phases carry current:
- **PWM leg:** high-side (HS) conducts for duty D, and the low-side (LS) synchronous rectifier conducts for 1−D.
- **Return leg:** LS is on 100 % of the commutation sector.

With motor current I (flat-top, ripple added in §1.3):

$$P_{cond,PWM\,leg} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$
$$P_{cond,return} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$
$$\boxed{P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)}$$

The result is independent of D. This is why synchronous rectification ("complementary PWM", AM32 default) matters.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence and self-consistent solution

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,(1+\alpha)^{(T_j-25)}$$

T_j and P are coupled through the thermal impedance. Solve by fixed-point iteration:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_c + P_{FET}\!\left(T_j^{(n)}\right)\cdot Z_{\theta JC}(t)$$

The loop converges if $\partial P/\partial T_j \cdot Z_{\theta} < 1$, i.e. $P\,\alpha\,Z_\theta < 1$.

Design point T_j = 125 °C gives (1.005)^100 = 1.647, so R_DS = 1.65 mΩ.

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.65\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{21.1\ W}$$

Per-FET distribution (worst FET = LS during its return sector):
- Each FET is the "return" LS for 1/3 of the electrical cycle and the PWM-leg device for 1/3 of it.
- Peak instantaneous dissipation in one FET is I²R = **10.6 W**. The cycle average is lower, but at low eRPM (stall, launch) the sector dwell is long enough that the instantaneous figure governs T_j.

### 1.3 Ripple correction

Ripple current is worst at D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L\,f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\mu\text{H}\times 48\text{kHz}} = 6.56\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.59$$
