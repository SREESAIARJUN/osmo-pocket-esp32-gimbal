# STM32H743 AIO 4-in-1 ESC Nano FC: Architecture Package

**Scope:** Job #51, 80 A burst / 48 kHz PWM / ArduPilot BDShot600

---

## 0. Assumptions

I don't have the Job #51 BOM or stack-up, so this model is **parametric**, populated with representative values. Replace the values in Table 0 with your datasheet numbers and every result below recomputes directly.

**Architecture note:** On an AIO board the H743 runs ArduPilot and does **not** drive the bridge. Each ESC channel (or one 4-channel MCU) runs AM32 or BLHeli_32 on a separate MCU, such as an STM32G071 or AT32F421. The H743 talks to those MCUs over bidirectional DShot. The power-stage math below applies to the ESC side.

## 1. Conduction loss model (80 A burst)

### 1.1 Commutation topology

The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal control with complementary (synchronous) PWM:

- **Active phase pair:** In any 60° sector, one high-side FET and one low-side FET carry I_ph.
- **Switching leg:** One leg chops at duty D. Its HS conducts for fraction D and its LS (synchronous rectifier) for 1−D.
- **Current path:** The sum of conduction is always 2 × R_ds in series with I_ph.

### 1.2 Equations

Hot on-resistance:

$$R_{ds,hot} = R_{ds,25}\cdot k_T = 0.80\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.55 = 1.24\,\text{m}\Omega$$

Per-motor conduction loss, valid for any duty because the HS/LS of the PWM leg sum to the full period:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds,hot} = 2(80)^2(1.24\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{15.9\ W}$$

Copper and shunt losses, with an assumed R_trace+shunt of about 0.35 mΩ:

$$P_{Cu} = I_{ph}^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.35\,\text{m}\Omega = 2.2\ \text{W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each FET is active for 1/3 of the electrical cycle, over two sectors out of six.

**Hottest-FET worst case:** a non-chopping FET held fully ON for its 120° interval, or the LS on the PWM leg at low duty.

$$P_{FET,max} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}\,I^2 R_{ds,hot} = \tfrac{1}{3}(6400)(1.24\text{m}) = 2.65\ \text{W (average over cycle)}$$

The **instantaneous** dissipation while conducting is 7.9 W. This matters for the transient Z_θ analysis in §3.4 when eRPM is low (stall or punch-out from hover).

## 2. Switching loss model (48 kHz)

Only the chopping leg switches. Per motor at V_bus = 25.2 V (worst case) and I = 80 A:

| Term | Equation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = ½(25.2)(80)(20 ns)(48 k) | **0.97 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 60 n × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.073 W |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss·V·f_sw = 50 n × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.060 W |
| Dead-time diode | 2·V_f·I·t_dt·f_sw = 2(0.8)(80)(50 n)(48 k) | **0.31 W** |
| Gate drive (driver IC) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw = 2(60 n)(10)(48 k) | 0.058 W |
| **Total P_sw per motor** | | **≈ 1.47 W** |

### Key sensitivities

- **Overlap loss scales linearly with f_sw.** Moving 48 kHz → 96 kHz adds about 1.5 W per motor.
- **Dead time is the cheapest lever.** Going 50 ns → 25 ns saves 0.15 W per motor. Verify shoot-through margin with the driver's propagation-delay mismatch first.
- **The 48 kHz choice is justified.** It sits above audible range, gives low current ripple on low-inductance 20xx motors, and keeps switching loss at about 9% of conduction loss at 80 A.

## 3. Thermal model

### 3.1 Total burst dissipation

$$P_{motor} = 15.9 + 2.2 + 1.47 = 19.6\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{board,4\times80A} = 4 \times 19.6 \approx \mathbf{78\ W}\ (\text{plus} \sim 1.5\ \text{W FC, regulators})$$

### 3.2 Board-level lumped RC

Steady-state resistance to ambient, assuming forced prop-wash convection with h ≈ 80 W/m²K over both faces:

- Both faces: A = 2 × (0.0305)² = 1.86×10⁻³ m²

$$R_{\theta BA} = \frac{1}{hA} = \frac{1}{80 \times 1.86\times10^{-3}} \approx 6.7\ \text{K/W}$$

Thermal capacitance, assuming about 8 g effective mass (8 L FR-4 + Cu + FETs + caps) at a weighted c_p of about 0.65 J/g·K:

$$C_{\theta} \approx 5.2\ \text{J/K}, \qquad \tau = R_{\theta}C_{\theta} \approx 35\ \text{s}$$

### 3.3 Burst duration limit

$$\Delta T(t) = P\,R_{\theta}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right)$$

With T_amb = 35 °C and a board limit of 110 °C, so ΔT_allow = 75 K:

$$t_{burst} = -\tau\,\ln\!\left(1 - \frac{\Delta T_{allow}}{P R_\theta}\right) = -35\ln\!\left(1 - \frac{75}{78 \times 6.7}\right) \approx \mathbf{5.4\ s}$$

**Continuous rating:** P_max = 75 / 6.7 ≈ 11 W per board. Solving 4 × (2I²R_ds,hot + I²R_Cu) = 11 W gives:

$$I_{cont} \approx \mathbf{26\text{–}28\ A \text{ per motor (all four loaded)}}$$

> **Design-review flag:** "80 A" on a nano AIO is a **≤5 s, all-motor burst** rating. Specify it that way in the datasheet, e.g. "80 A burst 5 s / 28 A continuous, 6S, forced airflow". Without airflow, R_θBA rises to roughly 20–25 K/W and continuous current drops to about 15 A.

### 3.4 Junction rise within a burst

Pad temperature tracks the board. Junction rise above the pad uses the instantaneous dissipation during conduction:

$$\Delta T_{JC} = P_{inst}\cdot Z_{\theta JC}(t_p)$$

**Normal running.** At eRPM > ~5 k the conduction interval t_p is < 4 ms, so Z_θJC ≈ 0.3 K/W:

$$\Delta T_{JC} \approx 7.9 \times 0.3 \approx 2.4\ \text{K}$$

**Stall or locked rotor.** Here t_p → DC, so Z_θJC = R_θJC = 1.0 K/W:

$$\Delta T_{JC} = 7.9\ \text{K}$$
