## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFET bridges directly. ArduPilot runs on the H743 as the flight controller. It sends bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32), and those MCUs perform the commutation. This document models both halves and the link between them.

| Parameter | Value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max / 22.2 V nom | Worst case uses 25.2 V |
| Burst current | 80 A per motor (phase-RMS ≈ DC-link current in six-step) | Duration target: 2–5 s |
| Continuous | 45 A per motor | |
| PWM | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous rectification) | AM32 setting |
| MOSFET (1 per switch, 24 total) | 40 V, R_DS(on),25 = 0.90 mΩ typ / 1.1 mΩ max @ V_GS = 10 V, Q_g = 95 nC, Q_gs2 ≈ 10 nC, Q_gd = 15 nC, Q_rr ≈ 50 nC, Q_oss ≈ 60 nC, V_SD = 0.8 V, PQFN 5×6 | Representative of the BSC010N04LS class. Substitute your datasheet values. |

> ⚠️ **Feasibility flag:** A true 20×20 "nano" board cannot sustain 4 × 80 A, even for a burst. This design uses a 30.5 mm ESC stacked under a 20×20 FC. The loss numbers below show why: about 88 W of dissipation at full burst on all four motors.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

Use R_DS,25 = 1.0 mΩ, a design value between typ and max.

| T_j (°C) | R_DS (mΩ) |
|---|---|
| 25 | 1.00 |
| 100 | 1.34 |
| 125 | 1.45 |

### 1.2 Six-step bridge current paths

At any instant, two phases conduct and the third floats. With complementary PWM at duty D:

- **Active high-side:** conducts for fraction D.
- **Complementary low-side of the same leg:** conducts for (1 − D − 2t_d·f_sw). The remainder is body-diode conduction.
- **Return leg low-side:** conducts 100% of the sector.

Each switch is active for 1/3 of the electrical period. The per-switch loss is:

$$P_{cond,HS} = \frac{I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,D}{3},\qquad P_{cond,LS} = \frac{I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,(1-D)}{3} + \frac{I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)}{3}$$

The bridge total is close to duty-independent, because the current always flows through two channels:

$$P_{cond,bridge} \approx 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 1.3 Results at 80 A, T_j = 100 °C

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2(80)^2(1.34\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{17.2\ W\ per\ motor}$$

The worst single switch dissipates (80²)(1.34 mΩ)/3 × 1.0 ≈ **2.9 W**. That is a return-leg low-side, or a high-side at D → 1.

### 1.4 Copper and shunt losses (often ignored, and significant)

| Element | R | P @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase pad + trace (2 oz, ~8 mm, multilayer) | ~0.35 mΩ ×2 paths | 4.5 W |
| Current shunt (shared, 4-in-1) | 0.25 mΩ, carries ΣI | see §4 |
| Bus planes | ~0.2 mΩ | 1.3 W |

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg hard-switches per PWM cycle. The high-side turns on into a low-side whose body diode is conducting.

### 2.1 Gate current during the Miller plateau (V_pl ≈ 3.0 V)

$$I_{G,on} = \frac{V_{DRV}-V_{pl}}{R_{src}+R_G} = \frac{10-3}{4+2.2} = 1.13\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{G,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{sink}+R_G} = \frac{3}{2+2.2} = 0.71\ \text{A}$$

$$t_{on} = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{G,on}} = \frac{25\ \text{nC}}{1.13} = 22\ \text{ns},\qquad t_{off} = \frac{25\ \text{nC}}{0.71} = 35\ \text{ns}$$

### 2.2 V–I overlap loss

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I\,(t_{on}+t_{off})\,f_{sw} = \tfrac12(25.2)(80)(57\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = \mathbf{2.76\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery, output capacitance, and gate charge

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

The reverse-recovery current also adds roughly 10% to the turn-on loss in the high-side, giving about +0.25 W.

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.04\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{gate} = Q_g V_{DRV} f_{sw}\times 2 = 95\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k}\cdot2 = 0.09\ \text{W}$$

Most of the gate-charge loss is dissipated in the driver and R_G, not in the FET.

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\,I\,(2t_d)\,f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot600\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.84\ W}$$

> **Design lever:** Cutting dead time to 150 ns saves 0.9 W per motor. Verify for shoot-through margin with t_off = 35 ns plus driver propagation mismatch (typically ±20 ns).

### 2.5 Loss budget per motor at 80 A burst (T_j = 100 °C)

| Loss | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 17.2 |
| Switching V·I + Q_rr effect | 3.06 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.84 |
| C_oss + gate | 0.13 |
| Copper (phase + bus share) | ~5.0 |
| **Total per motor** | **≈ 27.2 W** |
| **4 motors (silicon only)** | **≈ 89 W** |
| **4 motors incl. copper** | **≈ 109 W** |

**Efficiency check:** Output power is about 22.2 × 80 = 1776 W per motor, so η_ESC ≈ 98.5%. This is plausible, and it confirms the problem is power density, not efficiency.
