# STM32H743 Nano AIO (4-in-1 ESC + FC): Reference Architecture Model, Job #25

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

I don't have Job #25's BOM, stack-up, or board outline. Everything below is a parameterized reference model. Each assumption is stated so you can substitute real datasheet values. **Treat the numbers as a design-review baseline, not sign-off.**

**Architecture issue to resolve first.** The STM32H743 should not commutate the motors directly. The standard partitioning, which ArduPilot supports, is:

- **H743 (flight controller):** runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600 on 4 timer channels.
- **ESC MCUs:** 4× small MCUs (e.g. AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32) do commutation, 48 kHz PWM and eRPM telemetry. A single-MCU 4-in-1 design is possible but less common.
- **Gate drivers + 24 MOSFETs:** 3 half-bridges per motor, 4 motors.

The power-stage math below applies regardless of which MCU drives the gates.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R = 1.2 mΩ × 1.51 = **1.81 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-step commutation with synchronous rectification

At any instant, one phase sources current and one phase sinks it.

- **PWM-on:** HS(A) and LS(B) conduct.
- **PWM-off:** LS(A) (synchronous) and LS(B) conduct.

Either way, exactly **two devices carry I** at all times. Therefore:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.81\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{23.2\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-device loss

Each FET conducts for about 1/3 of the electrical period, so the 23.2 W spreads across 6 devices:

- **Average per FET:** P_FET,avg ≈ 23.2 / 6 ≈ **3.9 W**
- **Instantaneous while conducting:** P_FET,pk = I²R = **11.6 W**

The commutation period at typical eRPM is much shorter than the FET thermal time constant, so the average value governs junction rise.

### 1.4 Copper and shunt losses (add these)

- **Shunt:** P_shunt = I²·R_sh. A 0.25 mΩ shunt gives 1.6 W per motor. Many nano AIOs omit the per-motor shunt and use a single bus shunt instead.
- **Traces and planes:** for 80 A through roughly 10 mm of 4-layer-parallel 2 oz copper, R ≈ 0.15 mΩ, so P ≈ 1 W per motor.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

With complementary 6-step PWM, **one half-bridge per motor hard-switches** at any time. Its two FETs share the transitions.

| Loss term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V_bus·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw | ½·25.2·80·40 ns·48 k = **1.94 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2t_d·f_sw | 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V_bus·f_sw | 30 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.04 W** |
| C_oss | ½·C_oss,er·V²·f_sw (C_oss,er ≈ 1 nF) | **0.015 W** |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver) | Q_g·V_gs·f_sw × 2 FETs | **0.048 W** |
| **Subtotal per motor** | | **≈ 2.65 W** |

**Sensitivity.** Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw and with (t_r+t_f). At 96 kHz it rises to about 5.3 W per motor. At 48 kHz, conduction loss dominates by about 9:1. This means **R_ds(on) and copper are your levers, not gate speed.**

## 3. Total Loss Budget at Burst

| Item | Per motor | 4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (T_j = 110 °C) | 23.2 W | 92.8 W |
| Switching + dead time + Q_rr | 2.65 W | 10.6 W |
| Copper + shunt | ~2.6 W | ~10.4 W |
| **Total** | **~28.5 W** | **~114 W** |

Efficiency at a 4×80 A burst: P_out ≈ 25.2 V × 320 A × duty. At full duty that is about 8 kW, so the loss is roughly 1.4% (≈98.6% stage efficiency). That figure looks good, but **114 W into a 9 cm² board cannot be sustained.**

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Steady state: not achievable

The effective θ_board-ambient for a 30.5 mm board in prop wash is about 8–12 °C/W. At 114 W:

$$\Delta T_{ss} \approx 114 \times 10 \approx 1100\,°\text{C}$$

**Conclusion: 80 A is strictly a transient rating.**
