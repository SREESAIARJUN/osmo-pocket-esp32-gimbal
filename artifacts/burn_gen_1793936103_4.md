> **Assumptions.** I don't have the Job #17 spec sheet, so this design uses the parameters below. Every value is a named constant in the code, so you can substitute your actual motor and IMU figures.

## 1. Loop Architecture and Timing

```
                  ┌──────────── 1 kHz ───────────┐  ┌──── 4 kHz ────┐  ┌──────── 20 kHz ────────┐
θ_ref ──►(+)─► Attitude P + ω_ff ──►(+)─► Velocity PI ──►(+)─► Current PI (d,q) ─► SVPWM ─► Motor
          ▲-                          ▲-                ▲ i_q,cog (anti-cog FF)    ▲-
          │                           │                 │                         │ i_abc (shunts)
     Mahony/quaternion        dual-IMU gyro ω     encoder θ_m ──► LUT/LMS          │
     (fused, notched)         (AAF+notch+LPF)                                     Clarke/Park
```

| Loop | Rate | Target bandwidth | Separation ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| Current (d/q) | 20 kHz | ω_c ≈ 2π·1500 rad/s | — |
| Velocity | 4 kHz | ω_v ≈ 2π·150 rad/s | ≥ 10× below current loop |
| Attitude | 1 kHz | ω_θ ≈ 2π·25 rad/s | ≥ 5× below velocity loop |
| IMU sampling | 8 kHz → decimated 4 kHz | — | — |

**Rule of thumb.** Each loop's bandwidth should be at most 1/5–1/10 of the inner loop's bandwidth, and at most 1/10 of its own sample rate. This keeps the ZOH and computation delay phase loss, φ_d ≈ ω·1.5·T_s, below about 10° at crossover.

## 2. FOC Current Loop (Inner, 20 kHz)

### 2.1 Transforms

Clarke transform (amplitude-invariant, assuming i_a + i_b + i_c = 0):

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \frac{1}{\sqrt3}(i_a + 2 i_b)
$$

Park transform (θ_e = p·θ_m − θ_offset, where p is the number of pole pairs):

$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d \\ i_q \end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e \\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha \\ i_\beta \end{bmatrix}
$$

### 2.2 PMSM Model (dq frame)

$$
v_d = R i_d + L_d \dot i_d - \omega_e L_q i_q
$$

$$
v_q = R i_q + L_q \dot i_q + \omega_e (L_d i_d + \lambda_m)
$$

$$
\tau_e = \tfrac{3}{2} p \left[\lambda_m i_q + (L_d - L_q) i_d i_q\right] \approx K_t\, i_q,\qquad K_t = \tfrac32 p \lambda_m
$$

### 2.3 PI with Pole-Zero Cancellation and Decoupling

Write the controller as C(s) = K_p + K_i/s. Place its zero on the electrical pole R/L. The closed loop then becomes first-order with bandwidth ω_c:

$$
K_{p,i} = \omega_c L,\qquad K_{i,i} = \omega_c R
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\frac{i}{i^*} = \frac{\omega_c}{s + \omega_c}
$$

Add decoupling feedforward to cancel the cross-coupling terms:

$$
v_d^{ff} = -\omega_e L_q i_q,\qquad v_q^{ff} = \omega_e (L_d i_d + \lambda_m)
$$

Discretize with backward Euler at T_s = 50 µs.

**Voltage limiting.** The vector is limited to the linear SVPWM region, |v| ≤ V_bus/√3. Priority goes to v_d. Use back-calculation anti-windup with gain K_aw = 1/K_p.

### 2.4 SVPWM (Min-Max Zero-Sequence Injection)

$$
v_{a,b,c} = \text{invClarke}(v_\alpha, v_\beta),\qquad
v_0 = -\tfrac12\left(\max(v_{abc}) + \min(v_{abc})\right)
$$

$$
\text{duty}_x = \tfrac12 + \frac{v_x + v_0}{V_{bus}}
$$

> **Note: voltage-mode fallback.** Many gimbal motors have high R (5–15 Ω) and no shunts. In that case, set v_q = R·i_q* + ω_e·λ_m directly (open-loop current). The outer loops stay unchanged. The code supports this through a `kVoltageMode` flag.

## 3. Cascaded Velocity / Angle Control

### 3.1 Mechanical Plant

$$
J\dot\omega = K_t i_q - B\omega - \tau_{cog}(\theta_m) - \tau_{dist}
$$

### 3.2 Velocity PI (4 kHz)

The plant seen from i_q* is K_t/(Js), with the current loop treated as unity. Choose crossover ω_v and put the PI zero a factor N below it (N = 4 gives about 76° phase margin before delays):

$$
K_{p,v} = \frac{J\,\omega_v}{K_t},\qquad K_{i,v} = \frac{K_{p,v}\,\omega_v}{N}
$$
