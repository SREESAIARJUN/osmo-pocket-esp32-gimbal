## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Assumptions

### 0.1 What the H743 does and does not control
On a 4-in-1 ESC AIO, the STM32H743 is the **flight controller**. It does not drive the power stage. Each ESC channel, or sometimes all four, has its own ESC MCU (typically AT32F421- or STM32G071-class running AM32, or EFM8/BB51 running BLHeli_S/Bluejay). That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM.

The H743 sends DShot600 frames and receives bidirectional eRPM telemetry. The model below covers both domains separately.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current distribution in six-step synchronous drive
At any instant, two phases carry current I. In the PWM phase, the high-side FET conducts for duty D and the low-side FET (synchronous rectification) conducts for 1−D. The return phase's low-side FET conducts continuously.

Per-FET RMS currents over one electrical cycle (each phase active for 1/3 of the time per polarity):

$$I_{HS,rms}=I\sqrt{\tfrac{D}{3}},\qquad I_{LS,rms}=I\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{3}}\ (\text{return}) \;\oplus\; I\sqrt{\tfrac{1-D}{3}}\ (\text{sync})$$

Summed over all six FETs, exactly two R_ds paths are always in series with I. The total is therefore independent of D:

$$P_{cond}=2\,I^2\,\big(R_{ds}(T_j)+R_{cu}\big)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{ds}=1.0\,(1+0.006\cdot85)=1.51\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

$$P_{cond}=2\cdot 80^2\cdot(1.51+0.3)\times10^{-3}=2\cdot6400\cdot1.81\times10^{-3}=\mathbf{23.2\ W\ per\ channel}$$

Of this, 19.3 W is in the FETs and 3.8 W is in the copper.

**Hottest FET.** The worst case is the low-side FET at low D, which carries both return and sync current:

$$P_{LS,max}\approx \tfrac{2}{3}\cdot I^2 R_{ds}\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot 2 \approx \tfrac{1}{3}\cdot6400\cdot1.51\text{m}\approx \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

The high-side FET also carries switching loss (Section 2).

### 1.4 Electro-thermal positive feedback
Because R_ds rises with T_j, loss rises with temperature. Solve this as a fixed-point problem:

$$T_j = T_{board} + P_{FET}(T_j)\,R_{th,jc} \quad\Rightarrow\quad T_j=\frac{T_b + k(1-25\alpha)R_{th}}{1-k\,\alpha\,R_{th}},\; k=P_{FET}/[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

With R_th,jc ≈ 1.2 K/W and ~4.5 W in the hottest FET, the loop gain is k·α·R_th ≈ 0.02. It is stable, and junction-to-case rise is only ~6 K. **The board, not the die, is the bottleneck.**
