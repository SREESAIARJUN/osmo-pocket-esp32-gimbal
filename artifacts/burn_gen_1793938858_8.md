# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #44): Engineering Design Package

**Scope:** Power-stage loss model (80 A burst, 48 kHz), thermal transient analysis, ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 timer and DMA plan, and a reference netlist.

---

## 0. Architecture Correction and Feasibility Flag

Two issues need to be resolved before layout begins.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production AIO, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot to four dedicated ESC MCUs (typically AT32F421 running AM32, or the BLHeli_32 equivalent). Each ESC MCU drives its own 3-phase bridge. ArduPilot has no field-oriented or 6-step commutation firmware for the H743. The architecture below reflects this.

**2. 80 A per channel cannot be sustained on a 20×20 mm "nano" board.** The math in §3 shows that a 20×20 board sustains about 20 A per channel continuous and about 80 A for roughly 1 s from a warm start. You need to choose one of three paths:
- Rate the board at 80 A **total-board** burst.
- Move to a 30.5×30.5 mm board with 5×6 mm FETs and a heatsink.
- Accept a 1 s burst rating with firmware current limiting.

The model below is parametric, so you can re-run it with your chosen FETs.

## 2. Loss Model (Per Motor Channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

Temperature-dependent resistance:

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.2 × 1.6 = **1.92 mΩ**.

In any 60° commutation sector, two phases carry current I:
- The high-side FET of the PWM phase conducts for fraction D.
- Its complementary low-side FET conducts for fraction (1−D).
- The low-side FET of the return phase conducts for the full sector.

$$P_{cond}=I^2R\,[D+(1-D)+1]=2I^2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This total is **independent of duty cycle** with synchronous rectification.

$$P_{cond}=2(80)^2(1.92\times10^{-3})=\mathbf{24.6\ W}$$

Per FET, averaged over an electrical cycle (6 FETs per motor): about **4.1 W**. During a sector, the always-on low-side FET instantaneously dissipates I²R = 12.3 W.

### 2.2 Switching Losses (Only the PWM'd FET Hard-Switches)

| Term | Equation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = ½(25.2)(80)(30 ns)(48 k) | **1.45 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 0.05 W |
| C_oss | Q_oss·V·f_sw | 0.06 W |
| Dead-time diode | V_f·I·(2t_dt)·f_sw = 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k | **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (in driver, not FET) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw | 0.06 W |

$$P_{sw,total}\approx 2.2\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Other Losses

- **Copper:** with a 6-layer 2 oz stack, the phase and bus path is about 0.3 mΩ effective, giving I²R ≈ 1.9 W per channel.
- **Bulk capacitor ripple:** I_rms,cap ≈ I·√(D(1−D)) ≈ 40 A at D = 0.5. With ESR at 15 mΩ, this is 24 W. **That would destroy a single capacitor.** You need low-ESR polymer plus MLCC banks, or the shared off-board 1000 µF low-ESR electrolytic. Model this per your actual capacitor selection.

### 2.4 Totals

| | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 24.6 W | 98.4 W |
| Switching | 2.2 W | 8.8 W |
| Copper | 1.9 W | 7.6 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 28.7 W** | **≈ 115 W** |

Efficiency at 80 A, D = 0.9 (≈ 1.81 kW electrical per motor): about 98.4% per channel. Losses are small relative to throughput. The problem is entirely **heat density**.
