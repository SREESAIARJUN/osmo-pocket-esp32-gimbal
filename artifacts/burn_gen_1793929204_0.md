# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC Nano Stack (Job #15): Engineering Model and Reference Design

## 0. Architecture Corrections

Three assumptions in the request need correcting before the numbers are useful.

1. **The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In an ArduPilot stack the H743 is the flight controller. It sends bi-directional DShot600 to a 4-in-1 ESC, and each channel on that ESC has its own motor-control MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32 (e.g., an AT32F421 or STM32G071). The 48 kHz figure is the ESC-side gate PWM. The model below keeps those two domains separate.
2. **80 A per channel in a nano form factor is a burst-only rating.** The math in §2 shows about 100 W of dissipation across four channels on a 20×20 or 30×30 mm board. That is survivable for a few seconds and not sustainable. The real design constraint is the adiabatic transient, not the steady state.
3. **All component values below are representative assumptions** (40 V, ~1 mΩ-class 5×6 mm MOSFETs, 6S bus). Substitute values from your actual BOM datasheets before sign-off.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction

With complementary (synchronous) 6-step commutation, the phase current always flows through exactly two MOSFETs:

- the active high-side FET plus the opposite low-side FET during on-time, or
- the low-side FET plus the opposite low-side FET during freewheeling.

The conduction loss is therefore:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C, R_ds = 0.9 × 1.5 = 1.35 mΩ (≈1.4 mΩ):

$$P_{cond} = 6400 \times 2 \times 1.4\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{17.9\ W}\ (\approx 9.0\text{ W per FET})$$

Parasitic resistances add to this:

$$P_{par} = I^2 (R_{shunt}+R_{cu}) = 6400 \times 0.75\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{4.8\ W}$$

### 2.2 Switching (only the PWM'd leg hard-switches)

**Overlap loss:**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = V_{bus} Q_{rr} f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 50\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} \approx V_{bus} Q_{oss} f_{sw} \cdot \tfrac12 = \mathbf{0.02\ W}$$

**Dead-time diode conduction (two edges per period):**

$$P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**Gate drive** (dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET):

$$P_g = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} \times 2 = 60\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

### 2.3 Totals

| Term | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 17.9 W | 71.6 W |
| Switching + Qrr + Coss | 2.0 W | 8.0 W |
| Dead time | 0.6 W | 2.4 W |
| Shunt + copper | 4.8 W | 19.2 W |
| **Total** | **≈25.3 W** | **≈101 W** |

Two observations follow:

- **Conduction dominates.** At 80 A, R_ds(on) and the copper path matter far more than switching speed.
- **f_sw is not the bottleneck.** Going from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves only about 1.3 W per channel. Use 48 kHz for motor smoothness; it is thermally cheap.

The hottest device is the PWM high-side FET:

$$P_{HS} \approx 9.0 + 1.94 + 0.06 + 0.02 + 0.3 \approx \mathbf{11.3\ W}$$

The 0.3 W term is that FET's share of the dead-time loss.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Electro-thermal fixed point and runaway criterion

The junction temperature depends on R_ds(on), which in turn depends on junction temperature:

$$T_j = T_a + R_{th,ja}\left[I^2 R_{ds,25}(1+\alpha(T_j-25)) + P_{sw}\right]$$

Solving for T_j:

$$T_j = \frac{T_a + R_{th,ja}\,[I^2R_{ds,25}(1-25\alpha) + P_{sw}]}{1 - \alpha I^2 R_{ds,25} R_{th,ja}}$$

**Stability criterion:** a finite operating point exists only if

$$\alpha I^2 R_{ds,25} R_{th,ja} < 1$$

At 80 A this gives 0.005 × 6400 × 0.9 mΩ × R_th,ja = 0.0288 · R_th,ja < 1. The steady-state limit is therefore **R_th,ja < 34.7 K/W per FET**.

That limit is easy to meet on paper. The real problem is the board-level heat path, analysed next.

### 3.2 Steady state: why 80 A continuous fails

A 30×30 mm stack in prop wash at roughly h ≈ 60 W/m²K, with two-sided area A ≈ 1.8×10⁻³ m², has:

$$R_{th,board-air} \approx \frac{1}{hA} \approx 9.3\text{ K/W}$$

At 101 W this predicts a steady-state rise of about 940 K. The design is therefore physically burst-only. The continuous rating is set by:

$$P_{cont} = \frac{T_{max}-T_a}{R_{th}} \approx \frac{(100-40)}{9.3} \approx 6.5\text{ W total}$$

Here T_max is a 100 °C board limit (also protecting the FC above it). This corresponds to roughly 20–25 A per channel continuous. Real airflow is often better than this estimate, and typical nano ESCs rated "40–45 A continuous" rely on aggressive airflow.

### 3.3 Transient (adiabatic) burst model

| FR-4 core, ~1.6 g | 1.6 g × 1.1 J/gK | 1.76 J/K |
| 24 MOSFETs (5×6 mm, ~0.1 g each) | 2.4 g × 0.7 J/gK | ~0.17 J/K |
