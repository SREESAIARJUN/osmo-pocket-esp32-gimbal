## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Effective resistance per switch position

$$R_{pos}(T_j) = \frac{R_{ds25}\cdot k_T}{n_p} = \frac{1.0\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.6}{2} = 0.80\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Current path in complementary 6-step

In each 60° sector, one phase's high side runs PWM at duty D and another phase's low side is held ON. During the PWM off-time, the low-side FET of the PWM phase conducts (synchronous rectification). The motor current I therefore always flows through **exactly two switch positions**:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2\left[D\,R_{HS} + (1-D)\,R_{LS,sync} + R_{LS,on}\right] \approx 2 I^2 R_{pos} \quad(\text{duty-independent with sync-rect})$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.8\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{10.24\ W}$$

### 1.3 Copper and interconnect loss

Assume a phase-to-bus path of R_Cu ≈ 0.5 mΩ, covering pads, vias, pours and solder. Copper has α = 0.0039 /K, so it is ×1.27 at 95 °C.

$$P_{Cu,ch} = I^2 R_{Cu}(1+\alpha\Delta T) = 6400 \times 0.5\text{m}\Omega \times 1.27 \approx \mathbf{4.1\ W}$$

This term is routinely ignored and is often larger than switching loss. Mitigations:
- Parallel the bus on at least 4 layers.
- Use stitched via fields of ≥ 20 × 0.3 mm vias per phase.

### 1.4 Per-FET worst case

The worst case is the high-side FET at D → 1:

$$P_{FET,cond} = \left(\tfrac{80}{2}\right)^2 \times 1.6\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{2.56\ W}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The held-on low side commutates only at sector rate (≤ a few kHz), which is negligible.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode loss

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = n_p Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.12\ W}$$

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 (2 n_p Q_{oss}) V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 240\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.15\ W}$$
