# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model (Job #12)

**Scope:** Power-stage loss model, thermal transient model, ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 timer/DMA plan, and reference netlists.

---

## 0. Architectural Assumptions and an Early Red Flag

The architecture assumes the following:

| Parameter | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal | Worst-case switching uses 25.2 V |
| Board | 20×20 mm "nano", 8-layer, 4× 2 oz inner/outer power layers | |
| FC MCU | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100), 480 MHz | Runs ArduPilot |
| ESC MCUs | 4× AT32F421 / STM32G071 running AM32 | The H743 does **not** commutate motors directly. It sends DShot to the ESC MCUs. |
| MOSFET | 40 V N-ch, PQFN 3.3×3.3, R_DS(on) = 1.2 mΩ max @ 25 °C, Q_g = 25 nC, Q_gd = 6 nC, Q_rr = 30 nC, C_oss ≈ 1 nF | 24 FETs total (6 per ESC) |
| Gate driver | 3-phase, ~1.5 A peak, V_GS = 10 V, dead time 100 ns | |
| PWM | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous rectification), 6-step trapezoidal | |
| "80 A" | Per-motor phase current, burst | |

> ⚠️ **Red flag, stated up front:** The math below shows that 80 A on all four motors dissipates roughly **107 W** on a 4 cm² board. That is only survivable for about **1–2 seconds** from a warm start. "80 A burst" on a 20×20 board is a marketing number unless the burst is strictly time-limited by firmware. Section 3 quantifies this, and Section 6 gives the mitigations.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.2 mΩ × 1.6 = **1.92 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current paths in 6-step with synchronous rectification

In any 60° sector, two phase legs carry current:
- **The PWM'd leg:** the high-side conducts for D·T, and the low-side conducts for (1−D)·T.
- **The fixed leg:** one FET stays on continuously.

Because the PWM'd leg's HS and LS share the current over the full period, each active leg contributes I²R regardless of duty:

$$
P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

Phase-current ripple adds a small term (I_rms² = I² + ΔI²/12). With a typical motor inductance of about 15 µH at 48 kHz, ΔI is about 5–10 A. This adds under 1% and is neglected.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Per conducting FET | 80² × 1.92 mΩ = **12.3 W** (instantaneous) |
| Per ESC channel | 2 × 12.3 = **24.6 W** |
| Per leg (time-averaged) | Each leg is active 2/3 of the electrical cycle, so **16.4 W per leg** |
| Hottest FET | At low duty, the LS of the PWM'd leg plus fixed-LS sectors: about 8–10 W average |
| **4 ESCs** | **98.3 W** |

### 1.4 Copper and shunt losses

- **PCB copper:** about 0.15 mΩ effective per phase path, so 2 × 80² × 0.15 mΩ = **1.9 W per ESC**.
- **Shunt:** the battery-side current shunt (0.25 mΩ) carries the battery current, not the phase current. At 80 A phase and roughly 70% duty, I_batt is about 56 A per ESC, or 224 A total. That gives 224² × 0.25 mΩ = **12.5 W**. This term is large, so use the low-side phase-current estimate or a Hall sensor instead.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Transition times (gate-charge method)

$$
t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{G,on}} ,\quad I_{G,on} = \frac{V_{DRV}-V_{plateau}}{R_{G,tot}}
$$

The inputs are V_plateau = 3.5 V and R_G,tot = 4.7 Ω, which gives I_G of about 1.4 A.

The results are:
- t_r ≈ 6 nC / 1.4 A ≈ 4.3 ns ideal. Adding loop inductance and the Miller tail gives about **15 ns** in practice.
- t_f ≈ **15 ns** in practice.
