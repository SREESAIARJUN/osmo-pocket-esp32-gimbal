# Job #31: STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (20×20 "Nano")
## Mathematical Models for Losses, Thermal Limits, and Bidirectional DShot DMA

---

## 0. Read First: Three Findings That Change the Design

**1. The STM32H743 does not switch the MOSFETs.**
On every production AM32 or BLHeli design, each motor channel has its own small ESC MCU (AT32F421, STM32G071 or GD32E230 class). That MCU runs commutation, 48 kHz PWM and the eRPM telemetry encoder. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to the ESC MCUs over bidirectional DShot600.
- Combining the two roles on one MCU is not supported by ArduPilot.
- It would also put 4×48 kHz commutation interrupts on the flight-control core.
- This document therefore models a **1× H743 FC + 4× AT32F421 (AM32)** architecture.

**2. "80 A burst" on a 20×20 mm board is a ~1 second rating, not a sustained one.**
- Section 3 shows the realistic continuous rating is **about 30 A per channel with prop-wash airflow**.
- 80 A is sustainable for roughly **0.6–1.0 s** before junction limits are reached.
- Marketing material must state burst duration and conditions.

**3. Four channels at 80 A on 6S implies roughly 250–290 A of battery current.**
- No 20×20 power-entry pad or XT30/XT60 connector carries that.
- The battery input path, not the FETs, becomes the first failure point.
- Treat 80 A as a per-channel transient peak that does not occur on all four channels at once.

All component parameters below are **representative values for a 40 V, 3.3×3 mm DFN MOSFET class**. Substitute the datasheet values of the selected part before release; every equation is written parametrically so you can do that.
