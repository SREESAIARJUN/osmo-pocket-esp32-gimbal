## 0. Architecture Clarification and Key Assumptions

**The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each ESC channel has its own small MCU (AT32F421 running AM32, or EFM8BB21 running Bluejay). The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bi-directional DShot600 to those four ESC MCUs. The architecture below follows that pattern:

```
[STM32H743 / ArduPilot] --DShot600 bidir x4--> [4x AT32F421 / AM32 @ 48 kHz PWM]
                                                        |
                                                [FD6288Q gate drivers] --> [6x N-FET per motor]
```

**Feasibility flag.** 80 A per channel on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board is thermally possible only as a **short burst of a few seconds**. The math in §2 shows why. Continuous rating will land around 25–35 A per channel.

## 1. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 1.1 Conduction model

Assumptions for trapezoidal six-step commutation with **complementary (synchronous) PWM**:
- At any instant, current flows through exactly **one high-side path** (either the HS FET, or the LS FET of the same leg during the off-time) and **one low-side FET** of the return phase.
- Phase current equals I_dc during conduction (the 2/3 duty per phase averages out across the bridge).

The conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle, to first order:

$$P_{cond}(T_j) = I^2 \cdot \left[\,2\,R_{DS}(T_j) + R_{sh} + R_{cu}\right]$$

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)]$$

### 1.2 Switching losses (only the PWM'd leg switches)

**Hard-switching overlap:**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2}\, V_{bus}\, I\, (t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}$$

$$P_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{e-}9 \times 48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{0.968\ W}$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} \times 2 = 1\text{e-}9 \times 635 \times 48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{0.030\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 50\text{e-}9 \times 25.2 \times 48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period):**

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_d)\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{e-}9 \times 48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{1.229\ W}$$

**Gate drive** (dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET):

$$P_{g} = 2\, Q_g\, V_{GS}\, f_{sw} = 2 \times 45\text{e-}9 \times 10 \times 48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{0.043\ W}$$

> **Design lever:** Dead-time loss exceeds hard-switching loss. Cutting t_d from 200 ns to 100 ns (verify against the FD6288Q propagation mismatch) saves about 0.6 W per channel. This is a cheaper fix than a better FET.

### 1.3 Electrothermal fixed point

R_DS depends on T_j, and T_j depends on loss, so the two must be iterated:

$$T_j = T_{case} + R_{\theta JC}\cdot P_{FET}$$

Iteration result at a burst-end case temperature of 100 °C:

| Iteration | T_j (°C) | R_DS (mΩ) | P per FET in path (W) | P_cond, channel (W) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 100 | 1.34 | 8.6 | 17.2 + 2.9 = 20.1 |
| 1 | 112 | 1.39 | 8.9 | 20.7 |
| 2 | 113 | 1.39 | 8.9 | **20.7** ✔ converged |

The 2.9 W term is shunt plus copper: 6400 × 0.55 mΩ = 3.52 W, of which roughly 2.9 W is on-board (the remainder is in the motor leads).

### 1.4 Per-channel and board totals at 80 A burst, hot

| Loss term | W / channel | W / board (×4) |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (2 × I²R_DS @ 113 °C) | 17.8 | 71.2 |
| Shunt + copper | 2.9 | 11.6 |
| Switching overlap | 0.97 | 3.9 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 | 4.9 |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.09 | 0.4 |
| Gate drive | 0.04 | 0.2 |
| **Total** | **≈ 23.0 W** | **≈ 92 W** |

**Efficiency check.** Output power at 80 A × 25.2 V × 4 is about 8.06 kW. Board losses of 92 W give 98.9 % electrical efficiency. The efficiency is fine; the problem is power density.

## 2. Thermal Model

### 2.1 Lumped RC network (per board)

```
P_total ──┬── R_θJC/n (FETs → copper) ──┬── R_θSA (board/heatsink → air) ── T_amb
          │                              │
        C_j (negligible)            C_board (copper + FR4 + Al plate)
```

**Heat capacity, 25.5 mm board with 1 mm aluminium heat-spreader plate:**

| Element | Mass | c_p | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copper (8 layers, mixed oz) | ~4.5 g | 0.385 J/g·K | 1.73 J/K |
| FR4 | ~3.0 g | 1.1 J/g·K | 3.30 J/K |
| Aluminium plate | ~1.8 g | 0.90 J/g·K | 1.62 J/K |
| FETs, solder, passives | ~1.5 g | ~0.5 J/g·K | 0.75 J/K |
| **C_th total** | | | **≈ 7.4 J/K** |

**Steady-state sink-to-air resistance:**
- About 8 K/W in still air.
- About 3 K/W in strong prop wash, since nano boards usually sit in the airflow.

### 2.2 Burst transient

$$T(t) = T_{amb} + P\,R_{\theta SA}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right), \qquad \tau = R_{\theta SA}\,C_{th} = 3 \times 7.4 \approx 22\ \text{s}$$

Starting from cruise equilibrium (≈ 60 °C board temperature) and applying 92 W:

| Burst duration | Board ΔT | Board T | T_j (+13 K) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 s | +11.8 K | 72 °C | 85 °C | OK |
| 5 s | +28 K | 88 °C | 101 °C | OK |
| 10 s | +52 K | 112 °C | 125 °C | Marginal (FR4 / capacitor limit) |
| 20 s | +88 K | 148 °C | 161 °C | **Fail** |

The initial heating slope is P/C = 92 / 7.4 ≈ 12.4 K/s.
