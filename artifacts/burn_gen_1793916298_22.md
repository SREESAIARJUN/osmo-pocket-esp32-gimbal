## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology

Each channel uses a 3-phase bridge (6 FETs) with complementary (synchronous) PWM. AM32 uses this by default. In any 6-step sector:
- one high-side leg PWMs (HS conducts for D, LS conducts for 1−D),
- one low-side FET is fully on,
- one phase floats (BEMF sensing).

The motor current I therefore always flows through exactly **two channel resistances in series**:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2\left[D\,R_{HS} + (1-D)\,R_{LS} + R_{LS,on}\right] + I^2 R_{cu} \approx I^2\,(2R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + R_{cu})$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \mathrm{K^{-1}}$$

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| R₂₅ | 1.2 mΩ (at V_GS = 10 V) |
| T_j (design) | 125 °C → factor 1.50 → **R = 1.80 mΩ** |
| R_cu (phase pad + battery pour, 2 oz, ~6 mm path) | ≈ 0.25 mΩ per channel |

Note that R_DS depends on T_j, which depends on P, which depends on R_DS. This electro-thermal loop is solved iteratively in §4.4.

### 1.3 Results at 80 A burst

$$P_{cond,ch} = 80^2\,(2\cdot1.80 + 0.25)\,\mathrm{m\Omega} = 6400 \cdot 3.85\ \mathrm{m\Omega} = \mathbf{24.6\ W}$$

Copper share: 6400 × 0.25 mΩ = 1.6 W. FET share: **23.0 W per channel**, so 11.5 W in each of the two conducting FETs at any instant.

**Per-FET time average.** Each FET carries current for 1/3 of the electrical cycle (HS and LS shares sum the same way). The averaged loss is therefore 23.0 / 6 ≈ **3.84 W per FET**, but the **instantaneous** load is 11.5 W. The instantaneous figure matters below about 3 ms per sector. A blocked rotor or low eRPM at full throttle is the worst case: a single FET pair carries the full 11.5 W each.

### 1.4 DC-link capacitor ripple (often overlooked)

$$I_{C,rms} \approx I\sqrt{D(1-D)} \;\xrightarrow{D=0.5}\; 0.5 \cdot 80 = 40\ \mathrm{A_{rms}}\ \text{per channel}$$

The 4 channels are not phase-synchronized, so they partially cancel. Budget about 40–60 A_rms total:
- MLCC bank: 4 × (4×10 µF 50 V X7R 1206) right at the bridges.
- Bulk: low-ESR 35 V 1000 µF electrolytic at the pigtail. Typical ripple rating is about 2–3 A, so it handles **low-frequency** energy only. The MLCCs take the 48 kHz component.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Operating point: V_bus = 25.2 V (6S full), I = 80 A, gate driver FD6288Q-class (~1.0 A source / 1.5 A sink at V_CC = 12 V, plus external R_g).

### 2.1 Hard-switching transition loss (active HS FET)

The transition time is dominated by the Miller plateau:

$$t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,src}},\quad t_{f}\approx\frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,snk}}$$

With Q_gs2 + Q_gd ≈ 14 nC, I_src ≈ 0.8 A (after R_g = 4.7 Ω), and I_snk ≈ 1.0 A:

t_r ≈ 17.5 ns, t_f ≈ 14 ns, so t_r + t_f ≈ 31.5 ns.

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 31.5\,\text{ns}\cdot 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.52\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction (synchronous LS)

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.80 \cdot 80 \cdot 2(100\,\text{ns}) \cdot 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

This is the term most sensitive to dead time. The AM32 dead-time setting should be the minimum safe value for the chosen FETs.
