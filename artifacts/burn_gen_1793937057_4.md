# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal, and DShot Architecture (Job #10)

## 0. Architectural Corrections

Four issues change the design direction and should be resolved first.

| # | Issue | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each ESC channel (or a shared ESC MCU) runs its own firmware, such as AM32 on an AT32F421 or STM32G071, or BLHeli_32. The H743 runs ArduPilot and only *sends* DShot600 and *receives* bidirectional eRPM telemetry. | The 48 kHz PWM, dead time, and gate drive belong to the ESC MCU domain. The DMA model in §4 applies to the H743 side only. |
| 2 | **"80 A burst" must be defined as phase current per channel.** At full throttle (D → 1), battery current ≈ 4 × 80 A = 320 A, which is about 8 kW at 6S. | No XT60 (60 A continuous) or 12–14 AWG lead survives this. The battery interface needs XT90 or AS150 class connectors and 10 AWG leads. |
| 3 | **A "nano" (20×20 mm) footprint cannot host 24 power FETs, an H743, and 80 A copper.** | I model a **30.5×30.5 mm AIO** as the baseline and give 20×20 scaling numbers to show why it fails. |
| 4 | **The component values below are representative, not validated.** The netlists are architecture-grade. | Every part number and parameter must be checked against current datasheets, and the thermal model must be calibrated with a thermocouple/IR burst test before release. |
