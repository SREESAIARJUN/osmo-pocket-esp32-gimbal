# STM32H743 FC + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC Stack (Job #34): Reference Architecture

## 0. Assumptions and Scope

I have no access to Job #34's internal documents. Everything below is a self-consistent reference design built on stated assumptions, and every parameter is marked so you can swap in the real values. Before using it for layout or BOM release, check the items in §6.

### Architectural ground rules

1. **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz motor PWM.** In any real 4-in-1 stack, each motor has a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, for example an AT32F421 or STM32G071. That MCU does the commutation and the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 runs ArduPilot and only sends bidirectional DShot600 commands and receives eRPM telemetry back. The switching-loss model in §2 is therefore an ESC-firmware parameter, not an ArduPilot one.
2. **"Nano" with 80 A burst per motor** is only plausible as a 30.5×30.5 mm stack, with the FC and ESC on separate boards. A single 20×20 mm board cannot carry 4×80 A, either thermally (§3) or in copper cross-section. I assume a two-board stack:
   - FC board: 30.5×30.5 mm, 6-layer.
   - ESC board: 30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, two FETs carry the phase current at every instant:
- The PWM-active high-side FET on phase A, with its complementary low-side FET on A during the off-time.
- The static low-side FET on phase B.

The bridge-leg pair therefore always carries I across 2 × R_DS(on).

**Per motor:**

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I^2 \, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

**Temperature dependence:**

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

### 1.2 Distribution per FET

Over one electrical revolution, each FET conducts 1/3 of the time as a static low side, or splits its conduction with its complement as the PWM leg. At 80 A, the worst-case instantaneous dissipation in one FET while conducting is:

$$P_{FET,inst} = I^2 R_{DS(on)} = 6400 \times R_{DS(on)}$$

Averaged over commutation, each of the 6 FETs carries ≈ 1/3 of P_cond,motor. Because commutation (~1–5 kHz electrical) is far faster than the die thermal time constant (~ms), the electrical-cycle average is what matters thermally.

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx \tfrac{2}{6} \cdot 2 I^2 R = \tfrac{2}{3} I^2 R$$

### 1.3 Self-consistent junction temperature

Conduction loss raises T_j, which raises R_DS(on), which raises the loss. Solve iteratively, using R_θJ-board ≈ 2.5 K/W (junction to case, plus solder and via field):

| Iteration | T_j assumed | R_DS(on) | P_cond,motor |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 25 °C | 0.70 mΩ | 8.96 W |
| 1 | 100 °C | 0.96 mΩ | 12.3 W |
| 2 | 115 °C | 1.02 mΩ | 13.1 W |
| converged | ~118 °C | 1.03 mΩ | **≈ 13.2 W** |

The worst-case instantaneous loss in a single FET is 6400 × 1.03 mΩ = **6.6 W**.

**Option: two FETs in parallel per switch position (12 FETs per motor).** R_eff halves, so P_cond drops to about 6.0 W per motor. At 80 A this is strongly recommended if the board area allows it.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-active leg switches. Per motor, the components are:

### 2.1 Hard-switching (V-I overlap) loss in the high-side FET

$$t_{r,f} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{gate}} = \frac{16\,\text{nC}}{1.0\,\text{A}} = 16\,\text{ns}\ \text{(turn-on)}, \quad \frac{16}{1.5}\approx 11\,\text{ns}\ \text{(turn-off)}$$

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 27\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.31\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction in the low-side FET

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

Dissipated mostly in the high-side FET.

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

The Q_rr datasheet value is specified at low di/dt. At 80 A and fast edges, the real value can be 2–3× higher, so budget **0.15 W**.

### 2.4 Output-capacitance loss

$$P_{oss} = C_{oss,er} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 2\text{nF}\times635\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate-drive loss

This is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET. Only the two FETs of the PWM leg switch at f_sw.

$$P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\times60\text{nC}\times10\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.058\ W}$$

### 2.6 Per-motor loss summary at 80 A, 6S, 48 kHz

| Loss | Watts |
|---|---|
| Conduction (T_j ≈ 118 °C) | 13.2 |
| Switching overlap | 1.31 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61
