## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. In any viable 4-in-1 design:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot)
   ──DShot600 bidirectional (×4, single-wire, half-duplex)──►
4× ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, running AM32 or BLHeli_32)
   ──6× PWM @ 48 kHz──►
3-phase gate driver (FD6288Q)
   ──►
6× N-MOSFET per motor (24 total)
```

The 48 kHz switching loss belongs to the ESC MCU and power stage. The H743 owns DShot timing, DMA, and telemetry decode. Both domains are modelled below.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds(on),25}\,[1+\alpha_R (T_j - 25)]$$

### 1.2 Conducting devices per motor

In 6-step drive, two phases carry current at every instant. With complementary PWM, each active leg always has exactly one FET conducting, either the HS/LS pair or the synchronous freewheel path. Therefore **two R_ds(on) are always in series with I_phase**, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

Each FET is active for 1/3 of the electrical period, split D / (1−D) between HS and LS. The per-FET averages are:

$$P_{cond,HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I^2 R_{ds}, \qquad P_{cond,LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)\, I^2 R_{ds} + \tfrac{1}{3}I^2R_{ds}\ \text{(LS commutated leg)}$$

Average per FET = P_cond,motor / 6. For hotspot design, use the worst-case FET, which is the LS at low D or the HS at high D. That FET carries roughly 0.5 × P_cond,motor at the extremes of the commutation sector, averaged over its active third.

### 1.3 Numeric evaluation

Initial guess T_j = 110 °C:

- R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0045 × 85) = **1.38 mΩ**
- P_cond,motor = 2 × 80² × 1.38 × 10⁻³ = **17.7 W**

Copper path loss: phase trace + bus plane + pad ≈ 0.5 mΩ round trip.

- P_Cu = 80² × 0.5 × 10⁻³ = **3.2 W per motor**

After the iteration in §3.4 (T_j ≈ 124 °C), R_ds = 1.45 mΩ and P_cond,motor = **18.6 W**.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In 6-step drive only **one leg hard-switches per PWM period**: one HS turn-on and one turn-off, with the LS acting as synchronous rectifier.

### 2.1 V–I overlap

Gate current during the Miller plateau:

$$I_g = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}{R_{g,int}+R_{g,ext}} = \frac{10-3}{1.5+4.7} \approx 1.13\ \text{A}$$

- Intrinsic: t_sw ≈ Q_sw / I_g ≈ 7 ns.
- Derated for layout parasitics (common-source inductance ≈ 0.5 nH, driver sourcing limit): **t_r = t_f = 15 ns**.

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

The LS body diode conducts during dead time, then the HS turns on into it:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

Hard recovery also causes ringing. Snubber guidance is in §5.

### 2.3 C_oss discharge (HS + LS)

$$P_{oss} = 2 \times \tfrac12 C_{oss} V^2 f = 1.5\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.046\ W}$$
