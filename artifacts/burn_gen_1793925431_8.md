# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #22): Electro-Thermal Model, DShot DMA Plan, and Reference Netlist

## 0. Architecture Clarifications

These points shape everything that follows.

1. **The H743 does not switch the MOSFETs.** A production 4-in-1 ESC uses four dedicated ESC MCUs, typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or EFM8 running BLHeli_S/Bluejay. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by those MCUs. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to them. I model it that way.
2. **"80 A" must be defined.** I treat 80 A as **RMS phase current per motor, burst**. Battery-side DC current is roughly duty × I_phase, so it is lower.
3. **Assumed operating point:** 6S (25.2 V full), "nano" means a 20×20 mm stack-sized PCB, 8-layer with 2 oz copper.
4. **Feasibility.** The thermal result (§3) shows 80 A on all four motors on a 20×20 board is limited to about 1 second, not a sustained or multi-second burst. This should drive the product rating.

All device parameters are **representative assumptions**, labeled as such. Replace them with datasheet values for the selected BOM.

## 2. Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 2.1 Conduction loss with electro-thermal feedback

With complementary (synchronous) 6-step commutation, phase current always flows through **two conducting channels in series**: one high-side and one low-side path, each alternating with its synchronous partner. Therefore:

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2 \cdot R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond (per motor) |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.50 mΩ | 6400 × 2 × 1.5e-3 = **19.2 W** |
| 100 °C | 2.06 mΩ | **26.4 W** |
| 125 °C | 2.25 mΩ | **28.8 W** |

Because loss rises with temperature, the system is a positive-feedback loop. Thermal runaway is avoided only if the following condition holds:

$$\frac{\partial P_{cond}}{\partial T_j}\cdot R_{\theta,JA} < 1 \;\Rightarrow\; 2 I^2 R_{ds,25}\,\alpha\,R_{\theta,JA} < 1$$

At 80 A this gives 0.096 W/K × R_θJA < 1, so **R_θJA < 10.4 K/W per channel**. That is satisfiable in steady state only with good copper and airflow. In practice, the burst duration is limited by heat capacity (§3), not by runaway.

### 2.2 Switching loss (hard-switched edge, 48 kHz)

Only the PWM-switched FET in each commutation state hard-switches. The edge time is approximately Miller-dominated:

$$t_r \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g} = 8\text{ ns}$$

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 16\text{e-9} \times 48\text{e3} = \mathbf{0.77\ W}$$

### 2.3 Secondary switching-related terms

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Dead-time diode | V_f · I · 2t_dt · f_sw | 0.8×80×100e-9×48e3 = **0.31 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw | **0.048 W** |
| C_oss | Q_oss · V_bus · f_sw | **0.036 W** |
| Gate drive (driver dissipation) | 2 · Q_g · V_gs · f_sw | **0.038 W** |
| Shunt | I² · R_shunt | **1.28 W** |

### 2.4 Per-channel and board totals at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

$$P_{ch} \approx 28.8 + 0.77 + 0.31 + 0.05 + 0.04 + 0.04 + 1.28 \approx \mathbf{31.3\ W}$$

$$P_{board,4\times} \approx \mathbf{125\ W}\ (\text{plus ~1 W for H743, regulators, and ESC MCUs})$$

**Interpretation:**
- Conduction is about 92% of the loss. Switching frequency is not the problem at 48 kHz.
- Raising f_sw to 96 kHz adds only about 1.1 W per channel.
- The real lever is total R_ds. Paralleling FETs (if area allows) halves P_cond.

Electrical efficiency at full duty is about 1 − 31.3/(25.2·80) ≈ **98.4%**. Even so, 125 W in a 4 cm² board is extreme.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Junction-level (fast) transient

Per FET at 80 A: P ≈ 14.4 W (half the channel conduction loss, worst-case pairing).

$$\Delta T_{JC} = P \cdot R_{\theta JC} = 14.4 \times 1.5 \approx \mathbf{22\ K}$$

The die time constant is roughly milliseconds, so this offset is reached almost immediately. The junction therefore sits about 22 K above the local copper during a burst.

### 3.2 Board-level (slow) lumped heat capacity

| Component | Mass | c_p | C_th |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copper (8 L × 70 µm × 4 cm²) | 2.0 g | 0.385 J/g·K | 0.77 J/K |
| FR-4 (1.6 mm × 4 cm²) | 1.2 g | 1.1 J/g·K | 1.32 J/K |
| FETs, caps, solder | ~1.0 g | ~0.7 | 0.7 J/K |
| **Total** | | | **≈ 2.8 J/K** |

The single-node model is:

$$C_{th}\frac{dT_b}{dt} = P_{loss}(T) - \frac{T_b - T_a}{R_{\theta,BA}}$$

With R_θ,BA ≈ 6 K/W (20×20 board, prop wash, no heatsink), the steady-state rise would be 125 × 6 = 750 K. This is physically impossible, so **only transient operation exists at 80 A ×4**.

Adiabatic slope:

$$\frac{dT}{dt} \approx \frac{125}{2.8} \approx \mathbf{45\ K/s}$$

Burst time budget from T_b = 50 °C to T_j,max = 150 °C (T_b,max = 128 °C after the 22 K J-C offset):

$$t_{burst} \approx \frac{128-50}{45} \approx \mathbf{1.7\ s\ (all\ 4\ motors)}$$

For a single motor bursting with heat spreading across the board, the budget is about 5–7 s.
