## 0. Scope, Assumptions and a Reality Check

These points shape every result below.

1. **The H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC MCU.** It runs ArduPilot. The 4-in-1 ESC uses its own motor MCUs, typically 4× AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or one MCU per motor running BLHeli_32-class firmware. The H743 talks to them over bi-directional DShot600.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board is a transient rating, not a continuous one.** The math in §3 shows the board survives about 2–3 s at 80 A per motor with all four motors loaded. The thermally sustainable continuous current is about **27 A per motor**. Burst ratings on spec sheets for this form factor should be read in that light.
3. **All component parameters are representative, not taken from a specific datasheet.** Before freezing the design, substitute your real MOSFET, driver and PCB stack-up values into the equations. Each equation is written so that substitution is straightforward.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{ds} = 0.80 \times 1.45 = 1.16\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Current path in one commutation interval (60° electrical)

Two legs are active in each interval:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side conducts for a fraction D of each PWM period. The synchronous low-side of the same leg freewheels for the remaining (1−D).
- **Return leg:** the low-side is on continuously.

Summing these gives

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 R_{ds}\,[\,D + (1-D) + 1\,] = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}$$

**At 80 A:**

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.16\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{14.85\ W\ per\ motor}$$

### 1.3 Distribution across individual FETs (averaged over one electrical cycle)

Each switch is active for 120° of the electrical cycle.

**High-side FET:**

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} D \cdot I^2R = 0.30 \times 7.42 = 2.23\ \text{W}$$

**Low-side FET:**

$$P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,[1 + (1-D)]\cdot I^2R = 0.367 \times 7.42 = 2.72\ \text{W}$$

**Check:** 3 × (2.23 + 2.72) = 14.85 W ✓

The low-side FETs are the thermal hot spots. Give them the larger thermal-via fields.
