# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage & DShot Design Model

**Job #19 · Architecture Rev A**

## 0. Feasibility Verdict

**A 20×20 mm 4-in-1 ESC cannot sustain 80 A per motor.** On a nano footprint, the 80 A rating is valid for about **2–3 s** under good prop-wash cooling. Defensible continuous ratings are about **25 A/motor** for a 20×20 board and about **40 A/motor** for a 30.5×30.5 board. Section 2 derives this, and every assumption is stated so you can substitute datasheet values.

**Architecture:** The STM32H743 is the flight controller. It does not drive the FETs. Each motor needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32 (e.g., STM32G071 or AT32F421). The H743's job is to generate bidirectional DShot600 and capture the eRPM telemetry that comes back.

```
6S LiPo ──► 4-in-1 ESC: [ESC MCU ×4] → [3-ph gate driver ×4] → [6 MOSFETs ×4] → Motors
                 ▲ bidir DShot600 (4 lines), Vbat, Curr, GND, TLM-UART
STM32H743 FC ────┘
```

## 2. Loss Model (per motor)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

The ESC uses 6-step commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. At every instant, the phase current flows through exactly two channels in series:

- the PWM leg (its high-side FET, or the low-side FET acting as synchronous rectifier), and
- the low-side FET of the return leg.

Because of this, the conduction loss is independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

- R_DS = 0.80 mΩ × 1.38 = **1.10 mΩ**
- **P_cond = 2 × 80² × 1.10 mΩ = 14.1 W**

Additional resistive paths:

- PCB copper and solder joints, about 0.3 mΩ per path: 2 × 80² × 0.3 mΩ ≈ **3.8 W**
- Shunt resistor (0.25 mΩ on the DC bus, using the I_DC ≈ I approximation at high duty): ≈ **1.6 W**

The R_DS loss has positive feedback with temperature. The electrothermal fixed point is:

$$T_j = T_b + R_{\theta}\,P(T_j)$$
