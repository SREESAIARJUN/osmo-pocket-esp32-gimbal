# STM32H743 Nano AIO with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #19): Architecture, Loss Model, DShot/DMA Plan, Netlists

## 0. Architecture Decisions and Assumptions

Some implicit assumptions in the brief would cause problems, so they are fixed here first.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot has no in-tree FOC or 6-step commutation at 48 kHz. The topology is therefore:
   - An H743 flight controller sends DShot600 to four ESC MCUs (AT32F421 running AM32, one per motor).
   - Each ESC MCU drives a 3-phase gate driver and six N-channel MOSFETs.
   - The 48 kHz figure is the AM32 PWM frequency. It is not an H743 timer rate.
2. **"80 A burst" is per-motor phase current, and it is transient only.** Section 3 shows that a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board cannot sustain this. The rating must be defined as a time-limited burst, for example "80 A for ≤ 1 s, single motor" or "4×80 A for ≤ 0.5 s". This is an engineering result, not a marketing number.
3. **Device parameters are representative placeholders.** They describe a 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm PDFN MOSFET. Every symbol is kept explicit so you can substitute the values from your selected datasheet.

| Symbol | Meaning | Value used |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation state

AM32 trapezoidal operation uses complementary (synchronous) PWM on the active high-side leg. In any 60° sector:

- **Switched leg:** the HS is on for duty D and the LS is on for (1−D). Current flows through exactly one device of that leg at all times, apart from the dead time.
- **Return leg:** the LS is on 100 % of the sector.
- **Floating leg:** off, used for BEMF sensing.

So two R_ds,on are always in series with the phase current I_ph, and conduction loss is nearly independent of D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2\,\big[2R_{ds}(T_j) + R_{cu}\big]\cdot\Big(1 - \tfrac{2t_{dt}}{T_{sw}}\Big)$$

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

### 1.2 Evaluation at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

- R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0055 × 100) = 1.55 mΩ
- I² = 6400 A²
- 2R_ds + R_cu = 3.10 + 0.40 = 3.50 mΩ
- Dead-time correction: 1 − 2(100 ns)(48 kHz) = 0.9904

$$P_{cond} = 6400 \times 3.50\times10^{-3} \times 0.990 = \mathbf{22.2\ W\ per\ motor}$$

Of this, 19.6 W is in the FETs and 2.6 W is in the copper.

### 1.3 Per-device distribution (hottest device)

Each LS conducts for one 120° sector as the return leg (100 %) and during the (1−D) portion of an adjacent 120° sector. Averaged over an electrical cycle at D = 0.5:

$$P_{LS} = I^2 R_{ds}\left[\tfrac{1}{3} + \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)\right] = 9.92 \times 0.5 = 4.96\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{HS} = I^2 R_{ds}\cdot\tfrac{1}{3}D = 1.65\ \text{W (plus all switching loss, see §2)}$$

**Design consequence:** in synchronous trapezoidal drive, the low-side FETs carry most of the conduction loss. Place LS devices over the best copper and via fields.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the switched leg's HS sees hard transitions. The LS turns on via ZVS after body-diode conduction, but it suffers the dead-time diode loss and causes Q_rr loss in the HS.

| Term | Equation | Value at 80 A, 25.2 V |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw | ½·25.2·80·30 ns·48 k = **1.45 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 40 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.048 W** |
| Coss | ½·C_oss,er·V²·f_sw | **0.015 W** |
| Dead-time diode | V_f·I·2t_dt·f_sw | 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (6 FETs, dissipated in driver) | 6·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw | **0.144 W** |

Total switching-related loss is **2.27 W per motor**.

Sensitivity: P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.1 W per motor at 80 A, at the cost of audible noise and higher current ripple on low-inductance motors.

**Transition time from gate charge:** t_sw ≈ Q_gd / I_g = 8 nC / 0.5 A ≈ 16 ns. This confirms the 15 ns assumption only if the driver sources about 0.5–1 A at the plateau voltage. Keep the gate resistor R_g ≤ 5 Ω, and add a fast turn-off diode.
