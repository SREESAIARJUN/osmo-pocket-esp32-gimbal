## 0. Two Architecture Corrections Before the Math

**1. The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In a 4-in-1 design, the H743 is the flight controller. It runs ArduPilot and generates DShot. Each motor channel needs its own ESC MCU running the commutation firmware:
- Typical choices: AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or EFM8BB21 running BLHeli_S/Bluejay.
- The math in Sections 1–3 applies to the ESC power stage.
- Section 4 applies to the H743.

**2. 80 A burst on a 20×20 "nano" board cannot be sustained.** Section 3 shows that 80 A is a thermal-capacitance-limited event lasting about 1 s, not a rating.
- Commercial 20×20 boards usually claim 40–60 A burst.
- If 80 A is a hard requirement, move to a 30×30 mm board with 5×6 mm FETs.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (Per ESC Channel)

### 1.1 Topology

- Three half-bridges, six N-FETs per channel, 24 FETs total.
- Trapezoidal six-step commutation (AM32 default).
- Complementary (synchronous) PWM on the active high-side leg.

During any commutation step:
- **PWM leg:** the high-side (HS) FET conducts for a fraction D of each period. The synchronous low-side (LS) FET of the same leg conducts for (1−D).
- **Return leg:** the LS FET is on continuously.
- **Third leg:** floating, used for back-EMF sensing.

### 1.2 Equations

Temperature-dependent on-resistance:

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.004\text{–}0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

With phase current $I_{ph}$, ignoring ripple:

$$P_{cond,PWMleg} = I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}(D) + I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}(1-D) = I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}$$
$$P_{cond,return} = I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}$$
$$\boxed{P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + I_{ph}^2 R_{Cu}}$$

Ripple correction, when needed:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}-E_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

Battery current and phase current are related by $I_{batt} \approx D\cdot I_{ph}$. At burst (near full throttle) D → 1, so the two are approximately equal. At partial throttle, phase current exceeds battery current. Make sure you know which one the "80 A" specification refers to.

### 1.3 Numbers (Assumed FET Class: 40 V, 3.3×3.3 DFN)

| Parameter | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| $R_{25}$ at $V_{GS}$ = 10 V | 1.2 mΩ |
| $T_j$ | 125 °C, so factor 1.5 → $R_{DS}$ = 1.8 mΩ |
| $R_{Cu}$ (phase path, 2 oz, short pours) | 0.3 mΩ |
| $I_{ph}$ | 80 A |

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2(80^2)(1.8\text{m}) + 80^2(0.3\text{m}) = 23.0 + 1.9 = \mathbf{24.9\ W/channel}$$

- The hottest FET (continuous return LS) dissipates **11.5 W**.
- With all four channels at burst, the board dissipates about **100 W** of conduction loss alone.

## 2. Switching Loss at $f_{sw}$ = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The synchronous LS turns on after dead time (body diode first), so it is approximately ZVS.

$$P_{sw} = \underbrace{\tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw}}_{\text{V-I overlap}} + \underbrace{Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}}_{\text{reverse recovery}} + \underbrace{\tfrac12 Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}}_{\text{C}_{oss}} + \underbrace{2V_F I_{ph} t_{dt} f_{sw}}_{\text{dead-time diode}} + \underbrace{2Q_gV_{drv}f_{sw}}_{\text{gate drive}}$$

Assumptions:
- 6S bus: $V_{bus}$ = 25.2 V
- Combined rise and fall: $t_r+t_f$ = 40 ns (FD6288-class driver, ~10 Ω gate path)
- $Q_{rr}$ = 40 nC, $Q_{oss}$ = 30 nC, $Q_g$ = 50 nC
- $V_F$ = 0.8 V, dead time $t_{dt}$ = 100 ns, $V_{drv}$ = 10 V

| Term | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | 0.5·25.2·80·40n·48k | 1.94 W |
| Reverse recovery | 40n·25.2·48k | 0.05 W |
| C_oss | 0.5·30n·25.2·48k | 0.02 W |
| Dead-time diode | 2·0.8·80·100n·48k | 0.61 W |
| Gate drive (mostly in driver/R_g) | 2·50n·10·48k | 0.05 W |
| **Total** | | **≈ 2.7 W/channel** |

Notes on switching loss:
- Switching loss scales linearly with both $f_{sw}$ and $I_{ph}$.
- Moving from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves about 1.3 W per channel at 80 A, at the cost of audible noise and current ripple.
- At 100 % duty, switching loss goes to zero.
- **Worst-case total occurs near D ≈ 0.85–0.95 at high current.**

**Channel total:** about **27.6 W**. **Board total (4 channels):** about **110 W**, plus about 1–2 W for the H743, regulators, and ESC MCUs.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Steady State (Shows Why 80 A Is Burst-Only)

- Board area is 20×20 mm, top and bottom: A = 8×10⁻⁴ m².
- With prop-wash forced convection (h ≈ 80 W/m²K): $R_{\theta,BA} = 1/(hA)$ ≈ 15.6 K/W.
- Predicted steady rise: $\Delta T$ = 110 × 15.6 ≈ 1700 K.

This is physically impossible, so **80 A has no steady-state operating point.** The sustainable continuous current is set by the maximum allowed board rise:

$$I_{cont} \approx \sqrt{\frac{\Delta T_{max}/R_{\theta,BA}}{4\,(2R_{DS}+R_{Cu}) + \text{sw}}}$$

With ΔT = 70 K, the total board budget is about 4.5 W, which gives roughly **12–15 A per channel continuous** in still air on 20×20. Real flight airflow helps substantially, so measure h on your frame.
