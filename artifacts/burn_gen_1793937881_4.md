## 0. Architecture Clarification

The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs directly. A production 4-in-1 architecture has two layers:

```
 ┌───────────────────────────┐   4× DShot600 (bidir, 1-wire)   ┌─────────────────────────┐
 │ STM32H743 (ArduPilot FC)  │ ──────────────────────────────▶ │ 4× ESC MCU (AT32F421 /  │
 │ 480 MHz, timers + DMAMUX  │ ◀── GCR eRPM telemetry ──────── │ STM32G071, AM32 fw)     │
 └───────────────────────────┘                                 │ 48 kHz PWM, 6-step/FOC  │
                                                               └──────────┬──────────────┘
                                                                          │ 3-ph gate driver
                                                                   24× N-MOSFET (6 per motor)
```

- **48 kHz is the ESC firmware PWM frequency.** AM32 supports up to 48 kHz. It is not an H743 timer rate.
- **The H743 only generates DShot frames and captures the eRPM replies.**

### Feasibility Warning

At "nano" form factors, 80 A per motor is a **burst-only** figure. Section 3 shows that a 30×30 mm board is limited to about 25–30 A continuous per motor, and to roughly 2–4 s at 80 A, even with prop-wash cooling. A 20×20 mm board is worse. Size the marketing rating to the math, not the reverse.

## 2. Loss Model (per motor, then ×4)

### 2.1 Commutation Topology

In 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM, current always flows through **two conducting devices in series**:

- **During PWM on-time:** HS(phase A) → winding → LS(phase B).
- **During off-time:** LS(phase A) synchronous rectification → winding → LS(phase B).

At any instant, the conduction path is two MOSFET channels. Under 100 % or near-100 % throttle at burst, assume a continuous two-device path.

### 2.2 Conduction Loss

$$
P_{cond} = I_{pk}^2 \cdot 2 \cdot R_{DS,hot} = 80^2 \cdot 2 \cdot 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W}
$$

Including partial-throttle current ripple: $I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \Delta I^2/12$. With a ripple of $\Delta I \approx 10\,\text{A}$, the correction is +0.13 %, which is negligible.

### 2.3 Hard-Switching Loss

One device per motor hard-switches at f_sw. In the linear V-I overlap approximation:

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{pk}(t_r + t_f) f_{sw}
= 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

### 2.4 Dead-Time (Body-Diode) Conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I_{pk} \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

### 2.5 Reverse Recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{nC}\cdot 25.2\cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}
$$

### 2.6 Coss Loss

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \approx 0.5\cdot 25\text{nC}\cdot 25.2\cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.015\ W}
$$

### 2.7 Gate Drive Loss

The two complementary devices of the PWM leg switch:

$$
P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\cdot 60\text{nC}\cdot 10\cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.058\ W}
$$

This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET die.

### 2.8 Copper Loss

$$
P_{cu} = I^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \cdot 0.5\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.2\ W}
$$
