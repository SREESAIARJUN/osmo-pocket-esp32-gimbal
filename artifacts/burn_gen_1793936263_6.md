## 0. Design Assumptions and Feasibility Findings

| Parameter | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal | Requires 40 V FETs |
| Burst current | 80 A per motor, 4 motors | **320 A battery-side at full duty** |
| PWM frequency | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous rectification) | Typical for AM32 / BLHeli_32 |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal, 2 legs active | |
| ESC MCUs | 4 × AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 | The H743 does **not** drive gates directly |
| FC MCU | STM32H743 at 480 MHz, timer clock 240 MHz | |
| Form factor | "Nano" (20 × 20 mm mount; board ~25 × 25 mm) | |

**Key findings to review before reading further:**

1. **80 A per motor on a nano board is a ~1–2 s burst rating, not a continuous one.** Section 3 shows roughly 85 W of dissipation against about 2.2 J/K of board heat capacity, which gives a rise of about 39 K/s.
2. **Commutation loop inductance is the critical risk.** At 80 A and 15 ns edges, 10 nH of loop inductance produces a 53 V spike on 40 V FETs (Section 2.3). Layout and edge-rate control are mandatory.
3. **Bus capacitor ripple current reaches about 40 A RMS per motor.** Bulk capacitance must be shared and interleaved.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Path

With complementary PWM and 6-step commutation, the motor current always flows through **exactly two FETs** at any instant:
- one FET in the PWM leg (high-side during on-time, low-side during off-time);
- the low-side FET in the static leg.

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \cdot I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature-Dependent R<sub>DS(on)</sub>

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}\ \text{(Si trench, 40 V)}$$

Reference FET: 40 V, PQFN 3.3 × 3.3 mm, R<sub>25</sub> = 1.0 mΩ max at V<sub>GS</sub> = 10 V.

| T<sub>j</sub> | R<sub>DS(on)</sub> | P<sub>cond</sub> per motor at 80 A | 4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W | 51.2 W |
| 100 °C | 1.375 mΩ | 17.6 W | 70.4 W |
| 125 °C | 1.50 mΩ | **19.2 W** | **76.8 W** |

### 1.3 Per-FET Distribution (Hottest Device)

Each leg is active for 240° of the 360° electrical cycle:
- 120° as the PWM leg;
- 120° as the static leg (low-side on).

At duty D:
- High-side FET: $P_{HS} = I^2 R \cdot \tfrac{1}{3} D$
- Low-side FET: $P_{LS} = I^2 R \left[\tfrac{1}{3}(1-D) + \tfrac{1}{3}\right]$

At D → 1 and 125 °C (I²R = 9.6 W):
- P<sub>HS</sub> ≈ 3.2 W
- P<sub>LS</sub> ≈ 3.2 W

The six FETs sum to 19.2 W, which checks against Section 1.2.

**Note: thermal runaway feedback.** Because R<sub>DS(on)</sub> rises with T<sub>j</sub>, solve iteratively:

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + P(T_j)\cdot R_{\theta jc}$$

The solution converges when $I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_{\theta jc} \ll 1$. Here that term is 6400 × 0.001 × 0.005 × 1.5 ≈ 0.05, so it is stable.
