# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #11): Architecture, Loss Model, DShot DMA Plan and Netlists

## 0. Feasibility Constraints

Several requirements conflict and must be resolved before layout:

1. **"Nano" vs. 80 A burst.** A 20×20 mm board cannot carry 24 MOSFETs rated for 80 A per motor. At four simultaneous 80 A motors the stack dissipates about 110 W (Section 3). This model therefore assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm** ESC board with 5×6 mm FETs. The flight controller can stay 20×20 if stacked, or the design can be a combined 30.5 mm AIO. "80 A" is treated as a **≤ 2 s per-motor burst**, not continuous.
2. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** Each of the four ESC channels needs its own ESC MCU (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32) to generate 48 kHz complementary PWM and handle BEMF zero-crossing. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 and receives eRPM.
3. **The 48 kHz PWM and DShot600 are independent clock domains.** 48 kHz is the ESC switching frequency. DShot is the command link, with the loop rate set by ArduPilot (for example 400 Hz to 1 kHz frames).
4. **Rails.** The model assumes 6S: V_bus nominal 22.2 V, maximum 25.2 V, design value 25.2 V. Use 60 V FETs; 40 V parts on 6S are marginal under regen and ringing.

All numbers below are design estimates against stated assumptions. Final values must come from the chosen FET datasheet and bench thermography.
