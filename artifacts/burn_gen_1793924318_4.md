# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #66): Architecture, Loss Model, DShot DMA Plan, and Netlists

## 0. Feasibility Findings

These findings constrain everything that follows.

1. **The H743 should not drive the motors directly.** A 48 kHz, 6-step or FOC commutation loop for four motors, plus bidirectional DShot, needs dedicated ESC MCUs. The architecture below uses an **STM32H743 flight controller running ArduPilot** and **4× AT32F421 ESC MCUs running AM32**, which supports bidirectional DShot and 48 kHz PWM.
2. **80 A is a burst rating, not a continuous one.** On a 20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm board, the thermal model (§3) shows 80 A per channel is sustainable for about **1 s** before T_j approaches 125 °C. Realistic continuous current is about **25–35 A per channel** with prop-wash cooling.
3. **All four channels cannot burst simultaneously.** 4 × 80 A at 25 V is about 8 kW, which far exceeds an XT60 connector (~60 A continuous) and any nano-class pack. The board should be specified as "80 A per channel burst, ~120–150 A total pack-limited."
4. **The board needs an external bulk capacitor.** MLCCs alone cannot hold the DC link (§2.6). A low-ESR 35 V, ≥470–1000 µF electrolytic at the battery pads is mandatory.
5. **Recommended form factor:** a stacked FC board over an ESC board, each 20×20 mm, with M2 mounting. A single-PCB AIO with an H743 plus 24 MOSFETs only fits at 25.5×25.5 mm or larger.
