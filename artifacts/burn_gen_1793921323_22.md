# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model (Job #6)

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

Two issues need resolving before the math, because they change the model.

**1. The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz motor PWM.** In every practical 4-in-1 ESC, each bridge is commutated by its own ESC MCU running BLHeli_32 or AM32. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot600 to those MCUs. The design is therefore:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──DShot600 bidir──► 4× ESC MCU (e.g., AT32F421, AM32) ──► 4× 3-phase gate driver ──► 24 MOSFETs
```

**2. "Nano" and "80 A burst" conflict thermally.** Section 3 shows that a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 board can survive 80 A only for about 1–2 s. Continuous ratings will be roughly 25–35 A per channel. The model gives you the burst-duration equation so the rating can be stated honestly.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model (6-step trapezoidal, complementary PWM)

During any 60° commutation sector, one phase is PWM-high, one is held low, and one floats. With synchronous rectification:

- The PWM leg conducts through the high-side FET for duty D and the low-side FET for (1 − D). Its share is I²R·(D + 1 − D) = I²R.
- The held-low leg contributes I²R continuously.

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

At 80 A and 125 °C:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 6400 \times 0.0024 = \mathbf{30.7\ W\ per\ ESC}$$

Each FET conducts during 2 of every 6 sectors, but the sync-rect sharing equalizes the load. Averaged over an electrical revolution (fine whenever f_elec ≫ 1/τ_die, i.e. above about 100 Hz):

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx P_{cond,ESC}/6 = 5.1\ \text{W}$$

**Copper loss.** With 2 oz copper (≈0.25 mΩ/□), a 3-square phase path adds about 0.75 mΩ. That is 80² × 0.75 m = 4.8 W per phase path, roughly 9.6 W per ESC with two active paths. It is not negligible: use 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner layers, parallel planes, and vias stitched under the FET drain tabs.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

Hard switching occurs in the PWM leg only: one hard-switched FET per sector.

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·30 ns·48 k | **1.45 W** |
| Output capacitance | ½·C_oss,er·V²·f_sw = ½·1.5 n·635·48 k | 0.023 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 30 n·25.2·48 k | 0.036 W |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_f·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k | **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (two FETs per leg) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw | 0.038 W (dissipated in driver/R_g) |
| **Total switching per ESC** | | **≈ 2.2 W** |

Rise time estimate: t_r ≈ Q_gd / I_g = 10 nC / ~0.7 A ≈ 15 ns.

**Interpretation:**
- Conduction dominates (30.7 W vs 2.2 W). Raising f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz costs only about 1 W per ESC, so 48 kHz is justified for current ripple and acoustic reasons.
- Dead time is the largest controllable switching term. Reducing t_dt from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per ESC. Verify shoot-through margin at temperature before doing so.

**Total burst dissipation:**

$$P_{ESC} = 30.7 + 2.2 + 9.6_{Cu} \approx 42.5\ \text{W}, \qquad P_{board} = 4 \times 42.5 = \mathbf{170\ W}$$

This assumes all four motors at 80 A simultaneously, which is the worst case. Realistic punch-outs are lower.
