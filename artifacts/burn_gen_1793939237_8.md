# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #21): Architecture, Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and DShot DMA Allocation

## 0. Feasibility Review

These findings shape every number that follows, so they come first.

| Requirement | Finding |
|---|---|
| H743 + 4-in-1 ESC | The H743 runs ArduPilot and cannot also commutate 4 motors. The ESC section uses four dedicated ESC MCUs (e.g., AT32F421 running AM32) commanded by the H743 over bidirectional DShot600. |
| 80 A per channel on a "nano" board (20×20 / 25.5×25.5 mm) | **Not sustainable.** The model in §3 shows about 88 W total dissipation at 4×80 A. Board thermal mass allows only about 1–3 s before limits are reached, and continuous capability is about 12–18 A/channel without a heatsink. The 80 A figure is treated as a **transient burst rating with a defined duration**. |
| 48 kHz PWM | Viable, but it costs about 2.7 W/channel at 80 A. 24 kHz recovers about 1.2 W/channel. |
| 6S operation | 40 V FETs are required (V_bus max 25.2 V plus ringing). |

The derivation is parametric. Swap in your actual FET datasheet values before release.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Definitions

| Symbol | Meaning | Example value |
|---|---|---|
| I | phase current (burst) | 80 A |
| V_bus | bus voltage | 25.2 V |
| f_sw | PWM frequency | 48 kHz |
| D | duty cycle | — |
| R_ds25 | R_ds(on) at 25 °C, V_gs=10 V | 1.0 mΩ (40 V, 3.3×3.3 or 5×6 class) |
| α | R_ds temperature coefficient | 0.005 /K |
| t_r, t_f | current rise and fall times | 20 ns each |
| Q_rr | body-diode reverse recovery charge | 40 nC |
| Q_oss | output charge | 50 nC |
| Q_g | total gate charge | 50 nC |
| V_drv | gate drive voltage | 10 V |
| V_f | body-diode forward voltage | 0.8 V |
| t_dt | dead time | 100 ns |

### 2.2 Temperature-Dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.0 mΩ × 1.5 = **1.5 mΩ**.

### 2.3 Conduction Loss: 6-Step Trapezoidal with Complementary (Synchronous) PWM

During each commutation sector:
- the PWM'd high-side FET conducts for a fraction D,
- its complementary low-side FET conducts for (1−D),
- the low-side FET of the return phase conducts 100% of the time.

$$P_{cond}=I^2R_{ds}\,D+I^2R_{ds}(1-D)+I^2R_{ds}=2I^2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

The result is independent of D, which is a useful worst-case property. At 80 A:

$$P_{cond}=2\times80^2\times1.5\,\text{m}\Omega=\mathbf{19.2\ W/channel}$$

Per-FET distribution over a full electrical cycle (each FET is active in 1/3 of sectors as switching and 1/3 as return):
- Return low-side: I²R/3 ≈ 3.2 W (time-averaged)
- PWM high-side: I²R·D/3

### 2.4 Switching Loss

**Hard-switching V–I overlap:**

$$P_{VI}=\tfrac12V_{bus}I(t_r+t_f)f_{sw}=0.5(25.2)(80)(40\text{ns})(48\text{k})=\mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}=40\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k}=0.048\ W$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss}\approx\tfrac12Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}=0.030\ W$$

**Dead-time diode conduction:**

$$P_{dt}=V_fI\,(2t_{dt})f_{sw}=0.8(80)(200\text{n})(48\text{k})=\mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**Gate drive** (2 switching FETs per sector; this is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs):

$$P_g=2Q_gV_{drv}f_{sw}=0.048\ W$$

**Total switching loss:**

$$P_{sw}\approx 2.68\ W/channel$$

### 2.5 Copper (PCB) Loss

Assume 6 layers of 2 oz copper carrying phase and bus current, with an effective path resistance of R_cu ≈ 0.4 mΩ (bus + phase pad to motor wire):

$$P_{cu}=I^2R_{cu}\approx 2.6\ W/channel$$

This term is often ignored and is significant.

### 2.6 Totals at 80 A, 48 kHz, T_j = 125 °C

| Loss term | Per channel | Board (×4) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching (all terms) | 2.7 W | 10.7 W |
| PCB copper | 2.6 W | 10.2 W |
| **Total** | **24.5 W** | **≈98 W** |

Changing to 24 kHz reduces the board total by about 5.4 W. **Conduction loss dominates**, so the effective levers are lower R_ds (paralleled FETs) and lower current, not lower frequency.
