# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #6): Power, Thermal, and Bi-Directional DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope and Governing Assumptions

Several points need to be fixed before any equations, because two of them change the conclusions.

1. **The STM32H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC MCU.** ArduPilot runs on the H743. Each motor bridge is commutated by its own ESC MCU (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32), and the two talk over bi-directional DShot600. The H743 never drives gates directly.
2. **"Nano" means 20 × 20 mm mounting.** That gives roughly 26 × 26 mm of board outline. As §3 shows, sustained 80 A per motor is not physically achievable on this form factor. "80 A burst" here means a time-limited transient, and I compute the actual time limit rather than assume it.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0067\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 100 °C: R ≈ 0.9 × 1.5 = **1.35 mΩ**.

Because R rises with T_j, the loss and junction temperature must be solved together (§3.3).

### 1.2 Six-step current sharing

Consider one 60° sector: phase A high side is PWM'd at duty D, and phase B low side is held on.

- The A high-side FET conducts for D of the time. The A low-side FET conducts as a synchronous rectifier for (1−D).
- The B low-side FET conducts for 100% of the sector.

Each phase spends 120° of 360° (1/3) as the "PWM-high" phase and 1/3 as the "solid-low" phase. Per FET, averaged over an electrical cycle:

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,D\,I^2R \qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)\,I^2R + \tfrac{1}{3}I^2R = \tfrac{1}{3}(2-D)\,I^2R$$

Per leg, P_HS + P_LS = (2/3)I²R. Per motor (3 legs):

$$\boxed{P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS(on)}(T_j)}$$

This is independent of D, which makes sense: two FETs are always in the current path. The low-side FET is always the hotter device.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 100 °C

- I²R = 6400 × 1.35 mΩ = 8.64 W (instantaneous, per conducting FET)
- P_cond per motor = **17.3 W**

Worst-case FET at D = 0.5 (low side):

$$P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1.5)(8.64) = 4.32\ \text{W}$$

**Copper path loss.** Assume 0.5 mΩ total from battery pad → bus → FET → phase pad (2 oz, very short):

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 3.2\ \text{W per motor}$$

Note that phase current ≠ battery current. Battery current is I_bat ≈ D·I_ph, so at D = 0.5 an 80 A phase current draws only about 40 A per motor from the pack. **"80 A burst" ratings are almost always phase current.**
