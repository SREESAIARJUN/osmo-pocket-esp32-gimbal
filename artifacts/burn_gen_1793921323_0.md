# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: 80A Burst Thermal, 48 kHz Switching, and Bidirectional DShot600 Architecture

## 0. Assumptions and Corrections

I don't have any prior context for "Job #10," so this document states its assumptions explicitly. Check each one against your actual requirements.

### 0.1 Architecture Correction

The STM32H743 should not commutate the motors directly. Every production AIO board uses a two-tier architecture, and this design does too:

- **Flight controller tier:** One STM32H743 runs ArduPilot. It sends bidirectional DShot600 to the ESCs and receives eRPM telemetry back.
- **ESC tier:** Each motor has its own ESC MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Each MCU drives a 3-phase gate driver and 6 MOSFETs.

The reasons are practical. ArduPilot has no motor commutation stack. Four sensorless 48 kHz commutation loops would also break the H7's real-time scheduling, and DMA would be oversubscribed.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-Dependent R_DS(on)

R_DS(on) rises with junction temperature:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{DS} = 1.0 \times 1.5 = 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Six-Step Trapezoidal Commutation

In each 60° sector, exactly one high-side FET and one low-side FET carry the phase current. The PWM duty cycle is D. The high-side FET conducts for D of each period, and its synchronous complementary FET conducts for (1−D).

Every switching period, the current flows through two FET channels in series (one HS plus one LS or its sync partner). Therefore:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j) \quad \text{(independent of D under synchronous rectification)}$$

Each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical cycle, so:

$$I_{RMS,FET} = I\sqrt{1/3} = 46.2\ \text{A at } I = 80\ \text{A}$$

### 1.3 Conduction Loss at 80 A

**Per channel:**

$$P_{cond,ch} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = 19.2\ \text{W}$$

**Per FET (time-averaged):**

$$46.2^2 \times 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

**Peak instantaneous per FET while conducting:**

$$80^2 \times 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega = 9.6\ \text{W}$$

### 1.4 Copper and Shunt Losses

| Element | Estimate | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase/bus copper, ~0.3 mΩ per channel path | 80² × 0.3 mΩ | 1.9 W |
| Low-side shunt, 0.25 mΩ (if fitted) | 80² × 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |

**Conduction subtotal:** approximately 22.7 W per channel.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

The worst case for switching loss is high current at D ≈ 0.9. At D = 1.0, six-step commutation produces no PWM switching.

### 2.1 V–I Overlap (Hard-Switched Transitions)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

With V_bus = 25.2 V, I = 80 A, t_r + t_f = 20 ns, and f_sw = 48 kHz:

$$P_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\times10^{-9} \times 48{,}000 = 0.97\ \text{W}$$

The rise and fall times come from the gate drive conditions. The FD6288 has about 1.5 A peak drive, and the gate resistor is R_g = 4.7 Ω. Note that t_r + t_f scales directly with R_g; the trade-off is covered in §2.6.

### 2.2 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I \cdot 2t_{dead} \cdot f_{sw}$$

$$= 0.8 \times 80 \times 2 \times 100\times10^{-9} \times 48{,}000 = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse Recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\times10^{-9} \times 25.2 \times 48{,}000 = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

Note that Q_rr itself rises with di/dt and temperature.

### 2.4 Output Capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss} V^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 10^{-9} \times 635 \times 48{,}000 = 0.015\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate Drive (Dissipated in Driver and R_g)

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2\ \text{(switching FETs)} = 40\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = 0.04\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Channel Loss Summary at 80 A

| Loss term | Loss (W) |
|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 19.2 |
| Copper + shunt | 3.5 |
| V–I switching | 0.97 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.12 |
| **Total per channel** | **≈ 24.4 W** |
| **Four channels simultaneously** | **≈ 97.6 W** |

**Efficiency check:** Output power is about 25.2 × 0.9 × 80 ≈ 1.81 kW per channel, so η ≈ 98.7%. The efficiency is fine. The problem is power density: roughly 98 W dissipated in 6.5 cm² of board area.

**Frequency trade-off:** Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. Moving from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves about 0.8 W per channel. This is minor compared to conduction loss. At 80 A, loss is conduction-dominated, so lowering R_DS(on) (paralleling FETs or using larger packages) is the effective lever.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Thermal Network (per FET)

Heat flows from the junction to ambient through this chain:

```
T_j ─[R_θJC 1.5]─ T_case ─[R_θCB 2.0, solder+vias]─ T_board ─[C_board]─┬─[R_conv]─ T_amb
                                                                       └─[R_cond, arms/standoffs]
```

### 3.2 Junction-to-Board Temperature Rise

Using the peak per-FET dissipation of about 9.6 W (conservative), plus a switching share of about 0.8 W:

$$\Delta T_{jb} \approx 10.4 \times (1.5 + 2.0) = 36\ \text{K}$$

Using the averaged value of about 3.2 W + 0.5 W:

$$\Delta T_{jb} \approx 13\ \text{K}$$

**Design value:** 25 K (the junction thermal time constant is ~ms, so the junction partially averages the per-FET duty).

### 3.3 Board Heat Capacity

| Material | Mass | Specific heat | Heat capacity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copper: 8 layers × 70 µm × 650 mm² × ~60% fill ≈ 0.27 cm³ | 2.4 g | 0.385 J/g·K | 0.94 J/K |
| FR4 | ~1.5 g | ~1.1 J/g·K | 1.65 J/K |
| FETs, capacitors, solder | — | — | ~0.3 J/K |
| **Total C_board** | | | **≈ 2.9 J/K** |
