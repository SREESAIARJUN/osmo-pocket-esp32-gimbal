# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Reference (Job #22)

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Architecture Corrections

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 cannot directly commutate four BLDC motors at 48 kHz while also running ArduPilot. The standard and assumed topology is:

- **FC domain:** STM32H743 running ArduPilot. It sends bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs.
- **ESC domain:** four independent ESC MCUs (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32). Each drives a 3-phase gate driver and six N-MOSFETs.

**Electrical assumptions.** Verify every value marked † against the actual datasheets before release.

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus,max = 25.2 V |
| PWM frequency | f_sw = 48 kHz, synchronous (complementary) PWM, trapezoidal six-step |
| MOSFET† | 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN class (e.g., BSZ018N04LS6): R_ds(on),25 = 1.8 mΩ max @ V_gs = 10 V; Q_gs2+Q_gd ≈ 9 nC; Q_g ≈ 30 nC; Q_rr ≈ 30 nC; C_oss ≈ 1 nF; R_θJC ≈ 1.6 K/W |
| Gate drive | V_gs = 10 V, effective plateau current I_g ≈ 0.5 A |
| Dead time | t_dt = 100 ns |
| Burst rating | 80 A per channel |

**Form-factor warning.** A "nano" (20×20 mm) board cannot support 80 A per channel. The thermal mass and copper cross-section are not there, as Section 3 shows. This document therefore assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm ESC board** and quantifies exactly how long an 80 A burst can last. If Job #22 is truly 20×20, derate to roughly 35–45 A burst.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Conduction topology
In six-step commutation, two phases conduct at any instant. With synchronous PWM at duty D, during each 120° electrical sector:

- The **active-high phase** high-side (HS) conducts for fraction D. Its low-side (LS) conducts for fraction (1−D) as a synchronous rectifier.
- The **active-low phase** LS conducts for 100% of the sector.

Each phase is "high" for 1/3 of the electrical period and "low" for 1/3, giving these per-device RMS factors (current I flat-topped):

$$I_{HS,rms}^2 = I^2\frac{D}{3}, \qquad I_{LS,rms}^2 = I^2\left(\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1-D}{3}\right) = I^2\frac{2-D}{3}$$

Since current always flows through exactly two channel devices, the channel total is:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$
