# STM32H743 Flight Controller + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC (Job #45): Architecture, Loss Model, DShot DMA Plan, Netlist

## 0. Scope Corrections

Three points change the design before any math is done.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In a 4-in-1 stack, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical choices are the AT32F421 or STM32G071, either one MCU per motor or two dual-motor MCUs. The H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600. It receives eRPM telemetry back on the same wire. The 48 kHz switching frequency is an ESC-MCU parameter, not an H743 timer.
2. **An 80 A burst on a "nano" (20×20 mm) board is not thermally credible.** Section 2 shows roughly 100 W of dissipation at 80 A on all four channels. The minimum form factor I will sign off is a **30.5×30.5 mm stack** with 2 oz or heavier copper, ideally 4–6 layers with 3 oz inner power planes. A 20×20 version would need its rating cut to about 35–40 A burst.
3. **Everything below is a reference design, not a fab-ready release.** The netlist is a reference topology. Values marked † must be confirmed against the actual MOSFET and driver datasheets you procure, followed by thermal-camera validation.

## 1. Electrical Topology

Each motor has a 3-phase bridge of 6 FETs, for 24 FETs total. The commutation is 6-step trapezoidal, with high-side PWM on the active phase and the other phase's low side held on.

At any instant, motor current flows through **two FETs in series**:
- one leg alternates between its high-side FET (PWM on, duty D) and its sync-rectifying low-side FET (1−D);
- the other leg's low-side FET is held on.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ /\mathrm{K}$$

At a design-point junction of T_j = 110 °C: R_ds = 1.2 × 1.38 ≈ **1.66 mΩ** (use 1.7 mΩ).

### 2.2 Per-motor conduction loss

With synchronous rectification, the conduction path always contains exactly two R_ds(on). The loss is therefore independent of duty:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.7\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{21.8\ W}$$

### 2.3 Per-FET average

Over one electrical revolution the duty rotates symmetrically through all 6 FETs:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{I^2 R_{ds}}{3} = \frac{6400 \times 1.7\text{m}}{3} = \mathbf{3.63\ W}$$

### 2.4 PCB copper loss

Assume a phase path (bus → FET → pad) of 0.5 mΩ total for 2 oz copper on a 30×30 board, about 0.25 mΩ per path segment pair:

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.2\ W/motor}$$

This is distributed into the board, not into the junctions.

## 3. Switching Loss Model @ 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Its high-side FET hard-switches and its low-side FET soft-switches (ZVS via the body diode).

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V·I overlap (hard switch) | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f, with t_r+t_f ≈ 20 ns† | ½·25.2·80·20 ns·48 k = **0.97 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f | 50 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.06 W** |
| C_oss discharge | ½·C_oss·V²·f | ½·1.5 nF·635·48 k = **0.02 W** |
| Body-diode dead time | V_f·I·2·t_dt·f | 0.8·80·2·100 ns·48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f | 2·60 nC·10 V·48 k = **0.06 W** (dissipated in driver/R_g) |
| **Total switching / motor** | | **≈ 1.72 W** |

Spread over the 6 FETs, the per-FET switching average is ≈ 0.29 W.

**Design notes:**
- Dead-time loss (0.61 W) is the largest controllable term. Trimming dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per motor. Only do this after measuring shoot-through margin on the actual gate loop.
- Moving from 24 kHz to 48 kHz doubles all switching terms (+0.86 W/motor). In exchange it reduces current ripple and audible noise, and improves low-throttle resolution.
