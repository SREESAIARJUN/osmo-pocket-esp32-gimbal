# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model

**Scope:** 80 A burst conduction losses, 48 kHz switching losses, ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 DMA/timer allocation, reference netlist, and thermal budget.

> **Before reading:** No MOSFET part number, board stack-up or airflow spec was supplied for Job #42. All numbers below come from a **parameterized model** with representative component values, marked **[ASSUMED]**. Substitute your datasheet values into the equations. The netlist is a **reference architecture**. It must go through ERC/DRC and layout review before fabrication.

---

## 1. System Architecture

```
 VBAT (6S, 25.2 V max) ──┬── 4× 3-phase power stages (24 MOSFETs)
                         ├── 5V/10V buck (BEC + gate drive)
                         └── VBAT sense divider → H743 ADC

 STM32H743VIT6 (FC, ArduPilot)
   TIM1 CH1..CH4 ── bidir DShot600 ──► 4× AT32F421 (AM32 ESC firmware)
                                          │
                                          ▼
                                   3× half-bridge drivers (e.g. FD6288Q class)
                                          │
                                          ▼
                                   6 MOSFETs per motor
```

**Why a separate ESC MCU per motor:** the H743 runs the flight stack. Commutation, BEMF sensing and 48 kHz PWM run on the dedicated ESC MCUs (the AM32 model). The H743-to-ESC interface is purely bidirectional DShot600.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Assumption on the rating
"80 A" is interpreted as **80 A per channel, burst (≤5 s)**. If it means 80 A total, divide all per-motor current terms by 4. Per-motor I²R loss then drops by 16×.

### 2.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big]$$

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| R₂₅ (40 V, PDFN 3.3×3.3) | 1.2 mΩ **[ASSUMED]** |
| α | 0.005 K⁻¹ (typical 0.004–0.007) |
| Design T_j | 110 °C |
| R_DS(on) at 110 °C | 1.2 × 1.425 = **1.71 mΩ** |

### 2.3 Current path (6-step trapezoidal with synchronous rectification)
At any instant, motor current flows through **one high-side FET in one leg and one low-side FET in another**. With complementary (synchronous) PWM, the freewheeling path is also a FET channel, not a diode. Two channel resistances are therefore always in series:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + I_{rms}^2 R_{Cu}$$

For 80 A, ignoring copper first:

$$P_{cond,FET} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.71\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{21.9\ W\ per\ motor}$$

**Copper term:** assume 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner paralleled, phase trace ≈ 0.6 mΩ round-trip **[ASSUMED]**. That adds 80² × 0.6 mΩ = **3.8 W per motor**.

**Per-FET average:** each FET conducts about 1/3 of the electrical cycle on its own side. This gives ≈ 21.9 / 3 ≈ **7.3 W average per FET** during burst. The peak instantaneous value is 10.9 W.

**Mitigation:** use 2 FETs in parallel per switch position. This halves R, so P_cond drops to **10.9 W per motor**. It does not fit a 20×20 mm board, so it requires a 30.5×30.5 mm form factor.

## 3. Switching Loss Model (f_sw = 48 kHz)

In 6-step operation, **one leg per motor is hard-switching at a time**. Loss terms per motor:

$$P_{sw} = f_{sw}\left[\tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) + Q_{rr}V_{bus} + E_{oss}\right] + P_{dt} + P_{gate}$$

$$P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw}, \qquad P_{gate} = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw}\times 2 \;(\text{dissipated mostly in driver and R}_g)$$

| Term | Inputs **[ASSUMED]** | Result |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | V = 25.2 V, I = 80 A, t_r + t_f = 30 ns | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 30 ns × 48 kHz = **1.45 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr = 40 nC | 25.2 × 40 nC × 48 kHz = **0.05 W** |
| Coss | E_oss ≈ 1.2 µJ | **0.06 W** |
| Dead-time diode | V_F = 0.8 V, t_dt = 100 ns | 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 kHz = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive | Q_g = 45 nC, V_GS = 10 V, 2 FETs | **0.04 W** (driver side) |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.2 W per motor** |

**Sensitivity:** switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 96 kHz it would be ≈ 4.4 W per motor. At 48 kHz, conduction dominates by about 10:1. The right optimization target is **R_DS(on) and copper**, not edge speed. Slowing edges (higher R_g) to fix EMI or ringing costs only about 0.05 W per ns of added edge time.

### 3.1 Burst loss budget

| Item | Per motor | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 21.9 W | 87.6 W |
| Copper | 3.8 W | 15.2 W |
| Switching | 2.2 W | 8.8 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ) | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| **Total** | **29.5 W** | **≈ 118 W** |

**Efficiency check:** total input power is about 4 × 25.2 V × 80 A × duty. At full throttle that is ≈ 8 kW, so losses are ≈ 1.5%. Efficiency is acceptable; **heat density is the problem**.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Junction-to-board (Foster network, per FET)

$$Z_{th,jc}(t) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} R_i\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau_i}\right)$$

Typical PDFN 3.3×3.3 values **[ASSUMED]**: R₁ = 0.3 K/W at τ₁ = 0.5 ms, R₂ = 0.7 K/W at τ₂ = 15 ms, R₃ = 0.8 K/W at τ₃ = 0.3 s. At t ≥ 1 s, Z_th,jc ≈ 1.8 K/W.

$$\Delta T_{jc} = 7.3\ \text{W} \times 1.8 = \mathbf{13\ K}$$

### 4.2 Board bulk (lumped capacitance, burst-dominant)
A 5 s burst is much shorter than the board-to-air time constant, so the board absorbs the heat almost adiabatically:

$$\Delta T_{board} \approx \frac{P_{tot}\cdot t_{burst}}{C_{th}} \qquad C_{th} = m_{FR4}c_{FR4} + m_{Cu}c_{Cu}$$
