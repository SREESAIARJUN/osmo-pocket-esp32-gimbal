## 0. Architectural Ground Rules and Feasibility Flags

Three issues should be settled before anyone lays out copper.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.**
ArduPilot on the H743 *emits* DShot600. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071. The board is therefore one H743 plus four ESC MCUs, or two dual-motor MCUs.

**2. 80 A burst on a 20×20 nano board is thermally implausible except as a seconds-long transient.**
The model below shows roughly 80 W of dissipation at 4×80 A. A 20×20 stack can reject only about 3–6 W without forced airflow. The defensible rating is:
- 80 A per channel for ≤ 2–4 s, adiabatic.
- About 35–45 A continuous with prop-wash airflow.

The nameplate "80 A" should state the burst duration. The alternative is moving to a 30.5×30.5 form factor.

**3. ESC PWM frequency (48 kHz) is unrelated to DShot.**
The ESC MCU generates the 48 kHz power-stage PWM. DShot600 is the 600 kbit/s command link from the H743. Both are modeled below, kept separate.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At the design point T_j = 110 °C: R = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0045 × 85) = **1.38 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Per-FET conduction duty in 6-step synchronous commutation

Each electrical period has six 60° sectors. In any sector:
- One phase high-side PWMs at duty D.
- Its complementary low-side conducts for (1−D) of each PWM period (synchronous rectification).
- One other phase's low-side is held fully on as the current sink.

Each leg spends 1/3 of the period as source and 1/3 as sink. The per-FET RMS-squared averages are:

$$P_{HS} = I^2 R_{DS}\,\frac{D}{3}$$

$$P_{LS} = I^2 R_{DS}\,\frac{1+(1-D)}{3} = I^2 R_{DS}\,\frac{2-D}{3}$$

Per leg, P_HS + P_LS = (2/3) I²R. Over three legs, the per-channel total is:

$$\boxed{P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)}$$

**Sanity check:** current always flows through exactly two FETs in series. ✔

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, D = 0.5, T_j = 110 °C

I²R = 6400 × 1.38 m = 8.83 W

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| P_HS | 8.83 × 0.5/3 = **1.47 W** |
| P_LS | 8.83 × 1.5/3 = **4.42 W** (hottest device) |
| P_cond,ch | **17.7 W** |

Two parasitic conduction terms are added per channel:

| Path | Resistance | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ per channel, if used) | 0.25 mΩ | 6400 × 0.25 m = 1.6 W |
| Copper (2 oz, ~0.3 mΩ phase + bus path) | ~0.3 mΩ | ~1.9 W |

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

In 6-step, only the PWMing leg switches. Losses are accounted once per channel.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard-switched high-side)

$$P_{ov} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(35\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = \mathbf{1.69\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction (lands in the low-side)

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(200\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery (lands in the high-side at turn-on)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\,\text{nC}(25.2)(48\,\text{kHz}) = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 2.4 Output-capacitance energy

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5(1.5\,\text{nF})(635)(48\,\text{kHz}) = \mathbf{0.023\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in driver and gate resistors, not the FET channel)

$$P_g = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 40\,\text{nC}(10)(48\,\text{kHz}) = 0.019\ \text{W per FET}$$

### 2.6 Switching total per channel

$$P_{sw,ch} \approx 1.69 + 0.61 + 0.05 + 0.02 \approx \mathbf{2.4\ W}$$

**Sensitivity:** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. Raising to 96 kHz adds about 2.4 W per channel. At 80 A, the 48 kHz choice is correct. Do not let AM32 auto-raise PWM frequency at high load.
