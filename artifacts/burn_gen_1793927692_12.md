# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #41): Architecture & Analysis Package

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and a Feasibility Flag

**Topology.** In an AIO "nano" board the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does **not** commutate the motors. Each of the four ESC channels has its own small MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 class, running AM32 or BLHeli_32), a 3-phase gate driver, and 6 MOSFETs. Everything below assumes that split. Driving 4×3-phase bridges directly from the H743 is possible, but ArduPilot does not support it.

> ⚠️ **Feasibility flag.** As Section 3 shows, 80 A on all four channels on a nano-format board is a **~1–3 second burst rating**, not a continuous one. The thermal math does not support anything more. Marketing a "4×80 A" label on this footprint without a duration qualifier would be inaccurate.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path (trapezoidal BLDC, complementary/synchronous PWM)

At any instant, two phases are active:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for a fraction D of each period. The low-side FET acts as a synchronous rectifier for (1−D). So exactly one FET in this leg carries I at all times, ignoring dead time.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET is on continuously.

Two FET channels are therefore always in series with the motor current. This holds regardless of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j - 25)] \approx R_{25}\cdot k_T,\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At 125 °C: R = 1.2 × 1.6 = **1.92 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.92\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{24.6\ W}$$

At T_j = 25 °C (cold start of a burst), the same formula gives 15.4 W.

This creates positive feedback: R_DS rises with temperature, so loss rises, so temperature rises further. Solve iteratively with the thermal model in Section 3. If the loop gain dP/dT × R_th approaches 1, you are in thermal runaway.
