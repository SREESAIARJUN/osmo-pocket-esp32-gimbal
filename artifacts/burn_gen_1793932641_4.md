### Architecture decision

The STM32H743 should **not** commutate the motors directly. The standard and recommended split is:

- **Flight controller (FC) domain:** the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bi-directional DShot600 to the ESCs.
- **ESC domain:** four dedicated ESC MCUs (e.g. AT32F421 running AM32, or EFM8 running BLHeli_S/Bluejay). Each one runs 6-step commutation with 48 kHz complementary PWM.

The 48 kHz switching analysis therefore applies to the ESC MCUs and power stage. The DShot analysis applies to the H743.

### Red flags to escalate before layout

| # | Finding | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | "80 A burst" on a "nano" board (20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm) | About **105 W** of total dissipation with all 4 channels at 80 A. The board heats at roughly **50 K/s**. A burst is thermally limited to **about 1.5 s**, not seconds-to-minutes (see §3). |
| R2 | 4 × 80 A means 320 A of battery current | The XT30/XT60 connector and 12–14 AWG battery leads cannot support this. The burst rating is meaningful **per motor**, not simultaneously across all four, unless burst is under 0.5 s. |
| R3 | Single FET per switch on a nano footprint | Conduction loss dominates (about 87%). R_DS(on) is the design-driving parameter, not switching speed. |
| R4 | Board-level "80 A" claims in this class are usually 10 s-or-less marketing numbers | Define the rating formally, e.g. "80 A for 1 s from T_board = 40 °C, T_j ≤ 150 °C". |
