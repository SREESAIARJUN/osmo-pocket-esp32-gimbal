# STM32H743 AIO Nano Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #63): System Architecture, Loss Model, DShot DMA Map, and Netlists

## 0. Feasibility Findings

These findings change the design direction, so they come first.

1. **The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** ArduPilot has no on-chip motor commutation. A working architecture is:
   - STM32H743 (flight controller)
   - Bidirectional DShot600 links
   - 4× ESC MCUs (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32)
   - 3-phase gate drivers
   - 24 MOSFETs

   The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs, not the H743.
2. **80 A per channel burst is not thermally achievable on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board.** Section 3 shows the board heats at about 25–30 K/s at full burst. The rating must be redefined as one of:
   - a time-limited burst (≤1–2 s), or
   - 80 A *total battery current*.

   I model both.
3. **The netlists are a reference architecture.** They are not a released design. Pin assignments were checked against the STM32H743 alternate-function tables as I know them. Before layout, they must be verified against the current ST datasheet (DS12110), CubeMX, and ArduPilot's `hwdef` DMA resolver output.

**Commutation model:** 6-step trapezoidal with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM), as used by AM32/BLHeli_32. At any instant, phase current flows through exactly **two** conducting devices in series: one high-side or freewheeling low-side FET in the PWM leg, and one low-side FET in the return leg.

## 3. Conduction Losses

### 3.1 Temperature-Dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,(1+\alpha)^{(T_j-25)}$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{DS} = 1.0 \cdot 1.004^{85} = 1.0\cdot e^{0.339} \approx 1.40\ \text{m}\Omega$$

Because R_DS depends on T_j, and T_j depends on loss, the equations must be solved iteratively (Section 3.5).

### 3.2 Per-Channel Conduction Loss

With synchronous rectification, the conduction path is two FETs regardless of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2\,(2R_{DS}(T_j) + R_{cu})$$

At I_ph = 80 A:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 6400\,(2.80 + 0.30)\times10^{-3} = 19.8\ \text{W}$$

### 3.3 Per-FET Distribution

Over one electrical cycle, each of the 6 FETs carries current about 1/3 of the time in the PWM-weighted sense:

$$P_{FET} \approx \frac{2 I^2 R_{DS}}{6} = \frac{6400 \cdot 2.8\text{m}}{6}\approx 3.0\ \text{W}$$

### 3.4 Phase Current vs. Battery Current

Battery current and phase current differ:

$$I_{bat} \approx D \cdot I_{ph}\quad(\text{lossless approximation})$$

"80 A" therefore needs a definition:
- **Case A (80 A phase, per channel):** P_cond = 19.8 W × 4 = **79 W** for all FET conduction.
- **Case B (80 A battery total, 20 A per channel):** At D ≈ 0.85 (full throttle), I_ph ≈ 23.5 A, giving P_cond,ch = 23.5² × 3.1 mΩ = 1.71 W, so **6.8 W total**.

### 3.5 Electro-Thermal Fixed Point

Iterate until T_j converges:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_a + P_{tot}(T_j^{(k)})\cdot R_{\theta,ja}$$

The solution diverges (thermal runaway) when:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\,R_{\theta} = I^2\,2R_{DS}\ln(1+\alpha)\,R_\theta \ge 1$$

For Case A at the per-FET level: 3.0 W × 0.004 × R_θ ≥ 1, which gives runaway for R_θ ≳ 83 K/W.
