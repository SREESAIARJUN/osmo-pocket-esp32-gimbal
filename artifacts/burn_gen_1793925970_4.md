## 0. Architecture Decisions and Feasibility Flags

### 0.1 System partitioning
The H743 does **not** commutate the motors directly. The architecture is:

```
LiPo 6S ─┬─► 4× ESC power stages (3 half-bridges each = 24 FETs)
         │      ▲ gate drive
         │   4× ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32 or BLHeli_32 fw)  ← runs 48 kHz PWM
         │      ▲ bidirectional DShot600 (1 wire per motor)
         └─► STM32H743 FC (ArduPilot) ── TIM1 CH1–4 + DMA
```

The 48 kHz switching frequency is an **ESC firmware setting** (AM32 `PWM_FREQ`), not an H743 function.

### 0.2 Feasibility flags (resolve before committing)

| Issue | Concern | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| "Nano" form factor (20×20 mm) at 80A | Physically infeasible: 24 FETs plus 320A of copper will not fit on a 20×20 board | Use a 30.5×30.5 mm stack minimum. A single-board FC+ESC is realistic at roughly 36×36 mm or larger |
| 4 × 80A = 320A motor-side burst | Battery-side current is still roughly 200–250A at high throttle | XT60 is unsuitable. Use XT90 or direct-solder 8 AWG pads |
| FC co-located with 85 W of FET heat | Degrades IMU bias and drift | Use a separate stacked board, or isolate thermally with an IMU heater and a slot cut |

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current path (6-step trapezoidal, complementary PWM)
At any instant, two phases conduct:
- One high-side FET (phase A) is PWM-switched at duty D.
- During the off-time, the complementary low-side FET in the same leg carries current via synchronous rectification.
- One low-side FET (phase B) is continuously on for the 60° sector.

There are therefore always **two FET channels in series** with the motor current.

### 2.2 RMS current with ripple

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{dc}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph-ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

Example: L_ph-ph = 20 µH, V_bus − V_bemf = 3 V, D = 0.9:

$$\Delta I = \frac{3 \cdot 0.9}{20\,\mu\text{H} \cdot 48\,\text{kHz}} = 2.8\,\text{A} \;\rightarrow\; \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 0.65\,\text{A}^2 \;(\ll 6400\,\text{A}^2)$$

Ripple is negligible at 80A, so I_rms ≈ 80A.

### 2.3 Per-channel conduction loss

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.0 mΩ | 12.8 W | 51.2 W |
| 125 °C | 1.5 mΩ | **19.2 W** | **76.8 W** |

### 2.4 Worst-case single FET
Each leg conducts for 2/3 of the electrical period. At D → 1, the high-side carries all of its leg's current:

$$P_{FET,max} = \tfrac{2}{3}\,I^2\,R_{ds,hot} = \tfrac{2}{3}(6400)(0.0015) = \mathbf{6.4\ W}$$

### 2.5 Electrothermal convergence
R_ds depends on T_j, which depends on P. Iterate:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_{board} + P\!\left(T_j^{(n)}\right)\,R_{\theta,J\text{-}board}$$

Thermal runaway is avoided if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\cdot R_\theta < 1 \;\Rightarrow\; (0.005)(R_{25} \cdot 4267)(9) \approx 0.19 < 1$$

This condition is satisfied, so the iteration is stable.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches hard. The low-side FET switches with near-zero voltage (body-diode commutation).

**(a) V–I overlap (hard-switched FET)**
$$t_{sw} = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_g} = \frac{15\,\text{nC}}{1.5\,\text{A}} = 10\ \text{ns (each edge)}$$
$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12\,V\,I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 20\,\text{ns} \cdot 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery (sync FET body diode)**
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V\,f_{sw} = 40\,\text{nC} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\,\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

**(c) Output capacitance**
$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12\,C_{oss}\,V^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 1.5\,\text{nF} \cdot 635 \cdot 48\,\text{k} = 0.023\ \text{W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction**
$$P_{dt} = V_f\,I\cdot 2\,t_{dead}\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\,\text{ns} \cdot 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive** (2 FETs per leg; dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g)
$$P_g = Q_g\,V_{drv}\,f_{sw}\cdot 2 = 60\,\text{nC} \cdot 12 \cdot 48\,\text{k} \cdot 2 = 0.069\ \text{W}$$

### Switching loss summary per channel
| Term | W |
|---|---|
| V–I overlap | 0.97 |
| Dead-time | 0.61 |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.14 |
| **Total** | **≈1.72 W** |

**Frequency sensitivity:** P_sw ∝ f_sw. Each additional 24 kHz adds about 0.86 W per channel. Dead-time loss is about 40% of switching loss, so trimming AM32 dead-time to the minimum safe value (verified on a scope for shoot-through margin) is the cheapest efficiency gain.
