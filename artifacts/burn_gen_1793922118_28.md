## 0. Architectural Corrections Before Any Math

Four issues in the brief change the design.

| # | Brief assumption | Engineering reality | Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The H743 generates the 48 kHz motor PWM | ArduPilot on the H743 sends DShot commands. The 48 kHz commutation PWM is generated by the **per-motor ESC MCUs** running AM32 or BLHeli_32. | Two-tier architecture: H743 (FC) → DShot600 → 4× ESC MCU → gate drivers → FETs. |
| 2 | 80 A per motor on a "nano" (20×20 mm) board | The 24 FETs alone in 5×6 mm packages need 720 mm² against 400 mm² of board area. Thermally (Section 3), 80 A × 4 on 20×20 lasts about 1 s. | FC stays 20×20. The ESC moves to **30.5×30.5 mm** with 5×6 FETs. Alternatively, keep 20×20 and accept an 80 A rating that is a sub-second, single-motor transient (Section 3.4). |
| 3 | "80 A burst" left undefined | Without a duration, the number is meaningless. | Burst is defined here as **80 A per motor, all 4 motors, 10 s max, from 40 °C start, T_j ≤ 125 °C**. Section 3 shows what that requires. |
| 4 | A single-board H743 + 4-in-1 | Mixing the 80 A switching ground with IMU and baro on one board degrades gyro noise. | Stacked boards on an 8-pin JST-SH harness: VBAT, GND, CURR, TLM, M1–M4. |

## 2. Loss Model (per motor, 6-step trapezoidal, complementary PWM)

### 2.1 Conduction loss

In any 60° commutation sector of 6-step drive:
- The **PWM leg** conducts through the high-side (HS) FET for duty D and through the synchronous low-side (LS) FET for (1−D).
- The **static leg** conducts through its LS FET for 100% of the sector.

The current therefore always passes through exactly two R_DS(on) in series:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0045 × 100) = **1.45 mΩ**

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.45\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{18.56\ W}$$
