## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation with synchronous PWM, phase current always flows through exactly **two conducting FETs in series**:
- During the on-time: the high-side of phase A plus the low-side of phase B.
- During the off-time: the low-side of phase A (synchronous freewheel) plus the low-side of phase B.

So, independent of duty cycle D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot \left[\,2R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + R_{Cu} + R_{sh}\,\right]$$

The current split by device is:
- Commutating high-side: $I^2 R \cdot D$
- Commutating low-side: $I^2 R (1-D)$
- Return low-side: $I^2 R$ (it takes the full share)

### 1.2 Temperature dependence of R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0065\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R = 0.9 × 1.55 = **1.40 mΩ**.

This equation is coupled to temperature. Solve it iteratively with §4, or note that the loss rises about 55% from cold to hot.

### 1.3 Parasitic copper and shunt resistance

**Copper.** Assume 2 oz (70 µm) copper, 3 parallel layers, an effective trace of 8 mm length by 4 mm width per path:

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{w t n} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot 8\times10^{-3}}{4\times10^{-3}\cdot 70\times10^{-6}\cdot 3}\cdot 1.35_{(hot)} \approx 0.22\ \text{m}\Omega$$

**Shunt.** Current sense uses 0.25 mΩ per channel, or a low-side shunt. Count it only if it sits in the conduction path.

### 1.4 Result at 80 A (hot)

$$P_{cond,ch} = 80^2\,(2\cdot1.40 + 0.22 + 0.25)\,\text{m}\Omega = 6400 \times 3.27\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{20.9\ W}$$

The worst single FET (the return low-side) dissipates 80² × 1.40 mΩ = **9.0 W**.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Per channel, only **one switching leg** is active at any instant, so there is one hard-switched transition pair per PWM period.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss

The edge times come from the gate drive:

$$t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}+Q_{gs2}}{I_{g,on}},\quad I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{DRV}-V_{plateau}}{R_{g,int}+R_{g,ext}+R_{drv}} = \frac{10-3.2}{1.0+2.2+4}\approx 0.94\ \text{A}$$

With Q_gd + Q_gs2 ≈ 14 nC, this gives t_r ≈ 15 ns. Similarly, t_f ≈ 12 ns.

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot27\times10^{-9}\cdot48\times10^{3} = \mathbf{1.31\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

Q_rr rises with di/dt and temperature, so derate by ×2: **0.10 W**.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw}\ (\text{hard turn-on of complementary pair}) \approx 45\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.054\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F\,I\,(2 t_{dead}) f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

This is the largest switching-related term after overlap loss. Trimming dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns roughly halves it. Do this only after you have measured shoot-through margin.

### 2.5 Gate drive

The driver supplies two gates per transition:

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{DRV} f_{sw} = 2\cdot45\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k} = 0.043\ \text{W}$$

Most of this dissipates in the driver IC and gate resistors, not in the FET.

### 2.6 Switching total and frequency sensitivity

$$P_{sw,total,ch} \approx 1.31 + 0.10 + 0.054 + 0.61 + 0.043 = \mathbf{2.12\ W}$$

Every term except conduction scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz the total would be about 1.06 W. Moving to 48 kHz costs about 1 W per channel, in exchange for quieter operation and lower current ripple.

## 3. Total Loss Budget

| Term | Per channel @ 80 A | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (hot) | 20.9 W | 83.6 W |
| Switching (all terms) | 2.1 W | 8.5 W |
| Bus-cap ESR ripple (I_rms,cap ≈ 0.5·I·√(D(1−D)) ≈ 20 A, ESR_eff ≈ 2 mΩ) | 0.8 W | 3.2 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 23.8 W** | **≈ 95 W** |

Efficiency at the full 4 × 80 A point: P_out ≈ 4 × 80 × 25.2 × D_eff. At high duty (~0.95) this is about 7.7 kW, so η ≈ 98.8%. The efficiency is good. **The problem is where 95 W goes on a 30 × 30 mm board.**
