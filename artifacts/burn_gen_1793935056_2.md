## 0. Design Basis and Architectural Clarification

The STM32H743 is the **flight controller (FC)**. It does not commutate the motors. Each ESC channel uses a dedicated MCU, for example an AT32F421 running AM32 or an EFM8BB51 running BlueJay. The H743 sends **bi-directional DShot600** to those ESC MCUs. The ESC MCUs generate the **48 kHz PWM**. The two halves are therefore modelled separately (Sections 1–3 and Section 4).

| Parameter | Value | Note |

| Burst current | **80 A phase current per ESC channel** | Per-motor burst, not the total pack current |
| Burst duration target | ≤ 2 s | Derived in §3; 80 A continuous is not feasible on a nano board |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)] ,\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ /^\circ\text{C}$$

| T_j | R_DS(on) |
|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 1.375 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 1.50 mΩ (design value) |

Add the package and PCB path parasitics, about 0.3 mΩ per conduction path (copper pour, vias, solder):

$$R_{path} = 2R_{DS}(T_j) + R_{cu} = 2(1.5) + 0.3 = 3.3\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

At any instant, the phase current flows through the **high-side FET of phase A** and the **low-side FET of phase B**. During the PWM off-time, the complementary low-side FET of the PWM'd leg conducts instead of the high-side FET. The current path therefore always contains exactly two FETs, independent of duty cycle D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot R_{path}$$

At the 80 A burst (trapezoidal, flat-top, low ripple, so I_rms ≈ I_ph):

$$P_{cond,ch} = 80^2 \times 3.3\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{21.1\ W}$$

FET-only share: 80² × 3.0 mΩ = 19.2 W. Copper share: 1.9 W.

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each FET conducts for 120° of the electrical cycle, at commutation rates far faster than the thermal time constant. The loss therefore spreads evenly across the six FETs:

$$P_{cond,FET} = 19.2 / 6 = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

### 1.4 Ripple correction (optional term)

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{avg}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12},\qquad \Delta I = \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L_{ph} f_{PWM}}$$

Assume a typical low-inductance quad motor with L_ph about 20 µH. The worst case is D = 0.5:

- ΔI = 25.2 × 0.25 / (20e-6 × 48e3) = 6.6 A
- Correction: 6.6² / 12 = 3.6 A², which is +0.06 % at 80 A. **This term is negligible.**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In 6-step, **only one leg hard-switches at a time**: the PWM'd high side plus its synchronous low side. The ⅙-cycle rotation spreads this loss across all legs.

### 2.1 V-I overlap (hard switching)

Transition times are set by the Miller plateau:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{G,src}} = \frac{10\text{nC}}{\sim0.67\text{A (eff.)}} \approx 15\ \text{ns},\qquad t_f \approx 15\ \text{ns}$$

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{PWM} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery of the synchronous FET body diode

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f = 40\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

Q_rr scales with di/dt and I_F, so it can be about 2× higher at 80 A. Budget **0.10 W**.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f = 0.5 \times 50\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.030\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM period:

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in driver and gate resistors, not the FETs)

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{GS} f = 2 \times 60\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.058\ \text{W per switching leg}$$

### 2.6 Total loss budget per channel at the 80 A burst

| Term | W | Located in |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 19.20 | FETs |
| Conduction (copper) | 1.90 | PCB |
| Switching overlap | 1.45 | FETs |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 | FETs |
| Q_rr | 0.10 | FETs |
| Q_oss | 0.03 | FETs |
| Gate drive | 0.06 | Driver |
| **Total per channel** | **23.35 W** | |
| FET-only total | 21.39 W | That is **3.57 W per FET** |
| **4 channels** | **93.4 W** | |

**Efficiency check.** Assume an 80 A phase current at D ≈ 0.95, so P_out is about 80 × 25.2 × 0.95 ≈ 1.9 kW per channel. The resulting η of about 98.8 % is consistent with real 6S ESCs.

**Frequency sensitivity.** Switching terms scale linearly with f_PWM. At 24 kHz they drop from about 2.2 W to 1.1 W per channel. At 96 kHz they rise to about 4.4 W. Above roughly 48 kHz, conduction still dominates at burst current, so 48 kHz is a reasonable smoothness/loss compromise.
