## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Path

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous (complementary) PWM. In any commutation sector, two phases carry current:

* **PWM leg:** the HS FET conducts for duty D. The complementary LS FET conducts for (1−D), minus dead time.
* **Return leg:** the LS FET is fully on for the whole sector.

On the PWM leg, exactly one FET is always carrying I_ph, apart from dead time. Per motor, the conduction loss is therefore:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)\qquad R_{DS}(T_j)=R_{25}\left[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\right]$$

### 1.2 Per-Device Split

| Device | Loss |
|---|---|
| HS (PWM leg) | $D\,I^2R_{DS}$ |
| LS (PWM leg) | $(1-D-2t_{dt}f_{sw})\,I^2R_{DS}$ |
| LS (return leg) | $I^2R_{DS}$ for that sector; averaged over 6 sectors, each FET carries a 1/3 duty share |

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, Assumed T_j = 100 °C

* R_DS(100 °C) = 1.2 mΩ × 1.375 = **1.65 mΩ**
* P_cond = 2 × 6400 × 1.65 m = **21.1 W per motor**
* Instantaneous worst device (return-leg LS) = 6400 × 1.65 m = **10.6 W**

Because R_DS rises with temperature, the loss is a positive feedback loop. Solve it iteratively, or use the closed form:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + R_{th}P_{cond}(T_j) \;\Rightarrow\; T_j=\frac{T_{ref}+R_{th}\,2I^2R_{25}(1-25\alpha)}{1-R_{th}\,2I^2R_{25}\alpha}$$

Thermal runaway occurs when the denominator reaches zero:

$$R_{th}\cdot 2I^2R_{25}\alpha \to 1$$

At 80 A, that limit is R_th ≈ 2.6 K/W (motor-level lumped). This is the hard reason a burst rating must be time-limited.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

These are per-motor losses (one hard-switched PWM leg), using inductive clamped switching.

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | $\tfrac12 V_{bus}I(t_r+t_f)f_{sw}$ | 0.5·25.2·80·30n·48k = **1.45 W** |
| Reverse recovery | $Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}$ | 50n·25.2·48k = **0.06 W** |
| C_oss (HS + LS) | $2\cdot\tfrac12C_{oss}V^2f_{sw}$ | 1n·635·48k = **0.03 W** |
| Dead-time body diode | $2V_fI\,t_{dt}f_{sw}$ | 2·0.8·80·100n·48k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver) | $2Q_gV_{GS}f_{sw}$ | 2·40n·10·48k = **0.04 W** |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.2 W/motor** |

**Total per motor at 80 A burst:** 21.1 + 2.2 ≈ **23.3 W**
**Four motors:** ≈ **93 W** into the board.

### Sensitivity of 48 kHz vs. Alternatives

Switching loss scales linearly with frequency (k ≈ 0.045 W/kHz at 80 A):

* 24 kHz saves about 1.1 W per motor.
* 96 kHz adds about 2.2 W per motor.

At 80 A, conduction dominates by roughly 10:1. That means 48 kHz is a sound choice: it gives smooth current for small motors with low phase inductance, without a meaningful thermal penalty. Dead time is the cheapest knob to tune. Cutting it to 50 ns removes 0.3 W per motor.

### Bulk Capacitor Ripple

Per motor:

$$I_{C,rms}=I\sqrt{D(1-D)}$$

At D = 0.5 this is up to 40 A_rms per motor. Interleaving the four ESCs reduces the aggregate, but plan on:

* ≥1000 µF low-ESR bulk capacitance at the pads, plus
* 4 × 10 µF/50 V X7R ceramics per phase bridge, placed within 3 mm of the half-bridge.
