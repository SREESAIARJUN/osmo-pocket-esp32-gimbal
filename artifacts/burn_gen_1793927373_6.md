# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #31): Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, Bi-Directional DShot600 Timer/DMA Allocation, and Netlist

## 0. Scope Corrections and Assumptions

Three architectural points need to be settled before the math, because they change where each loss and timing budget lives.

1. **The H743 does not run the 48 kHz PWM.** In a 4-in-1 stack, each motor channel is commutated by the ESC's own MCU (typically AT32F421, STM32G071 or EFM8BB51) running AM32 or BLHeli_32/S. The H743 only sends bi-directional DShot600 commands and decodes eRPM telemetry. The 48 kHz switching losses therefore belong to the ESC board.
2. **"80A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is a transient rating, not a thermal steady state.** The math below shows the board survives roughly 2–3 s at 80 A per motor from a warm start. Continuous capability is an order of magnitude lower. This must be stated on the datasheet.
3. **The MOSFET part number is not specified.** All numbers below are parametric. They are evaluated with a representative 40 V, 5×6 mm PDFN device. **Replace these values with the datasheet of the part you actually select.**

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

**Temperature-dependent on-resistance:**

R_ds(on)(T_j) = R_25 · [1 + α(T_j − 25)]

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 0.75 · 1.6 = **1.20 mΩ**

**Current path.** Assume 6-step commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. During each sector:
- the high-side (HS) FET of phase A conducts for time D,
- the low-side (LS) FET of phase A conducts for (1 − D) as the synchronous rectifier,
- the LS FET of phase B conducts for the full period.

**Per-device losses:**

P_HS = I²·R·D/N_par = 6400 · 1.2 m · 0.9 = **6.91 W**
P_LS,sync = I²·R·(1−D) = **0.77 W**
P_LS,B = I²·R·1 = **7.68 W**

**Per-motor conduction loss:**

P_cond = I²·R·[D + (1−D) + 1] = 2·I²·R

| Condition | P_cond per motor |
|---|---|
| Hot (T_j = 125 °C) | **15.4 W** |
| Cold (T_j = 25 °C) | 9.6 W |

Commutation rotates these roles across the six FETs. Averaged over a revolution, each FET carries about 1/3 of the motor's conduction loss. Within a 60° sector, however, the peak instantaneous device loss is about 7.7 W. **Use this peak value for the junction calculation.**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Hard switching occurs on the PWM'd HS FET. The LS FET switches near zero voltage after dead time.

**a) V–I overlap loss**

P_ov = ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = ½ · 25.2 · 80 · 40 ns · 48 k = **1.94 W**

Here t_r ≈ t_f ≈ Q_gd / I_g = 15 nC / ~0.75 A ≈ 20 ns.

**b) Body-diode reverse recovery**

P_rr = V · Q_rr · f_sw = 25.2 · 60 n · 48 k = **0.073 W**

**c) Output capacitance**

P_oss ≈ ½ · Q_oss · V · f_sw = **0.048 W**

**d) Dead-time diode conduction (two edges per period)**

P_dt = V_f · I · 2t_dt · f_sw = 0.8 · 80 · 400 ns · 48 k = **1.23 W**

**e) Gate drive**

P_g = Q_g · V_gs · f_sw · 2 FETs = 90 n · 10 · 48 k · 2 = **0.086 W**

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistor, not in the FET.

**Switching total per motor ≈ 3.3 W.**

Design levers, in order of payoff:
1. Reducing dead time from 200 ns to 100 ns saves 0.6 W per motor.
2. A stronger gate driver (2 A) halves P_ov but raises dv/dt and EMI.
3. Each 8 kHz reduction in f_sw saves about 0.55 W per motor, at the cost of current ripple.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Junction rise (fast, millisecond time constant)

ΔT_jc = P_FET,peak · R_θJC = (7.7 + 1.9) · 0.8 ≈ **7.7 K**

The junction tracks the case closely. **The bottleneck is the board, not the die.**

### 4.2 Board heat capacity (burst regime, adiabatic approximation)

Estimated thermal mass of a 20×20 nano 4-in-1:

C_th = Σ m·c_p ≈ 2.5 g FR4 × 1.1 + 1.5 g Cu × 0.385 + 1.0 g FETs/solder × 0.6 ≈ **3.9 J/K**

Rate of temperature rise:

dT/dt = P / C_th ≈ 92 / 3.9 ≈ **23.6 K/s**

The rate is self-accelerating because R_ds(on) rises with temperature. Combining the loss and resistance models gives a coupled first-order ODE:

C_th · dT/dt = 4[2I²R_25(1 + α(T−25)) + P_sw + P_misc] − (T − T_amb)/R_θ,BA
