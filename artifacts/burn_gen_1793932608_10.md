> ⚠ **Form-factor reality check.** "Nano" boards are 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm. A sustained 80 A per motor on 24 single 3.3×3.3 mm FETs is beyond what such a board can dissipate. The analysis below shows 80 A is feasible only as a **time-limited burst**. Plan for 30.5×30.5 mm, paralleled FETs, or a heatsink if the duty cycle is high.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation geometry (6-step trapezoidal, complementary PWM)

In each 60° sector, two phases conduct:
- One leg's high-side (HS) switch is PWM'd at duty D. Its own low-side (LS) synchronously rectifies during (1−D).
- Another leg's LS is held fully on.

Over one electrical period, each leg spends 1/3 of the time in each role:

| Device | Conduction fraction of period |
|---|---|
| HS (PWM leg) | D/3 |
| LS (same leg, sync rectifier) | (1−D)/3 |
| LS (held-on role) | 1/3 |

Per-device conduction loss:

$$P_{cond,HS} = I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_J)\,\frac{D}{3}$$

$$P_{cond,LS} = I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_J)\,\frac{2-D}{3}$$

Per motor (3 legs), the sum simplifies because exactly two channels always conduct:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 3\left(\tfrac{D}{3}+\tfrac{2-D}{3}\right)I^2R = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_J)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS}(T_J) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_J-25)]$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, D = 0.9, T_J = 125 °C

- R_DS = 1.0 × (1 + 0.005·100) = **1.5 mΩ**
- P_cond,motor = 2 × 80² × 0.0015 = **19.2 W**
- P_cond,HS = 6400 × 0.0015 × 0.3 = **2.88 W**
- P_cond,LS = 6400 × 0.0015 × 0.367 = **3.52 W** (worst-case device)

### 1.4 Copper and interconnect (per motor)

Assume about 0.3 mΩ per phase path, covering PCB copper and solder joints. Two paths conduct, so:

P_Cu = 2 × 80² × 0.0003 = **3.84 W per motor**

This is spread across the board rather than concentrated in the FETs.
