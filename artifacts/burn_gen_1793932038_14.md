# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

**Architecture.** On a production AIO board, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and generates bi-directional DShot600. Four ESC-side MCUs (for example, AT32F421-class parts running AM32/BLHeli_32-style firmware) drive the three-phase bridges through a gate driver. The H743 does not commutate the motors directly. That architecture is assumed below.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths

The bridge uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. At any instant, phase current flows through exactly two device positions:

- the active high-side FET (or the synchronous low-side during the PWM off-time) in one phase, and
- the active low-side FET in the return phase.

Each FET position conducts for 120° of the electrical cycle. With complementary PWM at duty D, the switching phase splits its current: the high-side conducts for D and the synchronous low-side for (1 − D).

### 1.2 Per-device RMS currents

Over one electrical cycle:

$$I_{rms,HS} = I_{ph}\sqrt{\tfrac{D}{3}}, \qquad I_{rms,LS,sync} = I_{ph}\sqrt{\tfrac{1-D}{3}}, \qquad I_{rms,LS,return}=I_{ph}\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{3}}$$

Summed over the bridge, this collapses to a duty-independent result:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS,hot}, \qquad R_{DS,hot}=k_T R_{25}$$

### 1.3 Temperature dependence

Use an exponential model, or the datasheet curve:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left(\frac{T_j+273}{298}\right)^{\alpha}, \quad \alpha\approx 2.0\text{–}2.3$$

This gives k_T ≈ 1.55–1.65 at 125 °C. Loss and temperature are coupled, so iterate: T_j → R_DS → P → T_j until the values converge. If the iteration diverges, you are in thermal runaway and the burst rating is invalid.

### 1.4 Worked numbers

- R_DS,hot = 0.9 mΩ × 1.6 = **1.44 mΩ**
- P_cond,ch = 2 × 80² × 1.44 mΩ = **18.4 W per channel**
- Per-FET average (6 FETs, roughly even over a cycle): about **3.1 W**. The instantaneous loss while conducting is I²R = **9.2 W**.

### 1.5 PCB copper loss

This term is not negligible and is often forgotten.

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho_{Cu}(T)\, \ell}{w\, t\, n_{layers}}, \qquad \rho_{Cu}(T)=1.72\times10^{-8}\,[1+0.00393(T-20)]$$

Example: phase and bus path ℓ = 15 mm, w = 4 mm, 4 paralleled 2 oz layers (70 µm), T = 100 °C:

- ρ = 2.26×10⁻⁸ Ω·m
- R_Cu ≈ 2.26e-8 × 0.015 / (0.004 × 70e-6 × 4) ≈ **0.30 mΩ**

The current path crosses roughly two such segments, so:

- P_Cu ≈ 2 × 80² × 0.30 mΩ ≈ **3.9 W per channel**

Battery pad and wire terminations are additional.
