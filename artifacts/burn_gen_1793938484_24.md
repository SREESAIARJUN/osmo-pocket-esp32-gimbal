# STM32H743 Nano AIO / 4-in-1 ESC (Job #20): System Architecture & Derivations

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Architecture Corrections

Three points need to be settled first, because they shape the rest of the document.

1. **The H743 does not switch the MOSFETs.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. This document uses an AT32F421 running AM32, or one MCU per pair of channels. The 48 kHz PWM, dead-time and commutation live in that firmware. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to the ESCs only over bidirectional DShot600 and an optional telemetry UART. This document models both domains.
2. **No MOSFET part number was specified.** I use a representative 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm PDFN device, listed below. Every equation is parametric, so you can substitute your actual datasheet values. **All numerical results must be re-run against the selected part.**
3. **"80 A burst" is a short-duration rating, not a continuous one.** Section 1 shows that a 20×20 mm board cannot dissipate 80 A losses in steady state. The derivation below gives the actual burst time limit.

### Assumed Parameters

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 25.2 V | 6S fully charged (worst case for switching loss) |
| I_ph | 80 A | Burst phase current, per motor |
| f_sw | 48 kHz | PWM frequency |
| R_ds(on),25 | 1.0 mΩ @ V_gs = 10 V | Representative 40 V PDFN3.3 |
| k_T | 1.45 | R_ds multiplier at T_j = 100 °C |
| Q_g / Q_gd / Q_gs2 | 40 / 8 / 4 nC | Gate charge |
| V_plateau | 3.5 V | Miller plateau |
| C_oss(tr) | 1.5 nF | Effective output capacitance |
| Q_rr | 60 nC @ 80 A | Body-diode reverse recovery |
| V_f | 0.8 V | Body-diode forward drop |
| t_dt | 200 ns | Dead time per edge |
| V_drv | 10 V | Gate driver supply (e.g., FD6288Q) |
| R_g,total | 5 Ω | Driver + internal + external gate resistance |
| Modulation | Trapezoidal 120°, synchronous PWM on high-side leg | AM32 default |

**Note on 40 V parts at 6S.** The bus is 25.2 V, but the switch-node overshoot is roughly V_bus + L_loop·di/dt. With L_loop = 2 nH and di/dt = 80 A / 15 ns, the overshoot term is about 10.7 V, giving roughly 36 V peak. That is uncomfortably close to 40 V. **Recommendation:** use 40 V parts only if the hot-loop layout is ≤ 1.5 nH and you add an RC snubber. Otherwise move to 60 V parts and accept roughly 1.5× higher R_ds(on).

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current Path

In 120° trapezoidal commutation, two phases conduct and one floats at any instant. The current flows through:
- the PWM leg (either the high-side FET during t_on, or the synchronous low-side during t_off), and
- the low-side FET of the return leg, which is fully on.

So at every instant **exactly two FETs carry I_ph**, apart from dead-time intervals, which are handled in §2.

### 1.2 Per-Motor Conduction Loss

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds,25}\, k_T$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.45 = \mathbf{18.56\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET Average

Each of the six FETs conducts for one-third of the electrical period:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)}{3} = \frac{6400 \times 1.45\text{e-}3}{3} = \mathbf{3.09\ W}$$
