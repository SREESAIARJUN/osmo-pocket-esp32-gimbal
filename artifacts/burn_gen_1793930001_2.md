## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Design Risks

These points determine whether the rest of the document is valid.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot on the H743 sends DShot600 commands. Each motor channel needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32 to do 48 kHz complementary PWM, back-EMF sensing and bidirectional telemetry. This design uses AT32F421 MCUs running AM32 (STM32G071 also works). The H743 timer budget below covers only the DShot links.
2. **80 A burst on a nano footprint is thermally marginal.** The analysis in §3 shows about 27 W per channel at 80 A, and about 109 W if all four channels burst at once. A 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 board can absorb this for roughly 1 to 2 seconds, not continuously. The "80 A" figure should be specified as a **≤1 s burst per channel, 40–45 A continuous with airflow**. Any stronger claim fails qualification.
3. **All component parameters below are representative assumptions** for a 40 V PDFN3.3×3.3 MOSFET class. Before freezing values, replace them with the datasheet numbers of the selected part, especially R_DS(on) versus T_j, Q_gd, Q_rr and Z_th.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j)=R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At the design point T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{DS}=1.2\,\text{mΩ}\times(1+0.007\times 85)=1.91\ \text{mΩ}$$

### 1.2 Bridge conduction topology

In any six-step sector, two phase legs are active:

- **Leg A (PWM leg):** the high-side switch conducts for D·T_s. The synchronous low-side switch conducts for (1−D)·T_s.
- **Leg B (return leg):** the low-side switch conducts 100% of the sector.

The phase current therefore always passes through exactly two channels in series:

$$P_{cond,bridge}=I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}\,[D+(1-D)] + I_{ph}^2R_{DS}=2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

This total is independent of D because of synchronous rectification. PWM ripple adds a correction term:

$$I_{rms}^2=I_{ph}^2+\frac{\Delta I^2}{12},\qquad \Delta I=\frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}f_{sw}}$$

Example: L_ph = 15 µH, worst case D = 0.5, V_bemf ≈ 12 V. This gives ΔI ≈ 9.2 A, and the ΔI²/12 term is ≈ 7 A², which is negligible (≈0.1%).

$$P_{cond}=2\times80^2\times1.91\,\text{mΩ}=\mathbf{24.4\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-device distribution

Each FET carries current for 1/3 of the electrical period, as either the PWM device or the return device.

- Worst-case **instantaneous** FET (the return LS within a sector): I²·R = **12.2 W**.
- **Electrical-cycle average** per FET ≈ 24.4 / 6 ≈ **4.1 W**.

Electrical frequency at burst is about 1–3 kHz, i.e. a sector lasts about 50–170 µs. That is much shorter than the die thermal time constant (~1–5 ms), so the cycle-averaged value is valid for junction calculations. A stalled or low-speed rotor is the exception: there, the sector-peak value applies. **Stall/desync protection is therefore mandatory.**
