## 0. Architecture Corrections and Feasibility

Three issues change the design and need to be settled first.

| # | Issue | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production AIO, each motor has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, which generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bi-directional DShot600 to those MCUs. | The power stage below uses 4× ESC MCUs (STM32G071 or AT32F421 class). The H743 timer and DMA plan covers only DShot. |
| 2 | **80 A × 4 = 320 A aggregate.** A nano (20×20 mm) board cannot carry this through its battery pads, and neither can an XT60 or XT30. | "80 A burst" is treated as **per motor, short duration**, with an aggregate battery limit of about 120–150 A peak. |
| 3 | **Steady-state heat rejection of a 20×20 board is about 5–15 W.** At 80 A per motor, dissipation exceeds 100 W. | 80 A is a **transient (≲1 s) rating limited by thermal mass**, not a continuous rating (see §3). |

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step trapezoidal drive
In each 60° sector, two phases conduct. One leg is PWM-switched and one low-side FET stays continuously on:

- PWM leg high-side FET (HS): conducts for fraction **D**
- PWM leg low-side FET (LS, synchronous rectification): conducts for fraction **1 − D**
