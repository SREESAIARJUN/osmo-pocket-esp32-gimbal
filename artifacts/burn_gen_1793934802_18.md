## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths

The model assumes trapezoidal six-step commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM:

- At any instant, exactly two phase legs carry the motor current I.
- During the PWM on-time, current flows through the high-side switch of the PWM leg and the low-side switch of the return leg.
- During the off-time, the low-side switch of the PWM leg is synchronously rectifying, so current flows through the PWM-leg LS and the return-leg LS.

Either way, **two channels conduct the full current at all times**, apart from the dead-time intervals, which are treated in §2.

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

### 1.2 Electro-thermal coupling

Because R_DS(on) rises with T_j, the loss and the temperature must be solved together. Per conducting FET:

$$P_{FET} = I^2 R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)], \qquad T_j = T_{PCB} + P_{FET}\,R_{\theta JC}$$

Substituting and solving gives a closed form, valid when the denominator is greater than zero (otherwise thermal runaway):

$$T_j = \frac{T_{PCB} + I^2 R_{25} R_{\theta JC}(1 - 25\alpha)}{1 - \alpha I^2 R_{25} R_{\theta JC}}$$

With I = 80 A, R_25 = 1 mΩ, R_θJC = 1.5 K/W and T_PCB = 100 °C (end of burst):

- I²R_25 = 6.4 W
- αI²R_25R_θJC = 0.0528
- T_j = (100 + 9.6 × 0.8625) / 0.9472 = **114.3 °C**
- R_DS(on)(114 °C) = 1.49 mΩ
- P_FET = **9.53 W**
- P_cond,motor = **19.1 W**
- With all four motors at 80 A: **76.3 W**

A design-margin case uses R_DS(on) at its max datasheet value (typically 1.3× typical). That gives roughly 25 W per motor and 100 W across four motors.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Its HS and LS form a hard-switched half-bridge pair: the HS hard-switches, while the LS commutates via its body diode during dead time.

### 2.1 V-I overlap (HS hard switching)

Gate currents during the Miller plateau:

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{DRV} - V_{pl}}{R_g} = \frac{10-3}{5} = 1.4\text{ A}, \qquad I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_g} = 0.6\text{ A}$$

Transition times:

$$t_{on} = \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{g,on}} = 8.6\text{ ns}, \qquad t_{off} = \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{g,off}} = 20\text{ ns}$$

Overlap loss:

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_{on}+t_{off}) f_{sw} = \tfrac12 (25.2)(80)(28.6\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz}) = \mathbf{1.38\ W}$$

**Design lever:** t_off dominates this term. Using a driver with a low-impedance sink, or a turn-off diode in parallel with R_g, roughly halves P_VI.

### 2.2 Body-diode reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 60\text{ nC}\cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.073\ W}$$

The recovery event also adds current to the HS turn-on. That is partially captured in t_on, and the 60 nC figure is conservative.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,tr} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} \times 2 = \mathbf{0.046\ W}$$

The factor of 2 accounts for the HS and LS C_oss charge exchange.

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM period:

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2t_d)\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\text{ ns}\cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$
