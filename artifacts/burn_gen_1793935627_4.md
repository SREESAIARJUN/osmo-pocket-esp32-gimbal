# Job #5: STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC
## Electrothermal Model, Switching Loss Model, Bi-Directional DShot600 DMA Plan, and Reference Netlist

---

## 0. Feasibility Findings

These findings determine the rest of the design, so they come first.

| # | Finding | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | The STM32H743 cannot be the ESC commutation controller. ArduPilot does not run ESC firmware, and four 48 kHz commutation loops with BEMF sensing would conflict with the flight stack's real-time budget. | Use a **two-tier architecture**. The H743 runs ArduPilot. Four dedicated ESC MCUs (AT32F421K8 or STM32G071, running AM32) each drive one motor. Bi-directional DShot600 links them. |
| F2 | "80 A burst" on a 20×20 mm nano board is thermally credible only for **one motor, for a few seconds**. Four motors at 80 A means 320 A of bus current through an XT60/pad and roughly 120 W into about 3 J/K of thermal mass. | Rate the board as **80 A single-channel, ≤5 s burst; 35–40 A per channel continuous with airflow**. The math in §3 shows why. |
| F3 | 48 kHz PWM is high for 3.3×3.3 mm FETs at 80 A. Switching and dead-time losses are still small next to conduction loss, but they scale linearly with frequency. | Keep 48 kHz for low-KV, low-inductance motors. Allow 24 kHz as a thermal fallback in AM32 config. |

All values below are **parametric**. Replace the assumed parameters with your selected MOSFET datasheet values and re-run.
