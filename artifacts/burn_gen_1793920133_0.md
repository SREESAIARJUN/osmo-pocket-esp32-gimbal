I don't have Job #12's BOM, stack-up, or layout, so this document uses a stated set of reference parameters. Every equation is parametric. Replace the values in §0 with your actual MOSFET datasheet and stack-up numbers, and the results will carry through.

> **Architecture caveat:** In an AIO design, the H743 is the flight controller. ## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation topology
Trapezoidal 6-step control with synchronous (complementary) PWM is used. In each 60° sector, two legs are active:
- **Leg A, PWM leg:** Current alternates between HS (duty D) and LS (1−D, synchronous rectification).
- **Leg B, return leg:** The LS is held on continuously.

Current therefore always flows through exactly **two channel resistances** in series, regardless of D (ignoring dead-time, see §2.4).

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)
$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + k_T\,(T_j - 25)\right], \qquad k_T \approx 0.0059\ \text{K}^{-1}\ \Rightarrow\ \alpha_R(110^\circ\text{C}) \approx 1.5
$$

### 1.3 Per-motor conduction loss
$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)
$$
At 80 A with R_ds = 1.5 mΩ:
$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 6400 \times 0.0015 = \mathbf{19.2\ W}
$$

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution (worst-case device)
Each leg is active for 2/3 of the electrical cycle: 1/3 as the PWM leg and 1/3 as the return leg.
- **HS FET:** $P = I^2 R \cdot D \cdot \tfrac{1}{3}$
- **LS FET:** $P = I^2 R\left[(1-D)\tfrac{1}{3} + \tfrac{1}{3}\right]$

Averaged over a full electrical revolution, the six FETs share 19.2 W, about 3.2 W each. The **instantaneous** worst case is a return-leg LS FET carrying the full $I^2R = 9.6\ \text{W}$. This matters at low eRPM, where a sector lasts longer than the junction thermal time constant (≈ms). Size the design for the instantaneous value during low-speed, high-current events such as punch-outs from idle.

### 1.5 Copper/trace and shunt losses
$$
P_{Cu,motor} = 2\,I^2 R_{trace} = 2 \times 6400 \times 0.0005 = \mathbf{6.4\ W}
$$
If phase shunts are present (e.g. 0.25 mΩ), add $I^2 R_{shunt}\cdot k$, where k is the fraction of time the shunt conducts. Bus-input copper carries $I_{bus} = D \cdot I_{phase}$ and must be calculated separately.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Per motor, there is one hard-switched HS turn-on/turn-off pair per PWM period. The LS performs ZVS-like transitions after dead time.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard switching, HS FET)
$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}
= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance (C_oss) loss
$$
P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}
$$
(This uses the energy-equivalent approximation. The exact value is $E_{oss}(V)\cdot f$ from the datasheet curve.)

### 2.3 Reverse recovery (LS body diode → HS turn-on)
$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 60\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.073\ \text{W}
$$
(Q_rr scales with di/dt and I_F. At 80 A, use the datasheet value at matching conditions.)

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction
$$
P_{dt} = 2\,V_f\,I\,t_{dt}\,f_{sw} = 2 \times 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

### 2.5 Gate-drive loss (dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET)
$$
P_{gate} = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \times 40\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.038\ \text{W/motor}
$$

### 2.6 Loss budget at 80 A burst

| Component | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 19.20 W | 76.8 W |
| Copper/traces | 6.40 W | 25.6 W |
| V–I overlap | 1.94 W | 7.8 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W | 2.4 W |
| C_oss + Q_rr | 0.13 W | 0.5 W |
| Gate drive | 0.04 W | 0.15 W |
| **Total** | **≈28.3 W** | **≈113 W** |

**Efficiency at burst:** Output power is roughly $D \cdot V_{bus} \cdot I$. At D≈0.9, that is ≈1814 W per motor, so η ≈ 98.5%. The efficiency is acceptable. The problem is **heat density**.

**f_sw sensitivity:** $\partial P/\partial f_{sw} \approx (1.94+0.61+0.13)/48\text{k} \approx 56\ \mu\text{W/Hz}$ per motor. Dropping to 24 kHz saves ≈1.3 W/motor. Conduction loss dominates, so the 48 kHz choice is justified for motor smoothness and current ripple.
