## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Engineering Caveats

### Architectural clarification

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors directly. In every production AIO design, the H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller and talks bi-directional DShot600 to **four separate ESC MCUs** that run AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical ESC MCUs are the STM32G071 and AT32F421.

- The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs.
- The H743 owns the DShot timers and DMA.

This document models both sides accordingly.

### Reality check before you read further

> **An 80 A burst on all four channels in a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) form factor is thermally non-sustainable.**
>
> The model below shows roughly 100 W of dissipation at 4 × 80 A. That limits the burst to about **1–3 seconds** before the FET junctions or PCB laminate approach their limits, even with prop-wash cooling.
>
> - Treat "80 A" as a **≤1 s transient rating per channel**, not a sustained rating.
> - Twelve 5×6 FETs will not physically fit on a 20×20 board. Use **30.5×30.5 mm** for this current class, or move to 3.3×3.3 FETs and accept the higher R_DS(on).

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step commutation

In each of the six commutation sectors, two phases conduct and one floats.

- During PWM **ON**: current flows through the high-side FET of phase A, the motor, and the low-side FET of phase B.
- During PWM **OFF** (with synchronous rectification): current freewheels through the low-side FET of phase A and the low-side FET of phase B.

In both states, the current passes through exactly **two channels' worth of R_DS(on)**. Conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle, to first order:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

### 1.2 Temperature-corrected R_DS(on)

R_DS(on) rises with junction temperature, which creates positive feedback:

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ /\text{K}
$$

At T_j = 125 °C, R ≈ 1.5 mΩ. Solve the following iteratively with the thermal model in §3:

$$
T_j = T_a + P(T_j) \cdot R_{\theta,ja}
$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A (per motor channel)

| Term | Expression | Result |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (hot) | 80² × 2 × 1.5 mΩ | **19.2 W** |
| FET conduction (cold, 25 °C) | 80² × 2 × 1.0 mΩ | 12.8 W |
| Current shunt (0.25 mΩ, if fitted) | 80² × 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| PCB copper (≈0.4 mΩ path, 2 oz, 4 layers paralleled) | 80² × 0.4 mΩ | 2.6 W |

### 1.4 Per-FET breakdown (thermal hotspot)

Each FET conducts for 120° of the electrical cycle, plus freewheel intervals for the low-side devices. This gives:

- **Instantaneous peak per FET:** I²R = 6400 × 1.5 mΩ = **9.6 W**
- **Electrical-cycle average per FET:** ≈ P_total / 6 ≈ **3.5–4.5 W**

The low-side FETs run hotter at low duty cycle because they carry the freewheel current. With 6-step commutation, the electrical period at typical eRPM is ≪ the FET thermal time constant (ms range). The junction therefore effectively sees the **average** power plus a small ripple.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switched half-bridge incurs switching transitions. The second (sector-static) leg does not.
