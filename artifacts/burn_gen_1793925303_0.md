# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Design Package (Job #5)

I don't have prior context for Job #5, so this package rests on the explicit assumptions below. Every equation is parameterized, so you can substitute real datasheet values.

**Important about the architecture.** The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. It runs ArduPilot and sends **bidirectional DShot600** to four ESC MCUs, one per motor. Each ESC MCU runs AM32 or BLHeli_32 and generates the **48 kHz** bridge PWM. The losses below are therefore bridge-level, and the DMA section covers the H743 side only.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

### 1.2 Per-motor conduction loss

With complementary PWM in 6-step commutation, current always flows through one high-side and one low-side path. The high-side FET and the synchronous low-side FET share the duty cycle (D and 1−D) within the active leg. The sum is therefore independent of D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j) \;+\; I_{rms}^2 R_{shunt} \;+\; I_{rms}^2 R_{trace}$$

### 1.3 Iterative solution

The loss depends on T_j, and T_j depends on the loss. Solve iteratively:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_{ref} + P_{FET}(T_j^{(k)})\cdot Z_{th}(t)$$

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A

Taking T_j = 125 °C, so R_ds = 2.25 mΩ:

- **FET conduction:** 80² × 2 × 2.25 mΩ = 6400 × 4.5 mΩ = **28.8 W per motor**.
- **Shunt:** 0.5 mΩ gives 3.2 W per motor. Many 4-in-1 designs use a single DC-link shunt per ESC; it is included here.
- **Copper:** about 0.3 mΩ effective gives 1.9 W.
- **Total conduction:** ≈ **33.9 W per motor**, or **135.6 W** for four motors.

Per active FET this is ≈ 14.4 W. The load rotates among the 6 FETs every commutation, so the time-averaged load is ≈ 9.6 W per FET (28.8 W × 2 conducting / 6).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switching leg incurs hard-switching loss.

### 2.1 Hard-switching (V–I overlap)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = V_{bus} Q_{rr} f_{sw} = 25.2 \cdot 50\text{nC} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

### 2.4 Output-capacitance loss

C_oss,eff is assumed to be about 1 nF.

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 1\text{nF} \cdot 635 \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.015\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated in the driver, not in the FET. Two FETs switch per PWM cycle.

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 50\text{nC} \cdot 10 \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 2.6 Switching total

≈ **2.6 W per motor**. At 48 kHz, switching loss is under 10 % of conduction loss at burst. The 48 kHz choice is justified because it lowers current ripple and acoustic noise on low-inductance motors.

The ripple term (ΔI²/12 added to the RMS current) is negligible for L_phase ≳ 10 µH:

$$\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L f_{sw}} \le \frac{25.2 \cdot 0.25}{2 \cdot 10\mu H \cdot 48k} \approx 6.6\text{ A}_{pp}$$

The phase-to-phase inductance is 2L. The contribution is +3.6 A²: negligible.

## 3. Total Burst Loss Budget

| Loss | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (125 °C) | 28.8 W | 115.2 W |
| Shunt + copper | 5.1 W | 20.4 W |
| Switching + dead-time + Q_rr + C_oss | 2.6 W | 10.5 W |
| Gate drive + regulators (ESC MCU, BEC overhead) | ~0.3 W | ~1.2 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 36.8 W** | **≈ 147 W** |

Efficiency at 80 A × 4 on 22.2 V (7.1 kW electrical) is ≈ 97.9 %. However, **147 W into a ~30 mm board is purely a transient problem**.
