# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage, Thermal, and Bi-Directional DShot Design Package

I have no prior context for "Job #4", so this package rests on the explicit assumptions in §0. Change any of them and re-run the equations. All of the math is parametric for that reason.

---

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and One Correction

**Correction to the premise:** the STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. In every production AIO and 4-in-1 design, the H743 is the flight controller running ArduPilot. Each motor has a dedicated ESC MCU, typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 firmware, and that MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 talks to the ESCs over bi-directional DShot600. I model it this way.

| Parameter | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus,max = 25.2 V (design to 30 V transients) |
| Burst current | 80 A **per motor**, ≤10 s, 4 motors simultaneously as worst case |
| MOSFETs | 40 V N-ch, PDFN 3.3×3.3 or 5×6, R_DS(on) = 1.0 mΩ @ 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V. 6 per motor, 24 total |
| Datasheet figures | Q_g = 60 nC, Q_rr = 50 nC, C_oss = 1.5 nF, t_r + t_f ≈ 40 ns at 80 A |
| Gate driver | 3-phase half-bridge driver (e.g., FD6288Q class), ~1.5 A source/sink |
| Commutation | Trapezoidal 6-step, complementary (synchronous) PWM, dead time 200 ns |
| PWM frequency | f_sw = 48 kHz |
| Board | 30.5 × 30.5 mm mount, ~36 × 36 mm outline, 8-layer, 2 oz outer and inner power planes |
| Tj limit | 150 °C absolute, **125 °C design limit** |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

| T_j | R_DS(on) |
|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 1.38 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 1.50 mΩ |

### 1.2 Current paths in 6-step with synchronous PWM

During any commutation sector, phase X is PWM'd and phase Y is held low:

- **High-side X** conducts for duty fraction D.
- **Low-side X** (synchronous rectifier) conducts for (1 − D).
- **Low-side Y** conducts 100 % of the time.

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS} = 2 I^2 R_{DS}$$

The key result is that conduction loss is **independent of duty cycle** with synchronous rectification. Dead-time diode loss is handled separately in §2.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

With R_DS = 1.4 mΩ (T_j ≈ 105 °C, self-consistent iteration):

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.4\times10^{-3} = 17.9\ \text{W per motor}$$

This loss is shared across 6 FETs over a full electrical cycle. Each FET conducts 1/3 of the time as a low-side static leg, plus its PWM share. The average is ~3.0 W per FET, with **peak instantaneous ~9 W** in the conducting FET pair.

### 1.4 Copper and interconnect losses

Assume about 0.35 mΩ effective per motor current path. This covers the battery pad, planes, phase pads, and vias, using ρ_Cu (100 °C) = 2.2×10⁻⁸ Ω·m on 2 oz × 2 parallel planes.

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.35\times10^{-3} = 2.24\ \text{W per motor}$$

Shunt sense resistor: 0.25 mΩ shared on the bus. At 320 A total, P = 320² × 0.25 mΩ = **25.6 W**. That is unacceptable. **Use Hall current sensing**, e.g., an ACS7xx-class device or a contactless field sensor over the bus bar, or accept a shunt rated for ~10 s bursts on a 4 mm² copper pour.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per motor, only the PWM'd high-side FET hard-switches. The synchronous low-side turns on ZVS-ish after dead time.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$
$$= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{kHz} = 1.94\ \text{W}$$

The rise and fall times come from the gate charge:

$$t_{r,f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}+Q_{gs2}}{I_{gate}}$$

With Q_gd ≈ 12 nC at 1.0–1.5 A drive, t ≈ 10–20 ns each. **The gate resistor value directly trades this loss against ringing and EMI.**

### 2.2 Output capacitance loss

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss,eff} V^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{nF} \times 25.2^2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.023\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Body-diode reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

Recovery current also adds roughly 10–20 % to the high-side turn-on loss, which is absorbed into the 40 ns figure.

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F \, I \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 2(200\text{ns}) \times 48\text{k} = 1.23\ \text{W}$$

Reducing dead time from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per motor. Validate this against shoot-through margin with a scope at maximum temperature.

### 2.5 Gate drive loss

This is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistor, not in the FET.

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2 = 60\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = 0.058\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Frequency sensitivity

At 80 A, the frequency-dependent loss is P_sw + P_dt + P_rr ≈ **0.068 W/kHz per motor**.

| f_sw | Frequency-dependent loss per motor |
|---|---|
| 24 kHz | 1.6 W |
| 48 kHz | 3.2 W |
| 96 kHz | 6.5 W |
