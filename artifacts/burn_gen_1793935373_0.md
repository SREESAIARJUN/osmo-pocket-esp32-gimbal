**Main finding.** If "80 A burst" means 80 A per motor on a nano-class board:

- Instantaneous dissipation is about **85 W**.
- The board has a lumped heat capacity of about **5–6 J/K**.
- The result is roughly **+150 K in 10 s**, which is **not survivable**.

The rating is credible only in two cases:

1. 80 A is the **total** pack current (about 20 A per motor).
2. The per-motor burst lasts **at most about 1 s**, with forced airflow.

The derivation follows.

## 1. Electrothermal Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 1.1 Conduction loss

In trapezoidal (six-step) commutation with complementary PWM, two phase legs carry the motor current I at any instant.

- **Active leg:** the high-side FET conducts for D·T. The low-side FET conducts for (1−D)·T as a synchronous rectifier.
- **Return leg:** its low-side FET is held on for the whole interval.

Therefore, ignoring dead time, there are always exactly two R_DS(on) in series in the current path:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

This expression is **independent of the duty cycle D** because synchronous rectification is used. That is a useful simplification.

**Electrothermal fixed point.** R_DS depends on T_j, which in turn depends on P_cond:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + P_{FET}\,Z_{\theta}(t), \qquad P_{FET}\approx \tfrac{1}{2}P_{cond}+\tfrac{1}{2}P_{sw}$$

Iterate until T_j changes by less than 1 K. **Thermal runaway** occurs if

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\,Z_\theta \ge 1 \;\Rightarrow\; 2I^2R_{25}\,\alpha\,Z_\theta \ge 1$$

At 80 A with Z_θ = 10 K/W (FET pad to ambient, local):

$$2 \cdot 6400 \cdot 0.001 \cdot 0.006 \cdot 10 = 0.77$$

This is below 1, so the loop is stable, but the margin is thin.

**Evaluated at 80 A**, with T_j ≈ 85 °C so that R_DS = 1.0 × (1 + 0.006 × 60) = 1.36 mΩ:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.36\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{17.4\ W}$$

### 1.2 Switching loss at 48 kHz

There is one hard-switched leg per PWM period: the active leg. The high side turns on into an inductive current that is already commutating through the low-side body diode or channel.

**V–I overlap (hard-switching triangle approximation):**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5\times25.2\times80\times40\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = C_{oss(er)}\,V_{bus}^2\,f_{sw} = 1.5\text{nF}\times635\times48\text{k} = 0.046\ W$$

**Reverse recovery of the low-side body diode:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 50\text{nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.060\ W$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (2 edges per period):

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times400\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

**Gate drive** (2 FETs switched per period; this loss is mostly in the driver and gate resistor, not the FET):

$$P_{g} = 2\,Q_g\,V_{drv}\,f_{sw} = 2\times50\text{nC}\times10\times48\text{k} = 0.048\ W$$

### 1.3 Per-channel and board totals

| Loss term | 80 A per motor | 20 A per motor (80 A total) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (R_DS at T_j) | 17.4 W (1.36 mΩ) | 0.88 W (1.1 mΩ) |
| V–I switching | 1.94 W | 0.48 W |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 W | 0.31 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.11 W | 0.11 W |
| Gate | 0.05 W | 0.05 W |
| **Per ESC channel** | **≈ 20.7 W** | **≈ 1.83 W** |
| **Board (×4)** | **≈ 83 W** | **≈ 7.3 W** |
| Shunt + copper (see §2.3) | +3–5 W | +0.5 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 85–88 W** | **≈ 7.8 W** |

**Design levers, in order of impact:**

1. **Reduce R_DS(on).** Use paralleled FETs or 0.6 mΩ parts; conduction loss scales linearly with R_DS.
2. **Shorten dead time to about 100 ns.** This halves P_d
