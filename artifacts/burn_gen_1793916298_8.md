**Scope:** Flight controller (STM32H743) plus a 4-channel ESC power stage on a nano form factor (30.5 × 30.5 mm board, 25.5 mm M2 hole pattern).
**Design targets:** 6S input, 80 A burst per motor, 48 kHz PWM, ArduPilot with bidirectional DShot600.

1. **80 A is a burst rating, not a continuous one.** Physics limits a nano board to roughly 25–30 A continuous per motor in prop-wash airflow. Section 3 derives the allowable burst duration.
2. **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz motor PWM.** It sends DShot600 frames to the four ESC MCUs. The 48 kHz figure is an ESC-firmware parameter. The H743's job is DShot timing and DMA.
3. **The DShot600 bit timing is not exact at a 200 MHz timer clock.** Section 4 quantifies the error. It is within spec.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation with synchronous rectification:

- During the PWM on-time, current flows through the high side (HS) of phase A, the motor, and the low side (LS) of phase B.
- During the off-time, the HS of phase A turns off and the LS of phase A conducts in synchronous mode. Current still passes through two channels.

So whatever the duty cycle D, **two R_DS(on) are always in series** with the phase current (dead time excluded):

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j) \cdot (1 - 2t_{dt}f_{sw})
$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

R_DS(on) rises with junction temperature:

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ /\text{K} \;\Rightarrow\; k_T(125\,°\text{C}) = 1.5
$$

This creates a positive feedback loop: higher temperature raises resistance, which raises loss and temperature further. The loop is stable only if:

$$
\alpha \cdot P_{cond} \cdot R_{\theta,total} < 1
$$

This stability check governs survival during a burst.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

The dead-time factor is 1 − 2(200 ns)(48 kHz) = 0.981.

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega \times 0.981 = 18.8\ \text{W per motor channel}
$$

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

Each phase leg conducts for 2/3 of the electrical period. Its HS carries the current for a fraction D of that time and its LS for (1 − D).

- Averaged over one electrical revolution, each of the 6 FETs dissipates about 18.8/6 ≈ **3.1 W**.
- Peak instantaneous dissipation is at D → 1. During its 1/3-period window, the HS FET carries the full load:

$$
P_{FET,peak} = 80^2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 9.6\ \text{W}
$$

### 1.5 Copper and shunt losses

These are often ignored but matter at this current.

| Element | Resistance | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Current shunt, 0.25 mΩ (single shunt in the DC link) | 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| Phase pad and trace, 2 oz × 4 layers, ~0.3 mΩ | 0.3 mΩ | 1.9 W |

**Copper subtotal: about 3.5 W per channel.**

## 2. Switching Loss Model (f_sw = 48 kHz)

In each PWM cycle, exactly one leg hard-switches.

### 2.1 V–I overlap

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 1.94\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

The LS body diode recovers when the HS turns on:

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}
$$

At 80 A, Q_rr scales up roughly in proportion to di/dt. A conservative figure is ×3, giving **0.15 W**.

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per period:

$$
P_{dt} = V_F I_{ph} \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 1.23\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

Six gates per channel switch at f_sw in the worst case (complementary PWM). This loss is dissipated in the driver and R_g, not in the FET.

$$
P_{gate} = 6 \cdot Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 6 \times 50\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.144\ \text{W}
$$
