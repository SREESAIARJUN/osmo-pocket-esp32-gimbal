# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #4): Power-Stage, Thermal and DShot Timing Model

## 0. Architecture Baseline and Assumptions

A design like this needs one architectural clarification first. ArduPilot on the H743 does not commutate motors. The FC sends bidirectional DShot600 to **four dedicated ESC MCUs** (e.g., AT32F421 running AM32, or EFM8/BLHeli_S). Those ESC MCUs run the 48 kHz PWM and the gate drivers. The models below follow that split.

| Board | 30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz copper on all layers |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation, two phases conduct at any moment. With complementary PWM:
- The PWM leg carries current through either its high-side FET (on-time) or its synchronous low-side FET (off-time).
- The low leg's low-side FET carries current continuously.

So at every instant the current flows through **exactly two R_DS(on)** values, plus deadtime diode intervals (handled in §2.4):

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + I^2 R_{Cu}$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence (coupled problem)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.004\text{–}0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

Using α = 0.0045 and T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{DS(on)} = 0.80 \times 1.45 = 1.16\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{FET,cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.16\text{ m}\Omega = 6400 \times 2.32\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{14.85\ W}$$

### 1.3 Copper loss

Assume 70 µm plane, about 8 mm effective width, 15 mm path length per conducting leg, 4 parallel layers, and ρ_Cu(85 °C) = 2.1×10⁻⁸ Ω·m:

$$R_{Cu} \approx \frac{2.1\times10^{-8}\times 0.015}{70\times10^{-6}\times 0.008\times 4} \approx 0.14\ \text{m}\Omega \text{ per leg}$$

Two legs plus vias and the shunt region gives roughly 0.4 mΩ total:

$$P_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.4\text{ m}\Omega \approx \mathbf{2.6\ W}$$

### 1.4 Shunt (if present)

A 0.25 mΩ shunt adds 6400 × 0.25 mΩ = **1.6 W**.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches.

### 2.1 Transition times (gate-charge method)

Miller plateau V_pl ≈ 4.5 V. R_g,total = 4.7 Ω external + about 3 Ω (internal + driver) ≈ 7.7 Ω.

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{12 - 4.5}{7.7} = 0.97\text{ A},\qquad t_{r,V} = \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} \approx 8.2\text{ ns}$$

$$I_{g,off} = \frac{4.5}{7.7} = 0.58\text{ A},\qquad t_{f,V} = \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,off}} \approx 13.8\text{ ns}$$

Add the current-commutation (Q_gs2) intervals and power-loop inductance effects. A realistic total is **t_r + t_f ≈ 35 ns**.

### 2.2 V–I overlap loss

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 35\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.69\ W}$$

### 2.3 Capacitive and reverse-recovery losses

$$P_{oss} = C_{oss} V^2 f_{sw} = 1.5\text{ nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{ kHz} = 0.046\text{ W}$$

This counts both FETs of the leg at ½CV² each.

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = 40\text{ nC}\times 25.2 \times 48\text{ kHz} = 0.048\text{ W}$$

### 2.4 Deadtime body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I \cdot 2t_{dead} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$
