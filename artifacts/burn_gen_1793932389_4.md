## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C, R_DS = 1.5 × 1.5 = **2.25 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current paths in trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification

In each 60° sector, phase A is PWM'd (high side on for D, low side on for 1−D), and phase B's low side is held on (return path):

- High side of A: I² R · D
- Low side of A (sync rectification): I² R · (1 − D)
- Low side of B (return): I² R · 1

Summing these, the D terms cancel:

$$\boxed{P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) + I^2 R_{cu} + I^2 R_{shunt}}$$

The duty-independent result is a useful design property: conduction loss depends only on current, not throttle.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| Term | Calculation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| MOSFETs | 2 × 80² × 2.25 mΩ | 28.8 W |
| Copper (phase + bus traces, ≈0.3 mΩ path) | 6400 × 0.3 mΩ | 1.9 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, if used) | 6400 × 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| **Total per channel** | | **≈ 32.3 W** |

### 1.4 Per-FET average over one electrical cycle

Each FET carries every role in rotation, so the average per FET is P_FET ≈ 28.8 / 6 ≈ **4.8 W**.

The instantaneous peak per FET is 14.4 W while it acts as the return FET. That peak repeats at the electrical frequency, which is kHz range. The junction filters this ripple, adding only about 1–3 K of junction temperature ripple.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

With complementary PWM, only the PWM'd leg hard-switches. The return leg is static within a sector.

### 2.1 Hard-switching (V–I overlap)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40\text{ns} × 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 × 80 × 200\text{ns} × 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{nC} × 25.2 × 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

At 80 A, Q_rr scales up roughly as √(di/dt), so budget about 0.1 W.

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.02\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

Two FETs per leg switch each cycle:

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 × 40\text{nC} × 10 × 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.04\ W}$$

Most of this dissipates in R_g and the driver, not in the FET.

### 2.6 Switching total per channel

| Term | Value |
|---|---|
| Hard-switching | 1.94 W |
| Dead time | 0.61 W |
| Reverse recovery | 0.10 W |
| Output capacitance | 0.02 W |
| Gate drive | 0.04 W |
| **Total P_sw,ch** | **≈ 2.7 W** |

**Takeaways:**
- At 80 A, conduction loss is about 92% of the total. A 24 kHz PWM frequency would save only about 1.3 W per channel.
- Lower R_DS(on) is the more effective lever.
- Running 48 kHz is justified by motor current ripple and acoustics, not by efficiency.
