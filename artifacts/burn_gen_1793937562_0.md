# Job #43: STM32H743 Nano AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC) System Architecture Model

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

**The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFET bridges directly.** In every practical 4-in-1 design, the H743 is the flight controller (FC) running ArduPilot. Each motor bridge is commutated by a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, typically an AT32F421, STM32G071 or STM32F051. The FC talks to the ESC MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. The models below follow that split.

**All numerical results come from the assumed parameters in the table.** Replace them with values from your selected MOSFET datasheet before committing the design.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

The calculation is iterated to a self-consistent junction temperature of T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{ds}(110) = 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega \times (1 + 0.0055 \times 85) = 1.76\,\text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step complementary PWM

In any commutation sector, one phase is PWM-switched and one low-side FET is held fully on. The switched phase alternates between two states:

- its high-side FET conducts for duty D;
- its low-side FET (synchronous rectification) conducts for 1 − D.

Two FETs therefore carry I_pk at every instant, regardless of D:

$$P_{cond,leg} = I^2 R_{ds}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 1.3 Ripple correction

The RMS current includes the inductor ripple ΔI:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{pk}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}D(1-D)}{L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5" motor (L_ph ≈ 20 µH) at D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\times10^{-6}\times 48\,000} = 6.6\,\text{A}$$

The ΔI²/12 term is about 3.6 A², which is negligible against 6400 A². It is ignored below.

### 1.4 Result per motor channel

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.76\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{22.5\ W}$$

**PCB copper loss.** Each 80 A path is a 2 oz trace pair, 20 mm long and 5 mm wide, with vias. Using ρ_Cu(100 °C) = 2.2×10⁻⁸ Ω·m:

$$R_{trace} \approx \frac{\rho L}{w t} = \frac{2.2\times10^{-8} \times 0.02}{0.005 \times 70\times10^{-6}} \approx 1.26\,\text{m}\Omega$$

If two parallel layers carry the current, R ≈ 0.63 mΩ, giving 80² × 0.63 mΩ = **4.0 W** per channel. This term is frequently omitted, but it is comparable to a single FET's loss.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switched leg contributes. Each term uses V = 25.2 V and I = 80 A.

| Term | Formula | Result |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap (hard switching) | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 kHz | 1.94 W |
| C_oss energy | ½ · C_oss(er) · V² · f_sw = ½ × 1.2 nF × 635 × 48k | 0.018 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw = 40 nC × 25.2 × 48k | 0.048 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | 2 · V_f · I · t_dt · f_sw = 2 × 0.8 × 80 × 100 ns × 48k | 0.61 W |
| Gate drive (driver-side) | 2 · Q_g · V_cc · f_sw = 2 × 50 nC × 10 × 48k | 0.048 W |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.67 W** |

**Frequency sensitivity.** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw: about 1.3 W at 24 kHz and about 5.3 W at 96 kHz. At 80 A, conduction loss dominates (roughly 89 % of the total), so 48 kHz is a reasonable compromise between acoustic noise, current ripple and loss.

## 3. Total Loss and Thermal Model

### 3.1 Loss budget

| Item | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 22.5 W | 90.0 W |
| FET switching | 2.67 W | 10.7 W |
| PCB copper | 4.0 W | 16.0 W |
| **Total** | **29.2 W** | **116.7 W** |

**Distribution across FETs.** Each FET conducts during roughly 2 of the 6 sectors. Averaged over an electrical cycle, each of the 6 FETs dissipates:

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{25.2\,\text{W}}{6} \times 2 \approx 8.4\,\text{W}$$

The instantaneous peak per FET is about 11–14 W. Commutation periods are sub-millisecond, so the junction responds to the averaged value.

### 3.2 Junction steady-state check

Assumed thermal resistances:

- R_θJC = 1.5 K/W (PDFN3.3 exposed pad)
- R_θCB = 1.0 K/W (pad to board, including the thermal via array)
- R_θBA,board = 6 K/W (whole board, prop-wash airflow of about 5 m/s)

Board temperature rise in steady state:

$$\Delta T_{board} = 116.7 \times 6 = 700\,\text{K}$$

**Conclusion: 80 A per motor continuous is physically impossible on a nano board.** The rating can only be a short burst.

### 3.3 Burst model (Foster RC network)

The system is modelled as a two-node network:

- Node 1, junction: τ_j = R_θJC · C_j ≈ 1.5 × 0.03 ≈ 45 ms.
- Node 2, board: C_board ≈ (3.5 g FR4/Cu × 0.85 J/g·K) + (24 FETs × 0.03 J/K) ≈ 3.7 J/K.

The board's thermal time constant is:

$$\tau_{board} = R_{\theta BA} \times C_{board} = 6 \times 3.7 = 22\,\text{s}$$

For bursts with t ≪ τ_board, the board heats adiabatically:

$$\Delta T_{board}(t) \approx \frac{P_{tot}\,t}{C_{board}}$$

The FET junction then sits a quasi-static rise above the board:

$$\Delta T_{j-b} \approx P_{FET} (R_{\theta JC} + R_{\theta CB}) = 8.4 \times 2.5 = 21\,\text{K}$$
