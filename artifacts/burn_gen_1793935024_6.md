## 0. Assumptions and a Feasibility Flag

Four assumptions shape the numbers in this package. Change any of them and the model must be re-run.

| Parameter | Baseline | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Architecture | H743 FC + 4× AT32F421 ESC MCUs (AM32 firmware) | The H743 does not commutate. It sends bidir DShot600 to the ESC MCUs. |
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus,max = 25.2 V | 40 V MOSFETs give about 37% margin over the 29 V transient (with TVS) |
| Power FETs | 24× 40 V N-ch, PQFN 5×6 | R_ds(on) = 0.9 mΩ max at 25 °C, Q_g = 55 nC, Q_gd = 10 nC, Q_rr = 40 nC, Q_oss = 60 nC |
| PCB | 6-layer, 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner power planes | Exposed copper pads under the FETs for an optional heat-spreader |

**Feasibility flag ("nano" form factor).** A true 20×20 mm "nano" board cannot carry 80 A per channel.

- 24× PQFN 5×6 parts occupy about 720 mm² of footprint, against about 400 mm² of usable board area.
- Moving to 3.3×3.3 mm parts roughly doubles R_ds(on).
- A 20×20 board would therefore be limited to about 35–40 A burst.

This package is baselined on a **30.5×30.5 mm mount / 36×36 mm board**. "Nano" refers to the reduced component height (under 8 mm). If 20×20 is a hard requirement, the burst rating must be reduced. See §3.6.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM:

- At any instant, exactly **two legs** carry the phase current.
- In the PWM-driven leg, current alternates between the high-side FET (duty D) and the synchronous low-side FET (1−D).
- In the other active leg, the low-side FET is held on.

The instantaneous conduction loss is therefore **independent of duty cycle**:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot \left(2\,R_{ds}(T_j) + R_{Cu} + R_{shunt}\right)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence of R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

| T_j (°C) | R_ds (mΩ) |
|---|---|
| 25 | 0.90 |
| 100 | 1.27 |
| 125 | 1.40 |

### 1.3 Parasitic resistances

**Copper path.** The path runs from the battery pad, through the planes, the FET, and the phase pad, then back through the return. With 2 oz copper and stitched parallel planes:

$$R_{sq,2oz} = \frac{\rho_{Cu}}{t} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}}{70\times10^{-6}} = 0.246\ \text{m}\Omega/\square \quad (25^\circ\text{C})$$
