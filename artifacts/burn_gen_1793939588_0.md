## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Flags

**Flag 1: The H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC controller.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bi-directional DShot600. The 48 kHz six-step commutation should run on four dedicated ESC MCUs, each executing AM32 firmware. Suitable parts are the STM32G071 or AT32F421. Commutating four BLDC motors from the H743 while it also runs the EKF3 is technically possible, but it is not a sane production architecture.

**Flag 2: 80 A on a nano board is a short burst, not a continuous rating.** The math in §3 shows roughly **85 W of dissipation** at 4×80 A. On a 20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm board, this is survivable for only **seconds**. The numbers below quantify how many seconds.

**Flag 3: Device parameters are representative.** They are modeled on a 30 V / 1 mΩ-class PDFN3.3 MOSFET and an FD6288Q-class three-phase driver. Substitute datasheet values for your selected parts. Every equation is parameterized so you can do this directly.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Six-step commutation with synchronous rectification

In each 60° sector, two phase legs carry current:

- **PWM leg:** The high-side FET conducts for duty D. The complementary low-side FET (synchronous rectification) conducts for (1−D). The current path therefore always passes through exactly **one** FET in this leg.
- **Return leg:** The low-side FET is held fully on, so **one** FET carries current here as well.

The instantaneous conduction loss is therefore independent of D:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = I^2 \cdot R_{ds,hot}\cdot 2
$$

### 2.2 Numbers

$$
R_{ds,hot} = 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.5 = 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3}\times 2 = \mathbf{19.2\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Distribution across FETs

Each FET carries current for 120° of the electrical cycle, and the instantaneous sum across conducting FETs is 2·I²R. The average loss per FET is therefore:

$$
P_{cond,FET} = \frac{2 I^2 R}{6} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}
$$

### 2.4 Electrothermal convergence

R_ds depends on T_j. Iterate the following until it converges:

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + k_T (T_j - 25)\right],\quad k_T \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

If the loop diverges, so that each increase in T_j raises P faster than the thermal path can remove it, you have **thermal runaway**. That condition sets the hard continuous limit.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The three components are modeled below.

### 3.1 V–I overlap loss in the high-side FET

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\times25.2\times80\times30\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}
$$

### 3.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period)

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

### 3.3 Reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = V_{bus} Q_{rr} f_{sw} = 25.2\times50\text{nC}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}
$$

This term also adds a turn-on current spike. Budget for snubber or Coss loss as well:

$$
P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 C_{oss,eff} V^2 f \cdot 2 \approx 0.05\ \text{W}
$$

### 3.4 Gate drive loss (dissipated mainly in the driver, not the FET)

$$
P_g = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw}\times 2_{\text{(HS+LS)}} = 60\text{nC}\times10\times48\text{k}\times2 = 0.058\ \text{W}
$$

### 3.5 Per-motor and total power budget

| Component | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.20 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching overlap | 1.45 W | 5.8 W |
| Dead time | 0.61 W | 2.4 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.11 W | 0.4 W |
| Gate drive | 0.06 W | 0.2 W |
| **Total** | **21.4 W** | **≈ 85.7 W** |

**Insights:**

- **Conduction dominates at about 90%.** Reducing R_ds(on) is worth far more than lowering f_sw.
- Dropping to 24 kHz saves only about 1 W per motor and costs acoustic noise and current ripple.
- Paralleling two FETs per switch halves conduction loss, to 9.6 W per motor, but the extra parts will not fit a nano footprint.

### 3.6 Efficiency at burst

Using P_in ≈ V·I·D at D ≈ 1:

$$
P_{in} = 4\times25.2\times80 = 8064\ \text{W}
$$

$$
\eta \approx 1 - \frac{85.7}{8064} = 98.9\%
$$

Electrical efficiency is excellent. The board will fail on thermal limits long before efficiency becomes a concern.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Network

The thermal path is modeled as a Foster/Cauer chain:

```
P_FET → [Z_th,jc(t)] → T_case → [R_th,c-pcb] → T_pcb(shared node, C_pcb)
                                              → [R_th,pcb-air (airflow-dependent)] → T_amb
```

| Parameter | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| R_th,jc | 1.5 K/W | PDFN3.3 exposed pad |
| R_th,c-pcb | 3 K/W | Via array (≥ 9 × 0.3 mm vias), 2 oz copper |
| C_pcb (ESC section) | ≈ 6 J/K | ≈ 6 g of FR4 + copper + FETs at an effective c ≈ 1.0 J/g·K |
| R_th,pcb-air | 8 K/W still air, ≈ 2.5 K/W in prop wash | Board-level estimate |
| T_amb | 40 °C | Inside frame, summer |
| T_j,max | 150 °C, design to 125 °C | |

### 4.2 Steady-state check (proves continuous 80 A is impossible)

Even in prop wash:

$$
\Delta T_{pcb,ss} = 85.7\times2.5 = 214\ \text{K}
$$

**Conclusion: continuous 4×80 A is not achievable.** Solve for the continuous current per motor I_cont with T_pcb ≤ 95 °C (55 K rise allowed) and R = 2.5 K/W:

$$
P_{allow} = 55/2.5 = 22\ \text{W total}
$$

$$
\Rightarrow\ 4(2 I^2 R_{hot}) + P_{sw}(I) \approx 22
$$

$$
\Rightarrow\ I_{cont} \approx \mathbf{37\text{–}40\ A\ per\ motor}
$$

### 4.3 Transient burst duration

For t ≪ τ, where τ = R·C ≈ 15 s in airflow, the board heats roughly linearly:

$$
\frac{dT_{pcb}}{dt} \approx \frac{P}{C_{pcb}} = \frac{85.7}{6} \approx 14\ \text{K/s}
$$

The junction sits above the board node by:

$$
\Delta T_{j-pcb} = P_{FET}(R_{jc}+R_{c\text{-}pcb}) = 3.57\ \text{W}\times4.5 = 16\ \text{K}
$$

Here P_FET = 21.4 W / 6. This offset is established in about 10–50 ms.

The full first-order solution is:

$$
T_j(t) = T_0 + P R_{pa}\left(1-e^{-t/\tau}\right) + \Delta T_{j-pcb},\quad \tau = R_{pa}C_{pcb}
$$

The allowable burst time follows from the junction limit:

$$
t_{burst} = -\tau\,\ln\!\left(1 - \frac{T_{j,lim} - T_0 - \Delta T_{j-pcb}}{P R_{pa}}\right)
$$
