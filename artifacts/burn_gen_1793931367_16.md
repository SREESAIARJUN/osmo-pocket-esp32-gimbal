# STM32H743 + 4-in-1 ESC Nano Stack: 80 A Burst Thermal Model, 48 kHz Switching Losses, and ArduPilot Bidirectional DShot600 DMA Plan

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Corrections

These clarifications change the design.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each motor (or the whole board) is driven by a dedicated ESC MCU, such as an AT32F421, STM32G071 or EFM8BB51, running AM32 or BLHeli_32/BlueJay. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to that MCU. The 48 kHz PWM and the MOSFET losses belong to the ESC MCU's domain. The DMA plan belongs to the H743.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is the critical risk.** The math in §2–§3 shows that 4 × 80 A simultaneously is not thermally survivable on a 20×20 board for more than about 1–2 s. I give the model parametrically, so you can substitute your real MOSFET datasheet values.
3. **Every numeric device parameter below is a representative assumption**, not a vendor-certified value. Replace each one with your selected part's datasheet value before sign-off.

## 1. Electrical Model per Motor

In 6-step drive, at any instant one high-side (HS) and one low-side (LS) device carry the phase current I.

With complementary PWM:
- The "switching" leg alternates HS ↔ LS-sync (duty D / 1−D).
- The "return" leg's LS is held fully on.

Every 60° electrical, the roles rotate through all 6 FETs. Each FET carries current for 1/3 of the electrical period.

## 2. Conduction Losses

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 2.5 mΩ × 1.55 = **3.875 mΩ**.

### 2.2 Per-motor conduction loss

Two devices are always in the current path. Within the switching leg, the D and (1−D) intervals sum to 1, so loss is independent of duty:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per motor | P_cond, 4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 2.5 mΩ | 32.0 W | 128 W |
| 100 °C | 3.53 mΩ | 45.2 W | 181 W |
| 125 °C | 3.875 mΩ | **49.6 W** | **198 W** |

### 2.3 Average per-FET loss

Each FET conducts 1/3 of the time. At burst eRPM (≥ 20 k eRPM → electrical period ≤ 3 ms), the die thermal time constant smooths this, so:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} = \frac{I^2 R_{ds}}{3} = \frac{6400 \times 3.875\,\text{m}\Omega}{3} \approx 8.3\text{ W}$$

### 2.4 Copper and trace loss (often forgotten)

Assume a 4 mm wide, 2 oz, 20 mm phase-plus-bus path, with ~0.5 mΩ effective per current path:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 \cdot 2 \cdot 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 6.4\text{ W per motor}$$

Adding 6–8 inner layers of 2 oz copper is mandatory, not optional.

## 3. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the switching leg contributes, with one hard-switched transition pair per PWM period.

### 3.1 Transition times (Miller plateau ≈ 4.5 V)

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{source}} = \frac{8\,\text{nC}}{\sim0.7\,\text{A (effective at plateau)}} \approx 11\text{ ns}$$

$$t_f \approx \frac{8\,\text{nC}}{\sim1.0\,\text{A}} \approx 8\text{ ns}$$

### 3.2 V–I overlap loss

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 19\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.92\ W}$$
