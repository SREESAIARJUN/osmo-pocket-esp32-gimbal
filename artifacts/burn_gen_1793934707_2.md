# 3-Axis Gimbal: FOC Motor Control and Dual-IMU Sensor Fusion

I don't have the spec for "Job #5", so the design below uses the assumptions in §0. Every parameter is in one config block, so you can substitute your real values without changing the structure.

## 0. Assumed System Baseline

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Motors | 3× gimbal BLDC, 12N14P (pole pairs p = 7), R ≈ 5 Ω, L ≈ 2 mH, Kt ≈ 0.05 N·m/A |
| Encoders | 14-bit absolute magnetic (AS5048A / MT6701) on each axis, SPI |
| MCU | STM32G474 / H7 (FPU, ≥170 MHz), center-aligned PWM, 3-shunt or 2-shunt current sense |
| IMU-A, IMU-B | 2× ICM-42688-P **both on the camera payload**, mounted ≥30 mm apart and rotated 180° about Z, on separate SPI buses |
| Gimbal order | Yaw (base) → Roll → Pitch (camera) |
| Loop rates | Current/FOC **20 kHz** · Velocity **4 kHz** · Angle/attitude **1 kHz** · IMU ODR **8 kHz** |

Mounting the two IMUs 180° apart gives two benefits:
- Their sensor-specific errors (bias, cross-axis, local PCB resonance) are decorrelated.
- Rigid-body motion stays perfectly correlated, which is what makes the consistency check in §5.4 possible.

## 1. FOC Current Loop (20 kHz)

### 1.1 Transforms

Clarke transform (amplitude-invariant):

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \frac{i_a + 2 i_b}{\sqrt3}
$$

Park transform, with electrical angle θₑ = p·(θₘ − θ_offset):

$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix}=
\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}
$$

### 1.2 Plant (PMSM in the dq frame)

$$
v_d = R i_d + L\frac{di_d}{dt} - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad
v_q = R i_q + L\frac{di_q}{dt} + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e\lambda_m
$$

### 1.3 PI with Pole-Zero Cancellation and Decoupling

Choose the current bandwidth ω_c ≈ 2π·2 kHz (that is, ≤ f_PWM/10):

$$
K_{p,i}=L\,\omega_c,\qquad K_{i,i}=R\,\omega_c
\;\;\Rightarrow\;\; \frac{i}{i^*}=\frac{\omega_c}{s+\omega_c}
$$

The voltage command is the PI output plus decoupling and back-EMF feed-forward terms:

$$
v_d^* = \text{PI}_d(i_d^*-i_d) - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad
v_q^* = \text{PI}_q(i_q^*-i_q) + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e\lambda_m
$$

The current setpoints are:
- i_d* = 0 (surface PMSM).
- i_q* = i_q,vel + i_q,cog (velocity-loop output plus cogging feed-forward).

### 1.4 Voltage Limit and Anti-Windup

The dq voltage vector is circularly limited:

$$
|v_{dq}| \le V_{max}=\frac{V_{bus}}{\sqrt3}\cdot m_{max}\;(m_{max}\approx0.95)
$$

Integrators use back-calculation anti-windup:

$$
x_I[k+1]=x_I[k]+K_i T_s\,e[k] + K_{aw}\,(v_{sat}-v_{unsat})
$$

### 1.5 Inverse Park and SVPWM (Min/Max Zero-Sequence Injection)

$$
v_a = v_\alpha,\quad v_b = -\tfrac12 v_\alpha+\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}v_\beta,\quad v_c=-\tfrac12 v_\alpha-\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}v_\beta
$$

$$
v_0 = -\tfrac12(\max(v_{abc})+\min(v_{abc})),\qquad
d_x = \tfrac12 + \frac{v_x+v_0}{V_{bus}}
$$

### 1.6 Angle-Delay Compensation

The PWM update happens 1.5 sample periods after current sampling, so the Park inverse uses an advanced angle:

$$
\theta_e' = \theta_e + 1.5\,\omega_e T_s
$$
