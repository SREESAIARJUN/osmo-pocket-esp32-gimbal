### Architectural note: the H743 does not commutate the motors directly

The H743 has only two advanced timers with hardware dead-time and complementary outputs (TIM1 and TIM8). That supports at most two 3-phase bridges, not four.

Every production "H743 4-in-1" design therefore uses this split:

- The **H743 acts as the flight controller**. It sends DShot to the ESCs.
- **Four dedicated ESC MCUs** (AT32F421 or STM32G071 class) handle commutation, back-EMF sensing, and eRPM telemetry.

Everything below follows that topology.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \mathrm{K^{-1}}$$

This value of α matches the typical normalized curve, where R_DS ≈ 1.5× at 125 °C.

### 1.2 Current path in six-step commutation with synchronous rectification

In any 60° sector, exactly two phases carry the motor current I:

- **Active phase (PWM leg):** the high-side FET conducts for duty D, and the low-side FET conducts for (1 − D) as the synchronous freewheel path.
- **Return phase:** the low-side FET is held on for the full sector.

At every instant, therefore, the current crosses **exactly two FET channels**:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) \quad \text{(independent of D)}$$

Per-FET average over an electrical revolution (each leg is active for 120° of every 360°):

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}D\,I^2R_{DS}, \qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)\,I^2R_{DS} + \tfrac{1}{3}I^2R_{DS}$$

**Low-side FETs run hotter.** At partial throttle they carry both freewheel and return current. Place them closest to the thermal pad and via field.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

Assume a starting T_j = 110 °C, then iterate (see §3.4):

- R_DS(110 °C) = 1.0 × (1 + 0.005 × 85) = **1.425 mΩ**
- P_cond,motor = 2 × 80² × 1.425 × 10⁻³ = **18.24 W**
- Worst single FET (low side at D = 0.5): (1/3)(0.5)(9.12) + (1/3)(9.12) = **4.56 W average**, with **9.12 W instantaneous** while conducting

### 1.4 Parasitic copper

Add trace, plane, and solder resistance. Use about 0.3 mΩ per path on an 8-layer 2 oz board with stacked power planes:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{path} = 6400 \times 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 1.9\ \text{W per motor}$$

This heat goes directly into the board, not the FETs.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

With complementary PWM, only the **high-side FET hard-switches**. The low-side FET turns on after its body diode is already conducting, so its turn-on is a ZVS (zero-voltage switching) transition.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (high side)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

With an effective driver-limited t_r + t_f ≈ 20 ns [P]:

$$P_{VI} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 30\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.018\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Body-diode reverse recovery (energy lands in the high-side FET)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

Q_rr grows with di/dt and T_j. At 80 A, derate by ×1.5–2. Use **0.08 W** for design.

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction (low side, two edges per period)

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\,I\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 2(100\text{ns}) \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

Dead time is a major lever. Going from 100 ns to 50 ns (AM32 "dead time" setting) halves this term, but only if the driver propagation-delay mismatch allows it without shoot-through.

### 2.5 Gate drive

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 50\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.024\ \text{W per FET}$$

Most of this dissipates in the driver and gate resistor, not the FET. For six FETs per motor, that is 0.14 W per motor, which loads the 10–12 V gate-drive rail.
