# STM32H743 Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: 80 A Burst Design Model

## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

**Architecture correction.** An STM32H743 cannot directly commutate four BLDC motors under ArduPilot. It runs the flight stack and generates DShot. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32. The design below follows that split:

```
[STM32H743 FC] --4x bidir DShot600--> [4-in-1 ESC: 4x (MCU + 3ph driver + 6 MOSFETs)]
```

**Assumptions.** Replace the bracketed values with datasheet values for your chosen parts before sign-off.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | V_bus | 6S, 25.2 V max (worst case), 22.2 V nominal |
| Burst phase current | I_ph | 80 A per channel, 6-step trapezoidal, synchronous (complementary) PWM |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| MOSFET | — | 40 V, 5×6 mm. R_ds(on),25 = [0.80 mΩ max @ 10 V], Q_g = [50 nC], Q_rr = [40 nC], Q_oss = [50 nC] |
| R_ds(on) temperature coefficient | — | R(T) = R₂₅·(1 + 0.0045·(T − 25)), giving ≈1.56× at 150 °C |
| Switching times | t_r + t_f | 15 + 15 ns, with ~1.5 A driver and R_g ≈ 2–5 Ω |
| Dead time | t_d | 200 ns per edge. Body diode V_f = 0.8 V |
| Board | — | 30.5×30.5 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz copper |

A true 20×20 "nano" board cannot sustain this. Section 3 shows why 80 A is only a seconds-scale rating.

## 1. Conduction Losses

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step synchronous PWM

During any commutation sector, current flows through exactly two conducting channels: the high side of one phase and the low side of another. The PWM'd leg alternates HS (duty D) and LS (1 − D, synchronous rectification), so the path always contains two R_ds(on).

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Electro-thermal iteration

R_ds(on) depends on T_j, so solve iteratively.

| Iteration | Assumed T_j | R_ds | P_cond,ch |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 25 °C | 0.80 mΩ | 10.2 W |
| 1 | 100 °C | 1.07 mΩ | 13.7 W |
| 2 | 115 °C | 1.12 mΩ | **14.3 W** |

### 1.3 Copper and connection losses

These are not negligible at 80 A.

- Phase pad plus 2 oz trace, approximately 0.25 mΩ → 80²·0.25 m = **1.6 W**
- Battery-side current I_bat = D·I_ph is shared and treated separately.

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

This identifies the hottest device. Each FET carries current one-third of the electrical cycle. Over a full cycle:

- Non-PWM (static) FET: P = I²R/3 = 6400·1.12m/3 = **2.39 W**
- PWM'd HS + LS pair: shares 2.39 W by D : (1 − D), plus all switching loss (Section 2).
- **Worst single FET:** ≈ 2.39 + ⅓·P_sw ≈ **3.3 W average**, with up to 7.2 W instantaneous within a sector.
