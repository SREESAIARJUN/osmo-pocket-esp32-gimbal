# STM32H743 Flight Controller + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC (Job #42): Engineering Model

## 0. Assumptions and Architectural Clarification

I don't have the Job #42 requirements package, so the numbers below rest on stated assumptions. Substitute your actual BOM values, and re-run the equations before any of this goes to layout review.

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 does not drive the power stage directly. In this class of product:

- The **H743 runs ArduPilot**. It emits bi-directional DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry.
- **Four dedicated ESC MCUs** run the 48 kHz PWM and commutation. Typical parts are the AT32F421 or STM32G071, running AM32 or BLHeli_32.

So "48 kHz switching" is an ESC-firmware parameter, and "DShot DMA" is an FC-side resource problem. I treat them separately.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, at any instant the motor current flows through exactly **two FET positions in series**:

- **High-side position.** The HS FET conducts for duty D, and the complementary LS FET of the same leg conducts for (1−D).
- **Low-side position.** The LS FET of the return leg is held on.

Per motor channel, therefore:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \left[ D\,R_{HS}(T_j) + (1-D)\,R_{LS,sync}(T_j) + R_{LS,ret}(T_j) \right]$$

With matched FETs, this collapses to an expression that is independent of D:

$$\boxed{P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)}, \qquad R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right]$$

### 1.2 Thermal feedback (positive temperature coefficient)

Loss raises temperature, which raises R_DS(on), which raises loss. The steady-state fixed point is:

$$T_j = T_a + R_{\theta}\cdot 2I^2R_{25}\left[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\right]$$

$$\boxed{T_j = \frac{T_a + 2I^2R_{25}R_\theta(1-25\alpha)}{1 - 2I^2R_{25}R_\theta\,\alpha}}$$

**Thermal runaway** occurs when the denominator goes to zero:

$$R_\theta \geq \frac{1}{2I^2R_{25}\alpha}$$

At 80 A this gives R_θ,crit = 1/(2·6400·0.001·0.0045) = **17.4 K/W per channel pair**. Steady-state 80 A therefore needs a junction-to-ambient path well below this value. A nano form factor cannot provide that, which is why the 80 A rating must be a **burst rating** (see §4).

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond per channel |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W |
| 100 °C | 1.34 mΩ | 17.1 W |
| 125 °C | 1.45 mΩ | 18.6 W |

### 1.4 PCB copper loss (frequently ignored, and not small)

Sheet resistance of copper:

$$R_\square = \frac{\rho_{Cu}}{t}$$

With ρ_Cu = 1.72×10⁻⁸ Ω·m and 2 oz (70 µm) copper, R_□ = 0.246 mΩ/□ at 25 °C. The temperature coefficient is 0.0039 /K.

Assume a phase path of about 4 squares per layer, with 4 paralleled layers (2 oz outer, 1 oz inner treated as equivalent):

- R_trace ≈ 0.4 mΩ round trip
- **P_Cu ≈ 6400 × 0.4 mΩ ≈ 2.6 W per channel**

Bus-plane loss is extra, because the battery bus carries the sum of all four motor currents:

$$P_{bus} = (\sum I_{batt})^2 R_{bus}$$

Copper loss in a 20×20 or 25×25 mm board is comparable to FET loss. **Copper geometry is a first-order design variable here, not a detail.**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switching leg incurs hard-switching loss. The other active FET is held statically on.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard switching)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2}V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot40\text{ ns}\cdot48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f\,I\cdot 2t_{dt}\cdot f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot400\text{ ns}\cdot48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

This term argues for minimizing dead time (100 ns would halve it) or using a Schottky-integrated FET.

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 50\text{ nC}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

Reverse recovery also adds turn-on current overshoot. Model this as an increase in t_r, typically +5 to 10 ns.

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2}C_{oss}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw}\cdot 2 = 1.5\text{ nF}\cdot635\cdot48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (the driver dissipates this, not the FET)

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw}\cdot N_{switching} = 60\text{ nC}\cdot10\cdot48\text{ kHz}\cdot2 = \mathbf{0.058\ W/channel}$$

This is about 0.23 W total across four channels, and it lands on the driver and the bootstrap supply.

### 2.6 Frequency sensitivity

$$\frac{\partial P_{total}}{\partial f_{sw}} = \left[\tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) + 2V_fIt_{dt} + Q_{rr}V + C_{oss}V^2 + 2Q_gV_{GS}\right] \approx 69\ \mu\text{J per cycle}$$

Each additional 24 kHz therefore costs about **1.65 W per channel** at 80 A. Running 24 kHz instead of 48 kHz recovers about 1.6 W per channel, at the cost of current ripple and audible noise.

## 3. Total Loss Budget at 80 A Burst (T_j = 125 °C)

| Loss component | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 18.6 W | 74.4 W |
| PCB copper (phase) | 2.6 W | 10.4 W |
| Switching (V·I overlap) | 1.94 W | 7.8 W |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 W | 4.9 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.11 W | 0.4 W |
| Gate drive | 0.06 W | 0.2 W |
| **Total** | **≈24.5 W** | **≈98 W** |

**Efficiency check.** Output power is roughly 22.2 V × 80 A × 4 ≈ 7.1 kW electrical, assuming high duty. Losses are about 1.4%, which is plausible for the bridge alone.
