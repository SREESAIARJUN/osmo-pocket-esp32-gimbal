## 0. Architectural Clarification (Read First)

An STM32H743 does not commutate motors directly in this design. It is the **flight controller**. The "4-in-1 ESC" is four independent ESC MCUs, each running AM32 (or BLHeli_32-class firmware).

| Block | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| FC MCU | STM32H743VIT6 (480 MHz rev V) | ArduPilot, sends DShot600 bidir to ESCs |
| ESC MCU ×4 | AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071 [A] | AM32, 48 kHz complementary PWM, BEMF commutation, eRPM telemetry |
| Gate driver ×4 | FD6288Q (3-phase, 1.5 A src / 1.8 A sink) [A] | Bootstrapped high-side, ~10 V gate drive |
| MOSFET ×24 | 40 V N-ch, PDFN 5×6 [A] | 6 per motor |
| Bus | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V max / 22.2 V nominal | |

**Design-point interpretation:**
- "80 A burst" means **80 A per motor, ≤ 5 s**.
- The model below shows this is thermally achievable only as a short burst on a nano form factor. Continuous capability is roughly 30 A.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Commutation topology

AM32 uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM on the active high/low pair. At any instant, current I flows through:

- **High-side FET of phase A:** conducting for fraction D.
- **Low-side FET of phase A (sync rectifier):** conducting for fraction 1 − D, minus dead time.
- **Low-side FET of phase B:** conducting 100%.

So the conducting path always contains exactly **2 R_DS(on)** in series, independent of D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot R_{DS}(T_j)\cdot\left[D + (1-D-2t_{dt}f_{sw}) + 1\right] \approx 2\,I_{rms}^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 2.2 Ripple-corrected RMS current

Motor phase inductance L_ph ≈ 15 µH [A] at D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus} D (1-D)}{L_{ph} f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{15\mu \times 48k} = 8.75\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{rms} = \sqrt{I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}} = \sqrt{6400 + 6.38} = 80.04\ \text{A}$$

The ripple contribution is negligible at this current, so I_rms ≈ 80 A.

### 2.3 Temperature-coupled R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right]$$
