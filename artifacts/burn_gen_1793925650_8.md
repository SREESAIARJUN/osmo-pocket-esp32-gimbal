## 0. Architecture Assumptions

There is one architectural point to settle before the math.

ArduPilot runs on the STM32H743 as the **flight controller**. It does not commutate motors. The 80 A power stage needs **four dedicated ESC MCUs**, or one quad-capable ESC MCU, running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. The H743 talks to them over bi-directional DShot600.

The rest of this package assumes the following:

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{DS} = 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.45 = 2.18\,\text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Conduction intervals in 6-step synchronous mode

Each phase leg is active for 2 of the 6 sectors (120° each, so 1/3 of the electrical period per active state).

- **High-side PWM state (1/3 of period):**
  - HS FET conducts for duty D.
  - The complementary LS FET of the same leg conducts for (1 − D).
- **Low-side return state (1/3 of period):**
  - LS FET conducts at 100 %.

Per-FET RMS-equivalent conduction loss:

$$P_{HS} = \frac{D}{3} I^2 R_{DS}, \qquad P_{LS} = \frac{(1-D)+1}{3} I^2 R_{DS}$$

Per leg:

$$P_{leg} = \frac{2}{3} I^2 R_{DS}$$

This result does not depend on D.

Per motor (3 legs):

$$\boxed{P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)}$$

This matches the physical picture: exactly two channels conduct at every instant.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.50 mΩ | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| 100 °C | 2.01 mΩ | 25.7 W | 102.9 W |
| 125 °C | 2.18 mΩ | **27.9 W** | **111.6 W** |

Per FET at D ≈ 1 (full-throttle burst): HS ≈ LS ≈ 6400 × 2.18 m / 3 ≈ **4.65 W**.

### 1.4 Copper and interconnect loss (often larger than designers expect)

Assume 25 mm of 2 oz (70 µm) trace or plane, 6 mm wide, per current path, with 2 paths in series:

$$R_{Cu} = \rho \frac{L}{wt} = 1.72\times10^{-8} \cdot \frac{0.025}{0.006 \times 70\times10^{-6}} \approx 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega$$

This rises to about 1.3 mΩ at 100 °C. Across two paths, about 2.6 mΩ in total.

$$P_{Cu,motor} \approx 6400 \times 1.3\text{m} \times 2 \approx 16.6\,\text{W}$$

**Mitigation: this is mandatory.** Use phase current paths on 3–4 paralleled inner layers, with motor pads soldered directly to the plane. Do not route phase current through vias where avoidable. The target is R_Cu ≤ 0.4 mΩ per path, which gives about 5 W per motor.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd high-side FET hard-switches. The LS FET in the same leg switches at near-zero V_DS (soft) after its dead-time body-diode interval.

### 2.1 V–I overlap

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

Reverse recovery also adds roughly Q_rr/I-scale turn-on current overlap. Budget about 0.1 W.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.030\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction (2 edges per period)

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2 t_{dead}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors)

$$
