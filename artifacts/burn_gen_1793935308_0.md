# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Architecture Package (Job #37)

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and a Feasibility Warning

**Architecture interpretation.** I am treating this as an **AIO stack**: the STM32H743 is the flight controller running ArduPilot, and it drives four ESC channels. Each channel has its own ESC MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32) that commutates the three-phase bridge.

I am not assuming the H743 commutates the bridges directly. ArduPilot has no BLDC commutation firmware, and bi-directional DShot only makes sense with a separate ESC MCU.

**Assumed operating point.** Change these and re-run the equations.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S, full charge) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Burst phase current per channel | I_b | 80 A |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| MOSFET class (40 V, PDFN 3.3×3.3 or 5×6) | R_ds(on),25 | 0.9 mΩ |
| R_ds(on) temperature multiplier at T_j = 125 °C | k_T | 1.5 → 1.35 mΩ |
| Turn-on / turn-off time | t_r, t_f | 15 ns / 15 ns |
| Reverse recovery charge | Q_rr | 50 nC |
| Output capacitance | C_oss | 1.5 nF |
| Gate charge at 10 V | Q_g | 50 nC |
| Dead time (per edge) | t_dt | 100 ns |
| Body-diode forward voltage | V_f | 0.8 V |
| Board | 30.5 × 30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz on every layer | |

> **Warning:** 80A per channel on all four channels at once is a **burst-only** rating on any nano/30.5 mm board. Section 2.3 shows the honest continuous rating is about 38 A per channel with all four loaded. Any marketing number above that must state the duty cycle and duration.

## 1. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 1.1 Conduction Losses

With complementary (synchronous) 6-step PWM, the phase current always flows through exactly **two** conducting MOSFET channels. One is the PWM leg, either high-side or low-side by complement. The other is the static low-side of the return phase.

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_b^2\, R_{ds(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

Using α ≈ 0.005 /K (giving k_T = 1.5 at 125 °C):

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.35\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{17.28\ W}$$

Each conducting FET dissipates **8.64 W**.

**PCB copper.** Assume a phase-path resistance of about 0.3 mΩ (bus plane, vias, motor pad, return):

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{path} = 6400 \times 0.3\text{m} \approx \mathbf{1.9\ W}$$

### 1.2 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

**Hard-switching V·I overlap:**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_b (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery of the complementary body diode:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

**C_oss charge/discharge (two devices per switching leg):**

$$P_{oss} = 2 \times \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1.5\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = 0.046\ \text{W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period):**

$$P_{dt} = V_f I_b (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**Gate drive (dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET junction).** Six FETs, two switching actively in 6-step:

$$P_g = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} \times 2 = 50\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

**Switching subtotal:** P_sw,tot ≈ 1.45 + 0.06 + 0.05 + 0.61 ≈ **2.17 W**

### 1.3 Channel and Board Totals at 80 A Burst

| Loss term | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 17.28 W | 69.1 W |
| Switching (all terms) | 2.17 W | 8.7 W |
| PCB copper | 1.9 W | 7.6 W |
| **Total** | **≈21.4 W** | **≈85 W** |

**Efficiency check.** Output power at roughly 50% duty is about 1 kW per channel, so efficiency is about 98%. The issue is not efficiency. The issue is heat density: 85 W into about 9.3 cm² of board.

**Design levers at 48 kHz.** Switching loss is only about 10% of the total. Conduction dominates, so the effective lever is R_ds(on) (paralleling FETs, larger packages), not edge rate. Dropping to 24 kHz saves only about 1 W per channel.

## 2. Thermal Model

### 2.1 Junction Transient (FET level)

The thermal path is T_j = T_board + P_FET · Z_th,jc(t_p) + P_FET · R_th,cs.

For a PDFN 5×6 40 V device, Z_th,jc is about 0.3 K/W at 10 ms and about 0.8 K/W at steady state. R_th through solder and thermal vias is about 2 K/W per FET into the inner planes.

At 8.64 W + 0.7 W (switching share) ≈ 9.3 W per hot FET:

$$\Delta T_{j\text{-board}} \approx 9.3 \times (0.8 + 2.0) \approx 26\ \text{K}$$

The FET-level gradient is manageable. **The board is the bottleneck.**

### 2.2 Board Thermal Capacitance (lumped)

| Element | Calculation | C (J/K) |
|---|---|---|
| Copper, 8 × 70 µm | 0.0305² × 560 µm × 8960 kg/m³ = 4.7 g × 0.385 J/g·K | 1.8 |
| FR4 core/prepreg, 1.6 mm | 1.49 cm³ × 1.85 g/cm³ = 2.75 g × 1.1 J/g·K | 3.0 |
| 24 FETs + capacitors (estimate) | | 0.7 |
| **Total C_th** | | **≈5.5 J/K** |

**First-order model.** With R_th,ba as the board-to-ambient resistance in prop wash (about 3 K/W at 10 m/s airflow with a bare board):

$$\frac{dT}{dt} = \frac{P - (T - T_a)/R_{th,ba}}{C_{th}}, \qquad \tau = R_{th,ba}\,C_{th} \approx 16.5\ \text{s}$$

**Burst budget.** Start at T_a = 40 °C and hold all four channels at 80 A (85 W). The initial slope is 85 / 5.5 ≈ **15.5 K/s**.
