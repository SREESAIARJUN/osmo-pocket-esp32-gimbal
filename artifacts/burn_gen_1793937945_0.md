## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

**The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot has no field-oriented or 6-step commutation stack for the flight controller MCU. The correct partition is:

| Block | Device (reference choice) | Role |
|---|---|---|
| FC MCU | STM32H743VIH6 (480 MHz, TFBGA100) or VIT6 (LQFP100) | ArduPilot; emits 4× bidirectional DShot600 |
| ESC MCUs (×4) | AT32F421K8U7 (QFN32), AM32 firmware | 6-step commutation, 48 kHz PWM, eRPM telemetry |
| Gate drivers (×4) | FD6288Q (3-phase, 250 V, bootstrap) | Half-bridge drive |
| Power stage (×4) | 6× 40 V N-MOSFET, PQFN 5×6 | Bridge |
| Current sense | 1 shared shunt + amplifier, routed to H743 ADC | Battery current |

**Electrical and thermal parameters used throughout (replace with your BOM datasheet values):**

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 25.2 V | 6S, fully charged (worst-case switching) |
| I_burst | 80 A | Per-channel phase current, burst |

> **Board size.** A true 20×20 "nano" board cannot physically host 24 PQFN 5×6 FETs. The thermal model below therefore uses the 30.5 mm footprint. On 20×20, the thermal capacitance and convective area scale down by about 2.3×, so every burst time computed here shrinks by roughly that factor.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

At any commutation step, one leg PWMs (high side) and another leg holds its low side ON.

- **During t_on (duty D):** current flows through HS_A, the motor, then LS_B. That is two FETs in series.
- **During t_off (1 − D):** current freewheels through LS_A (synchronous) and LS_B. That is again two FETs in series.

The motor current therefore always passes through exactly two R_ds(on) elements, independent of duty:

$$
P_{cond} = 2\, I^2 \, R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1+\alpha (T_j - 25)\right]
$$

### 1.2 Electro-thermal fixed point

Junction temperature depends on R_ds, which in turn depends on junction temperature:

$$
T_j = T_{ref} + R_\theta \cdot I^2 R_{ds,25}\left[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\right]
$$

Solving gives the closed form:

$$
T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + R_\theta I^2 R_{ds,25}(1-25\alpha)}{1 - \alpha R_\theta I^2 R_{ds,25}}
$$

**Thermal runaway** occurs when the denominator reaches zero:

$$
\alpha R_\theta I^2 R_{ds,25} \ge 1
$$

At 80 A, I² R_ds,25 = 6.4 W per FET, so runaway requires R_θ ≥ 26 K/W. This is safe at the junction level, but the board-level R_θ derived in §4 exceeds 26 K/W per FET-equivalent. **Continuous 80 A is electro-thermally unstable on this form factor; 80 A is a burst-only rating.**

### 1.3 Numbers at T_j = 108 °C (R_ds = 1.5 mΩ)

$$
P_{FET} = 80^2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 9.6\ \text{W}, \qquad P_{cond,ch} = 19.2\ \text{W}
$$

**Shunt and copper losses (per channel share):**

| Element | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| Phase/bus copper, ≈0.3 mΩ effective | 6400 × 0.3 mΩ | 1.9 W |
| Shunt (shared, 0.25 mΩ, 320 A total) | 320² × 0.25 mΩ / 4 | 6.4 W |

The shunt figure is the dominant non-FET loss. Use **0.1–0.15 mΩ** or switch to per-channel phase sensing. With a 0.15 mΩ shunt, the share drops to 3.8 W per channel.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWMing leg's high-side switch sees hard-switching overlap; its complementary low side switches near-ZVS after dead time.

**Overlap (V–I crossover):**

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 1.94\ \text{W}
$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period):**

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 0.31\ \text{W}
$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$
P_{rr} = V_{bus} Q_{rr} f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}
$$

The diode's I_rr also adds overlap at turn-on. Fold this in by increasing t_r by Q_rr/I_rr if your scope data shows it.

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = 0.023\ \text{W}
$$
