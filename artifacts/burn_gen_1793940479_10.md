# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC (Nano Form Factor): Reference Engineering Model

**Scope:** I don't have Job #72's requirements documents, BOM, or layout files. Everything below is a reference model built on stated assumptions. Each assumption is tagged **[A#]** so you can replace it with your actual datasheet values and re-run the math.

---

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Feasibility Flags

Three issues need resolving before schematic freeze.

1. **The H743 should not commutate the motors.** In an FC + 4-in-1 stack, the H743 runs ArduPilot and emits DShot. Each motor channel needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, for example 4× AT32F421 or STM32G071. The architecture below assumes this split.
2. **80A on a nano board (20×20 mm mount, ~25×25 mm PCB) is a burst rating measured in seconds.** Section 2 shows the board reaches its thermal limit in roughly 2–5 s at 4×80A. Steady-state 80A per channel is physically impossible at this size without heavy airflow and paralleled FETs. The rating must be defined with a duration, for example "80A / 2 s, 45A continuous with prop-wash."
3. **"48 kHz" is the ESC PWM switching frequency.** It is unrelated to the DShot rate. Both are modeled separately.

## 1. Electrical Loss Model (Per Channel)

### 1.1 Conduction Losses

In 6-step commutation, current always flows through exactly two switch positions in series. One is the PWM leg, where the HS FET conducts for D and the synchronous LS FET conducts for 1−D. The other is the static leg. Therefore:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 100°C:

R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0045 × 75) = **1.34 mΩ**

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.34\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{17.2\ W}$$

Copper path loss:

$$P_{cu,ch} = I^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

Each FET carries current about 1/3 of the electrical cycle. At high eRPM the average loss per FET is therefore:

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{17.2}{6} \approx 2.9\ \text{W}$$

At low eRPM or stall, a single FET can carry full current for a long time. Its instantaneous loss is up to I²·R = 8.6 W. **Desync and stall is the thermal worst case**, not full throttle.

### 1.2 Switching Losses at f_sw = 48 kHz

Gate currents through the Miller region:

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_g} = \frac{10-3}{5} = 1.4\ \text{A}, \qquad I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_g} = \frac{3}{5} = 0.6\ \text{A}$$

Transition times:

$$t_r = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = \frac{12\ \text{nC}}{1.4\ \text{A}} = 8.6\ \text{ns}, \qquad t_f = \frac{12\ \text{nC}}{0.6\ \text{A}} = 20\ \text{ns}$$

| Loss term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V·I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 28.6 ns × 48k | **1.38 W** |
| Body-diode dead-time | 2·V_SD·I·t_dt·f_sw = 2 × 0.8 × 80 × 100 ns × 48k | **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 40n × 25.2 × 48k | **0.05 W** |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss·V·f_sw (approximation of E_oss) | **0.04 W** |
| **Total in FETs** | | **≈ 2.08 W** |
| Gate charge (dissipated in driver and R_g, not the FET) | 2 × Q_g·V_drv·f_sw | 0.04 W |
