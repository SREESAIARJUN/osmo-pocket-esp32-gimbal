# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

## 0. Architectural Corrections

These three points change the analysis, so they come first.

1. **The STM32H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC controller.** ArduPilot runs on the H743. Each of the four motor bridges needs its own ESC MCU running commutation firmware, for example 4× AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The 48 kHz figure is the ESC MCU's bridge PWM. It has no relationship to the DShot600 link, which carries commands from the H743 to the ESC MCUs.
2. **"80 A burst" must be defined.** It normally means 80 A phase current per channel. With all four channels at 80 A and high duty cycle, battery current approaches 320 A, or about 8 kW at 6S. No nano-format board, XT60, or set of pad solder joints survives that. Below, the burst is modelled honestly and the result shows it is only a few seconds long.
3. **All component values are representative assumptions.** Replace them with your selected parts' datasheet values before signoff. Every equation is written so that substitution is direct.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Derivation for trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification

During each 60° commutation sector, current flows through one high-side leg (PWM'd) and one low-side leg (held on).

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for D·T. The complementary low-side FET (synchronous rectification) conducts for (1−D)·T. That leg therefore always has exactly one R_ds(on) in the path.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET conducts continuously.

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2 \cdot R_{ds(on),hot}, \qquad R_{ds(on),hot} = k_T \cdot R_{ds(on),25}
$$

This result is independent of D, which is why sync rectification makes the model clean.

Ripple correction:

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I \approx \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L_{ph} f_{sw}}
$$

For a typical 5–10 kV motor (L_ph ≈ 10–20 µH line-to-line), ΔI is 5–10 A at D = 0.5. The ΔI²/12 term is then under 10 A², which is negligible against 6400 A².

### 2.2 Numbers at 80 A

$$
R_{ds(on),hot} = 1.6 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 2.4\,\text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 2.4\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{30.7\,W}
$$

### 2.3 PCB copper loss

Assume the phase and bus path is about 15 mm on 4 paralleled 2 oz layers, 4 mm wide:

$$
R_{Cu} = \rho \frac{\ell}{w\,t\,n} = 1.72\times10^{-8}\frac{0.015}{0.004 \times 70\times10^{-6} \times 4} \approx 0.23\,\text{m}\Omega \;\;(\times 1.3 \text{ hot} \approx 0.30\,\text{m}\Omega)
$$

$$
P_{Cu,ch} \approx 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.30\,\text{m}\Omega \approx \mathbf{3.8\,W}
$$

The current shunt adds I²R_sh. A 0.25 mΩ shunt carrying the D-weighted bus current dissipates about 0.5–1.6 W.
