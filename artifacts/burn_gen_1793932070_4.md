# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC: Loss, Thermal, and Bi-Directional DShot Design Model

## 0. Assumptions and Corrections

I don't have the Job #72 requirements, BOM, or stack-up, so this model is parameterized. Every number marked **[A]** is an assumption to replace with your datasheet values. The equations are general, and the worked numbers show the method and the order of magnitude.

Two corrections to the brief:

1. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In a FC + 4-in-1 stack, the H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bi-directional DShot600. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU (typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32) that generates the 48 kHz PWM. The model below keeps those two domains separate.
2. **"80A burst" on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board is thermally marginal.** Section 3 shows that 4×80A simultaneously is survivable only for roughly 1–3 s. Define the spec as *per motor, non-simultaneous, ≤ N seconds* or it will fail qualification.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

**Topology.** Each motor uses a 3-phase bridge with 6 FETs, 24 FETs total, and synchronous rectification under 6-step trapezoidal commutation. In any 60° sector:

- **ON state (duty D):** high-side of phase X plus low-side of phase Y carry I.
- **Freewheel (1 − D):** low-side X (sync-rect) plus low-side Y carry I.

So the current always flows through exactly **two** channels.

The ripple-corrected RMS current is:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}D(1-D)}{L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

For a 10 µH phase-pair inductance [A] at D = 0.5: ΔI = 25.2·0.25/(10e-6·48e3) = **13.1 A**, so I_rms² = 6400 + 14.3 ≈ **6414 A²** (ripple adds only 0.2%).

The per-motor conduction loss is:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j) + I_{rms}^2 R_{trace}$$

$$P_{cond} = 2(6414)(1.8\,\text{m}\Omega) + 6414(0.5\,\text{m}\Omega) = 23.09 + 3.21 = \mathbf{26.3\ W}$$

**Per-FET distribution.** The worst-case FET is a low-side device (conducts in both the ON and freewheel states):

$$P_{LS,avg} = I^2 R_{ds}\cdot\tfrac{1}{3}\left[1 + (1-D)\right] \approx 6414\cdot0.0018\cdot0.5 = \mathbf{5.8\ W} \;(D=0.5)$$

$$P_{HS,avg} = I^2 R_{ds}\cdot\tfrac{D}{3} = \mathbf{1.9\ W}$$

At full throttle (D → 1), the losses shift toward the high side: both average about 3.8 W.

**Electrothermal feedback.** R_ds depends on T_j, so solve iteratively:

$$T_j = T_a + P(T_j)\,R_{th,ja} \;\Rightarrow\; T_j^* = \frac{T_a + P_{25}R_{th}(1-0.1)}{1 - 0.004\,P_{25}R_{th}}$$

Thermal runaway occurs if 0.004·P₂₅·R_th ≥ 1. Check this in your Rth model.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the PWM-switched high-side and its complementary low-side switch at f_sw. Per motor:

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw | ½·25.2·80·20 ns·48k = **0.968 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 30 nC·25.2·48k = **0.036 W** |
| Output capacitance | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw·2 | 40 nC·25.2·48k = **0.048 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2t_dt·f_sw | 0.8·80·100 ns·48k = **0.307 W** |
| Gate drive (mostly in driver/R_g) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw | 2·60 nC·10·48k = **0.058 W** |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 1.42 W** |

**Sensitivity.** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. At 96 kHz it is about 2.8 W. Conduction dominates at 80A (about 18:1), so 48 kHz is a reasonable choice. Going lower reduces switching loss only marginally while increasing current ripple and audible noise.

**Dead-time trade-off.** Halving t_dt to 25 ns saves 0.15 W but risks shoot-through. Verify the minimum dead time against the driver's propagation mismatch plus FET t_d(off).

### Total loss budget at 80A burst

| | Per motor | 4 motors simultaneous |
|---|---|---|

Efficiency at 80A × 25.2V × 4 ≈ 8 kW input is 98.6%. Efficiency looks good, but the absolute heat on a few grams of PCB is the real problem.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Two-time-constant transient (Foster) network

$$T_j(t) = T_a + P_{FET}\sum_{i=1}^{2} R_{i}\left(1-e^{-t/\tau_i}\right) + P_{board}\,R_{b}\left(1-e^{-t/\tau_b}\right)$$

| Stage | R (K/W) | τ | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Junction→case (die) | 1.2 | 2 ms | FET datasheet Zth curve [A] |
| Case→PCB copper (local) | 8 | 150 ms | Thermal vias, 2 oz [A] |
| Board bulk→ambient | R_b = 6 (still air) / 2.5 (prop wash) | τ_b = R_b·C_b | [A] |

Board heat capacity: 30×30 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz, about 6 g Cu-equivalent plus FR4, giving **C_b ≈ 5 J/K** [A]. Weigh the actual board to replace this.

### 3.2 Adiabatic burst limit (board bulk)

For short bursts (t ≪ τ_b ≈ 12–30 s), the board behaves adiabatically:

$$\Delta T_{board} \approx \frac{P_{total}\,t}{C_b}$$

| Scenario | P | t | ΔT_board |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4×80A simultaneous | 112 W | 1 s | 22 K |
| 4×80A simultaneous | 112 W | 3 s | 67 K |
| 4×80A simultaneous | 112 W | 10 s | ~224 K (**failure**) |
| 1×80A, 3×30A | ~35 W | 10 s | 70 K |

### 3.3 Junction rise above board (worst low-side FET, 5.8 W)

$$\Delta T_{j-b} = 5.8\,(1.2 + 8(1-e^{-t/0.15}))$$

This reaches ≈ **53 K** within about 0.5 s.

**Worked result.** At T_a = 40 °C with 4×80A held for 3 s:

T_j ≈ 40 + 67 + 53 = **160 °C**. This exceeds 150 °C, so R_ds(Tj) must be re-iterated upward and the spec fails.

**Defensible rating:**
- **4×80A for ≤ 1.5 s**: T_j ≈ 127 °C
- **4×55A continuous** with prop wash, R_b = 2.5 K/W

Validate both with thermocouple tests and an IR camera on a bare board.
