## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Architectural Correction

### 0.1 The H743 cannot commutate the motors itself

The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and generates DShot. It does **not** commutate the motors. ArduPilot has no sensorless BLDC commutation firmware. The 4-in-1 ESC therefore carries **four independent ESC MCUs**, one per motor, running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. Typical choices are the AT32F421 or STM32G071.

Bi-directional DShot600 is the link between the H743 and those four MCUs. If Job #13 assumed "H743 drives 12 half-bridges directly," that architecture is not supportable with ArduPilot and should be rejected at design review.

### 0.2 "80 A burst" on a nano board is physically constrained

Nano boards are 20×20 mm (M2 mounting) or at most 25.5×25.5 mm. The model below shows that 80 A per motor is achievable only for a **time-limited burst of about 1–3 s**, and **not on all four motors at once**. The battery pads and wires cannot carry 320 A. Section 3 quantifies this.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step complementary PWM

In each 60° sector, one leg PWMs (high side ↔ low side) and one leg sinks (low side held ON).

- **On-time (D):** HS_A → motor → LS_B
- **Off-time (1−D):** LS_A (sync rectifying) → motor → LS_B

At every instant, **exactly two FETs carry the phase current I**.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

### 1.3 Bridge total (per motor)
