# STM32H743 Nano AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC): Power-Stage Thermal, Switching, and Bidirectional DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and a Feasibility Flag

The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot. It does not generate the 48 kHz motor PWM. Each ESC channel has its own MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071, and that MCU drives the gate drivers. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to those ESC MCUs. This split determines where each calculation belongs.

**Baseline parameters.** These are representative values. Replace them with your BOM values. Every equation below is written so you can substitute directly.

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 25.2 V | 6S, fully charged |
| I_burst | 80 A per motor | phase/DC-link current, 6-step |
| R_ds25 | 1.2 mΩ (max, V_gs = 10 V) | 40 V, 3.3×3.3 DFN-class MOSFET |

> ⚠️ **Feasibility flag.** 80 A per motor on a 20×20 mm board is a burst rating of roughly one second, not a continuous rating. The math below shows why. Treat any continuous rating above about 25–35 A on this form factor with skepticism unless the board has a heatsink or frame coupling.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model at 80 A Burst

### 1.1 Topology current path

In 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM, current always flows through one high-side and one low-side device. During the off-time, the synchronous low-side device carries the freewheel current. The instantaneous conduction loss per ESC channel is therefore:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2\left[2R_{ds}(T_j) + R_{cu} + R_{shunt}/4\right]
$$

The shunt term is divided by 4 because a 4-in-1 board typically uses one shared DC-link shunt.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

Because R_ds depends on T_j, and T_j depends on the losses, the system is coupled. Solve it by fixed-point iteration:

$$
T_j^{(k+1)} = T_{board} + P_{FET}\big(R_{ds}(T_j^{(k)})\big)\cdot R_{\theta,j\text{-}pcb}
$$
