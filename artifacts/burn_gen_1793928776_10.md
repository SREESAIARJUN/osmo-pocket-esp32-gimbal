## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

### 0.1 Architectural correction

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. In any production 4-in-1 design, each ESC channel has its own commutation MCU running BLHeli_32 or AM32 (e.g., AT32F421, STM32G071, or GD32E230). The partition is:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──DShot600 bidir (×4)──► ESC MCU ×4 (AM32) ──► 3-ph gate driver ──► 6 MOSFETs ──► motor
```

The H743's job is to generate the DShot frames, capture the GCR eRPM telemetry, and feed that RPM into the harmonic notch filter. The 48 kHz PWM and the switching losses are properties of the ESC MCU and power stage, not of the H743.

### 0.2 Feasibility flag (read before committing to the spec)

An **80 A per-channel burst on a "nano" (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) AIO is beyond what that class of board can deliver.** Commercial 20×20 AIOs are typically rated 35–45 A burst. The analysis below shows why: the board survives an 80 A event only for roughly 1–2 s, and only on one or two channels. I model the requirement as specified, but recommend one of two paths:

- Downgrade the burst rating to about 45–55 A, or
- Grow the board to 30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz copper.
