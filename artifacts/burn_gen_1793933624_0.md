## 0. Architectural Clarifications

**1. The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz bridge PWM.** In an ArduPilot AIO, the H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bi-directional DShot600. Each ESC channel has its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. This architecture assumes AT32F421 or STM32G071 parts. Each ESC MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM, commutation and eRPM telemetry. Driving four FOC bridges directly from the H743 is not supported by ArduPilot.

**2. 80 A on a 20×20 mm "nano" board is thermally infeasible as a sustained rating.** Section 3 shows this quantitatively. The model supports one of two interpretations:
- a short burst rating of about 1–5 s, or
- a 30.5×30.5 mm board.

Commercial 20×20 4-in-1 boards are typically rated 25–40 A per channel with propwash airflow.

**3. Package choice.** H743 in LQFP100 (14×14 mm) or TFBGA100 (8×8 mm). On a 20×20 board, use TFBGA100 on a separate stacked FC board. The FC and ESC are stacked via an 8-pin harness.

## 2. Loss Model (Per ESC Channel)

### 2.1 Commutation topology

AM32 uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification. In any 60° sector, current I flows through two conduction paths in series:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for duty D. The complementary low-side FET conducts for (1 − D).
- **Static leg:** the low-side FET is fully on.

**Conduction loss**

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\left[D + (1-D)\right] + I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Conduction loss is therefore independent of duty under synchronous rectification.

**Temperature dependence**

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right]$$

At T_j = 125 °C, R_DS = 1.8 mΩ.

$$P_{cond} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.8\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{23.04\ W}$$

**Copper loss**

$$P_{cu} = I^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \cdot 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.20\ W}$$

**Per-FET average loss** (6 FETs rotate through roles over one electrical cycle):

$$\bar P_{FET,cond} = \frac{P_{cond}}{6} = 3.84\ \text{W}$$

Using the average is valid because the electrical period (below 1 ms above about 1000 eRPM) is much shorter than the junction thermal time constant (about 10 ms).
