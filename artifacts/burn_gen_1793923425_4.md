## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Flags

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. It runs ArduPilot and sends DShot. Each of the four ESC channels needs its own commutation MCU, or one ESC MCU handles all four. The validated approach, and the one assumed here, uses four ESC MCUs:

| Function | Device (reference) | Firmware |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIH6 / VIT6 (480 MHz) | ArduPilot |
| ESC MCU ×4 | AT32F421K8 or STM32G071GB | AM32 (supports 48 kHz PWM and bidirectional DShot) |
| Gate driver ×4 | 3-phase half-bridge driver, about 10 V drive (FD6288Q class) | — |
| MOSFETs ×24 | 40 V, sub-1 mΩ, PQFN 3.3×3.3 or 5×6 | — |

**Red flags to resolve before layout:**

1. **80 A per channel on a nano (20×20 mm) board is a seconds-scale burst rating, not a continuous one.** Section 3 shows roughly 2 s with all four motors at 80 A. Commercial 20×20 4-in-1 boards are typically rated 40–55 A burst. For a meaningful burst rating, use a 30.5×30.5 mm ESC or a stacked design.
