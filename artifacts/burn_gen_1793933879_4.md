**Architecture note:** The STM32H743 is the flight controller. Each motor bridge is commutated by a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32 (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071), one per motor. ## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step complementary PWM, two conducting FETs are always in series with the motor current:
- **During on-time (D):** high-side of phase A and low-side of phase B.
- **During off-time (1–D):** low-side of phase A (synchronous rectification) and low-side of phase B.

The total conduction loss per motor channel is therefore independent of duty cycle, to first order:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

where the on-resistance rises with junction temperature:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big]$$

### 1.2 Per-channel conduction loss at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond per channel |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.20 mΩ | 2·6400·0.0012 = **15.4 W** |
| 100 °C | 1.70 mΩ | **21.7 W** |
| 125 °C | 1.86 mΩ | **23.8 W** |

### 1.3 Distribution among the FETs

- The loss is shared across the 6 FETs over one electrical revolution.
- Each FET conducts for roughly 1/3 of the electrical period.
- Averaged loss per FET ≈ P_cond / 6 ≈ **4 W**.
- The instantaneous peak in a conducting FET is I²R ≈ 11.9 W at 125 °C.
- At high eRPM (commutation periods well below the FET's thermal time constant of ~ms), the averaged value governs.
- At stall or very low RPM, the peak governs. Design for this: it is the classic desync/stall failure mode.

### 1.4 Parasitic copper loss

Assume a 2 oz inner/outer stack with roughly 0.3 mΩ of effective bridge and phase copper per channel:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.0003 \approx 1.9\ \text{W per channel}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switched leg hard-switches. The commutating leg switches at the electrical commutation rate, which is negligible next to 48 kHz.

### 2.1 Turn-on and turn-off (V–I overlap)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12\, V_{bus}\, I_{ph}\,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}$$

$$= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I_{ph}\, (2\,t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 40\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

Q_rr scales with di/dt and current. Measured values at 80 A are often 2–3× the datasheet figure, so budget **~0.15 W**.

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = C_{oss,eff}\, V_{bus}^2\, f_{sw} = 1.5\,\text{nF} \times 635 \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.046\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (2 switching FETs, dissipated mostly in the driver)

$$P_g = 2\, Q_g\, V_{GS}\, f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\,\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.058\ W}$$

### 2.6 Total per-channel loss at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

| Component | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction | 23.8 |
| Copper | 1.9 |
| Switching overlap | 1.45 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Q_rr (derated) | 0.15 |
| C_oss | 0.05 |
| Gate drive | 0.06 |
| **Total per channel** | **≈ 28.0 W** |
| **All 4 channels at 80 A** | **≈ 112 W** |
