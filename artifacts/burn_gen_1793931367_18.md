# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #17): Power-Stage Model, DShot DMA Plan, Netlists

## 0. Assumptions and Corrections

Before the math, three architectural points need to be settled:

1. **The H743 cannot commutate the motors.** On every production AIO board, the STM32H743 is the flight controller. Each motor channel needs its own ESC MCU, or one MCU for all four, running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Example parts are the AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 talks to those MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. This document models it that way.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano footprint is thermally a seconds-scale rating, not a continuous one.** The model below quantifies exactly how many seconds.
3. **All device parameters below are placeholders.** They represent a typical 40 V, 3×3 mm or 5×6 mm DFN trench MOSFET. Substitute your selected part's datasheet values; every equation is parameterized for that.

**Baseline parameters**

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

**Operating mode:** 6-step trapezoidal commutation, complementary (synchronous) PWM.

At any instant one phase leg conducts through its high side, either the HS FET at duty D or the sync-rectifying LS FET at 1 − D. The return leg conducts through its LS FET at 100%. The current therefore always passes through **two R_ds(on) in series**.

**Per-FET losses**

- Switching high-side FET:
  P_HS = I² · R_ds(T_j) · D
- Its complementary low-side FET:
  P_LS,sync = I² · R_ds(T_j) · (1 − D)
- Return-leg low-side FET:
  P_LS,ret = I² · R_ds(T_j)

**Per-channel total, independent of D:**

  P_cond = 2 · I² · R_ds(T_j)

**Temperature coupling.** R_ds and T_j depend on each other, so solve them iteratively:

  T_j = T_board + P_FET · R_θJC
  R_ds = R₂₅ · [1 + 0.006 · (T_j − 25)]

The fixed point is reached in about 3 iterations.

**At T_j ≈ 125 °C**, R_ds = 1.92 mΩ:

  P_cond = 2 × 6400 × 1.92 mΩ = **24.6 W per channel**

This loss is shared across 2 FETs per commutation step, and the steps rotate through 6 FETs per electrical cycle. However, the return-leg FET carries **12.3 W for each 1/3 of the electrical period**. The electrical period at 30 krpm on a 14-pole motor is about 0.29 ms, which is far below the thermal time constant of the die. The die therefore sees the averaged value:

  P_LS,ret,avg ≈ 12.3 W · (1/3) + sync contributions ≈ **4–6 W per FET**

The ripple component is filtered by Z_θJC.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the hard-switched FET (the HS of the PWM leg) sees V·I overlap. Each loss term:

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V·I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | ½·25.2·80·20 ns·48 k = **0.97 W** |
| Reverse recovery | V·Q_rr·f_sw | 25.2·50 nC·48 k = **0.06 W** |
| C_oss discharge | ½·C_oss·V²·f_sw | ½·1.5 nF·635·48 k = **0.02 W** |
| Dead-time body diode | 2·V_f·I·t_dt·f_sw | 2·0.8·80·200 ns·48 k = **1.23 W** |
| Gate drive (lost in driver/R_g) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw | 2·50 nC·10·48 k = **0.05 W** |
| **Total** | | **≈ 2.33 W per channel** |

**Design notes**

- Dead-time loss exceeds overlap loss. Trimming t_dt from 200 ns to 100 ns saves 0.6 W per channel. In AM32 this is the dead-time setting. Validate shoot-through margin against the driver's propagation-delay mismatch before trimming.
- Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it is about 1.2 W; at 96 kHz it is about 4.7 W.
- 48 kHz is a reasonable compromise for low-inductance 5–7" motors.

## 3. Board-Level Burst Budget

| Contributor | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| MOSFET conduction (125 °C) | 24.6 W | 98.4 W |
| Switching | 2.3 W | 9.3 W |
| Copper (≈0.15 mΩ effective path, 2 oz) | 1.0 W | 3.8 W |
| Current-sense shunt (0.1 mΩ, 320 A total) | — | 10.2 W |
| **Total** | | **≈ 122 W** |

**Efficiency check.** Output power is roughly 25.2 V × 80 A × D_eff × 4. At full throttle that is about 8 kW, so 122 W is a 98.5% efficient stage. The efficiency is fine; the problem is power density.

### Transient Thermal Model (Second-Order Foster)

**Network:** junction → case → board

  Z_θ(t) = Σ R_i · (1 − e^(−t/τ_i))

| Stage | Values |
|---|---|
| Die/case | R₁ = 1.5 K/W, τ₁ ≈ 5 ms |
| Board to air | R₂ ≈ 8 K/W, τ₂ = R₂ · C_board |

**Board heat capacity (30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz)**

- FR4 at ~4 g × 1.1 J/gK ≈ 4.4 J/K
- Copper at ~5 g × 0.385 J/gK ≈ 1.9 J/K
- Components ≈ 1.5 J/K
- **C_board ≈ 8 J/K**, so τ₂ ≈ 64 s

**Adiabatic regime** (t ≪ τ₂):

  dT_board/dt ≈ P / C = 122 / 8 ≈ **15 K/s**

### Burst Time Limit

Allow T_j,max = 150 °C with 20 °C margin, so the target is 130 °C. The per-FET junction-over-board rise is:

  ΔT_jc ≈ 6 W × 1.5 ≈ 9 K

| Starting state | T_board,start | Allowed T_board | t_burst |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cruise (hot) | 60 °C | 121 °C | (121 − 60)/15 ≈ **4.0 s** |
| Cold start | 40 °C | 121 °C | (121 − 40)/15 ≈ **5.4 s** |

**Recovery** at cruise of 4 × 20 A uses the same equations. P is about 8 W, giving an equilibrium rise of roughly 64 K in still air. Prop wash typically cuts R₂ by 3–4×, giving an equilibrium near +20 K. Expect about 60–90 s between full bursts.

**Specification statement:**

> 80 A per channel for ≤ 4 s from a 60 °C board, with all 4 channels simultaneous. Single-channel 80 A sustains ≈ 4× longer.
