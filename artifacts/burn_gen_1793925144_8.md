# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #15): Architecture, Loss Model, DShot DMA Plan, Netlist

## 0. Critical Findings

Several points need resolving before layout.

1. **The H743 cannot commutate the motors under ArduPilot.** ArduPilot is flight-control firmware and has no sensorless BLDC commutation. The correct architecture is:
   - H743 (ArduPilot) sends bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32).
   - Each ESC MCU drives a 3-phase gate driver and 6 MOSFETs.
   
   The model below uses this architecture.
2. **"80A burst × 4 channels" on a nano-class board (about 30×30 mm) is not thermally survivable for more than about 1–2 s.** Section 3 shows roughly 78 W of dissipation against a board heat capacity of about 3–4 J/K. The realistic continuous rating is about 23 A per channel. Either redefine the spec (for example, 80 A for 1 s on one or two channels and 35 A continuous) or move to a 30.5 mm board with heavy copper and a heatsink.
3. **System current at 4 × 80 A on 6S is 320 A, about 7 kW.** An XT60 or XT90 connector and nano-class battery pads cannot carry this. Pad and connector derating limits the system before the FETs do.

All component values below are **design assumptions**. Replace them with datasheet values for your selected parts and verify by thermal-camera bench testing.

**Commutation mode:** trapezoidal six-step with complementary (synchronous) PWM, which is the AM32 default. At any instant the phase current flows through **two** conducting channels, one high-side and one low-side path.

## 2. Loss Model for One Motor Channel at I = 80 A

### 2.1 Conduction losses

With synchronous rectification, current always flows through two FET channels regardless of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I^2 R_{ds,hot} = 2 (80)^2 (1.2\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{15.36\ W}$$

Each FET sees about 7.7 W while it is in the active commutation pair. This is averaged over a one-third electrical duty, but at burst timescales (more than 100 ms) the board, not the junction, dominates. See Section 3.

**PCB copper loss (frequently ignored, and significant):**

Assume a 2 oz (70 µm) path, 4 mm wide and 10 mm long, on one layer:

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{w t} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot 0.01}{0.004\cdot 70\times10^{-6}} = 0.61\ \text{mΩ} \;(\times1.3 \text{ hot}) \approx 0.8\ \text{mΩ}$$

$$P_{Cu} = (80)^2 \cdot 0.8\times10^{-3} \approx \mathbf{5.1\ W}$$

Mitigation:
- Parallel the power nets on all four layers with via stitching.
- Use 3 oz outer copper.
- Target R_Cu below 0.25 mΩ per path.

### 2.2 Switching losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing high-side FET hard-switches. The low-side commutates softly via the body diode.

Gate currents through the Miller region:

$$I_{G,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_G} = \frac{12-4.5}{10} = 0.75\ \text{A} \quad\Rightarrow\quad t_r = \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{G,on}} = 26.7\ \text{ns}$$

$$I_{G,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_G} = 0.45\ \text{A} \quad\Rightarrow\quad t_f = 44.4\ \text{ns}$$

Hard-switching V-I overlap loss:

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = \tfrac12(25.2)(80)(71.1\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz}) = \mathbf{3.44\ W}$$

Secondary terms:

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Dead-time diode conduction | 2·V_f·I·t_dt·f_sw | **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | V_bus·Q_rr·f_sw | 0.06 W |
| C_oss discharge | ½·C_oss·V²·f_sw | 0.03 W |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver/R_g) | 2·Q_g·V_drv·f_sw | 0.07 W |
