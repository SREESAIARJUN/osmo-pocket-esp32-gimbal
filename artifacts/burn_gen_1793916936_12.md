> **Architect's risk flag (read first).** 80 A per channel on a true "nano" (20×20 mm) board is not thermally credible. The math in §3 shows roughly 100+ W of dissipation into a few grams of PCB. I have therefore moved the design to **30.5×30.5 mm with 5×6 FETs**. Even then, 80 A must be treated as a time-limited burst rating, not a continuous one. If marketing requires 20×20 mm, the honest rating is about 35–40 A burst.
>
> **Architectural note.** The H743 does **not** commutate the motors. A single H743 cannot cleanly run ArduPilot's scheduler plus four independent 48 kHz sensorless BEMF loops. The H743 speaks bidirectional DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs. This is the industry-standard partition.

## 2. Conduction-Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Current waveforms in 6-step commutation

In 6-step drive, two phases conduct at any instant. Within each 60° sector:

- **High-side FET** of the PWM'd leg: conducts for fraction D (duty).
- **Low-side FET** of the same leg: conducts for (1−D) as the synchronous rectifier.
- **Low-side FET** of the return leg: conducts 100% of the sector.

So, ignoring ripple, exactly **two FETs carry the phase current I_ph at every instant**, regardless of D:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

Per-device RMS current: each high-side or low-side device is active for 120° of every 360°, giving

$$I_{FET,rms} = I_{ph}\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{3}} \;(\text{split between HS and LS of a leg according to } D)$$

### 2.2 Temperature dependence of R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ /^\circ\mathrm{C}$$

This is an iterative (electro-thermal) problem. At T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{DS(on)} = 1.0\,\text{mΩ}\times(1+0.0055\times 85) = 1.47\ \text{mΩ}$$
