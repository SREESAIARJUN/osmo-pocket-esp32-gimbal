# STM32H743 Nano AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #12): Architecture, Loss Model, DShot Timer Plan, and Netlist

## 0. Architecture Clarifications

Two points shape everything below.

1. **The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In every production 4-in-1 design, the H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs. These are typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 parts running AM32 (BLHeli_32 is discontinued). The ESC MCUs generate the 48 kHz complementary PWM, dead-time, and commutation. This document models both layers.
2. **80 A burst on a nano board (20×20 or 25×25 mm) is thermally marginal.** The math below shows that burst duration, not silicon current rating, is the binding constraint. I give a burst-time budget rather than a bare "80 A" claim.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Six-Step Synchronous Commutation

In any commutation sector, one phase is PWM-switched (HS at duty D, LS complementary) and one phase has its LS statically ON. The third phase floats for BEMF sensing. The current I flows through:

- **Switched leg:** HS for D·T, LS for (1−D)·T, so the leg always conducts one R_DS(on).
- **Static leg:** one LS continuously.

Per-channel conduction loss:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}\ (\Rightarrow k_T = 1.5\ @\ 125°C)$$

Copper and shunt add series terms:

$$P_{cond,total} = I^2\left[2R_{DS}(T_j) + R_{trace} + R_{shunt}\right]$$

### 1.2 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond (FETs only) |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.2 mΩ | 2·6400·0.0012 = **15.4 W** |
| 125 °C | 1.8 mΩ | 2·6400·0.0018 = **23.0 W** |

Phase copper: 2 oz is 70 µm thick, giving ~0.25 mΩ/sq. Assume ~0.3 mΩ effective, which adds 6400·0.0003 = **1.9 W**.

**Conduction subtotal (hot): ≈ 24.9 W per channel.**

**Electrothermal feedback.** Because R_DS rises with T_j, the system is self-heating. A stable operating point exists only if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\cdot R_{th} < 1 \;\Rightarrow\; 2I^2R_{25}\alpha\,R_{th,j-a} < 1$$

At 80 A: 2·6400·0.0012·0.005 = 0.077 W/K. Thermal runaway therefore occurs if R_th,j-a > 13 K/W per channel. Section 4.3 checks this.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switched leg incurs switching loss. Per commutation sector, the components are as follows.

### 2.1 Hard-Switching V·I Overlap (HS FET)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = \tfrac12 (25.2)(80)(40\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz}) = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Output Capacitance

$$P_{oss} = C_{oss,eff} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = (1.2\text{ nF})(635)(48\text{ k}) = \mathbf{0.037\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse Recovery (LS Body Diode into HS Turn-On)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = (50\text{ nC})(25.2)(48\text{ k}) = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction (Two Edges per Period)

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = (0.8)(80)(400\text{ ns})(48\text{ k}) = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

This is the term most worth optimizing. Cutting t_dt to 100 ns saves 0.6 W per channel. AM32 exposes dead-time as a setting; verify shoot-through margin on a scope first.

### 2.5 Gate Drive

This loss is dissipated in the driver and R_g, not the FET
