## 0. Architecture Corrections Before the Math

Three points affect every calculation below.

1. **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz motor PWM.** In a 4-in-1 stack, each ESC channel has its own MCU, typically an STM32G071 or AT32F421 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. That MCU runs the 48 kHz complementary PWM, commutation and BEMF sensing. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to the four ESC MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. The two timer domains are independent.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is thermally capacity-limited, not steady-state.** The math below shows roughly 90 W of dissipation at 80 A per channel with all four channels loaded. On a 20×20 board this is survivable only for about 1–2 s. Treat 80 A as a per-motor burst rating, not a continuous rating.
3. **Assumed operating point.** All numbers below use 6S (25.2 V max), a 40 V class 5×6 mm MOSFET, and synchronous (complementary) 6-step commutation. Swap in your actual datasheet values; every equation is parameterized.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-Dependent On-Resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha_R (T_j - 25)]$$

At the T_j = 110 °C design point:

$$R_{DS}(110) = 1.0 \times (1 + 0.006 \times 85) \approx 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega$$

This must be solved iteratively with the thermal model in Section 4, because the loss and the temperature depend on each other. For this design the loop converges within 3 iterations.

### 1.2 Six-Step Synchronous Commutation

At any instant, current flows through one high-side leg and one low-side leg.

- **Active leg:** the PWM'd FET conducts for a fraction D of each period, and its complementary sync-rectifier FET conducts for the remaining 1−D. Together they carry I_ph 100% of the time.
- **Static leg:** one FET stays fully on.

Because of this, the per-channel conduction loss in synchronous mode does not depend on duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\text{m} = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

Each of the 6 FETs conducts for 120° electrical. Averaged over the electrical period:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} = \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6} = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

The electrical frequency is roughly 1–3 kHz, far faster than the junction thermal time constant (about 10 ms). Using the period average is therefore valid.

### 1.3 Parasitic Conduction (Copper and Shunt)

$$P_{Cu,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2 R_{Cu} = 2 \times 6400 \times 0.5\text{m} = 6.4\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{shunt,ch} = I_{bus}^2 R_{shunt} \approx 6400 \times 0.25\text{m} = 1.6\ \text{W}$$

The shunt term assumes a worst case of D → 1, where I_bus ≈ I_ph.

**Design rule:** on a nano board, copper loss is about one third of the FET conduction loss. Use 6 layers with 2 oz on all of them, put phase and bus pours on at least 4 layers, and stitch with a dense array of 0.3 mm vias.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the active (PWM) leg switches. The sync FET switches under ZVS-like conditions after the dead time.

### 2.1 Hard-Switching Overlap Loss (Active FET)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction (Sync FET)

There are two edges per period:

$$P_{dt} = 2\,V_f I_{ph} t_{dt} f_{sw} = 2 \times 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 1.23\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse Recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

The H-side FET dissipates this loss.

### 2.4 Output Capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 60\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.07\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate Drive

Two FETs per PWM cycle:

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2 \times 80\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.077\ \text{W}$$

This loss is dissipated mostly in the gate driver and gate resistors, not in the FET.

### 2.6 Switching Loss vs. Frequency

$$P_{sw,total}(f) \approx 69.3\,\mu\text{J} \times f$$

| Frequency | Switching-related loss |
|---|---|
| 24 kHz | ≈ 1.7 W |
| 48 kHz | ≈ 3.4 W |
| 96 kHz | ≈ 6.7 W |

At 48 kHz and 80 A, conduction loss dominates by about 6:1. **Reducing R_DS(on) is worth far more than reducing switching speed.** Do not push edges faster to save switching loss; it worsens ringing and EMI on the DShot lines.

## 3. Loss Budget at 80 A Burst

### 3.1 Per Channel

| Term | W / channel |
|---|---|
| FET conduction | 19.20 |
| Overlap switching | 1.94 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.13 |
| Gate drive | 0.08 |
| PCB copper | 6.40 |
| Shunt | 1.60 |
| **Channel total** | **30.6 W** |

### 3.2 Board Total

Four channels at 80 A burst dissipate **≈ 122 W**. Of this, about 90 W is in the FETs and about 32 W is in copper and shunts.

Efficiency check: output power is about 25.2 × 80 × 4 × D. At D = 0.8 this is roughly 6.4 kW, giving about 98% efficiency. The efficiency is fine; the heat density is the problem.

### 3.3 Hottest FET

The hottest device is the high-side FET on the active leg:

$$P_{FET,max} \approx 3.2 + \frac{1.94 + 0.06 + 0.07}{2} + \text{share of }P_{dt} \approx \mathbf{4.9\ W}$$

The switching terms are halved because the active role rotates between the high side and low side across commutation steps.
