# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Basis (Job #19)

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

Two points about the architecture shape the rest of this document:

1. **Who generates the 48 kHz PWM.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller. The 48 kHz complementary gate PWM is generated by four dedicated ESC MCUs (AT32F421K8U7, running AM32), one per motor. The H743 only sends bidirectional DShot600 commands and decodes the eRPM telemetry that comes back.
2. **80 A burst on a nano board is thermally aggressive.** The model in §3 shows it is time-limited to a few seconds. The design closes only with explicit burst-duration limits and firmware current limiting.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 0.9 × 1.45 = **1.31 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Commutation topology

With 6-step (120°) commutation and complementary PWM:

- One low-side FET is held ON continuously for its 60° sector.
- The PWM leg alternates between its high side (fraction D of each cycle) and its low side via synchronous rectification (fraction 1−D).
- So **two FETs always carry the full phase current I**, regardless of D. Dead-time diode conduction is handled separately in §2.

Per-channel conduction loss:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

At 80 A and 125 °C:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.31\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{16.8\ W}$$

### 1.3 Copper and shunt contributions

| Element | R | P @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase path copper, ~2 conducting traces/planes | ≈ 0.25 mΩ effective | 1.6 W |
| Low-side current shunt (shared, 0.2 mΩ) | 0.2 mΩ | 1.3 W |
| Battery pad → bus plane (shared by 4 channels, 320 A total) | 0.05 mΩ | 5.1 W total → 1.3 W per channel |

$$P_{Cu,ch} \approx 4.2\ \text{W}$$

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution, used to find the hottest junction

Each FET carries current for 120° of the electrical cycle (1/3 of the time). Its share of that interval depends on whether it is the static FET or on the PWM leg:

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6} = 2.8\ \text{W}$$

At low duty, the low-side FETs dominate, because they carry current in both their static sector and their synchronous-rectification interval. Use a hot-FET factor of 1.25:

$$P_{FET,max} \approx 3.5\ \text{W (conduction)}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Each PWM cycle has one turn-on and one turn-off of the high side, with the low side switching at near-zero V_DS (soft, ZVS-like), plus two dead-time intervals.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 35\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.69\ W}$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 40\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{ k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Body-diode reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 30\text{ nC}\times 25.2 \times 48\text{ k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

This term is significant at high current.

$$P_{DT} = V_F\,I\,(2\,t_{DT})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{ ns}\times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

Dissipated mostly in the gate resistors and driver, not the FETs:

$$P_{G} = 2\,Q_g\,V_{GS}\,f_{sw} = 2\times 60\text{ nC}\times 10\times 48\text{ k} = 0.058\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Switching total

| Term | W per channel |
|---|---|
| V–I overlap | 1.69 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 |
| Coss | 0.05 |
| Qrr | 0.04 |
| Gate | 0.06 |
| **Total** | **3.07** |

**Frequency sensitivity:** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it would be about 1.5 W; at 96 kHz about 6.1 W. 48 kHz is a reasonable compromise for low-inductance racing motors (ripple versus loss).
