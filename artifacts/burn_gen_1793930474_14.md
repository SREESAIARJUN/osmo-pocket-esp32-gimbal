## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous PWM
At any instant two phase legs conduct:
- **PWM leg:** the high-side (HS) conducts for fraction D. The low-side (LS) conducts synchronously for fraction (1 − D).
- **Return leg:** the LS is held fully on for the 120° sector.

So at any instant **exactly two MOSFETs** carry the phase current I, independent of D (dead time ignored here):

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right]$$

This creates an electrothermal feedback loop. Iterate to convergence:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + P_{FET}(T_j)\cdot R_{th,j\text{-}ref}$$

For the worst-case burst, assume T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{hot} = 1.6\,\text{m}\Omega \times [1 + 0.0055 \times 100] = 2.48\,\text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.48\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{31.7\ W\ per\ motor\ channel}$$

### 1.3 RMS refinement (ripple)
With phase inductance L_ph and peak-to-peak ripple ΔI:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12},\qquad \Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{2L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

With L_ph ≈ 15 µH (a typical low-kV 5" motor is roughly 10–30 µH line-to-line), D = 0.5, and V_bus − V_bemf ≈ 12 V:

$$\Delta I = \frac{12 \times 0.5}{2 \times 15\times10^{-6} \times 48000} \approx 4.2\ \text{A}$$

The ripple term adds only about 1.5 A²/80² ≈ 0.02%, which is negligible at 48 kHz. *(This is a key benefit of 48 kHz over 24 kHz.)*

### 1.4 Per-device distribution
Over one electrical revolution each leg spends:
- 120° as the PWM leg (HS for D, LS for 1 − D),
- 120° as the return leg (LS fully on),
- 120° floating.

The per-device averages are therefore:

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I^2 R,\qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D) I^2 R + \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R$$

At full throttle (D → 1), HS ≈ LS ≈ I²R/3 = **5.3 W per FET**. At D = 0.5 the **low-side FETs run hottest** (7.9 W versus 2.6 W for HS). Place the LS FETs closest to the thermal vias and ground pour.
