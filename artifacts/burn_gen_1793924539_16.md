# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model

## 0. Scope, Architecture and Assumptions

The STM32H743 is the **flight controller**. It does not switch the MOSFETs. Each of the four ESC channels has its own small MCU (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32), which generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 commands and receives eRPM telemetry back.

No MOSFET part number was given, so the model uses a representative device. Replace these values with your datasheet numbers; every equation stays the same.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path
In 6-step drive, two phases conduct at any instant. With complementary (synchronous) PWM at duty D:

- During the on-time (fraction D), current flows through the high-side FET of phase A and the low-side FET of phase B.
- During the off-time (fraction 1−D), current freewheels through the synchronous low-side of phase A and the low-side of phase B.

Either way, the current always passes through two channels. Therefore:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

This result is independent of D, as long as dead time is neglected (dead time is treated separately in §2.4).

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

| T_j (°C) | R_DS (mΩ) | P_cond per motor (W) |
|---|---|---|
| 25 | 1.20 | 15.4 |
| 75 | 1.47 | 18.8 |
| 100 | 1.61 | 20.6 |
| 125 | 1.74 | 22.3 |

### 1.3 Per-device distribution
Over one electrical cycle, each of the 6 FETs carries current for 1/3 of the time. The high-side carries D of that window and its low-side partner carries 1−D.

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{DS} D,\qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{DS}(1-D) + \tfrac{1}{3}I^2R_{DS}$$

The worst single device is the low-side at low D. At T_j = 100 °C this is about **6.9 W peak average**. The low-side FETs are the hotspot; place them nearest the thermal escape path.

### 1.4 Copper loss
Assume 0.5 mΩ of total path resistance (pads, 2 oz inner planes, motor pads):

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{path} = 80^2 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 3.2\ \text{W per motor}$$

At 80 A this term is not negligible. Use ≥2 oz copper on at least 4 layers, and stitch phase pads with ≥20 vias of 0.3 mm.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing high-side FET hard-switches. The synchronous low-side turns on after its body diode is already conducting, so it switches at near-zero voltage.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot30\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery loss (low-side body diode)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance loss

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,eq} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.023\ \text{W}$$
