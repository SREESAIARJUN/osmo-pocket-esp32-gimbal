# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Basis (Job #40)

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

Two points need to be settled before the math, because they change the model.

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** A 4-in-1 ESC uses dedicated ESC MCUs. This design uses 4× AT32F421 running AM32, one per channel. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to each ESC over bidirectional DShot600. The 48 kHz figure is the **ESC PWM frequency**, not anything the H743 generates.
2. **An 80 A burst on a nano (20×20 / 25.5×25.5) board is thermally marginal.** The model below quantifies this. Expect about 4 s at 80 A on all four channels and about 30–35 A continuous in prop-wash airflow. Do not release a datasheet that claims more without a thermocouple test.

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max (22.2 V nominal) |
| MOSFET | 40 V N-ch, DFN3.3×3.3 class, 6 per channel, 24 total |
| R_ds(on) | 1.0 mΩ @ 25 °C, **1.5 mΩ @ T_j = 125 °C** (k_T ≈ 1.5) |
| t_r / t_f (with gate driver) | 20 ns / 20 ns |
| Q_g / Q_rr / C_oss | 60 nC / 50 nC / 1.5 nF |
| Dead time t_dt | 50 ns (each edge) |
| Body diode V_f | 0.8 V |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal, complementary (synchronous) PWM, f_sw = 48 kHz |
| Board | 30.5×30.5 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz outer and 2 oz power inner layers |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current Path
In 6-step synchronous mode, current always flows through exactly two MOSFETs:

- **ON state:** high-side of phase A, then the motor, then the low-side of phase B.
- **OFF state (complementary):** low-side of phase A, then the motor, then the low-side of phase B.

The motor phase current I_ph is therefore conducted through 2 × R_ds(on) for 100% of the period, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds(on)}(T_j) + I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot R_{trace}$$

Temperature dependence of R_ds(on):

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

### 1.2 Numbers at 80 A
- **MOSFETs:** P_cond,FET = 80² × 2 × 1.5 mΩ = **19.2 W per channel**
- **Copper:** a phase trace of about 8 mm × 3 mm on 2 oz Cu has R ≈ 0.6 mΩ including vias, giving 80² × 0.6 mΩ ≈ **3.8 W**. This loss is distributed across the board.
- **Battery-side current** (with duty D ≈ 0.75 at burst): I_bat ≈ D × I_ph = 60 A per channel, or 240 A peak pack current across 4 channels. This sets the input copper and connector sizing.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing leg switches. Per channel there is one hard-switched commutation pair per PWM period.

| Loss term | Formula | Value @ 25.2 V, 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw | ½·25.2·80·40 ns·48 k = **1.94 W** |
| Dead-time body diode | V_f · I · 2t_dt · f_sw | 0.8·80·100 ns·48 k = **0.31 W** |
| Reverse recovery | V · Q_rr · f_sw | 25.2·50 nC·48 k = **0.06 W** |
| C_oss (×2 devices) | 2 · ½ · C_oss · V² · f_sw | **0.05 W** |
| Gate drive (driver dissipation) | 2 · Q_g · V_gs · f_sw | 2·60 nC·10 V·48 k = **0.06 W** |
| **Total P_sw** | | **≈ 2.4 W per channel** |

**Sensitivity to switching frequency:** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it would be about 1.2 W. At 96 kHz it would be about 4.8 W. The choice of 48 kHz trades roughly 1.2 W per channel for lower current ripple and quieter motors.

## 3. Total Loss and Thermal Model

### 3.1 Loss Budget (Burst, All Four Channels at 80 A)

| Source | Per channel | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching | 2.4 W | 9.6 W |
| Copper | 3.8 W | 15.2 W |
| **Total** | **25.4 W** | **≈ 101 W** |

### 3.2 Junction-to-Board (Fast Time Constant)
Per FET, the conducting pair shares about 10.8 W, so roughly 11 W is the worst case on one device.

- R_th(j-c) ≈ 1.2 K/W, so ΔT_jc ≈ 13 K.
- The thermal time constant from junction to package is about 10 ms, so the junction tracks board temperature plus about 13 K within tens of milliseconds.

### 3.3 Board Lumped Thermal Mass (Slow Time Constant)

| Element | Mass | c_p | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| FR4 (30.5² × 1.6 mm) | 2.75 g | 1.1 J/g·K | 3.0 J/K |
| Copper (~0.25 cm³ effective) | 2.3 g | 0.385 J/g·K | 0.9 J/K |
| FETs, capacitors, solder | ~1.5 g | ~0.7 J/g·K | 1.0 J/K |
| **C_board** | | | **≈ 4.9 J/K** |

Convective resistance in prop wash: h ≈ 100 W/m²K and A ≈ 2 × 0.00093 m², so **R_ba ≈ 5.4 K/W** and **τ = R·C ≈ 26 s**.

### 3.4 Governing Equation

$$C\frac{dT_b}{dt} = P(T_b) - \frac{T_b - T_a}{R_{ba}},\qquad P(T_b) = P_0\,[1 + \alpha(T_b + \Delta T_{jc} - 25)]$$

### 3.5 Results (T_a = 35 °C, Limit T_board = 105 °C, so T_j ≈ 120 °C)

- **Adiabatic 4×80 A burst:** dT/dt ≈ 101 / 4.9 ≈ 20 K/s, giving **≈ 3.5–4 s** to reach the limit.
- **Single motor at 80 A** (yaw punch, 25 W): 25 W into 4.9 J/K gives about 5 K/s, so about 14 s.
- **Continuous rating:** ΔT_max / R_ba = 70 / 5.4 ≈ 13 W total. Solving 4·I²·(2·1.5 m + 0.6 m) + switching for 13 W gives **I_cont ≈ 30–33 A per channel**.
