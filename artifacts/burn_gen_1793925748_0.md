# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #63): System Architecture & Loss Model

**Rev A. Engineering baseline. All component values are reference-class and must be re-run with the datasheet numbers of the parts actually procured.**

## 0. Scope, Topology and Key Findings

### 0.1 Architecture

A 4-in-1 ESC does not run on the STM32H743. The real system has two parts:

| Domain | MCU | Firmware | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100, 480 MHz) | ArduPilot | Attitude control and estimation, bi-directional DShot600 master |
| ESC, ×4 channels | 4× STM32G071 (or AT32F421) | AM32 | 6-step BLDC drive, 48 kHz PWM, DShot slave with eRPM telemetry |

The model below covers both domains. The H743 is responsible for DShot and DMA. The ESC MCUs are responsible for 48 kHz switching.

### 0.2 Headline result (read this first)

**The "80 A" rating cannot be sustained on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board.** The derivation (§3) shows:

| Condition | Total heat | Time from 40 °C to 120 °C PCB |
|---|---|---|
| 4 channels × 80 A simultaneously | ≈ 106 W | ≈ 1.7 s |
| 1 channel × 80 A, others idle | ≈ 26 W | ≈ 7 s |
| Steady-state limit (prop-wash cooling) | ≈ 8 W | n/a (≈ 25–28 A per channel, all four) |

Recommended specification language: **"25 A continuous / 80 A burst ≤ 1 s, single channel."** Firmware current limiting is mandatory (§3.5).

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

**Current path.** In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, phase current always flows through exactly two FETs:

- During the on-time D, current flows through the high-side of phase A and the low-side of phase B.
- During the off-time (1−D), the high-side current transfers to the complementary low-side of phase A. The low-side of phase B stays on.

The sum is therefore independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond}=I_{rms}^2\,\big[\,2\,R_{DS}(T_j)\,\big]+I_{rms}^2R_{Cu}$$

$$R_{DS}(T_j)=R_{25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big]$$

Note: a 6-step phase current is close to flat-topped, so I_rms ≈ I_dc,motor during each 120° conduction interval. That interval is the 80 A burst.

**Self-consistent junction temperature.** R_DS(on) depends on T_j, which depends on loss. Solve with fixed-point iteration, T_j ← T_pcb + P_FET·R_θJ-PCB, with R_θJ-PCB ≈ 1.5 K/W. Hold T_pcb at 110 °C and T_j converges near 125 °C, giving R_DS ≈ 1.6 mΩ.

| Term | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 80² × 2 × 1.6 mΩ | **20.5 W** |
| Copper conduction | 80² × 0.5 mΩ | **3.2 W** |

The hottest FET is the phase-B low-side, which conducts 100% of the interval: 80² × 1.6 mΩ ≈ 10.2 W.

### 2.2 Switching losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. Its turn-on is hard: it commutates the opposite FET's body diode or channel.

**(a) V–I overlap loss**

Transition times from Miller-region gate current:

$$t_r=\frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{(V_{drv}-V_{pl})/(R_{g,on}+R_{g,int})}=\frac{12\text{ nC}}{7.5/11.5}=18.4\text{ ns}$$

$$t_f=\frac{12\text{ nC}}{4.5/(2.2+1.5)}=9.9\text{ ns}$$

$$P_{VI}=\tfrac12V_{bus}I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw}=\tfrac12(25.2)(80)(28.3\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz})=\mathbf{1.37\ W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery**

$$P_{rr}=V_{bus}\,Q_{rr}\,f_{sw}=25.2\times40\text{n}\times48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.048\ W}$$
