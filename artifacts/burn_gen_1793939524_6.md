**Correction 1: the H743 should not be the ESC controller.**
Running ArduPilot on the H743 *and* closing 4 independent commutation loops at 48 kHz on the same MCU is not a sound design:

- It would require 4 × 3 complementary PWM pairs = 24 gate outputs. The H743 has only TIM1 and TIM8 as advanced timers with dead-time insertion (6 complementary pairs total).
- It would require 12 BEMF/current ADC channels with sub-microsecond commutation timing, alongside the EKF and scheduler.

The correct architecture is the industry-standard one:

- **H743**: runs ArduPilot and outputs 4 × bidirectional DShot600.
- **4 dedicated ESC MCUs**: one per motor, e.g. AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 firmware.
- Each ESC MCU drives a 3-phase gate driver (e.g. Fortior FD6288Q class) and 6 MOSFETs.

All analysis below follows this partition.

**Correction 2: 80 A on all four motors is a burst rating, not a continuous one.** The numbers below show a survivable window of roughly 4 seconds.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

$$R_{ds}(125^\circ C) = 1.2\ \text{m}\Omega \times 1.5 = 1.8\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Six-step with synchronous rectification

In 6-step commutation, two phases conduct at any instant:

- **Active (PWM) leg:** the high-side FET conducts for duty D and the low-side FET (synchronous rectifier) for 1 − D. Current always flows through exactly one FET of the leg.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET is held on continuously.

So the motor current always sees exactly two R_ds(on) in series.

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Each FET is active for 1/3 of the electrical cycle. Averaged, the per-FET loss is:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{2 I^2 R_{ds}}{6} = \frac{I^2 R_{ds}}{3}$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| Condition | P_cond per ESC | P_cond per FET (avg) | Instantaneous per conducting FET |
|---|---|---|---|
| T_j = 25 °C (1.2 mΩ) | 2·6400·0.0012 = **15.4 W** | 2.56 W | 7.68 W |
| T_j = 125 °C (1.8 mΩ) | 2·6400·0.0018 = **23.0 W** | 3.84 W | 11.52 W |

**PCB copper loss.** Add trace and plane resistance. A realistic 2 oz, 6-layer power path from battery pad to phase pad is about 0.3 mΩ round trip:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.0003 \approx 1.9\ \text{W per ESC}$$

**For FOC/sinusoidal drive** (all 3 phases conduct), use RMS phase current instead:

$$P_{cond} = 3\,I_{ph,rms}^2\,R_{ds}$$

The equivalent DC bus current gives a similar total, about 5–10 % lower.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Within that leg, the high-side FET hard-switches and the low-side FET soft-switches (its body diode conducts during dead time).

### 2.1 Hard-switching transition loss (V-I overlap)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

$$= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Body-diode reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 50\text{ nC}\times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

The reverse-recovery current also adds to high-side turn-on loss. Add roughly $\tfrac{1}{2}V_{bus}I_{rr}t_{rr}f$, typically about 0.1 W.

$$P_{dt} = V_f\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{ ns} \times 48\text{k} = 0.31\ \text{W}$$
