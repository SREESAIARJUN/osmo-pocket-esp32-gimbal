## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{hot} = 0.7\ \text{m}\Omega \times 1.6 = 1.12\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

At every instant, motor current flows through exactly two conducting devices:

- During the PWM ON interval: the high-side FET of the driven phase plus the low-side FET of the return phase.
- During the PWM OFF interval: the complementary low-side FET of the driven phase (synchronous rectification) plus the same return low-side FET.

So the per-channel conduction loss is independent of duty cycle, to first order:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{hot} + I_{rms}^2\, R_{Cu}$$

### 1.3 RMS current including ripple

The phase current ripple is:

$$\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}$$

Taking a typical 2207-class motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH line-to-line), the worst case D = 0.5, and V_bus = 25.2 V:

$$\Delta I = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{15\times10^{-6}\times 48\,000} = 8.75\ \text{A}_{pp}$$

The RMS current is:

$$I_{rms} = \sqrt{I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}} = \sqrt{6400 + 6.4} = 80.04\ \text{A}$$

Ripple is negligible at 48 kHz, which is one benefit of the high PWM frequency.

### 1.4 PCB copper resistance

Assume a 10 mm effective path at 6 mm width on 2× 2 oz layers in parallel (70 µm each):

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{wt} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8} \times 0.010}{0.006 \times 140\times10^{-6}} \approx 0.20\ \text{m}\Omega$$

Hot copper (+40 %) gives about 0.28 mΩ per path segment.

### 1.5 Result

| Term | Value |
|---|---|
| FET conduction, 2 × 80² × 1.12 mΩ | 14.34 W |
| Copper, 80² × 0.28 mΩ | 1.79 W |
| **P_cond per channel** | **16.1 W** |
| **P_cond, 4 channels** | **64.5 W** |

### 1.6 Per-FET distribution

Each phase conducts for 240° of 360°: 120° on the high side, 120° on the low side.

- The PWM-chopped high-side FET carries D·I² during its 120° window.
- Its complementary low-side FET carries (1−D)·I² during the same window.
- The return-path low-side FET carries the full I² for 120°.

**Hottest device:** the return low-side FET. Its average loss is:

$$P_{FET,max} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}\, I^2 R_{hot} = \tfrac{1}{3} \times 7.17 = 2.39\ \text{W}$$

This loss is spread evenly across the three low-side FETs over a full electrical revolution.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-chopped leg switches; one hard-switched transition pair occurs per PWM period.

### 2.1 V·I overlap loss

First, estimate the rise and fall times. The plateau voltage is V_pl ≈ 3.0 V and Q_gd ≈ 10 nC.

With R_g,ext = 4.7 Ω, R_g,int = 1 Ω, and driver V_DD = 10 V:

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{10 - 3}{5.7 + R_{drv}} \approx 1.0\ \text{A (driver-limited)} \;\Rightarrow\; t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g} = 10\ \text{ns}$$

$$I_{g,off} = \frac{3}{5.7} \approx 0.53\ \text{A},\ \text{with sink diode} \approx 0.8\ \text{A} \;\Rightarrow\; t_f \approx 12.5\ \text{ns}$$

Then the overlap loss is:

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 22.5\times10^{-9} \times 48\,000 = 1.09\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Remaining switching terms

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Output capacitance | Q_oss · V · f = 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.048 W |
| Reverse recovery (sync FET body diode) | Q_rr · V · f = 50 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.060 W |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_SD · I · 2t_dt · f = 0.8 × 80 × 2(100 ns) × 48 k | 0.614 W |
| Gate charge (dissipated in driver and R_g) | 2 · Q_g · V_gs · f = 2 × 50 nC × 10 × 48 k | 0.048 W |

The dead-time term is significant. Reducing t_dt from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel, but you must verify shoot-through margin on the scope first. In AM32 the parameter is `DEAD_TIME`.

### 2.3 Per-channel switching total

$$P_{sw,ch} = 1.09 + 0.048 + 0.060 + 0.614 + 0.048 \approx \mathbf{1.86\ W}$$

### 2.4 Frequency sensitivity

| f_sw | P_sw per channel | 4-channel total |
|---|---|---|
| 24 kHz | 0.93 W | 3.7 W |
| 48 kHz | 1.86 W | 7.4 W |
| 96 kHz | 3.72 W | 14.9 W |
