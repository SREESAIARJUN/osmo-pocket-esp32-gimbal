# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Systems Architecture Model (Job #28)

## 0. Architectural Corrections

Three points in the request need correcting before any numbers are meaningful.

| Issue | Reality | Consequence for this model |
|---|---|---|
| "H743 4-in-1 ESC" | ArduPilot runs on the H743 as the **flight controller**. Each ESC channel needs its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 does not commutate motors. | The design is an **AIO stack**: H743 FC plus 4 ESC MCUs plus 12 half-bridges. |
| "48 kHz switching" | ESC PWM frequency is set in **ESC firmware** (AM32 `PWM_FREQ`), not in ArduPilot. | ArduPilot only sets the DShot rate and loop rate. |
| "80 A burst on a nano board" | 4 × 80 A on 20×20 or 30×30 mm is thermally and electrically marginal. Battery current at full throttle would be about 320 A. | Section 3 quantifies this. 80 A is achievable only as a **≤1–2 s single- or dual-motor burst**. |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R = 1.2 mΩ × 1.383 = **1.66 mΩ**.

This creates a positive feedback loop: loss raises T_j, which raises R, which raises loss. It is solved iteratively in §3.4.
