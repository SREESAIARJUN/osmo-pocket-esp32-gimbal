# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC: Reference Architecture and Mathematical Model

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

### Architectural correction

The STM32H743 should be the **flight controller MCU**, not the ESC MCU. The motor-control loop at 48 kHz should run on four dedicated ESC MCUs, such as AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. The H743 talks to them over bidirectional DShot600. This is the only practical partition:

- ArduPilot cannot do sensorless field commutation.
- A single H743 cannot cleanly drive 24 FETs with BEMF zero-cross detection for 4 motors while also running EKF3.

### Feasibility warning

80 A on all four motors simultaneously, on a 20×20 or 30.5×30.5 board, is **thermally a burst rating of roughly 1–3 s**, not a continuous rating. Section 3 proves this.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j)=R_{DS,25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big],\qquad \alpha\approx 0.004\text{–}0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C with α = 0.0045: R_DS = 1.2 × 1.45 = **1.74 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

During any 60° sector, current flows through exactly two FETs at every instant:

- The PWM leg: the high-side FET (fraction D) or its complementary low-side FET (fraction 1−D).
- The return leg: a low-side FET held fully ON.

Assuming flat-top current (good for trapezoidal at high load), the total per-motor conduction loss is:

$$P_{cond,motor}=2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each phase is high-side active for 120° and low-side active for 120° per electrical cycle. With PWM on the high side only (lowest-switching-loss scheme), the time fractions are:

- High-side FET: D/3
- Low-side FET (as complement): (1−D)/3
- Low-side FET (as return): 1/3

$$P_{HS}=\tfrac{D}{3}I^2R_{DS},\qquad P_{LS}=\tfrac{(1-D)+1}{3}I^2R_{DS}=\tfrac{2-D}{3}I^2R_{DS}$$

The low-side FET is always the hotter device. Worst case is at low duty with high current, i.e. a punch-out from low throttle: P_LS → (2/3)·I²R.

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS | I²R | P_cond, motor | P_LS, worst (D→0) | P_HS (D = 1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.20 mΩ | 7.68 W | 15.4 W | 5.1 W | 2.6 W |
| 100 °C | 1.61 mΩ | 10.3 W | 20.6 W | 6.9 W | 3.4 W |
| 125 °C | 1.74 mΩ | 11.1 W | 22.3 W | 7.4 W | 3.7 W |

**Add copper loss.** A 2 oz trace or plane path of about 0.3 mΩ per phase at 80 A adds 2 × 6400 × 0.3 mΩ ≈ **3.8 W per motor**. Shunt loss (Section 1.5) is separate.

### 1.5 Current-sense shunt (per-ESC DC bus)

$$P_{shunt}=I_{bus,rms}^2 R_{sh},\qquad I_{bus,avg}\approx D\cdot I_{ph}$$

With R_sh = 0.25 mΩ, I_bus,rms ≈ √D·80 A, and D = 0.9:

P = 0.9 × 6400 × 0.25 mΩ ≈ **1.4 W**.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. Over a full electrical cycle, exactly one leg is switching at any time. The per-motor switching loss therefore equals one leg's switching loss.

### 2.1 Gate-charge-derived transition times

$$I_{g,on}=\frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{drv}+R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}}=\frac{10-3.5}{6}=1.08\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{g,off}=\frac{V_{pl}}{R_{drv}+R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}}=\frac{3.5}{6}=0.58\ \text{A}$$

$$t_r=\frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}}=\frac{12\,\text{nC}}{1.08}=11\ \text{ns},\qquad t_f=\frac{12\,\text{nC}}{0.58}=21\ \text{ns}$$

Add roughly 40% for loop-inductance di/dt limiting at 80 A. This gives t_r ≈ 15 ns and t_f ≈ 29 ns, so **t_r + t_f ≈ 44 ns**.

### 2.2 Loss components (one leg)

$$P_{sw}=\underbrace{\tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f}_{V\!-\!I\ \text{overlap}}+\underbrace{Q_{rr}V f}_{\text{reverse recovery}}+\underbrace{\tfrac12 C_{oss}V^2 f}_{\text{output cap}}+\underbrace{2\,V_{SD}\,I\,t_{dt}\,f}_{\text{dead-time diode}}+\underbrace{2\,Q_g V_{drv} f}_{\text{gate (driver)}}$$

Evaluated at V = 25.2 V, I = 80 A, f = 48 kHz:

| Term | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 44 ns
