## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation, two phases conduct at any instant. One leg is PWM-switched with complementary switching. The other leg is held on.

During PWM ON, current flows through the high-side FET of one leg and the low-side FET of the other. During PWM OFF, synchronous rectification commutates the current into the complementary FET of the switched leg. So for **every instant**, motor current flows through exactly **two** channel resistances in series, independent of duty cycle:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j)
$$

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$
R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

$$
R_{ds(on)} = 1.3\ \text{mΩ} \times 1.51 = 1.96\ \text{mΩ}
$$

### 1.3 Ripple correction

The phase current ripple in motor inductance is:

$$
\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph} \cdot f_{sw}}
$$

For a typical 5" motor, L ≈ 15–25 µH. At D = 0.5, V_bemf ≈ 12 V and L = 20 µH:

$$
\Delta I \approx \frac{(25.2 - 12)(0.5)}{20\ \mu\text{H} \times 48\ \text{kHz}} \approx 6.9\ \text{A}_{pp}
$$

$$
I_{rms} = \sqrt{I_{dc}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}} = \sqrt{80^2 + 3.96} \approx 80.02\ \text{A}
$$

The ripple contribution is negligible at 80 A, but it matters near zero load.

### 1.4 Result

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.96\ \text{mΩ} = \mathbf{25.1\ W\ per\ motor}
$$

With synchronous rectification, the high-side and low-side FETs of the PWM'd leg split this loss by duty cycle, D and (1−D). The always-on FET of the other leg carries its full share, ≈ 12.5 W. **That always-on FET is the thermal hot spot.**

Because commutation rotates every 60° electrical, each FET is "always on" for 1/3 of the time and PWM'd for 1/3. Time-averaged per FET, at D = 0.5:

$$
\bar P_{FET} = \frac{1}{3}(12.5) + \frac{1}{3}(0.5)(12.5) \approx 6.3\ \text{W}
$$

At burst timescales below ~10 ms, use the 12.5 W instantaneous figure for junction ripple.

## 2. Switching and Auxiliary Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg hard-switches.

**(a) V-I overlap.** The active FET hard-switches on turn-on and turn-off:

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\ \text{ns} \times 48\ \text{kHz} = 1.94\ \text{W}
$$

**(b) Output capacitance.** The common hard-switching estimate is:

$$
P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\ \text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\ \text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}
$$

**(c) Reverse recovery.** The body diode conducts during dead time, then is hard-commutated:

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\ \text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\ \text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}
$$

The Q_rr charge also adds to turn-on current overshoot. Budget an extra ~10–20 % on P_VI.

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction.** There are two dead-time intervals per period:

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\ \text{ns} \times 48\ \text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}
$$

**(e) Gate drive.** Two FETs switch per PWM period. This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET:

$$
P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \times 45\ \text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\ \text{k} = 0.043\ \text{W}
$$
