# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with Integrated 4-in-1 ESC: Systems Architecture (Job #16)

## 0. Architecture Correction and Assumptions

**Topology.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller (FC). It does not commutate the motors. Each of the four ESC channels has its own commutation MCU (assumed AT32F421 running AM32, or BLHeli_32-class firmware). The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to each ESC MCU and receives eRPM telemetry back on the same wire. All switching-loss math below therefore applies to the ESC MCUs' 48 kHz PWM, not to H743 timers.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification

In any 60° sector, one high-side (HS) and one low-side (LS) FET carry phase current I.

- **During the PWM on-time (D):** the HS FET of the driven phase conducts.
- **During the off-time (1−D):** with complementary (synchronous) PWM, the HS-partner LS FET conducts.
- **Each leg** is active for 2/3 of the electrical cycle. Each individual FET carries current for 1/3 of it.

Per channel, the instantaneous conduction loss is independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Per FET, averaged over the electrical cycle:

$$P_{cond,FET,HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,D, \qquad P_{cond,FET,LS} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,(2-D)$$

At D → 1 this simplifies to P = I²R/3 for every FET.

Temperature dependence:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

### 1.2 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C (R_ds = 1.5 mΩ)

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

$$P_{cond,FET} = 6400 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} / 3 = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

At T_j = 25 °C the channel loss is 12.8 W. This 1.5× swing between cold and hot is the feedback term that makes thermal runaway possible (see §3.4).

**PCB copper loss is not negligible.** Assume phase traces of 2 oz copper, 4 layers paralleled, 4 mm wide, 10 mm long. Copper sheet resistance at 2 oz is about 0.25 mΩ/□, which gives:

- R_trace ≈ 0.25 mΩ × (10/4) / 4 ≈ 0.16 mΩ per path
- Two paths in conduction ≈ 0.31 mΩ
- Copper loss ≈ 6400 × 0.31 mΩ ≈ **2.0 W per channel**

Add the current-shunt loss: 0.25 mΩ × 6400 = **1.6 W**. If 80 A bursts are required, use a 0.1–0.2 mΩ shunt or sense current from Rds/hall instead.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg is hard-switching per sector: the PWM'd high side, with its LS partner commutating. Below, I_sw is the current at the switching instant.

| Mechanism | Formula | Value at 25.2 V, 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw | 0.5·25.2·80·20 ns·48 kHz = **0.97 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2·t_dead·f_sw | 0.8·80·100 ns·48 kHz = **0.31 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 30 nC·25.2·48 kHz = **0.036 W** |
| Coss charging | Q_oss·V·f_sw (≈½CV² × 2 edges) | 40 nC·25.2·48 kHz = **0.048 W** |
| Gate drive (in driver, not FET) | Q_g·V_gs·f_sw·2 | 40 nC·10·48 kHz·2 = **0.038 W** |
| **Total switching per channel** | | **≈ 1.4 W** |

**The worst case is not at full throttle.** At D = 1 there are no PWM edges, so switching loss goes to zero. The real worst case is around D ≈ 0.7–0.9 with I near the burst value, for example a punch-out from a high-throttle cruise.

Combined worst case per channel (D = 0.9, I = 80 A, hot):

$$P_{ch} \approx 19.2 + 1.4 + 2.0 + 1.6 \approx \mathbf{24\ W}$$

**Frequency sensitivity.** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw:

| f_sw | P_sw per channel |
|---|---|
| 24 kHz | 0.7 W |
| 48 kHz | 1.4 W |
| 96 kHz | 2.8 W |

48 kHz is a reasonable compromise between current ripple, motor iron loss, and FET switching loss.
