## 0. Architecture Clarification and Design Basis

On a "4-in-1 ESC nano flight controller," the STM32H743 is the **flight controller** (ArduPilot). Each ESC channel is commutated by its **own** ESC MCU, normally an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz motor PWM. It sends DShot600 frames and receives bi-directional eRPM telemetry. The model below follows that split.

None of the following were specified, so they are stated as assumptions. Every equation is parameterized, so you can substitute real datasheet values.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification

At any instant, two phases conduct. Take phase A as the PWM leg and phase B as the low-side-on leg:

- **PWM leg (A):** the high-side conducts for D·T_sw and the low-side (synchronous) for (1−D)·T_sw. The phase current flows through exactly one FET of that leg at all times, apart from dead time.
- **Return leg (B):** the low-side is on continuously for the 60° sector.

So a full R_DS(on) is always in series twice in the current path:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot \big[\, D\,R_{HS} + (1-D)\,R_{LS} + R_{LS} \,\big] \;\approx\; 2\, I^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

To include ripple, use the trapezoidal RMS value:

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I_{dc}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12},\qquad \Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}
$$

For a typical 5" motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH, line-to-line), the ripple at 48 kHz is about 8–10 A_pp near D ≈ 0.5. The ripple term adds less than 0.2 % at 80 A, so it is neglected.

Temperature dependence:

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}\ \Rightarrow\ 1.5\times \text{ at } 125^\circ\text{C}
$$

### 1.2 Numbers at 80 A

| Condition | R_DS(on) | P_cond per channel = 2·I²·R |
|---|---|---|
| T_j = 25 °C | 1.5 mΩ | 2 · 6400 · 1.5 m = **19.2 W** |
| T_j = 125 °C | 2.25 mΩ | 2 · 6400 · 2.25 m = **28.8 W** |

### 1.3 PCB copper conduction (often forgotten)

The phase and bus path is roughly 25 mm per channel. With 4 parallel 2 oz layers, 4 mm wide:

$$
R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho\, l}{w\, t\, n} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot 0.025}{0.004\cdot 70\times10^{-6}\cdot 4} \approx 0.38\ \text{m}\Omega \;\;(\times1.4 \text{ at } 125^\circ\text{C} \approx 0.54\ \text{m}\Omega)
$$

This gives P_Cu ≈ 6400 · 0.54 m ≈ **3.5 W per channel**. The shared battery bus adds more, because it carries the sum of all four channels.

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution (hot-spot driver)

- Return-leg low-side, continuous within its sector: I²R = 14.4 W while active, averaged over 1/3 duty across sectors.
- PWM-leg FETs split D/(1−D). At D ≈ 0.5, each FET carries about 7.2 W while active.

Averaged over a full electrical cycle, each of the 6 FETs dissipates:

$$
\bar P_{FET} \approx \frac{P_{cond,ch}+P_{sw,ch}}{6}\;\text{(balanced)},\quad \text{but peak instantaneous} \approx 14.4\ \text{W}
$$

The peak instantaneous value matters for Z_th at the commutation timescale. At 3 kHz electrical frequency, the 333 µs period is far below the junction τ, so the average governs.
