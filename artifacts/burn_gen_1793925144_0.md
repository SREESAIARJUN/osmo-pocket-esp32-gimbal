# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #20): Design Model and Thermal Analysis

## 0. Assumptions and Design Corrections

Two points need settling before the math.

1. **The STM32H743 does not generate the 48 kHz bridge PWM.** In any ArduPilot-compatible 4-in-1 stack, the H743 is the flight controller. It sends DShot600 to four ESC MCUs (e.g. AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32), and those MCUs drive the gate drivers at 48 kHz. The design below is partitioned that way. Folding commutation into the H743 is possible, but ArduPilot does not support it, and it would remove the DShot interface you asked for.
2. **80 A cannot be sustained on a nano (20×20 mm) board.** Section 3 shows that 80 A is achievable only as a short burst of about 1 s, limited by the board's thermal capacitance. The continuous rating is about 20 A per motor. The model makes this explicit rather than hiding it.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Path

The bridge uses 120° trapezoidal commutation with synchronous (complementary) PWM. At any instant, the phase current flows through exactly two conducting channels:
- the high-side (HS) FET of one active phase, and
- the low-side (LS) FET of the other active phase,

where the PWM phase alternates between its HS and LS device. Because the current always passes through two R_ds(on) elements, the duty cycle cancels out of the total:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j) \qquad (1)$$

For a trapezoidal waveform at full load, I_rms ≈ I_pk (flat-top current). This makes equation (1) conservative.

### 1.2 Temperature Coupling

R_ds(on) rises with junction temperature:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)] \qquad (2)$$

Junction temperature in turn depends on total loss:

$$T_j = T_{amb} + P_{tot}(T_j)\,Z_{th}(t) \qquad (3)$$

Equations (2) and (3) form a fixed-point problem. Iterate until T_j converges, which takes about 3–4 iterations. If the iteration diverges, the design is in thermal runaway. The divergence condition is:

$$2I^2 R_{ds,25}\,\alpha\, Z_{th} \ge 1$$

### 1.3 Evaluation at 80 A, T_j = 110 °C

- R_ds = 1.5 mΩ × (1 + 0.005 × 85) = **2.14 mΩ**
- P_cond = 2 × 80² × 2.14 mΩ = **27.4 W per motor**
- Add copper/trace loss: 2 oz copper, about 0.3 mΩ per phase path, so 2 × 6400 × 0.3 mΩ ≈ **3.8 W**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

These losses are borne mainly by the device being PWM-switched in each commutation sector.

**Hard-switching overlap loss:**
$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**Output capacitance loss:**
$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss}V^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = 0.015\ W$$

**Reverse-recovery loss:**
$$P_{rr} = V Q_{rr} f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 30\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 0.036\ W$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period):**
$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I \cdot 2t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 9.6\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**Gate drive (HS and LS of the PWM phase):**
$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 0.048\ W$$

This gate-drive power is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FETs.

### Per-Motor Loss Budget at 80 A Burst

| Loss term | Power (W) |
|---|---|
| FET conduction | 27.4 |
| Copper | 3.8 |
| Switching + C_oss + Q_rr | 1.99 |
| Dead time | 0.61 |
| **Total** | **≈ 33.8 W** |

For all four motors: **≈ 135 W.**

The sensitivity is dominated by conduction: P ∝ I². At 48 kHz, switching is only about 7% of the loss. Raising f_sw to 96 kHz would add roughly 2.6 W per motor.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Junction to Board (Fast, Millisecond Scale)

The worst-case single FET carries about half the conduction loss of its pair during its sector. That gives roughly 13.7 W instantaneous:

$$\Delta T_{jc} \approx 13.7 \times 1.5 \approx 21\ K$$

This is acceptable.
