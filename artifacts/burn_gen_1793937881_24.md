## 0. Feasibility Finding

Before the derivation, one constraint shapes everything below:

> **4 × 80 A on a 20×20 mm nano form factor cannot be sustained continuously, and cannot be held as a burst for more than a few seconds.** The math in §2 and §5 shows about 130 W of total bridge dissipation at 4 × 80 A. A board with about 7 J/K of heat capacity reaches its copper and solder limits in about 3 seconds.

This document therefore defines **"80 A burst"** as:

- **≤ 3 s** per motor, all four motors simultaneously,
- followed by a cooldown to ≤ 60 °C board temperature.

The firmware current limit and thermal derating (§5.5) enforce this.

**Recommendation:** If the requirement is "80 A for 10 s or more", move to a **30.5×30.5 mm** board with 5×6 mm PQFN MOSFETs. Do this before the layout begins.

### Architecture Assumed

| Block | Implementation |
|---|---|
| Flight controller (FC) | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100), 480 MHz, running ArduPilot |
| ESC controllers | 4 × AT32F421K8U7 (or STM32G071), running AM32 with bidirectional DShot and EDT |
| Gate drivers | 4 × FD6288Q (3-phase, 20 V bootstrap) |
| Power stage | 24 × 40 V N-MOSFET, PQFN 3.3×3.3 **[A]** |
| Bus voltage | 6S LiPo: 25.2 V maximum, 22.2 V nominal |
| PWM | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous rectification) |

The H743 does **not** commutate the motors. It generates DShot600 and decodes the eRPM returned by bidirectional DShot.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Commutation Topology

With trapezoidal 6-step commutation and complementary PWM, two phases are active in each 60° sector:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for D·T_sw. The low-side FET (synchronous rectifier) conducts for (1−D)·T_sw.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET conducts for the full period.

Instantaneous conduction loss per ESC, for phase current I:

$$
P_{cond}=I^2R_{DS}(T_j)\,D + I^2R_{DS}(T_j)(1-D) + I^2R_{DS}(T_j) = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

The result does not depend on D, which is the key benefit of synchronous rectification.

### 2.2 Ripple Correction

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I_{avg}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12},\qquad
\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph,ll}\,f_{sw}}
$$

**Example:** a typical 5" motor with line-to-line inductance L_ll ≈ 20 µH, at D = 0.5 and V_bus − V_bemf ≈ 12 V:

- ΔI ≈ 12 × 0.5 / (20 µH × 48 kHz) ≈ **6.3 A**
- ΔI²/12 ≈ 3.3 A², which is negligible against 6400 A²
