# STM32H743 AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC): Engineering Model, Job #65

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Feasibility Flags

No component part numbers were specified. All numbers below come from the stated assumptions. Swap in real datasheet values before release. Every formula is parameterised so that substitution is mechanical.

| Parameter | Assumed value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage | 6S, V_bus = 25.2 V max (22.2 V nominal) | Requires 40 V FETs (≥1.5× margin) |
| Phase current | 80 A burst per motor, 4 motors | Burst means ≤5 s (derived in §1.6) |
| PWM frequency | f_sw = 48 kHz, center-aligned, complementary | AM32/BLHeli_32 style |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal, synchronous rectification | |
| MOSFET (per switch) | 40 V N-ch, PDFN 5×6 | R_ds25 = 1.0 mΩ @ V_gs=10 V, Q_g = 50 nC, Q_rr = 50 nC, C_oss = 1.5 nF, t_r = t_f = 20 ns at driver |
| Gate driver | 3-phase bootstrap driver, 10–12 V V_cc | e.g. FD6288Q class |
| ESC MCU | 1 × Cortex-M0+/M4 per motor (AT32F421 / STM32G071 class) | Runs AM32 or BLHeli_32 |
| FC MCU | STM32H743VIT6 @ 480 MHz, APB1 timer clock 240 MHz | ArduPilot ChibiOS |

**Feasibility flag.** 4 × 80 A on a "nano" (20×20 mm) board is not physically achievable. The FET and copper area alone exceed the footprint, and the H743 is LQFP100/TFBGA100. The minimum realistic format is a 30.5×30.5 mm AIO on 8 layers with 2 oz copper, plus a stacked or separate ESC board if 80 A is genuinely needed. This document models a **30.5 mm AIO** and treats 80 A as a **short-duration burst rating**, not continuous.

## 1. Loss Model (per motor, then ×4)

### 1.1 Conduction topology

In 6-step operation, one high side (HS) of phase X and one low side (LS) of phase Y are active. With complementary PWM on phase X, the current flows through:

- the HS of X for duty D, or the LS of X for (1−D),
- plus the LS of Y for 100% of the interval.

So **two FETs always carry I_ph**, independent of duty:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence of R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}\ (\text{typ. 40 V trench})$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.0 × 1.5 = **1.5 mΩ**.

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

At T_j = 25 °C this is 12.8 W. Because the loss rises with temperature, the operating point must be solved self-consistently (§1.7).

**Copper and connection loss** (pads, vias, motor-wire solder joints): budget R_path ≈ 0.4 mΩ per current path.

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.4\text{ m}\Omega = 2.56\ \text{W}$$

### 1.3 Hard-switching loss (48 kHz)

Only the PWM'd leg switches. The LS of Y is static.

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

Reverse recovery of the complementary FET's body diode:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{ k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

Output capacitance loss:

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw}\times 2 = 0.5\times1.5\text{ nF}\times 635\times 48\text{ k}\times 2 = 0.05\ \text{W}$$

Dead-time body-diode conduction (t_dt = 100 ns per edge, V_f = 0.8 V at 80 A):

$$P_{dt} = V_f I_{ph}\,(2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{ ns}\times48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

Gate drive, dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors:

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2\times50\text{ nC}\times10\times48\text{ k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 1.4 Per-motor and board totals at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

| Loss | W / motor | W / board (×4) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 19.20 | 76.8 |
| Copper / joints | 2.56 | 10.2 |
| Switching (V·I overlap) | 1.94 | 7.8 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 | 2.4 |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.16 | 0.6 |
| Bus-cap ESR (§1.5) | 0.50 | 2.0 |
| **Total** | **≈25.0** | **≈100 W** |

The ESC efficiency at a 2 kW electrical load (4 × 80 A × ~6.3 V average phase voltage at mid-throttle; varies with D) is not the right metric here. Heat flux is what matters.

**Frequency sensitivity:** ∂P/∂f_sw ≈ (1.94 + 0.61 + 0.16)/48 kHz ≈ **56 mW/kHz per motor**. Dropping to 24 kHz saves ~1.35 W per motor, about 5%. The burst problem is therefore **conduction-dominated (~85%)**. R_ds(on) and copper are the levers; f_sw is not.

### 1.5 Bus capacitor ripple

The worst case is D = 0.5:

$$I_{C,rms} = I_{ph}\sqrt{D(1-D)} = 80\times0.5 = 40\ \text{A}_{rms}\ \text{per motor}$$

The four ESCs run asynchronously, so their ripple adds roughly in RMS: √4 × 40 ≈ 80 A_rms total, split between on-board ceramics and the external low-ESR electrolytic.

- **On-board:** 8 × 10 µF/50 V X7R 1206 per motor. Effective capacitance is about 25% of nominal at 25 V DC bias.
- **External:** a mandatory 1000 µF/35 V low-ESR electrolytic at the battery pads.
