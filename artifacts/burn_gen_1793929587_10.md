# STM32H743 Nano AIO with 4-in-1 ESC: Thermal, Switching, and Bi-directional DShot Architecture (Job #53)

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

**Architecture assumption.** "STM32H743 4-in-1 ESC" usually means one of two things:

- **(A) Conventional AIO board.** The H743 runs ArduPilot. Four small ESC MCUs (AT32F421 or STM32G071, running AM32 or BLHeli_32) each drive one motor. The H743 talks to them over bi-directional DShot.
- **(B) Direct drive.** The H743 commutates the MOSFETs itself.

Bi-directional DShot only exists in (A), so this document assumes (A). If you intend (B), the DShot section is moot, and the H743's timer budget goes to 4 × 3-phase complementary PWM instead. That would need TIM1, TIM8, and HRTIM, and it is not supported by stock ArduPilot.

**Reality check on "80 A burst, nano (20×20 mm)".** The thermal model below shows this rating is achievable only for about 1 s with all four motors loaded, and a few seconds for a single motor. Continuous capability is roughly 20 A per motor. Treat 80 A as a marketing burst figure. Do not use it for design margin.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, two FETs are always in series with the motor current:

- **PWM on:** HS(x) and LS(y)
- **PWM off:** LS(x) (sync rectifier) and LS(y)

Total conduction loss per ESC is therefore independent of duty cycle, apart from dead time:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

### 1.2 At 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

R_DS(on) = 1.5 mΩ × 1.5 = 2.25 mΩ

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.25\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{28.8\ W\ per\ ESC}$$
