# STM32H743 AIO + 4-in-1 80 A ESC (20×20 "Nano")
## Job #55: Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, Bidirectional DShot600 Allocation and Reference Netlist

---

## 0. Architect's Feasibility Finding

The math below shows that **an 80 A burst rating on a 20×20 mm 4-in-1 board is only defensible as a sub-second, single-channel transient**. With all four channels at 80 A, the board dissipates about 200 W into roughly 2.3 J/K of thermal mass. That is a temperature rise of about 85 K/s with no realistic way to remove the heat.

The model is therefore parameterized so the burst *duration* can be derived from the rating rather than asserted. My recommended marketing and spec language is:

> **"35 A continuous / 55 A 10 s / 80 A peak (≤0.5 s, single channel, 25 °C ambient, ≥15 m/s airflow)"**

Alternatively, move to a 30×30 or 30.5×30.5 form factor with 5×6 mm FETs if a true 80 A burst is a hard requirement.

A second architectural point: the STM32H743 does **not** drive the MOSFETs. Each half of the system has its own controller:

| Function | Device | Firmware | Interface |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flight control | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100) | ArduPilot | Bidirectional DShot600, 4 lines |
| ESC ×4 | AT32F421K8U7 (QFN32) or STM32G071 | AM32 | 48 kHz complementary PWM |
| Gate drive | 3× half-bridge driver per ESC (e.g. FD6288Q tri-driver) | n/a | n/a |

The 48 kHz switching analysis applies to the ESC MCUs. The DMA and timer analysis applies to the H743.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Commutation topology

In six-step (trapezoidal) drive with synchronous rectification, at any instant one phase is PWM-active, one is held low, and one floats. Current flows as follows:

- **Active leg:** high-side FET for duty fraction D, low-side FET (synchronous) for 1 − D.
- **Return leg:** low-side FET for 100 % of the period.

Per channel, the conduction loss is:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

This holds because the current always traverses exactly two FET channels in series. The result is independent of D, which is a useful property for a worst-case bound.

FOC/sinusoidal drive gives the same bound when you substitute I_rms per phase and sum over three phases with sinusoidal weighting. The result is ≈ 1.5·I_pk²·R, which is slightly better. AM32 uses six-step, so the factor of 2 applies.

### 2.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

| T_j | R_DS(on) |
|---|---|
| 25 °C | 2.50 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 3.44 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 3.75 mΩ |

### 2.3 Parasitic copper resistance (this is significant)

Phase path: battery pad → bus planes → FET → phase plane → motor pad, about 15 mm effective length.

For 2 oz copper (70 µm), sheet resistance ρ_□ = 1.72e-8 / 70e-6 = 0.246 mΩ/□ at 25 °C.

The phase trace is roughly 4 mm wide × 15 mm long, or 3.75 squares, paralleled on 4 layers:

$$
R_{Cu} \approx \frac{3.75 \times 0.246}{4} \approx 0.23\text{ mΩ per segment}
$$

There are 2 segments (in and out) plus bus planes, about 0.6 mΩ total. At 80 °C this rises to about 0.73 mΩ.

$$
P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu}
$$

At 80 A this is 6400 × 0.73e-3 = **4.7 W per channel**, dumped directly into the PCB.

### 2.4 Conduction results per channel

| I (A) | T_j (°C) | P_FET,cond (W) | P_Cu (W) | Total (W) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 35 | 100 | 2·1225·3.44e-3 = **8.4** | 0.9 | 9.3 |
| 55 | 110 | 2·3025·3.56e-3 = **21.5** | 2.2 | 23.7 |
| 80 | 125 | 2·6400·3.75e-3 = **48.0** | 4.7 | **52.7** |

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the active leg switches, so there is one hard-switched commutation pair per PWM period. In each PWM period the high-side FET turns on hard while the low-side body diode is recovering, and the high-side turns off hard into the low-side body diode.

### 3.1 V·I overlap loss

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}
$$

At 80 A: ½ · 25.2 · 80 · 20e-9 · 48e3 = **0.968 W**

### 3.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_f \, I \, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw}
$$

At 80 A: 0.8 · 80 · 400e-9 · 48e3 = **1.229 W**

This is the largest switching term. Reducing dead time to 100 ns halves it, but requires verified driver propagation matching.

### 3.3 Reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw}
$$

50e-9 · 25.2 · 48e3 = **0.060 W**

In practice Q_rr scales with di/dt and I_F. Budget ×2 to get **0.12 W**.

### 3.4 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw}
$$

Using the E_oss approximation: 40e-9 · 25.2 · 48e3 = **0.048 W**

### 3.5 Gate drive

All 6 FETs see gate activity, but only the active pair switches at f_sw. Commutation-rate switching is negligible.

$$
P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw}
$$

2 · 30e-9 · 10 · 48e3 = **0.029 W**

Most of this dissipates in the driver and gate resistor, not the F
