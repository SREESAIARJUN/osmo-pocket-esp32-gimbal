- **Stabilization uses the IMU, not only the encoder.** The camera IMU gyro, projected into joint space, closes the velocity loop. The encoder is used for commutation, cogging lookup, and the joint-angle kinematics.

## 1. Field-Oriented Control (Innermost Loop, 20 kHz)

### 1.1 Electrical Angle and Delay Compensation

The electrical angle comes from the mechanical encoder angle:

$$\theta_e = p(\theta_m - \theta_{\text{off}}) \bmod 2\pi$$

The voltage computed at sample k is applied on average about 1.5 T_c later (computation delay plus PWM zero-order hold). The angle used for the inverse transform is therefore advanced:

$$\theta_e^{\text{inv}} = \theta_e + 1.5\,T_c\,\omega_e$$

### 1.2 Clarke and Park Transforms (Amplitude-Invariant)

Clarke transform, assuming a balanced three-phase system (i_a + i_b + i_c = 0):

$$i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}(i_a + 2i_b)$$

Park transform:

$$\begin{bmatrix}i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}$$

### 1.3 Plant Model in the dq Frame

$$v_d = R i_d + L\dot i_d - \omega_e L i_q$$

$$v_q = R i_q + L\dot i_q + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e\lambda$$

$$\tau = \tfrac32 p\lambda\, i_q = K_t i_q$$
