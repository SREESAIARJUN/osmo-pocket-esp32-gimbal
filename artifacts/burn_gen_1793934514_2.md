# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Systems Architecture Model

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and a Feasibility Warning

I don't have the requirements package for Job #34, so this model rests on the assumptions below. Every equation is parametric. Substitute your actual MOSFET datasheet values and the numbers will update directly.

| Parameter | Assumed value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max | Use 4S (16.8 V) if applicable |
| Board | 30.5×30.5 mm ESC footprint, 6-layer, 2 oz copper on all layers | "Nano" 20×20 makes it worse |
| MOSFET | 40 V, DFN3.3×3.3, R_ds(on),25 = 1.2 mΩ max @ V_gs = 10 V | Q_g = 40 nC, Q_rr ≈ 40 nC, Q_oss ≈ 50 nC, R_th,jc ≈ 1.5 K/W |
| Gate driver | FD6288Q-class 3-phase, ~10 V gate drive | Dead time ~100 ns |
| Switching frequency | 48 kHz, complementary PWM (active freewheeling) | |
| ESC MCU | AM32 or BLHeli_32 on STM32G071 / AT32F421, one per motor | |
| Commutation | Trapezoidal 6-step | |

**Architectural note:** The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz bridge PWM. Each ESC channel has its own MCU. The H743 runs the flight stack and sends bi-directional DShot600 to the four ESC MCUs. The two domains share only ground, V_bat, DShot lines, and telemetry/current sense.

**Feasibility warning (derived in §3):** Four channels at 80 A simultaneously dissipate about 90 W. On a 30×30 mm board, that reaches thermal limits in roughly 1–3 seconds even with prop-wash cooling. "80A burst" must be specified as a time-limited, per-channel rating, for example 80 A for 1 s or 60 A for 5 s. ESC firmware needs temperature-based current foldback to enforce it.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 105 °C:
$$R_{ds} = 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.40 = 1.68\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

At any instant, two FETs carry the phase current I:

- During PWM **on** (duty D): high side of phase A and low side of phase B conduct.
- During PWM **off** (1−D): low side of phase A (synchronous freewheel) and low side of phase B conduct.

Total bridge conduction loss per motor is therefore independent of D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical cycle. Per-FET average losses are:

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} D\,I^2 R_{ds}, \qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(2-D)\,I^2 R_{ds}$$

These sum over 3 high-side and 3 low-side FETs to 2I²R_ds. **Low-side FETs run hotter at low duty**, so place thermal vias and sense points there.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A burst

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.68\times10^{-3} = 21.5\ \text{W}$$

Worst-case per FET (low side, D ≈ 0.5):
$$P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1.5)(6400)(1.68\text{m}) = 5.4\ \text{W}$$

### 1.4 Copper and shunt conduction

| Element | Resistance | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase pad + trace (per path, two in series) | ~0.3 mΩ each | 2 × 6400 × 0.3m = 3.8 W |
| Current shunt (if per-channel) | 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| Bus input/ground copper (shared) | ~0.2 mΩ | scales with total I² |

Trace resistance uses R = ρL/(wt), with ρ_Cu = 1.72×10⁻⁸ Ω·m × (1 + 0.0039 ΔT).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

In 6-step operation only one leg hard-switches at a time, so per-motor switching loss equals one leg's loss.

### 2.1 Switching-loss terms

**V–I overlap (hard switching):**
$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 0.97\ \text{W}$$

Rise and fall times can be estimated from the gate-driver current and the Miller charge:
$$t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,R_{g,tot}}{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}$$

**Reverse recovery of the complementary FET's body diode:**
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.05\ \text{W}$$

**Output capacitance (Q_oss approximation):**
$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \times 2 = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (two dead-time intervals per period):**
$$P_{dt} = V_f\,I\,(2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$
