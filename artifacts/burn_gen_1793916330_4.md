## 0. Executive Finding: Read This First

The model in Sections 2–4 leads to one conclusion that drives the whole architecture.

> **A 20×20 mm "nano" board cannot sustain 80 A per channel, even as a burst, unless the burst is a few seconds or less and starts from a cool board.**

Summary of the numbers derived below (6S, 48 kHz, per-channel 80 A, all 4 motors loaded):

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Conduction loss (4 ch, Tj≈100 °C) | ≈ 51 W |
| Switching + dead-time + Qrr + Coss loss (4 ch) | ≈ 8.8 W |
| Shunt + copper losses | ≈ 6–10 W |
| **Total dissipation** | **≈ 66–70 W** |
| Steady-state convective capability, 20×20 board in prop wash | ≈ 4–6 W |
| Adiabatic heating rate (≈4.5 J/K lumped capacity) | ≈ 15 K/s |
| **Time from 45 °C to 110 °C board temperature** | **≈ 4–5 s** |

There are three viable resolutions. Choose one before schematic freeze:

1. **Redefine the rating** as "80 A for ≤ 2 s, 25 A continuous" on a 20×20 board. This is honest marketing and matches the physics.
2. **Change the form factor**: use a 30×30 4-in-1 (80 A burst / 50–55 A continuous is achievable) and a separate 20×20 H743 FC stacked on it.
3. **Lower the current target**: a 20×20 AIO at 35–40 A burst is realistic with 3.3×3.3 mm DFN FETs.

There is also a topology clarification. An H743 does not commutate motors directly in standard ArduPilot designs. The 4-in-1 needs **four ESC MCUs** (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32-class firmware). The H743 talks to them over bi-directional DShot600. The netlist in Section 6 reflects this.

## 1. System Parameters and Assumptions

| Symbol | Meaning | Baseline value |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | Battery voltage (6S full) | 25.2 V |
| I_ph | Motor phase current (rated) | 80 A burst |
| f_sw | PWM frequency | 48 kHz |
| D | PWM duty | 0–1 (worst-case loss analyses use D→1 for conduction) |
| R_ds,25 | FET on-resistance @25 °C, V_GS=10 V | ⟨param⟩, example 1.5 mΩ (60 V, 5×6 PDFN class) |
| α | R_ds temperature coefficient | ≈ 0.0045 /K (Si trench, ≈1.45× at 125 °C) |
| t_r, t_f | Current rise/fall (driver-limited) | 15 ns each |
| t_dt | Dead time per edge | 100 ns |
| V_SD | Body-diode forward voltage at 80 A | 0.8 V |
| Q_rr | Body-diode reverse-recovery charge | ⟨param⟩, example 50 nC |
| C_oss,er | Energy-related output capacitance | ⟨param⟩, example 1.5 nF |
| Q_g | Total gate charge @10 V | ⟨param⟩, example 40 nC |
| R_sh | Per-channel current shunt | 0.25 mΩ (or 0.5 mΩ, see 2.4) |

**FET voltage rating:** 6S at 25.2 V plus inductive commutation spikes of 8–12 V on a nano layout requires **≥ 40 V absolute, 60 V recommended**. 40 V parts are acceptable only with tight power loops and ≥ 470 µF low-ESR bulk capacitance plus distributed MLCCs.

**Topology:** six-step (trapezoidal) commutation with **complementary/synchronous PWM** (AM32 default). In any commutation sector, one phase is PWM'd (its HS/LS pair alternates) and one phase has its low-side FET held ON.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)]$$

Example at T_j = 100 °C:

$$R_{ds}(100) = 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega \times (1 + 0.0045 \times 75) = 2.01\,\text{m}\Omega$$
