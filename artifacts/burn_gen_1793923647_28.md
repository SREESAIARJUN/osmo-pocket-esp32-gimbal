## 0. Architecture Clarifications

Three points shape the rest of the analysis.

1. **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz bridge PWM.** In any ArduPilot-compatible 4-in-1 design, the H743 is the flight controller. Each motor bridge is driven by its own ESC MCU (typically AT32F421 or STM32G071) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The ESC MCU produces the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The H743 only emits bidirectional DShot600 and reads eRPM telemetry.
2. **"80 A" is defined here as per-motor phase current, burst.** At full throttle (D ≈ 0.95–1.0), DC-link current ≈ phase current. Four motors at 80 A therefore means **~320 A from the battery**.
3. **80 A per channel is not a continuous rating on a "nano" (≤ 30.5 × 30.5 mm hole pattern) board.** Section 3 shows it is a **seconds-scale burst rating**. That is the correct way to specify it.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

In 6-step synchronous PWM, exactly two phase legs are active at any instant. Using commutation sector A→B as the example:

- **PWM ON:** current flows through Q_AH and Q_BL, carrying I_ph.
- **PWM OFF (synchronous freewheel):** current flows through Q_AL and Q_BL, carrying I_ph.

So two FETs always carry the full phase current, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

R_DS depends on T_j, so this is a fixed-point problem. Evaluate it at the design limit **T_j = 125 °C**:

$$R_{DS}(125) = 1.0\,(1 + 0.0045 \cdot 100) = 1.45\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.45\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{18.56\ W}$$

**Per-FET distribution.** Each leg conducts for 240° of every 360° electrical. Each FET is active about 1/3 of the time on average.

- Average per FET: $\bar P_{FET} = 18.56/6 = 3.09$ W
- Instantaneous during its 120° window: 9.28 W

At typical eRPM, the electrical period is about 1–3 ms. That is shorter than the package-plus-board thermal time constant, so use the average for board-level analysis. Use the peak with Z_θJC(t) for junction ripple (§3.3).

**PCB copper loss (bus plus phase pours).** Assume 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner copper with R_cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ effective per channel path [ASSUMED]:

$$P_{cu,ch} = I_{ph}^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \cdot 0.3\times10^{-3} = 1.92\ \text{W}$$

Battery-pad and bus-bar loss is shared: at 320 A through ~0.2 mΩ, that adds **20.5 W**. This is why the battery pads need direct 10 AWG soldering and a solid multi-layer bus.
