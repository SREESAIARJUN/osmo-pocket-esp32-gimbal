# STM32H743 Nano AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC), Job #32: Systems Architecture Package

## 0. Architectural Corrections and Assumptions

Three points need correcting before the math is useful.

1. **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz motor PWM.** On every production 4-in-1 design, the H743 is the flight controller. It emits DShot600 and receives bidirectional eRPM telemetry. Each motor channel (or a single quad-core ESC MCU) runs AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware on a dedicated MCU such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071. That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM. This document models both domains separately.
2. **80 A burst on a 20×20 mm "nano" board is thermally marginal to infeasible beyond about 1 second.** Section 3 shows this numerically. Treat 80 A as a short transient (punch-out) rating, not a sustained one.
3. **Component parameters are representative assumptions, not a verified BOM.** Replace them with datasheet values from your selected MOSFETs before sign-off.

| Parameter | Symbol | Assumed value |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | V_bus | 6S, 25.2 V max (worst case for switching) |
| MOSFET | — | 40 V N-ch, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN, 6 per motor (24 total) |
| R_DS(on) @ 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | R₂₅ | 1.5 mΩ |
| R_DS(on) temp factor @ 125 °C | k_T | 1.5 → R_hot = 2.25 mΩ |
| Rise / fall time at gate drive | t_r, t_f | 15 ns each |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Per-leg conduction pattern (6-step, complementary PWM)

Each half-bridge leg cycles through three electrical intervals of 120°:

| Interval (1/3 period each) | HS FET | LS FET |
|---|---|---|
| Sourcing (PWM leg) | on for D | on for (1−D), synchronous rectification |
| Sinking (return leg) | off | on 100 % |
| Floating (BEMF sense) | off | off |

The per-FET losses in one leg follow directly:

$$P_{HS} = \frac{D}{3} I^2 R_{hot}$$

$$P_{LS} = \frac{(1-D)}{3} I^2 R_{hot} + \frac{1}{3} I^2 R_{hot}$$

Summing over 3 legs gives the motor total:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 3\cdot\frac{1}{3}I^2R\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I^2 R_{hot}$$

This is intuitive: two transistor positions always carry the phase current.

### 1.2 Numbers

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.25\text{ mΩ} = \mathbf{28.8\ W}$$

Copper trace and via resistance must be added. On a nano board, assume about 0.5 mΩ in the current path per motor:

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.5\text{ mΩ} \approx 3.2\ W$$

**Conduction subtotal per motor: ≈ 32 W. Four motors: ≈ 128 W.**

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution (for junction ΔT)

- **HS FET:** (0.95/3)·6400·0.00225 = **4.56 W** while its leg is active, averaging about 4.6 W.
- **LS FET:** (0.05/3 + 1/3)·6400·0.00225 = **5.04 W**.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the PWM-leg HS FET hard-switches. The LS FET soft-switches through its body diode during dead time.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ W$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.036\ W$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction (2 edges per cycle)

$$P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in the driver, not the FET)

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2 = 40\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = 0.038\ W$$

### 2.6 Switching total

| Term | W per motor |
|---|---|
| V·I overlap | 1.45 |
| Q_oss | 0.05 |
| Q_rr | 0.04 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.31 |
| Gate drive | 0.04 |
| **Total** | **≈ 1.9 W** |

> **Sensitivity.** Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it would be about 0.95 W. At 96 kHz it would be about 3.8 W. 48 kHz is a reasonable tradeoff for nano-class motors with low inductance (current ripple).

### 2.7 Grand total at 80 A burst

| Scope | Loss |
|---|---|
| Per motor | 32 + 1.9 ≈ **34 W** |
| 4 motors | **≈ 136 W** |
| + H743 core, regulators, ESC MCUs | ~1.5 W |
| **Board total** | **≈ 138 W** |

At 80 A × 4 × ~23 V ≈ 7.4 kW input, this is about 98 % efficiency. The efficiency is fine; the absolute heat in 4 cm² is the problem.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Junction-to-case (per FET, fast transient)

- R_θJC ≈ 1.5 K/W, so ΔT_JC ≈ 5 W × 1.5 = **7.5 K**.
- This term is not the limiter.

### 3.2 Board lumped thermal capacitance (20×20 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz)

| Element | Calculation | Heat capacity |
|---|---|---|
| FR-4 | 0.64 cm³ × 1.85 g/cm³ × 1.1 J/g·K | ≈ 1.3 J/K |
| Copper (8 × 70 µm) | 0.224 cm³ × 8.96 × 0.385 | ≈ 0.77 J/K |
| FETs, caps, solder | — | ≈ 0.5 J/K |
| **C_th total** | | **≈ 2.6 J/K** |

### 3.3 Board-to-ambient resistance

With prop-wash airflow (~5–10 m/s), a 20×20 board is about **R_θBA ≈ 8–12 K/W**.

- **Steady-state check:** 138 W × 8 K/W ≈ 1100 K rise. **80 A continuous is physically impossible on a 20×20 board.**
- **Sustainable continuous power:** (125 − 50 °C) / 8 K/W ≈ 9.4 W total. That corresponds to I_cont ≈ √(9.4 / (4 × 2 × 2.25 mΩ))
