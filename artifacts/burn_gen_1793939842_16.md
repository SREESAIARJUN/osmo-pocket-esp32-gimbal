# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #31): Power-Stage, Thermal and Bi-Directional DShot Design Package

## 0. Scope, Architecture and Assumptions

### 0.1 Architecture correction

ArduPilot does not commutate motors. The STM32H743 is the flight controller. It sends bi-directional DShot600 to four independent ESC MCUs, for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Those MCUs drive the gate drivers.

The H743 therefore never touches the gates. The design below keeps that split:

```
[H743 FC] --4x BDShot600--> [4x ESC MCU] --> [3x half-bridge drivers] --> [24x MOSFET]
          <--eRPM telemetry--
```

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation topology

Assume 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous complementary PWM:

- During t_on, current flows through HS(phase A), the motor, then LS(phase B).
- During t_off, the synchronous LS(A) takes over freewheeling, so current flows through LS(A), the motor, then LS(B).

At every instant, exactly **two** channels carry the full phase current I. This holds whatever the duty cycle, because the synchronous rectifier replaces the diode.

### 1.2 Bridge-level equation

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha(T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha = 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

### 1.3 Per-device split

Each phase is active for 120° of every 360° electrical. Within that window, at duty D:

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,D\,I^2 R_{ds},\qquad P_{LS,sync} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)\,I^2 R_{ds},\qquad P_{LS,return} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,I^2 R_{ds}$$

### 1.4 Numerical result at 80 A

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.50 mΩ | 2·6400·0.0015 = **19.2 W** | 76.8 W |
| 100 °C | 2.06 mΩ | **26.4 W** | 105.6 W |
| 125 °C | 2.25 mΩ | **28.8 W** | 115.2 W |

**Thermal runaway check.** Conduction loss rises with temperature: dP/dT_j = 2I²R₂₅α = 0.096 W/K per motor.

For stability, the loop gain dP/dT_j · R_th(j-a) must be below 1. R_th(j-a) for one motor's share of the board is about 16 K/W (from §3.3: 4 K/W × 4). The loop gain is then 0.096 × 16 ≈ **1.5**.

Since 1.5 is greater than 1, **80 A is not thermally stable in steady state. It can only be a burst rating.** This drives the burst-time model in §3.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing leg switches in 6-step drive: one HS/LS pair per motor at any instant.

### 2.1 Hard-switching overlap loss (HS turn-on and turn-off)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

**Gate-current sanity check:** I_g,on ≈ (V_cc − V_plateau)/(R_g,ext + R_g,int + R_drv). That is (12 − 3)/(3.3 + 1 + 3) ≈ 1.2 A.

The intrinsic transition time is then t ≈ (Q_gd + Q_gs2)/I_g = 10 nC/1.2 A ≈ 8 ns. Common-source and loop inductance (around 1–2 nH) typically stretch this to about **20 ns**, which is the value used here.

$$P_{VI} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery of the LS body diode (it conducts during dead time)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$

The added HS turn-on current spike (I_rr) adds roughly 10–15 % to P_VI. That is covered by the 20 % margin applied in §2.7.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = 2\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}C_{oss}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1\text{n}\cdot 635\cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.030\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f\,I\,(2t_d)\,f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot 80\cdot 100\text{ns}\cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.307\ W}$$
