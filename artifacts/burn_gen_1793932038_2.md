# STM32H743 AIO + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Loss Model, Bidirectional DShot Allocation, and Reference Netlist

## 0. Scope and Architecture Constraints

These constraints shape everything that follows.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production AIO, the H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot to four (or one quad-core) ESC MCUs running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical ESC MCUs are the AT32F421 and STM32G071. The 48 kHz figure is the ESC MCU's PWM frequency. The model is partitioned that way.
2. **80 A burst per channel is not credible on a 20×20 "nano" board.** The thermal math below shows roughly 25 W per channel at 80 A. A 20×20 board has about 1.2 J/K of thermal mass, which gives under 1 s of burst. I model a **30×30 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz** board as the minimum for an 80 A rating. A 20×20 variant should be rated at about 35–40 A burst.
3. Device parameters are **representative assumptions**, not a specific part number. Substitute datasheet values. The equations are general.

### Assumptions

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S, max) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Burst phase current | I | 80 A |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| MOSFET (40 V, PDFN 5×6) R_DS(on) max at 25 °C | R₂₅ | 1.2 mΩ |
| R_DS(on) temperature coefficient | α | ≈0.006 /K |
| Gate charge, total / Miller | Q_g / Q_gd | 50 nC / 10 nC |
| Driver source/sink (e.g. FD6288Q class) | I_g | ≈1 A |
| Output charge | Q_oss | 40 nC |
| Body-diode reverse recovery | Q_rr | 30 nC |
| Body-diode forward voltage | V_f | 0.8 V |
| Dead time | t_d | 100 ns per edge |
| Gate drive voltage | V_gs | 10 V |
| Board copper path resistance per channel | R_cu | 0.5 mΩ |

> **Note:** a 40 V FET at 25.2 V leaves about 15 V of headroom for switching ringing. That is acceptable only with tight commutation-loop layout and local ceramics. A 60 V FET is safer at the cost of roughly 1.5× R_DS(on).

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

In 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous (complementary) PWM, phase current always flows through **two FETs in series**:

- During the on-time: the high side of phase A plus the low side of phase B.
- During the off-time: the low side of phase A (synchronous rectification) plus the low side of phase B.

Dead-time intervals are handled in §2. The conduction loss is therefore:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I^2\, R_{DS}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

The loss is electrothermally coupled, so it is solved iteratively. At T_j ≈ 100 °C:

$$R_{DS}(100) = 1.2 \cdot (1 + 0.006 \cdot 75) = 1.74\ \text{mΩ}$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.74\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{22.3\ W\ per\ channel}$$

Copper loss on top of this:

$$P_{cu} = I^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \cdot 0.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

Thermal runaway check: the loss is stable if

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T} \cdot R_{th} < 1$$

Here ∂P/∂T = 2I²R₂₅α = 0.092 W/K. With R_th,board ≈ 3.7 K/W (see §3), the loop gain is 0.34. That is stable
