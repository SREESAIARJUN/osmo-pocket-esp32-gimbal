## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths

In 6-step commutation with complementary PWM, two phases are active in each sector, so exactly two FET positions carry the motor current I<sub>m</sub> at any instant:

- **On-time (D):** HS(A) → winding → LS(B)
- **Off-time (1−D):** LS(A) (synchronous rectification) → winding → LS(B)

Either way, the current path always contains two R<sub>DS(on)</sub> drops. The per-channel conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{m,rms}^2 \cdot \left(2R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + R_{Cu,path}\right)
$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence of R<sub>DS(on)</sub>

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ /\mathrm{K}
$$

At T<sub>j</sub> = 125 °C: R = 0.9 × 1.45 = **1.31 mΩ**.

Ripple correction for a low-inductance drone motor (L ≈ 15 µH phase-to-phase):

$$
\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{L\, f_{sw}}, \qquad I_{rms}^2 = I_{dc}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}
$$

Worst ripple occurs at D = 0.5 with V<sub>bemf</sub> ≈ D·V<sub>bus</sub>:

$$
\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{12.6 \times 0.5}{15\,\mu\mathrm{H} \times 48\,\mathrm{kHz}} = 8.75\ \mathrm{A} \;\Rightarrow\; I_{rms}^2 = 6400 + 6.4 = 6406\ \mathrm{A^2}
$$

The ripple contribution is negligible (0.1 %).

### 1.3 PCB copper path

Path geometry: about 8 mm per phase of 3 oz copper, 4 mm wide, on 2 layers in parallel.

$$
R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho\, l}{w\, t\, n} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\times 0.008}{0.004\times 105\times10^{-6}\times 2} \approx 0.16\ \mathrm{m\Omega\ per\ phase\ leg} \;\Rightarrow\; \sim0.33\ \mathrm{m\Omega\ per\ path}
$$

At 100 °C, copper resistance rises about 30 %, giving **0.43 mΩ**.

### 1.4 Result per channel (T<sub>j</sub> = 125 °C)

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 6406 \times (2\times1.31 + 0.43)\,\mathrm{m\Omega} = 6406 \times 3.05\,\mathrm{m\Omega} = \mathbf{19.5\ W}
$$

This splits into **16.8 W in the FETs** and **2.7 W in the copper**.

Per-FET instantaneous dissipation while conducting is 80² × 1.31 mΩ = **8.4 W**. Each FET conducts about 1/3 to 2/3 of the electrical period, depending on its position.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switched leg switches in each sector: one HS/LS pair per channel.

**Assumed device parameters:**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Q<sub>g</sub> | 45 nC |
| Q<sub>gd</sub> | 9 nC |
| Q<sub>oss</sub> | 55 nC |
| Q<sub>rr</sub> | 40 nC |
| V<sub>F,body</sub> | 0.8 V |
| R<sub>g,int</sub> | 1 Ω |

**Assumed gate drive:** 12 V supply, R<sub>g,ext</sub> = 4.7 Ω, effective I<sub>g</sub> ≈ 1.2 A during the Miller plateau.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard-switched HS)

$$
t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g} + \frac{Q_{gs2}}{I_g} \approx \frac{9\,\mathrm{nC}+6\,\mathrm{nC}}{1.2\,\mathrm{A}} = 12.5\ \mathrm{ns}\ \text{(each edge)}
$$

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_m (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\times25.2\times80\times25\,\mathrm{ns}\times48\,\mathrm{kHz} = \mathbf{1.21\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (LS body diode, after dead time)

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 40\,\mathrm{nC}\times25.2\times48\,\mathrm{k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}
$$

Note that Q<sub>rr</sub> grows with dI/dt; a value of 100 nC would give 0.12 W.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \times 2 = 55\,\mathrm{nC}\times25.2\times48\,\mathrm{k} = \mathbf{0.07\ W}
$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_F\, I_m\, (2\,t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\,\mathrm{ns}\times48\,\mathrm{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs:

$$
P_g = Q_g V_{dr} f_{sw}\times 2 = 45\,\mathrm{nC}\times12\times48\,\mathrm{k}\times2 = 0.05\ \mathrm{W\ per\ channel}
$$

### 2.6 Switching total per channel

$$
P_{sw,ch} = 1.21 + 0.05 + 0.07 + 0.61 \approx \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

Even at 48 kHz, switching is only about 9 % of conduction loss. At 80 A, the design is conduction-dominated. The design choices follow from that:

- **Pick FETs for R<sub>DS(on)</sub>, not Q<sub>g</sub>.**
- **Shorten dead time** to about 50 ns if the driver and layout allow. This saves about 0.3 W per channel.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Loss budget at 80 A burst

| Item | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 16.8 W | 67.2 W |
| Copper I²R | 2.7 W | 10.8 W |
| Switching + dead time | 1.9 W | 7.8 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, 80 A) | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| Driver + LDO + MCU | 0.15 W | 0.6 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 23.2 W** | **≈ 92.8 W** |

At 80 A and 6S (~22 V under sag), each motor draws about 1.7 kW, so stage efficiency is roughly 98.6 %. The efficiency is fine. The problem is **power density: about 93 W into roughly 7 cm²**.
