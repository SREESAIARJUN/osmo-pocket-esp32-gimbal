# STM32H743 AIO 4-in-1 ESC Nano Flight Controller (Job #8): Power-Stage, Thermal, and Bidirectional DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and a Feasibility Warning

This board has two compute domains:

- **Flight controller (FC):** STM32H743 running ArduPilot. It generates bidirectional DShot600 on 4 lines.
- **ESC section:** 4 × ESC MCUs (reference choice: AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32). Each drives a gate driver and 6 N-MOSFETs per motor, 24 MOSFETs total. The H743 never switches the bridges directly, because commutation timing must be local to each ESC MCU.

All component parameters below are **reference assumptions**. Replace them with your selected parts' datasheet values. The equations are written so you can substitute directly.

| Parameter | Symbol | Assumed value |

| Battery (6S, full charge) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Burst phase current per motor | I_pk | 80 A |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| MOSFET (40 V, PDFN 5×6 class) | R_ds(on),25 | 1.0 mΩ (at V_gs = 10 V) |

> ⚠️ **Feasibility flag:** A true "nano" (20×20 mm mounting) footprint cannot physically hold 24 × PDFN 5×6 MOSFETs (≈720 mm² of FET area against ≈400 mm² single-sided). Section 3 also shows that 4 × 80 A simultaneous is not a sustainable operating point at any small form factor. This document therefore assumes:
> - a 30.5×30.5 mm AIO PCB (8-layer, 2 oz); and
> - 80 A as a **per-motor, short-duration burst** rating.
>
> Treat the numbers as a design envelope, not a marketing spec.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step trapezoidal drive (AM32 complementary PWM)

At any instant, two phases conduct:

- **Sourcing leg (PWMed):** the high side (HS) conducts for duty D; the synchronous low side (LS) of the same leg conducts for 1 − D.
- **Sinking leg:** the LS is held on continuously.

So at every instant, **exactly two MOSFET channels carry the phase current** (ignoring the dead time, treated in §2.4).

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.0 mΩ × 1.5 = **1.5 mΩ**.
