# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC: 80 A Burst Electro-Thermal Model, 48 kHz Switching, and ArduPilot Bi-Directional DShot600 Allocation

## 0. Assumptions and Scope

These points shape every number below, so they come first.

1. **Architecture.** ArduPilot runs on the STM32H743, which is the flight controller. ArduPilot does not run on ESC MCUs. The 4-in-1 ESC therefore carries its own per-motor MCUs running AM32 or BLHeli_32, with representative parts such as the STM32G071 or AT32F421. The H743 drives these MCUs over bi-directional DShot600. This document models both sides.
2. **Representative parts.** No specific MOSFET or driver was given, so I use representative 40 V parts in a 3.3×3.3 mm PDFN package, sized for 6S. Replace the parameters in Table 1 with your datasheet values; every equation is written so you can substitute directly.
3. **Feasibility.** On a 20×20 mm "nano" board, 80 A per motor is only a **burst** (sub-second to roughly 1 s) rating. The thermal math in §3 shows why it cannot be sustained. Section 3.5 states this explicitly so it does not become a marketing figure your test data cannot support.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per motor)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

With complementary PWM, current always flows through exactly two FETs in series:

- **On-state:** high-side of phase A plus low-side of phase B.
- **Freewheel state:** low-side of phase A plus low-side of phase B.

The conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 \left(2\,k_T R_{ds(on),25} + R_{cu}\right)$$

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \times (2 \times 1.5 \times 1.0 + 0.5)\,\text{m}\Omega = 6400 \times 3.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{22.4\ W}$$

This splits into:

- **FETs:** 19.2 W.
- **Copper:** 3.2 W.

Note that the copper loss is non-trivial on a nano board. Its 3.2 W is spread across the board and contributes directly to board heating.

**Per-FET average.** Over one electrical cycle, each leg's high-side and low-side FET each conduct for 1/3 of the time with PWM sharing. A useful approximation is P_FET,avg ≈ 19.2 W / 6 = 3.2 W. During any 60° sector, however, the low-side FET of the "B" phase carries the full 6400 A²·1.5 mΩ = **9.6 W**. Use 9.6 W for junction transient checks, because the commutation sector at low eRPM can last milliseconds.

### 2.2 Hard-switching (V–I overlap) losses

Only the active high-side FET hard-switches. Its synchronous partner switches at near-zero voltage after the dead time.

$$t_{r} \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_g} = \frac{15\,\text{nC}}{1.5\,\text{A}} = 10\,\text{ns}$$

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

In practice, parasitic source inductance (~1–2 nH in PDFN) slows di/dt. Budget a ×1.5 to ×2 multiplier, giving **1.5–1.9 W**.

### 2.3 Reverse recovery, C_oss, dead-time diode conduction, and gate drive

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss,er} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\,\text{nF} \times 635 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.023\ \text{W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead-time intervals per PWM period):

$$P_{dt} = V_f I_{ph} (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

**Gate drive** (two FETs actively toggling; this loss lands in the driver, not the FETs):

$$P_{gate} = 2\, Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\,\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.058\ \text{W}$$

> **Design lever:** Dead-time loss exceeds V–I switching loss here. Reducing t_dt from 200 ns to 100 ns saves 0.6 W per motor (2.5 W per board). Verify shoot-through margin on the bench with a current probe before shipping it.

### 2.4 Loss summary at 80 A burst

**Table 2: Loss budget**

| Term | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Copper conduction | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| V–I switching (×1.5 parasitics) | 1.45 W | 5.8 W |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 W | 4.9 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.07 W | 0.28 W |
| Gate drive | 0.06 W | 0.23 W |
| **Total** | **≈25.2 W** | **≈101 W** |
