# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Electrical, Thermal and Firmware Integration Model

**Architecture, power stage and ArduPilot integration reference**

---

## 0. Feasibility Warnings

Three issues should be settled before layout starts.

1. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32, BLHeli_32 or Bluejay. Typical parts are the AT32F421 and STM32G071. That MCU performs commutation and generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 only sends DShot600 frames and receives eRPM telemetry. The model below follows this split.
2. **An 80 A burst on a nano (20×20 mm) board exceeds the norm by about 3–4×.** Nano AIOs typically handle 12–25 A per motor. The analysis in §2 shows the requested burst produces about 85–90 W of heat into roughly 2 J/K of thermal mass, which allows about 1.5 s of burst. Treat 80 A as a short transient rating, not a continuous one. Alternatively, move to a 30.5×30.5 mm board or a split ESC stack.
3. **All component parameters below are representative assumptions.** Replace them with datasheet values for the chosen parts before sign-off.

## 1. Design Assumptions

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S, full charge) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Burst phase current per motor | I_pk | 80 A |
| PWM frequency (ESC firmware) | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| MOSFET (30–40 V, DFN3.3×3.3, 1 FET per switch position) | R_ds(on),25 | 1.0 mΩ |
| R_ds(on) temperature coefficient (25→125 °C) | k_T | 1.5 |
| Rise / fall time at 80 A (FD6288-class driver, ~5 Ω gate resistor) | t_r, t_f | 20 ns, 20 ns |
| Gate charge | Q_g | 50 nC |
| Gate drive voltage | V_gs | 10 V (FD6288 VCC; can be up to 12 V) |
| Output capacitance (effective) | C_oss,eff | 1.0 nF |
| Body diode recovery charge | Q_rr | 30 nC |
| Body diode forward voltage | V_f | 0.8 V |
| Dead time | t_dt | 50 ns |
| Current-sense shunt (bus, per ESC) | R_sh | 0.25 mΩ |

**Commutation model:** 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM, as AM32 and BLHeli_32 use by default. At every instant, one high-side and one low-side conduction path carry I_pk. With synchronous rectification, the PWM leg alternates between its high-side and low-side FETs, so current always flows through exactly two R_ds(on).

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

$$P_{cond} = I_{pk}^2 \cdot 2 \cdot R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) \approx R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ /K$$

At T_j = 125 °C, R = 1.5 mΩ:

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.5\text{ mΩ} = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

At T_j = 25 °C (start of burst), P_cond = 12.8 W. Conduction loss has positive feedback with temperature, so iterate it together with §3.

**Per-FET distribution:** Each FET position conducts for roughly 1/3 of the electrical cycle, and the PWM-leg pair shares time in a D : (1−D) ratio. The electrical period at typical eRPM (≥1 kHz electrical) is far shorter than the junction thermal time constant (tens of ms). Therefore the per-FET average applies:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx P_{cond}/6 \approx 3.2\text{ W}$$

The worst-case low-speed or stall condition is different. A single FET carries the full 80 A continuously:

$$P_{FET,stall} = 80^2 \times 1.5\text{ mΩ} = 9.6\text{ W}$$

This is the case that must be protected by firmware current limiting.

### 2.2 Hard-Switching (V–I Overlap) Loss

Only the PWM-leg high-side FET hard-switches. The synchronous low-side FET turns on into body-diode conduction, which is a near-ZVS transition.

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{pk} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

$$P_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.3 Dead-Time Body-Diode Loss

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM period:

$$P_{dt} = V_f I_{pk} (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}$$

### 2.4 Reverse-Recovery Loss

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}$$

Q_rr rises with di/dt and temperature. Apply a 2× margin, giving about 0.07 W.

### 2.5 Output Capacitance Loss

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,eff} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1\text{ nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.015\ W}$$

### 2.6 Gate Drive Loss

This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET. Two FETs switch per PWM period.

$$P_{g} = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{ nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{ k} = 0.048\text{ W}$$

### 2.7 Shunt Loss

$$P_{sh} = I^2 R_{sh} = 6400 \times 0.25\text{ mΩ} = \mathbf{1.6\ W}$$

The bus current equals D·I_phase. At high throttle D ≈ 1, so the full value is used conservatively.

### 2.8 Copper and Trace Loss

Assume a phase path plus a bus path of 15 mm, 4 layers × 2 oz (70 µm), 3 mm wide:

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{A} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8} \times 0.015}{4 \times 70\mu \times 3\text{ mm}} \approx 0.31\text{ mΩ}$$

With two paths: P_Cu ≈ 6400 × 0.6 mΩ ≈ **3.9 W**. This term is often forgotten and is significant at 80 A.

### 2.9 Loss Summary at 80 A Burst, T_j = 125 °C

| Term | Per ESC (W) | 4× ESC (W) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.20 | 76.8 |
| Switching overlap | 1.94 | 7.8 |
| Dead time | 0.31 | 1.2 |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.09 | 0.4 |
| Gate drive | 0.05 | 0.2 |
| Shunt | 1.60 | 6.4 |
| PCB copper | 3.90 | 15.6 |
| **Total** | **≈ 27.1** | **≈ 108** |

**Efficiency at burst:** P_out ≈ 25.2 × 80 = 2016 W per motor, so η ≈ 98.7%. The efficiency is good. The problem is power density: 108 W on a 4 cm² board.
