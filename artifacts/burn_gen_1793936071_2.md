## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

**Who does what.** ArduPilot running on the STM32H743 does **not** commutate motors. It emits **bi-directional DShot600** to four ESC MCUs and receives eRPM telemetry back. Each ESC MCU runs AM32 or BLHeli_32 on a device such as an STM32G071 or AT32F421, and drives its own 3-phase bridge at 48 kHz. The model is therefore split:

| Domain | Owner | Covered in |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction and switching losses at 80 A, 48 kHz | ESC power stage (×4) | §1–§3 |
| Thermal transient / burst capability | ESC PCB + FETs | §4 |
| DShot600 BDShot timer/DMA | STM32H743 / ArduPilot | §5 |
| Netlists | Both | §6 |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation-state current path

In 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous (complementary) rectification, one commutation sector looks like this, for example A+ / B−:

- **PWM-on (duty D):** current flows through Q_AH, the motor, then Q_BL.
- **PWM-off (1−D):** current freewheels through Q_AL (synchronous), the motor, then Q_BL.
- Q_BL conducts for 100% of the sector.

At every instant, exactly **two** channels carry I_ph:

$$
P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

The result is **independent of duty cycle**, which is a useful design property.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond per ESC @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 2·6400·1.00e-3 = **12.8 W** |
| 85 °C | 1.33 mΩ | **17.0 W** |
| 125 °C | 1.55 mΩ | **19.8 W** |

### 1.3 Parasitic copper and shunt

Add the series resistance of the PCB path:

$$
P_{Cu} = I_{ph}^2 (R_{trace} + R_{shunt})
$$

With 2 oz inner/outer stacking, R_trace ≈ 0.3 mΩ, plus a 0.25 mΩ shunt (if per-phase sensing is used):

$$
P_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.55\text{e-}3 = \mathbf{3.5\ W}
$$

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution (hot-spot driver)

Per FET, averaged over the six sectors:

- Each FET conducts as a "static" low-side for 1/3 of the electrical period.
- It also participates as a PWM high- or low-side for 1/3 of the period.

Peak instantaneous dissipation in the fully-on FET is I²R = 6400 × 1.55e-3 = **9.9 W**. The average per FET is about 19.8 W / 6 × (share factor) ≈ **3.3 W** at 125 °C.

At low eRPM, for example a locked rotor or punch-out from a hover, the sector lasts milliseconds. A single FET then sees the **peak 9.9 W**. This defines the Z_th(t) requirement in §4.3.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only one half-bridge switches per sector, so switching loss is counted for **one leg**.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard-switched turn-on and turn-off)

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r + t_f) f_{sw}
= 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\text{e-}9 \cdot 48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 60\text{e-}9 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{0.073\ W}
$$

Qrr increases with di/dt and temperature. Apply a ×2 derating, giving 0.15 W.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 50\text{e-}9 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{0.030\ W}
$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD} I_{ph} (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\text{e-}9 \cdot 48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

This term is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET.

$$
P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2\ \text{(HS+LS of switching leg)} = 60\text{e-}9 \cdot 10 \cdot 48\text{e}3 \cdot 2 = \mathbf{0.058\ W}
$$

### 2.6 Switching total per ESC

$$
P_{sw} = 1.94 + 0.15 + 0.03 + 0.61 = \mathbf{2.73\ W}\ (+0.06\ W\ \text{in driver})
$$

**Sensitivity:**

- ∂P_sw/∂f_sw ≈ 57 µW/Hz. Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.4 W per ESC, at the cost of acoustic noise and current ripple.
- Reducing dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns saves 0.3 W.

## 3. Total Loss Budget at 80 A Burst

| Term | Per ESC (T_j = 125 °C) | ×4 (all motors at 80 A) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (2I²R) | 19.8 W | 79.2 W |
| Copper + shunt | 3.5 W | 14.0 W |
| Switching | 2.7 W | 10.9 W |
| Driver/gate | 0.06 W | 0.25 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 26.1 W** | **≈ 104 W** |

**Efficiency check.** Input power is P_in = 25.2 × 80 × D. At D ≈ 0.9 that is about 1815 W per channel, so loss is about 1.4%. This is electrically reasonable. **The problem is thermal density, not efficiency.**

**Self-consistent junction temperature.** Iterate:

$$
T_j^{(n+1)} = T_{amb} + R_{th,ja}\,P(T_j^{(n)})
$$

Steady state diverges for the nano footprint (see §4.2). This confirms that 80 A is valid only as a transient rating.
