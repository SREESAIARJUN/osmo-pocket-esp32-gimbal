# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #10): Loss, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

## 0. Feasibility Findings

These results drive the rest of the design, so they come first.

1. **An 80 A per-channel burst is not thermally sustainable on a nano (20×20 mm) board.** The model below shows about 107 W of semiconductor loss at 4×80 A. With realistic prop-wash cooling and no heatsink, the board reaches MOSFET Tj(max) in about **1.2 s**. Sustained, the board can dissipate only about **6–7 W**. For the target to be credible, it needs one of the following:
   - a 30×30 mm or larger ESC board,
   - a bonded heatsink, or
   - a redefined spec, e.g. "80 A for ≤1 s, 35–40 A continuous."
2. **4×80 A means about 320 A battery current at full duty.** Battery wiring, XT60 connectors, and bulk capacitance cannot support this on a nano build.
3. **Partitioning:** the H743 is the flight controller. Each ESC channel is commutated by its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32 (or a single ESC MCU for all four). The H743 only generates bidirectional DShot600 and decodes eRPM telemetry. The DShot section is written for that split.

All device parameters below are **stated assumptions** for a generic 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN N-channel MOSFET class. Substitute your selected part's datasheet values into the equations.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

**Temperature-dependent on-resistance:**

R_ds(T_j) = R_ds,25 · [1 + α(T_j − 25)]

At 125 °C: R_ds = 1.2 mΩ × 1.6 = **1.92 mΩ**

**Commutation topology.** The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. At every instant the phase current flows through **two** conducting MOSFETs in series:
- the HS or LS device of the PWM leg, and
- the fully-on device of the return leg.

Independent of duty cycle:

P_cond,ch = 2 · I_ph² · R_ds(T_j)

| T_j | P_cond per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 2·6400·1.2 mΩ = **15.4 W** | 61.4 W |
| 125 °C | 2·6400·1.92 mΩ = **24.6 W** | 98.3 W |

**Copper loss** must be added to this. A 2 oz, 3 mm wide, 15 mm phase trace has R ≈ 2.4 mΩ, which adds about 15 W per channel. Use polygon pours on multiple layers stitched with vias to bring this down to about 0.3 mΩ, or about 2 W per channel.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In 6-step operation only one leg hard-switches per PWM period.

**(a) V–I overlap loss**

P_VI = ½ · V_bus · I_ph · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw
= 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 30 ns × 48 kHz = **1.45 W**

**(b) Reverse recovery loss**

P_rr = Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw = 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.048 W**

**(c) Output capacitance loss**

P_oss = ½ · C_oss · V_bus² · f_sw = **0.023 W**

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction**

P_dt = V_f · I_ph · (2 t_dt) · f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k = **0.61 W**

This term matters. Reducing dead time to 50 ns halves it, but dead time must stay above the gate driver's propagation mismatch.

**(e) Gate drive loss** (two FETs per period, dissipated in the driver and gate resistors)

P_g = 2 · Q_g · V_GS · f_sw = **0.048 W**

**Switching total:**

P_sw,ch ≈ 1.45 + 0.05 + 0.02 + 0.61 + 0.05 = **2.18 W per channel**

**Total semiconductor loss at T_j = 125 °C:**

P_ch = 24.6 + 2.18 = **26.8 W per channel**, **107 W for the full board**

**Frequency trade-off.** Switching loss scales linearly with frequency. Moving to 24 kHz saves about 1.1 W per channel, which is a minor gain because conduction loss dominates. The 48 kHz choice is justified by lower acoustic noise and current ripple.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Junction-to-board (per FET)

The hottest FET carries about half the channel loss, roughly 13.4 W. With R_th(j-c) ≈ 1.5 K/W (assumed):

ΔT_jc ≈ 13.4 × 1.5 = **20 K**

### 4.2 Lumped board heat capacity (20×20 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz)

| Material | Calculation | Heat capacity |
|---|---|---|
| Copper | 400 mm² × 0.42 mm = 0.168 cm³ → 1.5 g × 0.385 J/g·K | ≈ 0.58 J/K |
| FR-4 | 0.64 cm³ → 1.18 g × 1.1 J/g·K | ≈ 1.30 J/K |
| FETs and solder | | ≈ 0.1 J/K |
| **Total C_th** | | **≈ 2.0 J/K** |

### 4.3 Board-to-air

Assume h ≈ 100 W/m²·K (prop wash) and a two-sided area of 8×10⁻⁴ m².

R_th,ba = 1 / (h·A) = **12.5 K/W**
τ = R·C ≈ **25 s**

### 4.4 Transient response

Single-pole model:

T_board(t) = T_amb + P·R·(1 − e^(−t/τ))

**Allowed board rise:** 125 − 40 − 20 (ΔT_jc) = **65 K**

**Time to T_j,max:**

t_burst = −τ · ln(1 − ΔT_allowed / (P·R))
= −25 · ln(1 − 65 / 1340)
≈ **1.24 s**

This is optimistic, because R_ds is evaluated at 125 °C throughout. In practice the first part of the burst runs cooler, so the true time is about 1.3–1.5 s.

**Sustained capability:**

P_max = 65 / 12.5 ≈ **5.2 W total** → about **1.3 W per channel** → I_cont ≈ **18 A per channel**

### 4.5 Recommended thermal derating

- Firmware current limit: 80 A for ≤1.0 s, then fold back to 30 A, using an I²t integrator in AM32.
- ESC NTC on the hottest FET: begin linear derating at 90 °C, cut at 110 °C.
- Report the ESC temperature through extended DShot telemetry (EDT), and use ArduPilot `ESC_TLM` logging and `BATT_` failsafe.
- Optional heatsink: a 0.5 mm Al plate with thermal pad gives about 5 J/K added capacity and roughly 3× burst time.

### 4.6 Bus capacitor ripple

I_C,rms ≈ I_ph · √(D(1−D)) ≤ 40 A RMS per channel (at D = 0.5).

Four interleaved channels partly cancel, but this still requires:
- per-channel 4× 10 µF/50 V X7R ceramic capacitors, and
- a ≥1000 µF low-ESR electrolytic on the battery leads.
