# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #46): Power-Stage and DShot Engineering Model

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Architecture Corrections

I don't have Job #46's BOM, stack-up, or mechanical drawings. Every number below comes from **stated assumptions**. All equations are parameterized so you can substitute your real datasheet values. Treat the netlists as **reference-design netlists for schematic capture**, not fab-ready output.

**Architecture clarification.** An STM32H743 running ArduPilot does **not** commutate the motors.

- ArduPilot has no ESC firmware.
- The 4-in-1 ESC carries **four dedicated ESC MCUs** running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. Typical parts are the AT32F421 or STM32G071.
- Each ESC MCU drives its own 3-phase gate driver and 6 MOSFETs at 48 kHz.
- The H743 sends **bidirectional DShot600** to those MCUs and receives eRPM telemetry back on the same wire.

The model therefore splits into two parts:

- **ESC side:** power-stage thermal and switching model.
- **FC side:** H743 timer and DMA allocation for DShot.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous PWM

In any commutation sector, two phases conduct and one floats. Current I_ph flows:

- Through the high-side or low-side FET of the **PWM leg**, chopped at duty D. With synchronous rectification, the current always flows through a channel, not the diode, except during dead time.
- Through the static FET of the **return leg**, continuously.

So at every instant exactly **two channels** carry the phase current.

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Ripple-corrected RMS current

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{LL}\,f_{sw}}$$

Worst case is D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\,\mu\text{H} \times 48\,\text{kHz}} = 6.56\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{dc}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.6 = 6403.6\ \text{A}^2$$

Ripple adds only 0.06 % at 48 kHz. At 24 kHz it would be 4× larger, which is still minor. Ripple matters mainly for motor iron loss and capacitor stress, not FET conduction.

### 1.3 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on), solved self-consistently

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)]$$

Because R_DS(on) depends on T_j and T_j depends on loss, the loss is a fixed point. Iterate:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_{board} + P_{FET}\big(R_{DS}(T_j^{(k)})\big)\,\theta_{JB}$$

Converged result (§3) at T_j ≈ 115 °C:

$$R_{DS} = 0.9 \times [1 + 0.005 \times 90] = 1.305\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 6403.6 \times 1.305\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{16.7\ W}$$

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

Each FET conducts for 120° electrical, which is 1/3 of the cycle, in its active role. Averaged over an electrical cycle, the 2-channel loss spreads evenly across all 6 FETs:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{2 I^2 R}{6} = \frac{I^2 R}{3} = \mathbf{5.57\ W\ per\ FET}$$

This is averaged. The **instantaneous** loss in a conducting FET is I²R = 8.35 W. That figure matters if electrical frequency is low (startup or stall), because the junction then tracks instantaneous power rather than the average. See §3.4.
