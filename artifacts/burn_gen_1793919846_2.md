## 0. Baseline Assumptions & Architectural Clarification

**Important architectural point.** On every production AIO of this class, the STM32H743 does **not** generate the 48 kHz motor PWM. It runs ArduPilot and sends DShot600 to four ESC MCUs (typically AT32F421 / STM32G071 running AM32, or EFM8 running BLHeli_S/Bluejay). Those MCUs generate the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The rest of this document follows that split.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0065\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At the 125 °C design point:

$$R_{ds,hot} = 1.6 \times 1.65 = 2.64\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Six-step current paths

At any instant, two phase legs carry current and the third floats. With complementary PWM:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side (HS) FET conducts for a fraction D of each period, and the low-side (LS) FET conducts for 1 − D.
- **Static leg:** the LS FET is held fully on for that 60° sector.

Each leg is active for 2/3 of the electrical cycle, half of that as the PWM leg and half as the static leg (AM32 default rotation).

**Per-FET average conduction loss:**

$$P_{cond,HS} = I^2 R_{hot}\cdot D \cdot \tfrac{1}{3}$$

$$P_{cond,LS} = I^2 R_{hot}\cdot\left[(1-D)\cdot\tfrac{1}{3} + \tfrac{1}{3}\right]$$

**Numbers at 80 A** (I²R_hot = 6400 × 2.64e-3 = 16.9 W):

| Device | Expression | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| HS FET | 16.9 × 0.9 × 1/3 | **5.07 W** |
| LS FET | 16.9 × (0.1 + 1) × 1/3 | **6.20 W** |
| Per motor (3 HS + 3 LS) | 3 × (5.07 + 6.20) | **33.8 W** |

Cross-check: two FETs are always in series with the current, so the motor total is 2·I²R = 33.8 W ✓.

### 1.3 Copper (PCB) conduction loss

Assume a phase path of roughly 15 mm at 6 mm effective width through 2 oz copper, paralleled on 4 layers:

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{w t} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot 0.015}{0.006\cdot 70\times10^{-6}\cdot 4} \approx 0.15\ \text{m}\Omega$$

Including the shared bus planes, budget about 0.3 mΩ per current path:

$$P_{Cu} \approx 2 \cdot I^2 \cdot 0.3\text{ m}\Omega = 3.8\ \text{W per motor}$$

Copper resistance also rises about 0.39 %/K, so this grows during the burst.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. In complementary mode the HS FET takes the hard turn-on/turn-off, and the LS FET switches near zero voltage after body-diode conduction.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (HS FET)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 32\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.55\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (LS body diode → dissipated in HS)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 45\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.054\ W}$$

Q_rr scales with di/dt and current. Datasheet Q_rr is often specified at lower current, so derate by ×1.5–2 at 80 A, giving about 0.1 W.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 35\text{n}\cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.021\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction (LS FET)

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD} \cdot I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 400\text{ns}\cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

This term is often overlooked. It is nearly as large as the V–I overlap loss, which makes dead-time tuning a first-order thermal lever.

### 2.5 Gate drive

$$P_{gate} = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\cdot 10 \cdot 48\text{k} = 19\text{ mW per switching FET}$$

This energy is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET die.

### 2.6 Switching totals per motor

The switching legs rotate, so the average per leg is 1/3 of the PWM-leg value.

| Term | PWM-leg instantaneous | Per motor (avg) |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | 1.55 W | 1.55 W |
| Q_rr (derated) | 0.10 W | 0.10 W |
| C_oss | 0.02 W | 0.02 W |
| Dead time | 1.23 W | 1.23 W |
| **Subtotal** | | **≈ 2.9 W** |

**Conclusion:** at 48 kHz and 80 A, switching losses are about 8 % of conduction losses. The design is conduction-dominated, so R_ds(on) selection matters far more than f_sw. Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.4 W per motor. Its main costs are audible noise and current ripple, not efficiency.
