**Three findings to accept up front:**

1. **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz PWM.** The four ESC MCUs do. The H743 only sends bidirectional DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry. The schematic keeps these domains separate.
2. **"80 A" cannot be continuous on a 30×30 board, let alone a 20×20 "nano."** Section 3 shows that 4 × 80 A simultaneous burst is thermally bounded to about 4 s. A true 20×20 footprint cannot use 5×6 FETs at this rating; it would require 3×3 FETs (about 2× R_DS(on)), which roughly halves burst time.
3. **All component parameters are representative class values, not a specific part's datasheet.** Final numbers must be re-derived from the selected MOSFET's datasheet curves, especially R_DS(on)(T), Q_rr at the actual di/dt, and Z_th(j-pcb).

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths in six-step with complementary PWM

In each 60° commutation sector, one half-bridge is PWM'd and one low-side FET is held statically on:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for D·T_sw. The synchronous low-side FET conducts for (1−D)·T_sw, minus dead time.
- **Static leg:** the low-side FET conducts 100% of the sector.

At every instant, the phase current I flows through exactly two FET channels. Therefore:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + I^2 R_{Cu}$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence of R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At a design-point T_j = 110 °C: R = 1.0 mΩ × 1.47 ≈ **1.47 mΩ** (use 1.5 mΩ).

### 1.3 Copper path resistance

| Segment | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Battery pad → B+ plane → FET drain | ≈0.10 mΩ |
| Phase node → motor pad | ≈0.08 mΩ |
| Source → GND plane → battery pad | ≈0.10 mΩ |
| **R_Cu (2 oz, multi-layer stitched)** | **≈0.28 mΩ** |

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A

$$P_{FET,cond} = 2(80)^2(1.5\,\text{m}\Omega) = 19.2\ \text{W}$$
$$P_{Cu} = (80)^2(0.28\,\text{m}\Omega) = 1.8\ \text{W}$$

### 1.5 Per-FET distribution

Each FET is active for 120° of every electrical cycle. The electrical frequency is f_e = RPM·p/120, which gives ≈3.5 kHz for a 14-pole motor at 30k RPM. That period is short compared with the die thermal time constant (≈1–10 ms), so the losses average out:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx \frac{19.2}{6} = 3.2\ \text{W}$$
