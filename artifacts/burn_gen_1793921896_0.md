# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC Nano Stack: Electro-Thermal and Timing Model

## 0. Architecture Correction and Baseline Assumptions

**The STM32H743 should not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design, and in ArduPilot's architecture, the split is:

| Function | Device | Firmware |
|---|---|---|
| Flight control, DShot generation, bidir telemetry decode | STM32H743 (FC board) | ArduPilot |
| 6-step commutation, 48 kHz PWM, BEMF sensing | 4× AT32F421 / STM32G071 (ESC board) | AM32 or BLHeli_32 |

ArduPilot has no BLDC commutation firmware for the H7. Using a single H743 to drive 24 MOSFETs is also impractical: it would need 12 complementary PWM pairs with dead-time, and only TIM1, TIM8, TIM15, TIM16 and TIM17 have complementary outputs. The model below therefore covers the H743 as the DShot master and the ESC MCUs as the 48 kHz switching controllers.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

**Six-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification (AM32 default):**
- At any instant, current flows through one high-side path and one low-side path.
- The PWM leg alternates high-side (duty D) and complementary low-side (1−D).
- Each phase leg carries current for 2/3 of the electrical period.

**Temperature-dependent on-resistance:**

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\quad \alpha\approx0.0059/\text{K}\Rightarrow k_T(110^\circ\text{C})\approx1.5$$

$$R_{ds,hot}=1.5\ \text{m}\Omega$$

**Loss per motor (bridge total):** with synchronous rectification there are always two FETs in series in the current path.

$$P_{cond,motor}=I^2\cdot 2R_{ds,hot}=80^2\times 3.0\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

**Per-FET average.** Each switch conducts for 1/3 of the period, with the PWM leg split between D and 1−D:

$$P_{cond,FET}\approx \frac{I^2 R_{ds,hot}}{3}=\mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

**Copper conduction (often forgotten):**

$$R_{trace}=\frac{\rho\,\ell}{w\,t},\quad \rho_{Cu}=1.72\times10^{-8}\,\Omega\text{m}\ (\times1.3\text{ at }100^\circ\text{C})$$

Take a phase path of 8 mm length, 4 mm width, and 3 paralleled 2 oz layers (t = 210 µm):

$$R\approx\frac{2.24\times10^{-8}\times0.008}{0.004\times210\times10^{-6}}=0.21\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P \approx 80^2 \times 0.21\,\text{m}\Omega \times 2\ \text{paths} \approx \mathbf{2.7\ W\ per\ motor}$$

Add solder pads and the motor-wire joints (about 0.1 mΩ each) for roughly **+1.3 W**.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing leg switches. The commutation edges, at f_e = eRPM/60 × 6, are negligible next to 48 kHz.

**(a) V–I overlap, hard-switched, inductive load:**

$$P_{VI}=\tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}=0.5\times25.2\times80\times40\text{ns}\times48\text{k}=\mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery** of the low-side body diode on high-side turn-on:

$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}=30\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.036\ W}$$

The real cost is larger because the turn-on current is I + I_rr. Add about 10% to (a).

**(c) Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss}=Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}=25\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.030\ W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two transitions per cycle):

$$P_{dt}=V_{SD}\,I\,(2t_{dead})f_{sw}=0.8\times80\times200\text{ns}\times48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive** (2 FETs switching per active leg, dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors):

$$P_g=2Q_gV_{gs}f_{sw}=2\times40\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.038\ W}$$

**Switching total per motor ≈ 2.8 W** at D ≈ 0.5–0.9. Note that at D = 100%, (a) through (d) go to zero.
