# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage and DShot Design Package

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Design Flags

I don't have the Job #14 requirements document, BOM, or stack-up, so every parameter below is an **explicit assumption** you can replace. The equations are parameterized so you can re-run them with the actual MOSFET datasheet values.

### Architecture

The STM32H743 cannot practically commutate 4 sensorless BLDC motors at 48 kHz with zero-cross detection while also running ArduPilot. The standard and recommended architecture is:

```
H743 (ArduPilot) --DShot600 bidir x4--> 4x ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32 or BLHeli_32)
                                          |--> 3-phase gate driver --> 6 FETs per motor
```

The H743 owns DShot and telemetry. Each ESC MCU owns the 48 kHz PWM.

### Feasibility flags (read these first)

1. **80 A × 4 on a 20×20 board is burst-only.** Section 3 shows the thermal budget is about 1–3 seconds.
2. **Battery current at full throttle approaches 4 × 80 = 320 A.** XT60 connectors, pads and input copper are not rated for this. The input path is a design-critical item.
3. **Bulk capacitor ripple reaches about 40 A RMS per channel at 50% duty.** No nano-size capacitor bank handles this continuously.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

R_ds(T_j) = R_ds25 · (1 + α)^(T_j − 25), with α ≈ 0.0045 /K

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.5 mΩ × 1.566 ≈ **2.35 mΩ**.

Iterate T_j and P_cond to convergence (Section 3.4).

### 1.2 Six-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification

At any instant, one high-side (HS) FET and one low-side (LS) FET carry the phase current I.

- With complementary PWM at duty D, the conducting phase leg alternates HS (fraction D) and LS (fraction 1 − D).
- The opposite leg's LS FET is held fully on.

Instantaneous conduction loss per channel:

P_cond,ch = I² · R_ds · [D + (1 − D)] + I² · R_ds = **2 · I² · R_ds** (independent of D)

At 80 A and 125 °C:

P_cond,ch = 2 × 6400 × 2.35 mΩ = **30.1 W per motor**

### 1.3 Distribution across the six FETs

Each FET conducts for 120° of every 360° electrical cycle, so averaged over an electrical revolution:

- **Average per FET:** P_FET,avg = P_cond,ch / 6 = 5.0 W (if PWM is placed alternately on HS and LS)
- **Worst-case FET (static LS during its 120° window):** P_peak = I² · R_ds = 15.0 W

### 1.4 Four-channel total

P_cond,total = 4 × 30.1 ≈ **120 W**

### 1.5 Copper and connection losses (often forgotten)

- **Phase trace loss** = I² · R_trace. For 15 mm of 2 oz copper, 4 mm wide, on 2 parallel layers (≈ 0.47 mΩ): 3.0 W per channel.
- **Input path loss** = I_bat² · R_in. At 320 A and an optimistic 0.3 mΩ: **30.7 W**.

This input-path figure is why connector, pad and shunt design matters as much as FET selection.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switching FET pair switches. Per channel, per PWM transition set:

### 2.1 Hard-switching overlap loss

P_sw = ½ · V_bus · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw
= 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 kHz = **1.94 W**

### 2.2 Output capacitance loss

P_oss = ½ · C_oss(er) · V² · f_sw
= 0.5 × 1.5 nF × 635 × 48 kHz = **0.023 W**

### 2.3 Reverse recovery (body diode of the synchronous FET)

P_rr = Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw = 30 nC × 25.2 × 48 kHz = **0.036 W**

Q_rr also adds turn-on current to the active FET. Derate with ×1.5 if Q_rr is specified at low di/dt.

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

P_dt = V_f · I · 2 · t_dt · f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 100 ns × 48 kHz = **0.31 W**

### 2.5 Gate drive (two switching FETs)

P_g = 2 · Q_g · V_gs · f_sw = 2 × 50 nC × 10 × 48 kHz = **0.048 W**

This power is dissipated in the gate driver and gate resistors, not in the FETs.

P_sw,ch ≈ 1.94 + 0.023 + 0.036 + 0.31 + 0.048
