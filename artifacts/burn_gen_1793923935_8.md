# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #5): Power-Stage and DShot Architecture Model

## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility Flags

I don't have the Job #5 requirements, so every parameter below is a stated assumption. Replace them with your BOM datasheet values and rerun the equations; the structure of the model is what carries over.

**Flag 1: Firmware split.** In a 4-in-1 stack, the H743 runs ArduPilot and generates bidirectional DShot600. The commutation and the 48 kHz PWM run on the ESC-side MCUs, for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32. The H743 does not commutate the motors. The model below treats them as two subsystems.

**Flag 2: 80 A on a nano footprint is burst-only.** On a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm board with 3.3×3.3 DFN FETs, 80 A per motor is physically limited to about one to two seconds (§3). Treat 80 A as a burst rating and specify continuous current at roughly 30–40 A.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

**Temperature-dependent on-resistance:**

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At 125 °C this gives 2.0 × 1.6 = **3.2 mΩ**. This conservative value is used throughout.

**Six-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM.** During each 60° sector, two phases conduct:
- The PWM phase: the high side conducts for fraction D, and the low side synchronously rectifies for (1−D).
- The return phase: the low side conducts 100 % of the sector.

Summing these:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I^2R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle when synchronous rectification is used.

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2(80)^2(0.0032) = \mathbf{40.96\ W\ per\ motor\ channel}$$

**Per-FET average.** Each of the 6 FETs conducts for 120° of each 360°. At typical electrical frequencies (0.5–4 kHz), the die thermal time constant (ms range) averages this out:

$$\bar P_{FET,cond} = \frac{40.96}{6} \approx 6.8\ \text{W}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one device per channel hard-switches at any time: the PWM-phase high side.

**(a) V–I overlap loss.** First, the gate currents and transition times:

$$I_{g,on}=\frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_g}=0.7\text{ A},\quad I_{g,off}=\frac{V_{pl}}{R_g}=0.3\text{ A}$$

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}}=8.6\text{ ns},\quad t_f\approx\frac{6\text{ nC}}{0.3}=20\text{ ns}$$

Common-source inductance and layout parasitics add delay, so use **t_on = 15 ns** and **t_off = 25 ns**:

$$P_{VI}=\tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_{on}+t_{off}) f_{sw}=\tfrac12(25.2)(80)(40\text{ns})(48\text{k})=\mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery of the synchronous FET body diode:**

$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}=20\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k}=0.024\text{ W}$$

**(c) Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss}\approx Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}=0.018\text{ W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period):**

$$P_{dt}=2\,V_F\,I\,t_{dt}\,f_{sw}=2(0.8)(80)(50\text{n})(48\text{k})=\mathbf{0.31\ W}$$

**(e) Gate charge.** This is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET:

$$P_g = 2\,Q_gV_{drv}f_{sw}=0.024\text{ W}$$

**Switching total per channel:**

$$P_{sw,ch} \approx 1.94+0.02+0.02+0.31 \approx \mathbf{2.3\ W}$$
