**Document:** Job #30, Architecture Baseline Rev A
**Scope:** Power-stage loss model (conduction, switching, dead-time), transient thermal model, bidirectional DShot600 timer/DMA allocation for ArduPilot, and reference netlists.

## 0. Architecture Clarification (Read First)

The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the **flight controller**. It does **not** commutate the motors directly. ArduPilot has no field-oriented or six-step motor-control stack. The architecture is therefore:

```
                 ┌──────────────────────── 6S LiPo (25.2 V max) ────────────────────────┐
                 │                                                                       │
┌────────────────┴───┐  DShot600 bidir (1-wire ×4)  ┌──────────────────────────────────────┴─┐
│ STM32H743VIT6 (FC) │─────────────────────────────▶│ 4× AT32F421K8U7 (ESC MCU, AM32 fw)      │
│ ArduPilot          │◀── GCR eRPM telemetry ───────│ 4× FD6288Q 3-phase gate driver          │
│ TIM3/TIM5 + DMAMUX │                              │ 24× N-MOSFET (6 per channel), 48 kHz PWM│
└────────────────────┘                              └─────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- **FC:** H743 at 400 MHz (ArduPilot default), with APB1 timer clock = 200 MHz.
- **ESC:** four independent AT32F421 MCUs running AM32 with complementary (synchronous-rectified) six-step PWM at **48 kHz**.
- **Rating interpretation:** "80 A burst" means **80 A per channel**, evaluated for both single-channel and worst-case all-four-channel simultaneous bursts.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current Path in Six-Step Complementary PWM

In any of the six commutation sectors, one phase is held with its **low-side FET fully ON**, and a second phase switches complementarily at duty D. Current flows through the HS FET during D and the LS (synchronous) FET during (1−D). The third phase floats and is used for BEMF sensing.

Therefore, at every instant (excluding dead time), **exactly two FET channels** carry the phase current I:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot \left[ D\,R_{HS} + (1-D)\,R_{LS,sync} + R_{LS,static} \right] = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

when all FETs are identical. Note that this is **independent of D**, which is why synchronous rectification matters.

### 2.2 Temperature-Dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\left[1 + \alpha_R\,(T_j - 25)\right]
$$

Because R_DS depends on T_j, which in turn depends on dissipation, the system is a fixed-point problem. It is solved in §4.3.

### 2.3 Parasitic Copper / Shunt Resistance

| Element | R | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Shunt (per channel, in LS return) | 0.25 mΩ | 80 A → 20 mV full scale |
| PCB copper, bus → FET → phase pad | ≈0.30 mΩ | 8-layer, 2 oz outer / 1 oz inner, stitched |
| Solder pad + motor wire joint | ≈0.15 mΩ | |

$$
R_{par} = 0.70\ \text{m}\Omega \;\Rightarrow\; P_{par} = 80^2 \times 0.70\times10^{-3} = 4.48\ \text{W/ch}
$$

### 2.4 Numerical Result at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond (2 FETs) | per FET |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 0.750 mΩ | 9.60 W | 4.80 W |
| 100 °C | 1.003 mΩ | 12.84 W | 6.42 W |
| 125 °C | 1.088 mΩ | 13.92 W | 6.96 W |

## 3. Switching-Related Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the **switching leg** contributes switching losses: its HS FET hard-switches, and its LS FET soft-switches through its body diode. The static LS leg contributes none.

### 3.1 Gate Transition Times

Gate current during the Miller plateau:

$$
I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{GS} - V_{plateau}}{R_g + R_{drv,on}} = \frac{10 - 3.2}{4.7 + 2.0} \approx 1.01\ \text{A}
$$

$$
I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{plateau}}{R_g + R_{drv,off}} = \frac{3.2}{4.7 + 1.5} \approx 0.52\ \text{A}
$$

$$
t_r = \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{g,on}} = \frac{14\,\text{nC}}{1.01\,\text{A}} \approx 13.9\ \text{ns}, \qquad
t_f = \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{g,off}} \approx 26.9\ \text{ns}
$$

Turn-off is slower because the plateau voltage drives the sink current. If turn-off loss dominates, add a turn-off diode across R_g (schematic option DNP-D1).

### 3.2 V·I Overlap Loss (HS FET)

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}
= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40.8\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = 1.97\ \text{W}
$$
