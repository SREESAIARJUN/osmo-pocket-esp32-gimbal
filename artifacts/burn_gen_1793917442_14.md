# STM32H743 Nano AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC): Power, Thermal and DShot Architecture

## 0. Feasibility Findings

These items determine whether the rest of the design is meaningful.

| # | Finding | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | ArduPilot runs on the H743 as the **flight controller**. It does not commutate motors. A 4-in-1 ESC that uses bi-directional DShot needs **four separate ESC MCUs** (e.g., AT32F421 or GD32E230 running AM32/BLHeli_32). | The architecture is H743 → 4× DShot600 → 4× ESC MCU → 3-phase bridges. The H743 cannot drive 24 gate signals for 4 motors anyway: TIM1 and TIM8 cover only 2 motors with complementary outputs. |
| F2 | 80 A burst per motor means **320 A burst from the battery**. An XT60 is rated 60 A continuous. Practical battery leads are 10–12 AWG. | Use an XT90 or solder pads with 10 AWG leads. "80 A" can only be a single-motor, sub-second rating. |
| F3 | A nano board (20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm) has roughly 1–2 J/K of thermal capacitance. The model in §2 shows it reaches T_j,max in **about 1–2 s** at full 4×80 A. | 80 A is a **≤1 s transient rating**. Realistic continuous rating is about 25–35 A per motor. I recommend a 30.5×30.5 mm format if a 45 A continuous rating is required. |

Everything below is built against these findings. All assumptions are stated so you can substitute datasheet values.

## 2. Loss Model per Motor Channel

### 2.1 Conduction losses

With 6-step trapezoidal commutation and complementary (synchronous) PWM, one phase sources and one phase sinks at any instant. The high-side FET of the active leg conducts for duty D. The low-side FET of the same leg (synchronous rectification) conducts for 1−D. The sink-phase low-side FET conducts 100 % of the interval. Therefore:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds,hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds,hot} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds,hot}$$

The result is independent of D, which is a useful property of synchronous 6-step drive.

$$R_{ds,hot} = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)] \approx 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.6 = 1.6\,\text{m}\Omega$$

At the 80 A burst:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.6\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{20.5\ W}$$

Phase current is not flat DC. Including ripple, the RMS correction is:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

With motor L ≈ 15 µH, V_bus/2 across it, and 48 kHz at D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I \approx \frac{12.6 \cdot 0.5}{15\mu \cdot 48k} = 8.75\ \text{A}$$

The correction term is 6.4 A², which is under 0.2 % and therefore negligible.

Copper loss must also be included. The phase and bus copper path is estimated at about 0.4 mΩ per channel (via arrays plus planes), contributing I²R = **2.6 W**.

### 2.2 Switching losses (hard-switched high-side, 48 kHz)

**V–I overlap.** Only the high side hard-switches. The low side switches at near-zero voltage after the body diode conducts.

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

**Reverse recovery** (the low-side body diode is recovered by the high-side turn-on):

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (2 edges per period):

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times400\text{n}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

**Gate drive** (2 switching FETs per channel, dissipated in the driver):

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2\times45\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k} = 0.043\ \text{W}$$

Dead-time loss exceeds switching loss. Reducing t_dt from 200 ns to 100 ns (AM32 dead-time setting, validated by scope) saves 0.6 W per channel.

### 2.3 Totals at 80 A burst, 25.2 V

| Loss | Per channel (W) | ×4 channels (W) |

| Copper | 2.6 | 10.4 |

| Dead time | 1.23 | 4.9 |
