## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Assumptions

Three framing points shape all the math below.

1. **Partitioning.** The STM32H743 is the **flight controller**. Each ESC channel in a 4-in-1 runs its own motor MCU, typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 does not commutate motors. It emits bi-directional DShot600 and decodes the eRPM telemetry.
2. **"80 A burst"** is interpreted as **80 A per channel**, for a 320 A board total. That is very aggressive for a 30.5 × 30.5 mm "nano/AIO" footprint. The thermal section shows this is feasible only for **seconds**, not as a rating. If the intent was 80 A *board total* (20 A/ch), losses drop by about 16×. Please confirm.
3. **Component parameters** are representative of 40 V, ~1 mΩ PDFN5×6 MOSFETs and a 6S pack. All equations are symbolic first, so you can substitute datasheet values directly.

| Symbol | Meaning | Value used |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | Bus voltage (6S full) | 25.2 V |
| I_pk | Burst phase current | 80 A |
| R_ds(on),25 | FET on-resistance @ 25 °C, V_gs = 10 V | 1.0 mΩ |
| α_T | R_ds tempco factor @ T_j = 125 °C | 1.6 |
| t_r, t_f | Current rise/fall time (with driver/R_g) | 20 ns each |
| Q_g | Total gate charge | 50 nC |
| V_drv | Gate drive voltage | 10 V |
| Q_rr | Body diode reverse recovery charge | 50 nC |
| C_oss | Output capacitance (at V_bus) | 1.5 nF |
| V_f | Body diode forward drop @ 80 A | 0.8 V |
| t_dt | Dead time per edge | 100 ns |
| f_sw | PWM frequency | 48 kHz |
| R_pcb | Copper path resistance per channel | 0.3 mΩ |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-step commutation with synchronous rectification

At any instant in trapezoidal (6-step) drive, current flows through **two** phase legs. Consider the PWM'd leg with duty *D*:
- The high-side FET conducts for a fraction *D* of the time.
- The complementary low-side FET (synchronous rectification) conducts for (1−D), ignoring dead time.
- The return leg's low-side FET conducts continuously.

So at every instant exactly two FET channels carry the full current, regardless of *D*:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \left[\, D\,R_{ds} + (1-D)\,R_{ds} + R_{ds} \right] = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

The temperature dependence of on-resistance is:

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) \approx R_{ds,25}\,\left[1 + k\,(T_j - 25)\right], \quad k \approx 0.006/\mathrm{K} \;\Rightarrow\; \alpha_T(125^\circ\mathrm{C}) \approx 1.6
$$

Using trapezoidal current, I_rms ≈ I_pk at the flat top. A ripple correction is shown in §1.3.

### 1.2 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond,FET (2 FETs) | P_copper = I²·R_pcb | **P_cond,ch** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.0 mΩ | 12.8 W | 1.9 W | **14.7 W** |
| 125 °C | 1.6 mΩ | 20.5 W | 1.9 W | **22.4 W** |

### 1.3 Ripple correction

The phase current ripple is:

$$
\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}-E_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}, \qquad
I_{rms}^2 = I_{avg}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}
$$

For a typical 5" motor, L_ph ≈ 15 µH. At D = 0.9 and E_bemf ≈ 20 V, ΔI ≈ 6.5 A. The resulting correction is about +0.5 %, so it is negligible at 48 kHz. This is one of the arguments *for* 48 kHz over 24 kHz.

### 1.4 Electrothermal feedback

Conduction loss depends on T_j, and T_j depends on loss. Solving self-consistently:

$$
T_j = T_{ref} + R_{\theta}\, 2 I^2 R_{ds,25}\left[1 + k(T_j-25)\right]
\;\Rightarrow\;
T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + R_\theta\,2I^2R_{25}(1-25k)}{1 - R_\theta\,2I^2R_{25}\,k}
$$

**Thermal runaway** occurs when the denominator reaches zero:

$$
R_\theta \ge \frac{1}{2 I^2 R_{25} k}
$$

At 80 A this gives R_θ,crit ≈ 1/(2·6400·0.001·0.006) ≈ **13 K/W**.

A bare nano board in still air has an *effective* R_θ above this. **Steady-state 80 A is therefore thermally unstable without forced airflow.** This is formal proof that 80 A can only be a transient rating.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches hard. The return leg is static between commutations. Per channel:

### 2.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\,\mathrm{ns} \cdot 48\,\mathrm{kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery of the synchronous FET body diode

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\,\mathrm{nC}\cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\,\mathrm{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}
$$

Q_rr itself scales with I and di/dt, so treat this term as a lower bound.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} \times 2\ \text{FETs} = 0.5\cdot1.5\,\mathrm{nF}\cdot 635\cdot 48\,\mathrm{k}\cdot2 = \mathbf{0.05\ W}
$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period)

$$
P_{dt} = 2\, V_f\, I\, t_{dt}\, f_{sw} = 2\cdot0.8\cdot80\cdot100\,\mathrm{ns}\cdot48\,\mathrm{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

This term is large. Keep dead time ≤ 100 ns. AM32's dead-time setting directly trades shoot-through margin against this loss.

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in driver and R_g, not in the FET die)

$$
P_g = Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} \times 2 = 50\,\mathrm{nC}\cdot10\cdot48\,\mathrm{k}\cdot2 = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

### 2.6 Switching-loss total and frequency scaling

**Total switching-related loss is about 2.7 W per channel**, roughly 12 % of conduction loss at 80 A.

Every switching term is linear in f_sw:

$$
P_{sw,total}(f) \approx 2.7\,\mathrm{W}\cdot\frac{f}{48\,\mathrm{kHz}}
$$

Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.35 W per channel at 80 A. However, it increases ripple and audible noise, and costs some low-throttle efficiency. At burst current, conduction dominates, so 48 kHz is a defensible choice.
