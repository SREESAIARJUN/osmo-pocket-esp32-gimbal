# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with Integrated 80 A-Burst 4-in-1 ESC: Thermal, Switching-Loss and Bi-Directional DShot600 Design Package

**Job #50 · Revision A (analytical baseline)**

---

## 0. Architecture Assumptions

The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does not commutate the motors. Each ESC channel uses a dedicated low-cost MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. These MCUs generate the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 sends bi-directional DShot600 commands and receives eRPM telemetry.

| Parameter | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V max / 22.2 V nominal | Design for 25.2 V plus a ringing margin |
| Burst current | 80 A per channel | Burst only, not continuous (see §3) |
| PWM frequency (f_sw) | 48 kHz | ESC firmware setting |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal, complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM | AM32 default |
| MOSFET (reference) | 40 V N-channel, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN | R_DS(on) 1.2 mΩ at 25 °C, Q_g ≈ 40 nC, Q_rr ≈ 40 nC, C_oss ≈ 1 nF, R_θJC ≈ 1.5 K/W |
| Gate driver | 3-phase half-bridge driver (FD6288Q class) | ~1 A source / 1.5 A sink |
| PCB | 30.5×30.5 mm mount pattern, 8-layer, 2 oz on power layers | |

> ⚠️ **Feasibility flag.** "Nano" plus 80 A is the governing risk of this job. On a true 20×20 mm board, the result below is physically limited to bursts lasting well under one second. All numbers below use 30.5×30.5 mm. If the product must be 20×20, rescale the thermal capacitance and convection area by (20/30.5)² ≈ 0.43.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step synchronous commutation

During any sector, two phases carry current:

- **Leg A (PWM leg):** the high side Q_AH conducts for duty D. The complementary low side Q_AL conducts for (1 − D).
- **Leg B (return leg):** the low side Q_BL is held on for the whole sector.

So at any instant, current I flows through exactly two R_DS(on) in series:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{DS} = 1.2\ \text{mΩ} \times 1.45 = 1.74\ \text{mΩ}$$

### 1.3 Burst numbers (I = 80 A, T_j = 125 °C)

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.74\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{22.3\ W\ per\ channel}$$

**Per-FET distribution.** The return-leg low-side FET is the hottest device:

| Device | Loss | Conduction fraction |
|---|---|---|
| Q_BL (return leg, held on) | 80² × 1.74 m = 11.1 W | 100 % of sector |
| Q_AH + Q_AL (PWM leg, split by D) | 11.1 W combined | |

Commutation rotates these roles every 60° electrical. Averaged over an electrical revolution, each FET in the bridge carries 1/3 of the time in its "held" role. Thermally, though, the peak instantaneous loss of 11.1 W sets the junction ripple at low eRPM (see §3.3).

### 1.4 Parasitic copper

Add 0.3 mΩ for the PCB path and 0.5 mΩ per channel for the shunt plus solder:

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.8\times10^{-3} \approx 5.1\ \text{W per channel}$$

This loss sits in the board, not the junction, but it heats the same thermal mass.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per channel, only the PWM leg switches hard: one hard turn-on and one hard turn-off per cycle.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard switching)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

With t_r ≈ 20 ns and t_f ≈ 15 ns (driver-limited at R_g ≈ 4.7 Ω):

$$P_{VI} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 35\times10^{-9} \times 48\,000 = \mathbf{1.69\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (body diode of the complementary FET)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\times10^{-9} \times 25.2 \times 48\,000 = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

Note that Q_rr rises with di/dt and T_j. Apply 2× margin, giving ≈ 0.1 W.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss} V^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1\times10^{-9} \times 635 \times 48\,000 = 0.015\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

With two dead-time intervals per cycle, t_dt = 50 ns and V_f ≈ 0.8 V:

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\times10^{-9} \times 48\,000 = \mathbf{0.31\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in the driver and R_g, not the FET)

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 40\times10^{-9} \times 10 \times 48\,000 = 0.038\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Switching total

$$P_{sw,ch} \approx 1.69 + 0.10 + 0.015 + 0.31 + 0.04 \approx \mathbf{2.15\ W\ per\ channel}$$

**Frequency sensitivity:**

$$\frac{\partial P_{sw}}{\partial f_{sw}} \approx 45\ \mu\text{W/Hz}$$

| f_sw | P_sw per channel |
|---|---|
| 24 kHz | ≈ 1.1 W |
| 48 kHz | ≈ 2.15 W |
| 96 kHz | ≈ 4.3 W |
