## 0. Architecture Correction and Design Envelope (Read First)

Three points need to be settled before any math, because they change the design.

**0.1 The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot has no ESC commutation or FOC firmware. The correct architecture is:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot FC) --DShot600 bidir x4--> 4x ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32 or BLHeli_32)
                                                    └─> 3-phase gate driver ─> 6 MOSFETs per motor (24 total)
```

The H743's job is DShot generation and eRPM capture. Switching and conduction losses belong to the ESC power stage.

**0.2 "Nano" and "80 A burst per motor" are physically incompatible.** This is quantified in §3.5:

- 24 MOSFETs in 5×6 mm PQFN occupy about 720 mm² of footprint before keep-outs.
- A 20×20 mm board offers 400 mm² per side, shared with the MCU, drivers, and capacitors.
- At 80 A, 3×3 mm FETs dissipate about 50 W per motor. The board cannot absorb this for more than a fraction of a second.

**Recommendation:** use a 30.5×30.5 mm form factor with 5×6 FETs, or derate burst to 45–55 A. All math below is parameterized, so the numbers can be rerun either way.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Hot R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j)=R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0055 × 100) = **1.55 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Per-channel conduction loss in 6-step synchronous PWM

During each 60° commutation sector, current flows through two legs:

- **Switched leg:** the high side conducts for fraction D and the complementary low side conducts for (1 − D), ignoring dead time.
- **Return leg:** the low side is on for 100% of the sector.

Summing the loss over the sector:

$$P_{cond}=I^2R_{DS}\,[D+(1-D)+1]=2I^2R_{DS}$$

**Key result:** with synchronous rectification, conduction loss is independent of duty cycle. Burst throttle at any D costs the same I²R.

$$P_{cond}=2(80)^2(1.55\,\text{m}\Omega)=\mathbf{19.8\ W\ per\ motor}$$

By symmetry over an electrical revolution, each of the 6 FETs averages P_cond/6 = **3.3 W**.

For comparison, a 3×3 mm FET at 2.5 mΩ (25 °C) gives 3.9 mΩ hot, so P_cond = 49.6 W per motor (8.3 W per FET).

### 1.3 Thermal runaway check (stable operating point)

Junction temperature depends on R_DS, which depends on junction temperature:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + R_{\theta}\cdot\tfrac{I^2}{3}R_{DS,25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Here I²/3 is the per-FET share, (2I²R)/6.

Solving for T_j gives a stable solution only if:

$$R_\theta\cdot\tfrac{I^2}{3}R_{DS,25}\,\alpha<1$$

- 5×6 FET: Rθ_max < 1/(2133 × 1e-3 × 0.0055) = **85 K/W**. Safe.
- 3×3 FET: the limit falls to about 34 K/W. Still bounded, but close enough that it cannot be ignored when the PCB is the only heatsink.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switched high-side FET hard-switches. Its complementary low-side FET switches at near-zero voltage, because the body diode conducts during dead time.

### 2.1 Gate currents at the Miller plateau

$$I_{g,on}=\frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{on}+R_{g,int}}=\frac{10-3.3}{5.9}=1.14\text{ A}$$

$$I_{g,off}=\frac{V_{pl}}{R_{off}+R_{g,int}}=\frac{3.3}{3.4}=0.97\text{ A}$$

### 2.2 Transition times

$$t_{on}=\frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}}=\frac{14\text{ nC}}{1.14}=12.3\text{ ns}$$

$$t_{off}=\frac{14\text{ nC}}{0.97}=14.4\text{ ns}$$

### 2.3 Loss components (per motor)

| Mechanism | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_on + t_off)·f_sw = ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 26.7 ns × 48 k | **1.29 W** |
| C_oss discharge | ½·C_oss,er·V²·f_sw = ½ × 1.4 nF × 635 × 48 k | 0.021 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.048 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_SD·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.
