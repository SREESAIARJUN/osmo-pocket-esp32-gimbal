# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, Bidirectional DShot600 Timer/DMA Allocation, and Reference Netlists

## 0. Architecture and Key Finding

**Partitioning.** The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. A production-viable 4-in-1 uses:

- **FC:** STM32H743VIH6 (TFBGA100) running ArduPilot. It generates bidirectional DShot600 on 4 timer channels.
- **ESC:** 4 independent ESC MCUs (STM32G071 or AT32F421 running AM32 firmware, or EFM8BB51 running Bluejay/BLHeli_S). Each drives 3 half-bridges at 48 kHz through a 3-phase gate driver (e.g., FD6288Q class).
- **Power stage:** 12 half-bridges, 24 N-MOSFETs, 40 V class.

**Headline result.** 80 A per motor simultaneously on a 20×20 mm board is a **transient-only (burst) rating**:

- Dissipation is about 84 W total, against a board heat capacity of about 2 J/K.
- The board-to-air thermal resistance is about 12.5 K/W.
- The adiabatic burst limit is about 1.5 s from 40 °C.
- Continuous rating on 20×20 is realistically 35–45 A per motor.

Sections 1–3 derive this. All device parameters below are **stated assumptions**. Replace them with your selected MOSFET's datasheet values; the equations are parametric.

## 2. Loss Model (Per Motor, Then ×4)

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

In 6-step commutation, each leg spends:

- 120° as the "high" leg: HS on for D, LS on for (1−D) via synchronous rectification.
- 120° as the "low" leg: LS on 100%.
- 120° floating.

Time-averaged conduction loss per leg:

$$P_{cond,leg}=I^2R_{hot}\left[\tfrac13D+\tfrac13(1-D)+\tfrac13\right]=\tfrac23\,I^2R_{hot}$$

This is **independent of duty**, because synchronous rectification keeps current in exactly two channels at all times. Cross-check: two conducting switches give P = I²·2R at every instant, which equals 3 legs × (2/3)·I²R.

$$P_{cond,motor}=2I^2R_{hot}=2(80)^2(1.5\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

The average per switch is 19.2 / 6 = 3.2 W. The peak instantaneous per switch is I²R = **9.6 W**.

### 2.2 Switching Losses (Only One Leg Is Hard-Switching at Any Time)

**V-I overlap:**
$$P_{sw}=\tfrac12V_{bus}I(t_r+t_f)f_{sw}=\tfrac12(25.2)(80)(20\text{n})(48\text{k})=\mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (2 edges per period):**
$$P_{dt}=2V_fI\,t_{dt}f_{sw}=2(0.8)(80)(100\text{n})(48\text{k})=\mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**
$$P_{rr}=V_{bus}Q_{rr}f_{sw}=25.2(50\text{n})(48\text{k})=\mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

**C_oss:**
$$P_{oss}=\tfrac12C_{oss,er}V_{bus}^2f_{sw}=\tfrac12(2\text{n})(635)(48\text{k})=\mathbf{0.03\ W}$$

**Gate drive (2 FETs per leg; mostly dissipated in the driver and gate resistor):**
$$P_g=2Q_gV_{gs}f_{sw}=2(60\text{n})(10)(48\text{k})=\mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

**Switching subtotal ≈ 1.73 W per motor.**

### 2.3 Totals

| | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching + dead-time + other | 1.73 W | 6.9 W |
| Shunt (0.5 mΩ, if per-motor current sense) | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| Copper planes (≈0.3 mΩ effective loop) | ≈1.9 W | ≈7.7 W |
| **Total** | **≈26 W** | **≈104 W** (≈84 W in FETs) |

### 2.4 Sensitivity to f_sw

- dP_sw/df ≈ 36 µW/Hz per motor.
- 24 kHz saves ≈0.8 W per motor.
- 96 kHz adds ≈1.6 W per motor.

At 80 A the stage is **conduction-dominated** (≈92%). Reducing R_ds (paralleling 2 FETs per switch halves P_cond) matters far more than f_sw. 48 kHz is a sensible choice for low motor ripple and noise.

**Efficiency check.** Assume mechanical output ≈ V·I·D at D ≈ 0.9:
- Output ≈ 25.2 × 80 × 0.9 ≈ 1.8 kW per motor.
- Loss ≈ 26 W per motor.
- η_ESC ≈ 98.6%.

The *efficiency* is fine. The *power density* is the problem.
