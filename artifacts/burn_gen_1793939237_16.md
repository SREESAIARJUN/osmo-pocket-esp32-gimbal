## 0. Architecture and Feasibility Findings

Two constraints shape every calculation below.

**Finding 1: the H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot running on the H743 is the flight controller. The 48 kHz PWM and 6-step/FOC commutation must run on four dedicated ESC MCUs. A typical choice is the AT32F421 running AM32, which natively supports 48 kHz PWM, bidirectional DShot and eRPM telemetry. The H743 drives each ESC MCU with bidirectional DShot600.

```
            ┌──────────── STM32H743VIT6 (ArduPilot) ────────────┐
 IMU/Baro ──┤ SPI1/SPI4/I2C       TIM1 CH1..CH4 (BDShot600)     │
            └───────────────────────┬──┬──┬──┬────────────────────┘
                                    │  │  │  │  (single-wire, bidirectional)
                         ┌──────────┘  │  │  └──────────┐
                     AT32F421 #1   #2      #3       #4  (AM32, 48 kHz)
                         │
                 3-ph gate driver (FD6288Q) → 6× N-MOSFET → Motor
```

**Finding 2: "80A" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is a transient rating only.**
- 4 × 80 A = 320 A total, which exceeds any connector and battery lead a nano build can carry. An XT60 is rated ~60 A continuous.
- The thermal model in §3 shows the board survives an 80 A event for **~2–3 s** at 6S, not continuously.
- The rating should be specified as **"80 A burst ≤ 2 s per channel, non-concurrent; ~25–35 A continuous with prop-wash airflow."**

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (Per ESC Channel)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

With synchronous rectification in 6-step commutation, exactly **two FETs carry the phase current at every instant**: one high-side and one low-side, or the low-side freewheel during PWM-off.

Hot on-resistance:

$$R_{ds,hot} = k_T \cdot R_{ds,25} = 1.6 \times 0.70\ \text{m}\Omega = 1.12\ \text{m}\Omega$$

Include current ripple in the RMS value:

$$I_{rms} = I\sqrt{1 + \tfrac{1}{12}\left(\tfrac{\Delta I}{I}\right)^2}$$

At 48 kHz with typical motor inductance (~10–20 µH), ΔI/I is about 10%. The correction is +0.04%, so it can be neglected.

Conduction loss per ESC:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \cdot I^2 \cdot R_{ds,hot} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.12\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{14.3\ W}$$

Each FET conducts for about one-third of the electrical period. Averaged across the six FETs, that is ≈ **2.4 W per FET**.

**Duty-split refinement (for hot-spot analysis).** Over a PWM cycle with duty D:
- Active high-side FET: $I^2 R_{ds} D$
- Its complementary low-side FET: $I^2 R_{ds}(1-D)$
- Commutated low-side FET (on for the full sector): $I^2 R_{ds}$

The worst FET is therefore always the sector-on low-side. Size the low-side copper pour accordingly.

### 2.2 Switching losses (hard-switched leg, one per PWM cycle)

**V–I overlap:**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = V_{bus} Q_{rr} f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ W$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = V_{bus} Q_{oss} f_{sw} \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} = 0.021\ W$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two edges per cycle):

$$P_{dt} = 2 V_F I\, t_{dead} f_{sw} = 2 \times 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**Gate drive** (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g, not the FETs):

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2 = 50\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = 0.048\ W$$

### 2.3 Other loss terms

**Bus capacitor ripple.** The input RMS current is

$$I_{C,rms} \approx I\sqrt{D(1-D)}$$

This peaks at 40 A for D = 0.5. With 4× 470 µF low-ESR polymer capacitors in parallel (ESR ≈ 3 mΩ each, 0.75 mΩ combined), shared across four channels, the loss is about **1.2 W worst case**.

**PCB copper.** For a 2 oz outer + 2 oz inner phase path, 8 mm long, ~6 mm effective width with stitched layers:

- R ≈ 0.25 mΩ
- P ≈ 80² × 0.25 m ≈ **1.6 W** per active channel
