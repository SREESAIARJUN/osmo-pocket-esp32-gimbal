# STM32H743 Flight Controller + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC (Job #49): Design Package

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Architecture Corrections

I have no access to a Job #49 requirements document. Every input below is an explicit assumption. Change the parameter tables and the equations still hold.

### 0.1 Architecture correction (important)

ArduPilot does not run motor commutation. The H743 should be the **flight controller MCU**. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, for example an AT32F421 or STM32G071. Bidirectional DShot is the link between the two. The design below therefore has:

- **FC side:** STM32H743, ArduPilot, DShot600 bidirectional output on 4 timer channels.
- **ESC side:** 4 × (G071 or F421 + 3-phase gate driver + 6 MOSFETs + shunt), on a stacked or combined board.

## 1. Loss Model: One ESC Channel, Six-Step Synchronous Commutation

### 1.1 Conduction topology

In any commutation sector, current flows through exactly two device positions:

- the **PWM leg**: high-side (HS) during the on-time and the complementary low-side (LS, synchronous rectification) during the off-time;
- the **return leg**: LS held on continuously.

So in every part of the PWM cycle there are **two FETs in series with the motor current**, excluding the dead-time intervals.

### 1.2 Conduction loss

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I^2 R_{ds(on)}(T_j)\,(1 - 2 t_{dead} f_{sw})$$

The dead-time correction is 2·200 ns·48 kHz = 1.9 %. That is small, so it is kept conservative at 1:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{15.36\ W}$$

R_ds(on) depends on temperature:

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + k\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad k \approx 0.005/\text{K}$$

This creates positive feedback, because higher loss raises T_j, which raises R_ds(on). Solve it iteratively, as in Section 2.3.

### 1.3 Switching loss at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The HS turns on and off once per period against the full bus voltage.

**V-I overlap:**
$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead intervals per period):
$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2 t_{dead}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 400\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery** of the LS body diode at HS turn-on:
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{nC}\cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

**C_oss loss:**
$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 2\text{nF} \cdot 635 \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.03\ W}$$

**Gate drive** (2 switching FETs, dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g, not the FET):
$$P_{gate} = 2\, Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2\cdot 100\text{nC}\cdot 10 \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.10\ W}$$

**Shunt loss:**
$$P_{shunt} = I^2 R_{shunt} = 6400 \cdot 0.25\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{1.6\ W}$$

**Bus and phase copper.** Assume about 0.3 mΩ effective per channel:
$$P_{Cu} \approx 80^2 \cdot 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega \approx \mathbf{1.9\ W}$$

### 1.4 Per-channel totals at 80 A and T_j = 125 °C

| Loss term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (2 FETs) | 15.36 |
| V-I switching | 1.94 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.09 |
| Gate (driver side) | 0.10 |
| Shunt | 1.60 |
| Copper | 1.90 |
| **Total per channel** | **≈ 22.2 W** |
| **4 channels, simultaneous burst** | **≈ 89 W** |

At 80 A, conduction dominates switching by about 5:1.

**Design implications:**
- Raising f_sw to 96 kHz adds only about 3.2 W per channel.
- Halving R_ds(on), for example by paralleling FETs, saves about 7.7 W per channel.
- Spend board area on FET R_ds(on) and copper, not on reducing f_sw.

### 1.5 Instantaneous loss per FET

- The worst-case single FET (HS in the PWM leg at high duty, or the static LS) dissipates about 80²·1.2 mΩ + 1.94 + 1.23 ≈ **10.9 W**.
- The commutation-averaged loss per FET is about 22.2 − 3.5 (non-FET terms) ≈ 18.7 W / 6 ≈ **3.1 W**.
- The relevant thermal time scale: electrical commutation at 30k eRPM is about 3 kHz. That is much faster than the junction thermal time constant (~ms), so junctions see something between the instantaneous peak and the average.
- Use **5–6 W per FET** for T_jc estimates.
