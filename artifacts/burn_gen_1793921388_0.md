## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and an Architectural Correction

### 0.1 Architectural correction (read first)

The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. In any ArduPilot-compatible design the partitioning is:

| Function | Device | Firmware |
|---|---|---|
| Flight control, DShot master, telemetry decode | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100) | ArduPilot |
| Commutation, 48 kHz PWM, current sense | 4× ESC MCUs (STM32G071 / AT32F421 class) | AM32 or BLHeli_32-compatible |
| Gate drive | 4× 3-phase half-bridge drivers (FD6288 class) | n/a |
| Power stage | 24× N-ch MOSFETs (6 per channel) | n/a |

The 48 kHz switching frequency is an ESC-MCU parameter. The H743's job is the DShot600 bidirectional link. Both are modeled below, but they live in different silicon.

### 0.2 Design assumptions

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | V_bus | 6S, 25.2 V max (22.2 V nominal) |
| Burst phase current | I_pk | 80 A per channel |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| Board format | n/a | 30.5 × 30.5 mm mount, ~36 × 36 mm outline, 8-layer, 2 oz inner/outer |
| MOSFET class | n/a | 40 V, PDFN 5×6, R_DS(on) = 1.2 mΩ @ 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V |
| MOSFET charges | n/a | Q_g = 50 nC, Q_gd = 10 nC, Q_rr = 50 nC, Q_oss = 60 nC |
| Body diode | V_f | 0.8 V |
| Dead time | t_dt | 100 ns |
| Gate drive | n/a | ~1 A source / 1.5 A sink → t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 15 ns |

> ⚠️ Substitute datasheet values for your selected MOSFET. Every result below scales directly with R_DS(on), Q_gd, and Q_rr.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0067\ /\text{K}$$

At T_j = 100 °C:

$$R_{DS(on)} = 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.50 = 1.80\,\text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Six-step commutation with synchronous rectification

In each 60° electrical sector, exactly two phases carry current. With complementary PWM, current always flows through exactly two MOSFET channels:

- **During D:** high-side of phase A, low-side of phase B.
- **During 1−D:** synchronous low-side of phase A, low-side of phase B.

Conduction loss per ESC channel is therefore independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I^2 R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.8\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{23.0\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each phase conducts for 2/3 of the electrical cycle and splits that between its high-side and low-side devices. Averaged over a cycle:

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} \approx \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6} = 3.84\ \text{W}$$

The instantaneous peak while a FET is conducting is $I^2R = 11.5\ \text{W}$. The electrical frequency is roughly 0.5–3 kHz, far faster than the package thermal time constant (~10–100 ms), so the cycle average governs.

### 1.4 PCB copper loss

Assume a 0.4 mΩ round-trip path (battery pad → bus → FET → phase pad) with 2 oz copper:

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.4\times10^{-3} = 2.56\ \text{W per channel}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the PWM'd half-bridge switches at f_sw. The commutation-rate switching of the other leg is negligible.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap (high-side)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 1.45\ \text{W}$$

A more rigorous form uses the Miller plateau:

$$t_{r,f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\, R_g}{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (low-side body diode)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

(This term adds stress to the high-side turn-on current spike. Use FETs with a soft body diode.)

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM period:

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$
