## 0. Architecture Clarifications

These points constrain everything that follows.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In any ArduPilot-compatible 4-in-1, the H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bi-directional DShot600. The 48 kHz PWM and commutation are generated by dedicated ESC MCUs running AM32 or BLHeli_32/Bluejay. Common choices are AT32F421 or STM32G071, either one per motor or two dual-motor MCUs. ArduPilot has no native FOC/6-step ESC firmware for the H7.
2. **"80 A burst" is interpreted as 80 A phase current per motor**, applied simultaneously on all four channels as the worst case. The analysis shows this is only survivable for about one second on a 20×20 mm nano board. I flag this as a specification risk rather than design around it silently.
3. All MOSFET and driver parameters below are **representative assumptions** for a 40 V PDFN 3.3×3.3 device. Replace them with datasheet values from the selected part before sign-off.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Commutation Topology

With 6-step trapezoidal control and synchronous rectification, two phase legs conduct at any instant:

- During the PWM on-time: high-side (HS) of leg A plus low-side (LS) of leg B.
- During the PWM off-time: LS of leg A (synchronous freewheel) plus LS of leg B.

Either way there are **always exactly two R_DS(on) in series** with the motor current, independent of duty cycle.

### 1.2 Temperature-Dependent Resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C (iterated operating point):

$$R_{DS}(110) = 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.51 = 2.27\,\text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Per-Motor and Per-FET Loss

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j) \cdot k_{ripple}$$

The ripple factor is k_ripple = 1 + (ΔI_pp²/12)/I². With ΔI_pp ≈ 4 A at 48 kHz on a typical 5" motor, k_ripple ≈ 1.0002, which is negligible.

$$P_{cond,motor} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 2.27\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{29.1\ W}$$

Each of the 6 FETs per motor carries current for one-third of the electrical cycle. The LS FETs carry somewhat more because they also freewheel. Using the uniform approximation:

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx 29.1/6 = \mathbf{4.85\ W\ average}$$

**PCB copper (often ignored, but significant):** a 2 oz, 3 mm wide, 15 mm long trace has R ≈ 0.6 mΩ per path. At 80 A that adds about 7.7 W per motor. Use pours on multiple layers stitched with vias, plus the bus-bar pads.

### 1.4 Battery vs. Phase Current

$$I_{bat} = \sum_{k=1}^{4} D_k \, I_{ph,k}$$

At 80 A phase on all four motors and D ≈ 0.9, the battery current is about 288 A. No nano-class XT60 or pad interface carries this. The real-world limit is therefore the battery path as well as the FETs.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one FET per motor hard-switches at a time: the PWM-leg HS when using complementary PWM.

### 2.1 Gate Currents During Transitions

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{src}+R_{g}} = \frac{12-3}{6+3} = 1.00\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{snk}+R_{g}} = \frac{3}{4+3} = 0.43\ \text{A}$$

### 2.2 Transition Times

$$t_{on} = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = \frac{10\,\text{nC}}{1.0\,\text{A}} = 10\ \text{ns}$$

$$t_{off} = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,off}} = \frac{10\,\text{nC}}{0.43\,\text{A}} = 23\ \text{ns}$$

### 2.3 Energy per Cycle

| Term | Expression | Value |
|---|---|---|
| E_on (V–I overlap) | ½ V I t_on = ½·25.2·80·10 ns | 10.1 µJ |
| E_off | ½ V I t_off = ½·25.2·80·23 ns | 23.2 µJ |
| E_rr (recovery into HS) | Q_rr · V_bus | 0.76 µJ |
| E_oss | ½ C_oss V² | 0.19 µJ |
| **E_sw total** | | **34.3 µJ** |

$$P_{sw} = E_{sw}\cdot f_{sw} = 34.3\,\mu\text{J}\times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.65\ W/motor}$$
