**Document scope:** This is a first-pass engineering model. The numbers are parameterised so you can substitute values from your chosen datasheets. Anything marked **[VERIFY]** must be checked against the actual part datasheets before layout release.

## 0. Architecture Findings (Read First)

Three findings change the design before any math is applied.

**F1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.**
ArduPilot runs on the H743 and outputs DShot600 commands. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU, because 48 kHz PWM, back-EMF zero-cross detection and bidirectional eRPM telemetry are hard-real-time per-motor tasks.
- Recommended: 4× AM32-capable MCUs (STM32G071 or AT32F421).
- Each MCU drives a 3-phase gate driver such as the FD6288Q class.
- The "4-in-1 ESC" is therefore 5 MCUs on one board.

**F2. 80 A × 4 channels on a 20×20 "nano" board is thermally and electrically unrealistic.**
Section 3 shows roughly 150 W of dissipation at 4×80 A against a board heat capacity of about 2 J/K. That gives an adiabatic rise of about 70 °C per second.
- **Recommendation:** treat "80 A burst" as **≤1 s per event on 30.5×30.5 with 5×6 mm PQFN FETs**.
- On 20×20 with 3.3×3.3 FETs, derate to about **35 A continuous / 50 A burst (≤1 s)**.

**F3. 48 kHz PWM is a reasonable choice.** It is common for AM32 on small, low-inductance motors. Switching loss scales linearly with frequency, so 24 kHz roughly halves the switching term computed below.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Topology

AM32 uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. In any 60° sector, current flows through:
- the PWM leg: high-side for fraction D, its synchronous low-side for (1−D);
- the return leg: low-side ON for the whole sector.

So at any instant, current flows through exactly **two FET channels in series**. The result is independent of D, apart from the dead-time interval, which is handled in §2.4.

### 2.2 Per-channel conduction loss

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds,25}\, k_T$$

$$R_{ds,hot} = 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.6 = 2.4\,\text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.4\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{30.7\ W}$$

### 2.3 Per-FET distribution

Each phase conducts 120° of every 360° electrical:
- The high side is active for 120°, scaled by D.
- The low side is active for 120°, plus (1−D) of the high side's interval when it acts as the synchronous rectifier.

**Average per FET:** 30.7 W / 6 ≈ **5.1 W**.

**Instantaneous peak in the always-on return low-side FET:**

$$80^2 \times 2.4\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{15.4\ W}$$

This lasts for 120° electrical. At about 2,000 eRPS that is roughly 0.17 ms, which is far shorter than the FET thermal time constant. Use the averaged value for package temperature and the peak value only for Z_th(transient) checks (§3.3).

### 2.4 Parasitic conduction terms

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Copper path | I² · R_pcb = 6400 × 0.5 mΩ | 3.2 W |
| Shunt | I² · R_sh = 6400 × 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| **Conduction subtotal per channel** | | **35.5 W** |

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg switches at a time in 6-step operation, so these terms apply once per channel.

### 3.1 Loss terms

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 30 ns × 48 kHz | 1.45 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw = 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 kHz | 0.048 W |
| Output capacitance | ½ · Q_oss · V · f_sw | 0.018 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f · I · 2 t_dt · f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 kHz | 0.61 W |
| Gate drive (2 FETs, from 10 V rail) | 2 · Q_g · V_GS · f_sw | 0.038 W |
| **Switching subtotal per channel** | | **≈ 2.17 W** |

### 3.2 Sensitivity

- **Overlap term:** ∝ f_sw · (t_r + t_f).
- **Dead-time term:** ∝ f_sw · t_dt. Dead time is worth tuning down, but only if verified with a scope at the low-side V_DS to avoid shoot-through.

### 3.3 Totals

| Scope | Dissipation |
|---|---|
| Per channel (35.5 + 2.2) | **≈ 37.7 W** |
| 4-in-1 total | **≈ 151 W** |
| FC section (H743 at 400 MHz, ~0.5 W + regulators ~1.5 W) | ≈ 2 W |
| **Board total** | **≈ 153 W** |
