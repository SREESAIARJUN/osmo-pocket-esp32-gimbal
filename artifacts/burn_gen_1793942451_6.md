# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #49): Power, Thermal & Bidirectional DShot Design

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and generates bidirectional DShot600. It does **not** commutate the motors. Each 4-in-1 ESC channel group is driven by its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, so the 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCU, not the H7. The models below are split along that boundary.

**Feasibility flag.** 80 A per channel on a "nano" (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board is only survivable as a short transient. The math in §3 shows this is a 1–3 second burst rating at best. You should not market it as a continuous rating.

**Baseline assumptions.** Replace these with your BOM datasheet values before release. The equations are general; the numbers depend on these inputs.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S, full charge) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| Burst phase current | I | 80 A |
| MOSFET (40 V, 5×6 or 3.3×3.3 DFN) | R_ds(on),25°C | 1.0 mΩ |
| Temperature coefficient | k_T | ~1.5× at T_j = 125 °C, so R_hot ≈ 1.5 mΩ |
| Rise + fall time | t_r + t_f | 20 ns + 20 ns |
| Total gate charge | Q_g | 60 nC @ 10 V |
| Gate-drain charge | Q_gd | 15 nC |
| Reverse recovery charge | Q_rr | 50 nC |
| Effective output capacitance | C_oss(er) | 1.5 nF |
| Body diode forward voltage | V_f | 0.8 V |
| Dead time | t_dt | 100 ns per edge |
| PCB copper, phase + bus path | R_cu | ~0.5 mΩ per channel (2 oz inner/outer, short pours) |

**Commutation model.** Six-step trapezoidal with complementary (synchronous) PWM, which is the AM32 default.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

In six-step commutation, two phase legs are active at any instant:

- The **PWM leg** has its high side on for duty D and its synchronous low side on for (1 − D).
- The **sink leg** has its low side held fully on.

So the motor current always flows through exactly **two channels-on**:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{HS}D + I^2 R_{LS}(1-D) + I^2 R_{LS} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds(on)}(T_j)
$$

Conduction loss is independent of duty when synchronous rectification is used. At 80 A, hot:

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ channel}
$$

**Per-FET distribution.** Each phase spends about 1/3 of the electrical cycle as the PWM leg, 1/3 as the sink, and 1/3 floating:

$$
\bar P_{FET} \approx \frac{19.2}{6} \approx 3.2\ \text{W average}
$$

The worst case occurs at locked rotor or very low eRPM, where one FET can hold the sink current for many milliseconds. That gives:

$$
P_{FET,peak} = I^2R = 9.6\ \text{W}
$$

This is the case that kills ESCs on stalls.

**Copper loss:**

$$
P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 3.2\ \text{W per channel}
$$

**Temperature feedback.** R_ds(on) rises with T_j, so solve iteratively:

$$
R(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.005/^\circ\text{C}
$$

Thermal runaway is avoided as long as the loop gain stays below 1:

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\,\theta_{ja} < 1, \qquad \text{i.e.}\quad 2 I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\,\theta_{ja} < 1
$$

At 80 A this gives θ_ja < ~7.8 °C/W per conducting pair. That is achievable only with excellent copper spreading and airflow.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches hard. The synchronous FET turns on after dead time, with near-ZVS, because the body diode is already conducting.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard-switched FET)

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Body diode reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}
$$

The induced extra turn-on current also adds overlap loss. Budget about +10% on P_sw.

### 2.3 C_oss discharge

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss(er)} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = 0.023\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction (two edges per period)

$$
P_{dt} = V_f\, I\,(2t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated mainly in the driver and the gate resistors.

$$
P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{dr} f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.058\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.6 Gate resistor sizing for 20 ns edges

The Miller plateau sets the edge rate:

$$
I_g \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{t_{sw}} = \frac{15\text{nC}}{20\text{ns}} = 0.75\ \text{A}
$$

$$
R_g \approx \frac{V_{dr} - V_{plateau}}{I_g} - R_{drv,int} \approx \frac{10 - 3.5}{0.75} - 3.5 \approx 5\ \Omega
$$
