### 0.2 Architecture Flags

1. **The H743 does not switch the FETs.** In every production 4-in-1 stack, the H743 FC sends DShot to four (or one quad-capable) ESC-side MCUs that generate the 48 kHz PWM. The loss model below lives on the ESC board. The DMA model lives on the FC.
2. **"Nano" vs. 80 A conflict.** At 80 A/motor the stack draws about 290 A from the battery. A 20×20 mm "nano" board cannot carry that current or reject that heat. Commercial 20×20 4-in-1s are 25–45 A class. I've modeled a **30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz board with a 10 g aluminium heatsink**. §2 shows this is roughly the minimum viable configuration. Treat this as a design-review blocker if the nano form factor is a hard requirement.

## 1. Electrical Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 1.1 Temperature-Dependent On-Resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At the converged T_j ≈ 117 °C (iterated in §2.4):

$$R_{DS(on)} = 0.9\,\text{mΩ} \times 1.55 = 1.40\ \text{mΩ}$$

### 1.2 Conduction Loss: 6-Step with Synchronous Rectification

Each half-bridge spends one 120° window as the high-side (HS) active phase and one as the low-side (LS) active phase. Phase current is I = 80 A, and PWM duty is D.

- **HS FET:** conducts only during its 120° window, at duty D.
- **LS FET:** synchronous-rectifies during the HS window for (1−D), then conducts fully during its own window.

$$P_{cond,HS} = \frac{D}{3} I^2 R_{DS(on)} \qquad P_{cond,LS} = \frac{(1-D)+1}{3} I^2 R_{DS(on)}$$

With I²R = 6400 × 1.40 mΩ = **8.96 W** and burst duty D = 0.9:

| FET | Expression | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| HS | 0.9/3 × 8.96 | **2.69 W** |
| LS | 1.1/3 × 8.96 | **3.29 W** |

**Note:** The LS FET is the hotter device at any D < 1. It should get the better copper and thermal vias.

### 1.3 Switching Loss at 48 kHz

Only the HS FET hard-switches. The LS commutates via its body diode, which is quasi-ZVS.

**Gate-charge-limited transition times:**

$$t_{on} = \frac{Q_{gd}+Q_{gs2}}{(V_{drv}-V_{pl})/(R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int})} = \frac{20\,\text{nC}}{(10-3)/3.2\,\Omega} = 9.1\ \text{ns}$$

$$t_{off} = \frac{20\,\text{nC}}{V_{pl}/R_{total}} = \frac{20\,\text{nC}}{0.94\,\text{A}} = 21.3\ \text{ns}$$

**Overlap energy per cycle:**

$$E_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_{on}+t_{off}) = \tfrac12(25.2)(80)(30.4\,\text{ns}) = 30.6\ \mu\text{J}$$
