# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #27): Power, Thermal and DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Two Design Flags

The requested job does not specify a MOSFET, gate driver, form factor, or ESC MCU, so I use representative values and mark them `[A]`. Every equation is parametric, so substitute datasheet values to get final numbers.

**Flag 1: architecture.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller MCU. It does not commutate motors. A 4-in-1 ESC needs four dedicated ESC MCUs (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32), and the H743 talks to them over bi-directional DShot600. The design below follows that split.

**Flag 2: the 80 A burst figure.** On a nano board (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm), 80 A per channel can only be a few-second transient. Section 3 shows this quantitatively. The continuous rating will be about 25 to 30 A per channel. Marketing should state burst duration and the starting temperature it assumes.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation Topology

The model assumes six-step commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM, which is the AM32 default.

- One leg is PWM-switched: the high side (HS) is on for duty D, and the low side (LS) is synchronous for 1−D.
- One leg holds its LS fully on.
- One leg floats and is used for BEMF sensing.

At every instant, motor current flows through exactly two MOSFET channels in series. Ignoring dead-time intervals (handled in §2.4):

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature Dependence and Self-Consistent Junction Temperature

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

$$T_j = T_{ref} + P(T_j)\, R_{th} \;\Rightarrow\; T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + 2I^2R_{25}R_{th}(1-25\alpha)}{1 - 2I^2R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_{th}}$$
