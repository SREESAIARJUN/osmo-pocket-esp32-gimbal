# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model (Job #19)

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Design-Risk Flags

**Architecture assumed.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It drives four dedicated ESC MCUs (AT32F421K8U7, running AM32) over bidirectional DShot600. The H743 does not commutate the motors. A single H7 running four sensorless 48 kHz FOC/6-step loops alongside ArduPilot is not a supported configuration.

**Design risk flags. Read these before layout.**

1. **Total current.** 4 × 80 A = 320 A of battery current. An XT60 is rated about 60 A continuous. A "nano" (20×20 mm) board cannot carry this. You need a 30.5×30.5 mm board minimum, 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner power planes, 10 or more layers, and an AS150/XT90-class lead.
2. **Shunt dissipation.** A 0.25 mΩ shunt at 320 A dissipates 25.6 W. Use per-ESC 0.5 mΩ shunts, or a Hall sensor (e.g. ACS772/TMCS1100-class) on the battery lead.
3. **Duration limit.** The 80 A rating is a time-limited burst only (≈2–4 s with all four motors loaded; see §3). It cannot be published as a continuous rating.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-step synchronous PWM

During each 60° commutation sector, only two phases are active:

- **HS_A** switches at duty D.
- **LS_A** is complementary, conducting for (1−D).
- **LS_B** is held fully ON as the return path.

The current magnitude I flows through every conducting device, so the instantaneous conduction loss per motor is:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This loss is **independent of duty cycle**, which is the key result for burst sizing.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence and ripple correction

The on-resistance rises with junction temperature:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1+\alpha_R (T_j-25)]$$

Current ripple adds to the RMS value:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I \approx \frac{V_{bus}D(1-D)}{L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

**Worked example** with L_ph = 15 µH (line-to-line 30 µH), D = 0.5 and V = 25.2 V:

- ΔI = 25.2 × 0.25 / (30e-6 × 48e3) ≈ 4.4 A.
- The ripple term ΔI²/12 = 1.6 A², which is negligible against 6400 A².

### 1.4 Copper path loss

Assume a 2 oz copper path of about 15 mm per phase with an effective resistance of ≈0.3 mΩ:

$$P_{Cu} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 0.3\text{ mΩ} \approx 3.8 \text{ W per motor}$$

This heats the board, not the junction. Do not ignore it.
