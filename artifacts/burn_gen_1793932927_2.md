# Job #9: STM32H743 Nano FC with 4-in-1 ESC (80 A Burst)
**Architecture Package Rev A: Loss Model, Thermal Model, Bidirectional DShot Timer/DMA Plan, Reference Netlists**

> **Scope and assumptions.** In this architecture the **STM32H743 is the flight controller**. It runs ArduPilot and generates bidirectional DShot600. Each motor channel is commutated by a dedicated ESC MCU (AM32-class, e.g. AT32F421) generating the **48 kHz** bridge PWM. The H743 does not drive gates directly; that would conflict with ArduPilot's scheduler and the DShot timers.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0065 × 100) = **1.65 mΩ**

### 2.2 Current path in 6-step synchronous mode

Two phases conduct at any instant.

- **PWM leg:** The high-side FET conducts for duty D. The complementary low-side FET conducts for (1−D).
- **Return leg:** The low-side FET is on for the full 60° sector.

$$P_{cond,PWM\ leg}= D\,I^2R + (1-D)\,I^2R = I^2R$$
$$P_{cond,return}=I^2R$$
$$\boxed{P_{cond,ch}=2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)}$$

Total conduction loss is therefore **independent of duty** when synchronous rectification is used. Per FET, average over a 360° electrical cycle, each FET is active for 1/3 of the time:

$$P_{cond,FET,HS}\approx \tfrac{1}{3}D\,I^2R,\quad P_{cond,FET,LS}\approx \tfrac{1}{3}(2-D)\,I^2R\ \text{(LS sees PWM complement + return)}$$

### 2.3 Numbers at 80 A burst

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W | 51.2 W |
| 100 °C | 1.49 mΩ | 19.1 W | 76.4 W |
| 125 °C | 1.65 mΩ | **21.1 W** | **84.5 W** |

### 2.4 Copper and shunt additions

Assumed 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner layers, and roughly 0.3 mΩ effective trace and plane path per phase pair:

- P_Cu ≈ 2 × 80² × 0.3 mΩ ≈ **3.8 W per channel**
- Shunt at 0.5 mΩ: P_shunt = I_DC² × R. With DC link current I_DC ≈ D × I_ph = 0.8 × 80 = 64 A, P_shunt ≈ **2.0 W** (single shared shunt per channel).

## 3. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the PWM leg switches. Losses are evaluated at the worst case, V_bus = 25.2 V and I = 80 A.

### 3.1 V-I overlap (hard switching, high side)

$$P_{VI}=\tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}=0.5(25.2)(80)(35\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz})=\mathbf{1.69\ W}$$

### 3.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction (low side)

$$P_{dt}=V_f\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw}=0.8(80)(400\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz})=\mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

### 3.3 Reverse recovery, dissipated in the high side

$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}=40\text{n}(25.2)(48\text{k})=\mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

### 3.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss}\approx Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}=50\text{n}(25.2)(48\text{k})=\mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

### 3.5 Gate drive (dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, two FETs switching)

$$P_g=2\,Q_gV_{gs}f_{sw}=2(50\text{n})(10)(48\text{k})=\mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

### 3.6 Switching subtotal

**P_sw,ch ≈ 3.1 W per channel**, or ≈ 12.3 W for the full 4-in-1.

**Sensitivity.** Dead time is the largest controllable term. Reducing it from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per channel. Do this only after verifying shoot-through margin on the scope at 80 A, because dead time cannot be safely reduced without that measurement.

Board efficiency at burst: P_out ≈ 4 × 25.2 × 64 ≈ 6.45 kW, so η ≈ 98.2 %.

**Hottest single FET:** the low side, carrying complement plus return conduction. At full throttle (D ≈ 0.95), each FET's conducting interval carries roughly 80² × 1.65 mΩ ≈ 10.6 W. This peak instantaneous value is what drives the junction transient.

## 5. Thermal Model

### 5.1 Network (Cauer, lumped)

```
P_FET ─► [Zth_jc: 0.8 K/W, τ≈5 ms] ─► [Rth_c-board via 12× 0.3 mm vias: ~4 K/W, τ≈80 ms]
      ─► Board node C_b ─► [R_b-air] ─► T_amb
```

### 5.2 Board heat capacity

For a 30.5 × 30.5 mm, 8-layer board (2 oz copper, 1.6 mm, with FETs and caps):

- Copper ≈ 4.5 g × 0.385 J/gK = 1.7 J/K
- FR4 ≈ 3.5 g × 1.1 J/gK = 3.9 J/K
- Components ≈ 0.6 J/K

**C_b ≈ 6.2 J/K**

On the 20 × 20 mm nano footprint, C_b ≈ 2.8 J/K. That footprint is **not viable for 80 A**; see the verdict in 5.6.

### 5.3 Board-to-air resistance

Under prop wash at 10–15 m/s, with both sides exposed and roughly 18 cm² of area:

$$R_{b\text{-}air}=\frac{1}{hA}\approx\frac{1}{(60\ \text{W/m}^2\text{K})(0.0018)}\approx 9\ \text{K/W}$$

A clip-on aluminum heatsink brings this to about 4 K/W.

### 5.4 Board temperature during a burst

$$T_b(t)=T_{b0}+P\,R_{b\text{-}air}\left(1-e^{-t/\tau_b}\right),\quad \tau_b=R_{b\text{-}air}C_b\approx 56\ \text{s}$$

Because τ_b ≫ t_burst, the board is effectively adiabatic during a burst:

$$\Delta T_b\approx \frac{P\,t}{C_b}=\frac{120\,t}{6.2}\approx 19.4\ \text{K/s}$$
