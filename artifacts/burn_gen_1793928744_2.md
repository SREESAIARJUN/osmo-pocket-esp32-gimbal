# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 80 A 4‑in‑1 ESC: Design Model (Job #5)

**Scope:** loss model, electrothermal model, DShot600 bidirectional timer/DMA plan, reference netlist.

**How to read this:** Every number below comes from the stated assumptions. Swap in the datasheet values for your chosen MOSFET, driver and PCB stack-up and re-run the equations. The structure of the model stays the same.

---

## 0. Architecture Decision (read first)

The brief combines two things that should not share one processor.

**Recommended partitioning (standard practice):**

| Function | Processor | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Flight control, EKF, scheduling | **STM32H743** running ArduPilot | ArduPilot does not commutate BLDC motors. It outputs DShot. |
| Commutation, 48 kHz PWM, BEMF/zero-cross, telemetry | **4 × ESC MCU** (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or EFM8 running BlueJay) | Hard real-time. Isolated per motor. Firmware is field-proven. |
| Link between them | Bidirectional DShot600, 1 wire per motor | Gives eRPM telemetry for ArduPilot's harmonic notch |

The 48 kHz switching figure therefore belongs to the ESC MCUs. The DShot600 DMA allocation belongs to the H743. They are modelled separately below.

**Physics caveat on "nano" + 80 A:** A 20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm board cannot hold 80 A per motor continuously. The analysis below treats 80 A as a **burst rating**. It gives the burst duration the thermal mass can absorb, which is the honest way to state this spec.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current Distribution (6-step with synchronous freewheeling)

Over one electrical period, each phase leg spends:
- ⅓ of the time as the **PWM'd high leg**: high-side (HS) on for fraction D, low-side (LS) freewheeling for (1−D).
- ⅓ of the time as the **return leg**: LS fully on.
- ⅓ of the time **floating**: no current, BEMF is sensed here.

This gives the squared RMS currents with phase current I:

$$
I_{HS,rms}^2 = \frac{D}{3}I^2,\qquad
I_{LS,rms}^2 = \frac{1}{3}I^2 + \frac{1-D}{3}I^2 = \frac{2-D}{3}I^2
$$

### 2.2 Temperature-Dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

### 2.3 Per-FET Conduction Loss

$$
P_{cond,HS} = \frac{D}{3}I^2 R_{DS}(T_j),\qquad
P_{cond,LS} = \frac{2-D}{3}I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

**Worst case at 80 A burst, D = 0.95, T_j = 125 °C, R_DS = 1.305 mΩ:**

| FET | I²_rms (A²) | P_cond |
|---|---|---|
| HS | 0.3167 × 6400 = 2027 | **2.65 W** |
| LS | 0.35 × 6400 = 2240 | **2.92 W** |

- Per motor (3 HS + 3 LS): **16.7 W**
- Per board (×4 motors): **66.8 W conduction**

### 2.4 Copper and Shunt Loss (often underestimated)

Assume a 0.5 mΩ shunt plus 0.6 mΩ effective bus/phase copper per motor path. The return path always carries I during the active ⅔ of the cycle:

$$
P_{Cu} \approx \tfrac{2}{3}I^2 (R_{sh}+R_{Cu}) = \tfrac{2}{3}(6400)(1.1\,\text{m}\Omega) = 4.7\ \text{W per motor}
$$

That is **18.8 W per board**. Use 2 oz or 3 oz outer layers plus stitched inner planes, or this term dominates.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches, which is ⅓ of the electrical period. Let k = ⅓.

### 3.1 Hard-Switching Overlap (HS turn-on and turn-off)

Plateau charge Q_sw = Q_gs2 + Q_gd = 9 nC. Driver current is about 1.2 A, so each transition takes:

$$
t_{on}\approx t_{off}\approx \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_g}=7.5\ \text{ns}
$$

$$
P_{sw,HS} = k\cdot\tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_{on}+t_{off}) f_{sw}
= \tfrac13\cdot\tfrac12(25.2)(80)(15\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.242\ W}
$$

Add about 20 % for the source-inductance Miller penalty in a cramped layout: **≈0.29 W per HS FET.**

### 3.2 Reverse Recovery (LS body diode → HS turn-on)

$$
P_{rr} = k\,Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = \tfrac13(30\text{n})(25.2)(48\text{k}) = 12\ \text{mW}
$$

This is dissipated mostly in the HS FET.

### 3.3 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction (LS)

$$
P_{dt} = k\cdot V_f I \cdot 2t_{dt} f_{sw} = \tfrac13(0.8)(80)(100\text{n})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.102\ W}
$$

### 3.4 C_oss Loss (HS turn-on)

$$
P_{oss}= k\cdot\tfrac12 C_{oss}V^2 f_{sw}= \tfrac13\cdot\tfrac12(1.2\text{n})(635)(48\text{k}) = 6\ \text{mW}
$$

### 3.5 Gate Drive (dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET)

$$
P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw}\cdot k = 40\text{n}\cdot 10\cdot 48\text{k}\cdot\tfrac13 = 6.4\ \text{mW per FET}
$$

That is ≈0.15 W per board. This matters for the driver LDO budget.
