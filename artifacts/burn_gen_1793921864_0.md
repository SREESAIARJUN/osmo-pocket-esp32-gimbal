## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Flags

**Topology.** The STM32H743 is the **flight controller**. It does not commutate motors. A 4-in-1 ESC contains **four independent ESC MCUs** (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or an EFM8 running Bluejay). The 48 kHz PWM is an ESC-firmware setting. Bidirectional DShot600 is the link between the H743 timers and the four ESC MCUs.

```
 LiPo 6S ──┬── Bulk caps ── 4× [ESC MCU → 3-ph gate driver → 6× MOSFET → Motor]
           │                       ▲ DShot600 bidir (1-wire per motor)
           └── 5V/3V3 BEC ── STM32H743 FC ── IMU / Baro / OSD / UARTs
```

**Feasibility flags. Resolve these before layout:**

| # | Issue | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | **80 A burst per motor ×4 = 320 A phase.** Battery current is 320 A × D. At full throttle that is about 250–320 A from the pack. | XT60 (60 A continuous) and 12 AWG leads are inadequate. Use XT90 or a direct solder 10 AWG pigtail. |
| F2 | **"Nano" form factor.** A 20×20 mm board cannot carry 320 A or spread about 65 W. | Target **30.5×30.5 mm minimum**, with the ESC on a separate stacked board. The model below assumes a 30.5 mm ESC. |
| F3 | **"Burst" must be defined** as a duration and duty cycle. | The thermal model (§4) gives you t_max as a function of the burst current. "80 A" alone is not a spec. |
| F4 | **The H743 is EOL-stable but power hungry** (about 0.3–0.5 W at 400–480 MHz). | Keep it physically isolated from the FET copper on the stack. |
