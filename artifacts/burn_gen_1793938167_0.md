# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #31): Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and Bidirectional DShot Allocation

## 0. Feasibility Finding

**An 80 A/channel burst rating on a nano (20×20 mm) stack is not thermally supportable.** The derivation below puts total ESC dissipation at about **65–70 W at 80 A × 4**. A 20×20 board's thermal mass allows only about 3–4 s of adiabatic heating before copper and solder limits are reached. The model is valid as a parametric tool. Section 3.4 gives the ratings it supports for nano versus 30.5 mm formats.

Two architectural clarifications shape the rest of this document:

1. **The H743 does not switch the MOSFETs.** Each of the four ESC channels runs its own MCU (e.g., AT32F421 with AM32, or an EFM8 with BLHeli_S). The 48 kHz PWM, deadtime, and commutation are ESC firmware parameters. The H743 only generates bidirectional DShot600 and decodes eRPM telemetry.
2. **All MOSFET and driver parameters below are placeholders.** They are marked ⟨example⟩ and must be replaced with values from the datasheets of your chosen parts before sign-off.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

In 6-step commutation, two phases conduct. One leg is PWM-switched with high-side duty D and synchronous low-side for 1−D. The other leg's low-side is held on.

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

The current always passes through exactly two MOSFET channels in series:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot R_{ds,hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 \cdot R_{ds,hot} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds,25}\,k_T$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 0.70\text{ m}\Omega \cdot 1.5 = \mathbf{13.44\ W}$$

Add copper loss for the current path (pad → bus → FET → phase pad, about 0.25 mΩ effective on 2 oz copper ⟨estimate⟩):

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \cdot 0.25\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{1.6\ W}$$

### 2.2 Switching Loss (one hard-switched leg)

**Overlap (V–I crossover):**
$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery (low-side body diode → high-side turn-on):**
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

**Output capacitance:**
$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

**Deadtime diode conduction (two edges per period):**
$$P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 400\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

**Gate drive (two FETs switching per period; dissipated in the driver/R_g, not the FET):**
$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 60\text{n} \cdot 10 \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

### 2.3 Totals

| Component | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 13.44 W | 53.8 W |
| Copper | 1.60 W | 6.4 W |
| Switching overlap | 1.94 W | 7.8 W |
| Deadtime | 1.23 W | 4.9 W |
| Q_rr + Q_oss + gate | 0.18 W | 0.7 W |
| **Total** | **≈18.4 W** | **≈73.6 W** |

Efficiency at 80 A × 6S, roughly 2 kW per motor: 1 − 18.4/2016 ≈ **99.1 %**. Efficiency is not the problem. **Heat density is.**

Sensitivity notes:
- Conduction dominates and scales as **I²**. At 40 A, conduction drops to about 3.4 W per channel.
- Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. Moving from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves about 1.6 W per channel at 80 A, at the cost of audible noise and current ripple.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Steady-State Network (per FET pair, then board)

```
Tj ──Rθjc(1.0 K/W)── Tcase ──Rθcs(0.5 K/W, solder+vias)── PCB ──Rθpa── Ambient
```

| Board | Rθ_pa, still air | Rθ_pa, prop-wash (~5 m/s) |
|---|---|---|
| 20×20 nano, 6-layer 2 oz | ~14 K/W | ~5 K/W |
| 30.5×30.5, 6-layer 2 oz | ~8 K/W | ~2.5 K/W |

**Steady state is impossible for the nano at 73.6 W.** With prop-wash: ΔT = 73.6 × 5 = 368 K.

### 3.2 Transient (Burst) Model

Lumped board heat capacity for the nano:
- PCB ≈ 2.5 g FR4 × 1.1 J/g·K
- Copper ≈ 1.5 g × 0.385 J/g·K
- FETs/caps ≈ 0.6 g × 0.7 J/g·K

C_th ≈ 2.75 + 0.58 + 0.42 ≈ **3.75 J/K**

Thermal time constant: τ = Rθ·C = 5 × 3.75 ≈ 19 s.

Board temperature:
$$\Delta T(t) = P\,R_\theta\,(1 - e^{-t/\tau})$$

Maximum burst time to a 110 °C PCB limit, starting at 45 °C (ΔT_allow = 65 K):
$$t_{max} = -\tau \ln\!\left(1 - \frac{\Delta T_{allow}}{P R_\theta}\right) = -19 \ln(1 - 65/368) \approx \mathbf{3.7\ s}$$

The junction adds a fast rise on top of this. With per-FET dissipation of about 6.7 W and Z_θjc(10 ms) ≈ 0.4 K/W, the junction sits only about 3 K above the case. The FETs therefore survive. **The board, the solder joints (SAC305 creep), and the electrolytic capacitor ripple heating are what fail.**

### 3.3 Capacitor Ripple (often the real limit)

$$I_{C,rms} \approx I\sqrt{D(1-D)} \;\xrightarrow{D=0.5}\; 0.5 \cdot 80 = 40\text{ A}_{rms}\ \text{per channel}$$

Bulk capacitors on a nano stack cannot carry this. Plan for low-ESR ceramics per leg (≥4 × 10 µF/50 V X7R 1206) plus an external 35 V/1000 µF low-ESR electrolytic at the battery pad.

### 3.4 Recommended Ratings

| Format | Continuous | Burst (≤5 s, prop-wash) |
|---|---|---|
| 20×20 nano | 25–30 A | 40–45 A |
| 30.5×30.5 | 50–55 A | 70–80 A |

**Recommendation:** to keep the 80 A requirement, move to 30.5 mm with 3 oz inner layers and 5×6 mm FETs. Otherwise, de-rate the nano to 45 A burst.

## 4. Bidirectional DShot600 on the H743 (ArduPilot)

### 4.1 Protocol Timing

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Bit rate | 600 kbit/s → 1.667 µs/bit |
| Frame | 16 bits (11 throttle + 1 telemetry request + 4 CRC, inverted for bidirectional) |
| Frame duration | 26.7 µs |
| Turnaround | ~30 µs |
| ESC reply | 21-bit GCR at 5/4 × 600 = 750 kbit/s → 28 µs |
| Total exchange | ≈ 85 µs |
| Loop budget | ≤ 8 kHz loop rate feasible (125 µs period) |

The line is idle-high and inverted.
