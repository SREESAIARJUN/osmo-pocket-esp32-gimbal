### Architectural point

ArduPilot cannot commutate the motors directly from the H743. Bidirectional DShot implies the following structure:

- **H743 (ArduPilot)** sends DShot600 on 4 timer channels.
- **4 independent ESC MCUs** (AT32F421 or STM32G071 class, running AM32 or BLHeli_32) each drive a 3-phase gate driver and 6 MOSFETs.
- Each ESC MCU replies with eRPM telemetry over the same wire.

The model below covers both the power stage (ESC MCU domain) and the timer/DMA plan (H743 domain).

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A burst)

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step with synchronous rectification

During any 60° electrical sector, phase A is the PWM'd high side and phase B is the static low side.

- **PWM on-time (D):** current flows HS_A → motor → LS_B.
- **PWM off-time (1−D):** current freewheels LS_A (synchronous) → motor → LS_B.

Energy dissipated per sector, per unit time:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[\,D + (1-D) + 1\,] = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This result is **independent of duty cycle**, which is the key benefit of synchronous rectification. Without synchronous rectification, the (1−D) term becomes a diode term, V_SD·I·(1−D), which is far worse at 80 A.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{/K (typ. trench Si)}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds ≈ 1.45 × R_ds,25 ≈ **1.45 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.45\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{18.6\ W\ per\ motor}$$

Over a full electrical revolution, each of the 6 FETs carries 1/3 of the conduction energy:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{ds} = 3.1\ \text{W per FET}$$

### 1.4 Parasitic resistances (often larger than expected)

| Element | R | Loss @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Low-side shunt (per channel) | 0.5 mΩ | I²R·(active fraction ≈ 1) = **3.2 W** |
| Phase pad + trace, 2 oz, short | ~0.3 mΩ × 2 phases active | 2 × 80² × 0.3 m = **3.8 W** |
| Bus planes (shared) | ~0.2 mΩ | depends on combined current |

Treat copper as **≈ 4–7 W per channel at 80 A**. This loss is distributed across the board, which actually helps hot-spot temperature.
