# FOC Motor Control and Dual-IMU Sensor Fusion for a 3-Axis Gimbal

I don't have the Job #58 specification, so this design states its assumptions explicitly. Every parameter is a named constant you can replace with your actual motor, encoder, and MCU values.

---

## 0. Assumptions and Timing Architecture

| Item | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Motor | Gimbal BLDC (e.g., GBM4108-class): 12N14P, pole pairs `p = 7`, `R = 5.5 Ω`, `L = 2.0 mH`, `Kt ≈ 0.08 N·m/A` |
| Encoder | 14-bit absolute magnetic (AS5048A / MT6701 class) |
| MCU | Cortex-M4F/M7 with FPU (STM32G4/H7 class), 3-phase PWM with center-aligned ADC trigger |
| IMUs | 2× ICM-42688-P on SPI at 24 MHz, ODR = 8 kHz, both mounted on the camera plate |
| Bus | `Vbus = 12 V` |

**Loop rates and bandwidths.** Each loop runs at least 5× slower in bandwidth than the loop inside it.

| Loop | Rate | Closed-loop bandwidth |
|---|---|---|
| Current (FOC, d/q PI) | 20 kHz | ~1.5–2 kHz |
| Velocity (PI + feedforwards) | 4 kHz | ~150–250 Hz |
| Angle (P or PD + rate feedforward) | 1 kHz | ~20–40 Hz |
| IMU fusion / attitude | 8 kHz in, 1 kHz attitude out | — |

**Sensor roles:**
- The encoder supplies electrical angle for commutation and a PLL speed estimate for the inner damping term.
- The IMUs supply inertial camera attitude and rate. These are the primary stabilization feedback.

## 1. FOC Current Loop

### 1.1 Transforms

**Clarke transform** (amplitude-invariant, with `i_a + i_b + i_c = 0`):

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \frac{1}{\sqrt3}(i_a + 2 i_b)
$$

**Park transform**, with electrical angle $\theta_e = p\,(\theta_m - \theta_{\text{off}})$:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d \\ i_q\end{bmatrix}=
\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha \\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}
$$

The inverse Park transform is the transpose of this matrix.

### 1.2 Plant Model (Surface PMSM, $L_d = L_q = L$)

$$
v_d = R i_d + L\dot i_d - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad
v_q = R i_q + L\dot i_q + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e \lambda_m
$$

Electromagnetic torque:

$$
\tau = \tfrac32 p \lambda_m i_q = K_t i_q
$$

### 1.3 PI Tuning by Pole-Zero Cancellation

Each axis is the first-order plant $G(s) = 1/(Ls+R)$. A PI controller $C(s) = K_p + K_i/s$ with $K_i/K_p = R/L$ cancels the plant pole, giving an open loop of $\omega_c/s$:

$$
K_p = L\,\omega_c,\qquad K_i = R\,\omega_c,\qquad \omega_c = 2\pi f_c
$$

$$
f_c \le \frac{f_{\text{PWM}}}{10}\ \text{to}\ \frac{f_{\text{PWM}}}{20}
$$

The upper bound on $f_c$ accounts for the 1.5-sample transport delay of ADC sampling plus PWM update.

### 1.4 Discrete Implementation with Anti-Windup

The decoupling feedforward terms cancel the cross-coupling and back-EMF from the plant model:

$$
v_d^{ff} = -\omega_e L i_q,\qquad v_q^{ff} = \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e\lambda_m
$$

The PI update, with sample period $T_s$ and back-calculation anti-windup:

$$
\begin{aligned}
e[k] &= i^*[k]-i[k]\\
v_u[k] &= K_p e[k] + x[k] + v^{ff}[k]\\
v[k] &= \mathrm{sat}(v_u[k])\\
x[k+1] &= x[k] + K_i T_s\, e[k] + K_{aw}\,(v[k]-v_u[k])
\end{aligned}
$$

Use $K_{aw} \approx 1/K_p \cdot K_i T_s \cdot$ (tune in the range 0.5–1).

**Voltage-vector limit.** The linear SVPWM region is a circle:

$$
\sqrt{v_d^2+v_q^2}\le \frac{V_{bus}}{\sqrt3}
$$

Saturate with d-axis priority:
- $v_d = \mathrm{clamp}(v_d,\ \pm V_{max})$
- $v_q = \mathrm{clamp}\left(v_q,\ \pm\sqrt{V_{max}^2-v_d^2}\right)$
