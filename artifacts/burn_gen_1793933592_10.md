| Q_oss | 40 nC | |
| V_SD | 0.8 V | body diode forward |
| t_r, t_f | 12 ns each | gate driver about 1 A sink/source |
| Dead time t_dt | 100 ns per edge | |

### 1.2 Conduction Loss

In six-step drive, one high-side and one low-side FET carry the phase current at any instant. With synchronous rectification, the current is on a FET channel nearly 100% of the time (the dead time is handled separately).

$$
P_{cond,bridge} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

Each FET conducts 120° of 360°, so the duty share per FET is 1/3:

$$
P_{cond,FET} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

At I = 80 A and T_j = 125 °C, R = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0045 × 100) = **1.45 mΩ**:

- P_cond,bridge = 2 × 6400 × 1.45 mΩ = **18.6 W**
- P_cond,FET = **6.2 W** per conducting window, or about 3.1 W time-averaged

**PCB copper (not negligible).** Assume a phase path of about 15 mm at 6 mm width over 4 × 2 oz layers in parallel:

R_Cu ≈ ρL/(wt) = (1.72e-8 × 0.015)/(0.006 × 4 × 70e-6) ≈ 0.154 mΩ, and about 0.20 mΩ hot.

Battery bus plus phase path ≈ 0.4 mΩ, so I²R ≈ **2.6 W per motor**.

### 1.3 Switching Loss at f_sw = 48 kHz

In complementary PWM, only the PWM-modulated high-side FET is hard-switched. The low side commutates through its body diode.

| Term | Formula | Value @ 25.2 V, 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw | 0.5·25.2·80·24e-9·48e3 = **1.16 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 30e-9·25.2·48e3 = **0.036 W** |
| Output charge | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw | 0.5·40e-9·25.2·48e3 = **0.024 W** |
| Dead-time diode | V_SD·I·2t_dt·f_sw | 0.8·80·200e-9·48e3 = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (in driver, not FET) | Q_g·V_GS·f_sw·2 | 50e-9·10·48e3·2 = **0.048 W** |

**P_sw,total ≈ 1.83 W per motor.** Switching is only about 8% of the total at 80 A. This design is **conduction-dominated**, so R_DS(on) and copper are the levers, not f_sw. Dropping to 24 kHz saves only about 0.9 W per motor and costs current ripple and audible noise.

**Ripple check (justifies 48 kHz).** Assume L_phase-phase ≈ 15 µH and 50% duty:

ΔI = V·D(1−D)/(L·f) = 25.2 × 0.25 / (15e-6 × 48e3) ≈ 8.8 A pk-pk. This is acceptable. At 24 kHz it would be about 17.5 A.

### 1.4 Total Burst Loss

| Contributor | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (hot) | 18.6 W | 74.4 W |
| Copper | 2.6 W | 10.4 W |
| Switching + dead time | 1.83 W | 7.3 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, if on the bus) | ~1.6 W (I_bus < I_phase) | ~6 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 24.6 W** | **≈ 98 W** |

Efficiency at 80 A: P_out ≈ 22.2 V × 80 A × 4 ≈ 7.1 kW, so η ≈ 98.6%. The efficiency is fine. **Heat density is the actual problem.**

## 2. Electro-Thermal Coupling (Runaway Check)

Because R_DS rises with temperature, the loss feeds back on itself:

$$
T_j = T_a + R_{th,ja}\left[ P_{sw} + k I^2 R_{25}\left(1+\alpha(T_j-25)\right)\right]
$$

Solving for T_j gives:

$$
T_j = \frac{T_a + R_{th}\left[P_{sw} + kI^2R_{25}(1-25\alpha)\right]}{1 - R_{th}\,k I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha}
$$

**Stability requires R_th · k·I²·R₂₅·α < 1.**

Per FET, k = 1/3, I = 80 A, so kI²R₂₅α = 2133 × 1e-3 × 0.0045 = 0.0096 W/K. Runaway occurs only if R_th > 104 K/W. That is impossible here, so the design is **stable** and the burst limit is purely thermal mass.
