# Gimbal Job #9: FOC Motor Control and Dual-IMU Sensor Fusion

The specification below makes assumptions about Job #9's hardware. Each is listed in §0. Replace those parameters with the measured values for your build. The structure, equations and code apply regardless.

## 0. System Assumptions and Timing Architecture

| Item | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| MCU | STM32G4 / H7 class, FPU, 170+ MHz |
| Motors | 3× gimbal BLDC, 12N14P (pole pairs `Np = 7`), R ≈ 5–10 Ω, L ≈ 2–4 mH |
| Encoders | 14-bit absolute magnetic (AS5048A / MT6701), SPI |
| Current sense | 2-phase low-side shunts, sampled at PWM center |
| IMUs | 2× ICM-42688-P on separate SPI buses, mounted on the camera plate symmetrically about the payload CoM, with the second IMU rotated 180° about z |
| Supply | 12–16 V |

The loops are rate-separated by at least 5× bandwidth at each stage:

```
PWM / Current loop (FOC)     20 kHz   BW_i ≈ 1.5–2 kHz
Encoder PLL + velocity loop  10 kHz   BW_ω ≈ 150–250 Hz
IMU read + filters + fusion   8 kHz   (ICM ODR = 8 kHz)
Attitude (angle) loop         2 kHz   BW_θ ≈ 20–40 Hz
Cogging LMS adaptation        1 kHz   (slow, μ small)
```

## 1. Field-Oriented Control (Inner Current Loop)

### 1.1 Coordinate transforms

Measure the phase currents `i_a`, `i_b`. Then `i_c = −i_a − i_b`.

**Clarke transform (amplitude-invariant):**

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \frac{1}{\sqrt3}(i_a + 2 i_b)
$$

**Electrical angle.** Here `θ_m` is the mechanical encoder angle and `θ_0` is the calibrated d-axis offset:

$$
\theta_e = N_p(\theta_m - \theta_0) \bmod 2\pi
$$

**Park transform:**

$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix}=
\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}
$$

### 1.2 Plant in the dq frame (SPM motor, L_d = L_q = L)

$$
v_d = R i_d + L\dot i_d - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad
v_q = R i_q + L\dot i_q + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e\lambda_m
$$

Torque is proportional to `i_q` only:

$$
\tau_e = \tfrac32 N_p \lambda_m\, i_q = K_t\, i_q
$$

### 1.3 PI current controller with decoupling and back-EMF feedforward

**Gain selection.** Choose the gains so the PI zero cancels the electrical pole `R/L`. The closed loop then becomes first order with bandwidth `ω_c`:

$$
K_{p,i}= L\,\omega_c,\qquad K_{i,i}= R\,\omega_c
\quad\Rightarrow\quad \frac{i_q}{i_q^*}=\frac{\omega_c}{s+\omega_c}
$$

A typical choice is `ω_c = 2π·1500` rad/s at a 20 kHz loop rate. Keep `ω_c·T_s < 0.5`.

**Discrete law.** `T_s` is the sample period and `e_x = i_x^* − i_x`:

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_d[k] &= I_d[k-1] + K_{i,i}T_s\,e_d[k] + K_{aw}\,(v_d^{sat}[k-1]-v_d^{uns}[k-1])\\
v_d^{uns}[k] &= K_{p,i}e_d[k] + I_d[k] - \hat\omega_e L\, i_q[k]\\
v_q^{uns}[k] &= K_{p,i}e_q[k] + I_q[k] + \hat\omega_e L\, i_d[k] + \hat\omega_e\lambda_m
\end{aligned}
$$

The anti-windup term is back-calculation with `K_aw ≈ 1/K_{p,i}`, scaled by the integration rate.

**Voltage limiting.** Saturation is circular, with `d` given priority:

$$
v_d^{sat} = \mathrm{clamp}(v_d,\pm V_{max}),\quad
v_q^{sat} = \mathrm{clamp}\!\left(v_q,\ \pm\sqrt{V_{max}^2 - v_d^{sat\,2}}\right),
\quad V_{max} = \tfrac{V_{bus}}{\sqrt3}\cdot m_{max}
$$

Use `m_max ≈ 0.95` to leave a sampling window for the low-side shunts.

### 1.4 Inverse Park and SVPWM

**Phase-advance compensation.** Use `θ_e' = θ_e + 1.5 ω̂_e T_s` to compensate for the one-and-a-half-sample PWM delay. Then:

$$
v_\alpha = v_d\cos\theta_e' - v_q\sin\theta_e',\qquad
v_\beta = v_d\sin\theta_e' + v_q\cos\theta_e'
$$

**Phase voltages:**

$$
v_a = v_\alpha,\quad v_b = -\tfrac12 v_\alpha + \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}v_\beta,\quad
v_c = -\tfrac12 v_\alpha - \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}v_\beta
$$

**Min-max zero-sequence injection.** This is equivalent to SVPWM:

$$
v_0 = -\tfrac12(\max(v_{abc}) + \min(v_{abc})),\qquad
D_x = \tfrac12 + \frac{v_x + v_0}{V_{bus}}
$$
