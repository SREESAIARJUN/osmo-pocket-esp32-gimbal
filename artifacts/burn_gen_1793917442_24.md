### Architectural note before the math

80 A *per channel* on a nano AIO is a burst rating. It is not a simultaneous four-motor rating. 4 × 80 A = 320 A from the pack, which exceeds:
- the XT60 rating (~60 A continuous), and
- realistic 12–14 AWG pigtails.

The analysis below covers the worst case (all four channels at 80 A) and shows it is only survivable for well under a second. The burst spec should be defined as single-channel, ≤ 1–2 s, with a total-pack current limit enforced in firmware. This is set via ArduPilot `BATT_*` limits and AM32 current limit.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation topology

With six-step trapezoidal commutation and complementary (synchronous) PWM, the current path at any instant is:

- **Driven leg** (PWM-chopping): the high-side (HS) conducts for duty D. The low-side (LS) synchronously rectifies for (1−D), minus dead time.
- **Return leg:** the low-side conducts continuously for that 60° sector.
- **Floating leg:** off, used for back-EMF zero-crossing detection.

So current always flows through exactly two channels' worth of R_DS(on) in series. The per-motor conduction loss is therefore:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 RMS current including PWM ripple

Peak-to-peak ripple (worst case at D = 0.5), assuming L_LL ≈ 15 µH for a 2306-class motor:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L\,f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{15\mu \times 48k} = 8.75\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{DC}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 6.4 = 6406.4\ \text{A}^2$$

Ripple is a 0.1 % effect at 48 kHz, so it is negligible. This is one of the arguments for 48 kHz: it gives low ripple and a quieter motor, at the cost of switching loss (Section 2).

### 1.3 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on) (closed-loop solution)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond per motor | P_cond, 4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.50 mΩ | 19.2 W | 76.9 W |
| 75 °C | 1.95 mΩ | 25.0 W | 99.9 W |
| 100 °C | 2.18 mΩ | 27.9 W | 111.5 W |
| 125 °C | 2.40 mΩ | 30.7 W | 123.0 W |

Because loss rises with temperature, a positive-feedback criterion applies. A thermal runaway check must satisfy:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\cdot R_{th,ja} < 1 \;\Rightarrow\; I^2\cdot 2R_{25}\,\alpha \cdot R_{th} < 1$$

At 80 A: 6400 × 2 × 1.5 mΩ × 0.006 = 0.115 W/K. Steady-state runaway therefore occurs if R_th(ja, per motor) > 8.7 K/W. On a nano board the effective per-motor R_th to ambient is roughly 15–25 K/W.

**80 A continuous is thermally unstable on this form factor.** The burst can only be survived through thermal capacitance (Section 4).
