## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation, at any instant two phases carry the motor current I. The current flows through one high-side (HS) FET and one low-side (LS) FET.

During the PWM off-time, synchronous rectification turns on the complementary LS FET. The current path therefore always contains exactly **two conducting FETs**, apart from the dead-time intervals handled in §2.

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

With an ideal trapezoidal current, I_rms ≈ I_pk during conduction, so the worst case is I_rms = 80 A.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R = 1.0\,\text{mΩ} \times 1.5 = 1.5\,\text{mΩ}$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each of the 6 FETs conducts for 1/3 of the electrical period, either as the active switch or as the synchronous rectifier.

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} = \frac{19.2}{6} = 3.2\ \text{W}, \qquad P_{cond,FET,inst} = I^2R = 9.6\ \text{W}$$

The electrical frequency at high eRPM is roughly 1–3 kHz. That is much faster than the die thermal time constant (about 1–10 ms), so the junction mostly sees the **average** loss. The ripple term is covered in §3.4.

### 1.4 Copper and shunt losses

These are not negligible at 80 A.

**Phase pad/trace.** A typical 2 oz path, 5 mm long, 3 mm wide, with 4 paralleled layers gives about 0.3 mΩ:

$$P = 80^2 \times 0.3\,\text{mΩ} = 1.9\ \text{W per motor}$$

**Current shunt** (0.25 mΩ, if fitted on the DC bus path):

$$P = I_{bus}^2 \times 0.25\,\text{mΩ}$$

At I_bus ≈ D·I_ph, this is up to about 1.6 W.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing leg switches. One HS/LS pair commutates at f_sw.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap (HS FET)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Body-diode conduction during dead time (LS FET)

There are two dead-time edges per period:

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery (dissipated mostly in the HS FET)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Output-capacitance loss

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive loss

This is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET.

$$P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.058\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Switching total

$$P_{sw,motor} \approx 1.45 + 0.61 + 0.05 + 0.04 = \mathbf{2.15\ W}$$

**Sensitivity.** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. Going from 24 kHz to 48 kHz costs about 1.1 W per motor at 80 A. It buys lower current ripple and quieter motors. For 80 A bursts on a nano board, **24 kHz is the thermally safer choice**.
