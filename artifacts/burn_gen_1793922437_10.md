## 0. System Architecture and an Early Design Flag

```
 6S LiPo (25.2 V max) ──► XT60 ──► VBAT plane ──┬──► 4× ESC power stage (6 FETs each, 24 total)
                                                 ├──► 5 V / 9 V buck (VTX, receiver, servos)
                                                 └──► 3.3 V LDO (MCU, IMU, baro)

 STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ── DShot600 bidirectional (4 lines) ──► 4× AT32F421 (AM32 firmware)
                                                                └► FD6288Q 3-phase gate driver ► FETs
```

**The H743 does not commutate the motors.** Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. The H743 sends DShot frames and receives eRPM telemetry. This split matters for three reasons:

- The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs.
- The DMA problem is solely on the H743 side.
- The thermal problem is solely in the power stage.

> **Design flag: aggregate current.** 80 A burst on all four channels at once means **320 A** at the battery input. A nano-format board (about 35 × 35 mm, 25.5 mm mounting) cannot carry that through an XT60 (rated about 60 A continuous) or through its own copper for more than seconds. The model below shows that "80 A" is credible only as a **per-channel, ≤ 2–3 s burst rating**. Continuous capability is roughly 30–35 A per channel with airflow. The model makes these limits explicit so the datasheet rating is defensible.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current path

With trapezoidal (120°) commutation, two phase legs carry current at any instant: one high-side path and one low-side path.

- **PWM on-time (duty D):** current flows through HS_a and LS_b.
- **PWM off-time (1 − D):** current freewheels through LS_a and LS_b, because synchronous rectification is active.

At every point in the PWM cycle, the motor current therefore flows through **exactly two FET channels**, independent of D (dead time is treated separately in §3.5).

### 2.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{ds} = 0.90 \times (1 + 0.0065 \times 85) = 0.90 \times 1.553 = 1.40\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 2.3 RMS current including ripple

The phase inductance produces a triangular ripple ΔI on top of the DC current:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{2 L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}$$

Worst case, using a low-inductance racing motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH line-to-line), D = 0.5, and V_bemf ≈ 12 V:

$$\Delta I = \frac{13.2 \times 0.5}{2 \times 15\times10^{-6}\times 48\,000} \approx 4.6\ \text{A} \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 1.76\ \text{A}^2$$

This is negligible against 6400 A², so I_rms ≈ 80.0 A.

### 2.4 Conduction loss per ESC channel

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j) = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.40\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{17.9\ W}$$

### 2.5 Per-FET average

Over one electrical revolution, each leg conducts for 2/3 of the time, and that time is split between its HS and LS devices. The loss therefore spreads evenly across all 6 FETs:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{17.9}{6} \approx 3.0\ \text{W (average)}, \qquad \text{peak instantaneous} \approx 9\ \text{W}$$

The electrical period at burst speed is about 0.3–1 ms. That is shorter than the die thermal time constant (about 2–5 ms), so the average value is appropriate for junction temperature calculations.
