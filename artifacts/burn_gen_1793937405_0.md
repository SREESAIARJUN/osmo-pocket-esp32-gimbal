## 0. Architecture Corrections Before We Start

These three points change the model, so they come first.

1. **The H743 does not run the ESC.** In a 4-in-1 stack, each motor bridge is commutated by a dedicated ESC MCU (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). The H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600. The 48 kHz PWM and all switching losses live on the ESC MCU side. I model both, but they are separate firmware domains.
2. **"80A burst" must be defined.** I take it as **80 A RMS phase current per motor, for ≤ 2 s**, at 6S (25.2 V full charge). Battery current is roughly D × I_phase, so this is not 320 A from the pack unless all motors are at full duty.
3. **80 A × 4 on a 20×20 / 30×30 board is a transient rating only.** Section 2 shows roughly 120 W of dissipation. Steady state is physically impossible without forced airflow and a heatsink. The math below sets the burst time limit.

## 2. Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, exactly **two FETs carry I_ph at every instant**: one in the PWM'd leg (HS during D, LS during 1−D) and one in the static leg.

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

$$
P_{cond} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

- R_ds = 1.5 mΩ × (1 + 0.0045 × 100) = **2.175 mΩ**
- P_cond = 2 × 80² × 0.002175 = **27.8 W per motor**

Copper trace and via resistance adds roughly 0.3–0.5 mΩ per current path. Budget about +4 W per motor unless the phase pours are 2 oz across 4+ layers.

### 2.2 Switching losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·30n·48k | 1.45 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·(2·t_dt)·f_sw = 0.8·80·200n·48k | 0.61 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 30n·25.2·48k | 0.036 W |
| C_oss | ≈ ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw | 0.024 W |
| Gate drive | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw = 2·40n·10·48k | 0.038 W (dissipated mostly in driver/R_g) |
| **P_sw total** | | **≈ 2.16 W** |

**Design insight:** at 48 kHz, conduction dominates (93%). Dropping to 24 kHz saves only about 1 W per motor and adds current ripple. Raising to 96 kHz costs about 2 W per motor. 48 kHz is a reasonable choice. The bigger lever is R_ds and copper, not f_sw.

### 2.3 Totals

| Scope | Power |
|---|---|
| Per motor | 27.8 + 2.16 ≈ **30 W** |
| Per FET (average, 6 FETs) | **≈ 5 W** |
| 4-in-1 board, all motors at 80 A | **≈ 120 W** (+ ~16 W copper) |

### 2.4 Bus capacitor ripple (often overlooked)

$$
I_{C,rms} \approx I_{ph}\sqrt{D(1-D)} \;\xrightarrow{D=0.5}\; 40\ \text{A}_{rms}\ \text{per motor}
$$

This requires:
- Per motor: ≥ 4 × 10 µF 50 V X7R 1210 placed directly at the half-bridges.
- Shared: ≥ 1000 µF low-ESR 35 V bulk at the pads, plus a TVS (e.g., SMBJ30A).

Undersized decoupling here is the #1 cause of FET avalanche failure on 4-in-1 boards, ahead of thermal limits.

### 3.2 Burst (adiabatic) heating rate

$$
\frac{dT_{board}}{dt} = \frac{P_{total}}{C_{th}} = \frac{120\ \text{W}}{5.2\ \text{J/K}} \approx 23\ \text{K/s}
$$

$$
