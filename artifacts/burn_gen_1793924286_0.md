# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC (Job #56): Power-Stage, Thermal, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Design Model

## 0. Architecture Corrections

Three points need settling before the math, because they change the results.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In a 4-in-1 ESC stack, the H743 runs ArduPilot on the flight controller. Each ESC channel has its own small MCU (typically AT32F421, STM32G071, or GD32E230) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM and decodes DShot. So the H743's job here is DShot generation and eRPM capture, not commutation.
2. **"80A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is thermally marginal.** The model below shows it is survivable only for seconds, not tens of seconds. Treat 80A as a per-motor burst rating with a defined duration (calculated in Section 2.5).
3. **Datasheet parameters are placeholders.** All component values below are representative of 40 V PDFN3.3×3.3 FETs. Substitute your actual MOSFET datasheet values into the equations. The structure of the model is what transfers.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

### 1.2 Six-step synchronous commutation

At any instant, two phases carry current. During the PWM on-time the path is HS(A) → LS(B). During the off-time, synchronous rectification makes the path LS(A) → LS(B). In both cases the current flows through two channels in series:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

This holds independent of duty cycle D, because synchronous rectification replaces the body diode for all but the dead time.

Per FET, over one electrical cycle:

- **High-side:** conducts for D × 1/3 of the cycle, so P_HS = (D/3)·I²·R
- **Low-side:** conducts for 1/3 of the cycle, plus the freewheel share (1−D)/3, so P_LS = ((2−D)/3)·I²·R

Low-side FETs run hotter at partial throttle. At D = 1 (burst), the load is symmetric.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80A

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond per motor |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W |
| 100 °C | 1.34 mΩ | 17.1 W |
| 125 °C | 1.45 mΩ | 18.6 W |

### 1.4 Electrothermal self-consistency (thermal runaway check)

The junction temperature depends on the loss, and the loss depends on the junction temperature:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + R_{\theta}\cdot\left[k R_{25}(1+\alpha(T_j-25))\right]$$

Solving for T_j:

$$T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + R_\theta k R_{25}(1-25\alpha)}{1 - \alpha R_\theta k R_{25}},\quad k = I^2\ \text{(per-FET share)}$$

**Stability condition:** α·R_θ·k·R₂₅ < 1.

- At 80A per FET (k = 6400 A²) with R₂₅ = 1 mΩ, the loop gain is 0.0288·R_θ.
- Runaway therefore occurs if the effective R_θ (junction to ambient) exceeds about 35 K/W.

A nano board without forced airflow can reach that value. This is why the design must use a burst rating, not a continuous one.

### 1.5 PCB copper loss (often forgotten)

$$R_{trace} = \frac{\rho_{Cu}\,L}{w\,t},\quad \rho_{Cu}=1.72\times10^{-8}\,\Omega\text{m}\,(1+0.0039\,\Delta T)$$

Example: a 10 mm phase path, 3 mm wide, 2 oz copper (70 µm), on 2 layers in parallel:

- R = 0.41 mΩ
- At 80A, P = 2.6 W per phase path (two phases active, so roughly 5 W per motor)

For a 4-in-1 nano board, use 3 oz or heavier copper on inner layers and stitch with vias (≥ 0.3 mm drill, ~1 A continuous each).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per motor, one HS/LS commutation pair switches each PWM period.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard-switched HS)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(20\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = 0.97\text{ W}$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.036\text{ W}$$

### 2.3 Body-diode reverse recovery (LS diode, after dead time)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.048\text{ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction (two edges per period)

$$P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = 0.61\text{ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET die)

$$P_{g} = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 0.048\text{ W}$$

### 2.6 Burst loss budget at T_j ≈ 100 °C

| Loss term | Per motor | 4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 17.1 W | 68.4 W |
| Copper traces | ~5 W | ~20 W |
| V–I switching | 0.97 W | 3.9 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W | 2.4 W |
| Q_rr + Q_oss + gate | 0.13 W | 0.5 W |
| **Total** | **~23.8 W** | **~95 W** |

**Design insight:** at 80A, conduction loss is about 90% of the total. Switching loss is minor. Reducing f_sw to 24 kHz would save under 1 W per motor and add current ripple. The real lever is R_DS(on): parallel FETs per switch, or move to a 0.6 mΩ class part. Dead time is the second-best lever; tune it down to the minimum safe value (50–80 ns is typical with an FD6288-class driver).

### 2.7 Current ripple sanity check at 48 kHz

$$\Delta I_{pp} \approx \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

With L_ph = 15 µH (typical racing motor, line-to-line) and D = 0.5:

- ΔI_pp ≈ 8.75 A
- This adds an RMS contribution of ΔI/√12 ≈ 2.5 A, which is negligible next to 80A.

## 3. Thermal Model and Burst Duration

### 3.1 Junction rise above the PCB, per FET

At D = 1, about 4 FETs carry significant current per motor at any time (averaged). Per-FET conduction during its active third of the cycle is I²R = 8.6 W instantaneous, or ~2.9 W averaged. Use the transient Z_θJC at the 120° electrical window, which is a few ms at typical eRPM and therefore effectively averaged:

$$\Delta T_{jc} \approx 2.9\text{ W} \times 1.5\text{ K/W} \approx 4.4\text{ K}$$

Conclusion: the die is not the bottleneck. The board is.
