# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Systems Architecture Reference (Job #16)

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Feasibility

Three issues need resolving before any numbers are useful.

1. **The H743 does not drive the power stage.** In a 4-in-1 ESC, each motor (or the whole board) has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. That MCU is typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071, and it generates the 48 kHz PWM and the commutation. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to the ESC MCUs only over bidirectional DShot600. The math below treats these as two separate domains.

2. **"80A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is thermally implausible.** The model in §2 shows roughly 17 W dissipated per motor at 80 A. Across four motors that is about 70 W in roughly 4 cm² of copper. That is survivable only for a few seconds, and only with a heavy copper stack. Choose one of these options:
   - **Option A (recommended):** a 30×30 mm or 36×36 mm stack, with 80 A as a ≤ 2 s burst rating.
   - **Option B:** keep 20×20 mm and derate to about 35–40 A burst.

3. **This is a reference design, not a released schematic.** All derivations show their assumptions so you can substitute real datasheet values. The netlist in §5 is a structural netlist for capture into KiCad or Altium. ERC/DRC and part-number verification are still required.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

In six-step operation, exactly two phase legs carry current at any instant. With synchronous rectification, each active leg always has one conducting switch: the high-side FET during the on-time and the low-side FET during the off-time. The current path therefore always passes through two FET channels, independent of duty cycle D:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I^2\, R_{ds(on)}(T_j) \cdot \underbrace{[D + (1-D)]}_{=1}$$

Temperature dependence of on-resistance:

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) \approx R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \mathrm{K^{-1}} \Rightarrow k_T \approx 1.5 \text{ at } 110\ ^\circ\mathrm{C}$$

At 80 A:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times (0.8\ \mathrm{m\Omega} \times 1.5) = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.2\ \mathrm{m\Omega} = \mathbf{15.4\ W\ per\ motor}$$

PCB copper loss adds:

$$P_{Cu} = 2 \times 6400 \times 0.3\ \mathrm{m\Omega} = 3.8\ \mathrm{W}$$

This loss is spread across the board, not concentrated in the FETs.

Two consequences:

- **Per-FET load.** Each FET conducts for about 1/3 of the electrical cycle. Per-FET average loss is therefore about 15.4 / 6 × 2 ≈ 5.1 W, but instantaneous peaks reach 7.7 W. The thermal model in §4 uses the peak value for junction transients.
- **Parallel FETs help quadratically.** With two FETs in parallel per switch (n = 2), P_cond drops to 1/n of the single-FET value, giving 7.7 W per motor. At 80 A, paralleling is strongly recommended.

## 3. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

In six-step operation, only one leg switches during PWM; the other active leg is static. Each loss term is listed below, using V = 25.2 V, I = 80 A, and f = 48 kHz.

**(a) V–I overlap (hard switching):**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V I (t_r + t_f) f = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\,\mathrm{ns} \times 48\,\mathrm{k} = 0.97\ \mathrm{W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f = 30\,\mathrm{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\mathrm{k} = 0.036\ \mathrm{W}$$

**(c) Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V f = 0.024\ \mathrm{W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction (two dead-time intervals per period):**

$$P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2 t_{dt} f = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\,\mathrm{ns} \times 48\,\mathrm{k} = 0.61\ \mathrm{W}$$

**(e) Gate drive (2 FETs switching):**

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{gs} f = 2 \times 50\,\mathrm{n} \times 10 \times 48\,\mathrm{k} = 0.048\ \mathrm{W}$$

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET.

**Total switching loss:**

$$P_{sw} \approx \mathbf{1.69\ W\ per\ motor}$$

Design levers:

- **Dead time is the largest lever after V–I overlap.** Cutting t_dt to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per motor. Shortening dead time requires a well-matched gate driver.
- **Raising f_sw to 96 kHz** doubles P_sw to about 3.4 W, so it is not justified at 80 A.

### Total Dissipation at 80 A Burst

| Term | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 15.4 W | 61.4 W |
| Switching | 1.7 W | 6.8 W |
| PCB copper | 3.8 W | 15.4 W |
| **Total** | **20.9 W** | **83.6 W** |

### Input Capacitor Ripple

The worst case occurs at D = 0.5:

$$I_{C,rms} = I\sqrt{D(1-D)} = 80 \times 0.5 = 40\ \mathrm{A_{rms}} \text{ per motor (uncorrelated across motors)}$$

The board therefore needs MLCC banks with at least 8× 10 µF 50 V X7R 1206 per motor bridge, close to the half-bridges. In addition, an external low-ESR bulk electrolytic (≥ 1000 µF, 35 V) must sit at the battery leads. Without it, ringing will exceed the FETs' 40 V rating.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Steady-State Thermal Network

```
T_j ─[R_jc 1.2 K/W]─ T_case ─[R_cb 0.5 K/W]─ T_board ─[R_ba]─ T_amb
```

Assumed values:
- R_jc ≈ 1.2 K/W per FET, the thermal pad to copper.
- R_cb ≈ 0.5 K/W through solder and vias.
- R_ba (board to ambient, prop-wash convection at h ≈ 50 W/m²K):
  - 30×30 mm board, 2 faces, A ≈ 1.8×10⁻³ m²: R_ba ≈ 1/(hA) ≈ **11 K/W**
  - 20×20 mm board: R_ba ≈ **25 K/W**

**Continuous current limit** for T_board ≤ 100 °C at T_amb = 35 °C, which gives ΔT = 65 K:

$$P_{max} = 65/11 \approx 5.9\ \mathrm{W}\ (30\times30), \quad 65/25 = 2.6\ \mathrm{W}\ (20\times20)$$

Total loss scales as P(I) ≈ 4 × 2I²R_eff with R_eff ≈ 1.5 mΩ (FET plus copper, hot). Solving for current:

- 30×30 mm: I_cont ≈ √(5.9 / (8 × 1.5 mΩ)) ≈ **22 A per motor, all four loaded**
- 20×20 mm: I_cont ≈ **15 A per motor**

### 4.2 Burst Transient (Lumped Capacitance)

The board heat capacity is estimated from its components:
- 30×30 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz copper: copper ≈ 3.5 g × 0.385 J/gK, FR4 ≈ 2.5 g × 1.1 J/gK.
- FETs and solder add roughly 1 J/K.
- Total: C_th ≈ **5 J/K**.
