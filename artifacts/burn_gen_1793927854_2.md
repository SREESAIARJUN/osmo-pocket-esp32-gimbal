# STM32H743 AIO "Nano" Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Systems Model (Job #43)

## 0. Architecture Correction and Assumptions

**The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical choices are the STM32G071, AT32F421 or GD32E230. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to those four MCUs. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs. The model below follows this split.

**Plausibility flag.** The figures below are an "80 A burst per channel" on a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 nano board.
- On 6S, four channels at 80 A simultaneously is 320 A and about 8 kW. Neither the board copper nor the pads can sustain that.
- Treat "80 A" as a single-channel or short-burst rating. The thermal section quantifies how short.

All MOSFET parameters are **representative placeholders** for a 30–40 V, ~1 mΩ PQFN 3.3×3.3 or 5×6 part. Replace them with your actual datasheet values.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (6-step trapezoidal, complementary PWM)

### 1.1 Commutation model

At any instant two phases conduct and one floats. Assume high-side PWM with synchronous rectification on the active leg, and the return leg's low-side held fully ON. Each phase leg spends its 360° electrical cycle as follows:

| Sector | Duration | High-side (HS) | Low-side (LS) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Source | 120° (1/3) | ON for D | ON for (1−D) (sync rectification) |
| Sink | 120° (1/3) | OFF | ON 100 % |
| Float | 120° (1/3) | OFF | OFF |

### 1.2 Per-FET average conduction loss

$$P_{HS} = I^2 R_{ds,hot}\,\frac{D}{3}$$

$$P_{LS} = I^2 R_{ds,hot}\,\frac{(1-D) + 1}{3}$$

Summing over one leg gives a result independent of D:

$$P_{leg} = \tfrac{2}{3}\,I^2 R_{ds,hot}$$

Summing over the motor's three legs:

$$\boxed{P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds,hot}}$$

This is physically consistent: exactly two FETs are always in the current path.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, R_ds,hot = 1.6 mΩ

The base term is I²R = 6400 × 0.0016 = 10.24 W.

| D | P_HS | P_LS | P_leg |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.9 | 3.07 W | 3.75 W | 6.83 W |
| 0.5 | 1.71 W | **5.12 W** (worst FET) | 6.83 W |

- MOSFET conduction per motor: P_cond,motor = **20.5 W**.
- PCB copper per motor: I²·(2·R_trace) = 6400 × 0.001 = **6.4 W**.
- The low-side FET is the thermally critical device at mid-throttle bursts. Give it the best copper and via fields.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches at any instant, so one leg's switching loss is the per-motor average.

### 2.1 Transition times (gate-charge method)

Turn-on gate current:

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}+R_{drv,src}} = \frac{7}{2.2+1+2} \approx 1.35\ \text{A}$$

$$t_{on} = \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{g,on}} \approx 8.9\ \text{ns}$$

Turn-off gate current, using the diode bypass so R_g,ext ≈ 0:

$$I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{g,int}+R_{drv,snk}} \approx \frac{3}{2.5} = 1.2\ \text{A}$$

$$t_{off} \approx 10\ \text{ns}$$

### 2.2 Loss components (per switching leg, 80 A, 25.2 V)

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_on + t_off)·f_sw = ½ × 2016 × 18.9 ns × 48k | 0.91 W |
| Coss | Q_oss·V·f_sw | 0.05 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 0.04 W |
| Dead-time diode | V_f·I·2·t_dead·f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48k | 0.61 W |
| Gate drive (×2 FETs) | Q_g·V_drv·f_sw ×2 | 0.04 W (in driver and R_g) |
| **Total per motor** | | **≈ 1.65 W** |

**Sensitivity.** Dead-time loss is 37 % of switching loss. Cutting t_dead from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per motor. AM32 exposes dead-time per target; validate it against shoot-through on a scope.
