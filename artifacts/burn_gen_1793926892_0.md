## 0. Architectural Correction (Read First)

The STM32H743 should **not** commutate the motors directly. ArduPilot does not run ESC commutation firmware, and bi-directional DShot is a protocol between an FC and separate ESC MCUs. The correct partitioning is:

| Block | Device | Firmware |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 at 400 MHz (the ArduPilot default; 480 MHz is not used) | ArduPilot (ChibiOS) |
| ESC ×4 | AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071 (one per motor) | AM32 or BLHeli_32 |
| Gate driver ×4 | FD6288Q (3-phase half-bridge, 250 V-class bootstrap) | n/a |
| Power stage ×4 | 6× N-MOSFET per motor (24 total) | n/a |

The 48 kHz PWM is an ESC-MCU setting. The H743 only emits DShot600 frames and captures the eRPM telemetry that comes back.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

**Current path.** In six-step commutation with synchronous rectification, phase current flows through exactly two MOSFETs at all times:
- the high-side FET of one leg, or its low-side complement during the off-time, and
- the low-side FET of another leg.

Because the complementary FET carries the current during the off-time, the conduction loss is nearly independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

**Thermal runaway check.** R_DS(on) depends on junction temperature, so loss and temperature are coupled:

$$T_j = T_a + R_{\theta,ja}\, P(T_j)$$

This converges only if $R_{\theta,ja}\cdot 2I^2R_{25}\alpha < 1$.

**Copper and connection losses.** Add the board copper and solder pads:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{trace}$$

A typical value is R_trace ≈ 0.5 mΩ per phase path at 2 oz with plane stitching.

## 3. Switching Loss Model (per motor, at 48 kHz)

Only one leg is PWM-switched at any instant. The loss terms are:

$$P_{sw} = \underbrace{\tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f}_{\text{V-I overlap}} + \underbrace{Q_{rr}V f}_{\text{recovery}} + \underbrace{\tfrac12 Q_{oss}V f}_{\text{C}_{oss}} + \underbrace{2V_F I t_{dt} f}_{\text{dead-time diode}} + \underbrace{6\,Q_g V_{GS} f_g}_{\text{gate}}$$

where f_g is the average gate-toggle rate. For simplicity, the gate term below assumes 2 FETs toggling at f_sw.

### Case A: I = 80 A, V = 25.2 V

| Term | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | 0.5 · 25.2 · 80 · 35 ns · 48 k | 1.69 W |
| Q_rr | 25.2 · 40 nC · 48 k | 0.048 W |
| C_oss | 0.5 · 30 nC · 25.2 · 48 k | 0.018 W |
| Dead time | 2 · 0.8 · 80 · 100 ns · 48 k | 0.61 W |
| Gate (driver dissipation) | 2 · 50 nC · 10 · 48 k | 0.048 W |
| **Total P_sw** | | **≈ 2.42 W per motor** |

### Case B: I = 20 A

The overlap and dead-time terms scale linearly with current. Total P_sw ≈ 0.42 + 0.15 + 0.11 ≈ **0.68 W per motor**.

Efficiency looks excellent in both cases. The problem is **power density**, not efficiency.
