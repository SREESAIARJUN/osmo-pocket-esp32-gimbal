# Job #62: STM32H743 Nano AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC
## Electro-Thermal Model, Switching Analysis, Bidirectional DShot600 Timer Allocation, and Reference Netlist

---

## 0. Architecture Corrections

Three issues in the brief need resolving before modelling:

1. **The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In any 4-in-1 ESC, each motor has its own ESC MCU running AM32, Bluejay or BLHeli_32. That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends **DShot600** to those four MCUs. These are two separate timing domains:
   - The 48 kHz switching frequency is set in ESC firmware.
   - The DShot600 timing is generated by H743 timers and DMA.
2. **80 A per motor on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board is not thermally or electrically sustainable.** The model below quantifies this. With realistic parts, 80 A is a **burst rating of roughly 1–3 s**, not a continuous rating. Commercial nano AIOs are honestly rated at 12–25 A continuous.
3. **A 4×80 A battery feed (320 A) through a nano PCB** needs a dedicated copper-bar or wire-solder strategy. Current-sense shunts at that current dissipate watts. Both points are addressed below.

All numbers are engineering estimates from stated assumptions. They must be validated against the final MOSFET datasheet, the actual stack-up, and IR thermography on a prototype.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current path in 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation with synchronous rectification

At any instant, current flows through exactly one high-side-equivalent and one low-side-equivalent conduction path:
- During PWM on-time: HS(phase A) → motor → LS(phase B).
- During off-time: LS(phase A) (synchronous freewheel) → motor → LS(phase B).

Two R_ds(on) elements are therefore always in series:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2 \, I_m^2 \, R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

With temperature dependence:

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ /\mathrm{K}$$

### 2.2 Per-FET distribution

Each FET conducts during 120° of the 360° electrical cycle.
- High side: duty D during its interval.
- Synchronous low side: (1 − D) during freewheel, plus 100% when it is the "return" leg.

Averaged over a full electrical cycle:

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} \approx \frac{P_{cond,ESC}}{6}$$

In the worst case, the low-side FET carries more. At low duty it is near-continuous during its 120°, giving up to ~2× the HS share.

### 2.3 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per ESC | ×4 ESCs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.0 mΩ | 12.8 W | 51.2 W |
| 125 °C | 1.5 mΩ | **19.2 W** | **76.8 W** |

### 2.4 PCB copper conduction loss (often ignored, significant here)

Phase trace example: 3 oz (105 µm), 5 mm wide, 8 mm long:

$$R = \rho \frac{L}{w\,t} = 1.72\times10^{-8} \cdot \frac{0.008}{0.005 \cdot 105\times10^{-6}} = 0.26\ \mathrm{m\Omega}$$

$$P = 80^2 \times 0.26\,\mathrm{m\Omega} \approx 1.7\ \mathrm{W\ per\ phase\ trace}$$

Paralleling across 3–4 layers with via stitching (≥ 20 vias of 0.3 mm per transition) gives roughly 0.5 W per active path, so:
- About **1–1.5 W per ESC** for phase and bus copper combined.
- **Bus plane at 320 A**: even 0.1 mΩ of plane resistance dissipates **10 W**. The battery pads must be placed directly at the bus capacitors and FET drains, with the plane split radially.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In 6-step drive, only **one leg per ESC is PWM-switched** at a time.

### 3.1 Hard-switching (V-I overlap) loss

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_m (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}
= 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\,\mathrm{ns} \cdot 48\,\mathrm{kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 3.2 Reverse-recovery loss (synchronous FET body diode)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\,\mathrm{nC} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\,\mathrm{k} = 0.060\ \mathrm{W}$$

### 3.3 Output capacitance loss

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 40\,\mathrm{nC} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\,\mathrm{k} = 0.024\ \mathrm{W}$$

### 3.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM period:

$$P_{dt} = V_f I_m (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 100\,\mathrm{ns} \cdot 48\,\mathrm{k} = 0.31\ \mathrm{W}$$
