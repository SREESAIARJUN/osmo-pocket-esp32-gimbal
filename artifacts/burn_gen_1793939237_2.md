**Architecture note.** The H743 runs ArduPilot and does not commutate the motors directly. Each motor needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. The H743 sends DShot frames to these ESC MCUs and receives eRPM telemetry back from them.

**Feasibility flag.** 80 A per motor on a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm "nano" board is at the edge of physical plausibility, even as a burst:
- The board would dissipate about 80 W at burst (see §3).
- 320 A total would have to flow through board copper and the battery pad.

Most real nano AIOs are rated 25–45 A continuous. The math below shows that 80 A is achievable only as a short burst, on heavy copper, with forced airflow. Treat the 80 A figure as a transient rating, not a continuous one.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0045 × 85) = **1.38 mΩ**

### 1.2 Trapezoidal (6-step) commutation with synchronous rectification

In each commutation step, two half-bridges are active:
- **PWM leg:** current flows through the high-side switch for duty D and through the complementary low-side switch for (1−D). Because rectification is synchronous, the current always flows through one channel resistance.
- **Static leg:** the low-side is fully on for the whole step.

The conduction loss per motor is therefore approximately independent of duty:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS} = 2 I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 1.3 Copper path

Add the board copper resistance in series with the switches:
- R_pcb ≈ 0.3 mΩ per phase path, assuming 2 oz copper across multiple layers.
- Add the current shunt if one is fitted (assume 0.5 mΩ on the DC bus; this term is not multiplied by 2).

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2I^2(R_{DS} + R_{pcb}) + I^2 R_{shunt}$$

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 110 °C

| Term | Value |
|---|---|
| 2 · 6400 · 1.38 mΩ | 17.66 W |
| 2 · 6400 · 0.30 mΩ | 3.84 W |
| 6400 · 0.5 mΩ | 3.20 W |
| **P_cond per motor** | **24.7 W** |

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

All values below are per motor. Only one leg is hard-switching at any instant.

**(a) V·I overlap loss (hard-switched high-side):**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 20\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.968\ \text{W}$$

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction (2 edges per period):**

$$P_{dt} = V_F I \cdot 2t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.614\ \text{W}$$

**(c) Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

**(d) Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 20\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.024\ \text{W}$$

**(e) Gate drive (2 switching devices, dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors):**

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 30\text{n} \cdot 10 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.029\ \text{W}$$

**Total P_sw per motor ≈ 1.67 W.**

**Design insights:**
- At 48 kHz, switching loss is about 6% of total. Conduction dominates, so prioritize low R_DS(on) over low Q_g.
- Dead time is the largest switching term. Reducing it from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per motor, but only if the driver propagation-delay matching allows it.
- The f_sw sensitivity is dP/df ≈ 1.67 W / 48 kHz = **34.8 mW per kHz** per motor.

## 3. Total Loss and Thermal Model

### 3.1 Power budget at 80 A burst

| | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 24.7 W | 98.8 W |
| Switching | 1.67 W | 6.7 W |
| ESC MCU + driver + LDO | ~0.15 W | 0.6 W |
| **Total** | **26.5 W** | **~106 W** |

If all four motors simultaneously draw 80 A, the board dissipates about 100 W.

### 3.2 Device-level junction rise

The 6 FETs of a motor share its loss over one electrical cycle. At 20,000 rpm with 14 poles, the electrical frequency is 2.33 kHz, a period of 0.43 ms. That is far shorter than the MOSFET thermal time constant of about 1 ms or more, so the average loss per device can be used:

$$P_{FET} \approx \frac{P_{cond,FET} + P_{sw}}{6} \approx \frac{17.66 + 1.67}{6} = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

$$\Delta T_{JC} = P_{FET} \cdot R_{\theta JC} = 3.2 \cdot 1.5 = 4.8\ \text{K}$$

The die is not the bottleneck. The board is.

### 3.3 Board-level transient: lumped RC model

$$C_{th}\frac{dT_b}{dt} = P_{tot} - \frac{T_b - T_a}{R_{\theta BA}}$$

$$T_b(t) = T_a + P_{tot}R_{\theta BA}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right), \quad \tau = R_{\theta BA}C_{th}$$

**Thermal capacitance.** For an 8-layer 25.5 mm board with 3 oz copper:
- Copper ≈ 4.5 g × 0.385 J/g·K
- FR4 ≈ 3 g × 1.1 J/g·K
- Components ≈ 2 g × 0.7 J/g·K

C_th ≈ 1.7 + 3.3 + 1.4 ≈ **6.4 J/K**
