# STM32H743 Nano AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC): 80 A Burst, 48 kHz PWM, Bidirectional DShot600 Design Package

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Red Flags

I don't have the Job #12 BOM or layout files, so every device parameter below is a stated assumption. Swap in your actual datasheet values. The equations are written so you can re-run them directly.

**Assumed architecture.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does **not** commutate the motors. The ESC half uses one dedicated ESC MCU per motor (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32-class firmware), each driving a 3-phase bridge through a gate driver. The H743 talks to the ESC MCUs over bidirectional DShot600.

**Red flags to resolve before layout:**

1. **80 A per motor on a nano (20×20 mm) board is not thermally sustainable.** The math below shows about 25 W per motor and about 100 W per board at full burst. This is a ~1–2 s burst rating, not a continuous one.
2. **Copper capacity.** Total battery current of 4 × 80 A = 320 A peak through 20 mm-wide copper requires stacked 2 oz pours on multiple layers plus direct solder-pad battery entry.
3. **48 kHz PWM is high for 80 A.** Switching loss scales linearly with frequency. 24 kHz roughly halves the switching term, which section 2 quantifies.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (Per Motor, 6-Step with Synchronous Rectification)

### 1.1 Topology current paths

In each 60° commutation sector, one phase is PWM-switched (high-side Q_H on for duty D, low-side Q_L sync-rectifying for 1–D). A second phase has its low-side held on (return path). The third phase floats (BEMF sensing).

Conduction energy per sector:

$$P_{cond} = I_{rms,ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)\,[\underbrace{D}_{Q_H} + \underbrace{(1-D)}_{Q_L\ sync} + \underbrace{1}_{Q_L\ return}] = 2\,I_{rms,ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This form is independent of D, which is a useful result: with synchronous rectification, worst-case conduction loss does not depend on throttle duty, only on current.

### 1.2 Ripple correction

With phase inductance L_ph and peak-to-peak ripple ΔI:

$$\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}-E_{bemf})\,D}{2L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}, \qquad I_{rms}^2 = I_{avg}^2\left(1+\frac{r^2}{12}\right),\ r=\frac{\Delta I}{I_{avg}}$$

For a typical 5" motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH line-to-line, so 7.5 µH per phase), V_bus = 25.2 V, E ≈ 12.6 V, D = 0.5, f = 48 kHz:

- ΔI ≈ 8.75 A
- r ≈ 0.11
- Correction ≈ +0.1% (negligible at 80 A)

### 1.3 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on), solved self-consistently

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\quad \alpha\approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

**Assumed FET:** 40 V, PDFN 3.3×3.3, R_ds,25 = 1.2 mΩ at V_gs = 10 V, Q_g = 30 nC, Q_gd = 6 nC, Q_rr = 30 nC, C_oss(tr) ≈ 1 nF, R_θJC = 1.5 K/W.

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per motor (80 A) |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.20 mΩ | 15.4 W |
| 100 °C | 1.70 mΩ | 21.8 W |
| 125 °C | 1.86 mΩ | **23.8 W** |

**Add copper.** Bus and phase copper adds roughly 0.3 mΩ per conducting path (2 oz, short), so:

$$P_{cu} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 3.8\ \text{W per motor}$$

This heat is distributed into the board.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

There is one hard-switched commutation cell per motor per sector.

### 2.1 V–I overlap

Gate current during the Miller plateau, with V_drv = 12 V, V_pl ≈ 4.5 V, and R_g,tot = 2.2 Ω external + 1 Ω internal + driver resistance:

$$I_{g,on} \approx \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{g,tot}+R_{drv,src}},\qquad t_{sw}\approx\frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_g}$$

With practical drivers (about 1.5 A source / 1.8 A sink, for example the FD6288 class) plus ringing control, assume t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 20 ns:

$$P_{VI}=\tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\times25.2\times80\times40\text{n}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Remaining terms

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Body-diode reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f | 25.2 × 30 nC × 48 kHz = 0.036 W |
| Coss | ½·C_oss·V²·f × 2 | ≈ 0.030 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction (t_dt = 100 ns) | V_f·I·2t_dt·f | 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 kHz = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver/R_g, not the FET) | Q_g·V_drv·f × 2 | 0.035 W |

**Total switching loss ≈ 2.6 W per motor at 48 kHz.** At 24 kHz it would be ≈ 1.3 W.

### 2.3 Per-motor and board totals at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

| Item | Per motor | Board (×4) |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 23.8 W | 95.2 W |
| Switching | 2.6 W | 10.4 W |
