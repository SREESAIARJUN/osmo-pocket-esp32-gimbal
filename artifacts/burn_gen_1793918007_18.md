> ⚠️ **Feasibility warnings**
> - **40 V FETs on 6S** leave about 37% margin over 25.2 V. Ringing during regenerative braking with bidirectional DShot and active braking can exceed this. A 60 V device is safer, at roughly 1.5× the R_DS(on).
> - **A "nano" form factor (20×20 mm) cannot carry 4×80 A**, either thermally or in terms of copper cross-section. The model below assumes 30.5×30.5 mm mounting. The thermal result (§3) shows that even this board supports 80 A only as a burst of about 5 s.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \mathrm{K^{-1}}$$

At T_j = 100 °C:

$$R_{DS} = 1.0 \times (1 + 0.45) = 1.45\ \text{mΩ}$$

### 1.2 Current-path analysis (six-step with synchronous rectification)

In each 60° commutation sector, one leg is PWM-switched (HS on for D, LS on for 1−D) and one leg's low-side stays fully ON. Current therefore always flows through exactly **two FETs**, independent of duty cycle (ignoring dead time):

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) + I^2 R_{Cu}$$

At 80 A and 1.45 mΩ:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.45\times10^{-3} = 18.56\ \text{W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each phase is active for 2/3 of the electrical cycle (1/3 PWM leg, 1/3 static low-side), so averaged over a cycle:

$$\bar P_{HS} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}\,D\,I^2R,\qquad \bar P_{LS} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)\,I^2R + \tfrac{1}{3} I^2R$$

The **low-side FETs carry the larger share.** At D = 0.8:

| FET | Loss |
|---|---|
| HS | 2.47 W |
| LS | 3.71 W |
| Leg total | 6.19 W |
| 3 legs | 18.56 W ✔ |

### 1.4 Copper loss

Assume 10 mm wide, 3 oz (105 µm) traces over a 15 mm path, with phase and bus segments combined:

$$R_{Cu} = \rho\frac{L}{A} = 1.72\times10^{-8}\frac{0.015}{0.01\times105\times10^{-6}} \approx 0.25\ \text{mΩ}\ \text{(after 40 \% thermal derating)} \Rightarrow 1.6\ \text{W}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

### 2.1 Hard-switching transition loss (PWM HS FET only)

The LS turns on after dead time with the body diode already conducting, so it switches near ZVS.

$$t_{r,f} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2} + Q_{gd}}{I_{g}} = \frac{15\,\text{nC}}{1\,\text{A}} = 15\ \text{ns}$$

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(30\times10^{-9})(48\,000) = 1.45\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Secondary loss terms

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse recovery | $V Q_{rr} f$ = 25.2·60n·48k | 0.073 W |
| Output capacitance | $\tfrac12 Q_{oss} V f$ | 0.048 W |
| Dead-time body diode | $V_f I \cdot 2t_d \cdot f$ = 0.8·80·200n·48k | 0.614 W |
| Gate charge (dissipated in driver/R_g) | $Q_g V_{GS} f \cdot 2$ | 0.048 W |
| **Switching subtotal** | | **≈ 2.23 W/ch** |

### 2.3 Total loss budget at 80 A burst

| | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 18.56 W | 74.2 W |
| Copper | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| Switching + diode | 2.23 W | 8.9 W |
| **Total** | **22.4 W** | **≈ 89.5 W** |

Efficiency check: P_out ≈ 25.2 × 80 × 4 ≈ 8.06 kW, so η_ESC ≈ 98.9% (loss figure excludes the motor).

> **Design lever:** Raising f_sw to 96 kHz adds about 2.2 W per channel, while halving R_DS(on) by paralleling FETs saves about 9 W per channel. **At 80 A this design is conduction-dominated**, so spend money on R_DS(on), not on faster switching.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Two-tier network

- **Tier 1, junction → local copper:** per FET R_θ,j-pcb ≈ R_θjc (0.8 K/W) + via-array spreading (≈ 4 K/W) ≈ **5 K/W**.
- **Tier 2, board → ambient:** lumped RC.
  - Board heat capacity: C ≈ m·c ≈ (9 g Cu-heavy PCB, effective c ≈ 0.65 J/g·K) ≈ **6 J/K**.
  - Convective conductance with prop wash, h ≈ 100 W
