# Job #35: STM32H743 Nano AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC)

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Baseline Assumptions

The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors directly. Production 4-in-1 boards use one dedicated MCU per motor running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Using the H743 for commutation would require 24 complementary PWM outputs plus 4 BEMF comparator sets, which the H743 cannot support while also running ArduPilot.

This design therefore uses the following architecture:

| Block | Part | Role |
|---|---|---|
| FC MCU | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100), 480 MHz | ArduPilot, bi-dir DShot600 master |
| ESC MCUs (×4) | STM32G071GBU6 (or AT32F421) | AM32 firmware, 48 kHz PWM, BEMF commutation |
| Gate drivers (×4) | FD6288Q (3-phase, 250 V, bootstrap) | Half-bridge drive, ~0.5 A / 1 A |
| MOSFETs (×24) | 40 V N-ch, DFN5×6, R<sub>DS(on)</sub> ≤ 1.0 mΩ @ 10 V (e.g., NTMFS5C404N class) | 6 per motor |
| Current sense | 1 × 0.25 mΩ shunt (battery return) + INA181A1 / ESC-side amp | Total current |
| Form factor | 30.5 × 30.5 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz outer / 2 oz inner power planes | Nano AIO |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Commutation topology

Under 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM), exactly two phases carry current in each 60° electrical sector:

- **Leg A (PWM leg):** the high-side conducts for duty D, and the low-side conducts for (1 − D) as a synchronous rectifier.
- **Leg B (return leg):** the low-side is held ON for the entire sector.

So at any instant two MOSFETs carry I<sub>ph</sub> in series. The conduction loss per motor is:

$$
P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 \, R_{DS}(T_j)\,\big[D + (1-D)\big] + I_{rms}^2 \, R_{DS}(T_j) = 2\, I_{rms}^2 \, R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

This total is independent of D, which is why synchronous rectification matters. The distribution between FETs does depend on D, as shown in §1.4.

### 1.2 Ripple correction

The phase inductance ripple is:

$$
\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{BEMF})\,D}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}
$$

Typical values for a 5″ motor are L<sub>ph</sub> ≈ 15 µH (line-to-line, so the phase-pair loop is 15 µH), and the worst ripple occurs at D = 0.5 with V<sub>BEMF</sub> ≈ 12.6 V:

$$
\Delta I = \frac{12.6 \times 0.5}{15\times10^{-6} \times 48\times10^{3}} = 8.75 \text{ A}
$$

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2\left(1 + \frac{1}{12}\left(\frac{\Delta I}{I_{ph}}\right)^2\right) = 6400\,(1 + 0.00100) = 6406\ \text{A}^2
$$

The ripple contribution is +0.1%, so it is negligible at 48 kHz. This is one argument for 48 kHz over 24 kHz, where the correction would be about 0.4%.

### 1.3 Temperature-dependent R<sub>DS(on)</sub>, solved self-consistently

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

| T<sub>j</sub> | R<sub>DS</sub> | P<sub>cond</sub> per motor | P<sub>cond</sub>, 4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W | 51.3 W |
| 100 °C | 1.34 mΩ | 17.2 W | 68.7 W |
| 125 °C | 1.45 mΩ | 18.6 W | 74.3 W |
| 150 °C | 1.56 mΩ | 20.0 W | 80.1 W |

There is a positive feedback loop: P rises with T<sub>j</sub>, and T<sub>j</sub> rises with P. A steady state exists only if:

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\cdot R_{th} < 1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
2 I^2 R_{DS,25}\,\alpha\, R_{th,ja} < 1
$$

$$
2(6400)(0.001)(0.0045)\,R_{th} < 1 \;\Rightarrow\; R_{th,ja} < 17.4\ \text{K/W per motor}
$$

This condition is satisfied on any reasonable board. Thermal runaway is therefore not the risk; absolute temperature is.
