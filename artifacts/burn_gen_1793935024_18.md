## Electro-Thermal Model, Bidirectional DShot600 DMA Plan, and Reference Netlist

## 0. Architecture Decisions and Constraints

These decisions shape all the math below.

| Item | Decision | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller MCU | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100), 400 MHz core, 200 MHz timer clock | ArduPilot-supported, enough DMA streams for bidirectional DShot plus IMU/OSD/UARTs |
| ESC control | **4× AT32F421K8U7 running AM32** (one per motor), 6-step trapezoidal with complementary PWM | The H743 does not commutate the motors. Bidirectional DShot is a link *between* the H743 and the ESC MCUs. Having one MCU run flight control and four-motor sensorless commutation is not a supportable ArduPilot configuration. |
| Gate driver | FD6288Q (3-phase half-bridge, bootstrap) ×4 | Common in 4-in-1 ESCs, with 5×5 QFN and integrated dead time |
| MOSFETs | 40 V, ~1.0 mΩ typical at 10 V, PDFN 5×6 (class representative) ×24 | Required for the 80 A burst target |
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max (worst case for switching loss) | |
| PWM | f_sw = 48 kHz, center-aligned | As specified |
| Board | **30.5 × 30.5 mm** mount pattern, 8-layer, 2 oz outer / 2 oz inner power planes | See the warning below |

> ⚠️ **Feasibility warning:** "80 A burst per motor" on a 20×20 nano layout is not physically credible. Section 3 shows the dissipation is about 90 W total at burst. A 20×20 board has too little copper mass and area to absorb that for more than about 0.5 s. I model a 30.5×30.5 board and define "burst" as **≤2 s at 80 A, from a 50 °C start**. If marketing insists on 20×20, the honest rating is roughly 45–55 A burst. The equations below are parameterized, so you can re-run them for any footprint.

## 2. Conduction-Loss Model

### 2.1 Six-step commutation with complementary PWM

In each 60° sector, two phases conduct:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET is on for duty D. The synchronous low-side FET is on for (1 − D).
- **Static leg:** the low-side FET is on for 100% of the sector.
