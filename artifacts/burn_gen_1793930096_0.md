## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-step synchronous commutation

In each 60° commutation sector:
- The high-side FET of the PWM'd phase conducts for duty D.
- Its complementary low-side FET conducts for (1 − D).
- The low-side FET of the sinking phase conducts 100% of the time.

So the instantaneous conduction loss is independent of D:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ /°C$$

### 1.3 At 80 A

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per channel |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.0 mΩ | 2 × 6400 × 0.0010 = **12.8 W** |
| 125 °C | 1.5 mΩ | 2 × 6400 × 0.0015 = **19.2 W** |

### 1.4 Copper and shunt losses (often forgotten)

- **Power-plane resistance:** R_pcb ≈ 0.3 mΩ per phase path (2 oz, short pours). Loss is 2 × I² × R_pcb = **3.8 W**.
- **Current shunt:** a 0.25 mΩ shunt in the common return carries the bus current, I_bus ≈ D·I. At D = 0.8 the loss is (64)² × 0.00025 ≈ **1.0 W** per channel, or **4.1 W** if one shared shunt carries all four channels.

### 1.5 Per-FET distribution

Over an electrical revolution, each FET spends:
- 1/3 of the time as the "always-on" sink (I²R),
- 1/6 as the PWM device (D·I²R),
- 1/6 as the synchronous rectifier ((1 − D)·I²R).

Average per FET:

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx P_{cond}/6 \approx 3.2\ \text{W}$$

Peak instantaneous loss on a sinking FET: I²R = **9.6 W** at 125 °C.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Hard-switching overlap (V–I crossover)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = \tfrac12 (25.2)(80)(20\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz}) = 0.97\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,eff} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = 0.023\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction (2 edges per period)

$$P_{dt} = V_f \, I \, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ ns} \times 48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

This is the second-largest term. Tightening dead time to 50 ns halves it, provided shoot-through margin is verified.

### 2.5 Gate drive (2 switching FETs)

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

This is dissipated mostly in the gate driver and gate resistors, not the FETs.

### 2.6 Per-channel total at 80 A burst (T_j = 125 °C)

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.20 |
| PCB copper | 3.80 |
| Shunt (shared, 1/4 share) | 1.00 |
| Switching overlap | 0.97 |
| Dead time | 0.61 |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.08 |
| Gate | 0.05 |
| **Total per channel** | **≈ 25.7 W** |
| **4-channel total** | **≈ 103 W** |

**Efficiency check.** Output power at D = 0.8 is ≈ 25.2 × 0.8 × 80 ≈ 1.6 kW per channel. A 25.7 W loss gives ≈ 98.4% efficiency. The silicon is efficient; the problem is power density.

### 2.7 Frequency sensitivity

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial f_{sw}} \approx \frac{0.97 + 0.61 + 0.08 + 0.05}{48\text{k}} \approx 36\ \mu\text{W/Hz}$$

- Moving to 24 kHz saves ≈ 0.85 W per channel.
- Moving to 96 kHz costs ≈ 1.7 W per channel.

At 80 A the design is conduction-dominated. 48 kHz is justified for low-inductance motors (current ripple and acoustics), not penalized by losses
