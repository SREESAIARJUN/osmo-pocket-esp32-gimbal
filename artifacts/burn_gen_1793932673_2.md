# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #70): Engineering Model

**Before you use this:**
- I have no Job #70 documents, so I don't know your MOSFET part, stack size, battery cell count, or layer stack. Every number below comes from a stated assumption. Substitute your datasheet values. The equations are general, but the results depend on your inputs.
- The netlist is a reference design for review and adaptation. It has not been verified for production. Check footprints, pinouts and DMA maps against the current datasheets and the ArduPilot `hwdef` documentation before layout.
- The headline thermal result is that a 20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm nano board **cannot sustain 80 A per motor**. It can survive an 80 A *burst* of roughly 1–2 seconds. Section 3 quantifies this.

## 0. Architecture Decision

The STM32H743 has only **two advanced-control timers (TIM1, TIM8)**. Each provides three complementary pairs with dead-time insertion. That means the H743 alone can natively commutate only 2 BLDC motors. HRTIM could stretch this, but it would also consume the FC's real-time budget.

**Adopted architecture:**

| Block | Device (representative) | Role |
|---|---|---|
| FC MCU | STM32H743VIH6 (400/480 MHz) | ArduPilot, bidirectional DShot600 master ×4 |
| ESC MCUs | 4× AT32F421 or STM32G071 | AM32 firmware, 48 kHz complementary PWM |
| Gate drivers | 4× FD6288Q-class 3-phase half-bridge driver | 250 V / ~1.5 A gate drive, bootstrap |
| Power stage | 24× 30–40 V N-MOSFET, 3.3×3.3 DFN | 6 per motor |
| Current sense | 1 shunt (0.25 mΩ) + INA/op-amp → FC ADC | Total current |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (6-Step Trapezoidal, Synchronous Rectification)

During any 60° commutation sector, current flows through two phases:

- **PWM phase:** the high-side FET conducts for D·T_s. The low-side (synchronous) FET conducts for (1−D)·T_s.
- **Return phase:** the low-side FET is fully on.

At every instant the motor current therefore passes through exactly **two series FET channels**, independent of D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

Temperature-corrected resistance:

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right]$$

With α ≈ 0.0059 /K, this gives about 1.5 × R₂₅ at 110 °C.

**At 80 A, T_j = 110 °C:**

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ motor}$$

**Per-FET RMS.** Each FET carries current for 1/3 of the electrical cycle, and the high/low split within that third depends on D. Worst-case single-device loss occurs when one device conducts the full sector:

$$P_{FET,max} \approx I^2 R \cdot \tfrac{1}{3} \times 3\ (\text{instantaneous during sector}) = 9.6\ \text{W instantaneous}$$

On a time-averaged basis this is about 3.2 W per FET. The instantaneous value governs junction ripple at low eRPM. The average governs board temperature.

**Copper and path resistance.** This term is often larger than designers expect. A 2 oz (70 µm) outer layer plus 1 oz inner layers, 6 layers paralleled, over a roughly 10 mm path gives an estimated 0.3–0.5 mΩ per phase path.

$$P_{Cu} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.4\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 5.1\ \text{W per motor}$$

**Mitigation:** paralleling 2 FETs per switch position halves P_cond to 9.6 W. It requires 48 FETs, which a nano footprint cannot fit, so this is usually infeasible.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap
$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I\, (t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}$$
$$= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (sync-FET body diode)
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 50\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance
$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \times 2 = 40\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.05\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction
$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2 t_d)\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in the driver and gate resistor, not the FET)
$$P_g = 2\, Q_g\, V_{GS}\, f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Frequency sensitivity

| f_sw | P_sw + P_rr + P_oss + P_dt |
|---|---|
| 24 kHz | 1.64 W |
| **48 kHz** | **3.28 W** |
| 96 kHz | 6.56 W |

At 80 A, switching loss is about 15% of total loss, so conduction dominates. Raising f_sw to 96 kHz for quieter motors costs about 3.3 W per motor at this current.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Loss budget at 80 A burst

| Term | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (110 °C) | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Copper | 5.1 W | 20.4 W |
| Switching + dead time | 3.3 W | 13.2 W |
| Driver, LDOs, MCUs | ~0.3 W | 1.2 W |
| **Total** | **27.9 W** | **~112 W** |

Electrical efficiency at this point: P_out ≈ 25.2 V × 80 A × 4 × D. At high throttle, losses are about 2–3% of roughly 8 kW. That efficiency looks fine, but **112 W in a 25 mm board is a thermal problem, not an efficiency problem.**

### 3.2 Junction rise (steady-state, per FET)
$$\Delta T_{JC} = P_{FET} \cdot R_{\theta JC} = 9.6 \times 1.5 = 14.4\ \text{K}$$

The junction-to-case path is not the bottleneck. The case → PCB → air path is.
