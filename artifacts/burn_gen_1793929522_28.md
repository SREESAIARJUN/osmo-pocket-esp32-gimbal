## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Problems Found in the Brief

**1. Architecture.** An STM32H743 is not used as the ESC MCU in production 4-in-1 designs. This package assumes the standard split:

- **FC board:** STM32H743, running ArduPilot.
- **ESC board:** 4× dedicated ESC MCUs (STM32G071 or AT32F421, running AM32 or BLHeli_32) with 3-phase gate drivers.
- **Link:** DShot600, bi-directional, over an 8-pin JST-SH harness.

If you intend the H743 to commutate all four motors directly, that is a different design (12 complementary PWM pairs plus BEMF ADC). It is not covered here.

**2. Component values.** I don't have a Job #2 BOM. Every component value below is a stated assumption. Replace these numbers with your datasheet values and the equations still apply.

**3. Main finding:** an 80 A per-motor rating is not thermally sustainable on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board. Section 3 quantifies this. At that size, 80 A is a **1–3 second burst** when all four motors are loaded, not a continuous rating.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current Path

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous (complementary) PWM. At every instant, exactly one high-side path and one low-side path carry the full phase current I:

- During PWM on-time (fraction D): the current flows through HS(x) and LS(y).
- During off-time (fraction 1−D): the HS(x) current freewheels through LS(x), which is switched on synchronously.

So two R_DS(on) are always in the loop, independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS}(T_j) \approx R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ /\mathrm{K}$$

### 1.2 Per-FET Distribution

Each phase leg conducts for 2/3 of the electrical period. The high-side FET sees duty D; its low-side partner sees (1−D) plus its own 1/3 of the period. Averaged over one electrical revolution:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6}$$

The worst-case FET is the low-side at low D (heavy freewheeling), reaching up to about 1.5× the average.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per PWM cycle, one leg performs one hard-switched turn-on and one turn-off. The synchronous partner switches at near zero volts (ZVS) and instead contributes dead-time body-diode conduction.
