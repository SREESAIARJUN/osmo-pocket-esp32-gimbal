# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC: Architecture, Loss Model, DShot DMA Plan, Netlists

**Job #21, Rev A (engineering draft)**

## 0. Feasibility Constraints

Three constraints shape the rest of this document.

1. **The H743 does not switch the MOSFETs.** In every production 4-in-1 stack, the H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600 to the ESC. A separate ESC MCU per motor generates the 48 kHz commutation PWM. Suitable ESC MCUs include the AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or the EFM8BB51 running BLHeli_S/Bluejay. Section 3 covers the H743 side. Sections 1, 2 and 5 cover the ESC side.
2. **"80A burst" must be bounded by duration.** At 80 A per motor the stage dissipates about 21 W per motor and about 85 W total (Section 2). A 20×20 "nano" board cannot sustain that. The rating is only valid as a time-limited burst (Section 2.6). I recommend a 30.5×30.5 ESC footprint for 80A-class burst, with the 20×20 FC stacked on top.
3. **Every number below rests on stated assumptions.** Replace the assumed MOSFET parameters with your selected part's datasheet values before release. Validate with thermocouple and IR testing on a populated board.

## 2. Mathematical Loss Model (ESC Power Stage)

### 2.1 Conduction state

In six-step commutation, two phase legs carry current at any instant. With complementary PWM at duty D:
- The high-side FET of the sourcing leg conducts for D.
- The low-side FET of the same leg conducts for (1−D), outside dead time.
- The low-side FET of the sinking leg conducts continuously over the 60° sector.

Motor current is approximately continuous because winding inductance is much greater than the ripple scale. Therefore, at every instant the current flows through exactly two channel resistances in series:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I^2\, R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

This expression is independent of D, to first order, because synchronous rectification replaces the diode.

### 2.2 Electro-thermal fixed point

R_ds depends on T_j, and T_j depends on loss, so the two must be solved together:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + R_{\theta,eff}\, P_{FET}(T_j)$$

$$P_{FET}(T_j) = P_0\,[1+\alpha (T_j - 25)] + P_{sw}$$

Solving for T_j gives a closed form:

$$T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + R_{\theta}\,(P_0(1-25\alpha) + P_{sw})}{1 - R_\theta P_0 \alpha}$$

**Thermal runaway criterion:** the solution is stable only if R_θ · P_0 · α < 1. Keep this ratio below 0.5 for margin.

### 2.3 Conduction numbers at 80 A

Evaluated at T_j = 125 °C (worst case), R_ds = 1.45 mΩ:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.45\times10^{-3} = 18.6\ \text{W}$$

Each of the 6 FETs averages 1/3 conduction duty. The per-FET average is therefore 18.6 / 6 × 2 ≈ 6.2 W during its active sectors, averaged over the electrical cycle.

### 2.4 Switching losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing leg switches hard. Assume one hard-switched leg per motor at any instant.

| Mechanism | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r+t_f) · f_sw | ½ · 25.2 · 80 · 30 ns · 48k = **1.45 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f · I · 2t_dt · f_sw | 0.85 · 80 · 400 ns · 48k = **1.31 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw | 50 nC · 25.2 · 48k = **0.06 W** |
| C_oss | Q_oss · V · f_sw | 40 nC · 25.2 · 48k = **0.05 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs in leg) | 2 · Q_g · V_gs · f_sw | 2 · 60 nC · 10 · 48k = **0.06 W** (dissipated in driver/R_g) |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.9 W / motor** |

Two observations:
- Dead-time loss is comparable to overlap loss. Reducing t_dt to 100 ns, which the AM32 dead-time setting allows if the gate driver permits, saves about 0.65 W per motor.
- Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz the total would be about 1.45 W per motor. Choosing 48 kHz trades roughly 1.5 W per motor of extra loss for lower audible noise and lower current ripple.

### 2.5 Total stage loss

$$P_{motor} = 18.6 + 2.9 + P_{shunt} + P_{trace}$$

| Term | Value |
|---|---|
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, 80 A) | 1.6 W |
| Copper (≈0.15 mΩ effective per path) | ≈1 W |
| **Per motor** | **≈ 24 W** |
| **4 motors, simultaneous burst** | **≈ 97 W** |

**Bulk capacitor ripple:**

$$I_{C,rms} \approx I\sqrt{D(1-D)}$$

The maximum is 40 A rms per motor at D = 0.5. Use the following:
- 4 × 10 µF/50 V X7R 1210 ceramics per motor leg pair.
- 1 × 1000 µF/35 V low-ESR electrolytic on the pigtail. This one is mandatory.

### 2.6 Burst thermal model (transient)

Use a lumped two-node RC model:

```
Pj ──[Rθjc=1.0/6 K/W per stage]── Tcase/PCB ──[Rθ,pcb-a]── Ta
                                     │
                                  C_th (board + heatsink)
```

**Thermal mass:**

| Element | Calculation | C_th |
|---|---|---|
| 30.5×30.5 8-layer board (6 × 2 oz Cu) | ≈ 6.3 g Cu × 0.385 J/g·K | ≈ 2.4 J/K |
| FR4 | ≈ 6 g × 1.1 J/g·K | ≈ 6.6 J/K |
| 10 g Al heatsink plate | 10 g × 0.9 J/g·K | 9 J/K |
| **Total** | | **≈ 18 J/K** |

**Steady-state path to air:** with prop wash, R_θ,pcb-a ≈ 2.5 K/W. Sustained loss of 97 W is therefore impossible, since it would imply a 240 K rise.

**Time to limit:** T_case starts at 50 °C. The limit is T_j,max = 150 °C with a junction-to-case delta of (97/24 FETs) × 1.0 ≈ 4 K. That leaves an allowable case temperature of about 140 °C, i.e. ΔT_case = 90 K.

$$\tau = R_\theta C_{th} = 2.5 \times 18 = 45\ \text{s}$$

$$t_{burst} = -\tau \ln\!\left(1-\frac{\Delta T}{P R_\theta}\right) = -45\ln\!\left(1-\frac{90}{242}\right) \approx 21\ \text{s}$$

**Specification:** 80 A burst for ≤ 10 s on all four motors simultaneously (2× margin), from a ≤ 50 °C start. Continuous rating:

$$I_{cont} = \sqrt{\frac{90/2.5\ /4 \;-\; 2.9}{2 \times 1.45\,\text{m}\Omega}} \approx 45\ \text{A per motor}$$

Without the heatsink the time constant drops to about 22 s and the burst is limited to about 9 s. The specification should then become ≤ 5 s.
