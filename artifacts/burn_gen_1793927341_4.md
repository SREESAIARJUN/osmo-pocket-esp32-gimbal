## 0. Architecture Decision and Feasibility Warnings

Three issues determine the rest of the design.

**1. The H743 should not commutate the motors.**
Four sensorless 3-phase bridges need 12 complementary PWM pairs with dead-time:

| Source | Complementary pairs |
|---|---|
| TIM1 | 3 |
| TIM8 | 3 |
| HRTIM (A to E) | 5 |
| TIM15/16/17 | 1 each |

The H743 can technically supply this, but the flight stack would then also have to run four 48 kHz FOC/trapezoidal commutation loops with BEMF zero-cross ADC sampling. ArduPilot does not do this.

**Adopted architecture:** the H743 runs ArduPilot. Four dedicated ESC MCUs run AM32, each on an STM32G071 or AT32F421 with an FD6288Q-class gate driver. The H743 talks to them over bi-directional DShot600. This is how every production AIO is built.

**2. "80A burst" on a nano form factor is a transient rating, not a thermal rating.**
The numbers below show a 20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm board reaches thermal limits in about 1 to 3 seconds at 80 A per channel. The model lets you set the honest burst duration.

**3. Treat all component parameters as placeholders.**
The values below are representative of a 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN MOSFET class. Replace every parameter with your selected part's datasheet values at the right temperature before sign-off.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

In 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification, one high-side and one low-side FET carry the phase current at any instant. The duty cycle redistributes the current between the high-side and its complementary low-side, but the total RMS current through the conducting leg stays approximately I. So the loss per channel is:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot \left(2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j) + R_{Cu}\right)
$$

$$
R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds(on),25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

Worked numbers at T_j = 125 °C:

- R_ds(on) = 1.0 mΩ × 1.5 = 1.5 mΩ
- P_cond = 80² × (2 × 1.5 m + 0.3 m) = 6400 × 3.3 mΩ = **21.1 W per channel**

**Electrothermal feedback.** R_ds(on) rises with T_j, so solve the following iteratively, or accept the 125 °C worst case as above:

$$
T_j = T_{case} + P_{FET}\,R_{th,jc}
$$

## 3. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

**Hard-switching loss.** Only the high-side FET hard-switches. The low side commutates through its body diode under dead-time.

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}
= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 0.97\ \text{W}
$$

**Dead-time (body diode) loss.** Two edges per period:

$$
P_{dt} = V_f\,I\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 600\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 1.84\ \text{W}
$$

This term is larger than the switching loss. Reducing dead-time to 150 ns, if the gate driver propagation matching allows it, saves about 0.9 W per channel.

**Reverse recovery loss:**

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}
$$

**Output capacitance loss:**

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,C_{oss}\,V_{bus}^2\,f_{sw} = 0.015\ \text{W}
$$

**Gate drive loss.** This is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET.

$$
P_g = 2\,Q_g\,V_{drv}\,f_{sw} = 2 \times 30\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.029\ \text{W}
$$

### Per-channel and total loss budget at 80 A

| Loss term | W per channel | W for 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET + copper) | 21.1 | 84.5 |
| Hard switching | 0.97 | 3.9 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.84 | 7.4 |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.08 | 0.3 |
| **Total** | **≈ 24.0** | **≈ 96** |

Bus-level efficiency is about 1 − 96/(4 × 80 × 25.2 × duty). Conduction dominates, so **at 80 A the design lever is R_ds(on) and copper, not f_sw**. Dropping to 24 kHz saves only about 1.4 W per channel and adds acoustic noise and current ripple.

**Per-FET dissipation (hottest high-side):** about (21.1 − 1.9)/2 + 0.97 + 0.92 ≈ **11.5 W**.

