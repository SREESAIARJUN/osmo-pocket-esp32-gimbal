# STM32H743 AIO 4-in-1 ESC Flight Controller (Job #37): Power, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

## 0. Feasibility Findings

**F1. The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** On any production 4-in-1 design, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM and does commutation and back-EMF sensing. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to those four MCUs. This document models the system that way. Driving 24 FETs directly from the H743 is not supported by ArduPilot.

**F2. 80 A per motor on a nano board is a burst rating of 1–2 seconds, not a continuous rating.** Section 3 shows the board heats at roughly 40 K/s at full burst. A nano board can dissipate about 8–15 W continuously, which supports roughly 25–35 A per motor. This matches what commercial nano AIOs are rated for. Treat "80 A" as a datasheet burst number. Firmware current limiting must enforce it.

**F3. All numbers below rest on stated, parameterised assumptions.** Replace them with values from the actual FET datasheet once one is selected. The equations are the deliverable; the numbers illustrate them.

## 2. Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 2.1 Commutation structure

AM32 uses 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM. In every 60° electrical sector:

- **Phase A (PWM phase):** the high-side FET conducts for duty D. The low-side FET carries synchronous rectification current for (1 − D).
- **Phase B (sink phase):** the low-side FET is fully on.
- **Phase C:** floating.

Over 360° electrical, each phase spends 120° as the PWM phase, 120° as the sink phase, and 120° floating.
