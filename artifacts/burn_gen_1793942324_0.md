# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #34): Power, Thermal & DShot Architecture

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

**Architecture.** On a 4-in-1 ESC with bi-directional DShot, the STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFET bridges. The system has two tiers:

- **FC tier:** STM32H743 running ArduPilot. It generates bi-directional DShot600 frames and captures eRPM telemetry.
- **ESC tier:** four independent ESC MCUs running AM32 or BLHeli_32 at 48 kHz PWM. Each drives a 3-phase gate driver and 6 MOSFETs.

The models below follow this split. If you intended the H743 to commutate the motors directly (FOC from the FC), say so. The timer and DMA plan changes completely in that case: TIM1/TIM8 would run complementary PWM, and DShot would not exist.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

**Current path.** With trapezoidal six-step commutation and complementary PWM, motor current always flows through exactly two MOSFETs:

- the active high-side FET or its synchronous low-side partner, and
- the low-side FET of the return phase.

Per-motor conduction loss:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds,hot}\cdot k_{ripple},\qquad k_{ripple}=1+\frac{1}{12}\left(\frac{\Delta I_{pp}}{I}\right)^2$$

Ripple is small at 48 kHz with typical motor inductance (≈20–40 µH line-to-line), so k_ripple ≈ 1.

$$P_{cond}=80^2\times 2\times 2.4\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{30.7\ W\ per\ motor}$$

**Per-device split.** Each FET conducts for 120° of every electrical cycle:

- Instantaneous dissipation while conducting: I²·R_hot = **15.4 W**
- Cycle-averaged dissipation (period ≪ thermal τ): 30.7 / 6 = **5.1 W**

**Copper and shunt losses.** Add these to the FET loss:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 (R_{trace}+R_{shunt}) \approx 80^2(0.3\text{m}\Omega + 0.25\text{m}\Omega)\approx 3.5\ \text{W per motor}$$

**Electrothermal feedback.** R_ds(on) rises with temperature, which raises loss, which raises temperature:

$$R(T_j)=R_{25}\left[1+\alpha (T_j-25)\right],\ \alpha\approx 0.006/\text{K}$$

$$T_j = T_c + R_{\theta JC}\,I^2 R(T_j)\ \Rightarrow\ T_j=\frac{T_c + R_{\theta JC} I^2 R_{25}(1-25\alpha)}{1-R_{\theta JC}I^2R_{25}\alpha}$$

This has a stable solution only when the loop gain is below one:

$$R_{\theta JC} I^2 R_{25}\alpha < 1$$

At 80 A: 1.5 × 6400 × 1.5 mΩ × 0.006 = **0.086**. The junction is stable. The board is the limiting factor (Section 4).

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only one leg per motor is PWM-switching at any instant. That leg's high-side FET sees hard switching, and its low-side FET sees dead-time diode conduction and reverse recovery.

| Term | Formula | Value at 25.2 V / 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw | ½·25.2·80·40 ns·48 k = **1.94 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 50 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.06 W** |
| C_oss | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw | **0.02 W** |
| Dead-time diode | V_f·I·2t_dt·f_sw | 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (2 switching FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw | **0.04 W** (dissipated in driver) |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.67 W per motor** |

**Sensitivity.** The overlap term scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz the switching total drops to ≈1.4 W. Dead time is the second-largest term: trimming t_dt from 100 ns to 50 ns saves 0.3 W per motor, but you must verify shoot-through margin with a scope at the hot corner.
