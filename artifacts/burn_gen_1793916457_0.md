**Key finding:** at 80 A per motor, a 20×20 mm stack can only survive a **burst of about 1–2 s**. The board is thermal-mass-limited, not heatsink-limited. The math below shows why and gives you the governing equations.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha(T_j - 25)] = 1.0\,\text{mΩ}\times(1 + 0.005\times100) = 1.5\,\text{mΩ}$$

### 1.2 Current path

With 6-step trapezoidal commutation and synchronous rectification, two FETs always carry phase current:
- one high-side FET (during the PWM on-time), or its complementary low-side FET (during the off-time), and
- one low-side FET of the return phase.

Therefore:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

This holds for both on and off intervals; dead time is handled separately in §2.4.

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each FET conducts for 120° of the 360° electrical cycle. Its share is weighted by duty cycle D (high-side) or 1−D (low-side, sync-rect):

$$P_{FET,HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,D\,I^2 R_{DS}, \qquad P_{FET,LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,(2-D)\,I^2 R_{DS}$$

The low-side figure includes its 120° return-phase conduction at 100 %. At D ≈ 1 (full-throttle burst), the loss is evenly spread at about **3.2 W per FET**.

### 1.4 PCB copper loss

A phase or bus path is roughly 15 mm long, 3 mm wide, and paralleled across 4 × 70 µm layers:

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{A} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\times0.015}{3\times10^{-3}\times280\times10^{-6}} \approx 0.31\,\text{mΩ}\ \text{(at 25 °C; ×1.39 at 125 °C ≈ 0.43 mΩ)}$$

There are about 3 such segments (bus+, phase, bus−). Including via arrays, budget roughly 1.0 mΩ per motor path:

$$P_{Cu} \approx 80^2 \times 1.0\times10^{-3} \approx \mathbf{6.4\ W per motor}$$

This is a large fraction of the total and is often omitted. **Via count matters:** a 0.3 mm via with 25 µm plating is about 1 mΩ and carries roughly 1–2 A continuous. Use **≥ 40 vias per 80 A transition**.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

With sync-rect complementary PWM, only the **high-side FET hard-switches**. The low side commutates after the body diode has already clamped, so it switches at near-zero voltage.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = \tfrac12 (25.2)(80)(30\times10^{-9})(48\times10^3) = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Output-capacitance loss (hard turn-on)

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse-recovery loss (low-side body diode)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

In practice, recovery also adds overlap current to the high-side turn-on, so multiply by about 1.5–2 for margin.

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f I_{ph}\,(2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{n}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate-drive loss

This is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET.

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times N_{active} = 40\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k}\times2 = 0.038\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Per-motor loss summary at 80 A

| Loss term | W |
|---|---|
| FET conduction (T_j = 125 °C) | 19.20 |
| PCB copper and vias | 6.40 |
| V–I switching | 1.45 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Q_oss + Q_rr (×1.5 margin) | 0.13 |
| Gate drive | 0.04 |
| **Total per motor** | **≈ 27.8 W** |
| **4 motors** | **≈ 111 W** |

Electrical efficiency check: P_out ≈ 25.2 × 80 × D ≈ 2 kW per motor, so the stage is about 98.6 % efficient. The losses are small electrically but enormous for a 20×20 mm board.

### 2.7 Sensitivity to f_sw

Switching terms scale linearly with frequency: about 2.2 W at 48 kHz versus 1.1 W at 24 kHz. At 80 A, conduction loss dominates about 10:1. **Lowering PWM frequency buys little at burst. Lower R_DS(on) and lower copper resistance are the levers.** 48 kHz is justified for low-current smoothness and acoustic noise.
