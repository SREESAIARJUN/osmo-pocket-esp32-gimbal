## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At the design point T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{DS}(110) = 1.8\,(1 + 0.005 \cdot 85) = 2.565\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Six-step (trapezoidal) conduction with synchronous rectification

In each 60° commutation sector, exactly one phase is high-side driven and one phase is low-side return.

- During PWM-on (duty D): current flows through HS(a) and LS(b).
- During PWM-off with sync rectification: current freewheels through LS(a) and LS(b).

So two FETs always carry the full phase current I. Per channel:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 2.565\times10^{-3} = 32.8\ \text{W}$$

Add the trace and plane resistance from bus to motor pad. Assume R_pcb ≈ 0.4 mΩ in the current path:

$$P_{pcb,ch} = I^2 R_{pcb} = 6400 \cdot 0.4\times10^{-3} = 2.6\ \text{W}$$

Add the current shunt, if fitted per channel (0.5 mΩ): **+3.2 W**. Most nano AIOs use a single bus shunt instead. Assume a single shared 0.25 mΩ shunt carrying ≈ ΣI·D. This is negligible per channel at partial duty but can reach ~10 W at full throttle on all channels.

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each FET conducts for roughly 1/3 of the electrical cycle (HS) plus freewheel time (LS). Averaged:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6}\cdot 1.0 \approx 5.5\ \text{W per FET}$$

The peak instantaneous dissipation in a conducting FET is I²R = 16.4 W. With f_elec in the 1–3 kHz range, the electrical period is far shorter than the die thermal time constant (~ms). The **average** value therefore governs junction temperature to within a few kelvin.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switching leg incurs hard-switching losses. The return leg is static within a sector.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard turn-on and turn-off)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

With t_r + t_f = 20 ns (driver source/sink ≈ 1–1.5 A, R_g ≈ 2–4 Ω):

$$P_{VI} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 20\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.97\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM period:

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\,I\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 400\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = 1.23\ \text{W}$$

This term exceeds the V–I term. Reducing dead time to ~100 ns (once ringing is controlled) gives a 0.6 W saving per channel.

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = V_{bus}\,Q_{rr}\,f_{sw} = 25.2 \cdot 50\text{nC} \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

Reverse recovery also adds to HS turn-on current overshoot, which shows up as EMI rather than heat.

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss,eff} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 1\text{nF} \cdot 635 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.015\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in driver plus R_g, not the FET)

$$P_g = Q_g\,V_{GS}\,f_{sw} \cdot N_{switching} = 40\text{nC} \cdot 10 \cdot 48\text{k} \cdot 2 = 0.038\ \text{W/ch}$$

With 4 channels this is ≈ 0.15 W on the 10 V rail, so size the gate-drive LDO or buck accordingly.

### 2.6 Total per channel at 80 A, 110 °C

| Loss | W/channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 32.8 | 131.2 |
| PCB copper | 2.6 | 10.4 |
| V–I switching | 0.97 | 3.9 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 | 4.9 |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.08 | 0.3 |
| **Total** | **≈ 37.7** | **≈ 151** |

Conduction loss is 87% of the total. At this current level, switching frequency is a secondary lever and R_DS(on) is the primary one. Dropping from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves only ~4 W across the board but increases current ripple and acoustic noise.

**Efficiency check:** P_out ≈ 22.2 V × 80 A × 4 ≈ 7.1 kW. Electrical efficiency ≈ 97.
