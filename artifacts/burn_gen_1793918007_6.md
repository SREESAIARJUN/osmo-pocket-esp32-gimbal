# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with Integrated 4-in-1 ESC: Systems Model

**Scope:** 80 A burst conduction losses, 48 kHz switching losses, transient thermal model, ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 timer and DMA allocation, and reference netlists.

> **Assumptions.** I have no access to prior Job #47 documentation, so the parameters below are stated assumptions. Every equation is parameterized, so you can substitute your actual BOM values.
>
> **Architecture.** An STM32H743 cannot drive 4 × 3-phase bridges with sensorless commutation while also running ArduPilot. The standard architecture, and the one assumed here, is:
> - **H743 as flight controller (ArduPilot)**, sending DShot600 to the ESCs.
> - **4 × dedicated ESC MCUs** (AT32F421 or STM32G071) running **AM32**, which supports a 48 kHz PWM setting.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Six-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification

At any instant, two of the three legs carry current. With complementary PWM, the current in each active leg always flows through exactly one MOSFET:

- During the on-time, it flows through the high-side FET.
- During the off-time, it flows through the synchronous low-side FET.

The current path therefore always contains **two R_DS(on) elements in series**, independent of duty cycle (dead-time diode intervals are handled separately in §3).

**Hot on-resistance:**

$$R_{hot} = R_{25}\,k_T = 1.2\,\text{mΩ} \times 1.4 = 1.68\ \text{mΩ}$$

**Per-channel conduction loss:**

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{hot} = 2(80)^2(1.68\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{21.5\ W}$$

**Per-FET average.** Losses distribute roughly evenly across the 6 FETs over one electrical revolution:

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{21.5}{6} \approx 3.6\ \text{W}$$

**Shunt loss:**

$$P_{sh} = I^2 R_{sh} = 6400 \times 0.25\times10^{-3} = 1.6\ \text{W per channel}$$

**PCB copper loss.** For phase and bus traces, 2 oz copper is about 0.25 mΩ/sq. Assuming about 0.3 mΩ of effective path resistance:

$$P_{Cu} \approx 6400 \times 0.3\times10^{-3} \approx 1.9\ \text{W per channel}$$

### 2.2 Thermal runaway check

R_DS(on) rises with T_j, which in turn raises loss. The loop is stable if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\,R_{th} < 1$$

With α ≈ 0.004 /K:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T} = P\,\alpha \approx 3.6 \times 0.004 = 0.0144\ \text{W/K per FET}$$

With R_th ≈ 30 K/W, the loop gain is about 0.43. This is stable, but gives about **1.75× loss amplification** (1/(1 − 0.43)) at steady state. This is another reason 80 A must be treated as a burst rating only.
