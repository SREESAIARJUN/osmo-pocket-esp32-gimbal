# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #11): Power-Stage and DShot Architecture Model

## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility Flags

The following points matter more than any equation in this document.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production AIO, the FC MCU (H743) sends DShot to four, or one shared, ESC MCUs. These are typically AT32F421, STM32G071 or EFM8, running AM32 or BLHeli_32/S, and they generate the 48 kHz PWM. The switching model below applies to the ESC bridge. The DMA model applies to the H743.
2. **80 A per motor on a 20×20 / 25.5×25.5 mm board is a seconds-scale burst, not a rating.** The model below shows that roughly 3 s at 80 A takes the board from 40 °C to about 110 °C. Sustainable current on this form factor is about 40 to 50 A per channel. 4 × 80 A = 320 A of battery current also exceeds any XT60/XT30 lead and most 6S packs.
3. **Representative parameters are used, not a specific datasheet.** Replace them with your chosen part's numbers.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (Per ESC Channel)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent resistance

R_DS(T_j) = R₂₅ · [1 + α (T_j − 25)]

At T_j = 105 °C: R_DS = 1.0 × 1.40 = **1.40 mΩ**

### 1.2 Six-step synchronous (complementary) PWM

At any commutation step:

- **PWM leg:** the high side (HS) conducts for D·T. The low side (LS) conducts for (1−D)·T minus the dead time, during which the body diode conducts.
- **Return leg:** the LS is held fully on.

Conduction loss for the step is therefore:

P_cond = I²R_DS · D + I²R_DS · (1−D) + I²R_DS = **2 · I_ph² · R_DS(T_j)**

This is independent of duty cycle, because synchronous rectification removes the duty dependence.

### 1.3 Additional resistive paths

P_cu = I_ph² · R_cu  
P_sh = I_DC² · R_sh, with I_DC ≈ D·I_ph (≈ I_ph at full throttle)

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 105 °C

| Term | Expression | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| MOSFET conduction | 2 × 80² × 1.40 mΩ | **17.9 W** |
| PCB copper | 80² × 0.3 mΩ | 1.9 W |
| Shunt | 80² × 0.5 mΩ | 3.2 W |
| **Subtotal** | | **23.0 W / channel** |

### 1.5 Per-device loss

Six FETs share the commutation cycle, so the average per FET is about 17.9 / 6 ≈ 3.0 W.

The instantaneous peak while conducting is I²R = 9.0 W. The thermal impedance from junction to case at 10 ms is roughly 0.3 K/W, so the ripple on junction temperature is only about 3 K. Junction temperature is therefore dominated by the board's bulk temperature (Section 3).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Hard-switched transition (one HS/LS pair per PWM period)

t_r ≈ t_f ≈ Q_sw / I_g = 12 nC / 1 A = 12 ns. Allowing for loop inductance and driver dynamics, use 15 ns.

P_sw = ½ · V_bus · I_ph · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw
= 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 30 ns × 48 kHz = **1.45 W**

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

The LS body diode conducts during dead time and is then recovered by the HS turn-on.

P_rr = Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw = 40 nC × 25.2 × 48k = **0.048 W**

### 2.3 Output capacitance

P_oss ≈ Q_oss · V_bus · f_sw = 30 nC × 25.2 × 48k = **0.036 W**

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction (two edges per period)

P_dt = V_f · I_ph · 2 t_dt · f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48k = **0.61 W**

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FETs.

P_g = 2 · Q_g · V_GS · f_sw = 2 × 40 nC × 10 V × 48k = **0.038 W**

**≈ 2.2 W / channel**
