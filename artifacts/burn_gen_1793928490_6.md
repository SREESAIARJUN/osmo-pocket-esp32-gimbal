# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Systems Architecture Package (Job #72)

## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility Notes

The brief did not include a MOSFET part, battery voltage, board size or burst duration. I have fixed these as explicit parameters so you can substitute your own. **Every number below follows from the parameters in §0.2.** Verify all part numbers against current datasheets and stock before layout.

### 0.1 Three feasibility issues

1. **The H743 should not commutate the motors directly.**
   - ArduPilot does not run ESC firmware.
   - A 4-in-1 that is driven directly by the FC needs 12 complementary PWM channels with dead-time, plus BEMF comparators. That would consume TIM1, TIM8 and more, and conflict with the DShot outputs.
   - **Architecture decision:** H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to 4× AT32F421 ESC MCUs running AM32 (or BLHeli_32). Each ESC MCU drives a 3-phase gate driver. This is the industry-standard AIO topology.
2. **"Nano" and 80A burst are in tension.**
   - At 6S and 80A, each motor channel handles about 2 kW peak.
   - A 20×20 mm board cannot physically carry this current. Copper cross-section and connector pads alone exceed the available area.
   - The minimum credible size is **30.5×30.5 mm mounting / about 36×36 mm outline**, with 8-layer construction and 3 oz outer / 2 oz inner copper. The calculations below use this geometry.
3. **80A is a burst rating, not continuous.** The thermal model (§3) shows a burst window of roughly 2–4 s with no airflow at 4×80A. You should publish the rating as "80A burst (≤2 s), 55–60A continuous with prop-wash airflow."

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

The ESC uses trapezoidal 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM).

- At any instant, current flows through **one high-side leg and one low-side leg**.
- In the PWM-active phase leg, current alternates between the high-side (fraction D) and the low-side sync FET (fraction 1−D).
- So **two FET channels are always in series with the motor current**, regardless of duty cycle.

$$
P_{cond,motor} = I^2 \cdot \left(2\,R_{ds,hot} + R_{Cu} + R_{shunt}\right)
$$

$$
R_{ds,hot} = R_{ds,25}\cdot k_T = 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.6 = 1.6\,\text{m}\Omega
$$

### 1.2 Parasitic resistances

**Copper path R_Cu (pad to FET to pad, both phases):**
- Effective trace length is about 2 × 12 mm, width about 6 mm.
- The 3 oz outer layer is paralleled with 2×2 oz inner layers, giving an effective thickness of about 245 µm.

$$
R_{Cu} = \rho \frac{L}{wt} = 1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot\frac{0.024}{0.006\cdot245\times10^{-6}}\cdot 1.39_{(hot)} \approx 0.39\,\text{m}\Omega
$$

**Shunt:** Use a 0.25 mΩ shunt, or omit it and use a common shunt per ESC for current telemetry. In this model, R_shunt = 0.25 mΩ is applied only to the DC-link return. Since it carries the DC-link current I·D, its contribution at full throttle is about I²·0.25 mΩ.

### 1.3 Result per motor at 80A, D ≈ 1

$$
P_{cond} = 80^2(3.2 + 0.39 + 0.25)\,\text{m}\Omega = 6400 \times 3.84\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{24.6\ W}
$$

| Component | Loss |
|---|---|
| MOSFETs | 20.5 W |
| Copper | 2.5 W |
| Shunt | 1.6 W |

**Per-FET loss:** Each FET conducts during 1/3 of the electrical cycle, so the averaged loss is:

$$
P_{FET,avg} = \frac{20.5}{6}\approx 3.4\ \text{W}
$$

The instantaneous loss while conducting is 80²·1.6 mΩ = **10.2 W**.

- At 100k eRPM, the electrical frequency is about 1.67 kHz, so conduction slices last about 200 µs.
- The die thermal time constant (about 1–5 ms) partially averages these slices.
- Use a ripple factor of 1.3 on ΔT_j to account for this.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In 6-step operation, **only one leg is PWM-switched at a time** per motor. Each PWM period produces one turn-on and one turn-off hard transition on that leg.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 20\text{ns}\cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

The sync FET body diode conducts during dead-time, then recovers when the high-side turns on.

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 45\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.054\ W}
$$

This term charges and discharges one of the two FETs per transition.

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot100\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}
$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET channel.

$$
P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\cdot55\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k} = 0.053\ \text{W per motor}
$$
