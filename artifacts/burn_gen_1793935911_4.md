## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

Two things need to be settled before any math is meaningful.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** A "4-in-1 ESC" with 48 kHz PWM implies four independent ESC MCUs (or one multi-channel ESC MCU) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical choices are the AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks bidirectional DShot600 to those MCUs. The 48 kHz switching analysis applies to the ESC power stage. The DMA analysis applies to the H743.

**2. "80 A burst" on a nano footprint is a transient rating, not a thermal operating point.** Section 3 shows that steady-state dissipation at 80 A on all four channels is about 87 W into roughly 9 cm² of board. That is not sustainable. The useful deliverable is therefore a burst-duration budget.

**Parameter set.** All results scale with these values. Substitute your actual datasheet numbers.

| Symbol | Meaning | Assumed value |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 6S pack, full charge | 25.2 V |
| I_pk | Burst phase current per channel | 80 A |
| R_ds(on),25 | Per-FET on-resistance (40 V, 3×3/5×6 DFN class) | 1.0 mΩ |
| k_T | R_ds(on) multiplier at T_j ≈ 100 °C | 1.5 |
| t_r + t_f | Switching edge times at the chosen gate resistance | 20 + 20 ns |
| Q_rr | Body-diode reverse-recovery charge | 30 nC |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

The ESC uses six-step (trapezoidal) commutation with synchronous rectification. At any instant, current flows through exactly two FETs in series:
- one in the source phase, and
- one in the sink phase.

During the PWM off-time, the complementary low-side FET of the PWM'd leg takes over. So two R_ds(on) are always in the path:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds(on),25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

Here α ≈ 0.0067 /K, which gives k_T ≈ 1.5 at 100 °C.

At burst the DC-link ripple is small relative to 80 A, so I_rms ≈ I_pk.

### 1.2 Numbers

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 80^2 \cdot 2 \cdot 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 6400 \cdot 0.003 = \mathbf{19.2\ W}
$$

$$
P_{cond,4ch} = \mathbf{76.8\ W}
$$

**Per-FET share.** Each FET conducts about 1/3 of the electrical cycle as a series element. The PWM-complement current shifts some of the sink phase's share between high-side and low-side.

- Average per FET: 19.2 / 6 ≈ **3.2 W**
- Instantaneous while conducting: 80² × 1.5 mΩ = **9.6 W**

The instantaneous figure is what drives junction ripple (Section 3.3).

### 1.3 Electrothermal feedback

Because R_ds(on) rises with T_j, conduction loss and temperature are coupled:

$$
T_j = T_a + R_{th}\,P(T_j)
$$

The stable solution exists only when:

$$
R_{th}\cdot I^2\cdot 2R_{25}\,\alpha < 1
$$

With R_th(board) ≈ 5.6 K/W (Section 3.1):

$$
5.6 \cdot 6400 \cdot 0.002 \cdot 0.0067 \approx 0.48
$$

This is below 1, so there is no thermal runaway. It is still strongly coupled: about a 2× gain on the ΔT you would predict with a fixed resistance.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In six-step operation only one leg is hard-switched at a time. Per channel:

$$
P_{sw,ch} = \underbrace{\tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f}_{\text{V–I overlap}}
+ \underbrace{Q_{rr} V f}_{\text{recovery}}
+ \underbrace{\tfrac12 C_{oss}V^2 f}_{\text{C}_{oss}}
+ \underbrace{2 V_f I\, t_{dead} f}_{\text{dead-time diode}}
$$

| Term | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | 0.5 · 25.2 · 80 · 40 ns · 48 kHz | 1.94 W |
| Q_rr | 25.2 · 30 nC · 48 kHz | 0.036 W |
| C_oss | 0.5 · 1.5 nF · 25.2² · 48 kHz | 0.023 W |
| Dead-time diode | 2 · 0.8 · 80 · 100 ns · 48 kHz | 0.61 W |
| **Per channel** | | **≈ 2.61 W** |
| **Four channels** | | **≈ 10.4 W** |

**Gate-drive power.** This is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs:

$$
P_g = Q_g V_{gs} f \cdot 2 = 40\,\text{nC}\cdot10\cdot48\,\text{k}\cdot 2 \approx 38\ \text{mW/ch}
$$

**Design levers:**
- **Edge times dominate.** Halving t_r + t_f (stronger driver, lower R_g) saves about 1 W per channel, but costs EMI and V_ds overshoot. Check the ringing against the 40 V rating at 25.2 V plus the L·di/dt spike.
- **Dead time is second.** Dropping 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel.
- **Frequency tradeoff.** Moving from 48 kHz to 24 kHz halves P_sw but increases current ripple and motor iron loss.
