## 0. Architecture Review: Three Issues to Resolve First

Before the derivations, three points in the brief will change the design. They are flagged here rather than buried in the math.

| # | Issue | Finding | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **"80 A burst" on a nano form factor** | The lumped thermal model (§3.4) shows that 4 × 80 A simultaneous on a 20×20 mm board heats the board at roughly 80 K/s. Junctions reach their limit in about 0.5 to 1 s. | Re-rate to about **35 A continuous / 45–55 A burst (≤5 s) per channel** for 20×20. Alternatively, keep 80 A but move to 30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 3 oz copper, with a heatsink. |
| 2 | **H743 as the ESC MCU** | The H743 runs ArduPilot (the FC side). Commutation/FOC firmware such as AM32 or BLHeli_32 runs on separate ESC MCUs. | The AIO needs either **4× AT32F421 / STM32G071** (one per channel, AM32) or a dual-motor-capable MCU. The H743 talks to them over bi-directional DShot. The architecture below assumes 4× ESC MCUs. |
| 3 | **Battery current vs. phase current** | At partial throttle, phase current exceeds battery current (I_phase ≈ I_batt / D). An "80 A burst" rating must state which current it means. | All losses below use **phase (FET) current = 80 A**, the worst case. |

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

With synchronous rectification, the commutation current always flows through one high-side and one low-side FET path in series. During PWM, the switched leg alternates between its high-side FET (duty D) and low-side FET (1−D).

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{ds,hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{ds,hot} = 2\,I_{ph}^2 R_{ds,hot}$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.4\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{30.7\ W}$$

Per-FET distribution:

- **Continuously-on FET** (opposite leg): 15.4 W.
- **Switched leg:** D · 15.4 W on the high side and (1−D) · 15.4 W on the low side.

Including the RMS ripple term gives:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

This is negligible at 48 kHz with typical motor inductance (ΔI ≈ 1–3 A).

**Temperature feedback.** R_ds(on) rises with T_j:

$$R(T) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ /\text{K}$$

The coupled solution is solved iteratively in §3.

### 2.2 Hard-Switching (V-I Overlap) Loss

Only the switched leg's high-side FET hard-switches. The low-side FET commutates at near-zero voltage.

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2}\, V_{bus}\, I_{ph}\,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}$$

$$= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse Recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}$$

This loss is deposited in the high-side FET at turn-on.

### 2.4 Output Capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.024\ W}$$

### 2.5 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I_{ph} \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

This loss is deposited in the low-side FET.

**Lever:** cutting t_dt to 50 ns (AM32 adjustable, if the driver allows) saves 0.3 W per channel.

### 2.6 Gate Drive

This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g, not in the FET.

$$P_{g} = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} \times 2 = 60\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = 0.058\ \text{W}$$

### 2.7 Per-Channel and Board Totals at 80 A

| Loss | W / channel |
|---|---|
| Conduction | 30.70 |
| Switching | 1.94 |
| Dead time | 0.61 |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.06 |
| Gate (driver) | 0.06 |
| Shunt (0.5 mΩ, if per-channel) | 3.20 |
| Copper traces/vias (est. 0.3 mΩ phase path) | 1.92 |
| **Total** | **≈ 38.5 W** |
| **4 channels** | **≈ 154 W** |

**Key insight:** conduction dominates (about 80% of losses). Switching frequency is not the problem at 48 kHz, so lowering f_sw to 24 kHz saves only about 1 W per channel. **R_ds(on) and copper resistance are the levers.** Paralleling two FETs per switch position halves P_cond, but that does not fit on a 20×20 board.

Efficiency check at 80 A:

- P_out ≈ 25.2 × 80 × D. At D ≈ 1, this is 2 kW per channel.
- Loss is about 1.9%, which is a fine number electrically. The problem is purely thermal density.
