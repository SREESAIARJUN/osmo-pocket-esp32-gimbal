# STM32H743 AIO 4-in-1 ESC Nano FC: Reference Design Model

**Job #50 · Revision A (preliminary)**

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

No component selections or form factor were specified, so this document fixes a baseline. Every result below is parametric, so you can substitute datasheet values later.

### 0.1 Architecture correction

The STM32H743 should **not** commutate the motors. A production-viable AIO uses one of two layouts:

- **Recommended: 4 dedicated ESC MCUs.** Use STM32G071 or AT32F421, one per motor, running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Each runs its own 48 kHz PWM. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to each ESC MCU.
- **Alternative: 2× dual-ESC MCUs.** This reduces part count but is less common for AM32.

The H743 handles flight control, DShot generation, eRPM telemetry decoding, and harmonic notch filtering. The motor-drive model in §1–2 applies to the ESC power stage regardless of which MCU drives it.

**Physical reality check:** a 20×20 mm "nano" board cannot fit 24× 5×6 mm FETs. Even 3.3×3.3 FETs (typically 1.5–2.5 mΩ) on a 20×20 board is extremely tight, and 80 A per motor on that footprint is a marketing-level burst figure. This model treats 80 A as a **sub-second transient** and shows why (§3). For a credible 80 A burst rating, a 30.5×30.5 mm or larger ESC board is strongly advised.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology

The power stage is six-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM. In every 60° sector, exactly two phase legs carry current:

- **PWM leg:** high-side (HS) on for D·T, low-side (LS) sync-rectifying for (1−D)·T.
- **Return leg:** LS on continuously for the whole sector.

At any instant, the current path therefore contains **two R_DS(on) in series**, independent of duty cycle.

### 1.2 Junction-temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)] \approx R_{25}\,(T_j/25\,°C\ \text{scaling per datasheet curve})$$

Use R(100 °C) ≈ 1.34 mΩ and R(125 °C) ≈ 1.45 mΩ.

### 1.3 Per-motor conduction loss

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2 R_{DS}(T_j) + I_{ph}^2 R_{sh} + I_{ph}^2 R_{Cu}$$

Over one electrical cycle, the switching-device term distributes across the six FETs. Each FET is in the path for 120° electrical:

- **HS of PWM leg:** $P = I^2 R \cdot D \cdot (1/3)$, since it is the PWM leg for half its conduction sectors and the return/freewheel arrangement for the rest.
- **Average per FET:** ≈ $P_{FET,cond} ≈ \frac{I^2 \cdot 2R}{6}$, with the LS devices biased higher at low duty because they carry the freewheel current.

**Worst-case per-FET** (low duty, LS freewheeling plus return):

$$P_{LS,max} ≈ I^2 R \left[\tfrac{1}{3} + \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)\right]$$

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A

| Term | Expression | @ T_j = 100 °C | @ T_j = 125 °C |
|---|---|---|---|
| FET path | 80² × 2 × R_DS | 6400 × 2.68 mΩ = **17.2 W** | 6400 × 2.90 mΩ = **18.6 W** |
| Shunt | 80² × 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W | 1.6 W |
| PCB copper (≈0.3 mΩ path, 2 oz ×4L) | 80² × 0.3 mΩ | 1.9 W | 2.1 W |
| **Per motor** | | **20.7 W** | **22.3 W** |
| Avg per FET (FET term ÷ 6) | | 2.9 W | 3.1 W |
| Worst LS FET (D→0) | 80² × R × 2/3 | 5.7 W | 6.2 W |

**Positive feedback:** loss rises with temperature, and temperature rises with loss. The thermal runaway condition is

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j} \cdot R_{th,ja} \ge 1 \quad \Rightarrow \quad I^2\,2R_{25}\,\alpha \cdot R_{th} \ge 1$$

At 80 A with R_th,ja,eff ≈ 30 K/W per motor-quadrant (§3), this gives 6400 × 2 × 1e-3 × 0.0045 × 30 ≈ **1.7**. **Steady-state 80 A is unconditionally unstable on a nano board.** Burst operation only.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz
