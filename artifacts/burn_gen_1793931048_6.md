# STM32H743 AIO / 4-in-1 80A ESC: Systems Architecture Package (Job #15)

## 0. Architectural Corrections and Assumptions

Three points need to be fixed before any math is meaningful.

1. **The H743 must not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot on the H743 is the flight controller. It sends DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry. Each motor channel needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071. The 48 kHz figure is the ESC's power-stage PWM frequency, set in the ESC firmware. It is not an H743 timer.

2. **"80A burst" must be defined.** This analysis assumes **80A per motor channel for ≤10 s at 6S (25.2 V full charge)**, with all four channels loaded simultaneously as the worst case. The results below show this is **not thermally sustainable on a nano (20×20/25.5×25.5) form factor**. The design limits are derived quantitatively.

3. **Component values are placeholders until datasheets are locked.** All parameters are listed in tables so they can be substituted. The baseline is a 40 V, ~1.0 mΩ, DFN5×6 N-MOSFET.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0055 × 100) = **1.55 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-step trapezoidal, synchronous (complementary) PWM

During each 60° commutation sector, two phases carry the current I:

- **PWM leg:** current flows through the HS FET for fraction D and through the synchronous LS FET for (1−D). Loss = I²R·D + I²R·(1−D) = **I²R**, independent of duty.
- **Return leg:** the LS FET is on for the full sector. Loss = **I²R**.

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2I^2R_{DS}(T_j) + I^2 R_{trace}$$

The result for each channel at 80 A:

- **Silicon:** 2 × 6400 × 1.55 mΩ = **19.8 W**
- **Copper path:** assume R_trace ≈ 0.25 mΩ effective (bus + phase pads, 2 oz inner planes), giving 6400 × 0.25 mΩ = **1.6 W**
- **Total conduction:** **≈ 21.4 W per channel**

Each conducting FET dissipates roughly 9.9 W. Over a full electrical cycle, each FET conducts about 1/3 of the time. Thermal time constants at the die (ms) are comparable to commutation periods at low eRPM, so peak die heating should use the **instantaneous 9.9 W**, not the averaged ~3.3 W.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Each PWM period contains one turn-on and one turn-off hard transition on the HS FET, with the LS FET acting as the freewheel diode during dead time.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard switching)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery of the LS body diode

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$

Q_rr scales roughly with I and di/dt. At 80 A, derate the datasheet value by the test-current ratio.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \times 2 = 0.5 \times 60\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = \mathbf{0.073\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

This term is significant. Reducing dead time to 50 ns (AM32 setting) halves it, but only do this if the gate driver's propagation-delay mismatch allows it.

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in driver and gate resistors, not the FET)

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2 = 60\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = \mathbf{0.058\ W}$$

### 2.6 Switching total

$$P_{sw,ch} \approx 1.94 + 0.06 + 0.07 + 0.61 = \mathbf{2.68\ W}$$

### 2.7 Frequency trade-off

P_sw scales linearly with f_sw, so going from 24 kHz to 48 kHz costs about +1.3 W per channel at 80 A. That buys lower current ripple and acoustic benefit. With typical 2–5 µH motor phase inductance, ripple at 48 kHz is

$$\Delta I \approx \frac{V_{bus}D(1-D)}{L f_{sw}}$$

which gives about 13 A peak-to-peak at D = 0.5 with L = 10 µH line-line. 48 kHz is justified for low-inductance, high-KV motors.

## 3. Total Loss Budget

| Item | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (silicon) | 19.8 W | 79.2 W |
| Conduction (copper) | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| Switching | 2.7 W | 10.7 W |
| Gate drive / LDO / MCU | 0.3 W | 1.2 W |
| **Total** | **24.4 W** | **≈ 97.5 W** |

Efficiency check: output power is about 4 × 25.2 × 80 × D_eff. At full throttle that is roughly 8 kW, so efficiency is around 98.8%. Efficiency is not the problem. **Heat density is.**

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Board lumped heat capacity (25.5 × 25.5 mm ESC board, 8-layer, 1.6 mm)

| Element | Volume / mass | c_p | C_th |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copper (6 × 70 µm effective, ~70% fill) | 650 mm² × 0.42 mm ≈ 0.27 cm³ → 2.45 g | 0.385 J/gK | 0.94 J/K |
| FR-4 | 1.04 cm³ → 1.93 g | 1.1 J/gK | 2.1 J/K |
| 24 FETs + caps + solder | ~3.5 g | ~0.6 J/gK | 2.1 J/K |
| **Total** | | | **≈ 5.2 J/K** |

### 4.2 Adiabatic board heating rate at 97.5 W

$$\frac{dT_{board}}{dt} = \frac{P}{C_{th}} = \frac{97.5}{5.2} \approx \mathbf{18.8\ K/s}$$

### 4.3 Convective sink (prop wash)

Take h ≈ 60–120 W/m²K with forced air, and exposed area A ≈ 2 × 650 mm² + edges ≈ 1.5×10⁻³ m².

$$R_{conv} = \frac{1}{hA} \approx 5.5\text{–}11\ \text{K/W}$$

This gives only **9–18 W** of steady-state removal at 100 K rise. That is **≥5× short of 97.5 W**, so the system is essentially adiabatic during a burst.
