# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Budget, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Allocation

## 0. Architectural Assumptions

The H743 cannot drive the power stage at 48 kHz. On every production 4-in-1 stack, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU, so I assume this partition:

| Subsystem | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100), 480 MHz | ArduPilot. Generates DShot600 and decodes bidirectional eRPM. |
| ESC MCUs (×4) | STM32G071 or AT32F421 class, AM32 firmware | 48 kHz complementary PWM, commutation, telemetry |
| Gate drivers (×4) | 3-phase half-bridge driver (FD6288Q class) | Bootstrap high-side drive |
| Power FETs (×24) | 40 V N-ch, PDFN3.3×3 or 5×6 | 6 per channel |

**Electrical operating point:** 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max (22.2 V nominal). The burst is 80 A phase current per channel.

**Representative FET parameters.** Replace these with your selected part's datasheet values. All equations below are parametric.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| On-resistance @ 25 °C | R_DS(on),25 | 1.2 mΩ |
| R_DS(on) temperature factor @ T_j = 110 °C | k_T | 1.5 |
| Rise / fall time (with driver) | t_r, t_f | 8 ns / 8 ns |
| Output capacitance | C_oss | 1.5 nF |
| Reverse recovery charge | Q_rr | 30 nC |
| Total gate charge | Q_g | 40 nC @ 10 V |
| Body diode forward voltage | V_f | 0.8 V |
| Dead time | t_dt | 50 ns |

> **Feasibility finding (stated up front).** Section 2 shows that a true 20×20 mm "nano" ESC cannot sustain 80 A on all four channels for more than about 1–2 seconds. This is set by heat capacity, not by the silicon. "80 A burst" ratings on small boards are generally single-channel, sub-second, or marketing figures. Accordingly, I specify the design to a **30.5×30.5 mm ESC board with a 2 s, 4-channel burst envelope**, with the nano FC stacked on top. If 20×20 is mandatory, derate to about 45–55 A burst.

## 1. Loss Model (Per Motor Channel)

### 1.1 Conduction Topology

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM. At any instant, phase current flows through exactly two FET channels:

- one high-side FET (PWM-switching), plus
- one low-side FET (static on), or
- the synchronous LS partner during the PWM off-time.

Because of synchronous rectification, the current path always has a resistance of 2·R_DS regardless of duty cycle. Body-diode conduction is confined to the dead time.

### 1.2 Conduction Loss

The conduction loss is:

$$
P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{DS(on),25}\,k_T + I_{ph}^2 R_{Cu}
$$

FET term at 80 A:

$$
P_{cond,FET} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.5 = 6400 \times 3.6\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{23.0\ W}
$$

**Copper term.** Model 15 mm of current path at 4 mm width. With 2 oz copper on three paralleled layers:

- R_sheet ≈ 0.25 mΩ/□ per layer, giving about 0.083 mΩ/□ for the three layers combined.
- 15/4 = 3.75 squares, so R_Cu ≈ 0.31 mΩ.
- At 100 °C, the copper factor is about 1.3, so R_Cu ≈ 0.4 mΩ.

$$
P_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.4\,\text{m}\Omega \approx \mathbf{2.6\ W}
$$

The copper loss is not negligible. It heats the same thermal mass as the FETs.

**Temperature dependence.** k_T itself depends on T_j:

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) \approx R_{DS,25}\left(\frac{T_j+273}{298}\right)^{\alpha},\quad \alpha \approx 2.0\text{–}2.3
$$

This creates positive electrothermal feedback. Section 2.4 checks it for runaway.

### 1.3 Switching Loss at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switching FET incurs hard-switching loss. The synchronous FET switches at zero voltage, apart from the dead-time diode.

**(a) V–I overlap loss**

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}
$$

$$
= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 16\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.77\ W}
$$

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss} V^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\,\text{nF} \times 635 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.023\ W
