# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Thermal Model and Bi-Directional DShot600 Design Package

**Job #41 · Rev A (Engineering Draft)**

## 0. Architectural Clarifications and Assumptions

**Silicon partitioning.** The STM32H743 is the **flight controller**. It does not commutate the motors. Each of the four ESC channels has its own commutation MCU, assumed here to be an STM32G071 running AM32. The H743 talks to each ESC over one bi-directional DShot600 line.

**Design assumptions.** Replace these with your BOM datasheet values. Every equation below is parameterized so you can re-run it.

| Parameter | Symbol | Assumed value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S, full charge) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Burst phase current per ESC | I_b | 80 A (DC-equivalent motor current) |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| MOSFET (40 V, DFN3.3×3.3) R_ds(on) @ 25 °C, V_gs = 10 V | R_25 | 1.0 mΩ |
| R_ds(on) temperature coefficient | α | ×1.5 at T_j = 125 °C |
| Rise / fall time (at the gate driver's real drive strength) | t_r, t_f | 20 ns / 15 ns |
| Total gate charge | Q_g | 60 nC @ 10 V |
| Output charge | Q_oss | 40 nC |
| Reverse-recovery charge | Q_rr | 50 nC |
| Body-diode forward voltage | V_f | 0.8 V |
| Dead time per edge | t_dt | 100 ns |
| Gate driver | — | 3-phase half-bridge, bootstrap (FD6288Q class) |
| Commutation | — | Trapezoidal, complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM |

> ⚠️ **Feasibility flag (read first).** 80 A per channel (320 A aggregate) on a 20×20 / 25.5×25.5 mm nano board is a **seconds-scale burst rating only**. The math in §3 shows roughly 1.5–3 s before junction limits are reached. Continuous capability realistically sits at 30–40 A per channel with prop-wash airflow. Marketing should not publish "80A" without the duration and conditions attached.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

In trapezoidal six-step control, two phases conduct at any instant:
- The PWM'd high-side, whose synchronous complement low-side takes the current during the off-time.
- The static low-side of the return phase.

The motor current therefore always flows through **exactly two MOSFET channels in series**:

$$
P_{cond,ESC} = 2\, I_b^2\, R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + k\,(T_j - 25)\right],\; k \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

### 1.2 Evaluation at 80 A

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per ESC |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.0 mΩ | 2·6400·0.0010 = **12.8 W** |
| 125 °C | 1.5 mΩ | 2·6400·0.0015 = **19.2 W** |

### 1.3 Per-device distribution

Each phase leg is active for 2/3 of the electrical cycle. Within that window, the high-side carries current for duty D and its low-side complement for (1−D). Averaged over a cycle at high throttle (D ≈ 0.9):

- High-side FET: P ≈ I²R·(1/3)·D + switching (§2)
- Low-side FET: P ≈ I²R·(1/3)·(2−D)

At D = 0.9 and 125 °C:
- **HS ≈ 2.9 W conduction**
- **LS ≈ 3.5 W conduction**

The low-sides are the hottest devices at high throttle. Place them closest to the thermal pad / heatsink path.

### 1.4 Parasitic copper and shunt (frequently ignored, not negligible)

2 oz Cu sheet resistance is about 0.245 mΩ/□ at 25 °C, rising to about 0.34 mΩ/□ at 125 °C.

**Phase pad to motor pad.** Assume roughly 1.5 squares effective with paralleled inner layers, giving about 0.25 mΩ per path:

$$
P_{Cu} = I_b^2\cdot R_{Cu}\cdot 2 \approx 6400\cdot 0.00025\cdot 2 = 3.2\ \text{W per ESC}
$$

**Battery current shunt.** For 4 × 80 A = 320 A at 6S, battery current is lower than motor current by the duty ratio, so take I_batt ≈ 290 A at D ≈ 0.9. With a 0.2 mΩ shunt:

$$
P_{shunt} = 290^2 \cdot 0.0002 \approx 16.8\ \text{W}
$$

**Recommendation:** use 0.1 mΩ with a ×100 amplifier (INA186-class), or two shunts in parallel. This cuts the shunt loss to about 8.4 W.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

Hard switching occurs on the high-side PWM leg only, at one switching event pair per period. The low-side static FET does not switch at f_sw.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_b (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot35\text{ns}\cdot48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.69\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance loss

$$
P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{nC}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

The complementary low-side's body diode conducts during dead time, then recovers when the high-side turns on:

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{nC}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}
$$

This loss is deposited mainly in the high-side.

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time windows per period:

$$
P_{dt} = 2\,V_f\, I_b\, t_{dt}\, f_{sw} = 2\cdot0.8\cdot80\cdot100\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

This loss is deposited in the low-side.

### 2.5 Gate-drive loss

The driver dissipates this, not the FETs. Two FETs are actively switched per active leg; the conservative figure below counts all six as switching:

$$
P_g = Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw}\cdot n = 60\text{nC}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k}\cdot 2 = 0.058\ \text{W (active leg)},\quad \le 0.17\ \text{W worst case}
$$

### 2.6 Bootstrap capacitor sizing

The longest on-time occurs at 100 % duty during a 60° electrical sector. At the lowest eRPM where full duty is possible, assume a 2 ms worst-case hold:

$$
C_{boot} \ge \frac{Q_g + I_{qbs}\,t_{hold}}{\Delta V_{boot}} = \frac{60\text{nC} + 150\,\mu\text{A}\cdot2\text{ms}}{0.5\text{ V}} \approx 0.72\,\mu\text{F} \;\rightarrow\; \text{use } 1\,\mu\text{F X7R 25
