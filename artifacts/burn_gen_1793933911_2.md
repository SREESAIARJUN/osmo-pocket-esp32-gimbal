## 0. Architectural Clarification and Feasibility Flags

Two points need settling before the math, because they change where each requirement applies.

1. **The STM32H743 does not generate the 48 kHz motor PWM.** In a 4-in-1 ESC stack, the H743 runs ArduPilot and emits DShot600. Four ESC MCUs run AM32 or BLHeli_32 and generate the 48 kHz 6-step PWM. Typical ESC MCUs are the AT32F421 or STM32G071, either one per motor or a dual-MCU variant. The loss model (§1–3) applies to the ESC board. The DMA plan (§4) applies to the H743.
2. **An 80 A per-motor burst on a nano board is a short transient only.** As derived in §3, four channels at 80 A dissipate about 90 W into a board with roughly 5 J/K heat capacity. The usable burst is a few seconds. It is not a continuous rating. I recommend specifying this as **"80 A / 2 s burst, 1 motor or 4 motors, from 40 °C board start"** and defining a continuous rating separately.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per motor channel, 6-step synchronous)

### 2.1 Conduction Topology

During each 60° commutation sector, two phases conduct:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side (HS) conducts for D·T_sw. The low-side (LS) conducts synchronously for (1−D)·T_sw.
- **Static leg:** the LS is fully on.

At every instant the current therefore flows through exactly two channels in series, independent of D.

### 2.2 Conduction Loss

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha_R (T_j-25)]$$

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

At T_j = 100 °C: R = 1.5 mΩ.

$$P_{cond} = 2(80)^2(1.5\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

Averaged across 6 FETs this is about 3.2 W/FET. The static-leg LS FETs run somewhat hotter than average.
