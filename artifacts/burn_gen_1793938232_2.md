## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step trapezoidal or sinusoidal (FOC-like) synchronous drive with complementary PWM, current always flows through **two** MOSFET positions in series:

- **PWM-on (duty D):** HS(phase A) → motor → LS(phase B)
- **PWM-off (1−D):** LS(phase A) (synchronous rectification) → motor → LS(phase B)

So at any instant, two channels conduct I_ph, and the loss is independent of D:

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)\,\big[D + (1-D) + 1\big]\cdot\tfrac{1}{1}\;\;\Rightarrow\;\; P_{cond} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big] \approx R_{25}\,k_T$$

### 1.2 Numbers

$$R_{DS(on)}(110^\circ C) = 0.90 \times 1.5 = 1.35\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.35\times10^{-3} = 17.3\ \text{W per motor channel}$$

### 1.3 Copper / interconnect conduction

Include the PCB path loss. A representative value is battery pad → bus plane → FET → phase pad, with an effective resistance of R_cu ≈ 0.4 mΩ round trip per channel. This assumes 2 oz outer copper and 1 oz inner copper, with shared planes. **[A], extract with a field solver or 4-wire measurement.**

$$P_{cu} = I^2 R_{cu} = 80^2 \times 0.4\,\text{m}\Omega = 2.56\ \text{W}$$

Copper resistance also rises at about 0.393 %/K. At a +60 K board rise, this gives about 3.2 W.

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

Over an electrical revolution each FET carries current about 1/3 of the time, with the HS/LS split set by D. The average per-FET loss is therefore:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx \frac{P_{cond}}{6} = 2.9\ \text{W}$$

The worst-case FET is the LS during low-duty, high-current operation (punch-outs at low throttle with high load). That FET carries:

$$P_{LS,max} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}\,I^2 R\,(1 + (1-D)) \to \text{up to } \tfrac{2}{3}I^2R = 5.8\ \text{W}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd half-bridge switches each cycle. The hard-switched device is the HS turning on against the LS body diode.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12\, V_{bus}\, I\,(t_r+t_f)\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 0.97\ \text{W}$$

The effective t_r and t_f come from the Miller plateau charge and the driver current:

$$t_{r}\approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{G,on}},\quad I_{G,on}=\frac{V_{DRV}-V_{plateau}}{R_{G,ext}+R_{G,int}+R_{DRV,pu}}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 50\text{nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12\, Q_{oss}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 0.5\times40\text{nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.024\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two edges per period:

$$P_{dt} = 2\, V_F\, I\, t_{dt}\, f_{sw} = 2\times0.8\times80\times100\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

This term is the strongest argument for minimizing dead time. AM32 and BLHeli_32 dead-time settings should be tuned on the bench with V_DS ringing measured.
