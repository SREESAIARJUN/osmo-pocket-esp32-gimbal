## 0. Scope and Assumptions

I don't have prior context for "Job #15," so this document states its assumptions explicitly. Swap in your actual BOM values. Every equation is parameterized for that purpose.

**Architectural correction.** The STM32H743 runs the **flight controller** (ArduPilot). Each ESC channel is commutated by a small ESC MCU, typically an AT32F421, STM32G071 or GD32 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 does not drive the FET gates. All loss math below lives on the ESC side. The DShot/DMA math lives on the FC side.

**Feasibility flag.** 80 A per motor is not survivable on a 20×20 mm "nano" board beyond about 1 s. This design assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm ESC footprint (≈36×36 mm PCB)**. Even then, 80 A is a **time-limited burst**, about 5 s (derived in §3).

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A burst)

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. At any instant, motor current flows through two legs:

- **The PWM leg:** the high-side conducts for duty D and the low-side (sync rectifier) for 1−D. Together they carry I for 100 % of the period.
- **The return leg:** the low-side is held fully on and carries I for 100 % of the period.

Instantaneous conduction loss per motor is therefore independent of D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds(on)}(T_j)=R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)] \approx k_T R_{25}$$

$$R_{hot}=1.5 \times 0.70 = 1.05\text{ mΩ}$$

$$P_{cond,motor}=2(80)^2(1.05\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{13.4\ W}$$

Add the trace and copper loss. With a 2 oz inner+outer stack, assume about 0.3 mΩ effective per current path:

$$P_{Cu}=80^2 \times 0.3\text{ mΩ} \approx 1.9\text{ W/motor}$$

**Positive feedback check.** R_ds rises with T_j. The model converges if

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\,R_{th} < 1 \;\Rightarrow\; 2I^2R_{25}\alpha R_{th,jA}<1$$

With α = 0.004/K and R_th,jA,eff ≈ 3.85 K/W (§3), the left side is 2·6400·0.7e-3·0.004·3.85 ≈ **0.14**. That is stable, with no thermal runaway at the board level.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Per motor, per switching event pair:

**(a) V–I overlap (hard switching, inductive load)**

$$I_{g,on}=\frac{V_{dr}-V_{pl}}{R_g}=1.4\text{ A},\quad t_r=\frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{g,on}}=10\text{ ns}$$
$$I_{g,off}=\frac{V_{pl}}{R_g}=0.6\text{ A},\quad t_f=\frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{g,off}}=23\text{ ns}$$
$$P_{VI}=\tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = \tfrac12(25.2)(80)(33\text{ ns})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.60\ W}$$

Turn-off dominates. Use an asymmetric gate path (a diode across the turn-off resistor, or a lower R_g,off) to cut t_f.

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead times per period)

$$P_{dt}=V_f\,I\,(2t_d)\,f_{sw}=0.8(80)(400\text{ ns})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

This is nearly as large as the overlap loss. Trimming dead time from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per motor, but you must validate shoot-through margin against the driver's propagation skew.

**(c) Reverse recovery, Coss, gate charge**

$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}=0.060\text{ W}$$
$$P_{oss}=\tfrac12 Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}=0.036\text{ W}$$
$$P_{gate}=2\,Q_gV_{dr}f_{sw}=0.058\text{ W (dissipated mostly in driver/R}_g)$$

**Switching total per motor:** ≈ **2.98 W**

### Loss summary at 80 A burst

| Term | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs, hot) | 13.4 W | 53.8 W |
| Copper / traces | 1.9 W | 7.7 W |
| V–I switching | 1.60 W | 6.4 W |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 W | 4.9 W |
| Qrr + Coss + gate | 0.15 W | 0.6 W |
| **Total** | **18.3 W** | **73.4 W** |

Efficiency at burst: P_out ≈ 4 × 25.2 × 80 × D_avg. At full throttle (D ≈ 0.95), P_out ≈ 7.7 kW and η ≈ 99.0 %. Losses are small in relative terms but enormous for a 36 mm board.

**f_sw sensitivity.** Switching terms scale linearly with frequency. 48 → 24 kHz saves about 1.5 W/motor but increases current ripple. Ripple is ΔI ≈ V_bus·D(1−D)/(L_ph·f_sw). For L_ph = 20 µH at D = 0.5, ΔI ≈ 6.6 A at 48 kHz versus 13 A at 24 kHz.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Junction-to-board (per FET)

Electrical frequency (≫100 Hz at speed) is far faster than the FET thermal time constant (tens of ms). So use the **average** loss per FET: 6 FETs share 18.3 W − 1.9 W copper ≈ 16.4 W.

$$P_{FET,avg}\approx 16.4/6 = 2.7\text{ W}$$

R_th(j-board) for PQFN 3.3×3.3 on 2 oz copper with ≥9 thermal vias (0.3 mm) ≈ 8 K/W. That gives ΔT_jb ≈ **22 K**.

**Low-RPM / stall case.** At stall, a single FET pair carries DC continuously, which gives P_FET ≈ 6.7 + 1.5 ≈ 8 W and ΔT_jb ≈ **64 K**. This is the real worst case and why stall/desync protection matters.
