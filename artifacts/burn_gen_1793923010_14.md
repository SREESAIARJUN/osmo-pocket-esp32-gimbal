# STM32H743 Flight Controller + 4‑in‑1 ESC: Design Model (Job #5)

This document gives the thermal and loss model, the bi-directional DShot600 timer and DMA plan, the netlists, and the hwdef and parameter configuration.

---

## 0. Feasibility Issues

Three items need to be settled before the math, because they change what the numbers mean.

**1. The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.**
- In every production 4‑in‑1 stack, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical parts are the STM32G071 or AT32F421.
- The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot600 to those four ESC MCUs.
- The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs, not the H743.
- I have architected it this way. Driving 24 MOSFETs directly from the H743 under ArduPilot is not supported firmware.

**2. "Nano" (20×20 mm) and 80 A burst are in tension.**
- 24 MOSFETs at 3.3×3.3 mm each, plus drivers, MCUs and capacitors, physically fits only on a ≥30×30 mm board, or with the ESC on a separate 20×20 layer.
- The model below shows that 80 A is achievable only as a short burst, about 2 s. It is not a continuous rating.

**3. Assumed component values.**
- Loss figures use representative 40 V MOSFET parameters, not a specific datasheet.
- Before release, replace them with the actual part's R_DS(on)(T_j), Q_gd, Q_rr, Q_oss and Z_th curves.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

In 6‑step commutation, two phases carry current at any instant. With complementary PWM at duty D:
- The high side (HS) of phase A conducts for D.
- The low side (LS) of phase A synchronously rectifies for 1−D.
- The LS of phase B conducts continuously.

There is therefore always exactly **two R_DS(on) in series** with the motor current:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = I^2 \left(2\,k_T R_{DS(on),25} + R_{Cu}\right)$$

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 80^2\,(2\cdot1.5\cdot1.0 + 0.3)\,\text{m}\Omega = 6400 \times 3.3\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{21.1\ W}$$

Per-FET average (each FET conducts about 1/3 of the electrical cycle):

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{80^2 \cdot 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega \cdot 2}{6} = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

The commutation period at flight eRPM is roughly 0.1–1 ms, which is much shorter than the die-to-board thermal time constant. The die therefore sees the average. The instantaneous peak is 9.6 W per conducting FET.

**R_DS(on) feedback (thermal runaway check).** Losses rise with temperature, so iterate:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_{board} + P(T_j^{(n)})\cdot R_{th,jb}$$

The iteration converges because

$$\frac{dP}{dT}\cdot R_{th} = 0.0045 \cdot P \cdot R_{th} \ll 1$$

At these values the product is about 0.1, so the operating point is stable.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing high side hard-switches. The LS synchronous FET turns on after dead time with its body diode already conducting, so it switches at near zero voltage (ZVS).

| Term | Formula | Value per ESC |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f = ½·25.2·80·30 ns·48 k | **1.45 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_F·I·2t_dt·f = 0.8·80·400 ns·48 k | **1.23 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f = 30 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.036 W |
| C_oss | ½·Q_oss·V·f | 0.024 W |
| Gate drive (2 active FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f | 0.038 W (dissipated in driver/R_g) |
| **Total P_sw** | | **≈ 2.78 W** |

**Design levers:**
- Dead-time diode loss is almost as large as overlap loss.
- Cutting t_dt from 200 to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per ESC. This needs a driver with tight propagation matching, such as an FD6288Q or a discrete bootstrap driver.
- Raising f_sw to 96 kHz doubles all of the switching terms to about 5.6 W.

**ESC MCU timer resolution** (STM32G071, TIM1 at 64 MHz):
- Edge-aligned: ARR = 64 MHz / 48 kHz = 1333, which gives 10.4‑bit throttle resolution.
- Center-aligned: ARR = 666, about 9.4 bits.
