### Architecture constraints

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot outputs DShot. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU. This design uses 4× AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, which supports bidirectional DShot and EDT telemetry. The architecture therefore has two parts:
- the H743 flight controller,
- a 4-in-1 ESC with four ESC MCUs.

These can be built as a stack or an AIO.

**2. "Nano" (20×20) cannot physically host an 80A/channel stage.**
- 24 PDFN 5×6 FETs need about 720 mm² of footprint.
- A 20×20 board offers 400 mm² total, before drivers, caps, and the MCU.
- 80A burst requires a 30×30 ESC, or dual-sided placement with 3.3×3.3 FETs at a higher R_ds(on).
- The figures below assume 30×30.

**3. The system is battery-limited before it is FET-limited.**
- 4 × 80 A = 320 A from the pack, about 7 kW.
- XT60 connectors and typical 6S packs cannot sustain this.
- The "80A burst" figure is therefore realistically a per-channel transient, not all four channels simultaneously.
- The worst case (all four at 80 A) is still computed below because it bounds the thermal design.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\quad \alpha\approx0.006\ /\mathrm{K} \;\Rightarrow\; k_T(125^\circ C)\approx1.6$$

This creates positive feedback, because P_cond depends on T_j. Section 3.4 solves the loop.

### 1.2 Six-step trapezoidal with synchronous rectification

At any instant, two phase legs carry I:
- one leg is PWM'd (the high-side FET for duty D, and its complementary low-side FET for 1 − D),
- one low-side FET is held on.

Because synchronous rectification is used, the PWM leg always has one FET conducting. Per channel:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Plus the PCB copper path (bus → FET → phase pad → return), R_cu ≈ 0.4–0.6 mΩ per channel:

$$P_{cu,ch} = I^2 R_{cu}$$

**Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C:**
- P_cond,ch = 2 × 6400 × 1.6 mΩ = **20.5 W**
- P_cu,ch = 6400 × 0.5 mΩ = **3.2 W**
