## 0. Assumptions and Scope

| Parameter | Assumed value | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal | 80 A burst class |

| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal | AM32 default |

**Key finding, stated up front:** an 80 A per-motor burst on a 20×20 board is thermally feasible only for about 1–2 seconds from a warm start. The math below shows why. If the requirement is sustained 80 A, the board must grow to 30.5×30.5 mm or larger, or the rating must be revised.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ /^\circ\text{C (typical trench MOSFET)}$$

| T_j | R_ds(on) |
|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.50 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 2.01 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 2.18 mΩ |

### 1.2 Six-step synchronous conduction

During each 60° commutation sector, one phase is PWM'd high, one is held low, and one floats.

- The high-side FET conducts for duty fraction D.
- The synchronous low-side FET on the same leg conducts for 1−D.
- The return low-side FET conducts continuously.

The motor current path therefore always passes through exactly two FET channels:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}(D) + I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}(1-D) + I_{rms}^2 R_{ds} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This form is independent of D in synchronous mode, which is a useful simplification.

### 1.3 Additional resistive losses

Add copper trace, via, and solder resistance, R_pcb ≈ 0.4 mΩ per path on a 2 oz, 8-layer board with via farms:

$$P_{cond,total,ch} = I^2\,(2R_{ds} + R_{pcb})$$

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | Per ESC channel | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 6400 × (3.0 + 0.4) mΩ = **21.8 W** | 87 W |
| 100 °C | 6400 × (4.02 + 0.4) mΩ = **28.3 W** | 113 W |

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches. Per channel, that is one high-side and one low-side FET at any moment.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap (high side)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus}\, I\,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}$$

The transition times come from gate charge and drive current:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}+Q_{gs2}}{I_{g,on}}, \quad I_{g,on}=\frac{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}{R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}}$$

Assume Q_gd + Q_gs2 ≈ 12 nC, V_drv = 10 V, V_pl ≈ 3.5 V, R_g total = 12 Ω. This gives I_g ≈ 0.54 A, t_r ≈ 22 ns, and t_f ≈ 15 ns (stronger sink path).

$$P_{VI} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 37\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.79\ W}$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance energy

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 35\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.042\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{DT} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2 t_{dead})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.4 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw}$$

At 80 A, di/dt-dependent Q_rr ≈ 60 nC gives 0.073 W.

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g, not the FET)

$$P_{gate} = Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} \times N_{switching}$$

With 45 nC × 10 V × 48 kHz × 2 = 0.043 W per channel.

### 2.6 Switching loss summary per channel

| Term | Watts |
|---|---|
| V–I overlap | 1.79 |
| Dead time | 0.61 |
| Q_rr | 0.07 |
| C_oss | 0.04 |
| Gate | 0.04 |
| **Total** | **2.55 W** |

### 2.7 Total power stage loss at 80 A, T_j = 100 °C

$$P_{ch} = 28.3 + 2.55 \approx \mathbf{30.9\ W}, \qquad P_{4ch} \approx \mathbf{124\ W}$$

Efficiency at 80 A × 22.2 V × 4 = 7.1 kW input is about 98.3%. The efficiency is fine; the problem is power density.

**Design lever:** dropping f_sw from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves only about 1.3 W per channel. Conduction loss dominates, so reducing R_ds and R_pcb pays off far more than reducing switching frequency.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Thermal network (Foster/Cauer hybrid)

```
P_FET ──[Zth_jc ≈ 1.2 K/W]── Case ──[Rth_solder+via ≈ 4 K/W per FET]── Board node (C_board)
                                                                          │
                                                      [Rth_board-amb ≈ 6–10 K/W (still air)
                                                       ≈ 2.5–4 K/W (prop-wash, 10 m/s)]
                                                                          │
                                                                        T_amb
```

### 3.2 Board thermal capacitance (20×20 mm)

| Material | Mass | c_p | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cu, 8 layers × 2 oz × 4 cm² | 2.0 g | 0.385 J/g·K | 0.77 J/K |
| FR4, 1.6 mm | 1.2 g | 1.10 J/g·K | 1.30 J/K |
| 24 FETs + caps + connectors | ~1.0 g | ~0.7 J/g·K | 0.70 J/K |
| **Total** | | | **≈ 2.8 J/K** |

### 3.3 Burst duration (near-adiabatic, since τ = RC ≈ 3.5 × 2.8 ≈ 10 s ≫ burst)

$$T_{board}(t) = T_{amb} + P R_{th}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right) + T_{0,offset}e^{-t/\tau}$$

For short t:

$$t_{max} \approx \frac{C\,(T_{board,max} - T_{board,0})}{P}$$

The junction rides above the board by a fixed ΔT:

$$\Delta T_{j-b} = P_{FET}\,(Z_{jc} + R_{via})$$

The hottest FET is the continuously-on low side, carrying I²R_ds ≈ 6400 × 2.0 mΩ = 12.9 W, so ΔT_j-b ≈ 12.9 × 5.2 ≈ **67 K**.

With a T_j limit of 150 °C, the board must stay at or below 150 − 67 ≈ **83 °C**.

### 3.4 Burst duration results

| Starting board temp | Allowable ΔT | Time to limit at 124 W |
|---|---|---|
| 35 °C (cold) | 48 K | **1.08 s** |
| 55 °C (after hover) | 28 K | **0.63 s** |

### 3.5 Steady-state current limit

With prop-wash cooling, R_th,b-a ≈ 3 K/W:

$$P_{ss} = \frac{83 - 40}{3} \approx 14\ \text{W total} \Rightarrow I_{ss,per\,motor} \approx \sqrt{\frac{14/4}{4.4\text{m}\Omega}} \approx \mathbf{28\ A}$$
