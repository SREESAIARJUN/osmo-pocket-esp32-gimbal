# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #19): Electrothermal Model, Bi-Dir DShot600 Timer/DMA Plan, and Reference Netlists

## 0. Architecture Correction

The STM32H743 should **not** run the motors. ArduPilot has no ESC commutation firmware, and four-channel BEMF zero-cross detection plus 48 kHz PWM on one H7 alongside the flight stack is not a supported or sane configuration. The architecture used throughout this document is:

| Block | Device | Firmware |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100), 400/480 MHz | ArduPilot (ChibiOS) |
| ESC MCUs (×4) | AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071 (QFN32) | AM32 (bi-dir DShot, EDT) |
| Gate drivers (×4) | FD6288Q (3-phase, 250 V, bootstrap) | n/a |
| Power stage | 24× 40 V N-MOSFET, PDFN3.3×3.3 | n/a |

The H743 talks to the four ESC MCUs over bi-directional DShot600 (Section 3).

**Design assumptions.** All numbers below follow from these values. Replace them with your FET datasheet values and re-run the equations.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-step current path

In trapezoidal (6-step) commutation, each 60° sector has exactly two phases conducting. One leg is PWM'd with complementary synchronous rectification, and the opposing low-side FET is held on. The instantaneous resistance in the current path is therefore:

$$
R_{path} = D\,R_{HS} + (1-D)\,R_{LS,sync} + R_{LS,static} = 2\,R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

This holds independent of duty D, because with sync rectification one FET from the switching leg always conducts.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right]
$$

### 1.3 Per-motor and per-FET conduction loss

$$
P_{cond,motor} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

Each of the six FETs conducts for 1/3 of the electrical cycle, so on average:

$$
P_{cond,FET} = \frac{P_{cond,motor}}{6}
$$

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond/motor @ 80 A | P/FET (avg) | 4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.80 mΩ | 23.0 W | 3.84 W | 92 W |
| 100 °C | 2.70 mΩ | 34.6 W | 5.76 W | 138 W |
| 125 °C | 3.01 mΩ | 38.5 W | 6.42 W | 154 W |

### 1.4 Electrothermal fixed point (runaway check)

The junction temperature and the loss depend on each other:

$$
T_j = T_a + R_{th}\,I^2\,2R_{25}\left[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\right]
$$

Solving for T_j:

$$
T_j = \frac{T_a + k(1-25\alpha)}{1 - k\alpha}, \qquad k = 2R_{25}I^2R_{th}
$$

A stable solution exists only if **kα < 1**. With an effective per-motor R_th (motor share of the board to ambient) of about 24 K/W at 80 A:

$$
k = 2(1.8\times10^{-3})(6400)(24) = 553\ \text{K} \;\Rightarrow\; k\alpha = 3.7
$$

**There is no steady state at 80 A. It is thermally runaway-unstable.** 80 A can only be a time-limited burst (Section 4).

### 1.5 Non-FET conduction losses (often ignored, not negligible)

| Element | Resistance | Current | Loss |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shared current shunt | 0.25 mΩ | 4×80 = 320 A* | **25.6 W** |
| Battery pads + bus copper (2 oz) | ~0.3 mΩ | 320 A* | 30.7 W |
| Phase pads/traces, per motor | ~0.4 mΩ | 80 A | 2.6 W ×4 |

\*This is phase current. Battery current is I_phase·D, so at D≈0.8 the bus current is about 256 A. With that correction the shunt dissipates 16 W and the bus copper 20 W. Both are still large, and the battery current is itself absurd for a 20×20 board's XT-pad entry.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per motor, only one leg is hard-switching at any instant.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = \tfrac12(25.2)(80)(20\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = 0.968\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period)

$$
P_{dt} = V_F\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(200\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = 0.614\ \text{W}
$$

This is the second-largest switching term. Trimming dead time to 50 ns halves it, but requires verifying shoot-through margin on the scope.
