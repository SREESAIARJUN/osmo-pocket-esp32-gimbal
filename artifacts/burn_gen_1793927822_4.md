## 0. Architectural Ground Truth (read first)

1. **The H743 does not commutate motors.** ArduPilot on the H743 generates DShot600 frames and decodes eRPM telemetry. Each motor channel needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32 (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071), driving three half-bridges through a gate driver. The system is therefore 1× H743 plus 4× ESC MCUs plus 24 MOSFETs (or 12 dual-FET packages).
2. **80 A per channel on a nano (20×20 or 30×30) board is a burst rating, not a continuous one.** The thermal model in §3 shows a sustainable continuous current of roughly 25–30 A per channel with propwash, and about 3 s of all-four-motor 80 A burst from 40 °C. Marketing labels must reflect this.
3. At 6S (25.2 V), 80 A is about 2 kW per motor and about 8 kW system peak. Verify battery C-rating, XT60/XT90 connector, bulk capacitance, and TVS energy against that figure.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Topology
With 6-step / 120° trapezoidal commutation and complementary PWM, two legs carry current at any instant:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side conducts for D, and the low-side sync-rectifies for (1−D). Together the switch is on 100% of the period.
- **Static leg:** the low-side is on for 100% of the period.

$$P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 R_{DS(on)}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{rms}^2 R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

With ripple included, $I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \Delta I_{pp}^2/12$. This adds less than 1% for typical motor inductance (L ≈ 10–30 µH) at 48 kHz, so it is neglected here.

$$P_{cond} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.6\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{20.48\ W}$$

The copper path adds:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \cdot 0.5\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

### 2.2 Switching Losses (PWM leg only)

**Hard-switching overlap:**
$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot24\cdot80\cdot30\text{ns}\cdot48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.38\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (2 edges per cycle):
$$P_{dt} = V_F I \cdot 2t_{dead} f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{ns}\cdot48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = 40\text{nC}\cdot24\cdot48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.046\ W}$$

**Output capacitance** (2 FETs per switching node):
$$P_{oss} = 2\cdot\tfrac12 C_{oss(er)} V^2 f_{sw} = 1.2\text{nF}\cdot576\cdot48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.033\ W}$$

**Gate drive** (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g, not the FET):
$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\cdot40\text{nC}\cdot10\cdot48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.038\ W}$$

### 2.3 Per-Channel and System Totals @ 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction | 20.48 |
| Copper | 3.20 |
| Switching overlap | 1.38 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.12 |
| **Per channel** | **≈25.8 W** |
| **4 channels** | **≈103 W** |
| ESC efficiency at ~1.9 kW out | ≈98.6% |

**Design insight:** conduction loss is 80% of the total. At this current, cutting f_sw from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves only about 1 W per channel. Choosing a FET with 30% lower R_DS(on), or adding copper weight, saves about 7 W per channel.

### 2.4 Hottest Single FET
The static-leg low-side is on for 100% of the period:

$$P_{FET,max} = 80^2\cdot1.6\text{m}\Omega = 10.24\text{ W}$$

Commutation rotates this duty across all 6 FETs at electrical frequency, but each FET still sees about 10 W pulses lasting milliseconds. Use the transient Z_th, not the steady-state value, at electrical rates above 100 Hz:

$$\Delta T_{j\text{-}c} \approx 10.24 \cdot R_{th(jc)} \approx 10.24\cdot1.2 = 12\ \text{K}$$
