## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths

In 6-step commutation, exactly two phase legs carry current at any instant: one high-side path and one low-side path.

- During the PWM on-time, current flows through HS(a) → motor → LS(b).
- During the off-time with synchronous rectification, current freewheels through LS(a) → motor → LS(b).

The result is that two channel resistances are always in series with the phase current.

At full burst, the duty cycle D approaches 1, so phase current approximately equals the motor's DC current:

$$I_{bat} = D \cdot I_{ph} \quad\Rightarrow\quad I_{ph}\approx 80\text{ A at } D\to 1$$

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

| T_j | Multiplier | R_DS(on) |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 | 1.00 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 1.50 | 1.50 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 1.67 | 1.67 mΩ |

### 1.3 Per-motor and per-FET conduction loss

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

At 80 A and T_j = 100 °C:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.5\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

Each of the six FETs conducts for 1/3 of the electrical period. Averaged over a revolution, the loss per FET is:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}}{3} = 3.2\text{ W}$$

**Caveat on low-eRPM operation.** At low electrical frequency, for example during a stall or desync, the junction can track instantaneous loss. The instantaneous dissipation is I²R = 9.6 W in one FET. Use the transient thermal impedance Z_th(t) curve at t = 1/(3·f_elec). Do not use the averaged value for this case.

### 1.4 Electrothermal stability

Self-heating raises R_DS(on), which raises loss, which raises temperature. Combining the loss and resistance equations gives:

$$T_j = T_a + R_{th}\, I^2 R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Solving for T_j:

$$T_j = \frac{T_a + R_{th} I^2 R_{25}(1 - 25\alpha)}{1 - \alpha R_{th} I^2 R_{25}}$$

**Runaway condition:** α · R_th · I² · R₂₅ ≥ 1.

For the per-FET average loss (I²R₂₅/3 ≈ 2.13 W at 25 °C), the effective R_th to air must stay below 1/(0.0067 × 2.13) ≈ **70 K/W**. With six FETs per motor sharing a common copper spreader, this is achievable, but it is the hard ceiling.

### 1.5 PCB copper loss (frequently ignored, and significant)

A nano-format phase path from FET to motor pad, with roughly 10 mm of 2 oz copper, vias, and pad, typically measures **0.3–0.6 mΩ**.

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{trace} \approx 6400 \times 0.45\text{ m}\Omega \approx 2.9\text{ W per motor}$$

The battery-side bus is shared by all four motors (4 × 80 A = 320 A total). Every 0.1 mΩ in the main bus adds about 10 W.

**Design requirements:**
- Use ≥ 6 layers with 2 oz inner planes.
- Stitch bus and phase planes with ≥ 30 vias per phase.
- Solder the battery leads directly to plane pads.
