# STM32H743 Nano AIO with 4-in-1 80 A ESC: Electrothermal and Timing Design Package (Job #13)

## 0. Design Review Finding

**Key result:** An 80 A per-phase burst is thermally feasible only for bursts of a few hundred milliseconds with one 3.3×3.3 mm FET per switch position. It needs paralleled or 5×6 mm FETs for anything longer. The math in §3 shows that the junction-to-board rise, not board heating, is the limiting factor.

**Architecture assumption:** The H743 does not commutate the motors. Each of the four phases-groups is run by a dedicated ESC MCU (e.g., AT32F421 running AM32, or BLHeli_32-class firmware), which generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to the ESC MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. The models below are split along that boundary.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation topology
In 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM, the phase current always flows through two FETs in series:

- the PWM leg: the high-side FET for fraction D, its low-side complement for (1−D);
- the fixed low-side FET of the sinking phase.

So, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 \left[ R_{ds}(T_j)\cdot D + R_{ds}(T_j)(1-D) \right] + I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence
$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1+\alpha (T_j - 25)\right]$$

### 1.3 Ripple correction
Phase inductance L_ph (about 15 µH for a typical 5–7" motor) produces this peak-to-peak current ripple:

$$\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - E_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}, \qquad I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

With E_bemf ≈ D·V_bus at D = 0.5, ΔI ≈ 8.75 A. The correction is ΔI²/12 ≈ 6.4 A², against 6400 A², i.e. 0.1%. It is negligible at burst and is dropped below.

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_ds | P per FET | P_cond per channel | All 4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.50 mΩ | 9.6 W | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| 100 °C | 2.06 mΩ | 13.2 W | 26.4 W | 105.6 W |
| 125 °C | 2.25 mΩ | 14.4 W | 28.8 W | 115.2 W |

Conduction loss has positive feedback: P rises with T_j, and T_j rises with P. This is closed in §3.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM half-bridge switches; the fixed low-side FET does not. Per channel, the components are as follows.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss (hard-switched edge)
The gate current through the plateau is:

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{pl}}{R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}+R_{drv,on}}, \qquad I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}+R_{drv,off}}$$

Using V_pl = 3.5 V, R_total,on = 6 Ω, and R_total,off = 3 Ω (a diode-bypassed turn-off resistor):

- I_g,on ≈ 1.08 A, so t_r ≈ Q_sw / I_g,on = 10 nC / 1.08 A ≈ 9.3 ns. With layout inductance, use **t_r ≈ 15 ns**.
- I_g,off ≈ 1.17 A, so t_f ≈ 8.6 ns. Use **t_f ≈ 12 ns**.

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(27\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = \mathbf{1.31\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction
$$P_{dt} = V_f\,I_{ph}\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\,\text{ns} \cdot 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

Cutting dead time to 50 ns halves this. Check shoot-through margin against t_f plus driver propagation skew first.

### 2.3 Reverse recovery
Q_rr scales roughly with I_F and di/dt. Scaling the datasheet value from 20 A to 80 A by about 2× gives ≈ 80 nC:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 80\,\text{nC}\cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.10\ W}$$
