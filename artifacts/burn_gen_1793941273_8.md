## 0. Architecture Assumptions and an Early Warning

| Item | Baseline |
|---|---|
| Form factor | 20×20 mm (M2 mounting holes), 8-layer, 2 oz inner/outer copper |
| Battery | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V max. Bus design voltage 30 V. FETs rated 40 V |
| FC MCU | STM32H743VIH6 (TFBGA100), 400 MHz, ArduPilot |
| ESC MCUs | 4 × AT32F421K8U7 (QFN32) running AM32, one per motor |
| Gate driver | 4 × FD6288Q (3-phase, 250 V, ~1.5 A source / 1.8 A sink) **[A]** |
| Power stage | 24 × N-FET in 3.3×3.3 mm (PDFN/TSDSON-8) |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM) |
| f_sw | 48 kHz |
| Rating | 80 A phase current per channel, burst |

> **Key finding up front:** on a 20×20 mm board, 80 A per channel is thermally a **≈1–4 second burst** rating, not a continuous one. The defensible continuous rating is **≈25–30 A per channel**. The math in Sections 3–4 shows why. Write marketing and firmware current limits against this result.

Note that the H743 does not switch FETs directly. It commands four ESC MCUs over BDShot600, and each ESC MCU runs its own commutation loop. Placing a 400 MHz H7 in a 20×20 stack with 100 W of power stage also creates an IMU thermal problem; Section 4.5 covers it.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous commutation

In each 60° sector, exactly two phases carry current I_ph:

- **PWM leg:** current alternates between the high-side (HS) FET (fraction D of the time) and the low-side (LS) FET acting as a synchronous rectifier (fraction 1−D).
- **Return leg:** current flows through one LS FET for 100% of the sector.

Ignoring dead time, there are always **exactly two R_DS(on) in series** in the current path:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS}(T_j)=R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

Each FET conducts for 120° of 360° electrical, so on average across one electrical revolution:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 2.2 Phase current vs. battery current

$$I_{bat} = D\cdot I_{ph}$$

"80 A burst" must be defined as **phase** current. That is the worst case for FET loss, typically reached at high throttle when D → 1 and I_bat ≈ I_ph.

### 2.3 Self-consistent junction temperature

Junction temperature and R_DS(on) depend on each other:

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}(T_j)\,R_{\theta JB}$$

Solve by fixed-point iteration (it converges in 3–4 steps). With T_pcb = 90 °C and R_θJB = 5 K/W (Section 4), the result is T_j ≈ 113 °C, so R_DS ≈ 1.2 × 1.53 = **1.83 mΩ**.

### 2.4 Results at 80 A

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.83\text{ mΩ} = \mathbf{23.4\ W}$$

$$P_{cond,FET} = \tfrac{1}{3}\times 6400 \times 1.83\text{ mΩ} = \mathbf{3.9\ W}$$

### 2.5 Copper interconnect loss

This term is often forgotten. Each channel has a phase pad, a bus path, and a ground return.

Use an effective path resistance of R_cu ≈ 0.25 mΩ. This assumes 2 oz copper, parallel planes, and via arrays at ~0.1 mΩ per 20-via field.

$$P_{cu,ch} = I_{ph}^2 R_{cu} = 6400\times 0.25\text{ mΩ} = \mathbf{1.6\ W}$$

### 2.6 Shunt loss (if fitted)

Use a 0.25 mΩ shared bus shunt carrying I_bat,total.

- **Channel-only burst:** 80² × 0.25 mΩ = 1.6 W.
- **All four channels at 80 A:** 320² × 0.25 mΩ = **25.6 W**.
