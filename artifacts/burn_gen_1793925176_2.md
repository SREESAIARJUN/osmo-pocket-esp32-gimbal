## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-step trapezoidal with synchronous rectification

In each 60° commutation sector, one leg's high side is PWM'd at duty D. That same leg's low side conducts during (1−D) (sync rectification), and the opposite phase's low side is held fully on.

For one leg over one electrical cycle:
- **+120° (sourcing):** the high side conducts for D and the low side for (1−D). Together they carry current 100% of the time.
- **−120° (sinking):** the low side is fully on.
- **Remaining 120°:** the leg is floating (back-EMF sense).

The leg loss is therefore:

$$
P_{leg} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\left[\tfrac13 (D + 1 - D) + \tfrac13\right] = \tfrac23 I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

The loss is **independent of duty** thanks to sync rectification. For one motor (3 legs), which always has exactly 2 FETs in the current path:

$$
\boxed{P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{DS,25}\, k_T(T_j)}
$$

### 1.2 Ripple and temperature corrections

The RMS current includes the ripple term:

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I^2\left(1 + \frac{(\Delta I/I)^2}{12}\right)
$$

With motor inductance of about 20 µH and 48 kHz switching, ΔI is roughly 13 A peak-to-peak. The correction is only **+0.2%**, so it is neglected.

The temperature factor is:

$$
k_T(T_j) \approx 1 + \alpha (T_j - 25),\quad \alpha \approx 0.0059\ /\text{K} \Rightarrow k_T(110^\circ C) \approx 1.5
$$
