# Job #42: STM32H743 Nano FC with 80 A-Class 4-in-1 ESC
## Architecture, Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, DShot Allocation, and Reference Netlist

This analysis rests on several assumptions. The brief names no MOSFET, gate driver, battery voltage or board size, so every number below is parametric. The example values are typical of 30–40 V DFN3.3×3.3 FETs used in this class of ESC. Replace them with your datasheet values before signing off.

## 0. Architecture Correction (Read First)

An STM32H743 does not commutate the ESC directly. ArduPilot running on the H743 has no motor-commutation firmware. The viable architecture is:

| Block | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIH6 (or VIT6) | ArduPilot. Emits DShot600 and receives bidirectional eRPM telemetry. |
| ESC MCUs (×4) | STM32G071GBU6 or AT32F421K8U7 | AM32 / BLHeli_32. Handle 48 kHz PWM, commutation, bidir DShot decoding. |
| Gate drivers (×4) | FD6288Q (3-phase, 20 V max VCC) | High-side bootstrap and low-side drive |
| Power stage (×4) | 6 × N-MOSFET per motor, 24 total | 3-phase bridge |

This can be built as a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 stack, or as an AIO board. The rest of this document assumes that split.

**"80 A on a nano board" is a burst rating, not a continuous one.** Section 3 shows quantitatively that the board survives 80 A per motor for roughly 2 seconds, not indefinitely.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (Per Motor)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous (complementary) PWM. At every instant, current flows through exactly two FET channels:
- the active high-side switch or its complementary low-side, and
- the return-leg low-side.

Because the complementary FET conducts during the off-time, the conduction path never drops below two channels.

$$P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2 R_{ds(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Phase-current ripple adds a correction term:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}D(1-D)}{L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

**Ripple check.** With L_ph = 10 µH (a typical small motor):
- ΔI = 25.2 × 0.25 / (10 µH × 48 kHz) = 13.1 A
- ΔI²/12 = 14.3 A², which is negligible against 6400 A².

**Result at 80 A:**
- At T_j = 110 °C: P_cond = 6414 × 2 × 1.4 mΩ ≈ **17.96 W**
- At 25 °C: P_cond = 12.8 W
