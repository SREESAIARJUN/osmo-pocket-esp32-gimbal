# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model and Allocation

This is an engineering model, not a release-ready design. Every device parameter below is a placeholder to replace with datasheet values for the parts you actually select. The numbers matter less than the structure of the calculations.

The main result is that **80 A per motor cannot be sustained on a nano-format board.** It is achievable only as a burst of roughly 1–3 s, and only with a hard current limit in the ESC firmware. Section 2 shows the derivation.

---

## 0. Architecture Correction

The STM32H743 should be the **flight controller MCU running ArduPilot only**. It should not also commutate the motors. The reasons:

1. **Not enough complementary PWM.** Four 3-phase bridges need 12 complementary pairs. The H743 has only TIM1 and TIM8 (3 pairs each, 6 total) plus HRTIM. Covering all 12 would consume the timers ArduPilot needs.
2. **ArduPilot cannot do it.** ArduPilot is not a motor commutation firmware. It cannot run 4× sensorless 6-step or FOC at 48 kHz alongside its scheduler.
3. **Bi-directional DShot assumes a separate ESC MCU.** BDShot only makes sense if a separate MCU is replying with eRPM.

**Reference architecture:**

```
[STM32H743 FC / ArduPilot] --4x BDShot600--> [4x AT32F421 / STM32G071 running AM32]
                                                   |
                                         4x 3-phase gate driver + 24x N-MOSFET
```

**Phase current vs battery current.** The 80 A figure is phase current. Battery current is I_bat ≈ D·I_ph, so the battery draws much less at partial throttle. The bridge FETs still carry the full phase current.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

In 6-step with synchronous rectification, two FETs are in the current path at every instant: one in the PWM leg (high side or low side, depending on D) and the low side of the return leg.

**Bridge conduction loss:**

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha_T (T_j-25)\right]$$

**At 80 A, T_j = 125 °C:**
- R_ds(on) = 1.5 mΩ × 1.5 = 2.25 mΩ
- P_cond = 2 × 6400 × 0.00225 = **28.8 W per motor**

**Per FET.** Each phase conducts 2/3 of the electrical period, so the loss is shared by 6 FETs. The average is ≈ **4.8 W/FET**. The peak instantaneous loss is 14.4 W while the FET carries the full current.

**Copper loss.** Phase pads and the battery bus add to this. As an example, a 2 oz trace 5 mm wide and 10 mm long, in parallel over 4 layers:
- R ≈ (1.72e-8 × 0.01)/(70e-6 × 5e-3) / 4 ≈ 0.12 mΩ
- P = 2 × 6400 × 0.12e-3 ≈ **1.5 W per motor**

**Thermal runaway check.** Because R_ds(on) rises with temperature, loss is a positive feedback loop:

$$\frac{dP}{dT_j} = 2I^2R_{25}\alpha_T \cdot R_{th(j-a)} < 1$$

This condition must hold or the FET runs away thermally. With α_T ≈ 0.005/K:
- 2 × 6400 × 1.5e-3 × 0.005 = 0.096 W/K
- Stability therefore requires R_th(j-a) < ~10 K/W per FET pair. A nano board satisfies this only transiently, which is one more reason this is a burst rating.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the active PWM leg switches. Its high side switches hard; its low side switches at near-zero voltage but conducts through its body diode during dead time.

**Transition time:**

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g} \approx \frac{5\,\text{nC}}{0.33\,\text{A}} \approx 15\,\text{ns per edge}$$

**Loss components (per motor):**

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap (hard switching, high side) | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = 0.5·25.2·80·30e-9·48e3 | 1.45 W |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_f·I·2·t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·200e-9·48e3 | 0.61 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 40e-9·25.2·48e3 | 0.05 W |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss·V·f_sw (approx.) | 0.04 W |
| Gate drive (driver dissipation) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw | 0.03 W |
| **Total P_sw** | | **≈ 2.2 W per motor** |

- **Dead time.** At this frequency it is the second-largest term. Cutting dead time to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per motor.
