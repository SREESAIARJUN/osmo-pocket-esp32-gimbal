# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal and DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Constraints

I have no prior context for "Job #3", so this document is self-contained. All component values are **representative assumptions**. Replace them with datasheet values for your chosen parts before you sign off.

**System partitioning.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller MCU. A 4-in-1 ESC does not commutate motors from the H743. Each motor gets a dedicated ESC MCU, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to those four MCUs and receives eRPM telemetry back. This document models both sides.

**Feasibility warning on "80 A burst" in a nano form factor (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm):**
- 80 A per motor is only plausible as a **sub-second to few-second burst**.
- The FET package limit (bond wire and clip), PCB copper current density and bulk capacitor ripple will all bind before the silicon does.
- The model below quantifies this. Do not market it as a continuous rating.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology basis
In 6-step commutation, two phase legs conduct at any instant:
- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for D·T. The complementary low-side FET (synchronous rectification) carries the freewheel current for (1−D)·T.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET is held on for the full period.

### 1.2 Per-FET conduction loss

$$P_{cond,HS} = I^2 \cdot R_{DS}(T_j)\cdot D \qquad P_{cond,LS,sync} = I^2 \cdot R_{DS}(T_j)\cdot(1-D)$$

$$P_{cond,LS,return} = I^2 \cdot R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Total per motor, independent of D under synchronous rectification:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Temperature dependence:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right]$$

### 1.3 Worked numbers at 80 A
I² = 6400 A².

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond per FET (full conduction) | P_cond per motor |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 6.40 W | 12.8 W |
| 100 °C | 1.34 mΩ | 8.56 W | 17.1 W |
| 125 °C | 1.45 mΩ | 9.28 W | 18.6 W |

### 1.4 Electrothermal coupling (thermal runaway check)
Let θ be the junction-to-reference thermal resistance:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + \theta\,P(T_j) \;\Rightarrow\; T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + \theta\,(P_0 - \alpha\,25\,P_0) }{1 - \theta\,\alpha\,P_0}$$

Here P₀ = I²R₂₅ for a single FET.

The system is stable only if:

$$\theta\,\alpha\,P_0 < 1 \;\Rightarrow\; \theta < \frac{1}{0.0045 \times 6.4} = 34.7\ \text{K/W}$$

A soldered clip package on 2 oz copper (θ_ja ≈ 25–40 K/W with no airflow) is **marginal** for continuous 80 A. This confirms 80 A must be burst-only.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Losses are computed per motor.

### 2.1 Gate-current-limited transition times

$$t_r \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g} = \frac{12\,\text{nC}}{0.6\,\text{A}} = 20\,\text{ns}$$

### 2.2 V-I overlap loss (hard-switched high-side)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

$$= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery
The low-side body diode conducts during dead time and then recovers:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12\,Q_{oss}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f\,I\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{n}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

This is significant at 48 kHz. Reducing dead time to 50 ns halves it, but requires verified shoot-through margin.

### 2.6 Gate drive
This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET:

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g\,V_{GS}\,f_{sw} = 2\times60\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k} = 0.058\ \text{W}$$

### 2.7 Per-motor and board totals at 80 A, T_j = 100 °C

| Loss term | Per motor |
|---|---|
| Conduction (2 legs) | 17.1 W |
| V-I switching | 1.94 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.10 W |
| Gate drive | 0.06 W |
| **Total** | **≈19.8 W** |
| **Four motors simultaneously** | **≈79 W** |

At 48 kHz, switching is about 12 % of the total. Doubling the gate drive current or halving f_sw to 24 kHz recovers about 1 W per motor.

### 2.8 Hottest single FET
The return-leg low side carries 8.56 W of conduction loss. The PWM high side at D → 1 carries 8.56 W conduction plus 1.94 W switching, which is about **10.5 W**.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network
The model is a Cauer/Foster lumped network:

```
Pj ─[Rθjc 1.0 K/W]─ Tc ─[Rθcb 1.5 K/W (solder+vias)]─ Tboard ─[Rθba]─ Tamb
         Cj≈0.05 J/K                         Cboard
```

### 3.2 Board thermal mass (25.5 mm board, 8-layer, 2 oz)

| Contributor | Thermal capacitance |
|---|---|
| Copper: ~3.5 g × 385 J/kg·K | 1.35 J/K |
| FR4: ~3 g × 1100 J/kg·K | 3.3 J/K |
| 24 FETs, solder, capacitors | ≈1.0 J/K |
| **C_board** | **≈5.6 J/K** |

Board-to-air resistance R_θba:
- In propwash: ≈2.5 K/W (h ≈ 60 W/m²K over both faces plus FET tops).
- Still air: ≈8 K/W.
