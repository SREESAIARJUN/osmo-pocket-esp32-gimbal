## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step synchronous commutation

In any 60° commutation sector, two phases conduct and one floats.

- **PWM leg:** the high-side (HS) conducts for D·T_sw. The complementary low-side (LS) conducts for (1−D)·T_sw minus dead time.
- **Return leg:** the LS stays on for the whole sector.

So at every instant, exactly two channels are in series with the motor current, whatever the duty cycle:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{Cu} + I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{shunt}
$$

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\left[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0059\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

$$
R_{DS(on)}(110^\circ C) = 1.0 \times (1 + 0.0059 \times 85) \approx 1.50\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

This creates positive thermal feedback. The loss converges only if:

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\cdot R_{th} < 1 \;\Rightarrow\; 2I^2R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_{th,eff} < 1
$$

At 80 A: 2 × 6400 × 1e-3 × 0.0059 × R_th < 1, so R_th,eff < 13.2 K/W per motor-pair path. This is satisfied, so there is no thermal runaway from R_DS(on) alone. The board-level limit in §3 is what binds.

### 1.3 Numbers per motor

| Term | Expression | Value |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 80² × 2 × 1.5 mΩ | 19.20 W |
| PCB copper (phase + bus, ~0.15 mΩ effective) | 80² × 0.15 mΩ | 0.96 W |
| Current-sense shunt (0.25 mΩ, in bus return) | (D·I)² × R, D = 0.8 | 1.02 W |
| **Subtotal** | | **≈ 21.2 W** |

**Per-FET averaged loss** (each of the 6 FETs carries 1/3 of the conduction burden over a full electrical cycle): 19.2 / 6 ≈ **3.2 W average**. Peak instantaneous dissipation in a conducting FET is 80² × 1.5 mΩ = **9.6 W**.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches; the return leg commutates at electrical frequency, which is negligible. In synchronous mode, one HS transition pair is hard-switched per PWM period. The LS transitions are soft (ZVS via the body diode).

### 2.1 Transition times

With an FD6288Q-class driver (≈ +0.6 A source / −1.2 A sink at V_GS = 10–12 V) and a 4.7 Ω gate resistor, using the Miller plateau V_pl ≈ 3.2 V:

$$
t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = \frac{15\text{ nC}}{(12-3.2)/(4.7+R_{drv})} \approx 15\text{–}20\ \text{ns},\qquad t_f \approx 12\text{–}18\ \text{ns}
$$

Design value: **t_r + t_f = 40 ns**, which includes the current-rise interval.

### 2.2 Loss components

**1. Hard-switching V·I overlap:**

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 1.94\ \text{W}
$$

**2. Body-diode dead-time conduction** (2 dead times per period):

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD} I_{ph} \cdot 2t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}
$$

**3. Reverse recovery** (LS body diode recovered by the HS turn-on):

$$
P_{rr} = V_{bus} Q_{rr} f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 50\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}
$$

**4. Output capacitance:**

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2}C_{oss,tr}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 2\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = 0.03\ \text{W}
$$
