**Document scope:** This package covers power-stage loss modelling at 80 A burst and 48 kHz PWM, the thermal model, ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 timer and DMA allocation, and reference netlists.

**Status:** These are engineering estimates. Every component parameter below is an assumption, marked **[A]**. Replace each one with datasheet values for the parts you actually select, then validate on a thermal-camera bench before release.

## 0. Architectural Clarifications

Three points must be settled first, because they change the design.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In a 4-in-1 AIO, each ESC channel normally runs on its own small MCU, for example an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 runs ArduPilot and speaks DShot600 to those MCUs. The 48 kHz PWM is therefore an ESC-firmware setting, not an H743 timer. The alternative is a single MCU driving 12 half-bridges plus ArduPilot, which is not a supported ArduPilot architecture.
2. **"80 A burst" is interpreted as per channel, at most 10 s, on one or two motors at once.** Four channels at 80 A each is 320 A on a nano footprint. As §3 shows, that is thermally impossible without a heatsink, and it is also not practical through the battery pad and XT60.
3. **Form factor.** For an 80 A-class rating, a 30.5×30.5 mm board is assumed. A 20×20 mm "nano" cannot credibly carry 80 A burst; §3.4 quantifies this.

## 2. Loss Model per ESC Channel

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

With 6-step trapezoidal commutation and synchronous (complementary) PWM, the motor current flows through exactly two MOSFETs at any instant. In the PWM-on interval these are the high side of phase A and the low side of phase B. In the PWM-off interval they are the synchronous low side of A and the low side of B. Therefore:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha(T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0067/\text{K}$$

At T_j = 100 °C:

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.35\,\text{m}\Omega = 6400 \times 2.7\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{17.3\ W}$$

Copper paths add to this: phase traces, bus planes, and solder joints, assumed at 0.3 mΩ per channel **[A]**:

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{1.9\ W}$$

### 2.2 Switching Loss at 48 kHz

**Hard-switching V–I overlap.** One device hard-switches per PWM period:

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.02\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction**, two dead-time intervals per period:

$$P_{dt} = V_f \, I \, (2 t_{dead}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**Gate drive**, dissipated in the driver and R_g rather than the FET die. Six FETs per channel is a conservative upper bound:

$$P_g = 6\,Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 6 \times 50\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.14\ W}$$

### 2.3 Loss Summary per Channel at 80 A, 25.2 V, 48 kHz

| Mechanism | Loss (W) | Share |
|---|---|---|
| MOSFET conduction (100 °C) | 17.30 | 79.6 % |
| Copper / interconnect | 1.90 | 8.7 % |
| V–I switching | 1.94 | 8.9 % |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 | 2.8 % |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.08 | 0.4 % |
| **Board total per channel** | **≈ 21.8 W** | |
| Gate driver (separate) | 0.14 | |

**Key insight:** At 80 A the design is conduction-dominated. Raising f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz adds only about 1.3 W, while moving R_ds(on) from 0.9 to 0.6 mΩ saves about 5.8 W. Put budget into R_ds(on) and copper, not slower switching.

**Efficiency check.** Assuming ~80 % duty, input power is about 25.2 × 80 × 0.8 ≈ 1.6 kW, so η ≈ 98.6 % per channel.

### 2.4 Per-Device Distribution

This is needed for the junction calculation in §3.3. Each phase leg carries current for two-thirds of the electrical cycle. Averaged over a commutation cycle:

- Conduction loss per FET ≈ 17.3 / 6 ≈ **2.9 W**.
- The high-side FET of the active PWM leg additionally takes the switching loss, about 2.5 W, during its third of the cycle, which averages to about 0.85 W.

The worst-case FET average is therefore about **3.75 W**.
