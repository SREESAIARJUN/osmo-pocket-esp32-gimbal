# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #15): 80 A Burst Power Stage, 48 kHz Switching, and ArduPilot Bi-Directional DShot600 Architecture

## 0. Architectural Ground Truth (Read First)

Two constraints shape everything that follows.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each ESC channel runs its own MCU (AT32F421, STM32G071, or EFM8 running AM32 or BLHeli_32/S). The H743 runs ArduPilot and only *sends* DShot600 and *receives* bidirectional eRPM telemetry. The 48 kHz PWM lives on the ESC MCUs. This document models both sides.
2. **80 A burst on a "nano" footprint is thermally marginal to non-viable.** The math below shows this quantitatively. The deliverable gives you the model and tells you where the design breaks. It does not tell you what you'd like to hear.

**Baseline assumptions.** Change these and re-run the equations.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation topology

The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification and complementary PWM, which is the AM32/BLHeli_32 default. Within each 60° sector, one phase is PWM'd and one phase is low-side ON:

- **PWM'd leg:** the high side conducts for fraction D and the low side (sync rect) for 1−D. Together they carry I for the full period.
- **Return leg:** the low side carries I for 100% of the period.

$$P_{cond,ESC} = I^2 R_{HS} D + I^2 R_{LS}(1-D) + I^2 R_{LS} \approx 2\,I^2 R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

This holds independent of D when the FETs are matched.

### 1.2 Temperature-coupled R_ds(on): self-consistent solution

$$R(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$
$$T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}\,R_{\theta,j\text{-}pcb}$$

Each FET averages ≈ 1/3 of the ESC conduction loss, because 2 of 6 FETs are effectively conducting at any time. Using R_θ,j-pcb ≈ 6 K/W (R_θjc ≈ 1.5 K/W plus pad/via spreading ≈ 4.5 K/W) gives:

$$P_{FET} = \tfrac{2}{3} I^2 R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Solving for T_j:

$$T_j = \frac{T_{pcb} + k\,(1-25\alpha)}{1 - k\alpha},\quad k = \tfrac{2}{3}I^2 R_{25} R_{\theta}$$

At I = 80 A: k = (2/3)(6400)(0.0015)(6) = 38.4 K, and kα = 0.211. Taking T_pcb = 90 °C (mid-burst, see §4):

$$T_j = \frac{90 + 38.4(0.8625)}{0.789} = 156\ °C$$

Then R(156 °C) = 1.5 mΩ × 1.72 = 2.58 mΩ, and:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2(6400)(0.00258) = \mathbf{33.0\ W}$$

This is ≈ 11 W per conducting FET-equivalent, about **132 W of conduction alone across 4 ESCs.** The value converges in a few iterations. **Loop gain kα → 1 is thermal runaway**, which happens at roughly I ≈ 174 A with these parts. At 80 A you are stable but hot.

### 1.3 Copper / shunt path

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{path}$$

With R_path ≈ 0.25 mΩ (pads, planes, motor pads, plus a 0.25 mΩ shared shunt at 4× current), the result is ≈ 1.6 W per ESC plus shunt loss of (4·80)²·0.25 mΩ ≈ 25.6 W at full 4-motor burst.

→ **Recommendation:** use a lower-value shunt (0.1 mΩ) with a high-gain amp (INA240A4, G = 200) or Hall-free sensing per channel. A 0.25 mΩ shunt at 320 A is a heater.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches hard. With sync rect, the low side commutates softly through the body diode during dead time.

| Term | Equation | Value per ESC |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap (HS hard switching) | ½ V I (t_r+t_f) f | ½(25.2)(80)(20 ns)(48k) = **0.97 W** |
| Coss charge/discharge | ½ C_oss V² f | ½(1 nF)(635)(48k) = **0.015 W** |
| Body-diode dead-time conduction | V_f I (2 t_dt) f | 0.8(80)(100 ns)(48k) = **0.31 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr V f | 20 nC(25.2)(48k) = **0.024 W** |
| Gate drive (in driver, not FET) | 2 Q_g V_gs f | 2(40 nC)(10)(48k) = **0.038 W** |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 1.36 W** |

**Insights:**
- At 48 kHz, switching is ~4% of total loss. **Conduction dominates by 25×.** Dropping to 24 kHz saves only ~0.65 W per ESC. Raising frequency is not your thermal problem. R_ds(on) and current are.
- Dead time matters more than Q_rr. AM32 dead-time tuning (shorter, with verified shoot-through margin) is worth ~0.15 W per ESC.
- Layout loop inductance (target < 1 nH, HS-LS-ceramic loop) controls ringing. It is not captured in the ½VIt term but drives EMI and V_ds overshoot. On 6S, keep overshoot under 35 V for 40 V FETs.

**ESC timer resolution at 48 kHz** (AT32F421 @ 120 MHz / STM32G071 @ 64 MHz):
- 120 MHz / 48 kHz = 2500 counts, about 11.3 bits.
- 64 MHz / 48 kHz = 1333 counts, about 10.4 bits.
