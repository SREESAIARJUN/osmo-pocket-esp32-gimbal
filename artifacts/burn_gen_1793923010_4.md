## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

Several premises in the brief need correcting before any calculation is valid.

| Item | Brief implies | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| STM32H743 role | Runs the ESC | The H743 is the **flight controller** running ArduPilot. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32). The H743 drives four DShot lines. |
| 48 kHz PWM | Set by ArduPilot | 48 kHz is the **ESC firmware** PWM frequency (an AM32 setting). ArduPilot sets only the DShot protocol and rate. |
| "80 A burst" | Sustained capability | At 20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm "nano" size, 80 A is a **≤2–3 s adiabatic burst**, as section 2 shows. Sustained 80 A is not physically achievable in this form factor without forced cooling. |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

Using a typical trench-MOSFET normalized curve, ≈1.6× at 125 °C:

$$
R_{DS}(125^\circ C) \approx 1.6\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step with synchronous PWM

At any instant, two legs are active:
- **PWM leg:** current flows in the HS FET during D·T, and in the LS FET (sync-rectifying) during (1−D)·T.
- **Return leg:** the LS FET is on continuously.

So **exactly two FETs carry the phase current I at all times**, regardless of duty cycle:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) + I^2 R_{Cu}
$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

$$
P_{FET,cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.6\,\text{m}\Omega = 20.5\ \text{W per channel}
$$

**PCB copper:** assume a 15 mm path, 4 layers of 2 oz (70 µm) copper, 6 mm wide.

$$
R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{A} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8} \times 0.015}{4 \times 70\,\mu\text{m} \times 6\,\text{mm}} \approx 0.15\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

Increase ρ by 1.3× at 100 °C, giving ≈0.2 mΩ:

$$
P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.2\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 1.3\ \text{W}
$$

**Per-FET average.** Each of the 6 FETs carries current about 1/3 of the electrical period:

$$
P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{20.5}{6} \approx 3.4\ \text{W}
$$

Peak instantaneous loss during its conduction window is 6.8–10 W.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg switches at a time per channel, so the per-channel switching loss equals the loss of one half-bridge.

### 2.1 Voltage–current overlap

Assume ≈2 A gate drive with a plateau near 3 V, giving t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 15 ns:

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = \tfrac12 (25.2)(80)(30\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = 1.45\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.036\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

Assume t_dt = 50 ns, with two dead-time events per cycle:

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.31\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs:

$$
P_{gate} = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 80\,\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.077\ \text{W}
$$
