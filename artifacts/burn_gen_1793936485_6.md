## 0. Scope, Assumptions & Critical Flags

### 0.1 Critical Feasibility Flags

**Flag 1: An 80 A burst rating is not credible on a "nano" (20×20 / 25.5×25.5 mm) footprint.** The math below shows each channel dissipates about **20–23 W** at 80 A. With all four channels at 80 A, that is about **90 W** into a board weighing about 5 g. Commercial nano AIOs carrying "80A" labels rely on very short (≈1 s) marketing bursts. This model produces a defensible burst-time limit instead of a label. See §4.5.

**Flag 2: Architecture.** The H743 runs ArduPilot only. It does not commutate motors. Each ESC channel needs its own MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 talks to those MCUs over bidirectional DShot600.

**Flag 3: Placeholder parameters.** All MOSFET and driver parameters are representative placeholders. Replace them with your selected part's datasheet values before sign-off. The equations are exact; the numbers are only as good as the inputs.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Paths in Six-Step Commutation

In each 60° sector, two phases conduct and one floats:

- **Phase A, PWM'd:** the high-side (HS) conducts for D·T. The synchronous low-side (LS) conducts for (1−D)·T.
- **Phase B, static:** the LS conducts for the whole sector.

So the motor current always flows through exactly two R_DS(on) in series:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot R_{DS,hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{rms}^2 \cdot R_{DS,hot} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS,hot}
$$

### 1.2 Ripple Correction

The inductor ripple current is:

$$
\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}
$$

With L_ph = 15 µH (typical 2207-class motor): ΔI = 25.2·0.09 / (15e-6·48e3) ≈ **3.15 A p-p**.

The RMS current including ripple is:

$$
I_{rms} = \sqrt{I^2 + \tfrac{\Delta I^2}{12}} = \sqrt{6400 + 0.83} \approx 80.005\ \text{A}
$$

The ripple effect is negligible.

### 1.3 Conduction Loss at Burst

$$
R_{DS,hot} = 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.5 = 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega
$$
$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ channel}
$$

**Copper term.** Bus and phase traces of about 0.25 mΩ effective (2 oz outer + 2 oz inner, paralleled) add:

$$
P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.25\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{1.6\ W\ per\ channel}
$$

**Per-FET distribution, averaged over the electrical cycle:**

- Each phase leg's HS conducts 1/3 of the cycle at duty D.
- The LS conducts 1/3 static plus 1/3·(1−D) synchronous.
- Average per FET ≈ 19.2/6 = **3.2 W**.
- Instantaneously, the static LS carries the full 9.6 W during its sector. This matters for the junction thermal ripple at low eRPM (see §4.4).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Hard switching occurs only on the PWM'd leg: one HS turn-on and turn-off per PWM period, with the LS body diode commutating.

### 2.1 V–I Overlap Loss

The rise and fall times are set by the Miller plateau charge:

$$
t_r = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{G,src}} = \frac{19.5\,\text{nC}}{1.0\,\text{A}} = 19.5\,\text{ns}
$$
$$
t_f = \frac{19.5\,\text{nC}}{1.5\,\text{A}} = 13.0\,\text{ns}
$$

The overlap loss is:

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 32.5\text{e-9} \times 48\text{e3} = \mathbf{1.57\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Reverse Recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{e-9}\times25.2\times48\text{e3} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

Recovery current also adds to the HS turn-on overlap. Add about 10% to P_sw if Q_rr is not specified at your dI/dt.

### 2.3 Output Capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = 2\times\tfrac12 C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1.5\text{e-9}\times635\times48\text{e3} = \mathbf{0.046\ W}
$$

### 2.4 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$
P_{dt} = 2\,V_{SD}\,I\,t_{dt}\,f_{sw} = 2\times0.8\times80\times300\text{e-9}\times48\text{e3} = \mathbf{1.84\ W}
$$

**This is the largest switching-related term and is easily reduced.** At t_dt = 150 ns it drops to 0.92 W. Size the dead time to the measured gate fall time plus margin, not to a default.

### 2.5 Gate Drive

$$
P_{gate} = 2\,Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2\times60\text{e-9}\times12\times48\text{e3} = 0.069\ \text{W}
$$

This is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FETs.
