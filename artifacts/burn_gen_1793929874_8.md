**Architectural correction:** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot. It does not commutate the motors. ArduPilot has no BLDC commutation or FOC stack. The 4-in-1 ESC therefore needs four dedicated ESC MCUs, or one dual-core-capable part per pair. Typical choices are the AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32. The H743 talks to them over bidirectional DShot600. Everything below follows that split.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At a design-point T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{DS(on)} = 0.75 \times (1 + 0.006 \times 85) = 0.75 \times 1.51 = 1.13\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step complementary PWM

At any instant, two phases carry current:
- **Active leg:** the high-side FET conducts for D. Its low-side partner synchronously rectifies for 1−D.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET conducts continuously during that 60° sector.

The current always flows through **exactly two FETs**, whatever D is. Neglecting ripple (I_ripple ≪ 80 A at 48 kHz with typical motor inductance):

$$P_{cond,FET} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.13\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{14.5\ W\ per\ channel}$$

### 1.3 Copper and shunt losses

| Element | R | P at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| PCB copper (2 oz inner + 2 oz outer, battery pad → phase pad) | ≈ 0.30 mΩ | 1.92 W |
| Current-sense shunt | 0.20 mΩ | 1.28 W |
| **Subtotal** | | **3.2 W** |

### 1.4 Ripple correction (optional refinement)

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{dc}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12},\quad \Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

Example: L_ph = 20 µH, V_bus − V_bemf = 12 V, D = 0.5 gives ΔI = 6.25 A. The term ΔI²/12 = 3.3 A², which is under 0.1% of 6400. It is negligible at 80 A.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Hard-switching (V–I overlap)

Only the active leg's high-side FET hard-switches. With t_r + t_f = 35 ns (gate driver ~1 A sink/source, R_g ≈ 4.7 Ω):

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 35\text{ns} \times 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.69\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

With t_dt = 200 ns per edge, two edges per cycle, and V_F = 0.8 V:

$$P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{ns}\times 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

Reducing the dead time to 100 ns halves this, but only do so if the gate driver's propagation-delay matching allows it.

### 2.3 Reverse recovery, Coss, and gate charge

| Term | Formula | Inputs | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f | 60 nC | 0.073 W |
| Output capacitance | ½ Q_oss · V · f | 70 nC | 0.042 W |
| Gate drive (driver-side) | Q_g · V_gs · f · 2 FETs | 80 nC, 10 V | 0.077 W |

### 2.4 Per-channel and total budget (80 A burst, T_j = 110 °C)

| Loss | W / channel |
|---|---|
| FET conduction | 14.5 |
| Copper + shunt | 3.2 |
| Hard switching | 1.69 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.19 |
| **Total per channel** | **≈ 20.8 W** |
| **4-channel total** | **≈ 83 W** |

Efficiency check: P_out ≈ 22.2 V × 80 A × 4 = 7.1 kW, so η ≈ 98.8%. The efficiency is good, but the problem is the absolute heat, not the efficiency.
