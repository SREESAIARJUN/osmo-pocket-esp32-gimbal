# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: System Architecture Reference

**Job #34 · Revision A (Engineering Draft)**

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Critical Findings

I have no prior Job #34 documents, BOM, or layout. Every numeric value below comes from stated, parameterized assumptions. Replace the bracketed values with your selected-component datasheet values and re-run the equations. The math is structured so you can do that directly.

**Architectural clarification:** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does not commutate the motors. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware (assumed: AT32F421K8U7, the common AM32 target). The 48 kHz figure is an ESC-side PWM setting. The H743 only generates DShot600 and decodes the bidirectional telemetry.

### Critical findings

| # | Finding | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | 80 A on all four channels at once dissipates about 130 W on a nano-class (≤30.5 mm) board. | Not sustainable. The adiabatic burst limit is about 1.5–2 s (§3.4). |
| F2 | One channel at 80 A dissipates about 33 W. | Burst of about 7–9 s is feasible if the other three channels are lightly loaded. |
| F3 | Conduction losses are about 93 % of total dissipation at 80 A. Switching at 48 kHz is about 4–6 %. | Rds(on) and copper area dominate the thermal design. Dropping to 24 kHz saves only about 1 W per channel. |
| F4 | The steady-state thermal-runaway criterion is Rθ·P_cond,25·α ≥ 1. | Sustained 80 A cannot be rated without a heatsink (§3.3). |
| F5 | On the H7, DShot DMA buffers must live outside DTCM and either be cache-coherent or in non-cacheable memory. | ArduPilot handles this, but custom hwdef memory maps must preserve it. |

**Recommendation:** Rate the product as **"55 A continuous (with airflow) / 80 A burst ≤ 5 s single-channel."** Implement a firmware thermal derate from the ESC temperature telemetry.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Commutation topology

Assumptions:
- Six-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary PWM (synchronous rectification), which is the AM32 default.
- At any instant, current flows through exactly two device positions in series:
  - the high-side (HS) of phase X, PWM'd at duty D, with the low-side (LS) of phase X conducting during (1−D);
  - the LS of phase Y, held fully on.
- Each leg's HS and LS each own a 120° (1/3) electrical window.
