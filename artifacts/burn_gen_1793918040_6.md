## 0. Architectural Clarification and Executive Findings

**Topology.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller only. Each of the four ESC channels is commutated by its own small MCU, assumed here to be an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 **[A]**. The H743 does not drive the gate drivers directly. ArduPilot has no native FOC/6-step ESC firmware for the H7, and the H743 lacks enough advanced-control timers to run 4×3 complementary PWM pairs with independent dead-time.

**Headline findings:**

| # | Finding | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | 80 A on all four channels at once means about **100–110 W of FET dissipation**. On a nano board (≈4.2 J/K lumped thermal mass) this gives **~2.3 s** from 50 °C to Tj ≈ 110 °C. "80 A burst" must be specified as **≤2 s per channel, not simultaneous on all four**. | Critical |
| F2 | Convective cooling (≈0.18 W/K under prop wash) supports only about **20–22 A continuous per channel**. Any "80 A" label is a burst rating. | Critical |
| F3 | 4 × 80 A = 320 A battery-side worst case. An XT60 (≈60 A continuous) and 30×30 copper cannot carry this. The battery-side current limit must be enforced, e.g. via ArduPilot `BATT_*` limits and ESC current limiting. | Critical |
| F4 | At 48 kHz, switching losses (~2.1 W/ch) are about 9 % of conduction losses at 80 A. Conduction dominates, so **R_DS(on) and copper are the levers, not f_sw**. | Info |
| F5 | DShot600 bidirectional on 4 motors needs 5 DMA streams on one timer (UP + CH1–4). This fits comfortably in the H743's 16 DMAMUX-routed streams. | OK |

"Nano" is assumed to mean the **30.5×30.5 mm footprint, 26×26 mm usable**. A true 20×20 board makes F1 and F2 roughly 2× worse.
