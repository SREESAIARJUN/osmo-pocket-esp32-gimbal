## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step complementary PWM

At any commutation sector, two phases conduct:
- **Phase A** (PWM'd leg): current alternates between the HS FET (duty D) and the LS FET (synchronous rectification, 1−D).
- **Phase B** (static leg): the LS FET is on for the full sector.

Current therefore always passes through **exactly two channel resistances in series**, independent of D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Ripple contribution

Phase-to-phase inductance is L ≈ 20 µH for a typical 5″ motor. Ripple peaks at D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L\,f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\times10^{-6} \times 48\times10^{3}} = 6.6\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.6 = 6403.6\ \text{A}^2$$

The ripple term is under 0.1% and can be neglected at 48 kHz. This is one benefit of the high PWM frequency.

### 1.3 Hot resistance and loss

$$R_{DS(on)}(110^\circ\text{C}) = k_T \cdot R_{25} = 1.5 \times 1.5 = 2.25\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = 6404 \times 2 \times 2.25\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{28.8\ W}$$

### 1.4 Distribution across the six FETs

Over a full electrical cycle, each leg spends 1/3 of the time as the PWM leg, 1/3 as the static leg, and 1/3 floating.

| FET | Time-weighted conduction fraction | P @ D = 0.5 | P @ D = 0.9 |
|---|---|---|---|
| HS (each) | D/3 | 2.4 W | 4.3 W |
| LS (each) | (1−D)/3 + 1/3 | 7.2 W | 5.3 W |

These fractions sum to 1 per pair; total is 6 FETs × average 4.8 W = 28.8 W.

**Key result.** The **low-side FETs are the thermal hotspot** at partial throttle. Place them closest to the thermal vias and pad, or use a lower-R_DS(on) part for LS.

### 1.5 Copper and shunt losses (per motor)

$$P_{shunt} = 80^2 \times 0.5\times10^{-3} = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

PCB copper: assume a 2 mΩ effective path (traces, vias, pads; 2 oz, 6-layer with paralleled power planes):

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 2\times10^{-3} = 12.8\ \text{W (bus side, partially shared)}$$

Copper is a **first-order** loss term at 80 A. Every 0.5 mΩ of trace resistance is worth 3.2 W per motor. Use paralleled planes on L1/L2/L5/L6 and via fences of ≥ 20 vias (0.3 mm) per phase transition.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Transition times (gate-charge method)

Assume the Miller plateau is about 4.5 V, the driver is 12 V, and R_g = 0 Ω external (driver-limited).

$$t_r = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{src}} = \frac{12\ \text{nC}}{1.0\ \text{A}} = 12\ \text{ns}, \qquad t_f = \frac{12\ \text{nC}}{1.5\ \text{A}} = 8\ \text{ns}$$

### 2.2 Hard-switching V·I overlap (HS FET of PWM leg only)

The LS FET turns on after dead time with its body diode already conducting, so it switches at zero voltage (ZVS) and its overlap loss is ≈ 0.

$$E_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r + t_f) = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\ \text{ns} = 20.2\ \mu\text{J}$$

$$P_{sw} = E_{sw}\,f_{sw} = 20.2\ \mu\text{J} \times 48\ \text{kHz} = 0.97\ \text{W}$$
