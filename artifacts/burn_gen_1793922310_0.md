## 0. Architectural Findings

Read these before the math. They change the design.

| # | Finding | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | The STM32H743 should be the **flight controller only**. Commutation runs on four dedicated ESC MCUs (AT32F421 or STM32G071) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. | The H743 cannot run four sensorless FOC/6-step loops alongside ArduPilot's scheduler with deterministic latency. It is also the only way to get standard bi-directional DShot telemetry. |
| F2 | **80 A burst per motor (320 A total) is not feasible on a 20×20 "nano" footprint.** The minimum credible size is 30.5×30.5 mm with an 8-layer, 2 oz copper stackup. Even then it is limited to bursts of 5 s or less. | See §3.4. Steady-state rise exceeds 300 °C without forced airflow. |
| F3 | **48 kHz PWM doubles switching loss** compared with 24 kHz. Use 48 kHz only for low-inductance motors (fewer than about 10 µH phase-to-phase), or make it a firmware parameter. | About +1.3 W per ESC at burst (§2.3). |
| F4 | A single total-current shunt at 320 A is impractical. Use **per-ESC 0.25 mΩ shunts**, or a Hall sensor (e.g., ACS772/MLX91221) on the battery bus. | 1.6 W per shunt at 80 A. |

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC, i.e., one 3-phase bridge of 6 FETs)

### 2.1 Conduction topology

In 6-step operation, two legs are active in each 60° sector:
- One leg is held on: its low-side FET conducts continuously for the sector.
- The other leg PWMs: its high-side FET is on for D·T_sw, and its low-side FET synchronously rectifies for (1−D)·T_sw.

The motor current therefore always flows through **exactly two FET channels** (ignoring dead time). This gives:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1+\alpha (T_j-25)\right]$$

with α ≈ 0.006 /K, which gives the 1.6× factor at 125 °C.

Each FET conducts for 120° of each electrical cycle, so the per-device loss is:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{2 I^2 R_{ds}}{6} = \frac{I^2 R_{ds}}{3}$$

**Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C (R_ds = 1.44 mΩ):**

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.44\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{18.4\ W}$$

$$P_{cond,FET} = \mathbf{3.07\ W}\ \text{(average)}$$

The peak instantaneous loss in a conducting FET is 9.2 W.

### 2.2 Switching losses (48 kHz)

Only the PWM-ing leg switches hard. The terms are:

$$P_{sw,VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I\, (t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}$$
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw}$$
$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \quad\text{(approximating } E_{oss}\text{)}$$
$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw}$$
$$P_{gate} = N_{sw}\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw}, \quad N_{sw} = 2$$

Note that P_gate is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistor, not in the FET.

**Numbers at V_bus = 25.2 V, I = 80 A, f_sw = 48 kHz:**

| Term | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| V·I overlap | 0.5 · 25.2 · 80 · 40 ns · 48 k | **1.94 W** |
| Reverse recovery | 40 nC · 25.2 · 48 k | 0.05 W |
| Coss | 0.5 · 50 nC · 25.2 · 48 k | 0.03 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.8 · 80 · 200 ns · 48 k | **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (driver side) | 2 · 40 nC · 10 V · 48 k | 0.04 W |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.67 W** |

At 24 kHz the total would be ≈ 1.34 W, which is the basis for Finding F3.
