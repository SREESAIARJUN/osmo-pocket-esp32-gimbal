# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Budget, and Bidirectional DShot600 Allocation

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

I don't have access to a "Job #62" spec, so every numeric input below is an explicit assumption. Replace them with your BOM datasheet values. The equations are general; the numbers are a worked example.

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. A production 4-in-1 uses one ESC MCU per channel (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32) driving a 3-phase gate driver. ## 1. Conduction Loss Model

**Commutation scheme.** With six-step (trapezoidal) commutation, two phases conduct at any instant. With synchronous complementary PWM:

- **PWM leg:** current alternates between the high-side and low-side FET. One FET always carries I.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET is held on and carries I.

So two R_DS(on) are always in the current path, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + I_{rms}^2 R_{Cu}$$

**Temperature dependence.** Model R_DS(on) as linear in junction temperature:

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1} \Rightarrow k_T \approx 1.6 \text{ at } 125\,°C$$

This creates electrothermal feedback. Iterate:

$$T_j = T_a + P(T_j) \cdot R_{th}$$

The iteration diverges (thermal runaway) when:

$$I^2 \cdot 2R_{25}\,\alpha\, R_{th} \ge 1$$

**Worked numbers (hot, 125 °C):**

- R = 1.2 mΩ × 1.6 = 1.92 mΩ
- P_cond = 80² × 2 × 1.92 m = **24.6 W per channel**
- Copper path: assume R_Cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ per channel (pads, vias, phase pad). That adds 80² × 0.3 m = **1.9 W**.

**Phase vs. battery current.** Phase current is not battery current. At duty D:

$$I_{batt} \approx D \cdot I_{phase}$$

A "80 A ESC" rated on battery current sees more than 80 A phase current at part throttle. Size the model on phase current.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Per channel:

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw | ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 20 ns × 48k = **0.97 W** |
| Coss discharge | Q_oss·V·f_sw | 40n × 25.2 × 48k = **0.05 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 50n × 25.2 × 48k = **0.06 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | 2·V_f·I·t_dt·f_sw | 2 × 0.8 × 80 × 100n × 48k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (6 FETs, dissipated in driver/R_g) | 6·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw | 6 × 60n × 10 × 48k = **0.17 W** |
| **Switching subtotal** | | **≈ 1.86 W** |

**Per-channel total:** 24.6 + 1.9 + 1.86 ≈ **28.4 W**

**Four channels at simultaneous burst:** ≈ **114 W** into a 30×30 mm board.

**Design levers:**

- **Conduction dominates (~93%).** Lowering R_DS(on), or paralleling two FETs per switch (which halves conduction loss), matters far more than f_sw.
- **Dropping to 24 kHz** saves only about 0.8 W per channel. It costs current ripple and audible noise.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Junction rise (fast, millisecond scale)

Instantaneous dissipation per conducting FET ≈ 12.3 W. Using Z_th(j-c) ≈ 1.5 K/W steady state (about 0.5 K/W at 10 ms):

$$\Delta T_{j-c} \approx 12.3 \times 1.5 \approx 18\ \text{K (worst case)}$$

Commutation rotates conduction among the six FETs. Each FET's average share is roughly 1/3 to 1/2 of that, depending on PWM leg assignment. Use the 18 K worst case for margin.

### 3.2 Board heat capacity (the real limit)

| Element | Calculation | Heat capacity |
|---|---|---|
| FR-4 | 9.3 cm² × 0.16 cm × 1.85 g/cm³ × 1.1 J/gK | 3.0 J/K |
| Copper (8 × 70 µm) | 9.3 cm² × 0.056 cm × 8.96 g/cm³ × 0.385 J/gK | 1.8 J/K |
| FETs, components, solder | — | ~0.3 J/K |
| **C_th total** | | **≈ 5.1 J/K** |

### 3.3 Foster network: 2-pole board model

$$T_{board}(t) = T_a + P \, R_{ba}\left(1 - e^{-t/R_{ba}C_{th}}\right)$$

With prop-wash airflow, assume R_ba ≈ 6 K/W, so τ = 6 × 5.1 ≈ 31 s.

### 3.4 Burst-time limit

For burst durations t ≪ τ, the board heats roughly adiabatically:

$$t_{burst} \approx \frac{C_{th}\,(T_{max} - T_{start})}{P}$$

With T_start = 40 °C, board limit 110 °C (leaving ~15 K for j-c rise to stay under T_j 125 °C), and P = 114 W:

$$t_{burst} \approx \frac{5.1 \times 70}{114} \approx \mathbf{3.1\ s}$$

The temperature rise is approximately 22 K per second at full 4×80 A.

**Steady-state sustainable power:** (110 − 40) / 6 ≈ 11.7 W total. That corresponds to roughly √(11.7 / 4 / (2 × 1.92 m)) ≈ **27 A per channel continuous**.

**Verdict.**

- 80 A per channel on a nano 4-in-1 is a ~1–3 s burst figure, not a continuous rating.
- To defend 80 A burst, you need firmware limiting:
  - ESC-side current limit,
  - board NTC derating,
  - ArduPilot battery current monitoring with `BATT_*` failsafes.
- To improve margin: parallel FETs (≤ 0.7 mΩ effective per switch), add a heatsink plate, or derate the claim to "80 A, 1 s."

### 3.5 Electrothermal runaway check

$$I^2 \cdot 2R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_{th,total} = 6400 \times 2.4\text{m} \times 0.006 \times (6 \times 4) \approx 2.2 > 1$$

R_th,total is the per-channel effective value with four channels sharing the board.

This confirms that 80 A steady state is thermally unstable on this board. The ESC firmware current limit is mandatory, not optional.
