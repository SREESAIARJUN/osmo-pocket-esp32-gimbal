## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Feasibility Flags

Three issues must be resolved before the math is meaningful.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every practical 4-in-1 design, the H743 (running ArduPilot) generates DShot. A separate ESC MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32) performs commutation at 48 kHz. The "48 kHz switching" model below applies to the ESC MCU's PWM, not to H743 timers.
2. **80 A burst on a "nano" footprint is not thermally viable as continuous current.** The design can only absorb 80 A for a few seconds, using thermal capacitance. Section 3 quantifies this. I am assuming a **30.5 × 30.5 mm ESC board** stacked under a **20 × 20 mm H743 FC**. A single 20 × 20 board carrying 4 × 80 A is not credible: the FET count, copper area and bulk capacitance do not fit.
3. **"80 A" is interpreted as 80 A per motor, phase current, for ≤ 5 s bursts.** The 4-motor simultaneous worst case is used for board-level thermal analysis.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-Dependent Resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_J) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_J - 25)]$$

At T_J = 115 °C: R = 0.90 × (1 + 0.0055 × 90) = **1.35 mΩ**. This is the hot-burst design value.

### 1.2 6-Step Current Path

At any instant, two phases conduct:
- **Leg A (PWM leg):** the high-side FET conducts for D. With synchronous rectification, the low-side FET conducts for (1 − D).
- **Leg B (return leg):** the low-side FET is held on continuously.

The motor current I therefore always flows through exactly two channels, regardless of duty cycle (ignoring dead time):

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_J)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS}(T_J) = 2 I^2 R_{DS}(T_J)$$

**At 80 A, 115 °C:**

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.35\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{17.28\ W\ per\ motor}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET Loss (for Junction Temperature)

Each FET is active for 1/3 of the electrical period (120° conduction).
- Peak instantaneous per FET: P_FET,pk = I²R = 80² × 1.35 m = 8.64 W
- Averaged per FET (worst case, low-side return): P_FET,avg ≈ 8.64 / 3 + switching share ≈ 2.9–3.5 W
