# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #33): System Architecture and Mathematical Model

## 0. Scope and Ground Rules

The "80A burst" spec drives everything else, so two corrections come first.

1. **The H743 does not switch the MOSFETs.** In every production 4-in-1 stack, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32, or EFM8BB21 for BLHeli_S). That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM and does BEMF commutation. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to the ESCs over bidirectional DShot600. The model below keeps that partition.
2. **"80A burst" on a 20×20 mm board is a marketing number, not a design point.** The math below shows that four channels at 80 A dissipate about 90 W into a board with a thermal mass of about 4 J/K. That corresponds to roughly 2–3 seconds of headroom before the board limit. I model it honestly and give a derating recommendation.

**All component values are representative assumptions, not values for any specific part.** Substitute datasheet values for your chosen parts before release.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current Path

In six-step trapezoidal drive (or FOC near peak torque), the current path at any instant goes through one high-side FET and one low-side FET. With synchronous rectification (complementary PWM, the default in AM32/BLHeli_32):

- The high-side of the PWM'd phase conducts for duty fraction **D**.
- The low-side of the same phase conducts for **(1−D)**, freewheeling.
- The low-side of the return phase conducts for the full **100 %** of the commutation interval.

So the bridge always has two R_ds(on) in series:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

### 2.2 Temperature-Dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds(on),25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right]$$

Conduction loss and junction temperature depend on each other, so solve iteratively:

$$T_j = T_{case} + P_{FET}\,Z_{\theta JC}(t), \qquad P_{FET} = f(R_{ds(on)}(T_j))$$

At T_j ≈ 115 °C: R_ds(on) ≈ 1.0 × (1 + 0.004 × 90) = **1.36 mΩ**. Use **1.5 mΩ** for margin, which also absorbs package and solder resistance.

### 2.3 Numbers at 80 A

Burst current is near-DC within one PWM cycle, so I_rms ≈ 80 A:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ motor}$$

Per-FET share during a commutation sector:

| FET | Conduction fraction | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| Return low-side | 100 % | 9.6 W |
| PWM high-side | D (≈ 0.9 at burst) | ≈ 8.6 W |
| Freewheel low-side | (1−D) | ≈ 1.0 W |

Averaged over the six sectors, each of the 6 FETs dissipates about **3.2 W**. Peak instantaneous loss is about 9.6 W per FET.

### 2.4 Copper Losses (Often Forgotten)

**Phase pad to FET path:** about 0.3 mΩ per phase leg in 2 oz copper with vias. Two legs conduct at once:

$$P_{Cu,phase} = 80^2 \times 0.6\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 3.8\ \text{W per motor}$$

**Battery bus:** about 0.2 mΩ including pads.

- If all four motors burst together, I_bus ≈ 4 × 80 × D ≈ 290 A.
- That gives P_bus ≈ 290² × 0.2 mΩ ≈ **17 W**.

At that current the XT30/XT60 lead and pad are the real failure point; see Section 8.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 3.1 Hard-Switching (V–I Overlap)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

This loss lands only on the PWM'd high-side, because the low-side switches at near-zero voltage (ZVS-like) after the dead time.

### 3.2 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2t_d \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 600\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.84\ W}$$

This is comparable to the switching loss. **Reducing dead time from 300 ns to 150 ns saves about 0.9 W per motor.** That is the single cheapest optimization; AM32 exposes it in the config tool.
