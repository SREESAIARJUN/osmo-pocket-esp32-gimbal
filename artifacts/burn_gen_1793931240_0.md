## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step complementary (synchronous) PWM
During any commutation sector, one phase is PWM-switched, one is held low, and one floats.

- **PWM on-time (duty D):** HS(phase A) → winding → LS(phase B). Two FETs conduct.
- **PWM off-time (1−D):** LS(phase A, synchronous rectification) → winding → LS(phase B). Two FETs conduct.

Two channel-resistances are therefore always in series, independent of D:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)
\qquad
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

Averaged over 6 sectors, each of the 6 FETs carries 1/3 of the total:

$$
P_{cond,FET,avg} = \frac{I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)}{3}
$$

The instantaneous per-FET loss can be up to 3× this value within a sector. That matters only at very low eRPM, when a sector is longer than the thermal time constant of the die.

### 1.2 Electro-thermal fixed point
R_ds depends on T_j, and T_j depends on loss, so solve iteratively:

$$
T_j^{(k+1)} = T_{ref} + R_{\theta}\cdot P\big(R_{ds}(T_j^{(k)})\big)
$$

Thermal runaway occurs if the loop gain $\partial T_j/\partial T_j = R_\theta \cdot 2I^2 R_{ds25}\alpha > 1$.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A
- R_ds(110 °C) = 1.2 mΩ × (1 + 0.0045 × 85) = **1.66 mΩ**
- P_cond,ch = 2 × 6400 × 1.66e-3 = **21.2 W per channel**
- P_cond,FET,avg = **3.5 W**

### 1.4 Copper and interconnect conduction
At 2 oz copper the sheet resistance is about 0.25 mΩ/□ at 25 °C, rising roughly 0.39 %/K.

- A typical nano power path has about 4 □ across the bus and phase pours, plus vias, plus motor pads. That gives R_cu ≈ 1.0–1.5 mΩ per path.
- P_cu ≈ I² × R_cu ≈ 6.4–9.6 W per channel at 80 A.

This is the same order as the FET loss and is frequently forgotten. Mitigations:
- Use 3 oz or heavier outer layers.
- Stitch bus layers in parallel.
- Use solid-filled vias or ≥ 20 vias per phase pad.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Gate drive current and transition times

$$
I_g = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}{R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}} = \frac{10-3.5}{5.7} = 1.14\text{ A}
$$

$$
t_r \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_g} = \frac{15\text{ nC}}{1.14} \approx 13\text{ ns}
$$

Turn-off uses (V_plateau − 0)/R_g ≈ 0.61 A, so t_f is about 25 ns. A separate turn-off diode plus a smaller resistor is recommended. The numbers below use t_r = 13 ns and t_f = 25 ns.

### 2.2 Loss components (per channel; only the PWM-switched leg commutates hard)

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw | ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 38 ns × 48 k = **1.84 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 30 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.036 W** |
| C_oss | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw | 0.5 × 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.024 W** |
| Dead-time diode | V_f·I·2·t_dt·f_sw | 0.8 × 80 × 400 ns × 48 k = **1.23 W** |
| Gate drive (driver, not FET) | 2·Q_g·V_drv·f_sw | 2 × 60 nC × 10 × 48 k = **0.058 W** |
| **Total** | | **≈ 3.2 W** |

Key insight: at 48 kHz the **dead-time diode loss is about two-thirds of the V-I loss**. Before reaching for a slower f_sw, minimize dead time in AM32/BLHeli_32. Use the lowest value that keeps shoot-through margin, verified with a scope at 80 A.
