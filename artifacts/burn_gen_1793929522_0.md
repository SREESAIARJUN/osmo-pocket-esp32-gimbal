# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (20×20 Nano): Power-Stage, Thermal, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Design

## 0. Assumptions and Architecture Corrections

I don't have the original specification for Job #4, so these are the baseline assumptions. Change any of them and the models below can be re-run directly.

| Parameter | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal |
| Burst rating | **80 A per motor channel (phase current)**, 4 channels simultaneously |
| PWM | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous rectification), dead-time 200 ns |
| MOSFET | 40 V, 3.3×3.3 DFN, R_DS(on) = 1.5 mΩ @ 25 °C / V_GS = 10 V, Q_g = 50 nC, C_oss = 1.5 nF, t_r + t_f = 20 ns @ 80 A |
| FET count | 6 per channel, 24 total |
| Board | 20×20 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz inner / 2 oz outer copper |

There are two architecture issues to address before going further.

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In a 4-in-1 design, each channel (or a quad-core part) has its own ESC MCU, such as the AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32. The H743 runs ArduPilot and generates DShot frames to those MCUs. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs, not the H743. This document models both sides accordingly.
2. **An 80 A burst on a 20×20 board is a thermal-capacity limit, not a steady-state rating.** As shown in §3, the realistic burst duration is about 1–2 s. Marketing a "80 A burst" without a time qualifier is not defensible.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_DS = 1.5 × (1 + 0.0045 × 85) = **2.07 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-step bridge with synchronous rectification

During any commutation sector, one high-side (HS) leg and one low-side (LS) leg carry the phase current I_ph.

- **HS FET (PWM'd):** P_HS = I_ph² R_DS · D
- **LS sync FET of the PWM'd leg:** P_LS,sync = I_ph² R_DS · (1 − D − 2t_dt·f_sw)
- **LS FET of the return leg (fully on):** P_LS,ret = I_ph² R_DS

Per channel, the time-averaged total is:

$$P_{cond,ch} \approx 2\,I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}(T_j) \quad(\text{independent of } D \text{ to first order})$$

At 80 A:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 × 80^2 × 2.07\text{ mΩ} = \mathbf{26.5\ W}$$

### 1.3 Copper and shunt losses

- **Shunt** (0.25 mΩ low-side, or DC-link per channel): 80² × 0.25 m = 1.6 W per channel.
- **Phase traces and pads** (≈0.3 mΩ effective per path): ≈1.9 W per channel.
- **DC-link plane:** the battery current is I_bat ≈ D·I_ph. At 80 A phase and D = 0.85, that is 68 A per channel, 272 A total. Use the main pad with ≈0.1 mΩ: 272² × 0.1 m ≈ 7.4 W board-level.

> **Note:** 272 A of total battery current is beyond any XT60/XT90 and pack rating. In practice, four simultaneous 80 A bursts only occur at high duty during punch-outs. The 80 A figure should be specified as a **single-channel or desync-transient** rating.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd HS FET hard-switches. The LS sync FET switches at zero voltage, but it incurs body-diode and reverse-recovery losses.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 20\text{ns} × 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I_{ph}\,(2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 × 80 × 400\text{ns} × 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

Assuming Q_rr = 40 nC at 80 A:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} × 25.2 × 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss}V^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 × 1.5\text{n} × 635 × 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.023\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors. Two FETs switch per channel:

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 × 50\text{n} × 10 × 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 2.6 Switching-loss summary per channel

| Component | Loss |
|---|---|
| Hard-switching overlap | 0.97 W |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 W |
| Reverse recovery | 0.05 W |
| Output capacitance | 0.02 W |
| Gate drive | 0.05 W |
| **Total** | **≈2.3 W** |

**Design insight:** dead-time loss exceeds hard-switching loss. Reducing dead time to 100 ns, which AM32 supports, saves about 0.6 W per channel. Dropping f_sw to 24 kHz halves all of §2, but at 80 A conduction dominates (26.5 W vs. 2.3 W), so f_sw is not the lever here. **R_DS(on) and copper are.**

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Total burst dissipation (all four channels at 80 A, T_j ≈ 110 °C)

| Source | Watts |
|---|---|
| Conduction (4 × 26.5 W) | 106.0 |
| Switching (4 × 2.3 W) | 9.2 |
| Shunts (4 × 1.6 W) | 6.4 |
| Phase copper (4 × 1.9 W) | 7.6 |
| DC-link copper | 7.4 |
| Gate drivers, LDOs, MCUs (~1.5 W FC side) | ~3.0 |
| **Total** | **≈140 W** |

### 3.2 Steady-state check

A 20×20 board in prop-wash has R_th(board→ambient) ≈ 6–10 K/W. Even at 6 K/W:

$$\Delta T = 140 × 6 = 840\ \text{K}$$

**This is impossible.** The only valid analysis is transient.
