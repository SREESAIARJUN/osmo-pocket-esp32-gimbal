# STM32H743 AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC) Design Package — Job #29

**Scope:** 80 A burst conduction losses, 48 kHz switching losses, thermal transient model, ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 timer and DMA plan, and reference netlists.

---

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions (read first)

Three issues will invalidate the design if they are not addressed up front.

1. **The H743 does not switch the MOSFETs.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU (STM32G071 or AT32F421 running AM32, or EFM8/BB21 running BLHeli_S). That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The H743 only sends DShot600 frames and receives eRPM telemetry. The switching-loss model below therefore belongs to the ESC MCU and gate-driver domain, not to ArduPilot.

2. **80 A burst on a "nano" board is thermally implausible.** A 4-in-1 power stage needs 24 MOSFETs. On a 20×20 mm board these must be PDFN 3.3×3.3 parts with Rds(on) of roughly 2–4 mΩ. Section 3 shows that even 5×6 mm, ~1 mΩ parts on a 30.5×30.5 mm board only survive about 1 s at 80 A. Treat 80 A as a specification to verify, not a given.

3. **Netlists are reference-grade.** The part values are representative. Rds(on), Qg, Qrr and Zth must be replaced with datasheet values for the actual BOM before anyone signs off.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At 110 °C:

$$R_{ds}(110) = 1.2\,\text{mΩ} \times [1+0.0065 \times 85] = 1.2 \times 1.553 = 1.86\ \text{mΩ}$$

### 1.2 Current path during one 6-step sector

In each sector two phases carry current:

- **PWM leg:** the high side conducts for duty D, and the synchronous low side conducts for (1−D), excluding dead time.
- **Static leg:** the low side is on continuously.

$$P_{cond,PWM} = I^2 R_{ds}\,D + I^2 R_{ds}(1-D) = I^2 R_{ds}$$

$$P_{cond,static} = I^2 R_{ds}$$

$$\boxed{P_{cond} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)}$$

The result is independent of D because synchronous rectification is used. Without synchronous rectification, the (1−D) term would be a diode loss instead.

### 1.3 Numerical result

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.86\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{23.8\ W\ per\ motor}$$

### 1.4 Parasitic copper and shunt losses

These are frequently forgotten and they matter.

| Element | R | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase path copper (2 oz, ~0.3 mΩ per path × 2 paths) | 0.6 mΩ | 3.8 W |
| Shared current shunt (0.25 mΩ, sees 4 × I_avg) | — | see §3 |
| Pad and solder (×2) | 0.2 mΩ | 1.3 W |

These are board-level losses, so they heat the PCB rather than the junction directly.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard-switched PWM leg)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

$$= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

The edge times follow from gate drive: $t \approx Q_{gd}/I_g$ with $I_g = (V_{drv}-V_{plateau})/R_{g,tot}$. For example, (10 − 3) V / 5 Ω = 1.4 A gives 8 nC / 1.4 A ≈ 6 ns ideal. In practice t_r and t_f are 3–4× longer because of driver source current limits; the FD6288 class sources only a few hundred mA. Slow edges limit EMI but cost efficiency.
