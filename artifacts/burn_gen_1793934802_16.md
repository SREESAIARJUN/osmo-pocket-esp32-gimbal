# Job #44: STM32H743 Flight Controller + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC

## Engineering Model, DShot Timer/DMA Plan, Netlists and Thermal Budget

---

## 0. Architecture Decision and Assumptions

**Partitioning.** The STM32H743 should not commutate motors directly. Production 4-in-1 stacks use the H743 as the flight controller running ArduPilot. Each motor channel then has its own ESC MCU (an AT32F421 or STM32G071 class part running AM32 or BLHeli_32-compatible firmware) driving a 3-phase half-bridge. The H743 talks to the ESCs over bidirectional DShot600. This model assumes that split.

**Form factor reality check.** An 80 A per-channel burst rating is not credible on a 20×20 mm "nano" board. Section 2.6 shows the heat-capacity math. This design assumes:

- the ESC is 30.5×30.5 mm, or 36×36 mm with 30.5 mm mounting holes;
- the FC is a separate 20×20 or 30.5 board in a stack.

If "nano" is a hard constraint, de-rate to roughly 35–45 A burst.

## 1. Commutation Model

The ESC uses six-step (trapezoidal) commutation with complementary, synchronous-rectified PWM:

- At any instant two phases conduct and one floats for BEMF sensing.
- The PWM phase alternates between its high-side switch (for time D·T) and its low-side switch (for (1−D)·T).
- The return phase's low-side switch conducts continuously for the 120° sector.

So the motor current always passes through exactly two MOSFETs in series, regardless of duty cycle. This is the key simplification.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

R_ds(T_j) = R_ds,25 · [1 + α(T_j − 25)]

### 2.2 Per-motor conduction loss

P_cond,motor(T_j) = 2 · I² · R_ds(T_j)

At 80 A:

- T_j = 25 °C: 2 × 6400 × 1.0 mΩ = **12.8 W**
- T_j = 100 °C: R_ds = 1.375 mΩ, so P = **17.6 W**

### 2.3 Per-FET distribution

Each of the six FETs carries current for 1/3 of the electrical cycle.

- I_rms,FET = I/√3 = 46.2 A
- P_FET = I² · R_ds / 3 = **2.93 W** at 100 °C

### 2.4 Electro-thermal fixed point (closed form)

Let:

- P₀ = 2 I² R_ds,25
- R_θ = effective thermal resistance from junction to ambient for that loss

Then T_j = T_a + R_θ · P₀ · [1 + α(T_j − 25)], which solves to:

T_j = [T_a + R_θ · P₀ · (1 − 25α)] / [1 − R_θ · P₀ · α]

**Thermal-runaway criterion:** R_θ · P₀ · α ≥ 1.

For P₀ = 12.8 W per motor and α = 0.005, R_θ must stay below 15.6 K/W per motor channel. This is satisfied steady-state only with real airflow (see Section 2.6).

### 2.5 Copper and shunt losses (per motor, 80 A)

| Element | Resistance | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| Phase pad + 2 oz trace, about 0.25 mΩ | I²R | 1.6 W |
| Current shunt (shared bus, 0.25 mΩ) | (4 × I_avg)² R, burst only | see total |
| Battery lead pad + bus plane, 0.3 mΩ, at 320 A total | 320² × 0.3 mΩ | **30.7 W** board-level |

The bus-plane term matters. Use 3–4 oz outer copper, stitched planes, and solder-thickened battery pads.

### 2.6 Burst (transient) model

The board is treated as a lumped thermal capacitance:

C_th = Σ mᵢ cᵢ ≈ FR4 (6 g × 0.9) + Cu (4 g × 0.385) + FETs/caps (3 g × 0.7) ≈ **9 J/K** for a 36×36 mm, 4 oz board.
