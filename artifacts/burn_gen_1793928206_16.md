# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC "Nano" Stack (Job #61): Power-Stage, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

## 0. Architectural Corrections

These shape everything below.

1. **The STM32H743 cannot be the ESC controller.** It runs ArduPilot, which outputs DShot. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. This design uses the **Artery AT32F421** running AM32. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs. The H743's job is DShot600 plus the bidirectional telemetry decode.
2. **80 A per motor on a 20×20 mm, 3.3×3.3 mm-MOSFET stack is a burst rating, not a continuous rating.** The math below shows sustainable burst time is about **1–2 seconds**. Plan around this, or move to a 30.5×30.5 board with 5×6 mm FETs.
3. **4 × 80 A phase current on 6S can exceed 250 A of battery current** (about 6 kW). The battery lead, XT60, and pack C-rating are usually the real limits. I treat 80 A as a per-channel phase-current burst requirement.
4. **Part numbers are representative and must be verified.** All loss numbers derive from the parameter table, so substitute datasheet values and the equations still hold.

## 2. Conduction-Loss Model (6-Step Trapezoidal, Synchronous Rectification)

In each 60° commutation sector, one leg PWMs (high side ↔ low side, complementary) and one leg holds its low side on. The phase current always flows through **exactly two conducting FETs**: HS_A or LS_A, plus LS_B.

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS}(T_j)=R_{DS,25}\left[1+\alpha (T_j-25)\right],\ \alpha\approx0.006/K$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.4\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{30.7\ W\ per\ channel}$$

**Copper parasitics.** 2 oz copper has a sheet resistance of 1.72×10⁻⁸ / 70 µm = 0.246 mΩ/□. A typical phase path of about 4 squares on two paralleled layers gives roughly 0.5 mΩ per path, with two paths in series.

$$P_{Cu,ch} \approx 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega \approx \mathbf{6.4\ W}$$

This is easily overlooked on nano boards. Mitigate it by stacking 4 layers × 2 oz for phase and bus pours and by using solder-filled motor pads.
