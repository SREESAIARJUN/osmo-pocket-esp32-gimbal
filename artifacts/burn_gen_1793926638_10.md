# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80 A Burst 4-in-1 ESC: Engineering Model (Job #64)

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Early Warnings

**System partition.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It sends bidirectional DShot600 to the 4-in-1 ESC, which uses its own MCU (AM32 or BLHeli_32 class, e.g. STM32G071 or AT32F421). The 48 kHz PWM and commutation happen on the ESC MCU, not the H743. The two sections below are modeled separately for that reason.

**Baseline assumptions.** Replace these with your BOM values; every equation is parametric.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | V_bus | 6S, 22.2 V nominal, 25.2 V max |

| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |

**Feasibility flags. Read these before committing to the layout.**
1. **80 A burst on a 20×20 single-FET-per-switch board is a short-burst rating only (seconds), not a continuous one.** The math below shows this explicitly. Professional 80 A-class 4-in-1s are typically 30.5×30.5 or larger with paralleled FETs.
2. **40 V FETs on 6S leave about 15 V of margin.** Inductive commutation spikes can exceed that with poor layout. Consider 60 V parts if ringing measurements are marginal.
3. **The schematic netlists below are reference architecture.** They are not a verified, DRC-clean production design. Pinouts must be checked against the exact datasheets and package variants you buy.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation topology
Assume 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification and complementary PWM (the AM32 default). In each 60° electrical sector:
- **Phase A (PWM leg):** the high side conducts for fraction D, the low side for 1−D, and both carry I.
- **Phase B (return leg):** the low side conducts continuously and carries I.
- **Phase C:** floating.

Conduction loss per motor at any instant:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{hot} = 2\,I^2 R_{hot}
$$

This is independent of duty cycle, which is a useful property of synchronous 6-step drive.

Add the copper path. Assume 2 oz copper traces, vias, and phase pads at R_Cu ≈ 0.5 mΩ per current path, so R_Cu,path ≈ 1.0 mΩ:

$$
P_{cond,total} = I^2\,(2R_{hot} + R_{Cu,path})
$$

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + k\,(T_j - 25)\right], \quad k \approx 0.005\ \text{/K}
$$

- At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.3 × 1.5 = **1.95 mΩ**.
- This feeds back into temperature, so solve iteratively, or use the hot value as a conservative bound (done here).

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| Term | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction per motor | 2 × 80² × 1.95 mΩ | **24.96 W** |
| Copper conduction per motor | 80² × 1.0 mΩ | **6.4 W** |
| Total per motor | — | **31.4 W** |
| Average per FET | 24.96 W / 6 (role rotates every sector) | **4.16 W** |

The "average per FET" figure is valid because electrical frequency is much faster than die thermal time constants. Example: 14 poles at 30,000 rpm gives f_e = 3.5 kHz, while die τ is in the ms range.

Peak instantaneous per FET (continuous-on role) is 80² × 1.95 mΩ = 12.5 W. This is relevant only to Z_th at very low rpm or stall; see §3.4.
