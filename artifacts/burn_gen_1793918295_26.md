# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Loss, Thermal, and Bidirectional DShot600 Architecture (Job #38)

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

### 0.1 Architectural clarification
The STM32H743 is the flight controller. It does not generate the 48 kHz motor PWM. Each ESC channel (or a shared ESC MCU) runs AM32 or BLHeli_32 on its own MCU, and that MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The resulting split of responsibilities is:

| Domain | Device | Responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| FC | STM32H743 @ 400–480 MHz | DShot600 bidirectional, eRPM telemetry, harmonic notch |
| ESC (×4 or ×1) | e.g. AT32F421 / STM32G071 running AM32 | 6-step commutation, 48 kHz PWM, dead-time, current sense |
| Power stage | 3-phase gate driver + 24× N-MOSFETs | Conduction and switching |

### 0.3 Feasibility warning (stated up front)
A true 80 A per motor burst on a "nano" (20×20 or 30×30 mm) 4-in-1 is thermally marginal to infeasible beyond a few seconds. The numbers below show this. Many commercial "80 A" ratings on small boards are peak, single-motor, or marketing figures. Treat the model as the tool that sets a defensible burst rating.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_ds = 0.90 × (1 + 0.0065 × 85) = **1.40 mΩ**

### 1.2 6-step, synchronous complementary PWM
During any 60° sector, two phase legs carry current I.
- **PWM'd leg:** the high-side FET conducts for D and the low-side FET for (1−D). The leg's combined conduction is I²R regardless of D.
- **Static leg:** the low-side FET is held on, giving I²R.

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

### 1.3 Ripple contribution

$$\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L\,f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2\times0.25}{20\mu\times48k} = 6.56\ \text{A}_{pp}$$

ΔI²/12 = 3.6 A², which is under 0.06% of I² (6400 A²). It is negligible at 80 A.

### 1.4 Result per motor at 80 A

$$P_{cond} = 2\times6400\times1.40\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{17.9\ W}$$

Each FET conducts about 120° of the electrical cycle, so the average conduction loss per FET is about 17.9 / 6 ≈ **3.0 W**. Phases rotate faster than the die thermal time constant above ~3k eRPM, so averaging is valid.

### 1.5 Parasitic resistance (often larger than the FETs)

| Path | R | P @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Shunt (bus-side, single-shunt) | 0.5 mΩ | 80² × 0.5 m × ≈1 (duty-weighted ~D) ≈ **1.6–3.2 W** |
| Phase pad + copper (2 oz, 4 layers parallel, ~8 mm) | ~0.3 mΩ ×2 legs | **3.8 W** |
| Battery bus copper (shared, 4 motors) | ~0.15 mΩ | at 4 × I·D ≈ 160 A avg → **3.8 W** (board total) |
