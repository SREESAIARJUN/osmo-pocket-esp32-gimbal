## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 0.90 × 1.45 ≈ **1.31 mΩ**. The design value of **1.35 mΩ** includes about 0.04 mΩ of package and solder.

### 1.2 Ripple-corrected RMS current

With center-aligned PWM, the motor sees an equivalent ripple of:

$$\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{LL}\,f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{15\,\mu\text{H}\times 48\,\text{kHz}} = 8.75\ \text{A}_{pp}\ (\text{worst case at }D=0.5)$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 6400 + 6.4 \;\Rightarrow\; +0.1\,\%$$

Ripple is negligible at 48 kHz. This is one justification for 48 kHz over 24 kHz, where the term would be 4× larger.

### 1.3 Bridge conduction (6-step, synchronous)

In each 60° sector, current flows through two legs:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side conducts for D, and its complementary low-side conducts for 1−D. The net is always one R_DS in path.
- **Return leg:** the low-side is on 100%.

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.35\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{17.3\ W}$$
