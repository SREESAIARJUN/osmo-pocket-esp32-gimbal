# STM32H743 FC + Integrated 4-in-1 ESC (Job #17): Engineering Model

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and a Feasibility Warning

These assumptions matter more than any equation below, so they come first.

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Architecture | The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. The ESC runs on a **separate** MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071) with AM32 or BLHeli_32 firmware. The H743 does not commutate motors, and the 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCU. |
| Bus voltage | 6S, 25.2 V max (22.2 V nominal) |
| MOSFET (per switch) | 40 V N-ch, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN. R_DS(on) = 1.2 mΩ typ / 1.5 mΩ max at 25 °C. Q_g = 50 nC, Q_oss = 40 nC, t_r = t_f = 20 ns at R_g ≈ 5 Ω. |
| Gate driver | 3-phase 6-channel driver (FD6288Q class), 10–12 V gate drive |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal, complementary (synchronous) PWM. Dead time 200 ns. |
| Burst profile | 80 A per motor, all 4 motors simultaneously (worst case) |
| Board | 30.5×30.5 mm stack class, 8-layer, 2 oz outer / 1 oz inner |

> ⚠️ **Feasibility:** A true "nano" (20×20) board **cannot** sustain 4 × 80 A. Even 30.5×30.5 handles 80 A only as a seconds-long burst. Section 3 quantifies this. Treat "80 A" as a transient rating that must be backed by firmware current limiting.

> These calculations use representative datasheet values. Replace them with your selected parts' guaranteed max values and validate with thermocouple and IR testing before release.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C using max R_DS: 1.5 mΩ × 1.5 = **2.25 mΩ**. At typical: **1.8 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current path

In 6-step commutation, two phases conduct at any instant:
- During the PWM on-time: high-side FET (phase A) → motor → low-side FET (phase B).
- During the off-time with synchronous rectification: the complementary FET conducts instead.

So exactly **2 FETs carry I_phase** at all times (excluding dead time). For a flat-top current, I_RMS ≈ I_DC-link-equivalent phase current.

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) + I^2 R_{Cu}$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| Term | Typ (1.8 mΩ) | Worst (2.25 mΩ) |
|---|---|---|
| 2·I²·R_DS | 2 × 6400 × 1.8 mΩ = **23.0 W** | **28.8 W** |
| Copper (R_Cu ≈ 0.15 mΩ per path) | 0.96 W | 0.96 W |
| **P_cond / motor** | **≈ 24.0 W** | **≈ 29.8 W** |

### 1.4 Distribution across the six FETs

Each of the 6 FETs conducts ~1/3 of the electrical cycle. The average loss is roughly 24 W / 6 ≈ **4 W per FET**, but the **instantaneous** loss in a conducting FET is I²R ≈ 11.5–14.4 W. That instantaneous value is relevant at low eRPM, when a commutation step lasts longer than the FET's thermal time constant. At low-speed high-current conditions (prop wash, punch-out from hover), design for the instantaneous figure.

### 1.5 Self-consistent junction temperature

Conduction loss raises T_j, which raises R_DS, which raises loss. Solve by iteration:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + P(T_j)\,R_{th},\qquad P(T_j)=I^2R_{DS,25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Closed form:

$$T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + I^2R_{25}R_{th}(1-25\alpha)}{1 - \alpha I^2 R_{25} R_{th}}$$

**Stability condition:** α I² R₂₅ R_th < 1. At 80 A, R₂₅ = 1.2 mΩ, R_th = 40 K/W (FET to ambient, no airflow), the product is 0.005 × 6400 × 0.0012 × 40 = **1.54 > 1**. This is **thermal runaway** with no steady state. 80 A is therefore *only* survivable as a transient, or with prop-wash airflow bringing effective R_th below ~25 K/W.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per motor, only one half-bridge is hard-switched at a time: the PWM leg. The other active leg is static for the whole commutation step.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard switching)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = \tfrac12 (25.2)(80)(40\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance (Q_oss)

$$P_{oss} = V_{bus} Q_{oss} f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 40\text{ nC} \times 48\text{ kHz} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F I \,(2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

### 2.4 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = V_{bus} Q_{rr} f_{sw} \approx 25.2 \times 60\text{ nC} \times 48\text{ kHz} = 0.073\ \text{W}$$

Q_rr is at high di/dt.

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in driver and R_g, not the FET)

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{ nC} \times 12 \times 48\text{ kHz} = 0.058\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Sensitivity notes
- 48 kHz vs. 24 kHz doubles every term in 2.1–2.5 (~3.3 W vs. ~1.65 W per motor). In return you get lower current ripple and quieter motors.
- Dead time is the second-largest switching term. Reducing it from 200 to 100 ns saves ~0.6 W per motor, but only if the driver's propagation skew allows it without shoot-through.

### 2.7 Loss summary at 80 A burst

| Loss | Per motor (typ) | Per motor (worst) | 4 motors (worst) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 24.0 W | 29.8 W | 119 W |
| Switching (V–I) | 1.94 W | 2.5 W* | 10 W |
| Dead time | 1.23 W | 1.23 W | 4.9 W |
| Q_oss + Q_rr | 0.12 W | 0.15 W | 0.6 W |
| Gate drive | 0.06 W | 0.06 W | 0.24 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 27.4 W** | **≈ 33.7 W** | **≈ 135 W** |

\*Switching times lengthen at hot T_j and with gate-driver supply sag.

Efficiency check: 4 × 80 A × ~22 V ≈ 7 kW of input power, so ~1.9 % ESC loss. That efficiency is reasonable. The problem is entirely **power density**: 135 W in ~10 cm².

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network (per FET, Foster/Cauer simplified)

```
P_FET ──► [Rth_jc 1.2 K/W, Cth_j ~0.05 J/K] ──► case
       ──► [Rth_c-pcb 0.5 K/W (solder+vias)] ──► PCB node
PCB node: Cth_board ≈ 7 J/K (shared, all 24 FETs)
       ──► [Rth_pcb-amb] ──► ambient
            still air:      ~12 K/W (whole board)
            prop-wash:      ~2.5–4 K/W
```

**Board heat capacity:** For a 30.5×30.5 mm board with 8 layers and FETs/copper, the mass is ~7 g. Using c_FR4 ≈ 1.1 J/g·K and c_Cu = 0.385 J/g·K, the weighted value is **C_board ≈ 6–8 J/K**.

### 3.2 Junction rise above the board (fast, τ < 50 ms)

For a conducting FET at instantaneous 14.4 W (worst case):

$$\Delta T_{j\text{-}pcb} = 14.4 \times (1.2 + 0.5) = \mathbf{24.5\ K}$$

### 3.3 Board rise (slow, adiabatic during burst)

$$\frac{dT_{pcb}}{dt} = \frac{P_{total} - (T_{pcb}-T_{amb})/R_{th,amb}}{C_{board}}$$

The adiabatic initial slope is 135 W / 7 J/K = **19 K/s** worst case (typ 110 W, about 16 K/s).

Solution:

$$T_{pcb}(t) = T_{amb} + P R_{th}\,(1 - e^{-t/\tau}),\quad \tau = R_{th} C$$
