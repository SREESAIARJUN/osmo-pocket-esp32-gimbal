> **Feasibility caution (read first)**
> - **Package current limit.** 80 A per motor exceeds the package rating of most DFN3.3×3.3 MOSFETs (typically 40–70 A, clip/bond limited). A true 20×20 "nano" footprint cannot dissipate the roughly 86 W calculated below for more than a few seconds.
> - **Rating recommendation.** Treat 80 A as a burst rating (5 s or less with forward airflow). Size the continuous rating at about 40–45 A. If 80 A is a firm requirement, move to a 30.5 mm board with DFN5×6 FETs.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.0055\text{–}0.007\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 110 °C with α = 0.006:

R_DS = 1.0 × (1 + 0.006 × 85) = **1.51 mΩ**

### 1.2 Current path in synchronous 6-step drive

Within any 60° sector, the active phase PWMs complementarily and one low-side FET of the return phase is held on. Current therefore always flows through exactly two FETs in series (HS or its sync-rectifying LS, plus the return LS), apart from dead time.

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j) + I_{rms}^2 R_{Cu}$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

- **MOSFET conduction:** 80² × 2 × 1.51 mΩ = 6400 × 3.02 mΩ = **19.3 W per motor**.
- **Per-FET average:** each FET conducts about 1/3 of the electrical cycle (sync rectification splits the PWM'd phase's duty). The worst instantaneous FET carries the full 80 A at about 9.7 W. The time-averaged per-FET loss is about 19.3 / 6 × 2 ≈ **6.4 W**.
- **Copper loss:** a phase trace on 2 oz copper (70 µm), 6 mm wide, 15 mm long, paralleled on 2 layers:

  R = ρL/A = (1.72×10⁻⁸ × 0.015) / (6×10⁻³ × 70×10⁻⁶ × 2) ≈ 0.31 mΩ

  This gives 80² × 0.31 mΩ ≈ **2.0 W per motor**, before temperature correction (+0.393 %/K).

### 1.4 Iterative T_j solution

R_DS and T_j are coupled, so solve them together:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_{board} + P_{FET}\big(R_{DS}(T_j^{(k)})\big)\cdot R_{\theta JB}$$

Iterate until |ΔT_j| < 0.5 K. With R_θJB ≈ 4 K/W (DFN3.3 on a 2 oz pad with a via array):

| Iteration | T_j (°C) | R_DS (mΩ) | P_FET peak (W) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 25 | 1.00 | 6.4 |
| 1 | 25 + T_board + … | … | … |

This iteration only converges if T_board is bounded, which is governed by Section 4.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd half-bridge switches; the return-leg FET is static. Per motor:

$$P_{sw} = \underbrace{\tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}}_{\text{V–I overlap}} + \underbrace{Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}}_{\text{C}_{oss}} + \underbrace{Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}}_{\text{reverse recovery}} + \underbrace{V_{SD}\,I\,(2t_{dead})\,f_{sw}}_{\text{body diode}} + \underbrace{2Q_gV_{drv}f_{sw}}_{\text{gate}}$$

The gate-drive term is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FETs.

| Term | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 20 ns × 48 kHz | **0.968 W** |
| C_oss | 30 nC × 25.2 × 48 kHz | 0.036 W |
| Q_rr | 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 kHz | 0.048 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.8 × 80 × 400 ns × 48 kHz | **1.229 W** |
| Gate charge | 2 × 30 nC × 10 V × 48 kHz | 0.029 W |
| **Total switching per motor** | | **≈ 2.31 W** |

Two findings stand out:
- **Dead-time loss exceeds overlap loss.** Trimming dead time from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per motor. The safe floor is set by FD6288Q propagation delay plus FET t_d(off).
- **Frequency scaling.** Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it would be about 1.15 W, but 48 kHz is justified for low-inductance nano motors, which need it for current ripple:

  $$\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}D(1-D)}{L_{ph-ph}f_{sw}}$$

  With L ≈ 15 µH and D = 0.5: ΔI ≈ 25.2 × 0.25 / (15 µH × 48 kHz) ≈ **8.75 A pk-pk**. At 24 kHz this doubles to 17.5 A, which raises RMS conduction loss.
