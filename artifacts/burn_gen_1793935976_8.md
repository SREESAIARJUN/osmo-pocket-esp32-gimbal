**Architecture note.** The STM32H743 is the flight-controller MCU. It does not commutate the motors. Each of the 4 ESC channels has its own MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, for example an STM32G071 or AT32F421. These ESC MCUs generate the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to them.

**Physical-feasibility flag.** 80 A × 4 motors on a nano/AIO board is a **burst-only** rating. Section 3 shows the board cannot hold this load continuously, and it quantifies the allowable burst duration. Any spec sheet should state "80 A burst (≤ X s)", not "80 A continuous".

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Conduction topology (6-step trapezoidal, synchronous rectification)

At any instant, two phases carry current:
- one high-side FET (HS) is PWM'd at duty D;
- its complementary low-side FET (LS) conducts during (1−D), which is synchronous rectification;
- the low-side FET of the return phase stays fully ON.

So the motor current always flows through **exactly two R_DS(on) channels in series**, regardless of D. The exception is the dead-time intervals, which are handled in §2.4.

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 RMS current including PWM ripple

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph-ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

Worked example, with L_ph-ph = 20 µH and the worst case near D = 0.5, so (V_bus − V_bemf) ≈ 12.6 V:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{12.6 \times 0.5}{20\,\mu\text{H} \times 48\,\text{kHz}} = 6.6\,\text{A} \;\Rightarrow\; I_{rms}^2 = 6400 + 3.6 = 6403.6\,\text{A}^2$$

At 48 kHz the ripple term is negligible (< 0.1 %). This is one benefit of running 48 kHz instead of 24 kHz, where the ripple term is about 4× larger.

### 1.3 Temperature dependence of R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left(\frac{T_j + 273}{298}\right)^{\kappa}, \quad \kappa \approx 2.0\text{–}2.5 \;(⚠\text{ fit to the datasheet curve})$$

With κ = 2.3:

| T_j | 25 °C | 75 °C | 100 °C | 125 °C |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R_DS(on) | 1.00 mΩ | 1.37 mΩ | 1.58 mΩ | 1.80 mΩ |
