# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC: Design Model (Job #59)

## 0. Architecture Assumptions

This is a reference design with explicit assumptions. Every number below derives from the parameter table in §0.2. Replace the placeholder MOSFET and driver values with datasheet figures from your chosen parts before committing the layout.

### 0.1 Architectural Correction

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. A 4-in-1 ESC uses one ESC MCU per motor (AM32 or BLHeli_32 class, e.g. STM32G071 or AT32F421), which generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The H743:

- runs ArduPilot,
- emits bidirectional DShot600 frames,
- captures the eRPM telemetry returned on the same wire.

The thermal and switching model therefore applies to the ESC power stage. The DMA/timer model applies to the H743.

```
 6S LiPo ─┬─ TVS + bulk caps ─┬─ 4× [ESC MCU → 3-ph gate driver → 6 or 12 MOSFETs] → M1..M4
          │                    └─ VBAT sense / current shunt ─→ H743 ADC
          └─ Buck 5V/3A ─ LDO 3.3V ─→ H743, IMU, baro, OSD
 H743 TIM1 CH1..CH4 ⇄ (bidir DShot600, 1 wire each) ⇄ ESC MCU 1..4
```

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current Path, Six-Step Commutation with Synchronous Rectification

At any instant, two phases conduct:

- The **PWM-ing high side** carries I for fraction D. Its complementary low side carries I (synchronous freewheel) for fraction 1−D.
- The **opposing phase low side** is on continuously during that sector.

In every sub-interval, current flows through exactly two R_DS(on) in series. Assuming flat-top phase current:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I_{pk}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

### 1.2 Per-Device Distribution

Averaged over the six sectors, each FET conducts for 1/3 of an electrical cycle. Within that third, high-side FETs conduct for D and their sync partner for 1−D. The opposing low side is on for the full sector. Per device:

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,D\,I^2R_{ds}, \qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,[(1-D) + 1]\,I^2R_{ds}$$

> **Design consequence:** The low-side FETs run hotter (factor (2−D) vs D = 1.1 vs 0.9 at D=0.9). Give them more copper and vias.

### 1.3 Numerical Evaluation

Evaluated at T_j = 110 °C. This is the first iterate; it is closed in §3.4.

- R_ds = 1.0 mΩ × [1 + 0.0045 × 85] = **1.383 mΩ**
- P_cond,ESC = 2 × 6400 × 1.383e-3 = **17.7 W per ESC channel**
- Per FET:
  - P_HS = (1/3)(0.9)(6400)(1.383e-3) = **2.66 W**
  - P_LS = (1/3)(1.1)(6400)(1.383e-3) = **3.25 W**

With paralleled FETs (2 per switch position, 24 FETs total): R_eff = R_ds/2, so P_cond,ESC halves to **8.85 W**, and per-die loss drops by 4×. For a true 80 A rating in nano form factor, paralleling is strongly recommended.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing half-bridge switches at f_sw. The commutation-rate edges (≈ e-RPM/60 × 6) are negligible by comparison.

### 2.1 Hard-Switching Overlap (High-Side FET)

$$P_{ov} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

= 0.5 × 22.2 × 80 × 30e-9 × 48e3 = **1.28 W**

### 2.2 Reverse Recovery

The sync FET body diode conducts during dead time, then is hard-commutated off:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw}$$

= 50e-9 × 22.2 × 48e3 = **0.053 W**

### 2.3 Output-Capacitance Energy

Two FETs per leg:

$$P_{oss} = 2 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss(er)} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw}$$

= 1e-9 × 492.8 × 48e3 = **0.024 W**

### 2.4 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f\,I\,(2 t_{dt})\,f_{sw}$$

= 0.8 × 80 × 100e-9 × 48e3 = **0.307 W**

### 2.5 Gate Drive

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET:

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g\,V_{GS}\,f_{sw}$$

= 2 × 40e-9 × 10 × 48e3 = **0.038 W**

### 2.6 Switching Totals per ESC Channel

P_sw,ESC = 1.28 + 0.053 + 0.024 + 0.307 = **1.66 W** (FET-side), plus 0.04 W in the driver.

Sensitivity notes:

- P_ov scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz, the switching total falls to ≈0.85 W.
- At 96 kHz it rises to ≈3.3 W.
- 48 kHz is a reasonable compromise between acoustic noise, current ripple at low-inductance motors, and loss.
