## 1. Field-Oriented Control (inner current loop)

### 1.1 Transforms

Electrical angle from the encoder:
$$\theta_e = p\,(\theta_m - \theta_{\text{off}}) \mod 2\pi$$

Clarke transform (amplitude-invariant, with $i_a+i_b+i_c=0$):
$$i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}(i_a + 2 i_b)$$

Park transform:
$$\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}$$

The inverse Park transform is the transpose of this matrix.

**Delay compensation.** PWM update happens one sample after measurement, so the effective transport delay is $1.5\,T_s$. Use this compensated angle for the inverse Park only:
$$\theta_e^{\text{out}} = \theta_e + 1.5\,\omega_e T_s$$

### 1.2 Plant and decoupled PI current control

The motor model in the dq frame:
$$v_d = R i_d + L\dot i_d - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad v_q = R i_q + L\dot i_q + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e\lambda_m$$

The control law adds cross-coupling and back-EMF feedforward to a PI term:
$$v_d^* = \underbrace{K_p e_d + K_i\!\int e_d}_{PI} - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad v_q^* = K_p e_q + K_i\!\int e_q + \omega_e(L i_d + \lambda_m)$$

**Pole-zero cancellation tuning.** The plant is $1/(R+Ls)$. Choosing
$$K_p = L\,\omega_c,\qquad K_i = R\,\omega_c$$
