# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #50): Systems Architecture Package

This package contains the electrothermal loss model, the bidirectional DShot600 timer and DMA plan for ArduPilot, a representative netlist, and the thermal verdict.

---

## 0. Architecture Assumptions

The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. ArduPilot has no ESC firmware, and running four sensorless 6-step controllers on the flight-control MCU is not a supported configuration. The architecture is therefore:

| Block | Device | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller (FC) | STM32H743VIH6, 480 MHz | ArduPilot; sends bidirectional DShot600 to the ESCs |
| ESC MCU ×4 | AT32F421K8U7 (or STM32G071) | AM32 firmware, 48 kHz complementary PWM, eRPM telemetry |
| Gate driver ×4 | FD6288Q (3-phase, bootstrap) | Drives 6 N-FETs per channel |
| Power stage ×4 | 6 × 40 V N-MOSFET, ~1.0 mΩ typ at 25 °C, PDFN 5×6 | 3-phase bridge |
| Supply | 2–6S LiPo (25.2 V maximum) | — |

**Burst rating.** I assume "80 A burst" means 80 A per motor channel for a short burst. Section 3 shows this is thermally achievable only for about 1–2 s with all four channels loaded on a 30.5 × 30.5 mm board, and for less on a 20 × 20 "nano" board. Treat it as a marketing burst, not a design point.

All device parameters below are representative placeholders. Replace them with values from your selected MOSFET's datasheet before sign-off.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Switching scheme
AM32 uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. In any 60° sector:
- **Phase A, high-side FET:** PWM at duty D.
- **Phase A, low-side FET:** complementary, conducting for (1 − D), apart from dead time.
- **Phase B, low-side FET:** on 100 % (current return path).
- **Phase C:** floating; used for back-EMF zero-crossing detection.

Phase current I_ph is roughly constant over a PWM period (motor inductance ≫ 1/f_sw).

### 1.2 Per-channel conduction loss
Over one sector, the current always flows through exactly two FETs in series:

$$P_{cond,sector} = I^2 R_{ds}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds}\cdot 1 = 2I^2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Every sector looks the same, so the result holds over the full electrical cycle:

$$\boxed{P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j)}\qquad\text{(independent of duty cycle)}$$

The loss is distributed unevenly across the bridge. Per electrical cycle:

| FET | Mean-square current |
|---|---|
| High-side FET of a phase | $I^2 D/3$ |
| Low-side FET of a phase | $I^2[(1-D)/3 + 1/3]$ |

At low throttle the low-side FETs run hottest: up to $\tfrac{2}{3}I^2R$ each, versus $\tfrac{1}{3}I^2R$ average.

### 1.3 Temperature dependence of R_ds(on)
$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha(T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0067\ /\mathrm{K}\ \ (\approx1.5\times \text{at }100^\circ\mathrm{C})$$

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A
With R_ds,25 = 1.0 mΩ:

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.0 mΩ | 12.8 W | 51.2 W |
| 100 °C | 1.5 mΩ | **19.2 W** | **76.8 W** |
| 125 °C | 1.67 mΩ | 21.4 W | 85.5 W |

### 1.5 Copper and connection losses
Phase and battery paths: assume ~0.25 mΩ effective per current path (2 oz × 6 layers, vias, solder pads).

$$P_{Cu} \approx 2I^2R_{Cu} = 2(6400)(0.00025) = 3.2\ \mathrm{W/ch}$$

If a current-sense shunt is fitted (0.25 mΩ, total battery current, 4 × 80 A = 320 A):

$$P_{shunt} = 320^2 \times 0.00025 = 25.6\ \mathrm{W}$$

At this current a shunt is not acceptable. Use a Hall sensor (e.g., ACS7xx), or accept that current sensing is not valid at full burst.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (f_sw = 48 kHz)

Only one half-bridge switches per sector. Assumptions: V_bus = 25.2 V, I = 80 A.

### 2.1 Hard-switching overlap loss
$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

FD6288Q at ~10 V gate drive with R_g ≈ 10 Ω gives t_r + t_f ≈ 20 ns:

$$P_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(20\times10^{-9})(48\,000) = 0.97\ \mathrm{W}$$

The rise and fall times come from the Miller plateau:

$$t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}R_{g,tot}}{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}$$

With Q_gd = 8 nC, R_g,tot = 12 Ω, V_drv = 10 V and V_pl = 3.5 V, t_r ≈ 15 ns. The fall time is similar, so 20 ns combined is optimistic; 30 ns is conservative and gives 1.45 W.

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction
$$P_{dt} = V_f\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(2\cdot100\,\mathrm{ns})(48\,000) = 0.61\ \mathrm{W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery
$$P_{rr} = V_{bus}Q_{rr}f_{sw} = 25.2(50\,\mathrm{nC})(48\,000) = 0.06\ \mathrm{W}$$

There is a second-order effect on turn-on loss of about +0.1 W.

### 2.4 Output capacitance
$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,er}V^2 f_{sw}\times2 = (1.5\,\mathrm{nF})(635)(48\,000) = 0.05\ \mathrm{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive
This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs:

$$P_g = Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw}\times 2 = 50\,\mathrm{nC}\cdot10\cdot48\,000\cdot2 = 0.05\ \mathrm{W}$$
