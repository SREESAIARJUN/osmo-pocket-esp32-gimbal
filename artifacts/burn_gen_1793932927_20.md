## 0. Read This First: Architecture Assumptions and Feasibility

| Parameter | Assumption (change these and re-run the model) |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V (full), 22.2 V nominal |
| ESC topology | 4 independent 3-phase bridges, 6 FETs each (24 total), one ESC MCU per channel (AM32/BLHeli_32-class) |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal, complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM on the high side |
| PWM frequency | f_sw = 48 kHz |
| "80 A burst" | 80 A **phase** current per motor at ~100% duty, for a short duration (t ≤ a few seconds) |
| MOSFET (representative 40 V class) | R_ds(on),25 = 1.0 mΩ @ V_gs = 10 V, Q_g = 60 nC, Q_gd = 6 nC, Q_rr = 40 nC, C_oss(er) = 1.5 nF, V_SD = 0.8 V, R_θJC(bottom) ≈ 1.0 K/W |
| Board | ~25 × 25 mm ("nano" / 20 × 20 mounting), 6-layer, 2 oz copper on all layers |
| FC MCU | STM32H743 @ 480 MHz, APB1/APB2 timer clocks = 240 MHz |

**Feasibility warning.** A 25 × 25 mm AIO cannot sustain 4 × 80 A. The model below shows:
* Roughly 90 W total dissipation during the burst.
* Only about 10 W continuous heat-rejection capability.

So the "80 A" rating is a **thermally time-limited burst of about 1–2 s**. Realistic continuous current is about **25–30 A per channel**. Treat any marketing figure above that as a burst spec and say so on the datasheet.

The STM32H743 does **not** drive the bridges directly. It sends DShot to four ESC MCUs. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by those ESC MCUs.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)] , \quad \alpha \approx 0.0053\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At a design-point T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{ds}(110) = 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega \times (1 + 0.0053 \times 85) \approx 1.45\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Current paths in 6-step with synchronous rectification

In any 60° sector, two phases conduct and one floats.

* **PWM leg:** current flows through the HS FET for D·T_sw and through the LS FET for (1−D)·T_sw.
* **Return leg:** the LS FET conducts continuously for the whole sector.

At any instant, exactly two channels of R_ds are in series with the motor current:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \, R_{ds}\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{ph}^2 R_{ds} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Add copper path resistance (bus planes, phase pads, shunt). Assume R_Cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ per channel and R_shunt = 0.25 mΩ (shared return):

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2\,[2R_{ds} + R_{Cu} + R_{sh}]$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| Term | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| FETs | 2 × 80² × 1.45 mΩ | **18.56 W** |
| Copper | 80² × 0.30 mΩ | 1.92 W |
| Shunt | 80² × 0.25 mΩ | 1.60 W |
| **Total conduction per channel** | | **22.1 W** |

### 1.4 Per-FET average

Over one electrical cycle, each FET carries current for 120° (1/3 of the cycle). At D ≈ 1:

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx \tfrac{1}{3} I_{ph}^2 R_{ds} = \tfrac{1}{3} \times 9.28 \approx 3.1\ \text{W}$$

The instantaneous peak is 9.28 W.

At eRPM above roughly 10k, the electrical period (< 6 ms) is far shorter than the die-to-board thermal time constant. The average value therefore governs T_j. At stall or very low eRPM, design for the 9.3 W peak instead.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches, so there is one hard-switched commutation pair per channel.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss

Assume a driver with about 1.5 A plateau current, with R_g tuned for EMI:

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_g} \Rightarrow \text{allow } t_r = t_f = 25\ \text{ns (with layout parasitics)}$$

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 50\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{2.42\ W}$$

### 2.2 Secondary switching terms

| Loss mechanism | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse recovery (LS body diode) | Q_rr · V · f = 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.05 W |
| C_oss charge/discharge (2 FETs) | 2 × ½ C_oss V² f
