## 0. Feasibility Findings

Two points will shape the rest of the design.

1. **The STM32H743 does not drive the power stage.** ArduPilot has no field-oriented control or commutation firmware for the H7. The realistic architecture is:
   **H743 (ArduPilot) → bi-directional DShot600 → 4× ESC MCU (e.g., AT32F421 running AM32) → 3-phase gate driver → 6 MOSFETs per motor (24 total).**
   The 48 kHz figure is therefore the ESC MCU's PWM frequency, not an H743 timer.

2. **80 A per channel on a nano (20×20 / 25.5×25.5 mm) board is not thermally sustainable.** Section 3 shows roughly 160 W of dissipation with all four channels at 80 A. Under adiabatic heating the board survives about **1–2 s** before the MOSFETs approach their limit. Specify 80 A as a **≤1 s single-channel burst**, or move to a 30×30 board with 5×6 mm FETs.

## 2. Loss Model (Per Channel, 6-Step Trapezoidal)

During any commutation sector, one leg PWMs complementarily (HS ↔ LS) and a second leg holds its LS on. At every instant, current I flows through **two** conducting channels: the PWM leg's active FET plus the static LS.

### 2.1 Conduction Loss with Temperature Feedback

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Phase-current ripple at 48 kHz with typical motor inductance (≈10–30 µH) is small, so I_rms ≈ I_ph.

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond (80 A) |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.80 mΩ | 23.0 W |
| 100 °C | 2.61 mΩ | 33.4 W |
| 125 °C | 2.88 mΩ | **36.9 W** |

The design must be solved self-consistently, because loss raises T_j and T_j raises R_DS. Section 3.3 iterates this.

### 2.2 Dead-Time (Body-Diode) Conduction

Two dead-time intervals occur per PWM period:

$$P_{dt} = 2\,V_f\,I_{ph}\,t_{dt}\,f_{sw} = 2(0.8)(80)(150\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = \mathbf{0.92\ W}$$
