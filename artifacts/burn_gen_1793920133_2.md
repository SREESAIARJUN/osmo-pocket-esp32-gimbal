# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model (Job #39)

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

I have no prior design data for Job #39, so every parameter below is an explicit assumption. Replace these values with your MOSFET datasheet, stack-up, and layout data. The equations are written so you can substitute directly.

**Architecture correction.** In a standard ArduPilot design, the STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. It runs ArduPilot and generates bi-directional DShot600. Each ESC channel has its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32 that performs commutation:

```
[STM32H743 / ArduPilot] --DShot600 bidir (4 lines)--> [4x ESC MCU (e.g. STM32G071/AT32F421, AM32)]
                                                         --> [3-phase gate driver] --> [6x N-FET] --> motor
```

If you intend the H743 to drive the power stages directly (FOC), that requires custom firmware outside ArduPilot. Tell me and I'll re-derive for that case.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

**Current path.** In 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation with synchronous rectification, two FETs are always in the current path:
- During the on-time: the high side of phase A and the low side of phase B.
- During the off-time: the low side of A (complementary) and the low side of B.

So the channel conduction loss is approximately independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

**Worked example at T_j = 100 °C:**
- R_DS(on) = 1.0 × (1 + 0.005 × 75) = 1.375 mΩ
- P_cond = 80² × 2 × 1.375 mΩ = **17.6 W per channel**

**Copper loss.** Assume about 0.5 mΩ of effective trace and via resistance per channel:
- P_Cu = 6400 × 0.5 mΩ = **3.2 W per channel**

**Electrothermal feedback (iterate to converge):**

$$T_j = T_{board} + P_{FET}\,R_{\theta JC}, \qquad R_{DS(on)} \rightarrow \text{recompute}$$

The loop runs away (thermal runaway) if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T} \cdot R_\theta \ge 1, \qquad \text{i.e. } I^2 \cdot 2R_{25}\,\alpha\, R_\theta \ge 1$$

With R_θ ≈ 11 K/W to ambient: 6400 × 2e-3 × 0.005 × 11 ≈ 0.70. This is stable, but with little margin. That margin is the main reason the 80 A rating can only be a **burst**, not continuous.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per channel, only one leg is hard-switching at a time.

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw | ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 30 ns × 48 kHz = **1.45 W** |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss · V · f_sw | 40 nC × 25.2 × 48k = **0.048 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw | 30 nC × 25.2 × 48k = **0.036 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | 2 · V_f · I · t_dt · f_sw | 2 × 0.8 × 80 × 50 ns × 48k = **0.31 W** |
| Gate drive (2 active FETs) | 2 · Q_g · V_drv · f_sw | **0.058 W** (dissipated in the driver/regulator) |

**P_sw ≈ 1.85 W per channel.**

**Switching-time sanity check.** The transition time is roughly t ≈ Q_gd / I_g. With Q_gd ≈ 12 nC and about 0.8 A of effective gate current, t ≈ 15 ns. If your driver (e.g. FD6288Q-class) sources less current, scale t_r and t_f, and therefore P_sw, linearly.

**Frequency trade-off.** Raising f_sw from 24 kHz to 48 kHz roughly doubles the switching loss but reduces current ripple:

$$\Delta I \approx \frac{V(1-D)D}{L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

At 48 kHz, switching is still only about 9% of total loss, so the frequency choice is acceptable.

## 3. Total Loss Budget at 80 A Burst

| | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 17.6 W | 70.4 W |
| Switching + dead time | 1.85 W | 7.4 W |
| Copper | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 22.7 W** | **≈ 90.6 W** |

At 80 A per channel, power delivered is about 2 kW per motor at high duty cycle, so efficiency is around 98.9%. The problem is that about 90 W of heat is concentrated on a 9 cm² board.
