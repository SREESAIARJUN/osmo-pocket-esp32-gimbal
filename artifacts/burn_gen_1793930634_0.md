## 0. System Architecture

```
 6S LiPo (25.2 V max) ──► Bulk caps ──► 4× 3-phase bridges (24 N-FETs)
                                              ▲
                          4× ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32 or BLHeli_32-class)
                          + 3-phase gate drivers (e.g., FD6288Q-class)
                                              ▲  bidirectional DShot600 (1 wire per ESC)
                          STM32H743 @ 400–480 MHz running ArduPilot
```

The H743 does **not** perform motor commutation. ArduPilot sends DShot commands and receives eRPM telemetry over one GPIO per motor. Each ESC channel has its own commutation MCU. This is the standard AIO topology. It keeps the H743's timers free for DShot, and it is the only configuration ArduPilot supports.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (Per ESC Channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

Assume 6-step commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. At any instant, current flows through **two FET positions** in series:
- the PWM leg (HS during t_on, LS during t_off), and
- the static low-side of the return phase.

With synchronous rectification, both positions carry I_ph for the whole period. The conduction loss is therefore independent of D:

$$
P_{cond} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

Phase-current ripple adds to the RMS value:

$$
\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}, \qquad I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}
$$

For L_ph = 10 µH (a low-inductance racing motor): ΔI_pp = 25.2·0.25/(10e-6·48e3) = **13.1 A**. This adds 14.3 A², which is a 0.2 % increase over 6400 A², so it is negligible.

Results at T_j = 125 °C, where R_DS = 1.2 mΩ × 1.5 = 1.8 mΩ:

$$
P_{cond,FET} = 2 \times 6414 \times 1.8\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{23.1\ W}
$$
$$
P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.2\ W}
$$

There is a positive feedback loop here: R_DS rises with T_j, which raises the loss. Section 3.3 solves this loop explicitly.

### 2.2 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The synchronous partner turns on at near-zero V_DS, after the dead time.

| Component | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f | 0.5·25.2·80·30 ns·48 k = **1.45 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f | 60 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.073 W** |
| Coss | ≈ ½·Q_oss·V·f | 0.5·30 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.018 W** |
| Dead-time diode | V_F·I·2t_dt·f | 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs, mostly in driver) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f | 2·40 nC·10·48 k = **0.038 W** |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.2 W** |

Estimate the transition time from the driver strength:

$$
t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g}, \qquad I_g \approx \frac{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}{R_g + R_{drv}}
$$

For example, Q_gd = 8 nC at I_g ≈ 0.5 A gives about 16 ns, which is consistent with the 15 ns assumption.

**Sensitivity:**
- Doubling f_sw to 96 kHz increases the switching loss to about 4.4 W.
- Halving the dead time to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W, but risks shoot-through. Verify it with a double-pulse test.

### 2.3 Per-Channel and Board Totals (80 A burst, T_j = 125 °C)

| | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 23.1 W | 92.4 W |
| Copper | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| Switching | 2.2 W | 8.8 W |
| Capacitor ESR (Section 2.4) | ~0.4 W | ~1.6 W |
| **Total** | **~28.9 W** | **~115 W** |

Conduction dominates at 80 A, accounting for roughly 90 % of the loss. **The levers that matter are R_DS(on) (parallel FETs or larger packages) and copper**, not the switching frequency.

### 2.4 Bus Capacitor Ripple

Per channel:

$$
I_{C,rms} = I_{ph}\sqrt{D(1-D)} = 80 \times 0.5 = 40\ \text{A}
$$

The four channels are unsynchronized, so their ripple adds approximately in quadrature: √4 × 40 = **80 A rms**. If PWM edges align, the worst case is 160 A.
