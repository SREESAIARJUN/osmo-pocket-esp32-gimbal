## 0. Architecture Corrections and Feasibility Flags

These issues change the design, so they come first.

| # | Issue | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz motor PWM.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each ESC channel has its own MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071 / EFM8) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Running 4× six-step commutation from the H743 is not supported by ArduPilot. | H743 = flight controller. It sends DShot600 to 4× AT32F421 running AM32. The 48 kHz PWM lives in the ESC MCUs. |
| 2 | **80 A per motor on a "nano" board is thermally implausible as a sustained rating.** A typical nano board is 20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm. 4×80 A at 6S is about 8 kW of input power and about 84 W of dissipation (see §3). | Treat 80 A as a **≤1–2 s burst** rating, as derived in §4. ## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.45) = **1.45 mΩ** (1.5 mΩ is used below as margin).

### 2.2 Six-step current path

In any 60° sector, one phase is driven high and one is driven low, so the current I flows through two legs.

- **PWM on-time (D):** HS(A) → motor → LS(B). Two FETs carry I.
- **PWM off-time (1−D):** LS(A) as synchronous rectifier → motor → LS(B). Two FETs carry I.

The dead-time contribution is handled separately in §3.3. Total conduction loss per motor is therefore **independent of duty cycle**:

$$P_{cond,motor}=2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,motor}=2(80)^2(1.5\times10^{-3})=\mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

### 2.3 Per-FET distribution

Each phase leg is active for 240° of 360° electrical (120° high-sector, 120° low-sector).

- **LS FET of the low-driven phase:** conducts for the full 120° sector, giving I²R × 1/3 = 9.6 × 1/3 = 3.2 W.
- **HS FET of the high phase:** conducts for D × 1/3 of the period.
- **LS FET of the high phase (synchronous):** conducts for (1−D) × 1/3 of the period.

At full throttle (D → 1), the worst-case FET average is about **3.2 W**. The instantaneous dissipation in a conducting FET is **9.6 W**. That instantaneous figure is what drives the short-time Z_th calculation in §4.

## 3. Switching-Related Losses at 48 kHz

Only one leg hard-switches per PWM cycle (the HS/LS pair of the PWM'd phase).

### 3.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap

$$P_{sw}=\tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}=\tfrac12(25.2)(80)(20\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz})=\mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 3.2 Coss and reverse-recovery losses

$$P_{oss}=Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}=40\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k}=0.048\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}=50\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k}=0.060\ \text{W}$$

### 3.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt}=V_f\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw}=0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{n}\cdot48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

Lowering dead time to 50 ns halves this term. Verify shoot-through margin with the driver's propagation-delay mismatch before doing so.

### 3.4 Gate drive

Per switching FET:

$$P_g=Q_gV_{gs}f_{sw}=50\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k}=0.024\ \text{W}$$

This is about 0.05 W per motor. The power is dissipated mostly in the driver and Rg, not in the FET.

### 3.5 Loss budget at 80 A burst

| Term | Per motor (W) | 4 motors (W) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.2 | 76.8 |
| Switching overlap | 0.97 | 3.9 |
| Dead time | 0.61 | 2.4 |
| Qrr + Coss | 0.11 | 0.4 |
| Gate drive | 0.05 | 0.2 |
| **FET subtotal** | **20.9** | **83.7** |
| Copper (2 oz, ~0.3 mΩ per phase path) | ~3.8 | ~15 |
| **Board total** | **~24.7** | **~99 W** |

**Key insight:** conduction dominates at about 92%. Raising f_sw from 24 kHz to 48 kHz costs only about 1.7 W per motor. Lowering Rds(on) is the lever that matters, either with a better die or with paralleled FETs.

## 4. Thermal Calculations

### 4.1 Steady-state limit

Assume a 25.5×25.5 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz board in propwash. The effective board-to-ambient resistance is about R_θBA ≈ 2.5 K/W.

$$T_{board}=T_a+P\,R_{\theta BA}$$

With T_a = 40 °C and T_board ≤ 105 °C, the allowable dissipation is P ≤ 26 W.

Since total loss scales with I²:

$$I_{cont}=80\sqrt{26/99}\approx\mathbf{41\ A}$$

This is the per-channel value with all four channels loaded equally. Derate to **about 35 A continuous** for margin.

### 4.2 Burst limit (lumped thermal mass)

Thermal capacitance:

- Copper (about 6 g at 0.385 J/g·K) ≈ 2.3 J/K
- FR4 (about 3 g at 1.1 J/g·K) ≈ 3.3 J/K
- 24 FETs plus components ≈ 0.6 J/K
- **Total C_th ≈ 6.2 J/K**

Heating response:

$$T(t)=T_a+PR_\theta\left(1-e^{-t/(R_\theta C_\theta)}\right),\quad \tau=2.5\times6.2=15.5\text{ s}$$

The asymptote at 99 W is 287 °C, so the board would reach the 105 °C limit at:

$$t=-\tau\ln\left(1-\tfrac{65}{247}\right)=\mathbf{4.7\ s}\ \text{(starting from 40 °C)}$$

Starting from a hover-soaked 70 °C board:

$$t=-15.5\ln\left(1-\tfrac{35}{217}\right)\approx\mathbf{2.7\ s}$$

### 4.3 Junction rise during the burst

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Z_th(j-c) at 1 s (PDFN 3.3×3.3 class) | ≈ 1.5 K/W |
| Instantaneous FET power | 9.6 W |
| ΔT_j-c | ≈ 14 K |
| Board temperature at end of burst | 105 °C |
| **Resulting T_j** | **≈ 119 °C** (rated 150–175 °C, so OK) |

**Specification:** 80 A burst ≤ 2 s with a ≥ 10 s recovery interval; 35 A continuous per channel.

Enforce this in firmware:
- AM32 current limit (`CURRENT_LIMIT`).
- ArduPilot `BATT_CURR_MAX`, with ESC temperature reported back over bidirectional telemetry (EDT).
