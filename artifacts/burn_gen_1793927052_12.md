## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

**Who does what.** The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. It runs ArduPilot and sends DShot600 to four ESC MCUs (AT32F421 or STM32G071 class running AM32, or BLHeli_32 / Bluejay on the appropriate MCU). Each ESC MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The two halves are modeled separately below.

> **Key finding up front:** 80 A on all four channels in a 30.5 × 30.5 mm AIO footprint is a **~1–2 second burst rating**, not a continuous one. The math in sections 2–3 shows why. Marketing "80 A" on nano boards is usually per-channel, single-channel, or very short burst. Your spec sheet should state the burst duration explicitly.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.004\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 100 °C: factor = 1.30, so **R_DS = 1.69 mΩ (max)** / 1.30 mΩ (typ).

### 1.2 Current paths in 6-step synchronous PWM

In each 60° sector, one phase is the PWM leg and one is held on. With complementary PWM, the PWM leg's current alternates between its high-side FET (for D) and low-side FET (for 1−D). The phase current therefore always flows through exactly **two** channels:

$$P_{cond,leg} = I^2 R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

This is independent of duty, a useful property of synchronous rectification.

**At 80 A, worst-case R (1.69 mΩ):**

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.69\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{21.6\ W\ per\ ESC}$$

The typical-R value is 16.6 W.
