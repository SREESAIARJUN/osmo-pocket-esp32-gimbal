## 0. Architecture Baseline and Design Concerns

### Architectural correction

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. In every production-viable 4-in-1 design, the H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs. Those MCUs run AM32 or BLHeli_32/Bluejay-class firmware and handle commutation, back-EMF sensing, and gate drive. This document models that split architecture.

| Block | Assumed part (verify against your BOM) |
|---|---|
| Flight controller MCU | STM32H743VIH6 (TFBGA100) or VIT6 (LQFP100), 400 MHz |
| ESC MCU (×4) | AT32F421K8U7 (AM32) |
| Gate driver (×4) | FD6288Q, 3-phase half-bridge driver with bootstrap |
| MOSFET (×24) | 40 V N-ch, PDFN 3.3×3.3, R_DS(on) = 1.2 mΩ @ 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V |
| Bus | 6S LiPo: 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal |
| PWM frequency | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous rectification) |
| Motor (reference) | 2207-class, 1750 KV, 14 poles (7 pole pairs), L_LL ≈ 15 µH, R_LL ≈ 60 mΩ |

### Design concerns to resolve before layout

1. **The "80 A burst" figure must be defined.** It must state whether it is phase current or battery current, and give its duration, ambient temperature, and airflow. Below, 80 A is treated as **phase current per channel**, which is the worst case.
2. **Steady-state 80 A on a nano board is physically impossible.** Section 3 shows it is a roughly 2 s adiabatic event at most. The datasheet and marketing must reflect this.
3. **Verify the H743 temperature grade.** The "6" suffix is a −40 to 85 °C ambient part. This MCU sits millimetres from the power stage, so verify the T_J limit for your exact ordering code. The thermal model below uses 100 °C as the board-bulk ceiling for this reason.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{DS,hot} = 1.2\,\text{mΩ} \times 1.5 = 1.8\ \text{mΩ}$$

Use the vendor's normalised R_DS(on) vs. T_j curve in place of α when it is available.

### 1.2 Six-step commutation with complementary PWM

During each 60° sector, current I flows through two phases:

- **Phase A (PWM leg):** the high-side conducts for D·T_s. The low-side (synchronous) conducts for (1−D)·T_s.
- **Phase B (return leg):** the low-side is on for the whole sector.

Current therefore always flows through exactly **two** FETs in series. Averaged over the PWM period, the channel loss is:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{rms}^2 R_{DS} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}$$

This result is **independent of duty cycle**. That is the key consequence of synchronous rectification.

### 1.3 Ripple contribution

The worst ripple occurs with back-EMF ≈ D·V_bus:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{LL}\,f_{sw}}$$

$$\Delta I_{pp,max}\ (D = 0.5) = \frac{22.2 \times 0.25}{15\,\mu\text{H} \times 48\,\text{kHz}} = 7.7\ \text{A}_{pp}$$

The resulting RMS current is:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{DC}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 4.9 \approx 6405\ \text{A}^2$$

Ripple is therefore negligible (<0.1 %) at 80 A. It matters only at light load.

### 1.4 Numerical result at 80 A

| Condition | R_DS | P_cond per channel |
|---|---|---|
| Cold (25 °C) | 1.2 mΩ | 2 × 6405 × 0.0012 = **15.4 W** |
| Hot (125 °C) | 1.8 mΩ | 2 × 6405 × 0.0018 = **23.1 W** |

### 1.5 PCB copper loss

This term is often forgotten. Consider the phase path from FET drain to the motor pad and back through the bus copper. Assume about 15 mm of 2 oz copper (70 µm) at 4 mm width, on each of the two legs:

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho\,\ell}{w\,t} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\times0.015}{0.004\times70\times10^{-6}} \approx 0.92\ \text{mΩ per leg}$$

$$P_{Cu} \approx 2 \times 6400 \times 0.92\,\text{mΩ} \approx 11.8\ \text{W per channel (at 20 °C; +39 \% at 120 °C)}$$
