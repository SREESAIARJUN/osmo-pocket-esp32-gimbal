## 0. Architecture Assumptions & Key Findings

The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does not commutate the motors. Each ESC channel uses its own small MCU running AM32 (or BLHeli_32), for example an AT32F421 or STM32G071. This matters because of where each part of the design is controlled:

* **48 kHz PWM** is an ESC-firmware parameter.
* **Bidirectional DShot600** is the link between the H743 and the ESC MCUs.

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Supply | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V max (worst case), 22.2 V nominal |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal, complementary (synchronous) PWM |
| Burst rating | 80 A **per phase channel** |
| Board | "Nano" = 20×20 mm mounting, ~25×25 mm outline, 8-layer, 2 oz copper |
| FETs | 6 per channel, 24 total, 40 V class DFN3.3×3.3 or DFN5×6 |

> ⚠️ **Executive finding:** At 80 A on all four channels simultaneously, the power stage dissipates about **86 W**. A nano board's heat capacity is about 2.5 J/K, so 80 A all-four is a **~2-second burst rating at best**. It is not a sustainable rating. Section 3 quantifies this. For marketing honesty, rate it as "80 A burst (1 s), 45–55 A continuous with airflow," or move to a 30×30 layout.

## 2. Loss Model: Per Channel

### 2.1 Conduction Topology (6-step, synchronous)

In any commutation sector, two phase legs are active:

* **Static leg:** one FET held ON for the whole 60° sector.
* **PWM leg:** the high-side (HS) conducts for duty D, and the synchronous low-side (LS) conducts for (1−D).

The motor current therefore always flows through exactly **two R_DS(on)** in series:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2 \cdot R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

This holds independent of D, provided dead-time is negligible.

At I = 80 A and T_j ≈ 115 °C, so R_DS(on) = 1.0 × 1.495 ≈ **1.5 mΩ**:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 6400 \times 2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$
