# Job #51: STM32H743 Nano AIO with 4-in-1 ESC

## Engineering Design Package: Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, DShot Timer/DMA Plan, Netlist

---

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Headline Finding

**Partitioning.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and generates DShot600. It does not commutate the motors. Each motor bridge is driven by a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32, which generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM. This is the only practical partition: the H743 cannot also run four sensorless field-commutation loops alongside the flight stack.

**Headline result.** A nano (20×20 mm) board cannot sustain 80 A per motor. The model below shows about 34 W of loss per motor at 80 A, or about 134 W across the board. My recommendations:

- Rate the board **80 A for ≤1 s burst per motor**.
- Rate it **~28–30 A continuous per motor** under propwash.
- Firmware must enforce a current/thermal limit.

All device parameters below are **design assumptions** for a 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN MOSFET class. They must be replaced with datasheet values of the selected part before release.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current path in synchronous 6-step commutation

In each 60° commutation sector, one leg is PWM-switched, one leg is held low-side ON, and one leg floats.

- **During the ON time (fraction D):** current flows through HS_a → motor → LS_b.
- **During the OFF time (fraction 1−D):** the current is synchronously rectified through LS_a → motor → LS_b.

Two FET channels therefore carry I_ph at all times, independent of duty cycle.

### 2.2 Ripple correction

The RMS current includes the PWM ripple:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

The ripple amplitude is:

$$\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

Example: L_ph = 15 µH, V_bus − V_bemf ≈ 5 V, D = 0.8.

- ΔI = 5.6 A
- ΔI²/12 = 2.6 A²
- I_ph² = 6400 A²
- Ripple adds about 0.04%, which is negligible. Use I_rms ≈ I_ph.

### 2.3 Per-motor conduction loss

First, the hot channel resistance:

$$R_{hot} = R_{25}\,k_T = 1.6 \times 1.5 = 2.4\text{ m}\Omega$$

Then the conduction loss for the two channels in the current path:

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{hot} = 6400 \times 0.0048 = \mathbf{30.7\ W}$$

### 2.4 Per-FET distribution (worst case: high duty at burst)

Each leg is active for 2/3 of the electrical period. Within that, it is either the PWM leg (1/3 of the period) or the held-low leg (1/3 of the period).

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}D\,I^2R_{hot}$$

$$P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)\,I^2R_{hot} + \tfrac{1}{3}\,I^2R_{hot}$$

With D = 0.95 and I²R_hot = 15.36 W:

- **P_HS** = 4.86 W
- **P_LS** = 0.26 + 5.12 = 5.38 W
- **Check:** 3 × (4.86 + 5.38) = 30.7 W ✓

### 2.5 Copper and shunt losses

| Path | Resistance | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase pads, 2 oz copper, ~8 mm | ~0.3 mΩ | ~1.9 W |
| Battery bus path | ~0.25 mΩ | Carries ΣD·I ≈ 4 × 76 A ≈ 300 A peak |
| Current shunt | 0.2 mΩ | ~18 W total at 300 A |

The bus path is the dominant non-FET loss. Use stitched pours on 3 or more layers, and solder-wick or bus-bar the battery pads.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Its high-side FET hard-switches, while its low-side FET switches at zero voltage after body-diode conduction.

### 3.1 Hard-switching overlap

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus}\,I_{ph}\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

$$= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 3.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F\,I_{ph}\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 3.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.036\text{ W}$$

### 3.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.024\text{ W}$$

### 3.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistor, not the FET.

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2 \times 30\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.029\text{ W}$$

### 3.6 Switching subtotal

**About 2.64 W per motor.**

### 3.7 Frequency sensitivity

Switching loss scales as k·f_sw. Here k ≈ 55 µJ per cycle.
