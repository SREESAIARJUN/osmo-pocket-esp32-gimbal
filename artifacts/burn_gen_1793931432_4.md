# STM32H743 Nano AIO (Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC): Reference Design Package, Job #59

**Scope:** This document covers electro-thermal power-stage models, bidirectional DShot600 timer and DMA allocation for ArduPilot, reference netlists, and the thermal budget.

**Status:** These are pre-layout engineering estimates. Every device parameter marked **[A]** is an assumption. Before layout freeze, replace it with datasheet values for the parts you actually procure, and confirm it with thermocouple or IR testing on first articles.

## 0. Architecture Clarification

Several of the requirements involve two different processors. Clarifying this up front prevents a design error.

| Function | Processor | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Flight control, EKF, DShot command generation, bidirectional DShot telemetry decode | **STM32H743VIT6** (FC) | Runs ArduPilot |
| Commutation, 48 kHz PWM, gate drive timing, dead time | **4× ESC MCUs** (e.g., AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071GBU6, AM32 firmware) | One MCU per motor channel |

- The H743 never drives MOSFET gates.
- The 48 kHz switching frequency is an ESC-firmware parameter. In AM32 it is set through the PWM frequency setting.
- The DShot link is the only real-time interface between the two domains.

**Feasibility flag:** "80A burst per channel" on a 20×20 mm nano form factor is physically achievable only for sub-second to ~1.5 s bursts without heatsinking (Section 3). I recommend marketing it as **"80A burst (≤1 s), 35–40A continuous per channel with airflow."** Any stronger claim will generate RMA returns.

## 2. Loss Model (Per Motor Channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

With trapezoidal (6-step) commutation and complementary PWM, the current path always passes through **two bridge positions**: the high-side switch of one phase and the low-side switch of another.

In the PWM-ed leg, current alternates between HS (duty D) and LS (1−D) through the synchronous rectifier. The I²R loss for that position is therefore D·I²R + (1−D)·I²R = I²R, independent of duty (ignoring dead-time diode conduction, treated in 2.3).

The temperature-dependent resistance is:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

The conduction loss per channel is:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{ph,RMS}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

For trapezoidal drive at a given phase current, I_RMS ≈ I_pk during the conduction window, so I_pk is used directly as a conservative case.

**At 80 A and T_j = 135 °C:** R_DS = 1.2 mΩ × 1.495 ≈ **1.79 mΩ**

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 6400 \times 0.00179 = \mathbf{22.9\ W\ per\ channel}$$

For comparison, at T_j = 25 °C: 2 × 6400 × 0.0012 = 15.4 W.

This creates a self-heating feedback loop. Solve iteratively with T_j = T_ref + P·Z_th. Convergence requires α·P·Z_th < 1. With small-board thermal impedances this fails within seconds, which is effectively thermal runaway under continuous 80 A. This is the core reason the 80 A rating must be time-limited.

### 2.2 Switching Losses (Hard-Switched Leg Only)

Only one leg switches at f_sw at any moment.

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 2.3 Secondary Switching-Related Losses

| Loss | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_F · I · 2t_dt · f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 100 ns × 48 k | 0.31 W |
| Reverse recovery | V_bus · Q_rr · f_sw = 25.2 × 50 nC × 48 k | 0.06 W |
| C_oss charge/discharge | ½ C_oss(er) V² f_sw = 0.5 × 1.5 nF × 635 × 48 k | 0.02 W |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver/R_g, not FETs) | Q_g · V_GS · f_sw × 2 (active leg) = 60 nC × 10 × 48 k × 2 | 0.06 W |
