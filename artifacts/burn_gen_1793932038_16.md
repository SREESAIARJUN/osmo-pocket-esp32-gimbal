# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model (Job #22)

## 0. Scope and Architecture Corrections

Two issues in the brief need resolving before the math, because they change the results.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production AIO design, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071. That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM and performs commutation. The architecture modeled here is:

```
H743 (ArduPilot) --BDShot600 x4--> 4x ESC MCU (AM32) --> 4x 3-phase gate driver --> 24 MOSFETs
```

**2. "80 A burst" on a nano board is a thermal-time rating, not a capability.** The model in §3 shows that the safe burst duration depends on assumptions:

| Burst condition | Approximate safe duration |
|---|---|
| Per-motor 80 A, all four motors simultaneously | ~3–4 s |
| One motor at 80 A, others at moderate load | ~10–15 s |
| Continuous, with prop-wash cooling | ~28–32 A/motor |

Aggregate 4 × 80 A at 6S is about 320 A and roughly 7 kW. The battery connector, input pads and bulk capacitors cannot support that on a 30.5 mm board. The rating must be specified as per-motor and non-simultaneous.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-step synchronous commutation

In trapezoidal six-step drive, two phases conduct at any instant: one sourcing, one sinking. AM32 uses complementary PWM on the active leg, so the high-side FET conducts for duty D and the low-side of the same leg conducts for 1−D. The current path therefore always contains exactly **two channel resistances in series**, independent of duty:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right]
$$

At T_j = 120 °C:

$$
R_{DS(on)} = 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega \times [1 + 0.005 \times 95] = 2.21\,\text{m}\Omega
$$
