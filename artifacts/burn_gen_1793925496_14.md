| Supply | 2–6S. Analysis uses worst case V_bus = 25.2 V |
| PWM | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous rectification), 6-step trapezoidal |
| Copper | 8-layer, 2 oz outer / 2 oz inner power planes |

**Engineering warning.** The math below shows that 80 A on a single channel of a 20×20 board is **thermally a burst rating of a few seconds, not a continuous capability**. Per-channel dissipation at 80 A is about 25 W at T_j = 125 °C. A 20×20 board cannot sink 100 W (4 channels). The deliverables therefore include a burst time limit and a recommended firmware current/temperature limit. Do not publish "80 A continuous."

### 1.2 Current path in complementary 6-step

At any instant, exactly two phase legs carry motor current I_ph:

- **PWM leg:** HS conducts for D·T_sw, and the complementary LS conducts for (1–D)·T_sw. One device is always in the path, apart from dead time.
- **Return leg:** the LS is held fully on.

So the instantaneous conduction path is always **2 × R_DS(on)**, independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph,rms}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

Including PWM ripple (motor inductance L_m):

$$I_{ph,rms}^2 = I_{avg}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12},\qquad \Delta I_{pp}=\frac{V_{bus}D(1-D)}{L_m f_{sw}}$$

**Ripple example.** L_m = 15 µH (phase-phase, 2207-class), D = 0.5:

ΔI_pp = 25.2 × 0.25 / (15e-6 × 48e3) = **8.75 A**, so ΔI²/12 = 6.4 A².

This is negligible next to 6400 A² at 80 A, but it dominates at hover currents.

Each of the 6 switches carries current for 120° electrical. Averaged over an electrical revolution:

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} = \frac{2 I^2 R}{6} = \frac{I^2 R}{3}$$

This is valid when the electrical period (under 1 ms at flight RPM) is much shorter than the die thermal time constant (about 5 ms or more), which holds here.
