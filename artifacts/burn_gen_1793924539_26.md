# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 80A ESC
**Job #21: Power Stage Loss Model, Thermal Budget, Bi-Directional DShot600 DMA Allocation, and Netlists**

---

## 0. Architecture Decisions and Design-Review Flags

These points constrain everything that follows:

1. **The H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC MCU.** Each of the four motor channels needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. This design uses AT32F421 or STM32G071, one per motor, so there are four ESC MCUs. The H743 talks to them over bi-directional DShot600. Running 4× sensorless FOC/6-step plus ArduPilot on one H743 is not a supported ArduPilot configuration.
2. **"80A burst" on a nano form factor is a thermal-mass problem, not a silicon problem.** The model in §3 shows the board is **adiabatically limited to about 2–4 s at 80A on all four channels.** "80A" must be specified as *single-motor, ≤ 5 s, with airflow*, or the board must grow to 30.5×30.5 mm with 2 oz inner copper. On a 20×20 mm board I would rate it 45A continuous / 60A burst.
3. **Battery class:** 6S (25.2 V max). FETs are rated 40 V, about 1.6× margin. That is the minimum acceptable for regen spikes with active braking. Bulk capacitance is mandatory (§4.4).

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (Per Motor)

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

In any 60° commutation sector, current flows through:
- the PWM'd high-side FET for duty D,
- its complementary low-side FET for (1−D) (synchronous rectification), and
- the opposite phase's low-side FET for 100%.

So there are always exactly **two R_DS(on) in series** in the current path:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 \, R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

The on-resistance rises with junction temperature:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

This creates an electro-thermal feedback loop. Solve self-consistently:

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + R_{th,j\text{-}pcb}\cdot P_{FET}(T_j)$$

**Evaluated at T_j = 110 °C:**
- R_ds = 1.4 × (1 + 0.0045 × 85) = **1.94 mΩ**
- P_cond = 2 × 6400 × 1.94 m = **24.8 W per motor**

Each FET carries current about 1/3 of the electrical cycle, so averaged:

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{24.8}{6} = 4.1\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only one leg is PWM'd at a time, and each PWM period has one turn-on and one turn-off edge.
