# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #15): Architecture, Loss Model, DShot DMA Plan

## 0. Architectural Corrections

Three points need settling before any math.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** If the H743 drove the bridges directly, DShot would be unnecessary. In every practical AIO design, the H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to **four dedicated ESC MCUs** (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32). These MCUs own the 48 kHz PWM, commutation and back-EMF sensing. The rest of this document assumes that partition.
2. **80 A burst per channel on a "nano" (20×20 mm) board is not thermally credible.** The figures below show about 25 W per channel at 80 A, or about 100 W across the board. That needs at least a 30.5×30.5 mm board with 2–3 oz copper, and even then only for bursts of a few seconds. I model 80 A as **a short burst rating, not continuous**.
3. **Component values are assumptions.** No part numbers were specified, so parameters come from a representative 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN MOSFET class. Substitute your actual datasheet values into the equations.

## 1. Conduction Losses (6-step trapezoidal, synchronous rectification)

During each commutation interval, one phase is PWM'd high (duty D), its complementary low-side FET conducts for (1−D), and a second phase's low side is fully on:

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 R_{ds,hot}\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds,hot}$$

The result is independent of D. Note that battery current is I_bat ≈ D·I_ph, so 80 A phase current at partial throttle is less than 80 A at the battery.

$$R_{ds,hot} = 1.0 \times 1.5 = 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega$$
$$P_{cond} = 2(80)^2(0.0015) = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ channel}$$

Copper loss: P_cu = I² R_cu = 6400 × 0.0005 = **3.2 W**.

---

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

**Hard-switching (V–I overlap) on the PWM'd high-side FET:**
$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(20\text{n})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period):**
$$P_{dt} = 2 V_f I\, t_{dead} f_{sw} = 2(0.8)(80)(200\text{n})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$
This is the dominant non-conduction loss. Cutting dead time to 100 ns halves it, provided shoot-through margin is verified.

**Reverse recovery:**
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = 40\text{n}(25.2)(48\text{k}) = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

**Output capacitance:**
$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V f_{sw} = 0.018\ \text{W}$$

**Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, two switching FETs):**
$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2(50\text{n})(10)(48\text{k}) = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

**Switching subtotal: ≈ 2.31 W per channel.**

### Loss Budget at 80 A

| Loss | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Copper | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| Switching + dead time + recovery | 2.3 W | 9.2 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 24.7 W** | **≈ 99 W** |

At 80 A burst, efficiency is roughly 0.95–0.97 depending on D.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3a. Junction rise (short time scale)

The four MOSFETs in one phase-pair share P_cond, with the hottest FET dissipating about I² R_hot ≈ 9.6 W.

Using an assumed transient thermal impedance Z_th,jc(10 ms) ≈ 0.3 K/W:
- ΔT_jc ≈ 2.9 K, which is negligible.

The real limit is the board.

### 3b. Board-level adiabatic burst (seconds)

The effective heat capacity of a 30.5 mm board with 2 oz copper, FETs and caps is C_th ≈ 5 J/K (FR4 ≈ 1.1 J/g·K, Cu ≈ 0.385 J/g·K, about 6 g effective mass).

$$\frac{dT}{dt} = \frac{P}{C_{th}} = \frac{99}{5} \approx 20\ \text{K/s}$$

### 3c. Steady-state convection

Prop-wash convection gives roughly h ≈ 60 W/m²K. Over a two-sided 30.5² mm board (A ≈ 1.86×10⁻³ m²):

$$R_{th,ba} = \frac{1}{hA} \approx 9\ \text{K/W}$$

The thermal time constant is τ = R·C ≈ 45 s.

The board temperature rise follows:
$$\Delta T(t) = P R_{th}(1 - e^{-t/\tau})$$

### 3d. Burst limit

Allowable rise from 40 °C ambient to a 110 °C board limit is ΔT = 70 K.

$$t_{max} = -\tau \ln\!\left(1 - \frac{70}{99 \times 9}\right) \approx 45 \times 0.082 \approx \mathbf{3.7\ s}$$

**Specified rating:** 80 A for 3 s or less, at 25 % duty. Sustainable continuous loss is 70 K / 9 K/W ≈ 7.8 W total, which corresponds to roughly **22 A per channel continuous** after applying the I² scaling.

For a 20×20 mm "nano" board, C_th and A are about 2.3× smaller, so the burst limit drops below 1.5 s. This is the quantitative basis for correction #2 in section 0.

## 4. Bidirectional DShot600: Timing and ArduPilot DMA Allocation

### 4a. Timing

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Bit time | 1/600 kHz = 1.667 µs |
| Frame | 16 bits = 26.7 µs |
| Timer clock (H743, APB×2) | 240 MHz |
| Counts per bit (ARR) | 240 MHz / 600 kHz = 400 |
| Bit "1" high time | ≈ 0.75 × 400 = 300 counts |
| Bit "0" high time | ≈ 0.375 × 400 = 150 counts |

The bidirectional (inverted) signal works as follows:
- After a turnaround of about 30 µs, the ESC replies with 21-bit GCR at 5/4 × 600 kbaud = 750 kbit/s, which takes about 28 µs.
- Total transaction time is about 85–90 µs.
- This fits comfortably inside a 4 kHz loop (250 µs), and also inside 8 kHz (125 µs) if motor groups are not serialized.

### 4b. DMA strategy

ArduPilot's ChibiOS backend handles the transaction in three steps:
1. **Output:** a TIMx_UP DMA burst writes all CCR registers of a timer group.
2. **Reception:** the backend switches the pin to input capture and uses a per-channel TIMx_CHy DMA to timestamp edges.
3. **Sharing:** channel DMA streams can be shared with peripherals that tolerate it, because the backend releases them between frames.

The H743's DMAMUX lets any request route to any of the 16 DMA1/DMA2 streams. The real constraint is **stream count and contention, not pin mapping.**

The motor group is placed on **two timers** so each UP DMA serves two motors and failures are isolated: TIM3 (M1, M2) and TIM5 (M3, M4). TIM1 and TIM8 are kept free for LEDs or extra servo outputs.

### 4c. hwdef excerpt

```
MCU STM32H7xx STM32H743xx
define STM32_ST_USE_TIMER 12
# Motors – bidirectional DShot
PB0  TIM3_CH3 TIM3 PWM(1) GPIO(50) BIDIR
PB1  TIM3_CH4 TIM3 PWM(2) GPIO(51)
PA0  TIM5_CH1 TIM5 PWM(3) GPIO(52) BIDIR
PA1  TIM5_CH2 TIM5 PWM(4) GPIO(53)
# BIDIR on one channel per pair → paired channel shares IC DMA (CH3/CH4, CH1/CH2 pairing)
define HAL_WITH_BIDIR_DSHOT 1
DMA_PRIORITY TIM3* TIM5* SPI1* SPI4*
DMA_NOSHARE SPI1* SPI4* TIM3_UP TIM5_UP
# ESC telemetry fallback (if not using EDT)
PE7 UART7_RX UART7 NODMA
define HAL_DEFAULT_SERIAL7_PROTOCOL 16
```
