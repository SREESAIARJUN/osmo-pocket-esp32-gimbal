## 0. Architecture Clarifications

Three points need settling before the math:

1. **The STM32H743 does not switch the MOSFETs.** In any ArduPilot AIO, the H743 is the flight controller. It sends DShot600 to four ESC MCUs (typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). Those ESC MCUs generate the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The design therefore has two separate timing domains:
   - **FC domain (H743):** DShot600 output, bi-directional eRPM capture.
   - **ESC domain (4× AT32F421):** 48 kHz 6-step PWM, BEMF zero-cross detection, deadtime.

2. **"80 A burst" on a nano board is a transient rating, not a thermal steady state.** The numbers below show that a ~37×37 mm board can sustain roughly 24 A per channel continuously in prop-wash airflow. 80 A is limited by thermal capacitance to about 2–4 seconds.

3. **80 A × 4 simultaneously means 320 A from the battery.** No nano-class XT60 lead or pad supports that. The design assumes 80 A burst per channel, with a 150–180 A aggregate burst limit at the battery input.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At the design junction temperature T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{DS}(125) = 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega \times (1 + 0.0045 \times 100) = 2.18\,\text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Current path in synchronous 6-step drive

In each 60° sector, phase current flows through two switch positions in series:
- **Active leg:** high-side FET (during the PWM on-time D) or the synchronous low-side FET (during 1−D).
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET, held continuously on.

With synchronous rectification, the current always sees two R_DS(on) in series, independent of duty cycle (deadtime is excluded here and handled in §2):

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j) + I^2 R_{Cu}$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| Term | Calculation | Result |

| FET conduction per motor | 80² × 2 × 2.18 mΩ | **27.9 W** |

### 1.4 Electro-thermal feedback

R_DS(on) rises with T_j, and losses rise with R_DS(on), so the system must converge. Solve iteratively:
