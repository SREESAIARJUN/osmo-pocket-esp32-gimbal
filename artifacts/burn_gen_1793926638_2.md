# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC Stack: Power, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Early Warnings

**Architecture assumed.** An STM32H743 does not commutate the motors directly in any practical 4-in-1 design. Its timer and DMA resources are needed for DShot, IMU, and logging. This document therefore assumes the standard split:

- **Flight controller (FC):** STM32H743 running ArduPilot. Sends bi-directional DShot600 to the ESC.
- **4-in-1 ESC:** four AT32F421 MCUs running AM32 at 48 kHz PWM. Each drives one FD6288Q three-phase gate driver and six N-channel MOSFETs.

If you actually intend an AIO board where the H743 runs FOC on all four motors, tell me. The timer allocation in §4 changes completely, and it is likely infeasible on a single H743.

**Burst feasibility warning.** The thermal model in §3 shows that 4 × 80 A simultaneously is a ~2–5 s burst rating at best, even on 30×30 mm with prop-wash airflow. It is not a continuous rating. On a true 20×20 "nano" footprint it is not credible. Treat 80 A as per-channel, short-duration, single-motor-dominant.

**Placeholder MOSFET parameters.** No MOSFET was specified. All numbers below use a representative 40 V, 5×6 mm (PDFN) part. Substitute your selected part's datasheet values into the equations in §1–2. The model is parameterized for exactly that purpose.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

**Commutation model.** In 6-step trapezoidal drive (AM32 default), two phases conduct at any instant: one high-side and one low-side FET.

**Synchronous rectification.** AM32 runs complementary PWM. During the off-time, the opposite FET in the PWM'd leg conducts instead of its body diode. The current path therefore always passes through exactly two channels.

**Temperature-dependent resistance:**

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

**Instantaneous bridge conduction loss:**

$$P_{cond,bridge} = I^2 \left[ 2R_{ds}(T_j) + R_{pcb,path} \right]$$

**Per-FET average.** Each FET conducts for 120° of every 360° electrical cycle. The commutation period (≪1 ms) is far shorter than the die thermal time constant, so averaging is valid:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)}{3}$$

**Numbers at I = 80 A, T_j = 125 °C (R_ds = 1.6 mΩ):**

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| P_cond, bridge (FETs) | 2 × 6400 × 0.0016 = **20.48 W** |
| P_copper, bridge | 6400 × 0.0003 = **1.92 W** |
| P_cond per FET (avg) | 6400 × 0.0016 / 3 = **3.41 W** |

**Thermal runaway check.** The loop is stable if the loss slope stays below the junction's ability to shed heat:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\cdot R_{\theta ja} = I^2 R_{ds,25}\,\alpha \cdot R_{\theta ja} / 3 < 1$$

With R_θja ≈ 5.5 K/W effective (see §3): 6400 × 0.001 × 0.006 / 3 × 5.5 = **0.07**. This is stable, with a large margin.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (f_sw = 48 kHz)

Only the PWM'd high-side FET switches hard. Its complementary low-side switches near-ZVS after dead-time, so hard-switching terms are counted once per bridge.

**Hard-switching overlap:**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,er} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.015\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period):**

$$P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2t_{dead} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}$$

**Gate drive.** This is dissipated in the FD6288Q and R_g, not the FET. Two switching FETs per bridge:

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \times 40\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.038\ W}$$
