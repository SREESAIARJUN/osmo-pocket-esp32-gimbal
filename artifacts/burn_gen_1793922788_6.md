# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Systems Model (Job #61)

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

**Partitioning.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller MCU running ArduPilot. It does not commutate the motors. Each of the four ESC channels uses its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to those MCUs. ArduPilot has no in-tree firmware that drives MOSFETs directly from the H743, so the design is partitioned this way.

**Parameter values.** All component values below are representative placeholders for a 20×20/30×30 class AIO board. Before release, replace every `‹ ›` value with numbers from the selected MOSFET, gate driver, and PCB stackup.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology

In 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous (complementary) PWM, phase current always flows through exactly two MOSFETs in series, for every commutation sector and both PWM states:

- During the PWM on-time: high-side FET of phase A, then the motor winding, then low-side FET of phase B.
- During the PWM off-time: low-side FET of phase A (synchronous rectification), then the motor winding, then low-side FET of phase B.

Therefore:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j) \quad\text{(independent of duty when synchronous rectification is enabled)}$$

### 1.2 Temperature Dependence

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 100 °C:

$$R_{ds} = 1.2\,(1+0.0055\cdot75) = 1.70\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.70\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{21.8\ W}$$

For the hottest single FET, the low-side FET of the PWM'd phase conducts for (1 − D) of the time and the high-side FET for D. The FET on the non-PWM phase conducts continuously, giving 10.9 W during its 1/3 electrical-period sector. Averaged over a full electrical cycle, each of the 6 FETs dissipates:

$$P_{FET,avg} = \frac{21.8}{6}\cdot\ldots \approx 3.6\ \text{W}$$

The peak instantaneous dissipation of 10.9 W occurs over a sector of about 0.1–1 ms. That is short relative to the FET's case thermal time constant, so the average value governs.

### 1.3 Copper and Shunt Losses

- PCB copper: P_cu = I²·R_cu = 6400 × 0.4 mΩ = **2.56 W/channel**
- Current shunt (if per-channel, 0.25 mΩ): **1.6 W**

### 1.4 Thermal Runaway Check

Because loss rises with temperature, steady state is stable only if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\,R_{th,ja} < 1$$

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j} = 2I^2R_{25}\alpha = 0.084\ \text{W/K}$$

With R_th,ja ≈ 40 K/W for a single 5×6 FET on a small board, the product is about 3.4, which is greater than 1.

**Conclusion: 80 A continuous is not thermally stable on a nano board. 80 A is strictly a transient (burst) rating.** This constraint drives the burst-duration model in §3.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In each commutation sector, only one FET pair hard-switches: the active PWM leg. Each term below is per ESC channel.

### 2.1 V-I Overlap

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot40\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f = 0.85\cdot80\cdot200\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.65\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse Recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f = 30\text{nC}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}$$

The recovery current also adds about Q_rr/t_r ≈ 1.5 A of overshoot to the turn-on edge. That raises the V-I overlap term by roughly 2%.

### 2.4 Output Capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V^2 f = 0.5\cdot1.5\text{nF}\cdot635\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.023\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate Drive

Gate drive power is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET:

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f = 2\cdot40\text{nC}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.038\ W}$$

### 2.6 Switching Total

$$P_{sw,total} \approx \mathbf{2.65\ W/channel}$$

This is about 11% of conduction loss. Frequency sensitivity:

$$\frac{dP}{df} \approx 55\ \mu\text{W/Hz}$$

| f_sw | Switching loss per channel |
|---|---|
| 24 kHz | ≈ 1.3 W |
| 96 kHz | ≈ 5.3 W |

### 2.7 DC-Link Capacitor Ripple

$$I_{C,rms} = I\sqrt{D(1-D)}$$

This is at most 40 A rms at D = 0.5. Example: 4 × 470 µF low-ESR polymer at about 15 mΩ each gives 3.75 mΩ effective:

$$P_C = 40^2 \cdot 3.75\text{m} = 6\ \text{W (shared bulk)}$$

Practical consequences:
- Each channel needs local MLCCs of 4 × 10 µF/50 V X7R per channel.
- The design needs a low-ESR bulk electrolytic at the battery pads.
