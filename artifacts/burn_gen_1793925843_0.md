# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage, Thermal, and DShot/DMA Model

## 0. Architecture Decisions and Scope

Three points need settling before the math, because they change the results.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot has no in-tree sensorless BLDC commutation firmware. The production-proven architecture uses:
   - **STM32H743** running ArduPilot (attitude control, DShot600 master, bi-directional eRPM consumer).
   - **4× ESC MCUs** (e.g., AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071GBU6) running **AM32**, with complementary PWM at 48 kHz.
   - Each ESC MCU drives a 3-phase gate driver and 6 N-MOSFETs.
2. **"80 A burst" is defined here as 80 A phase current per channel, for ≤ 2 s.** Section 3 shows that a nano form factor (≤ 30.5 × 30.5 mm) cannot sustain this continuously. The thermal model sets the burst duration you can honestly print on a datasheet.
3. **The device parameters are design assumptions, not verified values.** Re-run the equations with datasheet values for your chosen MOSFET. Treat any part numbers as candidates to verify for footprint and availability.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (Six-Step, Complementary PWM)

In six-step commutation, each 60° sector has one leg PWM-switched and one leg held low. The third leg floats and is used for BEMF sensing. With synchronous (complementary) rectification:

- **The PWM leg (1/3 of the electrical period):**
  - The high-side FET conducts for duty D.
  - The low-side FET freewheels synchronously for (1 − D).
- **The held-low leg (1/3 of the period):** the low-side FET conducts 100%.
- **The floating leg (1/3 of the period):** carries no current.

**Per-FET RMS-equivalent losses** (with I = I_ph and R = R_ds,hot):

$$P_{HS} = I^2 R \cdot \frac{D}{3} \qquad P_{LS} = I^2 R \cdot \frac{(1-D) + 1}{3} = I^2 R\cdot\frac{2-D}{3}$$

**Per channel (3 legs):**

$$P_{cond,ch} = 3\,(P_{HS}+P_{LS}) = 2\,I^2 R$$

This is independent of D: at every instant, exactly two FETs carry the phase current in series.

**Numbers at 80 A:**
- I²R = 6400 × 1.5 mΩ = **9.6 W**
- P_cond,ch = **19.2 W** per channel

Worst case per FET at full-throttle burst (D = 0.9):
- P_HS = 9.6 × 0.9 / 3 = **2.88 W**
- P_LS = 9.6 × 1.1 / 3 = **3.52 W**

Note that at low D and high current (for example, a hard deceleration with active braking), the LS FET approaches 9.6 × 2/3 = **6.4 W**. That is the true hotspot case.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. Each FET pair hard-switches for 1/3 of the time.

### 2.1 Hard-switching (V–I overlap) loss

The HS FET turns on into the LS body-diode/channel current.

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 30\,\text{ns}\cdot 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}\ \text{per channel}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot 80\cdot 200\,\text{ns}\cdot 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery and C_oss

- Reverse recovery: P_rr = V·Q_rr·f = 25.2 × 50 nC × 48 kHz = **0.06 W**
- C_oss: P_oss = ½·C_oss·V²·f = 0.5 × 1.5 nF × 635 × 48k = **0.02 W**

### 2.4 Gate drive

This loss lands in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs.

- P_g = Q_g·V_gs·f·2 = 60 nC × 10 × 48k × 2 = **0.06 W**
