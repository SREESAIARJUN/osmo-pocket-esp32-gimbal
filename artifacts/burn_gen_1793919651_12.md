## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.5 mΩ × 1.5 = **2.25 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step synchronous PWM

In every commutation sector, current I flows through exactly two conducting devices:

- **ON interval (D):** HS_A (PWM) → motor → LS_B (static ON)
- **OFF interval (1−D):** LS_A (synchronous) → motor → LS_B

So there are always two R_ds(on) in series, independent of duty (excluding dead time):

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph,rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

The phase current relates to battery current by $I_{ph} \approx I_{bat}/D$. An 80 A phase current at low duty therefore corresponds to much less battery current, which matters for capacitor and battery-lead sizing.

### 1.3 Copper (PCB) conduction

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu},\qquad R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho_{Cu}(T)\,\ell}{w\,t}$$

with $\rho_{Cu}(T)=1.72\times10^{-8}[1+0.00393(T-20)]$ Ω·m.

For a nano board with ~0.5 mΩ total loop (battery pad → bus → bridge → phase pad) at 100 °C, R_Cu ≈ 0.66 mΩ.

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

| Term | Expression | Per channel |
|---|---|---|
| MOSFET conduction | 2 × 80² × 2.25 mΩ | **28.8 W** |
| PCB copper | 80² × 0.66 mΩ | **4.2 W** |

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

These losses occur only on the PWM leg (HS_A / LS_A pair). The static LS_B does not switch.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V-I overlap (HS device)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw} = \tfrac12(25.2)(80)(20\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction (LS device)

$$P_{DT} = V_{SD}\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times400\,\text{ns}\times48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery (lands in HS at turn-on)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 30\,\text{nC}\times25.2\times48\,\text{kHz} = 36\ \text{mW}$$

### 2.4 Output capacitance (C_oss,er ≈ 1 nF)

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,er}V^2 f_{sw}\times 2 = 30\ \text{mW}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in the driver/bootstrap, not the FETs)

$$P_g = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw}\times 2 = 40\,\text{nC}\times10\times48\,\text{kHz}\times2 = 38\ \text{mW/ch}$$
