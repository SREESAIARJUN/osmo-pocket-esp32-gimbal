## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At the design-limit junction temperature T_j = 125 °C, using the max datasheet value:

$$R_{DS(on)}(125) = 0.85\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.55 = 1.32\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Per-switch conduction in 6-step synchronous PWM

During any 60° commutation sector:

- **Phase A (PWM leg):** the high side conducts for duty D, and the low side (synchronous rectification) conducts for (1−D).
- **Phase B (sink leg):** the low side is fully on (100%).
- **Phase C:** floating (BEMF sensing).

$$P_{HS} = I^2 R_{DS} D, \qquad P_{LS,sync} = I^2 R_{DS}(1-D), \qquad P_{LS,sink} = I^2 R_{DS}$$

Sector total (independent of D):

$$\boxed{P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS(on)}(T_j)}$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| Condition | R_DS | P_cond per ESC | P_cond ×4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cold (25 °C, typ) | 0.70 mΩ | 2·6400·0.70e-3 = **8.96 W** | 35.8 W |
| Hot (125 °C, max) | 1.32 mΩ | 2·6400·1.32e-3 = **16.9 W** | 67.6 W |

### 1.4 Peak per-FET dissipation (hot-spot sizing)

The sink-leg low-side FET carries the full current for the whole sector:

$$P_{FET,peak} = I^2 R_{DS} = 6400 \times 1.32\times10^{-3} = 8.45\ \text{W (instantaneous, during its sectors)}$$

Averaged over an electrical cycle, each FET conducts roughly 1/3 of the time (120° of 360°):

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}\cdot 8.45 = 2.8\ \text{W}$$

The relevant figure depends on the electrical period relative to the FET's thermal time constant. At typical eRPM (above 10 kHz electrical), the period is far shorter than τ_jc, so averaging applies. At stall or startup with high current, the peak applies. AM32's stall protection must cover that case.

### 1.5 PCB copper (I²R in the board itself)

Phase and bus trace resistance on 2 oz (70 µm) copper:

$$R_{trace} = \frac{\rho L}{w\,t} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot 0.010}{0.005\cdot 70\times10^{-6}} = 0.49\ \text{m}\Omega \ \text{per layer (10 mm × 5 mm)}$$

With 4 paralleled power layers, the effective resistance is about 0.12 mΩ per segment. Assuming roughly 4 segments in the current path per ESC (bus+, bus−, two phases):

$$P_{Cu,ESC} \approx 6400 \times 4 \times 0.12\times10^{-3} \approx 3.1\ \text{W}$$

**This is not negligible.** It sits on the same board thermal mass as the FETs.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Hard switching occurs on the high-side FET. The low-side sync FET switches near zero voltage after dead time, but it incurs body-diode and Q_rr penalties.

### 2.1 Switching transition times (gate-charge method)

During the Miller plateau, with V_drv = 12 V (the FD6288 runs from a 12 V LDO in ESC designs), V_plateau ≈ 3.0 V, and R_g,total = R_g,ext (10 Ω) + R_g,int (1 Ω):

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_g} = \frac{9.0}{11} = 0.82\ \text{A}, \quad I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_g} = \frac{3.0}{11} = 0.27\ \text{A}$$

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = 12\ \text{ns}, \qquad t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,off}} = 37\ \text{ns}$$

> **Design action:** add a turn-off speed-up diode (BAT54 anti-parallel to R_g with 2.2 Ω) so that t_f ≈ 12 ns. Both cases are modeled below.
