# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Engineering Model (Job #14)

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Feasibility Caveats

**Architecture clarification.** The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. It runs ArduPilot and sends bi-directional DShot600 to the ESC section. The ESC section runs its own firmware (AM32 or BLHeli_32) on dedicated MCUs, typically one AT32F421 or STM32G071 per channel. The 48 kHz PWM frequency is therefore an ESC-firmware parameter, not an ArduPilot one.

**Feasibility warning.** An 80 A per-channel burst on a "nano" form factor (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm mounting) is at the extreme edge of physical plausibility. The calculations below show it is survivable only as a short burst of about 1 to 3 s, with heat soak into copper. It is not a continuous rating. Treat any marketing claim of "80A" on a nano board with skepticism until the transient thermal model below is validated by thermocouple and IR testing.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Topology
The ESC uses six-step (trapezoidal) commutation with synchronous rectification. At any instant, one phase leg is driven by PWM, a second leg's low side is held on, and the third phase floats for BEMF sensing.

During the PWM on-time, current flows through the high-side switch of phase A, the motor, and the low-side switch of phase B. During the off-time, it flows through the low-side of A (synchronous freewheel) and the low-side of B.

The current therefore always passes through two conducting MOSFETs:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot \left[ D\,R_{HS}(T_j) + (1-D)\,R_{LS,A}(T_j) + R_{LS,B}(T_j) \right]
$$

For identical FETs this reduces to $P_{cond,ch} \approx 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$. The ripple current is neglected because the motor inductance at 48 kHz gives roughly 5% ripple, which adds about 0.1% to I_rms².

### 1.2 Temperature Dependence

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

| T_j (°C) | R_ds (mΩ) | P per conducting FET at 80 A (W) | P_cond per channel (W) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 | 1.200 | 7.68 | 15.4 |
| 100 | 1.605 | 10.27 | 20.5 |
| 125 | 1.740 | 11.14 | 22.3 |
| 150 | 1.875 | 12.00 | 24.0 |

### 1.3 Per-Device Distribution

Assuming the commutation sector rotates evenly across the six switches, the time-averaged loss is shared among the FETs. The instantaneous peak, however, lands on the low-side FET that is held fully on for a whole sector: $I^2 R_{ds}$ for 1/3 of the electrical cycle. The time-averaged per-FET conduction loss is:

$$
\bar{P}_{FET} = \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6} \cdot \text{(sharing)} \approx \frac{2 I^2 R_{ds}}{3}\ \text{over the 3 legs} \;\Rightarrow\; \approx 6.8\ \text{W per leg at } T_j = 100\,°C
$$

For thermal sizing, use the hot-leg worst case. At very low eRPM, such as startup or a stalled prop, the sector dwell becomes long compared with the die thermal time constant (about 1 to 5 ms). In that case one FET effectively carries the full $I^2 R_{ds}$ (about 10 to 12 W) continuously. This stall case is the true design limit.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switched leg incurs hard-switching loss. The high side hard-switches, and the synchronous low side switches with near-zero voltage after the dead time.

### 2.1 V-I Overlap (High Side)

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}
= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.935\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Output Capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction (Low Side)

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.614\ W}
$$

### 2.4 Reverse Recovery

This loss is dissipated mostly in the high side, which absorbs the recovery current at turn-on:

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}
$$

*At 80 A, Q_rr scales up with di/dt and I_F. Budget 2 to 3× the datasheet value, which gives about 0.15 W.*

### 2.5 Gate Drive

Gate drive loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET:

$$
P_{g} = Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} \times 2\ \text{(HS+LS)} = 40\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = \mathbf{0.038\ W}
$$

### 2.6 Switching Summary per Channel

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| V-I overlap | 1.94 |
| Coss | 0.05 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Q_rr (derated ×2.5) | 0.15 |
| Gate (driver) | 0.04 |
| **Total P_sw** | **≈ 2.79 W** |

**Sensitivity.** P_VI scales linearly with f_sw. Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.4 W per channel, at the cost of audible noise and higher current ripple. Raising dead time is a poor trade: each extra 50 ns adds about 0.15 W.
