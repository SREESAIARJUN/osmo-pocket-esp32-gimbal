## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A burst)

In trapezoidal (6-step) BLDC drive, two phases conduct in each commutation sector. Current flows through one high-side leg and one low-side leg.

With complementary PWM at duty D:

- The PWM'd leg dissipates I²·R·D in its HS FET and I²·R·(1−D) in its LS FET.
- The static leg's FET carries current 100% of the time.

So the per-motor total is independent of D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 R_{DS(on)}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS(on)} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS(on),hot}$$

Numbers:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.92\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{24.6\ W/motor}$$

Per-FET peak (while active in a sector, static leg) = 80² × 1.92 mΩ = **12.3 W**. Each FET is active about 1/3 of the electrical cycle, giving a time-averaged ≈ 4.1 W per FET (6 FETs/motor).

**Thermal runaway check.** R<sub>DS(on)</sub> rises about 0.5%/K. Use the iterative form:

$$P(T_j) = 2I^2R_{25}\,[1 + 0.005(T_j - 25)]$$

The loop converges only if the effective thermal impedance satisfies Z<sub>θ</sub>·∂P/∂T < 1. With Z<sub>θ</sub> ≈ 2.4 K/W (see below): 2.4 × (2 × 6400 × 1.2 mΩ × 0.005) = 0.18. This is stable.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches: one HS/LS pair per motor at any time.

**(a) V–I overlap (hard-switched HS turn-on and turn-off):**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction (2 edges per period):**

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 600\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{1.84\ W}$$

This is the dominant switching term. Cutting t<sub>dt</sub> to 150 ns halves it, which is the single best tuning lever.

**(c) Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = 30\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{ k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

**(d) Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1\text{ nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{ k} = 0.015\ \text{W}$$

**(e) Gate drive** (2 switching FETs, V<sub>gs</sub> = 10 V; dissipated in driver and R<sub>g</sub>):

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{ nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{ k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### Loss budget at 80 A burst

| Term | Per motor | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 24.6 W | 98.4 W |
| V–I overlap | 1.94 W | 7.8 W |
| Dead time | 1.84 W | 7.4 W |
| Q<sub>rr</sub> + C<sub>oss</sub> + Q<sub>g</sub> | 0.10 W | 0.4 W |
| Copper/vias (≈0.3 mΩ path) | 1.9 W | 7.7 W |
| **Total** | **≈30.4 W** | **≈122 W** |

Efficiency at 80 A × 4 × ~22 V ≈ 7 kW gives about 98.3%. Even so, 122 W of heat in about 9 cm² is the core problem.

**Frequency sensitivity.** P<sub>sw</sub>(f) ≈ 3.8 W × (f / 48 kHz).

- At 24 kHz: saves about 1.9 W/motor, but adds audible noise and ripple.
- At 96 kHz: adds about 3.8 W/motor.

48 kHz is a reasonable compromise.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Junction (short time scale)

Peak per-FET power is about 12.3 W conduction plus about 1.9 W switching share ≈ 14 W.

- Z<sub>θJC</sub>(10 ms) ≈ 0.6 K/W → ΔT<sub>JC</sub> ≈ 8 K.
- Steady state: R<sub>θJC</sub> = 1.5 K/W plus about 0.9 K/W (solder and thermal-via array to inner plane) ≈ 2.4 K/W.
- At 4.7 W average per FET (14 W × 1/3 duty plus margin): ΔT<sub>J-board</sub> ≈ 11 K.

**The junction is not the limit. The board's bulk heat capacity is.**
