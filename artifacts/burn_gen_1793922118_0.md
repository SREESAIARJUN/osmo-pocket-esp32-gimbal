# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal and DShot Design Model

## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility Findings

These findings change the design scope, so they come first.

**Assumptions** (replace with datasheet values when parts are frozen)

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | V_bus | 6S, 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal |
| Burst current per motor (phase RMS ≈ DC-link equivalent at full duty) | I_b | 80 A, 10 s |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| MOSFET (3.3×3.3 mm DFN class, 40 V) | R_ds(on),25 | 1.0 mΩ @ V_gs = 10 V |
| | Q_g | 50 nC |
| | Q_oss | 60 nC |
| | Q_rr | 40 nC |
| | t_r + t_f | 20 ns (effective, with gate driver) |
| Dead time | t_dt | 100 ns per edge |
| Body diode forward voltage | V_f | 0.8 V |
| Junction-to-case thermal resistance | R_θJC | 1.5 K/W |
| Board | — | 20×20 mm (nano), 8 layers, 2 oz copper |

**Architecture note.** ArduPilot runs on the H743 and acts as the flight controller. It does not commutate motors. Each ESC channel therefore needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 drives these MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. Do not route H743 timers directly to the gate drivers.

**Feasibility findings**

1. **Bus current.** 4 × 80 A = 320 A at 25 V, about 8 kW. This exceeds XT60/XT90 connectors, nano solder pads, and most 6S packs' sustained capability. A realistic figure is 80 A per motor for under 1 s, or about 40 to 55 A continuous per motor.
2. **Thermal limit.** The calculation in Section 3 shows a 10 s, 80 A all-motor burst is not thermally survivable on a 20×20 board without forced convection. The safe burst is about 1.5 to 2 s.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification

At any instant, one high-side FET (phase A) and one low-side FET (phase B) carry the motor current. During PWM-off, the complementary FET of the switched leg conducts. Therefore:

- **Two FETs conduct at all times** (excluding dead time).
- Each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical period. Six FETs share the loss, but the instantaneous loss is always on two devices.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds ≈ 1.0 × 1.5 = **1.5 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Conduction loss per motor

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.5\,\text{mΩ} = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

Per FET, averaged over the electrical cycle: 19.2 / 6 = **3.2 W**. Instantaneous loss on a conducting FET is **9.6 W**.

### 1.4 PCB copper conduction loss

Phase and bus trace resistance, using 2 oz copper (≈ 0.25 mΩ/sq) and about 4 squares effective across paralleled layers:

$$R_{cu} \approx 0.25\,\text{mΩ},\quad P_{cu} = 80^2 \times 0.25\,\text{mΩ} \approx \mathbf{1.6\ W}\ \text{per motor}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg per motor is PWM-switched at any time.

| Loss term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | P_sw = ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 20 ns × 48 k | **0.97 W** |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | P_dt = V_f · I · 2 t_dt · f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k | **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | P_rr = Q_rr · V · f_sw = 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | **0.05 W** |
| Output capacitance | P_oss = Q_oss · V · f_sw = 60 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | **0.07 W** |
| Gate drive (dissipated in the driver) | P_g = 2 · Q_g · V_gs · f_sw = 2 × 50 nC × 10 × 48 k | **0.05 W** |
| **Switching subtotal** | | **≈ 1.75 W** |

**Note on 48 kHz.** Switching loss scales linearly with frequency. At 24 kHz it falls to about 0.9 W, but conduction loss dominates either way (91 %). Reducing R_ds(on) is therefore the main lever, not lowering f_sw.

## 3. Thermal Calculation

### 3.1 Total dissipation at 80 A on all motors

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Per motor (conduction + copper + switching) | 19.2 + 1.6 + 1.75 ≈ **22.6 W** |
| Four motors | **≈ 90 W** |
| H743, ESC MCUs, regulators | ≈ 1.5 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 92 W** |

### 3.2 Board thermal capacitance (lumped model)

| Component | Calculation | Heat capacity |
|---|---|---|
| Copper | 8 × 70 µm × 400 mm² = 0.224 cm³ → 2.0 g × 0.385 J/gK | 0.77 J/K |
| FR4 | 1.6 mm × 4 cm² = 0.64 cm³ → 1.18 g × 1.1 J/gK | 1.30 J/K |
| 24 FETs plus components | ≈ 1.5 g × 0.7 J/gK | 1.05 J/K |
| **Total C_th** | | **≈ 3.1 J/K** |

### 3.3 Convection

Prop wash at about 10 m/s gives h ≈ 60 W/m²K. With both board faces exposed (8 cm²):

$$R_{θ,conv} \approx \frac{1}{60 \times 8\times10^{-4}} \approx 21\ \text{K/W}$$

Steady-state capacity at ΔT = 60 K is therefore about **3 W**. The board is purely heat-capacity-limited during bursts.

### 3.4 Transient temperature rise

$$\Delta T_{board}(t) = P R_θ \left(1 - e^{-t/τ}\right), \quad τ = R_θ C_{th} \approx 65\ \text{s}$$

For t ≪ τ:

$$\frac{dT}{dt} \approx \frac{P}{C_{th}} = \frac{92}{3.1} \approx \mathbf{30\ K/s}$$

| Burst length | ΔT_board | T_board (from 40 °C) | T_j (add P_FET × R_θJC + local spreading ≈ 25 K) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 s | 30 K | 70 °C | 95 °C ✅ |
| 2 s | 59 K | 99 °C | 124 °C ⚠️ limit |
| 5 s | 146 K | — | ❌ destroyed |
| 10 s | ≈ 290 K | — | ❌ |

The lumped model is optimistic: local hotspots under the FETs run 10 to 20 K above the board average.

### 3.5 Design rating

| Rating | Value |
|---|---|
| Burst | 80 A per motor for ≤ 1.5 s, one or two motors simultaneously |
| Continuous | ≈ sqrt(3 W / (4 × 2 × 1.5 mΩ)) ≈ **16 A per motor** with no heatsink. With an aluminium heatsink or a 30×30 board, 35 to 45 A. |

### 3.6 Required mitigations

- Use two paralleled FETs per switch position. This halves R_ds, cutting conduction loss to 9.6 W per motor.
- Enable AM32 current limiting (e.g., 55 A) and thermal foldback at 110 °C.
- Place NTCs next to the FETs for ESC telemetry.
- Map ArduPilot `ESC_TLM` temperature into failsafe and logging.

## 4. Bidirectional DShot600: Timing and DMA Model

### 4.1 Protocol timing

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Bit rate | 600 kbit/s → T_bit = 1.667 µs |
| Command frame | 16 bits → 26.7 µs |
| Telemetry turnaround (ESC) | ≈ 30 µs |
| Telemetry response | 21-bit GCR at 5/4 × 600 = 750 kbit/s → 1.333 µs/bit → 28 µs |
| **Total transaction** | ≈ 26.7 + 30 + 28 + guard ≈ **90 µs** |
| Maximum loop rate | ≈ 11 kHz theoretical. ArduPilot at 1 to 8 kHz loop has ample margin. |
