## 0. Architecture Decisions and Assumptions

Two points need settling before the math.

### 0.1 The H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC MCU

ArduPilot does not run motor commutation on the flight-controller MCU. Bi-directional DShot also implies a separate ESC processor that returns eRPM telemetry. The architecture is therefore:

| Block | Device (proposed) | Role |
|---|---|---|
| FC MCU | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100) | ArduPilot, DShot600 master, bdshot capture |

| Gate drivers (×4) | FD6288Q-class 3-phase half-bridge driver | Bootstrap high-side drive, built-in dead time |
| Power stage (×4) | 6× 40 V N-ch, 3.3×3.3 mm PQFN | 24 FETs total |

### 0.3 Feasibility warning

80 A per motor means 320 A total at the battery pads. On a 20×20 board this is a seconds-scale burst rating only. The thermal analysis in §3 shows this quantitatively. Treat "80 A" as a transient spec and derive the continuous rating from the board's thermal resistance, not from FET datasheets.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in synchronous six-step drive

In any 60° commutation sector, one phase is PWM-switched (complementary, synchronous rectification), one phase has its low-side FET held on, and one phase floats. With PWM duty D:

- **PWM-leg high-side FET:** conducts I for fraction D of the sector.
- **PWM-leg low-side FET (sync rectifier):** conducts I for fraction (1−D).
- **Static low-side FET:** conducts I for 100 % of the sector.

So at every instant exactly two FETs carry the motor current, independent of D:

$$P_{cond,inst} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) \approx R_{ds,25}\left[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha\approx 0.006\ /\mathrm{K} \;\Rightarrow\; k_T\approx1.5 \text{ at } 110^\circ\mathrm{C}$$

### 1.2 Per-ESC and per-FET values at 80 A

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2(80)^2(1.5\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

Each FET carries current for 1/3 of the electrical cycle, because each leg is active in 4 of 6 sectors, split between high and low side. The cycle-averaged loss per FET is:

$$\bar P_{FET} = \frac{19.2}{6} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

The instantaneous peak while a FET is conducting is $80^2 \times 1.5\,\mathrm{m\Omega} = 9.6\ \mathrm{W}$. The electrical period is under 1 ms at flight eRPM, far shorter than the FET's thermal time
