## 1. Field-Oriented Current Loop (20 kHz)

### 1.1 Transforms

Electrical angle from the mechanical encoder angle:
$$\theta_e = p\,(\theta_m - \theta_{\text{off}}) \bmod 2\pi$$

Clarke transform (amplitude-invariant; i_c is implied by i_a + i_b + i_c = 0 when only two shunts are trusted):
$$i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}(i_a + 2 i_b)$$

Park transform:
$$\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}$$

The inverse Park transform is the transpose of this matrix.

### 1.2 Plant and Decoupling

$$v_d = R i_d + L\dot i_d - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad v_q = R i_q + L\dot i_q + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e\lambda_m$$

where ω_e = p·ω_m and λ_m = Kt / (1.5 p).

The cross-coupling and back-EMF terms are cancelled by feedforward:
$$v_d^{ff} = -\hat\omega_e L i_q,\qquad v_q^{ff} = \hat\omega_e L i_d + \hat\omega_e \lambda_m$$

### 1.3 PI Design by Pole-Zero Cancellation

With decoupling, each axis reduces to 1/(Ls + R). Choosing

$$K_p = L\,\omega_c,\qquad K_i = R\,\omega_c$$

cancels the electrical pole at R/L. The closed loop then becomes a first-order lag with time constant 1/ω_c.

The bandwidth is limited by the PWM and computation delay (about 1.5 Ts):
$$\omega_c \le \frac{1}{3\,T_{s,i}} \approx 2\pi\cdot 1\,\text{kHz}$$

Default: ω_c = 2π·1000 rad/s.

### 1.4 Discrete PI with Back-Calculation Anti-Windup

$$
\begin{aligned}
u_k &= K_p e_k + x_k + u^{ff}_k\\
\bar u_k &= \text{sat}(u_k)\\
x_{k+1} &= x_k + T_s\big[K_i e_k + K_{aw}(\bar u_k - u_k)\big],\qquad K_{aw}=K_i/K_p
\end{aligned}
$$
