## 0. Architecture Clarification and Baseline Assumptions

**The STM32H743 does not drive the power stage.** In this class of product:

- **Flight controller (FC):** The H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bi-directional DShot600 on 4 timer channels.
- **4-in-1 ESC:** Each motor has its own small MCU (STM32G071 or AT32F421, running AM32 or BLHeli_32). That MCU generates the 48 kHz commutation PWM and answers DShot with eRPM telemetry.

So the "48 kHz switching" lives on the ESC MCUs, and the "DShot DMA" lives on the H743.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous PWM

During each 60° commutation step:

- Phase X high side (HS) switches at duty D.
- Phase X low side (LS) conducts synchronously for (1 − D).
- Phase Y low side is held on at 100%.

So at every instant the phase current flows through exactly **two** MOSFETs in series. Dead-time intervals use one body diode instead; that is accounted for in §2.

**Total conduction loss per motor:**

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

**Per-device distribution.** Each leg is active for 2/3 of the electrical cycle: 1/3 as the PWM leg and 1/3 as the static low leg.

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I^2 R \qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D) I^2 R + \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R$$

- Worst case for the LS FET is low duty (D → 0): P_LS → ⅔·I²R.
- At full-throttle bursts (D ≈ 0.95), HS and LS losses are nearly equal at ≈ ⅓·I²R each.

### 1.2 Electro-thermal fixed point

R_DS(on) depends on T_j, and T_j depends on loss. Solve iteratively:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_{board} + P_{FET}(T_j^{(k)})\cdot R_{\theta JB}$$

Taking T_j ≈ 110 °C as the converged value:

- R(110 °C) = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0055 × 85) = **1.47 mΩ**
- P_cond,motor = 2 × 80² × 1.47 mΩ = **18.8 W**
- Per FET, worst LS at low D: ⅔ × 6400 × 1.47 mΩ = **6.3 W**
- Per FET, full throttle: ≈ **3.1 W**

### 1.3 Battery vs. phase current

Battery current is I_bat ≈ D·I_ph. At D = 0.95 and 80 A phase current, that is 76 A per motor, or about 304 A total if all four motors burst simultaneously. This drives the XT60/XT90 connector choice, pad sizing, and bulk capacitor sizing.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Each loss term is per motor, evaluated at V = 25.2 V and I = 80 A.

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 k = **1.94 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_SD·I·2t_dt·f_sw | 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 50 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.06 W** |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss·V·f_sw | 60 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.07 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw | 2 × 50 nC × 10 × 48 k = **0.05 W** (dissipated in the driver) |
| **Total** | | **≈ 2.7 W per motor** |

**Sensitivity.** Overlap loss scales linearly with f_sw and with t_r + t_f. Moving from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves about 1.3 W per motor. However, it increases current ripple:

$$\Delta I = \frac{V(1-D)D}{L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

This matters for low-inductance motors (L_ph ≈ 10–20 µH).

## 3. Thermal Calculation

### 3.1 Total dissipation at 4 × 80 A burst

| Component | Value |
|---|---|
| Per motor: conduction + switching + shunt (0.25 mΩ effective × 80² ≈ 1.6 W) | ≈ 23.1 W |
| Board total (4 motors) | **≈ 92 W** |

### 3.2 Steady state is not achievable

A 36×36 mm board in free air has R_θ,amb ≈ 8–12 K/W even with prop wash. At 92 W that implies ΔT of hundreds of kelvin. Therefore **80 A is strictly a transient (burst) rating** and must be modeled with thermal capacitance.
