## 1. Electrical Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 1.1 Conduction losses

In 6-step drive with synchronous rectification, motor current always flows through exactly two MOSFET channels:

- one high-side FET (or, during the PWM off-time, its complementary low-side FET), and
- the low-side FET of the sinking phase.

The instantaneous conduction loss is therefore:

$$P_{cond}(t) = I_{ph}^2 \cdot \big(R_{DS,HS}(T_j) + R_{DS,LS}(T_j)\big) = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Temperature dependence uses a linearized coefficient (typically α ≈ 0.004–0.006 /K for 40 V trench FETs):

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big]$$

Taking α = 0.005 and T_j = 125 °C gives R_DS = 1.6 × 1.5 = **2.4 mΩ**.

At I = 80 A:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 0.0024 = \mathbf{30.7\ W\ per\ motor}$$

How this loss splits across the FETs:

- **Per FET, while conducting:** 80² × 0.0024 = 15.4 W.
- **Sinking (low-side) FET:** conducts its full 120° commutation interval.
- **Sourcing leg:** the 15.4 W is shared between the high-side FET (duty D) and the low-side synchronous FET (1 − D).
- **Commutation-averaged, per FET:** 30.7 / 6 ≈ **5.1 W**.

**Electrothermal feedback.** Because R_DS rises with T_j, conduction loss and temperature must be solved iteratively (see §2.4). Thermal runaway occurs if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T} \cdot R_\theta > 1 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 2 I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_\theta > 1$$

### 1.2 Switching losses at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only one half-bridge per motor hard-switches at any instant: the PWM leg. Its components are:

**(a) V-I overlap.** Inductive load, clamped:

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\text{ ns} \cdot 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

The rise and fall times can be estimated from the gate plateau:

$$t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,R_{g,tot}}{V_{DRV}-V_{plateau}}, \qquad t_{f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,R_{g,tot}}{V_{plateau}}$$

The asymmetry means turn-off is usually the dominant edge. Tune R_g,off separately with a diode-bypassed gate resistor.

**(b) Reverse recovery** of the synchronous FET's body diode, after dead-time conduction:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 40\text{ nC}\cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{ k} = 0.048\ W$$

Add roughly the same amount again in the opposing FET from the recovery current, giving ≈ **0.1 W**.

**(c) Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,C_{oss(er)}\,V_{bus}^2\,f_{sw} \cdot 2 = 0.03\ W$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction**, with t_dt = 100 ns per edge:

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\,I\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\text{ ns}\cdot 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive.** This is dissipated in the driver and R_g, not the FET:

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g\,V_{DRV}\,f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 50\text{ nC}\cdot 12 \cdot 48\text{ k} = 0.058\ W$$

**Switching total:**

$$P_{sw} \approx 1.94 + 0.10 + 0.03 + 0.61 = \mathbf{2.7\ W\ per\ motor}$$

**Sensitivity to switching frequency.** f_sw is linear in P_sw. Dropping to 24 kHz saves ≈ 1.3 W per motor at 80 A. This is small compared with conduction loss at this current. At 48 kHz the design is **conduction-dominated (~92%)**.
