## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, **two MOSFETs always carry the full phase current**:
- **PWM on-time (duty D):** high side of phase X plus low side of phase Y.
- **PWM off-time (1−D):** low side of phase X (synchronous rectification) plus low side of phase Y.

So the instantaneous channel conduction loss does not depend on duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph,rms}^2 \, R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + I_{ph,rms}^2\left(R_{Cu} + R_{shunt}\right)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence of R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}\ \text{(40 V trench, typical datasheet curve)}$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0055 × 85) = **1.47 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Phase RMS current with ripple

The phase current ripple is:

$$\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}-E)\,D}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}$$

With 6S, a typical 5 µH phase-to-phase inductance, D = 0.85 at burst, and back-EMF E ≈ 0.8 V_bus:

$$\Delta I = \frac{(25.2-20.2)(0.85)}{5\times10^{-6}\cdot 48\times10^{3}} = 17.7\ \text{A}_{pp}$$

$$I_{rms} = \sqrt{I_{dc}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}} = \sqrt{80^2 + 26.1} = 80.16\ \text{A}$$

The ripple contribution is negligible at 48 kHz, which is one argument for 48 kHz over 24 kHz.

### 1.4 Copper and shunt resistance

- The 2 oz sheet resistance is about 0.25 mΩ/□ at 25 °C, rising to about 0.33 mΩ/□ at 100 °C.
- The effective path is about 1.5 squares per leg on two paralleled layers, roughly 0.25 mΩ per leg.
- Two legs in the path give R_Cu ≈ **0.5 mΩ**.
- The low-side shunt is 0.5 mΩ (one per channel, bus-referenced).

### 1.5 Result at 80 A, T_j = 110 °C

| Term | Equation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| MOSFETs | 2 × 80.16² × 1.47 mΩ | **18.9 W** |
| PCB copper | 80.16² × 0.5 mΩ | 3.2 W |
| Shunt | 80.16² × 0.5 mΩ | 3.2 W |
| **Conduction total per channel** | | **25.3 W** |

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing leg switches. The other leg is static for the 60° commutation sector.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V-I overlap

The transition time is dominated by the Miller plateau:

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g}, \qquad I_g = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}{R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}}$$

With Q_gd = 9 nC, V_drv = 12 V, V_plateau = 3.5 V, R_g = 4.7 + 1.0 Ω:

$$I_g = 1.49\ \text{A}, \quad t_r \approx t_f \approx 6\ \text{ns} \; (+\text{ current-commutation time} \approx 10\ \text{ns}) \Rightarrow t_r = t_f \approx 16\ \text{ns}$$

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(32\times10^{-9})(48\times10^{3}) = \mathbf{1.55\ W}$$

### 2.2 Secondary switching terms

| Term | Equation | Values | Loss |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw | 45 nC | 0.054 W |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss · V_bus · f_sw (both devices in the leg) | 55 nC | 0.067 W |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_F · I · 2t_dt · f_sw | 0.8 V, t_dt = 50 ns | **0.31 W** |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver and R_g) | 2 · Q_g · V_drv · f_sw |
