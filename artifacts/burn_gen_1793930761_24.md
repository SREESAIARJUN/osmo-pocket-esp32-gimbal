# STM32H743 + 4-in-1 ESC AIO: Power-Stage, Thermal & Bi-Directional DShot Architecture

## 0. Assumptions and Key Findings

I have no access to a prior "Job #13" spec, so this design is built on the assumptions below. Any wrong assumption should be corrected first, because every number downstream depends on it.

| Parameter | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V max (worst-case switching), 22.2 V nominal |
| Board | 30.5 × 30.5 mm hole pattern, 36 × 36 mm outline, 8-layer, 2 oz on the 6 power layers |
| Burst rating | 80 A per motor, 5 s, at 40 °C ambient |
| ESC PWM | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous rectification), 6-step trapezoidal |
| Topology | H743 is the flight controller only. Four AT32F421 MCUs run AM32 firmware, one per ESC. |
| MOSFET (representative) | 40 V, R_DS(on) = 0.9 mΩ @ 25 °C, Q_g = 100 nC, R_θJC = 1.2 K/W, 5×6 PowerPAK class |
| Gate driver | FD6288Q-class 3-phase bootstrap driver, 1.0 A source / 1.5 A sink |

On topology: the H743 should not drive the gates directly. Bi-directional DShot only exists because a separate ESC MCU sits on the other end of the wire. That is the architecture used here.

**Key findings:**
1. **80 A burst is achievable for about 5 s.** The power stage dissipates about 80 W total with all four motors at 80 A.
2. **80 A continuous is physically impossible at this form factor.** Steady-state rise would exceed 250 K. The realistic continuous rating is about 35 A per motor with propwash cooling.
3. **Conduction loss dominates switching loss by about 10×.** Choose the FET by R_DS(on), not Q_g.
4. **Bi-directional DShot600 needs 5 DMA streams on one timer.** H7 DMAMUX makes this straightforward if all motors sit on TIM1.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 0.9 × 1.60 = **1.44 mΩ**. Always design at hot R_DS(on).

### 1.2 Current distribution in 6-step commutation

Each electrical period has six sectors. In any sector, two phases carry current I and one floats. Each phase leg therefore conducts for 2/3 of the period: the high-side for 1/3 and the low-side for 1/3.

With complementary PWM at duty D, during a high-side sector:
- the high-side FET conducts for a fraction D,
- the synchronous low-side FET of the same leg conducts for 1 − D,
- the opposite leg's low-side FET conducts continuously.

**Per-FET RMS current (high-side, phase A):**

$$I_{rms,HS}=I\sqrt{\tfrac{D}{3}},\qquad I_{rms,LS}=I\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{3}\left[(1-D)+1\right]\cdot\tfrac12}\ \text{(averaged over sectors)}$$

**Key simplification:** at any instant, exactly two FET channels carry I, regardless of D. With synchronous rectification, the freewheel path is also a channel rather than a diode. So:

$$\boxed{P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)}$$

$$\boxed{P_{cond,FET,avg} = \frac{I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)}{3}}\quad(\text{averaged over the 6 FETs})$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A burst

| Quantity | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Per motor | 2 × 80² × 1.44 mΩ | **18.4 W** |
| Per FET (average) | 18.4 / 6 | **3.07 W** |
| Single FET, worst sector-locked case (low eRPM / startup) | 80² × 1.44 mΩ | **9.2 W** (transient) |
| 4 motors | 4 × 18.4 | **73.7 W** |

Near stall, commutation is slow, so one FET can hold full current for milliseconds. Its junction then sees the sector-locked 9.2 W against the fast Z_th(j-c) time constant. This is what kills ESCs on desync, not the averaged number.

### 1.4 PCB copper conduction loss

Sheet resistance of 2 oz (70 µm) copper:

$$R_\square = \frac{\rho_{Cu}}{t}=\frac{1.72\times10^{-8}}{70\times10^{-6}} = 0.246\ \text{m}\Omega/\square \;(\times1.3 \text{ at } 100\,°\text{C} \Rightarrow 0.32\ \text{m}\Omega/\square)$$

Path per motor: battery pad → FET drain plane → phase pad, about 3 squares, with 4 power layers in parallel.

$$R_{Cu}= \frac{3\times0.32}{4}=0.24\ \text{m}\Omega \Rightarrow P_{Cu}=80^2\times0.24\text{ m}\Omega = 1.5\ \text{W/motor}$$

The shared battery-entry region carries the sum of all motors. Size the battery pads for 160 A peak, because realistic simultaneous bursts are not all 4 × 80 A.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model (f_sw = 48 kHz)

Only the PWM-ing leg switches. One hard-switched transition pair per PWM cycle occurs in the high-side/low-side complementary pair.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V-I overlap

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I\,(t_r+t_f)\, f_{sw}$$

Transition time comes from Miller-plateau charge and gate drive:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}+Q_{gs2}}{I_{g,on}},\quad I_{g,on}=\frac{V_{DRV}-V_{plateau}}{R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}+R_{drv,on}}$$
