## 0. Assumptions and Up-Front Findings

This package is self-consistent engineering analysis, but the device parameters are **representative values, not a verified BOM**. Every number marked ⚠ must be replaced with datasheet values for the parts you actually select.

**Finding 1: 80 A per motor is not a sustainable rating for a nano board.** The math in §3 shows that at 80 A per motor the power stage dissipates about **135 W total**. A 25×25 mm board can reject roughly **5 W continuously**. Therefore:
- "80 A burst" can only be honoured for about **1–2 s**.
- Continuous capability is roughly 20–25 A per motor at this size.

Market nano AIOs rated 40–45 A have the same physics problem. Treat their ratings with similar suspicion.

**Finding 2: The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** It is the flight controller MCU and runs ArduPilot. Each motor bridge still needs its own ESC MCU (e.g. AT32F421 or STM32G071) running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. The H743 only generates the bidirectional DShot (BDShot) signals and decodes the eRPM replies.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

In 6-step drive, one high-side and one low-side FET of different phases carry the motor current at any instant.

With complementary PWM at duty cycle D:
- During the ON time, current flows through the HS FET of phase A and the LS FET of phase B.
- During the OFF time, the current freewheels through the LS FET of phase A (synchronous rectification) and the LS FET of phase B.

In both intervals the current flows through exactly two channels. The bridge conduction loss is therefore **independent of duty cycle**:

$$
P_{cond,bridge} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

The temperature dependence is closed iteratively:

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.0053\ /\mathrm{K}\ ⚠
$$

**Numerical result at 80 A, hot:**

$$
P_{cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 2.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 6400 \times 0.005 = \mathbf{32.0\ W\ per\ motor}
$$

Averaged over a commutation cycle, each of the 6 FETs conducts about 1/3 of the time on its own leg:

$$
P_{cond,FET} \approx 32/6 \approx 5.3\ \text{W average}
$$

The instantaneous loss in a conducting FET is $I^2R = 16$ W.

**Copper term (not negligible).** Assume 2 oz copper (70 µm), with a 10 mm wide × 15 mm long current path per phase:

$$
R = \rho \frac{L}{wt} = 1.72\times10^{-8} \cdot \frac{0.015}{0.010 \times 70\times10^{-6}} = 0.37\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

Hot, with two phases in the path, this is about 0.9 mΩ, giving 6400 × 0.9 m ≈ **5.8 W per motor**.

Mitigations:
- Use 3 oz outer layers.
- Parallel the inner layers.
- Solder the phase pads directly at the FETs.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd high-side FET hard-switches. The low-side synchronous FET switches at near-zero V_DS (ZVS) because its body diode conducts first during dead time.

### 2.1 Hard-Switching Overlap

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 25\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.21\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Reverse Recovery (Low-Side Body Diode, Charged into the HS FET)

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.3 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction (Two Edges per Period)

$$
P_{dt} = V_f \, I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.4 Output Capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss} V^2 f = 0.5 \times 1\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = 0.015\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.5 Gate Drive (Two Switching FETs Active per Period, Dissipated in the Driver)

$$
P_g = 2 \, Q_g V_{dr} f = 2 \times 40\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.038\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.6 Switching Subtotal

**About 1.93 W per motor.** At 48 kHz, switching is about 5% of the loss budget. Conduction dominates, so it is better to buy lower R_DS(on) even at the cost of higher Q_g. The 48 kHz choice is justified: it gives low current ripple on low-inductance quad motors, and it is above audible range.
