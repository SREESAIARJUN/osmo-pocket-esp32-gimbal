# STM32H743 AIO Nano Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #9): System Architecture and Mathematical Model

**Document scope:** power-stage loss model, transient thermal model, ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 timer and DMA plan, and net-level schematic netlists.

**Status:** Engineering derivation. Every device parameter marked **[P]** is a placeholder. Replace it with the selected part's datasheet value before release. The equations stay valid when the values change.

## 0. Architectural Corrections

Three requirements need correcting before any mathematics is meaningful.

1. **The STM32H743 cannot be the ESC.** ArduPilot does not perform motor commutation. The topology must be:
   - The **H743 flight controller** sends DShot600 to **4 dedicated ESC MCUs**.
   - The ESC MCUs (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071) run AM32 or BLHeli_32.
   - Each ESC MCU drives its own 3-phase bridge.
   - Bidirectional DShot carries eRPM back to the H743 for the harmonic notch.
2. **The H743 does not come in a "nano" QFN.** The smallest practical package is **TFBGA100 (8×8 mm)**. That forces at least 6 layers (8 recommended) with via-in-pad or laser microvias under the BGA.
3. **"80 A burst" must be bounded.** It must be defined as 80 A per channel for a stated duration t_b. Section 3 shows that on a nano board (20×20 or 30.5×30.5 mm), 4×80 A is thermally limited to **about 2–4 seconds**, not sustained. The datasheet rating must state this.

**Assumed operating point (used throughout):**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Bus voltage | 6S Li-ion/LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max (22.2 V nominal) |
| PWM frequency | f_sw = 48 kHz |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

Use the power-law fit, which is more accurate than a linear α:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left(\frac{T_j + 273}{298}\right)^{k},\quad k \approx 2.0\text{–}2.5$$

With k = 2.3 and T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{ds}(125) = 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega \times (398/298)^{2.3} = 1.2 \times 1.946 \approx 2.33\,\text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step synchronous commutation

In each 60° sector, phase X is PWM'd high-side (duty D) and phase Y is held low-side ON.

- During the ON time (D), current flows through HS_X, the motor, and LS_Y.
- During the OFF time (1−D), with synchronous rectification, current flows through LS_X, the motor, and LS_Y.

Either way, **exactly two channels conduct the full phase current at all times**:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) + I^2 R_{Cu}$$

R_Cu is the PCB copper path (pads, planes, solder joints). For a nano board it is typically about 0.3–0.6 mΩ. Use 0.4 mΩ **[P]**. Extract the real value with a 4-wire measurement on the first prototype.

### 1.3 Numbers at I = 80 A

| T_j | R_ds | 2I²R_ds | I²R_Cu | P_cond per ESC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.20 mΩ | 15.4 W | 2.6 W | **18.0 W** |
| 100 °C | 2.03 mΩ | 26.0 W | 2.6 W | **28.6 W** |
| 125 °C | 2.33 mΩ | 29.8 W | 2.6 W | **32.4 W** |

### 1.4 Distribution per device

Over one electrical revolution, each of the 6 FETs carries the conduction load about 1/3 of the time. The high-side is split D / (1−D) with its complementary low-side.

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} \approx \frac{2 I^2 R_{ds}}{6}$$

At 80 A and T_j = 100 °C this is about **4.3 W average per FET**. The **instantaneous** per-sector loss is 12.9 W. That figure drives the Z_th(jc) ripple check at low eRPM (Section 3.4).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd half-bridge switches. That means one hard-switched leg per sector, per ESC.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard switching)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 0.97\text{ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery of the complementary body diode

The diode conducts during dead time, then gets hard-commutated.

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.060\text{ W}$$
