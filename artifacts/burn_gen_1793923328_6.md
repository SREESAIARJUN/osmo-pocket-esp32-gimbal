## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

**Partitioning.** The STM32H743 does not commutate motors. It runs ArduPilot and generates DShot. Each of the four ESC channels has its own commutation MCU, an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32. 48 kHz PWM is a supported AM32 setting.

All numbers below derive from these baseline assumptions. Substitute datasheet values for your BOM; every equation is parametric.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths

With synchronous (complementary) PWM, 6-step commutation always has two bridge legs carrying the full phase current I:

- **Driven leg:** the high-side FET conducts for duty D. The low-side FET synchronously rectifies for (1 − D).
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET conducts 100%.

So exactly two R_DS(on) are in series at every instant, regardless of duty. Dead-time body-diode conduction is handled separately in §2.4.

### 1.2 Equations

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right] \approx k_T \cdot R_{25}$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot \left(2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + R_{cu}\right)$$

Ripple correction for a motor phase current with peak-to-peak ripple ΔI:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

This term is less than 1% at 48 kHz with typical motor inductance (≥ 20 µH), so it is neglected.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 110 °C

- R_DS(on),hot = 1.6 × 1.2 mΩ = 1.92 mΩ
- P_FET,cond = 80² × 2 × 1.92 mΩ = **24.6 W per channel** (about 12.3 W per conducting FET)
- P_cu = 80² × 0.5 mΩ = **3.2 W per channel**

### 1.4 Thermal-runaway check

R_DS(on) rises with temperature, so conduction loss and junction temperature feed back on each other. Iterating the two equations:

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}(T_j)\, R_{\theta J\text{-}PCB}$$

$$P_{FET}(T_j) = I^2 R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right]$$

where α ≈ 0.0071/K (fitted to k_T = 1.6 at 110 °C). Solving for T_j:

$$T_j = \frac{T_{pcb} + I^2 R_{25}\, R_\theta (1 - 25\alpha)}{1 - \alpha\, I^2 R_{25}\, R_\theta}$$

**Stability condition:** α · I² · R_25 · R_θ < 1.

At 80 A: 0.0071 × 6400 × 1.2e-3 × 3.0 = **0.164**. The loop is stable, with a gain margin of about 6×.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switched leg incurs switching loss. That is one hard-switched FET plus one synchronous-rectifier FET per commutation state.

### 2.1 V-I overlap (hard-switched FET)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}\, V_{bus}\, I\, (t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}$$

$$= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

The transition times can be derived from the gate driver rather than assumed:

$$t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2} + Q_{gd}}{I_{g}}, \qquad I_g = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}{R_{g,ext} + R_{g,int} + R_{drv}}$$

### 2.2 Body-diode reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 50\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2}\, C_{oss}\, V_{bus}^2\, f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.023\ W}$$

Strictly, use the charge-equivalent C_o(er) from the datasheet.

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = 2\, V_f\, I\, t_d\, f_{sw} = 2 \times 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated in the driver and R_g, not in the FET:

$$P_{gate} = Q_g\, V_{drv}\, f_{sw} \times 2 = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 2.6 Per-channel loss budget (80 A burst, 25.2 V, 48 kHz)

| Mechanism | Loss (W) | Share |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 24.6 | 80.6% |
| Copper/trace I²R | 3.2 | 10.5% |
| V-I switching | 1.45 | 4.8% |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 | 2.0% |
| Q_rr | 0.06 | 0.2% |
| C_oss | 0.02 | <0.1% |
| Gate drive (driver) | 0.05 | 0.2% |
| **Total per channel** | **≈ 30.0** | |
| **Total, 4 channels simultaneous** | **≈ 120** | |

**Key insight.** At 80 A the design is conduction-dominated, with roughly 20:1 conduction to switching. Moving from 24 kHz to 48 kHz costs only about 2 W per channel. The design lever is R_DS(on) and copper, not frequency.

**Optimal-frequency check.** Switching loss scales as k·f, so the marginal cost of frequency is:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial f} = \frac{1.45 + 0.61 + 0.08}{48\,\text{k}} \approx 45\ \mu\text{W/Hz}$$

48 kHz is justified by reduced current ripple and acoustic noise on low-inductance motors.

**Electrical efficiency at burst:**

$$P_{out} \approx 25.2 \times 80 \times D_{eff}$$

At full throttle (D ≈ 1), that is about 2016 W per channel. Efficiency is therefore η ≈ 1 − 30/2016 ≈ **98.5%**
