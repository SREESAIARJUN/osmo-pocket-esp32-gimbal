# STM32H743 AIO 4-in-1 ESC Flight Controller (Job #36): System Architecture & Mathematical Model

**Rev A, Engineering Baseline**

---

## 0. Feasibility Findings

These findings constrain everything that follows.

1. **An 80 A *phase* burst on all four channels at once is thermally transient-only on any nano/AIO form factor.** The model in §4 shows about 125 W of dissipation. On a 30.5 × 30.5 mm board the safe window is about **2–4 s**. On a 20 × 20 mm board it is shorter. "80 A burst" must be specified as **single-motor, ≤10 s** or **all-motor, ≤3 s at 25 °C ambient**. Otherwise the board needs active airflow credit, which props provide but which must be validated.
2. **The H743 does not drive the gates directly.** The standard and recommended architecture is H743 → DShot → **4 × dedicated ESC MCUs** (AT32F421 running AM32, or BLHeli_32-class). These generate the 48 kHz complementary PWM. ArduPilot does not run commutation.
3. **Specify 80 A as phase (motor) current, not battery current.** I_bus ≈ D · I_phase. Conduction loss scales with I_phase², so this distinction matters.
4. **The IMU must be thermally and mechanically isolated from the power stage.** The ESC copper reaches 90–110 °C in a burst. That causes IMU bias drift and violates the STM32H743xI**6** limit of 85 °C ambient. Use a stacked or isolated-island layout, or specify the ...**3** (125 °C) grade.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Commutation topology

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous (complementary) PWM. In every commutation sector, one phase has its high side PWM'd with a complementary low side, a second phase has its low side held ON, and the third phase floats for BEMF sensing.

Because of synchronous rectification, current always flows through **exactly two FET channels in series**, independent of duty D:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds(on)}(T_j)
$$

### 2.2 Temperature dependence

$$
R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big]
$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R = 1.5 mΩ × (1 + 0.0055 × 85) = **2.20 mΩ**

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.20\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{28.2\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Per-FET distribution

Each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical cycle, either as the PWM device, the sync device, or the held-on low side. Averaged over a commutation cycle:

$$
\bar P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{3} = 9.4\ \text{W (per FET, 6 FETs/ch)}
$$

The low-side FETs carry slightly more at low D, because the complementary device conducts (1−D). The high-side FETs carry more at high D. **Size for the low side at partial throttle.** That is the realistic burst case: a punch-out at 60–80 % D with peak current.

### 2.4 Copper and shunt losses

$$
P_{cu,ch} = I_{ph}^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.3\times10^{-3} = 1.92\ \text{W}
$$
