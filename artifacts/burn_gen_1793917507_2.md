# STM32H743 Nano AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Systems Architecture (Job #53)

## 0. Architectural corrections

The brief contains three problems that must be resolved before any model is meaningful.

| Issue | Problem | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| **H743 as ESC controller** | ArduPilot on the H743 cannot also run four sensorless BLDC commutation loops at 48 kHz. Bidirectional DShot also requires a separate ESC MCU to respond to. | H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. Four dedicated ESC MCUs (AT32F421 class, running AM32) handle the 48 kHz PWM. |
| **"Nano" + 80 A per channel** | On a 20×20 mm board, 4 × 80 A = **320 A** from the battery. No 20×20 copper stack or connector handles that continuously. | 80 A is treated strictly as a **time-limited burst**. Section 2.5 derives the permissible burst duration. A 30.5×30.5 mm form factor is strongly recommended. |
| **MOSFET package** | A 20×20 board fits at most 24 × 3.3×3.3 mm DFN FETs. It does not fit 5×6 mm parts or paralleled FETs. | Model uses one FET per switch position, 40 V, R_DS(on) ≈ 1.2 mΩ @ 25 °C (representative of 3.3×3.3 DFN class). **Substitute real datasheet values before release.** |

**Operating point:** 6S, V_bus = 25.2 V (fully charged, worst case for switching), f_sw = 48 kHz, I_phase = 80 A burst, T_ambient = 40 °C.

## 1. Conduction-loss model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.0065\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

At the design limit T_j = 110 °C: R_DS = 1.2 mΩ × 1.55 = **1.86 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Trapezoidal 6-step commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM

At any instant, two phase legs carry current:

- **PWM'd leg:** the high-side FET conducts for D, and the synchronous low-side for (1 − D). One FET is therefore always in the path (ignoring dead time).
- **Static leg:** one low-side FET is fully on.

$$
P_{cond,ESC} = I^2 \left[ D R_{HS} + (1-D) R_{LS} + R_{LS} \right] \approx 2 I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

$$
P_{cond,ESC} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.86\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{23.8\ W}
$$

Each FET conducts on average 1/3 of the electrical period, in either the PWM or static role:

$$
P_{cond,FET,avg} \approx 23.8/6 \approx 4.0\ \text{W}
$$

The **instantaneous** dissipation in the static FET is 80² × 1.86 mΩ = **11.9 W**. This drives junction ripple at low eRPM (Section 2.4).

### 1.3 PCB copper conduction

For 2 oz (70 µm) copper:

$$
R_\square = \rho / t = 1.72\times10^{-8} / 70\times10^{-6} = 0.246\ \text{m}\Omega/\square
$$

Assume a phase path of ≈5 squares, paralleled over 4 layers, with temperature correction. This gives ≈0.35 mΩ effective, plus the shared battery bus.

$$
P_{Cu,phase} \approx 80^2 \times 0.35\text{ m}\Omega = 2.2\ \text{W per ESC}
$$

The shared battery bus carries 320 A. Even at 0.1 mΩ it dissipates **10 W**. This is why the battery pad and bus layout dominate the design.

## 2. Switching-loss model at 48 kHz

The two PWM'd switches per ESC carry the hard-switching losses.

### 2.1 V-I overlap loss

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}
$$

Using t_r + t_f ≈ 40 ns (driver ≈ 1 A peak, Q_gd ≈ 8 nC, R_g ≈ 4.7 Ω):

$$
P_{VI} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two transitions per period, with t_dt = 200 ns and V_f ≈ 0.8 V:

$$
P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}
$$

Cutting dead time to 100 ns halves this. Validate shoot-through margin on hardware first.

### 2.3 Reverse recovery, C_oss, and gate charge

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse recovery (Q_rr ≈ 50 nC) | Q_rr · V · f | 0.06 W |
| C_oss (Q_oss ≈ 25 nC) | ½ Q_oss · V · f | 0.015 W |
| Gate drive (Q_g ≈ 30 nC, 10 V, 2 FETs) | 2 Q_g V_gs f | 0.03 W (in the driver) |

### 2.4 Per-ESC and board totals

| Loss | W / ESC | W / board (×4) |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 23.8 | 95.2 |
| Switching V-I | 1.94 | 7.8 |
| Dead time | 1.23 | 4.9 |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.11 | 0.4 |
| Phase copper | 2.2 | 8.8 |
| Battery bus | — | ~10 |
| **Total** | **≈29.3** | **≈127 W** |

**Key insight:** conduction dominates (~81%). Lowering f_sw from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves only ~1.6 W per ESC. **R_DS(on) and copper are the levers, not frequency.** 48 kHz is justified for acoustic and current-ripple reasons, not efficiency.
