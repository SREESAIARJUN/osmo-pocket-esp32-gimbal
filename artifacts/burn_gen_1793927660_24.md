# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #33): Power-Stage Loss Model, BDShot600 Timing and DMA Allocation, Thermal Budget and Netlist

## 0. Feasibility Finding

Before the derivation, here is the conclusion the math leads to:

> **An 80 A per-channel rating on a nano (20×20 mm) AIO cannot be continuous. It is an adiabatic burst rating of roughly 1 s.** The board dissipates about 100 W at 4×80 A. It has about 1.7 J/K of thermal mass and can reject only about 5 W to air. The realistic continuous rating is **15–20 A per channel**.

The rest of this document gives the model behind that number, so you can re-run it with your actual BOM.

**Architecture assumption.** The H743 runs ArduPilot only. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU (AT32F421 running AM32 is used as the example) driving a triple half-bridge gate driver. Running four sensorless motor-control loops on the H743 alongside ArduPilot is not a supported ArduPilot configuration. BDShot telemetry also requires a separate ESC firmware endpoint.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Commutation topology

Use 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM, the AM32/BLHeli default. In each 60° sector:

- **Active leg:** the high-side FET conducts for D·T. The synchronous low-side FET of the same leg conducts for (1−D)·T.
- **Return leg:** its low-side FET conducts 100% of the sector.
