### Architecture Clarification

The STM32H743 is the **flight controller** MCU running ArduPilot. It does not commutate motors. Each ESC channel has its own commutation MCU (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32). The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to those four MCUs and receives eRPM telemetry back on the same wire. Section 4 covers the H743 side, which is where ArduPilot's timer/DMA constraints apply.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Paths in Synchronous 6-Step

In any commutation sector, two legs carry current and the third phase floats:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for duty D, and the low-side conducts (synchronous rectification) for (1−D), excluding dead time.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET conducts for 100% of the sector.

Phase current is approximately constant over a PWM period because motor inductance makes ripple small at 48 kHz. Per ESC channel:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

The result is **independent of duty cycle**. That is the key property of synchronous rectification, and the reason 80 A burst at low throttle is just as thermally demanding as at full throttle. (Dead-time conduction through the body diode is accounted separately in §2.)

### 1.2 Temperature Dependence

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)]
$$

This creates positive electrothermal feedback. The self-consistent steady-state junction temperature is:

$$
T_j = T_{ref} + R_{\theta}\,\cdot 2I^2R_{ds,25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
\;\Rightarrow\;
T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + k\,(1-25\alpha)}{1 - k\alpha},\quad k = 2I^2R_{ds,25}R_\theta
$$

**Thermal runaway occurs when kα ≥ 1.** This criterion is used in §3.

|---|---|---|---|

### 1.4 Copper and Interconnect Loss

This term is not negligible at 80 A. Assume the phase path is 15 mm long, 4 mm wide, with 2 oz copper on 2 layers in parallel:

$$
R_{Cu} = \rho\frac{L}{A} = 1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot\frac{0.015}{0.004\cdot 70\times10^{-6}\cdot 2}\approx 0.46\,\text{m}\Omega
$$

Two phase paths are active: P = 2·80²·0.46 m ≈ **5.9 W per channel**. Add solder-pad and motor-wire joint resistance of about 0.2 mΩ, which is roughly another 2.6 W.

The DC bus copper carries I_bus = D·I_phase per channel, summed over all four channels. This term matters most at high throttle.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. The return-leg FET is static within a sector.

### 2.1 Hard-Switching V·I Overlap (High-Side Turn-On and Turn-Off)

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus}\, I\,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}
= 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot20\text{ ns}\cdot48\text{ kHz} = 0.97\text{ W}
$$

Switching times follow from gate charge:

$$
t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}+Q_{gs2}}{I_{g,on}},\qquad I_{g,on}=\frac{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}{R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}}
$$

With Q_gd + Q_gs2 ≈ 8 nC, V_drv = 10 V, V_plateau ≈ 3.5 V, and R_g,total ≈ 8 Ω: I_g ≈ 0.81 A, so t_r ≈ 10 ns. This is consistent with the assumption.

### 2.2 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\cdot 2t_{dt}\, f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot400\text{ ns}\cdot48\text{ kHz} = 1.23\text{ W}
$$

This exceeds the V·I loss. **Dead-time tuning is the highest-leverage switching knob.** Reducing it to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per channel, but only if shoot-through margin is verified on the bench.

### 2.3 Reverse Recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{ nC}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{ k}=0.036\text{ W}
$$

The bigger consequence of Q_rr is EMI and ringing at the switch node, not the loss itself.
