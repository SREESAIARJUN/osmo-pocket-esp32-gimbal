## 0. Architecture Assumptions and an Early Red Flag

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal |
| Phase current | 80 A **burst** per ESC (all 4 simultaneously = worst case) |
| PWM frequency | 48 kHz, center-aligned, complementary (synchronous rectification) |
| ESC MCU | Separate per-channel MCU (e.g., AT32F421 running AM32). The H743 does **not** commutate motors. It only sends DShot. |
| FC ↔ ESC | Bidirectional DShot600, one wire per motor |
| Form factor | 20×20 mm mounting (~25×25 mm board outline) |

**Red flag:** An 80 A/channel burst on a 20×20 stack is at the edge of physical feasibility. The analysis in §2 shows about 134 W of total dissipation at burst. On roughly 2 J/K of board thermal mass, that sustains only about 1–2 seconds before limits are hit. The math below quantifies this. The realistic outcome is an **80 A burst / ~40–45 A continuous** rating, or a move to a 30.5×30.5 board.

## 1. Power Stage Topology (per ESC channel)

- 3 half-bridges, giving 6 N-channel MOSFETs per motor and 24 total.
- 6-step trapezoidal commutation. At any instant, one high-side FET is PWM-switching and one low-side FET is conducting.
- Complementary PWM: during the high-side off-time, the same-leg low-side FET carries the freewheel current. The body diode conducts only during dead-time.

## 2. Loss Model

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

Temperature dependence of on-resistance:

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big]$$

High-side conduction over the PWM cycle is D·I², and the complementary low-side carries (1−D)·I². The switching leg therefore dissipates I²·R regardless of duty. The second active phase's low-side carries I continuously. **Per ESC:**

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\underbrace{\big[D + (1-D)\big]}_{\text{switching leg}} + \underbrace{I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)}_{\text{return leg}} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

At I = 80 A and T_j = 115 °C (R_DS ≈ 1.5 × 1.585 ≈ 2.38 mΩ):

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 6400 \times 0.00238 \approx \mathbf{30.4\ W\ per\ ESC}$$

This splits as ~15.2 W in the hottest two FETs. Commutation rotates the load across all 6 FETs. At high eRPM (commutation period ≪ FET thermal time constant of ~10 ms), the average per-FET loss is ≈ P_cond/3 ≈ 10 W.

**Copper/shunt term:** add I²·R_path. Assume 0.3 mΩ for traces, pads and solder per phase path × 2 paths:

$$P_{Cu} = 2 \times 6400 \times 0.0003 \approx 3.8\ W$$

### 2.2 Switching Losses at f_sw = 48 kHz

Hard-switched turn-on/off (linear V-I overlap approximation):

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 1.94\ W$$

Reverse recovery of the complementary FET's body diode:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.060\ W$$

Output capacitance energy:

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12\,Q_{oss}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 40\text{n}\times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.024\ W$$

Dead-time body-diode conduction (two dead-time intervals per cycle):

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\,I\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n}\times 48\text{k} = 0.61\ W$$

Gate drive (2 FETs switching per cycle; dissipated mainly in driver and gate resistors, not the FETs):

$$P_{gate} = 2\,Q_g\,V_{GS}\,f_{sw} = 2\times 50\text{n}\times 10\times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ W$$

### 2.3 Loss Budget at 80 A Burst

| Term | Per ESC (W) | ×4 (W) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 30.4 | 121.6 |
| Copper / shunt | 3.8 | 15.2 |
| V-I switching | 1.94 | 7.8 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 | 2.4 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.08 | 0.3 |
| Gate drive | 0.05 | 0.2 |
| **Total** | **≈ 36.9** | **≈ 147.5** |
| FET-only (junction heating) | ≈ 33.0 | ≈ 132 |

**Key insight:** At 80 A, conduction is about 90% of the loss. Dropping f_sw from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves only ~1.3 W per ESC. **R_DS(on) and copper are the levers, not switching frequency.** Keep 48 kHz for motor smoothness and acoustic/current-ripple reasons.

**Efficiency check** (approx. 80 A × 22.2 V × D ≈ 0.9 → ≈ 1.6 kW electrical per ESC): ≈ 97.7%. The efficiency is fine; the problem is power density.
