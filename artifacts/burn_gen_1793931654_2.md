## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\text{–}0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_DS ≈ 1.5 × 1.47 ≈ **2.2 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-step bridge with synchronous rectification

In any commutation sector, current I flows through two legs:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side (HS) conducts for fraction D, and the synchronous low-side (LS) conducts for (1−D).
- **Return leg:** the LS is fully on.

The total conduction loss per ESC channel is therefore:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

This result is **independent of duty cycle**, which is a useful property for worst-case design.

At I = 80 A and R_DS = 2.2 mΩ:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 6400 \times 0.0022 = \mathbf{28.2\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET loss

Each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical period, so the per-FET loss splits into an average and an instantaneous value:

- **Average per FET:** P_cond,ch / 6 ≈ **4.7 W**.
- **Instantaneous on the "fully-on" LS during its sector:** I²·R = **14.1 W**.

The instantaneous figure matters for Z_th at low eRPM, when sectors are long.

### 1.4 Converging the model

Iterate T_j → R_DS → P → T_j until the change is below 1 K. Starting at 110 °C, the model converges within 2–3 iterations if a heat path exists. If T_j diverges, the operating point is thermally unstable (runaway).

## 2. Switching Losses at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM-leg HS is hard-switched. The synchronous LS switches near zero volts but adds dead-time diode and reverse-recovery loss.

### 2.1 Hard-switching (V–I overlap)

Switching time is set by the gate charge and the driver current, assuming I_g,eff ≈ 1 A:

$$t_{r} \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{g}} = 15\ \text{ns}$$

Use t_r + t_f = 40 ns to include layout inductance. The overlap loss is then:

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

With t_dt = 100 ns per edge:

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery and output capacitance

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.05\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Gate drive

Gate-drive loss is dissipated mainly in the driver IC, not the FET:

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\times30\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k} = 0.03\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Totals at 80A burst

| Quantity | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 28.2 W | 112.8 W |
| Switching (overlap + dead-time + Q_rr + Q_oss) | 2.66 W | 10.6 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 30.9 W** | **≈ 123.4 W** |

**Key insight:** at 80A, conduction dominates (~91%). Raising f_sw from 24 kHz to 48 kHz costs only about 1.3 W per channel. **R_DS(on) and copper are the levers, not frequency.**
