# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #15): Power, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

## 0. Architecture Baseline and Feasibility Verdict

**System partition.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller and does not commutate motors. Each of the 4 ESC channels has its own MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The 48 kHz switching frequency is an ESC-firmware parameter. The H743's job is to generate bidirectional DShot600 and decode eRPM telemetry.

| Item | Assumption (replace with your BOM values) |
|---|---|
| Form factor | 20×20 mm (M3/M2 mounting), stacked FC + ESC |
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus,max = 25.2 V |
| MOSFET | 40 V N-ch, PDFN 3.3×3.3. R_DS(on) = 0.75 mΩ @25 °C (V_GS=10 V), Q_g = 60 nC, Q_gd = 12 nC, Q_rr = 50 nC, Q_oss = 60 nC |
| Gate driver | 3× half-bridge (FD6288Q-class), ~1.5 A source/sink effective |
| ESC MCU | AT32F421 (AM32), complementary PWM, dead time 100 ns |
| PWM | 48 kHz, center-aligned, active freewheeling |
| Burst | 80 A per channel |

**Verdict.** As derived below, each channel dissipates about 17.6 W at 80 A, so 4 channels dissipate about 70 W. On a 20×20 board this is a burst of roughly 1–2 s, not a continuous rating. Simultaneous 320 A battery current on a 20×20 board is outside realistic copper and connector limits.

Recommended spec label: **"80 A burst (≤1 s, single-channel-dominant), 45–55 A continuous with airflow."** If a sustained 4×80 A rating is mandatory, move to 30×30.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

$$R_{DS}(125\,^\circ C) = 0.75\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.6 = 1.20\ \text{m}\Omega$$

Use the hot value. This feedback is what causes thermal runaway in bursts.

### 1.2 Six-step trapezoidal with synchronous rectification

In any 60° commutation sector, phase A is PWM-driven (HS at duty D, LS complementary at 1−D) and phase B has its LS fully on. Current always flows through exactly two channels:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 R_{DS}\,D + I^2 R_{DS}(1-D) + I^2 R_{DS} = 2 I^2 R_{DS}$$

The result is independent of D, which is the key benefit of synchronous rectification.

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.20\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{15.36\ W\ per\ motor}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution (hottest device)

Each FET is "static-on" for 1/3 of the electrical cycle and PWM-active for 1/3. The worst-case instantaneous device is the static LS:

$$P_{LS,static} = I^2 R_{DS} = 7.68\ \text{W (instantaneous)}$$

Cycle-averaged per FET at D ≈ 0.9 (full-throttle burst):

$$\bar P_{HS} = \tfrac13 I^2R\,D + \tfrac13 I^2 R \approx \tfrac13(6.91 + 7.68) = 4.86\ \text{W}$$

At electrical frequencies above about 200 Hz, the FET thermal mass averages this, so 4.9 W per FET is the design number. Add switching loss to the HS (section 2).

### 1.4 Copper and shunt conduction

| Path | R | P @ 80 A / 320 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase trace, 2 oz × 4 layers, 3 mm × 6 mm | ≈ 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W/motor |
| Shunt, 0.25 mΩ, on battery return | 0.25 mΩ | **25.6 W @ 320 A (unacceptable)** |
| Shunt, 0.1 mΩ, 2512 | 0.1 mΩ | 10.2 W @ 320 A (burst only) |

Recommendation: use a 0.1 mΩ shunt with a high-gain amplifier (INA186A5, gain 200 V/V). That gives 320 A → 6.4 V, so clamp to 3.3 V. Scale for about 160 A full-scale, or use a Hall sensor (ACS772-class) on 30×30.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard-switched HS only; LS turns on ZVS after dead time)

Plateau-charge switching time:

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd} + Q_{gs2}}{I_{G}} = \frac{12\,\text{nC} + 3\,\text{nC}}{1.0\,\text{A}} = 15\ \text{ns (each edge)}$$

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$
