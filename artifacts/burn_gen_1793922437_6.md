## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-Dependent On-Resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 100 °C: R = 1.5 × (1 + 0.0055 × 75) = **2.12 mΩ**.

Add the PCB copper path per conducting FET. A typical value is ~0.3 mΩ for 2 oz inner pours plus vias:

$$R_{path} = R_{DS}(T_j) + R_{Cu} \approx 2.42\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Conducting Devices per Commutation Step

In 6-step drive, two phases conduct. Take phase A as the PWM'd high leg and phase B as the static low leg:

- **A-high:** conducts during D·T_sw
- **A-low (synchronous rectifier):** conducts during (1−D)·T_sw, minus dead time
- **B-low:** conducts 100 % of the step

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2\left[R_{HS}D + R_{LS}(1-D) + R_{LS}\right] \approx 2\,I_{ph}^2 R_{path}$$

This is independent of D when R_HS ≈ R_LS.

At 80 A and T_j = 100 °C:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 6400 \times 2.42\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{31.0\ W\ per\ motor\ channel}$$

### 1.3 Per-Device Distribution

This matters for junction temperature. The static low-side FET carries I² R for the full commutation step, which is ⅓ of the electrical period for that device:

| Device role | Instantaneous loss | Averaged over the electrical cycle (each FET spends ⅓ in each role) |
|---|---|---|
| Static LS | 6400 × 2.12 mΩ = 13.6 W | — |
| PWM HS (D = 0.9) | 12.2 W | — |
| Sync LS (D = 0.9) | 1.4 W | — |
| **Average per FET** | | ≈ (13.6 + 12.2 + 1.4)/3 + switching share ≈ **9.5–10 W** |

At high eRPM the role rotation is fast (>1 kHz), much faster than the FET's thermal time constant. The cycle average is therefore valid for T_j, as long as the motor is actually spinning. A **stalled or locked rotor at 80 A** puts 13.6 W continuously into one FET. That is the true worst case and must be protected in firmware.

### 1.4 Phase vs. Battery Current

$$I_{bat} \approx D \cdot I_{ph}$$

Marketing "80 A burst" ratings usually refer to battery current. At D = 0.85, 80 A battery current implies **~94 A phase**, so conduction loss scales up by 1.38×. Always specify which current you mean.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches. Each loss term below is per motor channel.

**1) V–I overlap (hard-switched high side):**
$$P_{ov} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(35\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = 1.69\ \text{W}$$

**2) Reverse recovery of the low-side body diode:**
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$
The Q_rr-induced current spike also adds overlap loss. Budget about +0.2 W.

**3) Output capacitance:**
$$P_{oss} = E_{oss} f_{sw} \approx 0.3\mu \times 48\text{k} = 0.014\ \text{W}$$

**4) Dead-time body-diode conduction:**
$$P_{dt} = V_f I_{ph}(2t_{dt})f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{n}\times48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

**5) Gate drive** (dissipated mainly in the driver and R_g, not the FET):
$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2\times30\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k} = 0.029\ \text{W}$$

$$\boxed{P_{sw,ch} \approx 2.6\ \text{W}} \qquad \boxed{P_{tot,ch} \approx 31.0 + 2.6 = 33.6\ \text{W}}$$

**Four motors at simultaneous 80 A burst: ≈ 134 W of heat.**

### 2.1 Frequency Sensitivity

$$P_{sw}(f) \approx 2.6 \times \frac{f}{48\text{k}}$$

At 96 kHz, switching loss rises to 5.2 W, while conduction loss is unchanged. At this current level, conduction loss dominates about 12:1. Lowering R_DS(on) or paralleling FETs pays off far more than slowing the PWM.

### 2.2 DC-Link Capacitor Ripple

This is often the real failure point.

$$I_{C,rms} \approx I_{ph}\sqrt{D(1-D)} \ \Rightarrow\ \max = 0.5\,I_{ph} = \mathbf{40\ A_{rms}}\ \text{per channel (at } D=0.5)$$

Required capacitance:
- Local MLCC per channel: ≥ 4× 10 µF/50 V X7R 1206 near each half-bridge. Account for DC-bias derating, which leaves roughly 2.5 µF effective each at 25 V.
- Bulk: low-ESR electrolytic (≥ 470 µF/35 V) at the pads.
