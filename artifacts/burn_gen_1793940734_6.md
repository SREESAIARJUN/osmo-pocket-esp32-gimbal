## 0. Architecture Decisions and Red Flags

| Item | Decision |
|---|---|
| Flight controller MCU | STM32H743VIH6 (TFBGA100, 8×8 mm), the only H743 package practical on a nano board |
| ESC MCUs | 4× AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32. The H743 does **not** commutate motors. It sends DShot600 and receives bidirectional eRPM telemetry. |
| Bridge | 4 × 3-phase, 24 N-MOSFETs, 40 V class, PQFN 3.3×3.3 |
| Gate drive | 3-phase driver per ESC (e.g., FD6288Q class), 10 V gate rail |
| Battery | 6S max (25.2 V full), 40 V FETs give ~37% margin over full charge plus ringing |

**Feasibility red flags. Read these before committing:**

1. **80 A per channel on a 20×20 mm board cannot be sustained.** The math below shows about 25 W of loss per motor at 80 A, or about 100 W with all four motors loaded. This is a **burst rating of roughly 1–2 s**, not a continuous rating. A credible continuous rating on 20×20 is about 25–35 A per channel. If 80 A is a hard requirement, move to a 30.5×30.5 mm form factor.
2. **Total battery current reaches 320 A at 4×80 A.** A 0.1 mΩ shunt would dissipate 10 W. Use a Hall-effect current sensor (e.g., ACS-type, off-board) or sum per-ESC sensing.
3. **Rated current depends on the FET choice.** Every number below scales linearly with R_DS(on) and Q_gd.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Topology Insight

The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification, which is AM32's default. At any instant, motor current flows through **exactly two conducting devices**:

- one in the high-side path of the sourcing leg (HS FET, or the LS FET acting as a synchronous rectifier during PWM off-time), and
- one LS FET in the sinking leg.

This holds regardless of duty cycle. It gives a compact bridge-level expression:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Per-Device Distribution

Over one electrical cycle each leg is active for 240° (120° sourcing, 120° sinking). With duty cycle D:

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,D\,I^2 R,\qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)\,I^2R + \tfrac{1}{3}\,I^2R$$

The LS FET carries more loss than the HS FET at all duty cycles. **Place LS FETs on the best thermal copper.**

### 1.3 Temperature Coefficient

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\text{–}0.007\ /\text{K}$$

### 1.4 Numbers

Assumptions:
- **[A]** R₂₅ = 1.2 mΩ
- T_j = 110 °C, giving R_hot ≈ 1.2 × 1.55 ≈ **1.86 mΩ**
- I = 80 A, D = 0.9 (full-throttle burst)

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| P_cond, bridge | 2 · 6400 · 1.86 mΩ = **23.8 W** |
| P_HS (each, averaged) | (1/3)(0.9)(6400)(1.86 m) = 3.57 W |
| P_LS (each, averaged) | (1/3)(1.1)(6400)(1.86 m) = 4.37 W |
| Check: 3·(3.57 + 4.37) | 23.8 W ✓ |

**Add PCB copper loss.** For phase and VBAT traces, use R = ρL/(w·t) with ρ_Cu(100 °C) ≈ 2.2×10⁻⁸ Ω·m.

Example: 8 mm path, 4 mm wide, 4 layers × 70 µm in parallel gives about 0.16 mΩ. The current flows through two such paths, so:

$$P_{Cu} \approx 2 \cdot 6400 \cdot 0.16\,\text{m} \approx 2.0\ \text{W per motor}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only one leg switches per PWM period in 6-step operation. Each event has a hard turn-on and a hard turn-off.

### 2.1 Transition Times from Gate Charge

The Miller plateau dominates:

$$t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,(R_{drv,on}+R_g+R_{g,int})}{V_{DRV}-V_{pl}},\qquad t_{f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,(R_{drv,off}+R_g+R_{g,int})}{V_{pl}}$$
