## 0. Feasibility Findings

Several things need to be settled before the math. They bound everything that follows.

| # | Finding | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | The STM32H743 is the **flight controller** MCU. It does not commutate motors. A 4-in-1 ESC needs its own commutation MCUs, typically 4× AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32 class firmware. The H743 talks to them over bi-directional DShot. | The architecture has two MCU domains: 1× FC and 4× ESC. |
| F2 | 4 × 80 A at 6S (25.2 V) is **≈ 8 kW and 320 A** of total battery current. | An XT60 (≈ 60 A continuous, roughly 90–100 A short burst) cannot support this. Use XT90 or direct-soldered 10 AWG leads. |
| F3 | Section 3 shows ≈ 25 W per channel at 80 A, so ≈ 100 W in a nano (20×20 / 30×30) board. | 80 A is a **time-limited burst rating**, about 1–3 s from warm start. It is not a continuous rating. The datasheet must state the burst duration and starting temperature. |
| F4 | A single common bus shunt at 320 A dissipates I²R = 25.6 W even at 0.25 mΩ. | Use per-ESC-MCU phase or low-side sensing, or a Hall-effect bus sensor. Do not use a 320 A resistive shunt. |
| F5 | **All component parameters below are stated assumptions** chosen to be representative of 40 V PDFN5×6 class MOSFETs. | Replace them with the selected part's datasheet values. The model is parametric, so the equations still hold. |

**Operating point (baseline):**
- V_bus = 25.2 V (6S full charge)
- I_ph = 80 A burst
- f_sw = 48 kHz
- Complementary PWM with synchronous rectification

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current path in 6-step complementary PWM

In any commutation sector, two phases conduct:

- **PWM leg:** the high side (HS) conducts during D·T_sw. The synchronous low side (LS) conducts during (1−D)·T_sw, minus dead time.
- **Static leg:** the LS is on for the whole sector.

At every instant, the phase current I flows through **exactly two channels**: one in the PWM leg and one in the static leg. So for one motor channel:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

This holds independently of duty D to first order, because synchronous rectification replaces diode conduction. Duty D only changes **how the loss is shared** between the HS and LS of the PWM leg:

$$
P_{HS} = D\,I^2R_{ds},\qquad P_{LS,sync} = (1-D)\,I^2R_{ds},\qquad P_{LS,static} = I^2R_{ds}
$$

These are instantaneous values within a sector. Each FET is active for 1/3 of the electrical cycle (two of six sectors in each role), so averaged over a cycle per FET:

$$
\bar P_{FET} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}\left(I^2R_{ds}\right)\;\text{(static role)} + \tfrac{1}{3}\left(D \text{ or } (1-D)\right)I^2R_{ds}\;\text{(PWM role)}
$$

### 2.2 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\left[1 + k_T\,(T_j - 25)\right]
$$

This gives R_ds(110 °C) ≈ 1.0 × (1 + 0.0055 × 85) ≈ **1.47 mΩ**. Use 1.55 mΩ, the typical datasheet normalised curve value, for conservatism.

Because P depends on R_ds(T_j) and T_j depends on P, the loss and temperature are **coupled**. Solve iteratively (section 4.3).

### 2.3 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 110 °C

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.55\,\text{m}\Omega = 2 \times 6400 \times 0.00155 = \mathbf{19.84\ W}
$$

Worst-case per-FET average, for the static-role LS with D → 1 during a full-throttle punch:

$$
\bar P_{FET,max} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}(9.92) + \tfrac{1}{3}(9.92) = \mathbf{6.6\ W}
$$

The **instantaneous** dissipation in a single FET is 9.92 W while it conducts.
