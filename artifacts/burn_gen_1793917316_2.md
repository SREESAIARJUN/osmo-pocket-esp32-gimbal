## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Design Flags

Some framing first, because it drives every number below.

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Form factor | 30.5×30.5 mm mounting pattern, ~36×36 mm board, 8-layer, 2 oz (70 µm) copper on all layers |
| Bus | 6S LiPo: 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal. Design for 25.2 V plus transient margin |
| FC MCU | STM32H743 at 400 MHz running ArduPilot. It **only generates DShot600** |
| ESC MCUs | 4× independent ESC MCUs (STM32G071 or AT32F421 class, AM32/BLHeli_32-style firmware). They do commutation and the 48 kHz PWM |
| Gate driver | 3-phase half-bridge driver per motor (FD6288-class: 20 V gate supply, bootstrap high side) |
| MOSFET | 40 V N-ch, PDFN 5×6, R<sub>DS(on)</sub> ≈ 1.0 mΩ at 25 °C/V<sub>GS</sub>=10 V, Q<sub>g</sub> ≈ 60 nC, Q<sub>rr</sub> ≈ 50 nC, t<sub>r</sub>+t<sub>f</sub> ≈ 40 ns effective at 80 A |
| Rating target | 80 A per motor **burst** (≤ 2–3 s), ~45–55 A continuous with airflow |

**Design flags:**

1. **Size versus current.** 80 A burst on a true 20×20 "nano" is not physically credible. Twelve 5×6 FETs plus four MCUs and drivers will not fit, and the copper cannot carry the current. This analysis assumes a 30.5 mm stack. If "nano" means 20×20 mm, re-rate to roughly 35–40 A burst using 3.3×3.3 FETs.
2. **Integrated FC plus ESC.** Putting the H743 on the same PCB as 320 A-class switching is an EMI and thermal risk. The usual production choice is a separate stacked FC and ESC. The netlist below supports either: the DShot nets cross a board-to-board connector, or remain on-board.
3. **Placeholder FET parameters.** All MOSFET parameters are representative placeholders. Substitute the actual datasheet values: R<sub>DS(on)</sub>(T), Q<sub>g</sub>, Q<sub>rr</sub>, and Z<sub>th(j-c)</sub> curves. The equations are what matter.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R<sub>DS(on)</sub>

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.004\text{–}0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

A power-law form, (T/298)<sup>2.3</sup>, is more accurate above 125 °C.

With R<sub>DS,25</sub> = 1.0 mΩ and α = 0.005:

| T<sub>j</sub> (°C) | R<sub>DS</sub> (mΩ) |
|---|---|
| 25 | 1.00 |
| 85 | 1.30 |
| 125 | 1.50 |
| 150 | 1.63 |

Add the PCB/package path, **R<sub>pkg+Cu</sub> ≈ 0.10 mΩ per switch** (clip, solder, and local copper). Design value at 125 °C: **R<sub>on</sub> ≈ 1.6 mΩ.**

### 1.2 Six-step (trapezoidal) commutation with synchronous rectification

At any instant, two phase legs carry current I<sub>ph</sub>. In each active leg, the current flows through either the high-side FET (PWM on) or the low-side FET (PWM off, synchronous freewheel). So the conducting path always contains **two** switches.

**Total per motor:**
$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{on}(T_j)
$$

**Per FET.** Each leg is active 120° of 360°, and current splits by duty D between HS and LS:
$$
P_{cond,HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,D\,I_{ph}^2 R_{on}, \qquad P_{cond,LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,(1-D)\,I_{ph}^2 R_{on} + \tfrac{1}{3}I_{ph}^2R_{on}\big|_{\text{return leg}}
$$

For an exact split you must model the firmware's PWM scheme (HS-PWM versus alternating). For a worst-case bound, use:
$$
P_{cond,FET,max} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}\,I_{ph}^2 R_{on}
$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, T<sub>j</sub> = 125 °C

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.6\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{20.5\ W}
$$
$$
P_{cond,FET,max} = \tfrac{1}{3}\times 6400\times 1.6\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{3.41\ W}\ \text{(average)},\quad 10.24\ \text{W instantaneous while conducting}
$$

### 1.4 Board-level conduction losses (often forgotten)

- **Battery current is not 4 × 80 A.** I<sub>bat</sub> ≈ Σ D<sub>k</sub>·I<sub>ph,k</sub>. At full throttle (D → 1), I<sub>bat</sub> → 320 A. In practice, punch-outs reach about 200–260 A.
- **Bus copper.** For a 6 mm-wide pour, 4 layers × 70 µm, 20 mm long, ρ<sub>Cu</sub>(80 °C) = 2.0×10⁻⁸ Ω·m:
  - R = ρL/A = 2.0e-8 × 0.02 / (6e-3 × 280e-6) ≈ **0.24 mΩ**
  - At 250 A: **15 W**
  - **Requirement: battery pads at board center, with ≥ 6 copper layers sharing the V+/GND bus.**
- **Current shunt.** For 0.25 mΩ at 250 A, P = 15.6 W burst. Use a 2512 or 3920 metal-strip resistor, or Kelvin-sensed 0.1 mΩ (6.25 W).
