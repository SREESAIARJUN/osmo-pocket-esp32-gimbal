## 0. Architecture Clarification and Governing Assumptions

**The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production AIO of this class, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot600 frames to four dedicated ESC MCUs. These are typically AT32F421 or GD32E230 running AM32 or BLHeli_32, and they generate the 48 kHz PWM. The model below uses that partition.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current distribution per FET (6-step with synchronous rectification)

Each phase is driven high for 120°, low for 120°, and floats for 120°. Consider the PWM'd high-side phase at duty D:

- The **HS FET** conducts only during the on-time.
- The **LS FET of the same phase** freewheels during the off-time. This is the complementary or synchronous part.
- The **LS FET of the return phase** conducts for the full 120° sector.

That gives the per-FET mean-square current, averaged over one electrical cycle:

$$
I_{HS,rms}^2 = \frac{D}{3} I^2 \qquad\qquad I_{LS,rms}^2 = \frac{1 + (1-D)}{3} I^2 = \frac{2-D}{3} I^2
$$

Summed over 3 phases × (HS + LS):

$$
\sum = 3\left[\frac{D}{3} + \frac{2-D}{3}\right] I^2 = 2I^2
$$

This confirms the expected result: **exactly two FETs are always in the current path**.

### 1.2 Temperature-corrected R_ds(on)

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0062\ /K
$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.3 mΩ × 1.62 ≈ **2.1 mΩ**. A typical-to-max blend gives a design value of **1.8 mΩ hot**.
