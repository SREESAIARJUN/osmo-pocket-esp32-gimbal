## 1. Conduction Loss Model

**Temperature-dependent on-resistance:**

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ /K \Rightarrow R_{ds}(100^\circ C) \approx 1.16\ \text{m}\Omega$$

**6-step, complementary PWM.** The current path is always one high-side FET (or its complementary low-side during the off-time) plus one low-side FET of the sinking phase. So two FET drops are present at all times:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

At 80 A:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.16\text{e-3} = \mathbf{14.85\ W\ per\ motor}$$

**Per-FET instantaneous dissipation** while conducting: 6400 × 1.16 mΩ = **7.4 W**. Each FET conducts about 1/3 of the electrical cycle, but at low eRPM it must withstand the full 7.4 W for one commutation interval.

**PCB copper loss.** Assume 0.5 mΩ per phase path (2 oz, 8 layers paralleled, vias stitched):

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{path} \approx 6400 \times 0.5\text{e-3} \times 2 \approx 6.4\ \text{W/motor}$$

This is a **heat source in the board**, not the FETs. It is often ignored, but on a nano board it is nearly half the FET loss. The mitigation is solder-coated exposed phase pours and paralleled inner layers.

> For FOC/sinusoidal firmware, substitute $P_{cond} = 3\, I_{ph,rms}^2 R_{ds}$ (one FET per leg conducting at any instant).
