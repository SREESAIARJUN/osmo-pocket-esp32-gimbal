# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage, Thermal, and DShot Architecture (Job #18)

## 0. Feasibility Check

Three constraints shape the rest of the design.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each motor channel has its own small ESC MCU, for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot600 frames. This document follows that partition.
2. **80 A per channel is a burst rating, not a continuous one.** On a nano (~30.5 × 30.5 mm) PCB, Section 3 shows the board is effectively adiabatic during a burst. The derived limit is about 1.5–3 s at 80 A on all four channels, depending on airflow and copper mass.
3. **The battery side carries up to 320 A in aggregate** when all four channels burst together. That current must flow through the connector, pads, and bulk capacitance, so it is treated explicitly in Section 2.5.

## 1. Commutation Model

The ESC uses 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation with complementary synchronous PWM on the active high-side leg.

At any instant two phase legs carry the full phase current I:

- **Leg A (PWM leg):** HS conducts during D·T_sw. LS conducts during (1−D)·T_sw minus dead time. The body diode conducts during the dead time.
- **Leg B (return leg):** LS is held on for the whole 60° sector.

Each FET is active for 120° of each electrical cycle. At 80 A the electrical frequency is typically above 500 Hz, so the period is far shorter than the die thermal time constant (~ms to tens of ms). The die therefore sees the cycle-averaged power, plus a small ripple term.

## 2. Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

The temperature-dependent on-resistance is:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At a design point of T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{DS}(110) = 0.80 \times (1 + 0.0045 \times 85) = 1.106\ \text{m}\Omega \;\to\; \text{use } 1.11\ \text{m}\Omega$$

Two channel resistances are always in series with the motor current:

- Leg A contributes R_DS, because HS and LS of that leg take turns.
- Leg B contributes R_DS from its static LS.
- PCB copper and the current shunt add R_pcb ≈ 0.4 mΩ for 2 oz inner planes plus a 0.25 mΩ shunt.

$$P_{cond} = I^2\,(2R_{DS}(T_j) + R_{pcb})$$

$$P_{cond,FET} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.11\times10^{-3} = 14.2\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{cond,pcb} = 6400 \times 0.4\times10^{-3} = 2.6\ \text{W}$$

Because complementary PWM is used, conduction loss is nearly independent of duty cycle.

**Electro-thermal coupling.** R_DS depends on T_j, so the solution must be iterated:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_a + P_{tot}(T_j^{(k)})\cdot Z_{th}(t)$$

This converges in 2–3 iterations. The 110 °C value above is the converged burst-end point (see Section 3).

### 2.2 Switching (V–I overlap) Loss

Only leg A switches hard. Hard switching occurs on the HS turn-on and turn-off edges; the LS edges are soft, because the diode is already conducting.

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

$$P_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\times10^{-9} \times 48\,000 = 1.94\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Secondary Switching Terms

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw | 50 nC × 25.2 × 48k = **0.060 W** |
| Output capacitance | ½ · C_oss(er) · V² · f_sw × 2 FETs | 2 × 0.5 × 2n × 635 × 48k = **0.061 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_F · I · 2t_dt · f_sw | 0.8 × 80 × 200n × 48k = **0.614 W** |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver/resistors) | Q_g · V_GS · f_sw × 2 | 60n × 10 × 48k × 2 = **0.058 W** |

### 2.4 Total per Channel at 80 A Burst, T_j ≈ 110 °C

| Loss | Value |
|---|---|
| MOSFET conduction | 14.2 W |
| Switching (overlap) | 1.94 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.12 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W |
| **Subtotal: in the 6 FETs** | **16.9 W** |
| Copper + shunt | 2.6 W |
| Gate drive | 0.06 W |
| **Channel total** | **≈19.5 W** |
| **Board total, 4 channels** | **≈78 W** |

**Per-FET split.** Averaged over a cycle, each of the 6 FETs dissipates about 16.9 / 3 ≈ **5.6 W while it is in its active 120° interval**. Because each FET is active for one third of the cycle, its long-run average is about **2.8 W**.

**Optimization levers, ranked by impact at 80 A:**

1. **Lower R_DS(on).** Conduction loss is about 73% of the total, so this dominates.
2. **Cut dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns.** This saves about 0.3 W per channel, and is possible if driver propagation matching allows it.
3. **Lower f_sw.** Dropping from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves about 1 W per channel, but increases ripple and audible noise.
