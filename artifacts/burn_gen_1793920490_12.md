## 0. Architectural Ground Truth and Design Assumptions

**Partitioning.** The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. In any production AIO, it runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 (BLHeli_32 is discontinued). This design uses 4× AT32F421K8U7, an STM32G071-class alternative. The H743 talks to the ESC MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. That link is where the DMA and timer budget is spent.

**Feasibility flag: "Nano" vs. 80A burst.**
- A 20×20 mm board with 24 single 3.3×3.3 mm FETs cannot sustain 4×80A.
- This model uses a **30.5×30.5 mm** board, 8-layer, 2 oz inner and outer copper.
- 80A is treated as a **burst rating of a few seconds per motor**. Section 3 shows it is thermally capacity-limited, not steady-state.
- If Job #92 truly mandates 20×20, re-run Section 3 with C_th ≈ 2.4 J/K. The burst window shrinks to about 2 s.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path topology

In 6-step commutation, exactly one high-side FET (phase X) and one low-side FET (phase Y) carry I_ph at any instant.

- **PWM on-time:** HS_X and LS_Y conduct.
- **PWM off-time (sync rectification):** LS_X and LS_Y conduct.

So the instantaneous conducted current always flows through **two R_ds in series, independent of duty D**:

$$P_{cond,motor}(T_j) = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$
