# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #37): Architecture, Loss Model, Thermal Budget, and DShot DMA Allocation

## 0. Feasibility Constraints

These issues change the design, so they come first.

| Issue | Finding | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| **"Nano" form factor** | A 20×20 mm board cannot carry an H743 (LQFP100/TFBGA), 24 power MOSFETs and the bulk capacitance, then dissipate about 94 W. | Minimum **30.5×30.5 mm mounting (≈36×36 mm PCB)**. All numbers below assume this. |
| **"80A" definition** | 80 A per motor gives 320 A total battery current. An XT60 is rated about 60 A continuous; XT90 or direct-solder pads are needed. | 80 A is a **≤5 s per-channel burst**. The board is rated **45 A continuous per channel, thermally limited**. |
| **H743 role** | The H743 does not commutate motors. It sends DShot600 to four ESC MCUs (AT32F421 or STM32G071 class running AM32 or BLHeli_32). The ESC MCUs return eRPM telemetry. | Two-tier architecture: FC MCU → 4× ESC MCU → 3-phase gate driver → 6 MOSFETs per channel. |
| **Steady-state** | Section 3 shows 80 A continuous is physically impossible on this board area without a heatsink. | Burst duration derived analytically. Firmware current limit is mandatory. |

## 2. Loss Equations

### 2.1 Conduction loss

In six-step commutation with complementary PWM, phase current always flows through exactly two conducting channels:
- the high-side of phase A, and
- the low-side of phase B, or its synchronous complement during PWM-off.

$$
P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{DS(on),25} \cdot k_T \cdot 2
= 80^2 \times 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.5 \times 2 = \mathbf{19.2\ W}
$$
