# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #15)
## Architecture, Loss Model, Thermal Budget, and Bi-Directional DShot Allocation

---

## 0. Architecture Clarification

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. A production-viable partition is:

| Domain | Device | Firmware | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIH6 (TFBGA100) or VIT6 (LQFP100), 480 MHz | ArduPilot | Estimation, control, mixing, DShot600 master |
| ESC ×4 | STM32G071 / AT32F421 (one per motor) | AM32 or BLHeli_32 | 6-step commutation, 48 kHz complementary PWM, eRPM telemetry |
| Power stage ×4 | 6× N-MOSFET + 3-phase gate driver | n/a | 80 A burst per channel |

The H743 talks to each ESC MCU over one bi-directional DShot600 line. The 48 kHz switching analysis applies to the ESC power stage.

**Feasibility warning.** An 80 A per channel burst rating on a "nano" (20×20 or 30.5×30.5 mm) board can only be survived for **seconds**. The thermal section (§3) shows that the honest continuous rating is about 20–25 A per channel. Section 3 quantifies this so the datasheet rating can be defended.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

In 6-step (trapezoidal) drive with complementary PWM (synchronous rectification), current always flows through exactly **two** conducting devices:
- the PWM high side or its complementary low side, and
- the static low side of the return phase.

**Temperature-dependent on-resistance:**

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{ds} = 1.3 \times 1.6 = 2.08\ \text{mΩ}$$

**Per-motor conduction loss (worst case):**

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.08\,\text{mΩ} = \mathbf{26.6\ W}$$

**Per-FET average.** Each FET carries the current for 120° of each electrical cycle (shared between the HS/LS pair according to duty D). The average per FET is:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \tfrac{26.6}{6} \approx 4.4\ \text{W}$$

The instantaneous peak while conducting is 13.3 W.

**Electro-thermal iteration.** Because R_ds depends on T_j, solve the coupled equations to convergence:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_c + P_{cond}(T_j^{(k)})\cdot Z_{th,jc}(t)$$

$$P_{cond}(T_j) = 2I^2 R_{ds25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

**Thermal runaway condition.** The system has a stable fixed point only if:

$$2I^2R_{ds25}\,\alpha\,R_{th,ja} < 1$$

At 80 A on a nano board this condition is **violated** under steady state. That is why 80 A must be treated as a time-limited burst.

**Copper (PCB) loss.** Phase plus bus path, roughly 10 mm × 5 mm, 4 layers × 2 oz, ρ ≈ 2.22×10⁻⁸ Ω·m at 100 °C:

$$R_{cu} \approx \frac{\rho L}{w\,t\,n} = \frac{2.22\text{e-}8 \times 0.01}{0.005 \times 70\text{e-}6 \times 4} \approx 0.16\ \text{mΩ}$$

$$P_{cu} \approx 80^2 \times 0.16\,\text{mΩ} \approx 1.0\ \text{W per channel}$$

### 2.2 Switching losses at 48 kHz

The inductive load is hard-switched only on the PWM leg.

**Gate currents during the Miller plateau:**

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{src}+R_{g,int}} = \frac{6.5}{3} = 2.17\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{sink}+R_{g,int}} = \frac{3.5}{3} = 1.17\ \text{A}$$

**Transition times.** The switching charge is Q_sw = Q_gs2 + Q_gd ≈ 6 + 10 = 16 nC.

$$t_{on} = 16\text{n}/2.17 = 7.4\ \text{ns}, \qquad t_{off} = 16\text{n}/1.17 = 13.7\ \text{ns}$$
