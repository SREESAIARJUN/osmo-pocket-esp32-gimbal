## 0. Architecture Corrections

Two points in the brief need correcting before the math is useful.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In any ArduPilot-compatible 4-in-1 design, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and *emits* DShot. Each ESC channel runs its own commutation firmware, for example AM32 on an STM32G071 or AT32F421, or BLHeli_32. The 48 kHz PWM, dead-time and synchronous rectification all live on the ESC MCU. The H743 only needs:
   - 4 DShot outputs,
   - eRPM telemetry input capture (bidirectional),
   - optional serial telemetry, current-sense ADC and NTC ADC.
2. **80 A per channel in a "nano" form factor (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) cannot be sustained.** The math below shows roughly 87 W of dissipation at 4×80 A. That is only survivable as a **time-limited burst of about 2–3 s**. This document therefore treats 80 A strictly as a burst rating with firmware-enforced limits.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, exactly two FETs carry the phase current at every instant:

- during PWM-on: one HS and one LS;
- during PWM-off: two LS FETs (freewheel through the synchronous LS).

Therefore, independent of duty cycle D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0067/\text{K} \Rightarrow 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega\ \text{@}\ 100\,°\text{C}$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2(80)^2(1.5\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

**Per-FET split** (each phase conducts 2/3 of the electrical period):

- High-side RMS: $I_{HS,rms} = I\sqrt{D/3}$
- Low-side RMS: $I_{LS,rms} = I\sqrt{(1/3) + (1-D)/3}$

At D = 0.8, worst case for HS:

| FET | RMS current | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| HS | 41.3 A | 2.56 W |
| LS | 51.9 A | 4.05 W |

**The LS FETs are the conduction hot spot at low duty.** Use the lowest-R_ds part on the low side if the two sides differ.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd high-side leg hard-switches.

**3.1 V–I overlap**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2}V_{bus}I(t_r+t_f)f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(40\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**3.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two edges per cycle)

$$P_{dt} = V_f\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(200\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**3.3 Reverse recovery**

$$P_{rr} = V_{bus}Q_{rr}f_{sw} = 25.2(50\text{nC})(48\text{k}) = 0.06\ W$$

**3.4 C_oss loss**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,eq}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5(1.5\text{n})(635)(48\text{k}) = 0.023\ W$$

**3.5 Gate drive** (switching pair; dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g)

$$P_g = 2\,Q_gV_{gs}f_{sw} = 2(40\text{n})(10)(48\text{k}) = 0.038\ W$$

**3.6 Totals**

| Loss | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.20 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching (overlap) | 1.94 W | 7.76 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W | 2.44 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.12 W | 0.48 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, if used) | 1.60 W | 6.4 W |
| **Total (with shunt)** | **≈23.5 W** | **≈94 W** |

**Efficiency at burst:**
- Mechanical input ≈ 25.2 V × 80 A × D. At D = 0.8 that is about 1.6 kW per motor.
- Loss is about 1.5 % electrically. The efficiency is fine; the problem is power *density*.

**Sensitivity:** conduction loss dominates (more than 80 %), so it scales with I².
- Halving R_ds by paralleling FETs saves about 9.6 W per motor.
- Dropping f_sw to 24 kHz saves only about 1.3 W per motor.
- So 48 kHz is acceptable; spend PCB area on copper and FETs, not on lower f_sw.
