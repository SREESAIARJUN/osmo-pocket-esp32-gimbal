## 0. Architecture Clarification and Design Risks

There are two processor domains:

| Domain | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743 (400–480 MHz) | ArduPilot, DShot600 generation, eRPM capture |
| ESC (×4 or ×1 shared) | AT32F421 / STM32G071-class running AM32 or BLHeli_32 | 48 kHz PWM, commutation, telemetry |

The H743 does **not** generate the 48 kHz PWM. That runs on the ESC MCU(s). The H743's only motor-side job is DShot.

**Main design risk.** "80 A burst" on a nano-format 4-in-1 is thermally marginal. The analysis below shows that 4 × 80 A simultaneously is limited to roughly **2–3 seconds** by board heat capacity, not by the MOSFETs. Section 3.4 quantifies this.

## 2. Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_DS = 1.0 × (1 + 0.006 × 85) = **1.51 mΩ**

### 2.2 Conduction Losses

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, each leg spends:
- 1/3 of the electrical cycle **active-high**: the high-side (HS) FET conducts for duty D and the low-side (LS) FET conducts for 1−D. Current always flows through one FET of the leg.
- 1/3 **active-low**: the LS FET is fully on.
- 1/3 **floating**: no conduction.

Per leg:

$$P_{cond,leg} = \tfrac{2}{3}\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Per motor (3 legs). This is equivalent to two FETs in series carrying I_ph at all times:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

At 80 A and 1.51 mΩ:
- P_cond,leg = (2/3) × 6400 × 1.51 × 10⁻³ = **6.44 W per leg**
- P_cond,motor = **19.3 W**

Worst-case per-FET dissipation is ≈ 6.4 W. It occurs at low D (low throttle, high current, e.g. a stall or punch-out start), where the LS FET carries most of the leg's share.

**Add copper loss:**

$$P_{Cu} = I_{ph}^2 R_{trace}$$

With 2 oz copper and ~0.3 mΩ per phase path ⚠: 6400 × 0.3 × 10⁻³ × 2 (two phases conducting) = **3.8 W per motor**
