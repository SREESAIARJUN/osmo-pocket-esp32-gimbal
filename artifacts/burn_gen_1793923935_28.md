## 0. Architectural Correction (Read First)

Two points in the brief need correcting before any math is meaningful.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot has no motor-commutation firmware. The viable architecture is:
   - **STM32H743** runs ArduPilot, which handles flight control and the BDShot600 master.
   - **4× ESC MCUs** (AT32F421 or STM32G071 class) run AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. These do the 48 kHz PWM, 6-step commutation and eRPM telemetry.
   - The DShot links between the two are internal PCB traces.
2. **"Nano" + 4×80 A is not thermally continuous.** On a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm board, 80 A is achievable only as a **burst rating**. Section 3 quantifies the burst window, roughly 4 s at full-quad 80 A. I recommend specifying the board as **"45 A continuous / 80 A burst (≤2 s)"**.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel, 6-step trapezoidal, synchronous complementary PWM)

### 2.1 Conduction Topology

In any 60° sector, current I flows through exactly two legs:

- **Leg "on":** its low-side (or high-side) device is held fully on.
- **Leg "PWM":** the HS switches at duty D, and the LS free-wheels synchronously for (1 − D).

With synchronous rectification, exactly **two channels conduct I at every instant**, regardless of D:

$$P_{cond} = I^2\left[2R_{DS}(T_j) + 2R_{PCB}\right]$$

Each MOSFET conducts for 120° of the electrical cycle. The per-device average is:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} = \frac{I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)}{3}$$

The worst case occurs when one device is held on for a full 60° sector. At eRPM > 6000 the sector lasts < 1.7 ms, which is comparable to the die thermal time constant. The 1/3 averaging is therefore valid for steady flight. It is **not** valid at stall or very low RPM (see 3.4).

### 2.2 Junction-Temperature Iteration (I = 80 A)

Set R(Tj) = 0.8 mΩ·[1 + 0.0068(Tj − 25)] and Tj = T_board + ΔT_jb.

- **Iteration 1:** assume Tj = 100 °C. Then R = 1.21 mΩ.
- **Iteration 2:** compute ΔT from 2.5 and 3.2. This gives Tj ≈ 98 °C.
