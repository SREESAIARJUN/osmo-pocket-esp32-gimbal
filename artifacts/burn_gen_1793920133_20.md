**Design package covering:** power-stage loss model, thermal model with burst limits, bidirectional DShot600 timer/DMA plan, and reference netlists.

## 0. Architecture and Design Basis

Two corrections shape everything that follows.

1. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** ArduPilot has no native per-motor commutation or field-oriented control loop. The production-viable architecture is:
   - The **H743 flight controller** sends DShot600 frames to the ESCs.
   - **Four independent ESC MCUs** run the motors. Each is an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or BLHeli_32.
   - Each ESC MCU feeds a 3-phase gate driver, which drives six N-channel MOSFETs.
2. **"80A per channel" on a 20×20 mm nano board is a burst-only rating.** The thermal section shows it is limited to roughly **1–3 s** without heavy forced convection. This should be stated in the datasheet as a burst rating with a defined duration and duty cycle.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation topology

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with **complementary (synchronous) PWM**, which is the AM32 / BLHeli_32 default. At any instant two phases conduct:

- **Phase A (PWM leg):** the high-side FET conducts for duty fraction D. The low-side FET conducts for (1 − D), with synchronous rectification.
- **Phase B (return leg):** the low-side FET is fully on.

The current path therefore always contains exactly **two R_ds(on)** in series, independent of D (dead-time intervals are ignored here and handled in §2):

$$
P_{cond,motor} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

### 1.2 Per-device split, worst case

Across one electrical cycle, each FET position carries current for 1/3 of the period. However, the burst thermal constant is long relative to the electrical period, and at low eRPM (stall or punch-out) a single FET can hold current for many milliseconds.

**Design rule: size each FET for the full I²R during a stall-burst.**

$$
P_{FET,max} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 80^2 \times R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

### 1.3 Temperature self-consistency (closed form)

Start from two relationships:

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\qquad T_j = T_{ref} + R_\theta P
$$

Here T_ref is the board or ambient reference and R_θ is the effective junction-to-reference resistance. Solving for T_j:

$$
\boxed{T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + R_\theta I^2 R_{25}(1-25\alpha)}{1-\alpha R_\theta I^2 R_{25}}}
$$

**Thermal-runaway criterion:**

$$
\alpha R_\theta I^2 R_{25} \ge 1
$$

**Numeric check (junction to PCB copper, R_θ = 1.2 K/W):**

$$
\alpha R_\theta I^2 R_{25} = 0.006 \times 1.2 \times 6400 \times 0.001 = 0.046
$$

This is well below 1, so the device itself is stable. The danger lies at the *board* level, covered in §3.

Take T_ref = 100 °C (a hot copper pour mid-burst):

$$
T_j = \frac{100 + 1.2 \times 6.4 \times 0.85}{1 - 0.046} = \frac{106.5}{0.954} \approx 111.7\ ^\circ\text{C}
$$

$$
R_{ds} \approx 1.52\ \text{m}\Omega,\qquad P_{FET} \approx 9.7\ \text{W}
$$

### 1.4 Per-motor and total conduction loss

| Item | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Per motor (2 FETs) | 80² · 2 · 1.52 mΩ | **19.5 W** |
| PCB copper, phase + bus (est. 0.3 mΩ) | 80² · 0.3 mΩ | 1.9 W |
| Current shunt (0.25 mΩ, if per-ESC) | 80² · 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| **Per motor total** | | **≈ 23 W** |
| **4 motors simultaneously** | | **≈ 92 W** |

If you parallel two FETs per position (12 per ESC, 48 total), conduction loss per motor halves to about 9.8 W. This is the single most effective lever available.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. The return-leg low-side FET is static. Use the worst-case operating point: V = 25.2 V, I = 80 A, D ≈ 0.5.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard-switched high side)

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$
