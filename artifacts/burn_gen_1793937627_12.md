# STM32H743 AIO 4-in-1 ESC Flight Controller (Job #59): Power-Stage, Thermal and DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope and Feasibility Findings

Three points shape the rest of this document.

1. **The H743 does not switch the MOSFETs.** In a 4-in-1 design, each ESC channel has its own motor MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical choices are the AT32F421 or STM32G071. That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bi-directional DShot600 to the four ESC MCUs. The models below reflect that split.
2. **No MOSFET part number was specified.** I used a representative 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm PQFN device. Every equation is parameterized, so you can substitute the datasheet values for the selected part.
3. **80 A per channel on a nano board is a short burst only.** The model shows roughly 113 W of dissipation with all four channels at 80 A. On a 25.5×25.5 mm board, the board-level limit is about 1–2 s, depending on airflow. It is not a sustainable rating. The battery side is also infeasible: four motors at 80 A and high duty means more than 300 A from the battery. The 80 A figure should be specified as **per-channel peak, ≤1 s, single or dual motor**, or the form factor must grow (30.5 mm, 5×6 mm FETs).

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.4 × 1.45 = **2.03 mΩ**.

### 2.2 Six-step commutation with complementary PWM

AM32 uses complementary PWM with active freewheeling. At any instant, two phase legs carry the current I:

- **The PWM'd leg:** the high-side FET conducts for duty D and the low-side FET for (1−D). Together they always carry I.
- **The static leg:** its low-side FET conducts I continuously.

The instantaneous conduction loss per channel is therefore independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{ds}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

With I = 80 A and R_ds = 2.03 mΩ:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 6400 \times 2.03\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{26.0\ W}$$

Copper adds to this. Assume the phase path (pours, vias and pads) totals about 0.5 mΩ:

$$P_{Cu,ch} = I^2 R_{Cu} \approx 6400 \times 0.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

### 2.3 Per-FET distribution

Each FET is active for 120° of each 360° electrical cycle. At burst, the electrical frequency is several kHz, which is far shorter than the junction thermal time constants. The per-FET loss can therefore be treated as its average:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx \frac{P_{cond,ch}+P_{sw,ch}}{6}$$
