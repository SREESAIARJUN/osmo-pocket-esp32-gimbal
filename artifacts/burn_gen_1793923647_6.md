## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A burst)

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step synchronous PWM, two phase legs conduct at any instant:

- The PWM leg alternates between HS (high side, duty D) and LS (low side, 1−D, synchronous rectification).
- The return leg's LS (or HS) is on continuously for that 60° sector.

At any moment, exactly two FET channels carry the full phase current I_ph. Therefore:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph,rms}^2 \, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.5 mΩ × 1.5 = **2.25 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.25\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{28.8\ W}$$

Copper trace and via loss adds to this. Assume about 0.3 mΩ of effective path resistance per channel:

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.3\times10^{-3} \approx 1.9\ \text{W}$$

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution (worst-case FET)

- **Continuously-on return FET** in its 60° sector: 80² × 2.25 mΩ = **14.4 W instantaneous**.
  - Its time-average over a full electrical cycle is 1/3 of that, about 4.8 W, because each FET conducts in 2 of 6 sectors.
- **PWM-leg pair:** splits 14.4 W in the ratio D : (1−D).

For junction transients shorter than the electrical period (about 1 ms at 1 kHz electrical), use the instantaneous 14.4 W figure.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. Per channel:

$$P_{sw} = \underbrace{\tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}}_{\text{V–I overlap}} + \underbrace{Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw}}_{\text{reverse recovery}} + \underbrace{Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw}}_{\text{output cap}} + \underbrace{2\, V_{SD} I_{ph}\, t_{dt}\, f_{sw}}_{\text{dead-time diode}}$$

At V = 25.2 V, I = 80 A, f = 48 kHz:

| Term | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 20 ns × 48 k | 0.97 W |
| Reverse recovery | 25.2 × 50 nC × 48 k | 0.06 W |
| Coss | 25.2 × 40 nC × 48 k | 0.05 W |
| Dead-time diode | 2 × 0.8 × 80 × 100 ns × 48 k | 0.61 W |
| **Total P_sw per channel** | | **≈ 1.69 W** |

Gate drive loss is dissipated in the driver, not the FET: P_g = 2 × Q_g × V_drv × f_sw = 2 × 60 nC × 10 V × 48 k ≈ 0.06 W.

**Key insight.** At 48 kHz, conduction loss dominates switching loss by about 17:1. Raising f_sw (for quieter motors and lower current ripple) costs little. Each +24 kHz adds about 0.85 W per channel.

### 2.1 Total burst dissipation

$$P_{ch} \approx 28.8 + 1.9 + 1.7 \approx \mathbf{32.4\ W}$$

$$P_{total} (4\ \text{ch at 80 A}) \approx \mathbf{130\ W}$$
