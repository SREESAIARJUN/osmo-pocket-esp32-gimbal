# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC (Job #14): Design Model and Reference Architecture

**Document status:** This is a reference design derived from first principles. Part values, MOSFET parameters, and pin assignments are representative assumptions. They are not a verified, released design. Before committing to layout, confirm every number against current datasheets and the current ArduPilot `hwdef` documentation.

---

## 0. Architectural Ground Truth

Three points need to be settled first, because they shape the entire design.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot sends DShot commands to ESCs. It does not run motor commutation. A 4-in-1 ESC that speaks bi-directional DShot600 therefore needs **four dedicated ESC MCUs**, typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. Each MCU runs one 3-phase bridge at 48 kHz. The H743 acts only as the DShot master.

2. **"80A burst" is a transient rating, not a thermal steady state.** The math in Section 2 shows this. On a nano form factor (20×20 or 30×30 mm), 80A per motor generates over 100W of loss across the board. That is survivable for roughly 1 to 2 seconds, not continuously.

3. **Form factor.** True 20×20 mm cannot fit 24 power FETs plus the H743. This document assumes a **stacked two-board design**:
   - FC board: 30×30 mm, M3 mounting.
   - ESC board: 30×30 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner copper.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation, two phases conduct at any instant. With complementary PWM:
- During the on-time, current flows through the high-side (HS) FET of phase A and the low-side (LS) FET of phase B.
- During the off-time, current freewheels through the synchronous LS FET of phase A (the "LS_A sync" path) and the LS FET of phase B.

So **exactly two FET channels carry the full motor current at all times**, independent of duty cycle. Only the dead-time intervals differ; they are covered in Section 3.

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2 \cdot I_{rms}^2 \cdot R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

### 1.2 RMS current including ripple

The phase current ripple at 48 kHz, with phase-to-phase inductance L_pp, is:

$$
\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\, D(1-D)}{L_{pp}\, f_{sw}}
$$

The RMS current is then:

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I_{avg}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}
$$

Worked example for a typical 5" motor (L_pp ≈ 20 µH) at D = 0.5:

$$
\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\times10^{-6} \times 48000} = 6.6\ \text{A}
$$

The ripple term is 6.6²/12 = 3.6 A², which is negligible against 6400 A². So at 80A, I_rms ≈ I_avg.

### 1.3 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

This gives a factor of ×1.6 at 125 °C.

### 1.4 Results

| Condition | R_DS(on) | P_cond per motor | P_cond, 4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|
| 80 A, T_j = 25 °C | 1.5 mΩ | 2 × 6400 × 0.0015 = **19.2 W** | 76.8 W |
| 80 A, T_j = 125 °C | 2.4 mΩ | **30.7 W** | 122.9 W |
| 45 A continuous, 100 °C | 2.18 mΩ | 8.8 W | 35.3 W |
| 80 A, 2 FETs paralleled per switch, 125 °C | 1.2 mΩ effective | 15.4 W | 61.4 W |

**Design implication:** Conduction loss dominates at 80A. Doubling the FETs (48 total) is the single most effective lever. It is not feasible on 30×30 mm with 5×6 packages, but it is borderline feasible with 3.3×3.3 packages on both board sides.

### 1.5 Shunt loss

Assume a single 0.25 mΩ low-side shunt per ESC:

$$
P_{shunt} = 80^2 \times 0.00025 = 1.6\ \text{W per ESC}
$$

### 1.6 Copper loss

Assume a 2 oz copper trace: ~0.25 mΩ/sq. A phase path is about 4 squares, so R_cu ≈ 1 mΩ.

$$
P_{cu} \approx 6.4\ \text{W per motor at 80 A}
$$

This is not negligible. It is why the power planes need **2 oz copper on all power layers plus via stitching.**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In 6-step commutation with complementary PWM, only **one leg** is hard-switched at a time. The other conducting leg is held statically on.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V-I overlap

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I\, (t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}
= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\times10^{-9} \times 48000 = 1.94\ \text{W}
$$
