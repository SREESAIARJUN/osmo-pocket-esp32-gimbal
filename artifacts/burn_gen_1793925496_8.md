# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: System Model (Job #65)

## 0. Architecture Decisions and Assumptions

Two points need to be settled before the math, because they change the results.

**A. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In a production AIO design, the H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs running AM32 (or BLHeli_32). Each ESC MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The design therefore has two separate problem domains:
- **FC domain:** H743 timer/DMA allocation for DShot and its telemetry.
- **Power domain:** the 4× three-phase bridges, with conduction, switching and thermal behavior.

**B. "80 A burst" on a nano board is the binding constraint.** The math below is fully parameterized. With realistic FETs on a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm board, four channels at 80 A simultaneously can only be sustained for a few seconds. Section 4 quantifies this. Treat 80 A as a per-channel, short-duration, non-simultaneous rating unless you move to a 30.5×30.5 mm board with 5×6 mm FETs and a heatsink.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-step synchronous rectification topology

Under trapezoidal six-step control (AM32 default), two phases conduct in each 60° sector:
- **Phase X (PWM'd):** high-side FET on for D·T_sw, complementary low-side FET on for (1−D)·T_sw.
- **Phase Y (return):** low-side FET on continuously for the whole sector.

Neglecting dead time, the current path always passes through exactly two R_ds(on) in series. Total conduction loss per channel is therefore nearly independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds(on),25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.2 mΩ × 1.6 = **1.92 mΩ**.

Because conduction loss raises T_j, which in turn raises R_ds(on), the loss is self-consistent:

$$T_j = T_a + R_{th,ja}\cdot I^2 \cdot 2R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Solving for T_j:

$$T_j = \frac{T_a + k(1 - 25\alpha)}{1 - k\alpha}, \quad k = 2I^2 R_{25}\,R_{th,ja}$$

**Stability condition:** thermal runaway occurs when kα ≥ 1. With I = 80 A and R₂₅ = 1.2 mΩ, k = 15.36·R_th,ja. Runaway therefore occurs at **R_th,ja ≥ 10.9 K/W** (effective, per channel). This number should be checked against the board's thermal model; it is the hard limit.

### 1.3 Ripple contribution

The PWM-induced phase current ripple, with back-EMF E across the two conducting phases, is:

$$\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - E)\,D}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}$$

This is worst near D = 0.5 at mid-throttle. At V_bus = 25.2 V, E ≈ 12.6 V and D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I = \frac{12.6 \times 0.5}{20\,\mu H \times 48\,kHz} = 6.6\ \text{A}_{pp}$$

The RMS current then becomes:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.6 \approx 6404\ \text{A}^2$$

At 80 A the ripple term is negligible (<0.1 %). It only matters for low-inductance motors below about 8 µH.

### 1.4 Result

$$P_{cond} = 6404 \times 2 \times 1.92\,m\Omega = \mathbf{24.6\ W\ per\ channel}\ (T_j = 125°C)$$

**Per-FET distribution:** each switch position carries current for about one third of the electrical cycle on average, so the time-averaged loss is about 24.6 / 6 ≈ **4.1 W per position**.

Instantaneous loss while conducting is I²R = **12.3 W**. Short-term peaking only matters when the electrical frequency is low compared with the FET's Z_th,jc time constant (about 1–10 ms). That condition applies at stall or startup (f_e < 100 Hz), which is exactly when burst current is most likely. **Desync/stall protection must therefore limit current.**
