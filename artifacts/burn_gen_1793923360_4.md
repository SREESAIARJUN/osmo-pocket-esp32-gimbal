## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.0 × 1.55 ≈ **1.55 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current paths in 6-step synchronous commutation

At any instant two legs are active:
- the sourcing leg (HS FET, chopped at duty *d*, LS conducting during off-time), and
- the sinking leg (LS FET held on).

Current therefore always flows through exactly **two conducting FETs** in series. During dead time the body diode carries the current instead (handled in §2).

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 1.3 Per-FET breakdown

Each FET carries current for 1/3 of the electrical period. For the chopped leg the conduction splits between HS (*d*) and LS (1−*d*). Over a full rotation this averages to:

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}}{3}$$

At 80 A and 1.55 mΩ:

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| P_cond,FET | 6400 × 1.55e-3 / 3 = **3.31 W** |
| P_cond,motor | 2 × 6400 × 1.55e-3 = **19.8 W** |
| P_cond,total (4 motors) | **79.4 W** |

### 1.4 PCB copper loss (often larger than designers expect)

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho_{Cu}(T)\,L}{w\,t}, \qquad \rho_{Cu}(T)=1.72\times10^{-8}[1+0.00393(T-20)]$$

The phase path runs B+ pour → HS → node → phase pad, plus GND return. Assume an effective series R_Cu ≈ 0.4 mΩ per motor (2 oz, multi-layer stitched pours, at 100 °C).

$$P_{Cu,motor} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.4\text{e-3} = \textbf{2.6 W}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-chopped leg switches at f_sw; commutation transitions occur at the electrical frequency and are negligible. Evaluated at V = 25.2 V, I = 80 A, f = 48 kHz.

| Mechanism | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap (hard switching) | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw | 0.5·25.2·80·40e-9·48e3 = **1.94 W** |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_F·I·2t_dt·f_sw | 0.8·80·200e-9·48e3 = **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 50e-9·25.2·48e3 = **0.06 W** |
| C_oss charge/discharge (2 FETs) | 2·½·C_oss·V²·f_sw | **0.05 W** |
| Gate drive (lost in driver/R_g) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw | **0.06 W** |
| **Total per motor** | | **≈ 2.7 W** |

**Sensitivity.** Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. Dropping to 24 kHz saves ~1.3 W/motor but raises current ripple and acoustic noise. At 80 A, conduction dominates by roughly 7:1, so **R_DS(on) and copper are the levers, not f_sw.**

**Loss-minimizing frequency.** The ripple-induced RMS increase is ΔI_pp²/12. Setting

$$\frac{d}{df}\left[P_{sw}(f) + P_{cond}(\Delta I(f))\right]=0$$

for a typical 5" motor (L_ph ≈ 15–30 µH) gives an optimum in roughly the 24–48 kHz range. 48 kHz is defensible.
