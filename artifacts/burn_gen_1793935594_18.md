# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC: System Architecture & Mathematical Model (Job #16)

## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility Checks

**Partitioning:** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does **not** commutate the motors. Each ESC channel has its own MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, such as an STM32G071 or AT32F421. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by those ESC MCUs. The H743 sends bi-directional DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry.

**Assumed operating point and parameters (replace with datasheet values when components are chosen):**

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S, full charge) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Burst phase current per channel | I | 80 A |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| MOSFET (40 V, 5×6 or 3.3×3.3 PDFN class), R_ds(on) max at 25 °C | R_ds25 | 1.3 mΩ |
| R_ds(on) temperature multiplier at T_j ≈ 125 °C | k_T | 1.5 |
| Rise + fall time at the driver's gate current | t_r + t_f | 20 + 20 ns |
| Body-diode reverse-recovery charge | Q_rr | 30 nC |
| Output charge | Q_oss | 40 nC |
| Total gate charge | Q_g | 50 nC at V_GS = 10 V |
| Gate driver (3-phase, bootstrap) | — | FD6288Q-class |
| Board | — | 30.5×30.5 mm stack-up candidate (see §3.4 for "nano" 25.5 mm) |

> ⚠️ **Feasibility flag:** 80 A per channel on a nano board is a **seconds-scale burst rating only**. It is not a continuous rating. §3 quantifies this. Marketing figures like "80A burst" usually assume about 1–2 s at 25 °C ambient with no airflow credit.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A burst)

### 1.1 Topology current paths

The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal commutation with **synchronous rectification** (AM32 default, complementary PWM).

- At any instant, one high-side and one low-side FET carry the phase current.
- During PWM off-time, the complementary FET carries the freewheel current instead of the body diode.
- Therefore the current path always contains **two** R_ds(on) elements in series, independent of duty cycle D.

$$
R_{ds,hot} = R_{ds25}\cdot k_T = 1.3\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.5 = 1.95\,\text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds,hot} = 80^2 \times 3.9\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{24.96\ W\ per\ channel}
$$

**Per-FET average.** Each FET conducts during 120° of each 360° electrical cycle, so the loss is shared roughly evenly among the 6 FETs:

$$
P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{24.96}{6} \approx 4.2\,\text{W}
$$

The **instantaneous** value while a FET is active is about 12.5 W. That figure drives the junction ripple calculation in §3.3.

### 1.2 Self-consistent temperature correction

R_ds(on) rises with temperature, which in turn raises the loss. A linearized coefficient captures this:

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\quad \alpha\approx0.005\,/\text{K}
$$

Iterating P → T_j → R_ds converges if

$$
I^2\cdot 2R_{ds25}\,\alpha\, R_{th,ja} < 1
$$

With R_th,ja,eff ≈ 2.5 K/W per channel cluster, the term evaluates to 0.21. This is stable: there is no thermal runaway in the steady state. The board heat capacity, not runaway, is what limits the burst (§3).

### 1.3 Copper / interconnect conduction

| Element | Estimate | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase pad + trace (2 oz, 6-layer parallel), R ≈ 0.3 mΩ | 0.3 mΩ | 1.9 W |
| Battery pad → bus planes, shared, R ≈ 0.2 mΩ at 4×80 A = 320 A total | 0.2 mΩ | 20.5 W board-wide |

> ⚠️ The **bus plane loss scales with the square of the summed current**. A 320 A simultaneous burst is unrealistic for a nano 4-in-1. Realistic multirotor bursts are about 160–200 A total, which gives roughly 5–8 W.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the PWM'd leg hard-switches. The commutated leg switches at the electrical frequency (≪ f_sw) and is neglected.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard-switching)

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\times25.2\times80\times40\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

The dead-time diode conduction occurs before the sync FET turns on.

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.036\,\text{W}
$$

The dead-time diode conduction itself, assuming V_F = 0.8 V and t_dt = 2 × 200 ns:

$$
P_{dt} = V_F I (2t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times400\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}
$$

> This term is often forgotten. At 48 kHz, dead time costs nearly as much as V–I overlap. Use the shortest dead time that the gate driver and layout tolerate (AM32 "dead time" setting).

### 2.3 Output capacitance and gate charge

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \approx 0.024\,\text{W}
$$

$$
P_{gate} = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw}\times 2 = 0.048\,\text{W}
$$

The gate charge loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors.

### 2.4 Per-channel and total summary

| Loss term | W / channel at 80 A |
|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 24.96 |
| Copper (phase) | 1.9 |
| V–I switching | 1.94 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss + gate | 0.11 |
| **Total** | **≈ 30.1 W** |

For **all 4 channels at 80 A**, the loss is about **120 W plus the bus copper**. Efficiency at 80 A is

$$
\eta \approx 1 - \frac{30}{25.2\times80\times D_{eff}}
$$

which works out to about 98.5 % at full throttle. The *efficiency* is fine; the *heat density* is the problem.

### 2.5 Frequency trade-off

Switching loss is linear in f_sw:

$$
\partial P/\partial f_{sw} \approx 66\,\mu\text{W/Hz}
$$

| PWM frequency | Change in switching loss per channel |
|---|---|
| 24 kHz | ~1.6 W saved |
| 96 kHz | ~3.2 W added |

48 kHz is a reasonable compromise between current ripple in low-inductance motors and switching loss.
