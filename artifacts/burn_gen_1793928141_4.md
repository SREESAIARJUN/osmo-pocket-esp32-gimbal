# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (80 A Burst): Architecture, Loss Model, Thermal Model, and BDShot600 Timer/DMA Plan

**Document:** Job #0 · Rev A (engineering baseline)

> **Status: Pre-layout baseline, not release-ready.** No MOSFET part number, board stack-up or airflow data was provided. All numbers below come from stated, representative assumptions (40 V-class PDFN5x6 MOSFETs, 6S, 2 oz copper). Every equation is parametric: substitute your actual datasheet values. Results must be validated by thermal-camera bench testing before any release.

## 0. Architecture Clarification

The STM32H743 does not generate the 48 kHz commutation PWM. In every production 4-in-1 design that runs ArduPilot, the system is split in two:

| Domain | Device | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (480 MHz) | ArduPilot. Emits DShot600 frames and captures bidirectional eRPM telemetry (GCR). |
| ESC (×4, or ×1 shared) | AT32F421 / STM32G071 / EFM8 running AM32 or BLHeli_32 | 6-step or FOC commutation at 48 kHz PWM. Drives the gate drivers. |

The loss model (§1–3) therefore applies to the ESC power stage. The timer/DMA model (§4) applies to the H743. Merging commutation into the H743 is possible, but it is not supported by ArduPilot.

## 2. Loss Model per ESC Channel (one motor, 6 FETs)

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

Assume 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous (complementary) PWM. At any instant two phases carry I:

- **PWM'd phase:** the high-side conducts for D. The low-side conducts for (1 − D) as the synchronous rectifier.
- **Return phase:** the low-side conducts for 100 % of the sector.

So the conducting resistance in the current path is always R_ds on the PWM'd leg plus R_ds on the return leg, independent of D:

$$P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds,hot} + I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{cu}$$

The ripple correction is:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}D(1-D)}{L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

For L_ph ≈ 15 µH (typical 5″ motor) at D = 0.5:

- ΔI ≈ 25.2·0.25 / (15e-6·48e3) ≈ 8.75 A
- ΔI²/12 ≈ 6.4 A², which is negligible against 6400 A²

Resulting conduction losses:

$$P_{cond,FET} = 6400 \cdot 2 \cdot 1.44\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{18.4\ W}$$
$$P_{cond,Cu} = 6400 \cdot 2 \cdot 0.30\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.84\ W}$$

### 2.2 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd half-bridge switches.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = ½·25.2·80·22 ns·48k | 1.06 W |
| C_oss | ½ · Q_oss · V · f_sw | 0.036 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw | 0.048 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_SD · I · 2t_dead · f_sw = 0.8·80·100 ns·48k | 0.31 W |
| Gate drive (driver-side) | 2 · Q_g · V_gs · f_sw | 0.048 W |
| **P_sw total** | | **≈ 1.50 W** |

### 2.3 Totals

| Scope | Loss |
|---|---|
| Per motor (FETs) | 18.4 + 1.5 ≈ 19.9 W |
| Per motor incl. copper | ≈ 23.7 W |
| 4 motors simultaneous burst | **≈ 95 W** |

Conduction dominates at about 78 % of the total. The design lever is therefore R_ds(on) and copper, not switching speed. Raising f_sw from 24 → 48 kHz costs only about 0.75 W per motor.

### 2.4 Per-FET Instantaneous Peak

During its conduction interval, a single FET dissipates I²·R_ds,hot = 9.2 W. Averaged over an electrical revolution, each FET carries about P_FET/6 ≈ 3.3 W.

At low eRPM the electrical period approaches the FET's thermal time constant. This is the stall/punch-out risk case.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network (Cauer, Lumped)

```
P_FET ──[R_jc 1.0 K/W]──●──[R_cb 0.5 K/W (vias)]──●──[R_ba 3 K/W]── T_a
                    C_j≈0.05 J/K              C_board≈6 J/K
```

### 3.2 Board Transient (Dominant Pole)

$$T_b(t) = T_a + P_{tot}R_{ba}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right), \qquad \tau = R_{ba}C_{board} = 18\ \text{s}$$

Steady state would be 95 W × 3 K/W = 285 K rise, which is physically impossible. **This board cannot sustain 80 A continuous on 4 motors.** It is a burst rating only.

Time to reach a board temperature of 110 °C (ΔT = 70 K):

$$t = -\tau\ln\left(1 - \frac{70}{285}\right) = 18 \cdot 0.282 \approx \mathbf{5.1\ s}$$

Without airflow (R_ba = 8 K/W), the answer is still about 4.5 s. The initial slope is P/C ≈ 16 K/s regardless of airflow.

### 3.3 Junction Over Board

The local junction rise over the board is:

9.2 W × (R_jc + R_cb) = 9.2 × 1.5 ≈ 14 K

So T_j at the end of a 5 s burst is ≈ 110 + 14 = 124 °C. This is consistent with the R_ds,hot assumption (k_T = 1.6), so the model is self-consistent. For T_j,max = 150 °C, the allowable burst is **≈ 5 s @ 80 A on all four motors from a 40 °C start**.

### 3.4 Recommended Ratings

| Rating | Value |
|---|---|
| Burst | 80 A / 5 s |
| Continuous (T_b ≤ 100 °C, R_ba = 3 K/W) | Solve P ≤ 60/3 = 20 W total → I ≈ √(20 / (4·2·(1.44+0.3) m)) ≈ **38 A/motor** |
