# STM32H743 AIO with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #51): Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Budget, and Bidirectional DShot600 DMA Allocation

## 0. Assumptions and Constraints

These numbers are a parameterized reference model, not a verified production design. I don't have your BOM, stack-up, or layout, so every assumed value is listed below for you to replace with your datasheet values.

**Architecture correction.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and generates DShot. It does **not** commutate the motors. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, for example an AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 talks to those MCUs over bidirectional DShot600.

**Feasibility warning.** Four channels at 80 A burst on a nano (20×20 mm) board is about 100 W of dissipation (§3). That is survivable only for roughly 1–3 seconds. Section 4 quantifies the limit.

## 1. Conduction Losses

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + k_T (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.2 mΩ × 1.55 = **1.86 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current path per commutation step

In 6-step drive with synchronous PWM, current always flows through exactly two MOSFETs:

- During the PWM on-time D: high-side of phase A, then low-side of phase B.
- During the off-time (1−D): low-side of phase A (synchronous rectification), then low-side of phase B.

So per channel:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2\left(1 + \frac{r^2}{12}\right)$$

Here r = ΔI/I_ph is the ripple ratio. Inductive motor windings keep r small. With r ≈ 0.3, the correction factor is 1.0075.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | P_cond per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 2 × 6448 × 1.2 mΩ = **15.5 W** | 62 W |
| 125 °C | 2 × 6448 × 1.86 mΩ = **24.0 W** | 96 W |

### 1.4 Distribution across the six MOSFETs (per channel)

Each phase conducts for 120° out of every 360° in each polarity. The heaviest-loaded device is the PWM-leg high-side at high duty cycle. At D ≈ 0.9:

$$P_{HS,max} \approx I^2 R_{ds}\cdot\tfrac{1}{3}\cdot D \cdot 2 \approx 6448 \times 1.86\text{m}\times 0.6 \approx 7.2\ \text{W (averaged over its active third, peaks shared)}$$

Design to **≈4–5 W average per FET**, with a hotspot margin for the active leg.

### 1.5 PCB copper loss (often omitted, but material at 80 A)

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho_{Cu}(T)\,\ell}{w\,t}, \qquad \rho_{Cu}(T)=1.72\times10^{-8}[1+0.00393(T-20)]$$

Example: 2 oz copper (70 µm), 4 paralleled layers, 8 mm wide, 15 mm path, at 100 °C:

- R = 2.26e-8 × 0.015 / (0.008 × 280e-6) ≈ **0.15 mΩ**
- Two paths (in and out) give P ≈ 2 × 6400 × 0.15 mΩ ≈ **1.9 W per channel**

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Per commutation step, only the PWM leg switches: one hard-switched high-side and its complementary low-side.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard switching)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns}\times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance discharge

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1\text{nF}\times 635 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.015\ W}$$

### 2.3 Body-diode reverse recovery (hard turn-on into the conducting low-side diode)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{nC}\times 25.2\times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction (two dead-time intervals per period)

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I_{ph}\, 2t_{dead}\, f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times100\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (two switching FETs; dissipated in the driver and gate resistors)

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2\times50\text{nC}\times10\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 2.6 Switching total

$$P_{sw,ch} \approx 1.45+0.015+0.048+0.31+0.048 \approx \mathbf{1.87\ W/channel}$$

**Sensitivity.** P_VI scales linearly with f_sw and with edge time.

- Going to 96 kHz roughly doubles it, to about 2.9 W.
- Slowing edges to 30 ns each (for EMI) also doubles it.
- Cutting dead time to 25 ns saves about 0.15 W.

## 3. Total Loss Budget at 80 A Burst, T_j = 125 °C

| Term | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| MOSFET conduction | 24.0 W | 96.0 W |
| PCB copper | 1.9 W | 7.6 W |
| Switching (all terms) | 1.9 W | 7.5 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, if per-channel sensing) | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| Bulk capacitor ESR (see §6) | ~0.3 W | 1.2 W |
| **Total** | **≈29.7 W** | **≈119 W** |

ESC efficiency at the burst point is P_out ≈ 25.2 V × 80 A × 0.9 duty ≈ 1814 W per channel, so η ≈ 98.4 %. The efficiency is fine. **The thermal density is the problem.**
