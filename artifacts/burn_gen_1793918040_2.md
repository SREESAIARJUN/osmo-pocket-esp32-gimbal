# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: System Architecture Package (Job #11)

**Scope:** 80 A burst conduction model, 48 kHz switching-loss model, transient and steady-state thermal model, ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 timer/DMA allocation, and reference netlists.

**Status:** Engineering derivation. Every MOSFET parameter below is a stated assumption. Swap in your selected part's datasheet values and re-run the equations; the structure of the model holds.

---

## 0. Architectural Ground Truth (read first)

Three facts constrain everything that follows.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot is a flight stack. It emits DShot commands and consumes eRPM telemetry. The 48 kHz PWM, commutation, and zero-crossing detection run on **four dedicated ESC MCUs**, typically AT32F421 or GD32E230 running AM32, or EFM8 running Bluejay. The H743's job is the DShot interface only.
2. **"Nano" form factor vs. 80 A.** The math in §3 shows a 20×20 mm board cannot sustain 80 A on more than about one motor for more than a fraction of a second. I model both 20×20 and 30.5×30.5 mm so the trade is explicit.
3. **80 A is treated as per-motor phase current (burst).** Four motors at 80 A simultaneously means about 320 A of battery current. That is beyond XT60/XT90 connector and pack capability. The thermal model therefore also evaluates a realistic duty mix.
