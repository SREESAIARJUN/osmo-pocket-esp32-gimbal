## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Flags

No component selections or mechanical envelope were provided, so I made the assumptions below. Every equation is parameterized, so you can substitute datasheet values once parts are frozen.

| Parameter | Assumed value | Notes |
|---|---|---|

| Burst current | 80 A per motor, 5 s max, 4 motors simultaneously (worst case) | |
| ESC PWM | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous rectification), trapezoidal 6-step | |
| ESC firmware | AM32 on a dedicated per-channel MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071) | The H743 does **not** commutate motors. It only sends DShot. |
| MOSFET | 40 V, PDFN 5×6, Rds(on) 0.85 mΩ typ / 1.0 mΩ max at 25 °C and 10 V Vgs | Representative of the Infineon OptiMOS 6 and Vishay Gen IV class. |

| Gate driver | FD6288-class 3-phase bootstrap driver, VCC = 12 V (gives ~10–11 V Vgs) | |
| Board | 30.5 × 30.5 mm mount, 36 × 36 mm outline, 8-layer, 2 oz copper | |

**Flag 1: form factor.** "Nano" usually means a 20 × 20 mm stack. Twenty-four 5×6 FETs, 80 A copper paths, and bulk capacitance will **not** fit 20 × 20 mm at 80 A. The thermal model below assumes 30.5 mm. On a 20 × 20 mm board, 80 A burst is not physically credible; budget about 35–45 A burst there.

**Flag 2: verification.** Datasheet values must be verified against your exact part numbers before tape-out. All numerical results here are engineering estimates, not qualification data.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation with complementary PWM, at any instant the phase current flows through exactly two conducting channels:

- **PWM on-time (D):** the high-side FET of the switching leg, plus the static low-side FET of the return leg.
- **PWM off-time (1−D):** the low-side FET of the switching leg (synchronous rectification), plus the same static low-side FET.

Therefore, per ESC channel:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j) + I_{rms}^2\,R_{pcb}$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.0058\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At Tj = 120 °C with max Rds(on): R = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0058 × 95) = **1.55 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Ripple correction

The phase inductance is assumed to be L ≈ 15 µH (typical for a 5" 2207 motor, line-to-line ~30 µH). Ripple is worst at D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{f_{sw}\,L_{LL}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{48\,000 \times 30\times10^{-6}} = 4.4\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{avg}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 1.6 \approx 6402\ \text{A}^2$$

Ripple is negligible at 48 kHz. This is one benefit of the higher PWM frequency.

### 1.4 Result at 80 A

| Term | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| FET channels | 2 × 6402 × 1.55 mΩ | 19.85 W |
| PCB copper (0.15 mΩ effective, 2 oz × 8 layers in parallel) | 6402 × 0.15 mΩ | 0.96 W |
| **Conduction total, per channel** | | **≈ 20.8 W** |

### 1.5 Per-FET distribution

Over one electrical cycle, each FET conducts during a 120° sector.

- The FET carrying full current sees an instantaneous **9.9 W**.
- Averaged over the cycle (6 FETs): **≈ 3.3 W per FET**.
