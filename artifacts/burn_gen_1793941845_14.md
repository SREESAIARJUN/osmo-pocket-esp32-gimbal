# STM32H743 Nano AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Design Package (Job #1)

## 0. Architecture Decisions and Feasibility

These points shape everything that follows.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot has no field-oriented or six-step motor-control path for the FC MCU. The H743 sends bi-directional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs running AM32. This design uses an AT32F421K8 per channel, with an STM32G071 as the alternative. Each ESC MCU drives a 3-phase gate driver and six N-FETs.
2. **"80 A burst" in a nano form factor is a short transient, not a rating.** The thermal math in §3 shows an 80 A, 4-channel burst on a 20×20 mm board lasts about 1–1.5 s before the PCB limit is reached. Continuous capability is roughly 20 A per channel. I recommend one of two options:
   - Rate the board **"25 A continuous / 80 A burst ≤ 1 s"**.
   - Move to **30.5×30.5 mm** to achieve about 3 s bursts.
3. **Assumptions:** 6S input (25.2 V maximum), 40 V FETs, f_sw = 48 kHz, synchronous rectification, 6-layer 2 oz PCB.

### 2.2 Conduction Loss

In six-step commutation with complementary PWM, current always flows through one high-side and one low-side path in series. The commutating leg alternates between the HS FET and the synchronous LS FET. Two FET resistances are therefore always in the current path.

R_DS(on) depends on junction temperature:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

Per-channel conduction loss is then:

$$P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j)$$

At 80 A and T_j = 125 °C:

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.45\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{18.6\ W}$$

At 25 °C the same calculation gives 12.8 W. This temperature dependence creates positive thermal feedback, which is solved iteratively in §3.4.

### 2.3 Switching Losses (one hard-switched leg per PWM period)

**Overlap (V-I crossover) loss:**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\times25.2\times80\times20\text{n}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

**Output capacitance loss:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5\times1.5\text{n}\times635\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.023\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period):**

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2t_{dead})\, f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{n}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

**Gate drive (6 FETs, dissipated in the driver and gate resistor, not in the FETs):**

$$P_g = 6\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \approx 6\times50\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.14\ W}$$

### 2.4 Total Loss

$$P_{ch} \approx 18.6 + 0.97 + 0.023 + 0.61 + 0.06 + 0.14 \approx \mathbf{20.4\ W/channel}$$

$$P_{4ch} \approx \mathbf{82\ W}$$

Additional burst losses to include:

- **Shunt** (0.5 mΩ, if used per channel): 3.2 W/ch. This is why a single total-current shunt on the bus is recommended.
- **Copper** (≈ 0.3 mΩ path): about 2 W/ch.

**Burst design figure: ≈ 90 W total.**
