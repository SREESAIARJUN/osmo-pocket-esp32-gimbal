**Engineering verdict up front:** 80 A per motor on a 20×20 board is achievable only as a short burst, roughly 1–2 s. It is not achievable continuously. The math below shows why.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

In any commutation sector, two phase legs are active. During the PWM on-time, current flows through the high-side FET of leg A and the low-side FET of leg B. During the off-time, the complementary low-side FET of leg A freewheels the current, and leg B's low-side stays on.

So the motor current always flows through exactly two channels in series, independent of duty D (ignoring dead time):

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{ph,rms}^2 \, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)\,/\,N_p$$

where N_p is the number of FETs paralleled per switch position.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C, this gives R ≈ 1.5 × R_25 = 1.5 mΩ.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A (flat-top current, so I_rms ≈ I_pk)

| Config | P_cond per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| N_p = 1 | 2·80²·1.5 mΩ = **19.2 W** | 76.8 W |
| N_p = 2 | **9.6 W** | 38.4 W |
