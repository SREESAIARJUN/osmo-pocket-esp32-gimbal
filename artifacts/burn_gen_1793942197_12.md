# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #59): Power-Stage, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

## 0. Assumptions and Corrections to the Brief

Three points need to be settled before the math.

1. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** ArduPilot is flight-controller firmware, not ESC firmware. This design uses the H743 as the FC and **four AT32F421 MCUs running AM32** as the ESCs, linked by bi-directional DShot600. Running sensorless FOC or 6-step for four motors on the H743 alongside ArduPilot is not viable.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm) board is a transient rating, not a thermal steady state.** The math below shows why and quantifies the allowed burst duration.
3. **The netlist below is a reference architecture.** It is not a released, DRC-verified design. Every part number must be checked against current datasheets, and the hwdef against the ArduPilot master `hwdef` documentation, before layout.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At an assumed T_j = 110 °C: R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0045 × 85) = **1.38 mΩ**.

This creates positive feedback, solved iteratively in §4.

### 1.2 Six-step conduction path

In each 60° electrical sector, two phases conduct:

- **Commutating-low phase:** the low-side FET is on 100% of the sector, so loss = I²R.
- **PWM phase:**
  - the high-side FET conducts for D, so loss = D·I²R;
  - the synchronous low-side FET conducts for (1 − D), so loss = (1 − D)·I²R;
  - the sum is I²R, independent of D.

$$P_{cond,motor}=2\,I^2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond}=2(80)^2(1.38\times10^{-3})=\mathbf{17.7\ W\ per\ motor}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each FET carries current for 120° of every 360°. Electrical frequency (≥1 kHz) is much faster than the junction thermal time constant (~ms), so averaging is valid:

$$P_{FET,avg}\approx\frac{17.7}{6}\approx 2.95\ \text{W (plus its share of the switching loss)}$$

Instantaneous dissipation in a conducting FET is ~8.8 W. The junction temperature ripple this causes is negligible (Z_th at sub-ms timescales is < 0.05 K/W).

### 1.4 PCB copper loss (frequently ignored, not negligible)

For 2 oz (70 µm) outer copper with 3 mm × 15 mm phase pours, using ρ_Cu,100°C = 2.3 × 10⁻⁸ Ω·m:

$$R=\frac{\rho L}{wt}=\frac{2.3\times10^{-8}\cdot0.015}{0.003\cdot70\times10^{-6}}=1.64\ \text{mΩ}$$

That is per path segment. With roughly 2 segments in series plus a shared bus return, budget about **2 mΩ per motor path**:

$$P_{Cu}\approx 80^2 \times 0.002 = 12.8\ \text{W per motor}$$

This is comparable to the FET loss. **Mitigation:** use 6-layer stackup, mirror phase pours on L1/L6 with via stitching (≥20 × 0.3 mm vias per phase), and solder-thicken the exposed phase pads. The target is ≤0.8 m
