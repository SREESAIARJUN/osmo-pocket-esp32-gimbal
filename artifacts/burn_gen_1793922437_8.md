# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #32): Power-Stage and Bi-Directional DShot Design Package

## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility Warnings

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. Each ESC channel uses a dedicated low-cost MCU (AT32F421 running AM32, or EFM8BB51 running BLHeli_S/Bluejay) that drives a 3-phase gate driver. The H743 does not commutate the motors directly, because ArduPilot has no FOC/6-step ESC firmware for it. If Job #32 really requires the H743 to commutate motors, this whole document changes and that must be clarified first.

**Component parameters.** All component parameters below are representative values for the classes of parts named. Before release, replace them with values from your chosen datasheets.

**80 A burst on a "nano" form factor is not physically sustainable.**
- Nano usually means a 20×20 mm mounting pattern on a board of about 25–30 mm.
- Section 3 shows roughly 130 W of loss at 4 × 80 A.
- A board that size can tolerate this for about 1–3 seconds even with prop-wash airflow.
- My recommendation is to rate the product at **55 A burst (5 s) / 40 A continuous per motor**. Alternatively, move to a 30.5×30.5 mm layout with 5×6 mm FETs if 80 A is a hard requirement.

## 2. Conduction-Loss Model (Trapezoidal 6-Step, Complementary PWM)

### 2.1 Current paths

In each 60° commutation sector:
- One high-side FET (phase A) and one low-side FET (phase B) carry the motor current I.
- The PWM leg switches between its HS and LS FETs, with duty D for the HS and (1−D) for the LS through synchronous rectification.
- The non-PWM leg's FET conducts continuously during the sector.

So at every instant, **exactly two FET channels are in series with the motor current**:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \, I^2 \, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 2.2 Distribution per FET

Each phase conducts high-side for 120° and low-side for 120° per electrical cycle. Averaged over that cycle, the per-FET loss is bounded as follows:

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} \le \frac{1}{3} I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This bound applies when the FET is the non-PWM switch. FETs on the PWM side carry D/3 or (1−D)/3 of that loss.

### 2.3 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per motor | Worst FET avg | 4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.50 mΩ | 19.2 W | 3.2 W | 76.8 W |
| 100 °C | 2.01 mΩ | 25.7 W | 4.3 W | 102.9 W |
| 125 °C | 2.18 mΩ | 27.9 W | 4.6 W | 111.6 W |

**Additional resistance terms.** Include these, because they are routinely forgotten:
- PCB copper: 2 oz internal + 2 oz outer, a 4 mm-wide phase trace, and about 8 mm length gives roughly 0.3–0.5 mΩ per path.
- Solder joints add about 0.1 mΩ per joint.

At 80 A this adds approximately:

$$P_{Cu} \approx I^2 (R_{trace,total}) \approx 6400 \times 0.8\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 5.1\ \text{W per motor}$$

This dumps directly into the PCB.

## 3. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches in each sector, so **one HS/LS pair per motor** is switching.

### 3.1 Hard-switching overlap (HS turn-on and turn-off)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} \, I \, (t_r + t_f) \, f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 0.97\ \text{W}$$

### 3.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period)

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD} \, I \, (2 t_{dead}) \, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 1.23\ \text{W}$$

### 3.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

This term grows with di/dt; if the layout is poor, multiply it by 3.

### 3.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

### 3.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET:

$$P_g = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} \times 2 = 35\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = 0.034\ \text{W}$$

### 3.6 Switching total per motor

**≈ 2.3 W.**

Dead-time loss dominates, so reducing dead time to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per motor. Raising f_sw to 96 kHz roughly doubles this term to about 4.6 W.

### 3.7 Total loss budget at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

| Term | Per motor | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 27.9 W | 111.6 W |
| Switching + dead time | 2.3 W | 9.3 W |
| PCB copper | 5.1 W | 20.5 W |
| DC-link capacitor ESR (I_rms,cap ≈ 0.5·I) | ~0.5 W | 2 W |
| **Total** | **~35.8 W** | **~143 W** |

The resulting efficiency is approximately 1 − 143 / (4 × 80 × 25.2 × D). At full throttle this is about 98.2%. That figure looks good, but the loss is concentrated in about 7 cm² of board.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Network (per hottest FET, Cauer-reduced)

```
P_FET ──[Rth_jc 1.5 K/W, Cth_j 0.02 J/K]──┬──[Rth_c-pcb 2 K/W]──┬── PCB node (shared by 24 FETs)
                                         case                    │
                                                         Cth_pcb ≈ 4.5 J/K
                                                         Rth_pcb-amb ≈ 4 K/W still air
                                                                   ≈ 1.2 K/W in prop wash (~8 m/s)
```

### 4.2 PCB thermal capacitance

Assumptions: a 30×30 mm, 8-layer board with 2 oz copper, a 1.6 mm FR4 core, plus the FET packages.
- Copper: 8 layers × 70 µm × 9 cm² × 70% fill gives about 0.35 cm³. That is 3.1 g, and at 0.385 J/gK it contributes 1.2 J/K.
- FR4: about 1.3 cm³ gives 2.4 g, and at 1.1 J/gK it contributes 2.6 J/K.
- Packages and solder: about 0.7 J/K.
- **Total C_th ≈ 4.5 J/K.**

### 4.3 Steady state (not achievable at 80 A)

In prop wash:

$$\Delta T_{pcb} = 143 \times 1.2 = 172\ \text{K}$$

This exceeds the limit, so **80 A continuous is impossible.**

### 4.4 Burst (adiabatic first-order bound, τ = R·C = 1.2 × 4.5 ≈ 5.4 s)

$$T_{pcb}(t) = T_0 + P R_{th}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right)$$

The hottest FET junction temperature is:

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}(R_{jc} + R_{c\text{-}pcb})$$

The worst FET carries about 4.6 W of conduction loss plus about 1.2 W of switching loss (PWM leg), for roughly 5.8 W total. Across R_jc + R_c-pcb (3.5 K/W) that is a **+20 K junction-to-PCB rise**.

Assuming T₀ = 40 °C and a T_j limit of 150 °C (keep 125 °C for margin), the PCB must stay at or below 105 °C:

$$t_{burst} = -\tau \ln\!\left(1 - \frac{105 - 40}{172}\right) = -5.4 \ln(0.622) \approx 2.6\ \text{s}$$
