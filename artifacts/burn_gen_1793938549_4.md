# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #56): Architecture, Loss Model, DShot/DMA Allocation, and Netlist

## 0. Feasibility Findings

These four findings shape everything that follows:

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In any 4-in-1 design, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs (or one quad-capable ESC MCU). This design uses four AT32F421 running AM32. AM32 supports a 48 kHz PWM frequency and bidirectional DShot. The 48 kHz switching analysis therefore applies to the ESC power stage, not the H743.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is a seconds-scale, single-digit-second rating at best.** The math in §2 shows about 128 W of heat when all four channels are at 80 A. That is only survivable adiabatically for roughly 1–1.5 s. Treat 80 A as a per-channel transient rating and size continuous current from the steady-state model.
3. **Four channels at 80 A on 6S is about 7.7 kW and about 320 A from the battery.** No nano-format battery lead, connector, or bulk capacitor bank supports that simultaneously. The realistic burst is 80 A on one or two channels during a transient.
4. **Treat the netlist in §5 as a reference design.** MOSFET and driver part numbers are placeholders with representative parameters. Every value marked ⚠ must be replaced with datasheet values for the parts you actually procure. Run SPICE and thermal-imaging validation before release.

## 2. Loss Model, Per Channel

### 2.1 Conduction losses

With synchronous six-step drive, the current always flows through exactly two FETs in series:

- During t_on: a high-side FET in phase A and a low-side FET in phase B.
- During t_off: the low-side in phase A (synchronous freewheel) and the low-side in phase B.

So the instantaneous conduction loss is independent of duty:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

Include ripple, using peak-to-peak inductor ripple ΔI:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

For low-inductance racing motors, ΔI is about 10–20 A, so the ripple term adds less than 1 %. It is neglected below.

The hot resistance is:

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = k_T \cdot R_{25} = 1.4 \times 1.6\,\text{m}\Omega = 2.24\,\text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 2.24\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{28.7\ W}$$

Averaged over an electrical revolution, each of the six FETs carries the series-path role one-third of the time:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6}\times... = \frac{I^2 R}{3}\cdot... \Rightarrow \approx \mathbf{9.6\ W\ per\ FET}$$

This is the same 28.7 W spread over six devices, since each FET handles 2/6 of the total series-path time. The hot-spot FET can momentarily carry more at low eRPM, because commutation is slow relative to the thermal time constant. Budget the worst-case device at 2× the average, about 19 W, for stall or launch from rest.

### 2.2 Switching losses (hard-switched leg)

Only the PWM-ing phase leg hard-switches, with two transitions per PWM period.

**V–I overlap loss:**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery** (body diode conducts during dead time, then is hard-commutated):

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$
