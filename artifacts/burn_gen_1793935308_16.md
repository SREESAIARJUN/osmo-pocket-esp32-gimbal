# STM32H743 Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #29): Power-Stage, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

## 0. Architecture Assumptions

These points determine whether the rest of the model applies to your board.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every ArduPilot-compatible 4-in-1 design, the STM32H743 is the flight controller. It sends bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs (AM32 or BLHeli_32 on AT32F421 / STM32G071 / GD32), and those MCUs run commutation at 48 kHz. ArduPilot has no in-tree FOC or 6-step commutation for an H743 driving gate drivers directly. This document models a "stack/AIO" with the H743 FC plus 4 AM32 MCUs.
2. **80 A on a "nano" board is a burst rating, not continuous.** On a 20×20 mm board it is physically implausible. On 30×30 mm with 3 oz copper and 3.3×3.3 mm or 5×6 mm FETs it is achievable for about 5 s with airflow. Section 3 quantifies this.
3. **Every device parameter below is an assumed, representative value**, typical of a 40 V, sub-1 mΩ trench MOSFET. Replace each one with the datasheet value for the selected part before signoff. The netlist is a reference design, not a fab-ready release.

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 25.2 V | 6S, fully charged |
| I_pk | 80 A | Per-motor burst phase current |
| f_sw | 48 kHz | ESC PWM frequency |
| R_ds(on),25 | 0.80 mΩ | At V_gs = 10 V |
| α | 0.0065 /K | R_ds temperature coefficient |
| t_r, t_f | 15 ns each | With 4.7 Ω gate resistor |
| Q_g | 60 nC | Total gate charge |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

At the design point T_j = 110 °C: R_ds = 0.80 × (1 + 0.0065 × 85) = **1.24 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-step, synchronous rectification

At any instant, one high-side FET (HS) and one low-side FET (LS) of different phases carry I_pk. On the PWM leg, HS conducts for duty D and its complementary LS conducts for (1−D):

$$P_{cond,HS} = D\,I^2 R_{ds},\quad P_{cond,LS,sync} = (1-D)\,I^2R_{ds},\quad P_{cond,LS,static}= I^2R_{ds}$$

The total per channel is independent of D:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{pk}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j) = 2(80)^2(1.24\,\text{m}\Omega) = \mathbf{15.9\ W}$$

The hottest single FET is the statically-on low-side, at 7.9 W. Six-step rotates this duty among the FETs every 60° electrical, so the time-averaged load per FET is about 5.3 W.

Ripple adds a small correction: $I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \Delta I^2/12$. With ΔI ≈ 10 A this is under 0.2 %, so it is ignored.

### 1.3 Shunt and copper losses

- **Shunt:** $P = I^2R_{sh} = 6400 \times 0.25\text{ m}\Omega$ = **1.6 W per channel**.
- **Phase copper:** a 3 oz trace (≈ 0.25 mΩ/sq/oz ÷ 3 ≈ 0.17 mΩ/sq) over about 3 squares gives ≈ 0.5 mΩ, so ≈ **3.2 W per channel** at 80 A. Reduce it with stitched pours on both layers and solder-loaded pads.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the PWM leg switches hard: one HS/LS pair per channel.

| Mechanism | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V·I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 30 ns × 48 k = **1.45 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 50 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.06 W** |
| C_oss (2 FETs) | 2 × ½·C_oss·V²·f_sw | 1.5 nF × 635 × 48 k = **0.05 W** |
| Dead-time diode | V_SD·I·2t_dt·f_sw | 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (6 FETs) | 6·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw | 6 × 60 nC × 10 V × 48 k = **0.17 W** (mostly dissipated in driver and R_g) |
| **Subtotal** | | **≈ 2.3 W** |

A refinement for the overlap term: use $t_{sw} \approx Q_{gd}\,R_{g,tot}/(V_{drv}-V_{plateau})$. With Q_gd = 12 nC, R_g = 4.7 + 1.2 Ω, and V_drv − V_pl = 10 − 3.5 V, this gives about 11 ns. That is consistent with the 15 ns assumption.

Lowering t_dt from 100 ns to 50 ns saves 0.3 W per channel. Verify shoot-through margin with a double-pulse test before doing so.

### 2.1 Total loss budget at 80 A burst (T_j = 110 °C)

| Per channel | W |
|---|---|
| MOSFET conduction | 15.9 |
| MOSFET switching and diode | 2.3 |
| Shunt | 1.6 |
| Phase copper | 3.2 |
| Driver and LDO share | 0.3 |
| **Channel total** | **23.3 W** |
| **4 channels** | **≈ 93 W** |

Efficiency check: P_mech ≈ 25.2 × 80 × D_eff. At D ≈ 0.85 this is about 1.7 kW per motor. Loss of 23 W is about 1.4 %, which is plausible.

### 2.2 Battery-side ripple current

For one channel, $I_{C,rms} = I\sqrt{D(1-D)}$ = 80 × 0.5 = **40 A** worst case at D = 0.5. AM32 does not phase-interleave the four ESCs, so design for about 50–60 A total cap ripple.

Required capacitance:
- On board: 6 × 10 µF/50 V X7R 1210, about 30 µF effective after DC bias at 25 V.
- External: a ≥ 1000 µF / 35 V low-ESR electrolytic on the pigtail. This is mandatory, because the on-board MLCCs alone cannot hold V_bus ripple below about 1 V.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Junction to board (steady-state per FET)

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}\,(R_{\theta jc} + R_{\theta,TIM/solder})$$

With R_θjc = 0.9 K/W and 0.5 K/W through the solder and vias, the 7.9 W hottest FET sits about **11 K above the board**. For bursts of 1 s or more, the transient Z_th(j-c) has nearly saturated, so the board temperature is the governing variable.
