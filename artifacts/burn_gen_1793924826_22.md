## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 100 °C: R_ds = 1.2 × 1.45 = **1.74 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-step conduction with synchronous rectification

At any instant, two phases carry current I:

- **PWM leg:** the high side conducts for duty D and the low side for 1−D (synchronous rectification). The leg therefore always has one FET carrying I.
- **Static leg:** one FET is on 100% of the time.

$$P_{cond} = I^2\big[R_{HS}D + R_{LS}(1-D)\big] + I^2R_{static} \approx 2I^2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This expression is independent of D for matched FETs.

Worked value:
$$P_{cond}=2(80)^2(1.74\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{22.3\ W\ per\ channel}$$

### 1.3 Copper and shunt losses

$$P_{Cu}=I^2(R_{trace}+R_{shunt})$$

With 0.3 mΩ of path resistance plus a 0.2 mΩ shunt: P_Cu = 6400 × 0.5 mΩ = **3.2 W per channel**.

Copper resistance also rises about 0.39 %/K. Copper loss is not negligible at this current; treat layout as a first-order loss term.

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

The electrical period (≈ 0.3–2 ms) is much shorter than the die thermal time constant (tens of ms). Each of the 6 FETs therefore sees the rotation-averaged loss:

$$P_{FET,avg}=\frac{P_{cond}+P_{sw}}{6}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Transition times (gate-charge method)

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}+Q_{gs}/2}{(V_{drv}-V_{pl})/R_{g,on}},\qquad t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}+Q_{gs}/2}{V_{pl}/R_{g,off}}$$

- t_r = 10 nC / 1.3 A ≈ 7.7 ns
- t_f = 10 nC / 1.17 A ≈ 8.6 ns

Real layouts with source inductance and ringing roughly double this. I use **t_r + t_f = 40 ns** (conservative).
