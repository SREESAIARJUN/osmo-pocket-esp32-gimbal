## 0. Architectural Correction (Read First)

The STM32H743 should **not** commutate the motors. ArduPilot runs on the H743 and *emits* DShot. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware.

- **Recommended ESC MCU:** 4 × AT32F421K8U7 (QFN32) running AM32. It supports 48 kHz PWM, bidirectional DShot and EDT telemetry.
- **Partitioning:** H743 → DShot600 (bidirectional) → 4 × AT32F421 → 4 × 3-phase gate driver → 24 × MOSFETs.

### Honest feasibility flag on "80 A burst" in a nano form factor

A nano board (20×20 or 30×30 mm) can only physically fit PDFN3.3 (3.3×3.3 mm) FETs. The analysis below shows:

- **80 A on one motor:** survivable for a few seconds.
- **80 A on all four simultaneously:** not thermally survivable beyond about 2–3 s.

Treat "80 A burst" as a per-channel, short-duration, single-channel rating.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Topology

The model assumes trapezoidal (6-step) commutation with synchronous rectification.

- At any instant, two phase legs carry I: one PWM leg and one leg held low.
- In the PWM leg, the high-side conducts for D and the low-side (synchronous) for 1−D.
- The return leg's low-side conducts continuously during its 60° interval.

Per commutation interval, the loss in the current path is:

$$P_{path}=I^2\left[D\,R_{HS}+(1-D)\,R_{LS}+R_{LS}\right]+I^2R_{Cu}$$

With R_HS = R_LS = R_DS(T):

$$\boxed{P_{cond,motor}=2\,I^2R_{DS}(T_j)+I^2R_{Cu}}$$

The result is independent of D, which is why burst loss at low throttle and high current (e.g., stalled or overloaded) is just as dangerous.

### 2.2 Temperature dependence (self-consistent)

$$R_{DS}(T_j)=R_{DS,25}\left[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\right]$$

Since T_j depends on P, solve iteratively:

$$T_j^{(n+1)}=T_{ref}+Z_{th}\cdot P\left(R_{DS}(T_j^{(n)})\right)$$

At the T_j ≈ 110 °C operating point, the multiplier is 1 + 0.0055·85 = 1.47, so R_DS ≈ **2.2 mΩ**.

### 2.3 Numbers at 80 A

| Term | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 2 · 80² · 2.2 mΩ | **28.2 W** |
| Copper | 80² · 0.5 mΩ | **3.2 W** |
| **Total conduction per motor** | | **31.4 W** |

The 28.2 W is spread across 6 FETs but is unevenly time-shared. The average is about 4.7 W per FET; instantaneous loss in a conducting low-side is 80²·2.2 mΩ = 14 W.
