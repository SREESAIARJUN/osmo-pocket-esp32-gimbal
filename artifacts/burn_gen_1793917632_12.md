## 0. Architecture Clarification

The STM32H743 does **not** drive the MOSFETs. It runs ArduPilot and emits bi-directional DShot600. The ESC side needs its own commutation MCU(s), running AM32 or BLHeli_32. That is where the 48 kHz PWM lives.

```
VBAT ─┬─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
      │                                                         │
  [5V/10V BEC]                                     4× [ESC MCU → 3-ph driver → 6× FET]
      │                                                         ▲
  STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ── 4× bidir DShot600 (single-wire, 3.3V) ┘
```

**Assumed ESC MCU:** an AT32F421 or STM32G071 per motor (AM32-compatible), plus a FD6288Q-class 3-phase gate driver.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Six-step with synchronous rectification

At any commutation step, two phases conduct and the third floats:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side conducts for D·T. The complementary low-side conducts for (1−D)·T.
- **Return leg:** the low-side conducts for the full period.

Instantaneous conduction loss, independent of D:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{hot}\left[D + (1-D)\right] + I^2 R_{hot} = 2\,I^2 R_{hot}
$$

Hot resistance:

$$
R_{hot} = k_T \cdot R_{25} = 1.5 \times 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega = 1.8\,\text{m}\Omega
$$

At 80 A:

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.8\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{23.0\ W\ per\ motor}
$$

Each of the 6 FETs averages 23/6 ≈ **3.8 W** over an electrical revolution. The instantaneous peak while a FET is in the conduction path is I²R = **11.5 W**.

### 2.2 Copper loss

Current traverses two phase paths:

$$
P_{cu} = 2 \cdot I^2 R_{cu} = 2 \times 6400 \times 0.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{6.4\ W}
$$

This is often ignored on nano boards and is a major contributor. Remedies:

- Use 2 oz outer copper plus 2 oz inner planes.
- Stitch with dense vias.
- Solder-fill the motor pads.

### 2.3 Self-heating feedback (R_DS(on) vs. T_j)

R_DS(on) rises with junction temperature:

$$
R(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ /\text{K}
$$

T_j in turn depends on the conduction loss:

$$
T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}(T_j)\cdot R_{\theta,j\text{-}pcb}
$$

Solve these iteratively. There is a thermal-runaway criterion: the loop diverges if

$$
\alpha \cdot P_{FET} \cdot R_\theta \ge 1
$$

With R_θ ≈ 10 K/W (DFN on a small board, junction to local copper), that threshold is P_FET ≈ 16.7 W average. We sit at 3.8 W, so the loop is stable, but the margin shrinks as the board heats.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The high-side turns on against the low-side body diode and its Q_rr.

### 3.1 Transition times

$$
t_{on} \approx \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{src}} = \frac{12\,\text{nC}}{1\,\text{A}} = 12\ \text{ns}, \qquad t_{off} \approx \frac{12\,\text{nC}}{2\,\text{A}} = 6\ \text{ns}
$$
