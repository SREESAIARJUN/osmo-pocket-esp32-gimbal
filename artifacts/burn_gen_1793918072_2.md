# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #57): Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, Bi-Directional DShot600 Timer/DMA Allocation, and Netlists

## 0. Scope, Architecture, and Design Risks

**Architecture.** The H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does **not** commutate motors. Each motor has its own ESC MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071, running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware) that does 48 kHz PWM and commutation. The H743 talks to the four ESC MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. This split is assumed throughout. Commutating four motors at 48 kHz from the H743 while also running ArduPilot is not a supported ArduPilot configuration.

**Risks to resolve before layout:**

| # | Risk | Consequence |
|---|------|-------------|
| R1 | 80 A per motor on a nano (20×20 / 25.5×25.5 mm) board | Only survivable as a **seconds-scale burst** (derived in §3). Continuous rating will be about 25–35 A. |
| R2 | DC-link ripple current | About 40 A RMS per motor at D = 0.5. Bulk capacitance and low-ESR MLCCs dominate the BOM and board area. |
| R3 | Copper current density | 80 A requires about 2 oz internal planes plus pad stitching. Phase pad I²t must be verified. |

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.0 mΩ × 1.45 = **1.45 mΩ**.

This creates an electro-thermal feedback loop. Solve it iteratively, or use the closed form for a single device:

$$T_j = T_c + R_{\theta JC}\,P(T_j) \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; P = \frac{I^2 R_{25}(1-25\alpha) + I^2 R_{25}\alpha\, T_c}{1 - I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_{\theta JC}}$$

**Stability condition:** I² R₂₅ α R_θJC < 1.

At 80 A: 6400 × 1e-3 × 0.0045 × 1.5 = 0.043, so there is no thermal runaway at the die level. Runaway risk lives at the **board** level (§3).

### 1.2 Six-step (trapezoidal) commutation

At any instant, current flows through one high-side FET and one low-side FET in series:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_B^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j)\quad(\text{synchronous rectification on})$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.45\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{18.6\ W}$$

Each FET conducts 1/3 of the electrical period:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,I_B^2 R_{DS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(6400)(1.45\text{m}) = \mathbf{3.09\ W}$$

With complementary PWM at duty D, the conduction is split between the high-side and low-side of the switching leg, but the series-path sum is unchanged. At high throttle (D → 1), the high-side carries nearly all of the leg's share.
