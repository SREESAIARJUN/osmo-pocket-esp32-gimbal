## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

At any instant, two phases are active: phase X is PWM'd high/low (complementary), and phase Y has its low-side held ON.

| Device | Conduction interval | RMS² contribution |
|---|---|---|
| X high-side | D · T_sw | I²·D |
| X low-side (sync rect.) | (1 − D) · T_sw | I²·(1 − D) |
| Y low-side | 100 % | I² |

Summing over the PWM period, exactly two MOSFET channels are always in series with the motor current:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

This result is independent of duty cycle. That is a useful property of sync-rect 6-step, but it also means you cannot "duty-cycle your way out" of conduction loss at full current.

### 1.2 Per-device worst case

Over one electrical cycle, each phase spends 1/3 as the PWM leg, 1/3 as the return leg, and 1/3 floating. The average per-FET loss is therefore balanced. The **instantaneous** worst device, however, is the return-leg low-side carrying 100 % current for 1/6 of the electrical period.

At high eRPM (≫ thermal time constant of the die, ~ms), use the average:

$$
P_{FET,avg} = \frac{2 I^2 R_{DS}}{6} = \frac{I^2 R_{DS}}{3}
$$

At low eRPM or during a stalled/locked rotor, use the peak:

$$
P_{FET,peak} = I^2 R_{DS}
$$
