# STM32H743 AIO Nano: 4-in-1 ESC Architecture Model (Job #13)

## 0. Feasibility Findings

Three points affect every calculation that follows.

1. **The H743 cannot commutate the motors directly.** In any viable 4-in-1 design, the H743 is the flight controller. Each motor channel needs its own ESC MCU (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. These MCUs generate the 48 kHz bridge PWM. The H743's job is the DShot600 link and telemetry. The model below reflects this split.
2. **80 A burst on all four channels of a 20×20 board is thermally implausible for longer than about 1 second.** The math in §2 shows why. "80 A burst" ratings are normally per-channel, brief (≤5–10 s), assume heavy prop-wash airflow, and are often quoted as phase current rather than battery current. I model 80 A **phase** current per channel and provide burst-duration limits.
3. **The schematic is reference-grade, not a release netlist.** Treat values as a starting point. Validate them against your exact MOSFET, regulator, and IMU datasheets, and against ArduPilot's `dma_resolver.py` output, before layout.

## 2. Loss Model

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

With 6-step complementary PWM, two FETs are always in the current path: one high-side (HS) on phase A and one low-side (LS) on phase B. During PWM off-time, the complementary LS of phase A conducts in place of the HS (synchronous rectification). The path resistance is therefore always 2·R_ds, independent of duty cycle D:

$$R_{hot} = k_T \cdot R_{ds(on),25} = 1.45 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 2.18\,\text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2 R_{hot} = 2(80)^2(2.18\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{27.9\ W}$$

Per FET in the active pair:
- Fixed LS FET: ≈ I²R = 13.9 W.
- Switching leg: the same 13.9 W, split between HS (share D) and LS (share 1−D).

Commutation rotates the active pair across all 6 FETs, so the time-averaged load is:

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} = 27.9/6 \times 2 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2}\ \approx\ 4.65\ \text{W}$$

**Copper losses** must also be included. These are PCB traces plus motor and battery pads. A 20×20 board with 6 layers of 2 oz copper typically gives about 0.5–1.0 mΩ of effective path resistance per channel:

$$P_{Cu,ch} \approx 80^2 \times 0.75\,\text{m}\Omega = 4.8\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Switching losses occur in the switching leg only, at one hard-switched edge pair per PWM period.

**Overlap (V–I crossover):**
$$P_{ov} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 20\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.968\ \text{W}$$

**C_oss discharge:**
$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 1\text{nF} \cdot 635 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.015\ \text{W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two edges per period):
$$P_{dt} = V_f I_{ph} \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 100\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.307\ \text{W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{nC} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

**Gate drive** (two switching FETs, dissipated mostly in the driver):
$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 40\text{nC} \cdot 10 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.038\ \text{W}$$

**Switching total:**
$$P_{sw,ch} \approx \mathbf{1.36\ W}$$

### 2.3 Per-Channel and Board Totals at 80 A Burst

| Term | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 27.9 W | 111.6 W |
| Copper / pads | 4.8 W | 19.2 W |
| Switching | 1.36 W | 5.4 W |
| Gate driver and LDO quiescent | ~0.2 W | 0.8 W |
| **Total** | **~34.3 W** | **~137 W** |

**Efficiency at the burst point:**
- Battery current: I_bat ≈ D·I_ph. At D = 0.9, I_bat = 72 A per channel.
- Delivered power: 22.2 × 72 ≈ 1600 W per channel.
- Efficiency: η ≈ 1 − 34.3/1600 ≈ **97.9 %**.

Efficiency is excellent. The problem is power density, not efficiency.

### 2.4 Sensitivity to Switching Frequency

$$\frac{\partial P_{sw}}{\partial f_{sw}} \approx 28.3\ \mu\text{W/Hz}$$

- Dropping from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves about 0.68 W per channel.
- That saving is negligible against 28 W of conduction loss.
- 48 kHz is justified for low-inductance motors (less current ripple and audible noise). It is not the thermal limiter.
