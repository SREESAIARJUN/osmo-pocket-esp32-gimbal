# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Nano Stack): 80A Burst Thermal, 48 kHz Switching, and Bidirectional DShot600 Architecture

## 0. Architectural Corrections

Three assumptions in the brief need correcting before the math is meaningful.

1. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In every production stack, the STM32H743 is the flight controller running ArduPilot. Each motor is commutated by a dedicated ESC MCU, typically four AT32F421, STM32G071 or GD32 parts, or a single-die 4-channel BLHeli_32/AM32 design. The H743 talks to them only over DShot. This document models both boards and the interface between them.

2. **"80A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) 4-in-1 is a marketing number, not a thermal rating.** The model below shows it survives for roughly 1–3 seconds at most. Plan the board's continuous rating around 25–35A per channel.

3. **48 kHz is the ESC PWM frequency**, which is independent of DShot. DShot600 is the command link.

**Assumed operating point.** The equations are parametric, so you can substitute your own values.

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V (fresh), 22.2 V nominal |
| MOSFET | 40 V N-ch, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN. R_DS(on) = 1.0 mΩ @ 25 °C, Q_g = 50 nC, Q_rr = 30 nC, C_oss ≈ 1 nF, t_r + t_f ≈ 40 ns in-circuit |
| Topology | 6 FETs per motor (24 total), synchronous complementary PWM, 6-step trapezoidal |
| Gate drive | V_GS = 10 V. Bootstrap 3-phase driver (FD6288Q class), dead time 100 ns |
| f_sw | 48 kHz |
| Burst current | I_ph = 80 A per motor (phase current) |

> ⚠️ **On 40 V FETs at 6S:** a 25.2 V bus with layout inductance and regen spikes routinely rings to 35–40 V. Either use 60 V FETs (with roughly 1.5–2× the R_DS(on)) or add a large low-ESR bulk cap plus a TVS. The thermal numbers below get **worse** with 60 V parts.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ /^\circ\mathrm{C}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS ≈ 1.0 × 1.45 ≈ **1.45 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current path

In 6-step with synchronous rectification, phase current always flows through exactly one high-side and one low-side FET. Over a PWM period, the high-side conducts for D·T. During the remaining (1−D)·T, the complementary low-side of the same leg freewheels. Either way, two channels are in series:

$$P_{cond,FET} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j)$$

This is independent of D, which is the benefit of synchronous rectification.

$$P_{cond,FET} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.45\,\mathrm{m\Omega} = 6400 \times 2.9\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{18.6\ W\ per\ motor}$$

### 1.3 PCB copper and interconnect

Assume about 0.4 mΩ effective for the phase pad, battery bus share and vias (2 oz outer and 2 oz inner, short runs):

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.4\,\mathrm{m\Omega} = \mathbf{2.6\ W}$$

Copper has α ≈ 0.0039/°C. Add about 30% when the board is at 100 °C, which gives roughly 3.3 W.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per PWM cycle, one leg hard-switches; the other conducting leg is static.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus}\, I_{ph}\,(t_r+t_f)\, f_{sw}$$

$$= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\times10^{-9} \times 48\,000 = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per cycle, with V_F ≈ 0.8 V at 80 A:

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I_{ph}\, (2 t_{dead})\, f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\,\mathrm{ns}\times48\,\mathrm{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 30\,\mathrm{nC}\times25.2\times48\,\mathrm{k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}$$

Q_rr scales with di/dt and current. At 80 A, real Q_rr is often 2–3× the datasheet value, so budget about 0.1 W.

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,eq} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5\times1\,\mathrm{nF}\times635\times48\,\mathrm{k} = \mathbf{0.015\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FETs.

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2\ \text{(complementary pair)} = 50\,\mathrm{nC}\times10\times48\,\mathrm{k}\times2 = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 2.6 Why 48 kHz costs you

Switching losses scale linearly with f_sw. Compared with 24 kHz, 48 kHz adds about 1.3 W per motor at 80 A. The benefits are lower current ripple, quieter motors, and better low-throttle linearity on low-inductance motors. **At burst current, the cost is roughly 6–7% more heat.** AM32 and BLHeli_32 both allow 24 kHz; consider it for heavy-lift builds.

## 3. Total Loss Budget at 80A Burst

| Loss term | Per motor (W) | ×4 motors (W) |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (T_j = 125 °C) | 18.6 | 74.4 |
| Copper / interconnect (hot) | 3.3 | 13.2 |
| Switching overlap | 1.94 | 7.8 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 | 2.4 |
| Q_rr + C_oss | ~0.12 | ~0.5 |
| Gate drive (in driver) | 0.05 | 0.2 |
| **Total** | **≈ 24.6 W** | **≈ 98.5 W** |

ESC efficiency at this point, with 4 × 80 A and D ≈ 0.95: P_out ≈ 4 × 80 × 25.2 × 0.95 ≈ 7.7 kW, so η ≈ 98.7%. The efficiency looks excellent, but **≈ 100 W on a 4 cm² board is the actual problem.**

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Foster/Cauer network (per FET to ambient)

```
 P_FET ──► Tj ──[Rθjc=1.2 K/W]──► Tcase ──[Rθcb=0.8 K/W, solder+vias]──► Tboard ──[Rθba]──► Tamb
           │                     │                                       │
          C_j≈0.01 J/K          C_c≈0.05 J/K                            C_board (shared, all 24 FETs)
```

Per-FET loss at burst: each motor's ~21.2 W of FET loss (conduction plus switching) is spread unevenly across its six FETs. The worst-case FET is the one carrying both conduction and the switching transitions. Over a full electrical revolution, each FET conducts about 1/3 of the time, so averaged per FET:

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{21.2}{6}\times 2 \approx 7\ \mathrm{W}$$

The ×2 accounts for two of six FETs being active at any moment, rotating every 60°.
