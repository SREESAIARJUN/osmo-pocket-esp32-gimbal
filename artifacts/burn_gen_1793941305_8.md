# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC (Job #66): Engineering Model

## 0. Architecture Decisions and Assumptions

One correction comes first because it shapes everything else.

**The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot is not motor-control firmware. The H743 is the flight controller, and it sends bidirectional DShot600 to four independent ESC MCUs. Each ESC MCU runs AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware and generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step with synchronous rectification

During any 60° sector, phase A is high (PWM) and phase B is low (always on):

- **PWM on (D):** current flows Q_AH → motor → Q_BL
- **PWM off (1−D):** current flows Q_AL (sync rectifier) → motor → Q_BL

So at every instant exactly **two FETs carry I_ph** (ignoring dead time):

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\right]$$

With α ≈ 0.005 /K, the factor k_T reaches 1.5 at 125 °C.

At 80 A and T_j = 125 °C:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ motor}$$

### 1.2 Per-FET distribution

Over an electrical cycle each FET conducts as follows:

- **High-side:** 120° × D
- **Low-side:** 120° as return, plus 120° × (1−D) as sync rectifier

Commutation frequency (kHz range) is far faster than the die thermal time constant (about 1–10 ms). The die therefore sees the average:

$$\bar P_{FET,HS} = \frac{1}{3} D\, I^2 R, \qquad \bar P_{FET,LS} = \frac{1}{3}(2-D)\, I^2 R$$

At D → 1 (full-throttle burst), each FET averages:

$$\frac{19.2}{6} = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

At low D, the low-side FETs carry up to 2/3 · I²R = 6.4 W. **The low-side FETs are the thermal hot spot at partial throttle with high current.** That condition occurs during prop-wash recovery and during a punch from a hover.

### 1.3 Sinusoidal/FOC variant (for reference)

$$P_{cond} = 3\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds(on)}, \qquad I_{rms} = I_{pk}/\sqrt{2}$$

This is equivalent loss for equal torque within about 5%.

### 1.4 Electrothermal convergence

R_ds depends on T_j, so solve iteratively:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_{ref} + Z_{th}(t)\cdot P\!\left(T_j^{(n)}\right)$$

Divergence of this iteration indicates thermal runaway. With the numbers below it converges in 3 iterations.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg (HS plus its complementary LS) switches at f_sw.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap (HS)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}\, V_{bus}\, I\, (t_r+t_f)\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 2.2 Body-diode reverse recovery (LS diode, dissipated in HS)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

Q_rr scales with di/dt and current, so derate ×2–3 at 80 A. That gives about 0.1 W.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} \approx 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction (LS)

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in driver plus R_g, not the FET)

$$P_{gate} = 2\, Q_g\, V_{GS}\, f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$
