# FOC Motor Control and Dual-IMU Sensor Fusion for a 3-Axis Gimbal

I don't have the Job #44 spec sheet, so this design assumes the following. Replace them with your actual values.

| Parameter | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Motor | Gimbal BLDC/PMSM, 12N14P (pole pairs p = 7), R_s ≈ 5 Ω, L_s ≈ 2 mH, K_t ≈ 0.08 N·m/A |
| Encoder | 14-bit absolute magnetic (AS5048A / MT6701 class) |
| IMUs | 2 × ICM-42688-P on the camera plate, SPI @ 24 MHz, gyro ODR 8 kHz |
| MCU | Cortex-M4F/M7 with FPU (STM32G4/H7 class) |
| Topology | Yaw (outer) → Roll → Pitch (inner) |

**Loop rates:**

| Loop | Rate | Bandwidth | Feedback |
|---|---|---|---|
| Current (FOC) | 20 kHz | ~1.5 kHz | Phase shunts + encoder |
| Rate (velocity) | 4 kHz | ~150 Hz | Fused gyro (camera) → joint |
| Angle | 1 kHz | ~20 Hz | Attitude estimate |
| Cogging learning | 4 kHz | slow | Rate-loop effort |

Each loop sits roughly 5–10× below the one inside it. This separation lets each inner loop be treated as unity gain when tuning the outer one.

## 1. FOC Current Loop

### 1.1 Transforms (amplitude-invariant)

**Clarke transform:**

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}(i_a + 2i_b)\quad(\text{using } i_a+i_b+i_c=0)
$$

**Park transform**, with electrical angle θ_e = p·θ_m − θ_offset:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix} =
\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}
$$

**Inverse Park:**

$$
v_\alpha = v_d\cos\theta_e - v_q\sin\theta_e,\qquad v_\beta = v_d\sin\theta_e + v_q\cos\theta_e
$$

### 1.2 Plant model in the dq frame

$$
v_d = R i_d + L\dot i_d - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad
v_q = R i_q + L\dot i_q + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e\lambda_m
$$

Torque:

$$
\tau_e = \tfrac32 p\lambda_m i_q = K_t i_q
$$

### 1.3 PI with decoupling and pole-zero cancellation

$$
v_d^* = \Big(K_p + \tfrac{K_i}{s}\Big)(i_d^*-i_d) - \hat\omega_e \hat L i_q
$$

$$
v_q^* = \Big(K_p + \tfrac{K_i}{s}\Big)(i_q^*-i_q) + \hat\omega_e\hat L i_d + \hat\omega_e\hat\lambda_m
$$

Choose K_p = L·ω_c and K_i = R·ω_c. The PI zero then cancels the electrical pole at R/L, and the closed loop becomes first order:

$$
\frac{i}{i^*}=\frac{\omega_c}{s+\omega_c},\quad \omega_c = 2\pi\cdot1500\ \text{rad/s}
$$

Keep ω_c ≤ ω_s/10 so the PWM/sampling delay (≈1.5 T_s) costs less than about 25° of phase margin.

**Voltage limiting:**
- Clamp the dq voltage vector magnitude: ‖v_dq‖ ≤ V_bus/√3 (the linear SVPWM region).
- Use back-calculation anti-windup (§2.3).
- Hold i_d* = 0. Gimbal motors never need field weakening.

### 1.4 SVPWM via min-max zero-sequence injection

$$
v_a = v_\alpha,\quad v_b = -\tfrac12 v_\alpha + \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}v_\beta,\quad v_c = -\tfrac12 v_\alpha - \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}v_\beta
$$

$$
v_0 = -\tfrac12\big(\max(v_{abc})+\min(v_{abc})\big),\qquad
D_x = \tfrac12 + \frac{v_x+v_0}{V_{bus}}
$$

This is mathematically equivalent to sector-based SVPWM and is branch-light.

## 2. Cascaded Rate/Angle Control

### 2.1 Mechanical plant (per joint)

$$
J\ddot\theta = K_t(i_q + i_{q,ff}) - b\dot\theta - \tau_{cog}(\theta) - \tau_{d}
$$

Here τ_d is the disturbance from base motion, cable drag, and imbalance.

### 2.2 Rate loop (PI + optional acceleration feedforward)

$$
i_q^* = \underbrace{K_{pv}e_\omega + K_{iv}\!\int e_\omega\,dt}_{\text{PI}} \;+\; \underbrace{\tfrac{\hat J}{K_t}\dot\omega^*}_{\text{inertia FF}} \;+\; i_{q,cog}(\theta)
$$

where e_ω = ω* − ω.

Treating the current loop as unity gain, the plant is K_t/(Js). Choosing

$$
K_{pv} = \frac{J\omega_v}{K_t},\qquad K_{iv} = K_{pv}\frac{\omega_v}{4}
$$

places the PI zero a factor of 4 below crossover, giving ≈ 75° of phase margin before delays, with ω_v = 2π·150 rad/s.
