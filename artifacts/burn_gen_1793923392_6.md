# STM32H743 AIO (Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC), 20×20 mm: Thermal, Switching and Bidirectional DShot Model (Job #61)

## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Assumptions

Three points shape the rest of this document:

1. **The STM32H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC MCU.** ArduPilot does not commutate motors. A real AIO uses the H743 for flight control plus four dedicated ESC MCUs (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). Those MCUs generate the 48 kHz PWM, and the H743 talks to them over bidirectional DShot600.
2. **"80 A burst" on a 20×20 board cannot be sustained.** The model below shows it is only survivable for about a second. I treat 80 A as a per-channel burst rating and derive the allowable burst duration.
3. **Component parameters are representative placeholders,** not quotes from a specific datasheet. Replace them with your chosen MOSFET and driver datasheet values before sign-off.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current Path

In 6-step trapezoidal control with synchronous rectification, two MOSFETs are always in the current path:

- **During PWM on-time:** the high-side of phase A and the low-side of phase B conduct.
- **During PWM off-time:** the low-side of phase A and the low-side of phase B conduct (synchronous freewheel).

The conduction loss is therefore approximately independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature-Corrected R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha (T_j - 25)\right]$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R = 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.5 = 2.25\,\text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Results

$$P_{cond,ch} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 2.25\text{m} = 6400 \times 4.5\text{m} = \mathbf{28.8\ W\ per\ channel}$$

Each conducting FET dissipates 14.4 W while active. In 6-step, each FET is in conduction for roughly one-third to two-thirds of the electrical cycle.

**PCB copper loss.** Assume a 0.5 mΩ path for bus, phase pad and vias at 2 oz:

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.5\text{m} = 3.2\ \text{W per channel}$$

**DC-link capacitor loss.** Worst case is D = 0.5:

$$I_{C,rms} \approx I\sqrt{D(1-D)} = 40\ \text{A}$$

- With ESR = 3 mΩ for the effective bank, P ≈ 4.8 W if all channels were synchronized.
- With unsynchronized channels, total ripple adds in RMS rather than linearly.
- This is a strong argument for a low-ESR polymer bulk capacitor at the XT30 pigtail, plus 4× 10 µF/50 V X7R ceramics per channel.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In 6-step, only the PWM'd high-side hard-switches. The synchronous low-side switches softly (ZVS-like, after dead-time diode conduction).

| Loss term | Equation | Per-channel value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap (hard switching) | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 k = **1.94 W** |
| Reverse recovery (low-side body diode) | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.048 W** |
| Output capacitance | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw | **0.018 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2t_dt·f_sw | 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (two FETs switching, dissipated in driver/R_g) | Q_g·V_GS·f_sw·2 | 50 nC × 10 × 48 k × 2 = **0.048 W** |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.67 W** |

Rise time is approximately t ≈ Q_gd / I_g, with:

$$I_g \approx \frac{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}{R_g + R_{g,int}} \approx \frac{10 - 3}{2.2 + 1} \approx 2.2\ \text{A}$$

That gives about 3.6 ns ideally. 20 ns is assumed because common-source and power-loop inductance (≈2–5 nH on a nano layout) dominate.

**Design levers:**
- Cutting dead time to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel.
- Dropping to 24 kHz halves the switching loss, at the cost of more current ripple and acoustic noise.
