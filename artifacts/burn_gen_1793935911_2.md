## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.2 mΩ × 1.5 = **1.8 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current distribution in 6-step with synchronous rectification

At any instant, two phases carry current: one high-side (HS) leg and one low-side (LS) leg. With duty D on the PWM'd switch, within each 120° conduction window:

- PWM'd HS FET conducts for fraction D.
- Complementary LS FET of the same leg conducts for (1 − D).
- The opposite leg's LS FET conducts 100%.

Each phase is active for 1/3 of the electrical cycle per side.

$$I_{rms,HS}^2 = \tfrac{1}{3}\,D\,I^2,\qquad I_{rms,LS}^2 = \tfrac{1}{3}\,I^2 \;+\; \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)\,I^2$$

The **total conduction loss per motor channel is independent of D**, because a path through exactly two R_DS(on) is always present:

$$\boxed{P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)}$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.8\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{23.0\ W\ per\ channel}$$

- Average per FET (6 FETs share it): ≈ 3.8 W. The worst-case FET at high D reaches ≈ 7.7 W during its active window.
- **All four channels: P_cond ≈ 92 W.**

PCB copper/trace loss must be added. At 320 A of total battery current, even 0.1 mΩ of shared bus copper dissipates 320² × 0.1 mΩ ≈ **10 W**. A 20×20 board cannot have negligible bus resistance.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd FET in each channel hard-switches. Its LS complement soft-switches (ZVS after dead time) but contributes reverse recovery and dead-time diode loss.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}
= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 30\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2}\,Q_{oss}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.024\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F\,I\,(2t_d)\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss lands mostly in the driver and gate resistor, not the FET. For the 2 switching FETs per channel:

$$P_g = Q_g\,V_{GS}\,f_{sw} = 50\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.024\ \text{W per FET}$$

### 2.6 Switching total

$$P_{sw,ch} \approx 1.94 + 0.036 + 0.024 + 0.31 + 0.05 \approx \mathbf{2.36\ W/channel},\quad \times 4 = \mathbf{9.4\ W}$$

**Note on 48 kHz versus 24 kHz:** switching loss scales linearly with f_sw, so 48 kHz costs about 1.2 W/channel more than 24 kHz. The benefit is lower current ripple, quieter motors, and better low-throttle linearity. At D → 1 (full throttle), the PWM'd FET stops switching and P_sw → 0. The worst switching case is therefore high current at mid-to-high duty (≈ 70–95%), typical of punch-outs.

## 4. Thermal Analysis

### 4.1 Steady-state (fails)

Junction temperature:

$$T_j = T_a + P\,(R_{\theta JC} + R_{\theta CB} + R_{\theta BA})$$

A 20×20 board in prop wash has an optimistic board-to-ambient resistance of R_θBA ≈ 3–5 K/W for the whole board. At 111 W:

$$\Delta T_{BA} \approx 111 \times 3 = 333\ \text{K}$$

**Steady-state 80 A is not achievable.** The sustainable whole-board dissipation for ΔT ≈ 70 K is about 15–23 W, which corresponds to roughly **30–35 A continuous per motor**.

### 4.2 Burst model (lumped thermal capacitance)

Board thermal mass:

| Element | Mass | c_p | C_th |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copper, 6 L × 70 µm × 400 mm² | 1.5 g | 0.385 J/g·K | 0.58 J/K |
| FR-4, 1.6 mm × 400 mm² | 1.2 g | 1.1 J/g·K | 1.3 J/K |
| FETs, caps, solder, driver | ~1.0 g | ~0.9 | ~0.9 J/K |
| **Total** | | | **C_th ≈ 2.8 J/K** |
