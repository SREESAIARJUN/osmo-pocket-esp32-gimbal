# Job #71: STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80A-Burst ESC

## System Architecture: Electrical, Thermal and DShot Timing Model

---

## 0. Scope and Engineering Flags

Three issues will drive the design. They should be resolved before layout.

1. **The 48 kHz PWM is generated on the ESC side, not by the H743.** A 4-in-1 ESC has its own MCU, typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or an EFM8 running BLHeli_S/Bluejay. That MCU commutates the FETs at 48 kHz. The H743 only sends DShot600 commands and receives bidirectional telemetry. Section 3 therefore covers the FC, and Sections 1–2 and 4 cover the ESC power stage.

2. **80 A burst per channel on a "nano" (20×20 mm) footprint is not physically sustainable for more than a few seconds.** If all four motors burst together, the board draws 320 A from the battery and dissipates more than 130 W. Section 2.4 quantifies the time-to-limit. Specify burst as **single-digit seconds at ≤ 25 % duty cycle**, or move to a 30.5×30.5 mm board.

3. **No MOSFET or driver part numbers were given.** All equations below are parametric. Example numbers use the representative assumptions in Table 0. Substitute datasheet values at your selected Tj.

## 1. Loss Model: One ESC Channel (6 FETs)

### 1.1 Conduction Topology

In any commutation sector, one phase is PWM-switched (high side at duty D, with its low side synchronously rectifying for 1−D). A second phase's low side is held fully on, and the third phase floats.

The current always flows through exactly two FET channels in series:

$$
P_{cond}(T_j) = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,\big[\underbrace{D + (1-D)}_{\text{switched leg}} + \underbrace{1}_{\text{static low side}}\big] = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

The result is independent of D, as long as synchronous rectification is used. Temperature dependence:

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

At T_j = 135 °C: R_DS = 1.5 × 1.495 ≈ **2.24 mΩ**.

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.24\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{28.7\ W}
$$

**Electrothermal feedback.** Because P depends on R(T) and T depends on P, solve iteratively:

$$
T_j = T_{ref} + R_{\theta}\cdot 2I^2 R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
\;\Rightarrow\;
T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + 2I^2R_{\theta}R_{25}(1-25\alpha)}{1 - 2I^2R_{\theta}R_{25}\alpha}
$$

**Thermal runaway** occurs when the denominator reaches zero, i.e. when 2I²R_θR_25·α ≥ 1. At 80 A this means R_θ ≥ 11.6 K/W per channel. Keep the effective channel R_θ (junction to ambient, steady state) well below that, with a target of < 3 K/W.

### 1.2 Dead-Time Body Diode Conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM period:

$$
P_{dt} = 2\,V_F\,I\,t_{dt}\,f_{sw} = 2(0.8)(80)(200\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz}) = \mathbf{1.23\ W}
$$

### 1.3 Switching (V-I Overlap)

Only the PWM leg hard-switches, so there is one turn-on and one turn-off per period:
