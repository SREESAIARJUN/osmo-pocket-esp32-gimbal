# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #52): Power-Stage, Thermal, and Bi-Directional DShot Architecture Model

## 0. Scope and Critical Feasibility Findings

**Architecture assumed.** In ArduPilot systems the STM32H743 is the flight controller MCU. It is not the ESC MCU. Each of the four ESC channels uses its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, for example an AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 generates bi-directional DShot600 and decodes eRPM telemetry.

If the intent is for the H743 to commutate four motors directly (sensorless FOC or 6-step on 24 FETs), ArduPilot does not support that. It would also exhaust the advanced-timer resources: the H743 has only two advanced timers, TIM1 and TIM8, while four 3-phase bridges need four.

**The 80A burst requirement fails on a nano form factor.** "Nano" usually means 20×20 mm mounting. Sections 2–4 show the problem quantitatively:

- Four motors at 80A each dissipate about 120W total.
- The board's thermal capacitance is about 2–3 J/K.
- The board therefore heats adiabatically at about 50 K/s, which exceeds safe limits in about 1–1.5 s.
- 24 power FETs in 3.3×3.3 mm DFN packages do not fit alongside drivers, ESC MCUs, and connectors in 400 mm².

The model below is fully parameterized, so you can re-run it for your actual MOSFET choice. Section 5 gives the recommended re-specification.

> All component parameters below are **representative assumptions**. Replace them with datasheet values from your selected parts before committing the design.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel, then ×4)

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

In 6-step commutation, at any instant two bridge legs conduct: a high-side path in one phase and a low-side path in another.

With complementary PWM, the switched leg alternates current between its high and low FET. Either way, exactly two R_ds,on are always in series with the motor current. Therefore:

$$P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2 R_{ds,on}(T_j)$$

For a flat-top trapezoidal current, I_rms ≈ I_pk = 80 A:

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 2.4\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{30.7\ W}$$

**Per-FET average.** Each FET conducts 120° of every 360° electrical cycle:

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} = \frac{30.7}{6} \approx 5.1\ \text{W}$$

During its own conduction interval, the instantaneous dissipation is 80² × 2.4 mΩ = 15.4 W.

**Temperature coupling.** R_ds,on(T) ≈ R₂₅ · (1 + α(T − 25)), with α ≈ 0.005 /K. This creates positive feedback, solved in Section 3.4.

### 2.2 Hard-Switching (V-I Overlap) Losses

Only the PWM-switched FET of the active leg hard-switches:

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{pk} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

$$= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 25\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.21\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse-Recovery, Output Capacitance, Dead-Time, and Gate Losses

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse recovery | P_rr = V_bus · Q_rr · f_sw | 25.2 × 30n × 48k = **0.036 W** |
| C_oss | P_oss ≈ ½ · V_bus · Q_oss · f_sw (dissipated in the switching FET) | **0.024 W** |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | P_dt = V_f · I_pk · 2 · t_dt · f_sw | 0.85 × 80 × 200n × 48k = **0.65 W** |
| Gate drive (complementary pair) | P_g = 2 · Q_g · V_drv · f_sw | 2 × 30n × 10 × 48k = **0.029 W** (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors) |

### 2.4 Additional Distributed Losses

These are often forgotten:

- **Shunt / current sense** (0.25 mΩ shared shunt): 80² × 0.25 mΩ = **1.6 W**.
- **PCB copper** (≈ 0.3 mΩ effective phase + bus path): **≈ 1.9 W**.
- **Bulk capacitor ESR.** The capacitor ripple current at duty D is I_C,rms ≈ I·√(D(1−D)). At D = 0.5 this is 40 A. With 4 × 470 µF low-ESR capacitors in parallel (≈ 5 mΩ effective), loss is about 8 W shared across all four channels. **This is significant: capacitor heating is a separate failure mode.**

### 2.5 Loss Budget Summary at 80 A Burst, T_j = 125 °C

| Loss | Per ESC channel | ×4 (whole board) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 30.7 W | 122.9 W |
| Switching overlap | 1.21 W | 4.84 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.65 W | 2.61 W |
| Q_rr + Q_oss + gate | 0.09 W | 0.36 W |
| Shunt + copper | 3.5 W | 14.0 W |
| Bus capacitors (ESR) | — | ~8 W |
| **Total** | **~36 W** | **~153 W** |

**Key insight.** At this current, conduction dominates by about 25:1 over switching losses. Raising or lowering f_sw (24 / 48 / 96 kHz) changes total loss by only about ±2–3%. The only levers that matter are:

- lower R_ds,on, through paralleled FETs or bigger packages, and
- more thermal mass and conductance.
