## 0. Architecture Decision: Why the H743 Should Not Drive the Bridges Directly

A single H743 cannot cleanly commutate four BLDC motors.

- **Each motor needs 3 complementary PWM pairs with hardware dead-time.** Four motors need 12 pairs.
- **The H743 has only 2 advanced timers (TIM1 and TIM8)**, giving 6 pairs. Reaching 12 would require HRTIM plus TIM15/16/17, which are asymmetric, poorly supported, and incompatible with ArduPilot.
- **Back-EMF zero-crossing detection** for four motors at 48 kHz would consume the comparator and ADC resources needed by the flight controller.

**Adopted architecture:**

| Block | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIH6 (VFBGA100) or VIT6 (LQFP100), 480 MHz | ArduPilot, sends bi-directional DShot600 |
| ESC ×4 | STM32G071GBU6 (or AT32F421) running AM32 | 6-step sensorless drive, 48 kHz PWM, eRPM telemetry |
| Gate driver ×4 | FD6288Q (3-phase, 250 V, bootstrap) | High-side/low-side gate drive |
| Power stage ×4 | 6× N-MOSFET, 40 V, 5×6 mm or 3.3×3.3 mm | 3-phase bridge |
| Current sense | Shared 0.25 mΩ shunt + INA186A3 (100 V/V) | Battery current to H743 ADC |

**Feasibility flag:** an "80 A burst" rating on a nano (20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm) AIO is thermally only possible for about 1–3 seconds. Section 3 quantifies this. Specify the rating as **80 A / 1 s burst, ~35 A continuous per channel** with airflow. Otherwise the product will fail in the field.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous drive

During any commutation sector, two legs are active:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for duty D. The complementary low-side FET (synchronous rectification) conducts for 1−D, minus dead-time.
- **Static leg:** the low-side FET conducts for 100% of the sector.

At every instant, exactly two channels are in series with the motor current I. The ESC conduction loss is therefore:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j) \;+\; I_{rms}^2\,R_{Cu}$$

Temperature dependence of on-resistance:

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big],\quad \alpha\approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}\ \text{(trench Si)}$$

### 1.2 Per-FET loss distribution

These terms are averaged over one electrical revolution. Each FET is active in 2 of 6 sectors per role.

| FET role | Loss term |
|---|---|
| High-side, PWM leg | $I^2 R\,D$ |
| Low-side, PWM leg (synchronous) | $I^2 R\,(1-D-2t_{dt}f_{sw})$ |
| Low-side, static leg | $I^2 R$ |

Per-FET averaged worst case: $P_{FET,avg} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}\,I^2 R$, plus the switching share.

### 1.3 Numbers

Assumed parameters:

- MOSFET: 40 V, R_DS(on),max = 1.0 mΩ at 25 °C, Q_g = 60 nC (e.g. BSC010N04LSI-class)
- Burst current: I = 80 A (DC-equivalent motor current at full throttle)
- Target T_j = 125 °C, giving R_hot = 1.0 × (1 + 0.006 × 100) = **1.6 mΩ**
- Copper path R_Cu ≈ 0.30 mΩ (2 oz outer + 2 oz inner paralleled, battery pad to motor pad)

Results:

$$P_{cond,FET} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.6\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{20.5\ W}$$
$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.30\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{1.92\ W}$$
$$P_{cond,ESC} = \mathbf{22.4\ W\ per\ channel}$$

Instantaneous peak in a single statically-on low-side FET: $80^2 \times 1.6\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{10.2\ W}$. This value drives the junction-transient analysis in §3.3.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches.

- **High-side FET:** hard-switched on both edges.
- **Low-side FET:** quasi-ZVS (soft-switched), because the body diode conducts during dead-time.

### 2.1 Loss components

**(a) V–I overlap loss (high-side FET):**
$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}V_{bus}I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw}, \qquad t_{r,f}\approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g}}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery of the low-side body diode:**
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}$$

$$P_{oss} = 2\cdot E_{oss}f_{sw}\approx Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}$$

$$P_{dt} = 2\,V_F\,I\,t_{dt}\,f_{sw}$$
