## 0. Architecture Decision

ArduPilot does not commutate brushless motors from the flight-controller MCU. It has no on-board ESC firmware path. A credible "STM32H743 4-in-1 ESC" product is therefore built as two cooperating subsystems:

| Subsystem | MCU | Firmware | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (480 MHz) | ArduPilot | Attitude control, emits bi-directional DShot600 |
| ESC (×4 channels) | 4× AT32F421 or STM32G071 | AM32 (or BLHeli_32-class) | 6-step commutation, 48 kHz PWM, returns eRPM telemetry |

The two can share one PCB stack (FC above, ESC power stage below) or be a single-board AIO. The math below holds either way.

**Design point:**

- 6S Li-ion/LiPo, V_bus,max = 25.2 V
- f_sw = 48 kHz, center-aligned complementary PWM with synchronous rectification
- 80 A per channel **burst** (seconds), not continuous

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Current path

In 6-step trapezoidal drive, two phases conduct at any instant: one high-side FET and one low-side FET carry the motor current I_ph.

With synchronous rectification, the off-time freewheel current flows through the complementary FET, not the body diode, so the R_DS(on) path is continuous. At full throttle (D → 1), I_ph ≈ I_DC.

### 2.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 115 °C:

$$R_{DS} = 0.90\,(1+0.0045\cdot 90) = 1.26\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 2.3 Per-channel conduction loss

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j) = 80^2 \cdot 2 \cdot 1.26\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{16.1\ W}$$

Each conducting FET dissipates I²R = 8.06 W.

Over a full electrical cycle, each of the 6 FETs conducts 1/3 of the time. The average per FET is therefore ≈ 5.4 W. The instantaneous 8 W is what drives the junction ripple.

### 2.4 Copper and shunt contribution

| Path | Resistance | Loss @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase pad + 2 oz inner/outer copper, ~15 mm | ~0.4 mΩ | 2.6 W |
| Current shunt, 0.25 mΩ (shared bus shunt per ESC) | 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |

### 2.5 Thermal runaway check

Conduction loss rises with T_j through R_DS. A stable operating point exists only if

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\cdot R_{th} < 1$$

Worked check: 8.06 W × 0.0045/1.405 × R_th(3 K/W) = 0.077 ≪ 1. **Stable.**

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-modulated leg switches hard each period. The complementary FET switches at near-zero voltage after the dead time.

### 3.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot40\text{ ns}\cdot48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 3.2 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 30\text{ nC}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{ k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

Add roughly the same again in the opposing FET from induced turn-on current: ≈ 0.07 W total.

### 3.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss}V^2 f_{sw}\times 2 = 0.5\cdot1.5\text{n}\cdot635\cdot48\text{k}\cdot2 = 0.046\ \text{W}$$

### 3.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f I_{ph}\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{ ns}\cdot48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 3.5 Gate drive

Two FETs switch per active leg:

$$P_g = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw}\times2 = 50\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k}\cdot2 = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

This loss lands mostly in the driver and R_g, not in the FET.

### 3.6 Per-channel total at 80 A

| Term | Loss (W) |
|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 16.10 |
| Switching overlap | 1.94 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.12 |
| Gate drive | 0.05 |
| Copper + shunt | 4.20 |
| **Channel total** | **≈ 23.0 W** |
| **4 channels @ 80 A simultaneous** | **≈ 92 W** |

**Efficiency check:** P_out ≈ 25.2 V × 80 A = 2016 W per channel, so η ≈ 98.9%. The losses are small relative to throughput but huge relative to a nano board's thermal mass.

### 3.7 Frequency sensitivity

Losses that scale with f_sw: 1.94 + 0.61 + 0.12 + 0.05 ≈ 2.7 W.

- At 24 kHz, switching-related loss halves (save ~1.35 W/channel).
- At 96 kHz, it doubles (add ~2.7 W/channel).

48 kHz is a reasonable compromise between acoustic noise, current ripple on low-inductance quad motors, and loss.
