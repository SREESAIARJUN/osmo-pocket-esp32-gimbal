# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with Integrated 4-in-1 ESC: Design Basis (Job #1)

## 0. Architecture Correction

**The STM32H743 cannot directly commutate the motors.** In every production AIO (all-in-one) design, the work is split between two kinds of processors:

| Domain | Processor | Firmware | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flight control | STM32H743 (480 MHz) | ArduPilot | Attitude, navigation, sends bidirectional DShot600 |
| Motor commutation (×4) | AT32F421 / STM32G071 (one per motor) | AM32 (or BLHeli_32-class) | 48 kHz PWM, zero-cross/BEMF sensing, returns eRPM over bidirectional DShot |

The 48 kHz switching and the 80 A conduction happen on the ESC side. The DShot/DMA allocation happens on the H743 side. These two domains are coupled only through four signal lines plus telemetry. This document models both domains.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.0 mΩ × 1.6 = **1.6 mΩ**.

### 2.2 Current path in six-step commutation

During any 60° sector, current always flows through exactly two FETs:

- **PWM on-time:** high-side of the chopped leg plus low-side of the return leg.
- **PWM off-time:** synchronous low-side of the chopped leg plus low-side of the return leg (freewheel).

Each leg is active for 120° out of 360°, which is 1/3 of the electrical period. Per-FET average conduction loss over the electrical cycle, with duty cycle D:

| FET | Conduction fraction | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| HS, chopping | D/3 | P = I²·R·D/3 |
| LS, synchronous (same leg) | (1−D)/3 | P = I²·R·(1−D)/3 |
| LS, return leg (fully on) | 1/3 | P = I²·R/3 |

Summing over the three legs:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) \quad \text{(independent of D)}$$

The worst-case single FET is any low-side FET, which carries a fraction (2−D)/3:

$$P_{LS,max} = I^2 R_{DS}\,\frac{2-D}{3}$$

Note: AM32 chops the high side by default. If the scheme alternates, losses redistribute but the total does not change.

### 2.3 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C, D = 0.9

- Per channel: P_cond = 2 × 6400 × 0.0016 = **20.5 W**
- Worst single FET: 6400 × 0.0016 × 0.367 = **3.76 W**
- PCB copper and solder parasitics: with ~0.3 mΩ of path resistance, add 2 × 6400 × 0.0003 = 3.8 W of distributed board heat.

### 2.4 Electrothermal fixed point

Because R_DS depends on T_j, and T_j depends on dissipated power P, iterate:

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + R_{\theta JC}\,P(T_j)$$

The iteration diverges, which is thermal runaway, if:

$$\alpha \, R_{\theta JC} \, P_{25} > 1$$

At these numbers it converges easily for the junction. The real limiter is the PCB temperature (Section 5).

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Hard switching occurs only on the chopped leg. That means one HS turn-on/turn-off pair, plus the complementary LS diode and reverse-recovery event, per PWM period.

| Loss term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V·I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 30 ns × 48 k = **1.45 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 60 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.073 W** |
| Output capacitance | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw | **0.030 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | 2·V_F·I·t_dt·f_sw | 2 × 0.8 × 80 × 100 ns × 48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw | **0.058 W** (mostly in driver and gate resistor) |
| **Total switching, per channel** | | **≈ 2.2 W** |

Sensitivities:

- The overlap term scales linearly with f_sw and with edge time. At 24 kHz it drops to ≈ 0.73 W.
- The dead-time term is the second-largest contributor. Reducing dead time to 50 ns saves 0.3 W per channel, but increases shoot-through risk. Verify on the bench with a current probe.
- These losses concentrate in the two FETs of the chopped leg. That leg rotates every 60°, so averaged over the cycle each leg carries about 1/3 of the total.
