**Architecture correction.** The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs. In every production 4-in-1 design, and in ArduPilot's model:


## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation, current flows through one high-side FET of phase A and one low-side FET of phase B. With complementary PWM at duty D:

- **On interval (D):** HS_A on, LS_B on
- **Off interval (1−D):** LS_A (synchronously rectified) on, LS_B on

So exactly two FET channels always carry I, regardless of D:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Ripple correction for inductor current ripple ΔI:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

ΔI is typically <10% of I at 48 kHz on low-inductance motors (~1% correction). Neglect it.

### 1.2 Temperature feedback (self-consistent R_ds)

$$P_{cond}(T_j) = 2 I^2 R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right]$$

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per motor @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.0 mΩ | 12.8 W |
| 100 °C | 1.375 mΩ | 17.6 W |
| 125 °C | 1.5 mΩ | **19.2 W** (design value) |

The per-FET split depends on duty: HS_A dissipates D·I²R, LS_A dissipates (1−D)·I²R, and LS_B carries the full I²R. The hottest single FET therefore dissipates I²R = **9.6 W @ 125 °C**. That FET sets the junction limit.

### 1.3 Other conduction paths (do not ignore)

| Element | Model | @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Current shunt, 0.25 mΩ | I²R | 1.6 W |
| PCB copper, phase + bus | I²R_cu, R_cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ (2 oz, ~15 mm, paralleled layers) | 1.9 W |
| Solder pads / motor wire joints | ~0.1 mΩ | 0.64 W |

## 2. Switching Loss Model @ 48 kHz

With complementary PWM, only the PWM'd leg (HS_A / LS_A) hard-switches. LS_B is static during the PWM period.

**(a) V-I overlap (hard switching on HS):**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 20\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.968\ \text{W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery (LS body diode recovered by HS turn-on):**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

Q_rr itself scales with di/dt and I. Use the datasheet value at your actual di/dt.

**(c) Dead-time body-diode conduction (2 edges per period):**

$$P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 100\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.307\ \text{W}$$

**(d) Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss(er)} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 1.5\text{n} \cdot 635 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.023\ \text{W}$$

**(e) Gate charge (2 switching FETs, dissipated in driver + R_g, not in the FET die):**

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 50\text{n} \cdot 10 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

**Switching subtotal: ≈ 1.38 W per motor.**

The frequency trade-off is approximately linear in f_sw:
- At 24 kHz: ≈ 0.69 W
- At 96 kHz: ≈ 2.8 W

48 kHz is a reasonable compromise between loss and acoustic/current-ripple performance.

## 3. Total Budget and Thermal Model

### 3.1 Power budget at 80 A burst (T_j = 125 °C design point)

| Loss | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching (a–d) | 1.33 W | 5.3 W |
| Shunt + copper + joints | 4.1 W | 16.6 W |
| Gate drive | 0.05 W | 0.2 W |
| **Total** | **24.7 W** | **≈ 99 W** |

Electrical efficiency at full burst is 1 − 24.7 / (25.2 · 80 · ~0.9) ≈ 98.6%. The efficiency is fine. The problem is power density: about 100 W into a 30×30 mm board.

### 3.2 Junction rise of the hottest FET (short-term)

Use a two-node network: junction → case (Z_th, transient) → board (lumped thermal capacitance).

$$T_j(t) = T_{board}(t) + P_{FET} \cdot Z_{th,jc}(t_p)$$

For a 3.3×3.3 DFN, R_th,jc ≈ 1.2 K/W steady state. Z_th,jc at ≥100 ms approaches this value.

$$\Delta T_{jc} = 9.6 \cdot 1.2 \approx 11.5\ \text{K}$$

The junction runs about 12 K above the board. **The board, not the die, is the limiting thermal element.**
