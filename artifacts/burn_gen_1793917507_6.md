# STM32H743 AIO (4-in-1 ESC + FC) — Power, Thermal & DShot Architecture Model

## 0. Architectural Clarifications and Assumptions

**Partitioning.** ArduPilot runs on the STM32H743 as the flight controller only. It does not perform motor commutation. Each motor channel needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical choices are the AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 drives four bidirectional DShot600 lines to those MCUs. The model below follows that split.

**Datasheet values are placeholders.** No specific MOSFET part was specified, so the numbers below are representative. Replace every symbol with your real datasheet value and re-run the calculations.

**Sanity flag on the 80 A rating.** 80 A on all four channels at D = 0.9 implies about 290 A of battery current. A nano-size board cannot sustain that thermally. "80 A burst" ratings are realistically about 1–3 s, typically on one or two motors at once. Section 4 quantifies this.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

**Commutation scheme.** The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM on the active high-side leg, which is the AM32 default.

In each 60° commutation sector:

- **Phase A high-side:** switches at duty D.
- **Phase A low-side:** conducts during the off-time (1−D) as a synchronous rectifier.
- **Phase B low-side:** held fully on.

Each phase spends one-third of the electrical period high, one-third low, and one-third floating.

**Per-device average power**, with R_j = k_T · R_ds(on),25:

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,D\,I^2 R_j \qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)\,I^2R_j + \tfrac{1}{3}I^2R_j = \tfrac{1}{3}(2-D)\,I^2R_j$$

**Per-motor total.** Two devices are always in the current path, so:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 3(P_{HS}+P_{LS}) = 2\,I^2 R_j$$
