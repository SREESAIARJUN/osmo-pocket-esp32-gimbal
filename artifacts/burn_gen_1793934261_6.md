| Form factor | 20×20 mm mount ("nano") stack | |

**Architecture note.** In a 4-in-1 ESC, the H743 does not drive the gates. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU doing commutation and PWM generation. The H743's job is:
- generating bidirectional DShot600,
- capturing the eRPM telemetry replies (for the harmonic notch filter), and
- reading the ESC current/voltage sense.

The math below follows that split: power stage (§1–4), then FC timing/DMA (§5).

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C this gives R_ds ≈ 1.5 × R_25 = **1.5 mΩ per die**.

With N_p = 2 dies in parallel (and good current sharing, since the positive temperature coefficient self-balances):

$$R_{sw} = \frac{R_{ds}(T_j)}{N_p} = 0.75\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Bridge current path (6-step trapezoidal, synchronous PWM)

At any instant, two of the three legs carry the phase current I:
- **PWM leg:** current alternates between the high side (duty D) and the synchronous low side (1−D). The leg is always conducting through one switch position.
- **Return leg:** the low side is fully on.

The total instantaneous conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = I^2 R_{sw}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{sw} = 2\,I^2 R_{sw}$$

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.75\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{9.6\ W\ per\ channel\ (hot)}$$

### 1.3 Per-die stress (worst-case FET)

The full-on low side of the return leg carries I/N_p = 40 A:

$$P_{die} = 40^2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{2.4\ W}$$

Because commutation rotates every 60° electrical, each position averages roughly 1/3 duty over an electrical cycle. However, motor electrical frequencies (hundreds of Hz to kHz) are fast relative to die thermal time constants, so the burst analysis should use the averaged value. The 2.4 W instantaneous figure is used only for the low-RPM / stalled-rotor check.

### 1.4 Copper conduction (often forgotten, often dominant)

$$P_{Cu} = 2\,I^2 R_{trace}, \qquad R_{trace} = \frac{\rho_{Cu}(T)\,\ell}{w\,t}$$

with ρ_Cu(T) = 1.72 µΩ·cm × [1 + 0.0039 (T − 20)].

Example: 2 × 3 oz inner pours plus outer 2 oz, phase path ≈ 0.15 mΩ per segment gives:

$$P_{Cu} \approx 2 \times 6400 \times 0.15\,\text{m}\Omega \approx \mathbf{1.9\ W/ch}$$

Add solder-pad and motor-wire joint resistance. Measure it; don't guess.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The incoming high-side FET commutates against the low-side body diode (or channel) under an inductive load. Baseline device parameters (replace with your datasheet values at the operating V_ds and I_d):

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| t_r + t_f | 15 + 12 ns | Effective V–I overlap with R_g ≈ 3–5 Ω |
| Q_rr | 40 nC | Body-diode reverse recovery (per position) |
| C_oss(er) | 1.5 nF | Energy-related output capacitance (per position) |
| Q_g | 60 nC/die | Total gate charge @ 10 V |
| t_dt | 100 ns | Dead time per edge |
| V_f | 0.8 V | Body-diode forward voltage at 40 A
