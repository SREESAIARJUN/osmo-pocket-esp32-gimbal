# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC Nano Stack (Job #53): Engineering Model and Design Baseline

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Red Flags

Three issues need to be settled before the math means anything.

1. **Architecture.** The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. In every production 4-in-1 design, the H743 is the **flight controller**. It sends bidirectional DShot600 to a 4-in-1 ESC that has its own MCU per motor (AT32F421 / STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32), plus gate drivers and MOSFETs. I model it that way:
   - **FC domain:** H743, ArduPilot, DShot generation and eRPM capture.
   - **ESC domain:** 4 × (MCU, 3-phase driver, 6 MOSFETs).
2. **"80A burst" is interpreted as per motor, 6S (25.2 V max).** If it means 80 A total, every loss below drops by about 16× on conduction terms.
3. **Physics check.** 80 A per motor on all four channels at once, on a nano (20×20 mm) board, is **not thermally sustainable beyond about 1 s** (Section 3.4). The math below quantifies this rather than hiding it. Treat the 80 A figure as a **≤1 s, single- or dual-motor transient rating**. Alternatively, move to a 30.5×30.5 board.

All MOSFET parameters are representative of a 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN part (e.g., the class of ~1 mΩ trench FETs). **Replace them with your actual datasheet values before release.**

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S full) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Burst phase current | I | 80 A |
| R_DS(on) @ 25 °C, V_GS=10 V | R₂₅ | 1.2 mΩ |
| R_DS(on) temp coefficient | α | ≈ 0.0065 /K (×1.5 at 100 °C) |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Commutation topology

Assume 6-step trapezoidal commutation (AM32/BLHeli) with complementary (synchronous) PWM:

- At any instant, two phases carry I. One leg is PWM-switched; the other leg's low-side (or high-side) is held on.
- Each MOSFET conducts for 120° of 360°, so its conduction duty is k_c = 1/3.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 100 °C: R = 1.2 × 1.49 ≈ **1.79 mΩ**.
At T_j = 125 °C: **1.98 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Per-motor conduction loss

With synchronous rectification, the PWM leg's current alternates between the high-side (fraction D) and the low-side (fraction 1–D). The sum is always one FET. The static leg contributes one FET. Therefore:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

For trapezoidal current, I_rms ≈ I during burst (flat-top assumption, conservative):

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.79\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{22.9\ W}$$

### 1.4 Worst-case single FET

A high-side FET at D → 1 conducts for its full 120° window:

$$P_{FET,max} = I^2 R_{DS}\,k_c = 6400 \times 1.79\text{m} \times \tfrac{1}{3} = \mathbf{3.82\ W}$$

### 1.5 Copper and interconnect conduction

Assume a 2 oz outer + 2 oz inner current path. The phase path (battery pad → FET → motor pad, return) is estimated at **R_Cu ≈ 0.5 mΩ** per motor loop at 80 °C. Copper's temperature coefficient is 0.393 %/K.

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\text{m} = \mathbf{3.2\ W/motor}$$

This term is routinely ignored and is about 14 % of FET conduction. On a nano board it dominates hot-spot formation at the battery pads.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. Its loss is per motor (one HS/LS pair commutating per PWM cycle).

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard switching)

Miller-plateau transition time:

$$t_r \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g} = \frac{9\,\text{nC}}{1.2\,\text{A}} = 7.5\ \text{ns}$$

Then derate ×2 for gate resistor, plateau voltage and layout inductance, giving **t_r = t_f ≈ 15 ns**.

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I\,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two dead-time intervals per cycle)

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

Also expect added high-side turn-on loss of about Q_rr × V. That is included in the 2× derating above.

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = C_{oss,eff}\, V_{bus}^2\, f_{sw} = 1.5\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.046\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in driver and R_g, not in the FET)

$$P_g = 2\, Q_g\, V_{dr}\, f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.058\ W/motor}$$

### 2.6 Frequency sensitivity

The switching terms scale linearly with f_sw:

| f_sw | P_sw + P_dt + P_rr + P_oss |
|---|---|
| 24 kHz | 1.08 W |
| 48 kHz | **2.15 W** |
| 96 kHz | 4.31 W |

At 80 A, conduction is roughly 10× switching loss. **48 kHz is justified** for acoustic noise and current ripple on low-inductance motors, and it does not drive the thermal problem.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Loss budget at 80 A burst, T_j = 100 °C

| Term | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 22.9 W | 91.6 W |
| Copper conduction | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| Switching (all) | 2.15 W | 8.6 W |
| Gate drive + ESC MCU + LDO | 0.15 W | 0.6 W |
| **Total** | **28.4 W** | **113.6 W** |

Efficiency check: output is about 2 × 80 × 25.2 × D. At D = 0.9 that is ≈ 3.6 kW per motor, so loss ≈ 0.8 %. Electrical efficiency is fine. The problem is **power density**, not efficiency.

### 3.2 Junction steady-state (single FET)

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}\,R_{\theta JC} = T_{pcb} + 3.82 \times 1.5 = T_{pcb} + 5.7\ ^\circ\text{C}$$

The FET die is not the bottleneck. **The board is.**

### 3.3 Board-level steady state

Assumptions:
- Nano board, 20×20 mm, 6-layer 2 oz.
- Effective convective area, both sides plus pads: ≈ 1.2×10⁻³ m².
- Prop-wash h ≈ 60–100 W/m²K.

$$R_{\theta,board-amb} \approx \frac{1}{hA} \approx \frac{1}{80 \times 1.2\times10^{-3}} \approx 10\ \text{K/W}$$

$$\Delta T_{ss} = 113.6 \times 10 \approx 1100\ \text{K}$$

**Steady 80 A ×4 is physically impossible.** Sustainable continuous loss for ΔT = 60 K is ≈ 6 W total. That corresponds to roughly:

$$I_{cont} \approx 80\sqrt{6/113.6} \approx \mathbf{18\text{–}20\ A\ per\ motor}$$

This is consistent with real 20×20 4-in-1 ratings.
