# STM32H743 + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage and DShot Architecture (Job #34)

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

Three issues in the brief need correcting before the math.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot runs on the H743 and sends DShot. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The design therefore has two parts:
   - An **FC section** with the STM32H743.
   - A **4-in-1 ESC section** with four AT32F421 or STM32G071 MCUs running AM32.
   
   48 kHz PWM is an *ESC firmware* setting. ArduPilot does not control it.
2. **"Nano" (20×20 mm) and 80 A burst are physically incompatible.** The thermal model below shows that even a 30.5×30.5 mm board only survives 80 A for about 5–10 s. I have baselined **30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz outer / 2 oz inner**. A 20×20 board should be rated around 35–45 A burst.
3. **Component values are representative.** I use a "40 V, 1.3 mΩ max, PDFN 3.3×3.3" MOSFET class rather than a specific part number. Re-run the equations with the datasheet values of the part you actually source.

**Operating assumptions**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus,max = 25.2 V, nominal 22.2 V |
| Burst | 80 A phase current per channel, 10 s, all four channels simultaneously (worst case) |
| Commutation | Six-step trapezoidal, synchronous (complementary) PWM, f_sw = 48 kHz |
| Switch positions | 2 paralleled dies per position, so 12 FETs per channel and 48 total |
| Gate drive | 10 V, FD6288Q-class 3-phase driver, R_g,ext = 4.7 Ω |
| Dead time | 100 ns |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path
In six-step operation with synchronous rectification, motor current I_m always flows through exactly **two switch positions in series**:
- During ON: one high-side and one low-side.
- During OFF: two low-sides, actively freewheeling.

Duty cycle therefore drops out of the conduction term. This is a key simplification:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_m^2\,R_{pos}(T_j) + 2\,I_m^2\,R_{Cu}$$

where R_pos is the effective resistance of one switch position (n paralleled dies):

$$R_{pos}(T_j) = \frac{R_{DS(on),25}}{n}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

### 1.2 Temperature-coupled solution
R_DS(on) rises with T_j, and T_j rises with loss. Iterating gives a fixed point:

| Iteration | T_j assumed | R_pos | P_cond (FETs) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 25 °C | 0.65 mΩ | 8.3 W |
| 1 | 90 °C | 0.90 mΩ | 11.5 W |
| 2 | 110 °C | 0.98 mΩ | 12.5 W |
| Converged | ≈110 °C | **0.98 mΩ** | **12.5 W/ch** |

Calculation at convergence: 2 × 80² × 0.98 mΩ = 12.5 W.

### 1.3 Copper and shunt loss
- **Phase and bus copper:** about 0.3 mΩ per path at 80 °C (2 oz, 4 mm pours, vias stitched). Loss = 2 × 6400 × 0.3 mΩ = **3.8 W/ch**.
- **Current shunt:** 0.25 mΩ effective (2× 0.5 mΩ 2512 in parallel) in the bus return. It sees I_bus ≈ D·I_m, so 80² × 0.25 mΩ = **1.6 W** for the whole board at D≈1. This assumes a single shunt for total current.

**Conduction total per channel: about 16.3 W.**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In synchronous six-step, **one half-bridge per sector is hard-switched**. Its low-side turns off into dead time, the body diode conducts, and then the high-side turns on hard.

### 2.1 Gate-charge-based transition times
Plateau current from the driver:

$$I_g \approx \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}+R_{drv}} = \frac{10-3.0}{4.7+1.0+2.0}\approx 0.9\ \text{A}$$

The Miller interval for a 2-die position is Q_gd ≈ 2 × 5 nC = 10 nC, so t_sw ≈ 11 ns ideal. Applying a layout and common-source-inductance derating of about 1.8× gives **t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 20 ns**.

### 2.2 Loss components (V = 25.2 V, I = 80 A, f = 48 kHz)

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 k = **1.94 W** |
| Dead-time diode | 2·V_F·I·t_dt·f | 2 × 0.8 × 80 × 100 ns × 48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | V·Q_rr·f | 25.2 × 80 nC × 48 k = **0.10 W** |
| Output charge | Q_oss·V·f | 2 × 25 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.06 W** |
| Gate drive (driver dissipation) | Q_g·V_drv·f × 2 positions | 60 nC × 10 × 48 k × 2 = **0.06 W** |
| **Total** | | **≈ 2.8 W/ch** |

### 2.3 Duty-cycle caveat
At D → 1 (full throttle), the PWM stops toggling and switching loss tends toward 0. The worst *combined* case is about D = 0.85–0.95 at high current. For margin, I conservatively add the full 2.8 W.
