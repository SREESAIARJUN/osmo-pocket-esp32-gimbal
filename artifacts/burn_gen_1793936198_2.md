## 0. Architecture Decision (Read First)

The phrase "STM32H743 4-in-1 ESC" admits two interpretations. Only one of them is viable.

| Option | Description | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A | H743 directly commutates all 4 motors (24 gate signals, 4× BEMF sensing) | **Reject.** Neither ArduPilot nor AM32/BLHeli supports it. You would need roughly 24 complementary PWM outputs with dead-time, and the H743 has only TIM1/TIM8 as advanced timers (6 complementary pairs total). That covers one motor cleanly, not four. |
| B | H743 runs ArduPilot (flight controller). Four dedicated ESC MCUs (STM32G071 or AT32F421) run AM32 firmware. The link between them is bidirectional DShot600. | **Adopt.** This is the industry-standard stack and the one your DShot requirement implies. |

The rest of this document assumes **Option B**. It can be built as either:
- a single-board "AIO", or
- an FC + ESC stack joined by a 1.25 mm 8-pin JST-SH harness.

For an 80A-burst rating, **a stack is strongly recommended.** H7 thermal derating and IMU drift both suffer when the IMU sits next to roughly 100W of dissipation. If you must build an AIO, the minimum board size is 30.5×30.5 mm mounting holes (≥36×36 mm outline). The "nano" 20×20 format cannot credibly carry 80A burst per channel. Justification follows in §3.

**Commutation model.** The model assumes trapezoidal 6-step commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM, which is AM32's default. At any instant:
- one high-side FET is PWM-switching,
- its complementary low-side FET in the same leg is synchronously rectifying, and
- one low-side FET in another leg is fully on.

Current therefore always flows through **exactly two channel resistances in series.**

## 2. Loss Model (Per ESC Channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

Temperature-dependent on-resistance:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At any instant the motor current I passes through two conducting channels:
- the PWM leg, where the high-side carries current for fraction D and the low-side synchronous FET for fraction (1−D), so their sum is one full R_DS;
- the fixed low-side FET of the return leg.

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j) \cdot \left(1 - \frac{2 t_{dt} f_{sw}}{1}\right)$$

The dead-time term removes the interval during which the body diode conducts instead of the channel (§2.4). It amounts to 2·200 ns·48 kHz = 1.9%, so it is kept for completeness.

Because R_DS depends on T_j, which in turn depends on loss, the equation is solved iteratively. At T_j = 110°C (the converged value from §3.3):

$$R_{DS}(110) = 1.0 \times [1 + 0.0045 \cdot 85] = 1.38\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond}(80\,A) = 6400 \times 2 \times 1.38\times10^{-3} \times 0.981 = \mathbf{17.3\ W}$$

At 55A continuous (T_j ≈ 95°C, R_DS = 1.32 mΩ): P_cond = 3025 × 2.64e-3 × 0.981 = **7.8 W**

**Add the copper.** Conduction loss is not only in the FETs. The board copper also dissipates:
- Phase-pad to MOSFET path (2 oz outer, 2 oz inner ×2, ~0.25 mΩ effective): 6400 × 0.25e-3 = **1.6 W**
- Shared V_bus/GND planes (for 4×80 A = 320 A total, ~0.05 mΩ shared): 320² × 0.05e-3 = 5.1 W total, i.e. **1.3 W per channel share**

### 2.2 Hard-Switching (V–I Overlap) Loss

Only the high-side FET of the PWM leg hard-switches. The low-side commutates softly via its body diode.

**Gate drive current during the Miller plateau** (V_pl ≈ 4.5 V at 80 A **[VERIFY]**):

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{pl}}{R_{g,ext} + R_{g,int} + R_{drv,src}} = \frac{10 - 4.5}{4.7 + 1.5 + 5} = 0.49\ A$$

$$I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{g,ext} + R_{g,int} + R_{drv,snk}} = \frac{4.5}{4.7 + 1.5 + 3} = 0.49\ A$$

Here R_drv is the effective driver output resistance (≈5 Ω source, ≈3 Ω sink, FD6288-class **[VERIFY]**).

**Transition times:**

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd} + Q_{gs2}}{I_g}, \quad Q_{gs2} \approx 6\ nC$$

$$t_r \approx t_f \approx \frac{18\ nC}{0.49\ A} = 37\ ns$$

**Overlap loss:**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

$$P_{sw}(80\,A) = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 74\times10^{-9} \times 48\times10^{3} = \mathbf{3.58\ W}$$

$$P_{sw}(55\,A) = \mathbf{2.46\ W}$$

### 2.3 Capacitive and Recovery Losses

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 45\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.054\ W$$

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ W$$

Q_rr scales with di/dt and current, so a ×2 margin is applied at 80 A: P_rr ≈ **0.10 W**

### 2.4 Dead-Time Body-Diode Loss

$$P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate Drive Loss

Six FETs per ESC are present, but only two gates toggle at f_sw (the complementary pair of the PWM leg):

$$P_{gate} = 2 \cdot Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.058\ W$$

This power is dissipated in R_g and the driver, not the FETs.
