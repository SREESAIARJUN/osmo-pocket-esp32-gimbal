# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #61): Design Package Rev A

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Corrections

The numbers below are only as good as these assumptions. Replace every **[ASSUMED]** value with datasheet data for your selected parts before freezing the design.

**Architecture correction.** In this class of product the **STM32H743 is the flight-controller MCU running ArduPilot**. It is not the ESC controller. Each ESC channel, or one shared MCU per 4-in-1, runs AM32 or BLHeli_32 on a small Cortex-M0+/M4, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071. This package models it that way. The DShot link runs H743 timer → ESC MCU.

**Feasibility flag.** "Nano" (20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm) and "80 A" are not compatible as a continuous rating. Section 3 shows that 80 A per channel is survivable only as a **short burst, around 1–3 s**, with all four motors loaded. Treat 80 A as a burst/transient spec. The realistic continuous rating is roughly 15–25 A per channel, depending on airflow.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0053\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

$$R_{DS(on)}(110^\circ C) = 1.2\,\text{mΩ}\times 1.45 = 1.74\ \text{mΩ}$$

Because R_DS(on) depends on T_j, the loss model is coupled to the thermal model. Iterate (Section 3.4).

### 1.2 Current paths in synchronous trapezoidal drive

In each 60° commutation interval, phase A is PWM'd and phase B is the low-side return:

- **On-time (D):** current flows through Q_AH and Q_BL.
- **Off-time (1−D):** current freewheels through Q_AL (synchronous) and Q_BL.

Q_BL therefore conducts continuously. The switching leg conducts for D + (1−D) = 1. Per channel:

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}\,\big[D + (1-D)\big] + I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.74\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{22.3\ W\ per\ channel}$$

With all four channels at 80 A, this is **89 W** of conduction loss alone.

### 1.3 Battery current versus phase current

$$I_{bat} \approx D\cdot I_{ph}\quad(\text{ignoring ripple})$$

At full throttle (D → 1), I_bat ≈ 80 A per channel, or **320 A total**. That is far beyond any XT60/XT90 harness and most 6S packs. In practice, battery-side current limits will cap I_ph well before this point. Specify ArduPilot `BATT_CURR_LIMIT` and the AM32 current limit accordingly.

### 1.4 Copper and interconnect conduction loss

Assume the following path resistances:

- Phase pad + trace: R_Cu ≈ 0.25 mΩ per phase.
- Bus plane share: R_bus ≈ 0.15 mΩ.
- Copper tempco: +0.39 %/K.

$$P_{Cu} \approx I_{ph}^2(2R_{Cu}) + I_{bat}^2R_{bus} \approx 6400(0.5\text{m}) + 6400(0.15\text{m}) \approx \mathbf{4.2\ W/ch}$$

### 1.5 Shunt loss

For a 0.2 mΩ, 2512 shunt placed in the low-side return:

$$P_{shunt} = 80^2 \times 0.2\,\text{mΩ} = 1.28\ \text{W}$$

This exceeds a 1 W 2512 part. Use a **0.1 mΩ shunt (0.64 W)** plus a gain-of-200 amplifier, or use phase-current sensing.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard switching, inductive load)

$$P_{sw,VI} = \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I_{ph}\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5\times24\times80\times20\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.92\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery of the synchronous FET body diode

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 30\text{nC}\times24\times48\text{k} = 0.035\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12\,C_{oss}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5\times1.5\text{nF}\times576\times48\text{k} = 0.021\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period)

$$P_{dt} = V_F\,I_{ph}\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times100\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

Two FETs switch per PWM period in the active leg. Most of this loss lands in the driver and R_g, not the FET.

$$P_g = 2\,Q_gV_{GS}f_{sw} = 2\times40\text{nC}\times10\times48\text{k} = 0.038\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Switching loss summary per channel

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| V–I overlap | 0.92 |
| Q_rr | 0.035 |
| C_oss | 0.021 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.31 |
| Gate (driver side) | 0.04 |
| **Total switching** | **≈1.33 W** |

At 48 kHz and 24 V, switching is about 5 % of conduction loss. The design is **conduction-dominated**, so spend money on R_DS(on), not on switching speed. Raising f_sw toward 96 kHz roughly doubles the 1.33 W. That remains small, but it increases EMI and driver heating.
