# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: 80 A Burst Power-Stage Model, 48 kHz Switching Model, and ArduPilot Bi-Directional DShot600 Allocation

**Job #45: System Architecture Reference, Rev A**

## 0. Architectural Ground Truth and Feasibility

Three facts constrain the design.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** A 4-in-1 ESC has its own commutation MCUs. Typically these are one AT32F421 or STM32G071 per motor running AM32, or a BLHeli_32 target.
   - The 48 kHz PWM and 80 A switching model belong to the ESC firmware and power stage.
   - The H743 runs ArduPilot. It talks to the ESC MCUs only via bi-directional DShot600.
   - The models below are split accordingly.

2. **80 A burst on a "nano" (20×20 mm) footprint is not thermally credible.** Section 3 shows roughly 120 W of dissipation at 4×80 A.
   - A 20×20 board has about 1.6 J/K of heat capacity and reaches its FET limits in under 1 s.
   - **Recommendation:** use a 30.5×30.5 mm stack with a separate FC board above the ESC, plus an aluminium spreader.
   - Alternatively, derate a true 20×20 AIO to about 40–45 A burst.
   - The numbers below are computed for both cases.

3. **Component values are representative of a class, not a vetted BOM.** Where a parameter is assumed (MOSFET R_DS(on), Q_g, gate-driver strength), it is listed explicitly. Before freezing the design, replace these values with datasheet values for your chosen parts.
