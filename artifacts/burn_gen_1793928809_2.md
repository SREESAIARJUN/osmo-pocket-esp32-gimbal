**Summary up front:** at 80 A per motor, the power stage dissipates about 21 W per motor and about 84 W per board. A 30×30 mm board survives that for roughly 1–2 seconds. A true 20×20 mm "nano" board cannot carry 80 A in any meaningful sense. Section 1.7 sets out the derating recommendation.

## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs. It runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs, which run the commutation firmware.

```
 6S LiPo ──┬── Bulk/ceramic caps ──┬── 4× [ESC MCU → 3-ph gate driver → 6× N-MOSFET] ── Motors 1–4
           │                       │
           └── 5V/3V3 BEC ─── STM32H743 FC ── TIM1 CH1–4 (bidir DShot600) ──┘
```

## 1. Power-Stage Loss Model

### 1.1 Conduction Loss

In 6-step commutation, current at any instant flows through one high-side path and one low-side path:

- During PWM on-time, the path is HS(A) to motor to LS(B).
- During off-time with synchronous rectification, the path is LS(A) to motor to LS(B).

Either way, exactly two channels are in series with the phase current at all times. Treating motor current as approximately DC over a PWM period, which holds because L/R is much longer than 1/f_sw:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C, R_DS(on) = 1.5 mΩ:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ motor}$$

Ripple current adds a small correction:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \tfrac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

With typical racing-motor inductance (~20 µH phase-to-phase) at 48 kHz, ΔI is about 6 A, which adds roughly 0.5 %. This is negligible.

**Per-FET distribution.** Each FET conducts for about 1/3 of the electrical cycle. At high eRPM the commutation period (~0.1–1 ms) is much shorter than the die-to-board thermal time constant, so average power is the right quantity:

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{19.2}{6}\times\frac{6}{3}\times\frac{1}{2}\approx 3.2\text{–}3.5\ \text{W}$$

Low-side FETs run hotter because they carry the freewheel current.

### 1.2 Switching (Overlap) Loss

Only one leg hard-switches at a time:

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 1.3 Capacitive (Coss) Loss

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

### 1.4 Dead-Time Body-Diode and Reverse-Recovery Loss

$$P_{dt} = V_f\,I\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

$$P_{rr} \approx Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} \approx 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

### 1.5 Gate-Drive Loss

$$P_{gate} = 2\,Q_g\,V_{GS}\,f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver IC and gate resistors, not in the FETs.

### 1.6 Totals and Frequency Sensitivity

| Loss term | Per motor (W) | 4 motors (W) | Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.20 | 76.8 | 92 % |
| Switching overlap | 0.97 | 3.9 | 4.6 % |
| Dead time + Qrr | 0.66 | 2.6 | 3.2 % |
| Coss + gate | 0.11 | 0.4 | 0.5 % |
| **Total** | **20.9** | **83.7** | |

A general expression for the frequency-dependent part:

$$P_{f}(f_{sw}) = f_{sw}\left[\tfrac{1}{2}V I (t_r+t_f) + Q_{oss}V + Q_{rr}V + 2 V_f I t_{dt} + 2Q_gV_{GS}\right] \approx 36\ \mu\text{J} \times f_{sw}$$

Going from 24 kHz to 48 kHz costs only about 0.9 W per motor. Conduction loss dominates. **R_DS(on) and copper are the design levers, not PWM frequency.**

**Copper losses are not optional.** Use 2 oz copper on a 6-layer board. A 15 mm power trace that is 3 mm wide with ~5 parallel layers has about 0.2 mΩ resistance per path. At 80 A through two paths:

$$P = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.2\text{m} \approx 2.6\ \text{W per motor}$$
