# STM32H743 Nano AIO with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #38): Power-Stage, Thermal and DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and a Feasibility Warning

**Architecture assumed.** A combined flight-controller and ESC board ("AIO"):

- **Flight controller:** STM32H743 running ArduPilot. It sends bidirectional DShot600 to the ESCs.
- **ESC section:** four independent ESC MCUs (AT32F421 or STM32G071 class, running AM32 or BLHeli_32). Each switches its own 3-phase bridge at **f_sw = 48 kHz**.
- **Why not drive the bridges from the H743?** ArduPilot does not do motor commutation. Putting commutation on the H743 is a non-standard design and is out of scope.

**Electrical assumptions.**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal |
| Burst current | 80 A per motor |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal, synchronous rectification, complementary PWM |
| MOSFETs | Representative 40 V DFN3.3×3.3 class (see table below) |
| Gate driver | FD6288Q-class 3-phase bootstrap driver, V_DRV = 10 V |
| Motor phase inductance | L ≈ 30 µH line-to-line |

> ⚠️ **Feasibility flag.** Four motors at 80 A from 6S is about **8 kW** of input power and over **100 W** of losses on a 20×20 mm nano board. The math below shows this works only as a burst of roughly 1 s with no heatsink. I strongly recommend one of two changes:
>
> - Re-rate the board to 40–45 A continuous / 55 A burst.
> - Move to a 30×30 mm format, ideally with a heatsink.
>
> Also, 40 V FETs on 6S leave thin avalanche margin. Ringing on a 25.2 V bus can easily exceed 35 V, so layout and snubbing are critical. Consider 60 V parts if R_DS(on) allows.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step commutation

In any 60° sector, two phases conduct:

- **PWM leg:** current flows through the high-side switch (HS) for fraction D of each PWM period, and through the synchronous low-side switch (LS) for (1 − D).
- **Return leg:** the LS is held on.

So the motor current always passes through **two MOSFET channels in series**:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j) \quad \text{(per motor bridge, independent of D)}$$

### 1.2 Per-device distribution

Each phase is driven for 120° as the PWM leg and 120° as the return leg, so each device's duty over an electrical cycle is:

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I^2 R_{DS} \qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\left[(1-D) + 1\right] I^2 R_{DS}$$

**Key consequence: the low-side FETs run hotter at partial throttle.** At D = 0.5 the LS dissipates 3× the HS.

### 1.3 Ripple contribution

$$\Delta I_{pp} \approx \frac{V_{bus}\, D(1-D)}{L\, f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{30\,\mu\text{H} \times 48\,\text{kHz}} = 4.4\ \text{A (worst case, } D = 0.5)$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 1.6 \;\Rightarrow\; \text{negligible (+0.025\%)}$$
