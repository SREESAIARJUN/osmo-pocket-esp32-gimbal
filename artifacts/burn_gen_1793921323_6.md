> **Architectural note:** The STM32H743 does **not** drive the MOSFETs. The standard and recommended architecture uses the H743 as the flight controller (ArduPilot), which sends bidirectional DShot600 to **four independent ESC MCUs**. These are typically AT32F421 parts running AM32, or STM32G071 parts. Each ESC MCU drives a 3-phase gate driver. The loss model applies to the ESC power stage. The DMA model applies to the H743.

> **Feasibility warning:** "Nano" with 80 A per motor is thermally a *burst-only* rating. Section 3 shows the board heats at roughly 15–20 K/s adiabatically at full burst on all four motors. A credible label is **"80 A burst (≤2–3 s), ~45–55 A continuous with airflow."** Do not market 80 A continuous on a 30×30 board. On a 20×20 board, 80 A is not credible at all.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big]
$$

At T_j = 100 °C: R = 1.3 mΩ × (1 + 0.0055 × 75) = **1.84 mΩ**

### 1.2 Six-step (trapezoidal) commutation with synchronous rectification

At any instant, one phase is PWMed, one phase is held low for 120°, and one phase floats.

- **PWM ON interval (duty D):** current flows through the HS FET of leg A, the motor, and the LS FET of leg B. That is two FETs.
- **PWM OFF interval (1−D):** current freewheels through the LS FET of leg A (synchronous) and the LS FET of leg B. That is still two FETs.

So, ignoring dead-time diode conduction (handled in §2), **exactly two R_DS(on) are always in the current path**:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

### 1.3 Ripple correction

For a triangular PWM ripple of peak-to-peak value ΔI:

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I_{avg}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12},\qquad
\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{2\,L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}
$$

Here 2L_ph is the line-to-line inductance of two phases in series. Take a typical 5-inch motor with L_ph ≈ 15 µH and worst-case D = 0.5 with V_bemf ≈ D·V_bus:

ΔI ≈ (12.6 × 0.5) / (2 × 15 µH × 48 kHz) ≈ 4.4 A, so ΔI²/12 ≈ 1.6 A². This is negligible against 6400 A².

**The ripple term matters only on low-inductance, high-kV motors.**

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

Each phase spends 120° HS-active, 120° LS-active, and 120° floating.

- **HS FET:** (1/3)·D·I²·R
- **LS FET:** (1/3)·I²·R from its own low-side interval, plus (1/3)·(1−D)·I²·R from synchronous freewheel

The **low-side FETs are hotter at low duty**, which is typical at mid-throttle. Place thermal vias and copper preferentially under the LS devices.

### 1.5 Worked numbers (80 A, T_j = 100 °C)

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.84\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{23.6\ W}
$$

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Per motor (6 FETs) | 23.6 W |
| Average per FET | 3.9 W |
| Worst LS FET at D = 0.3 | (1/3)(1 + 0.7)(6400)(1.84 m) ≈ **6.7 W** |
| All 4 motors | **94 W** |

### 1.6 Copper and parasitic conduction (do not skip)

| Path | Resistance estimate | Loss @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase pad → FET, 2 oz, ~8 mm | ~0.3 mΩ | 1.9 W/motor (×2 paths) |
| Shunt (if per-ESC) | 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W/motor (only in the low-side return) |
| Battery pad → bus (shared, 320 A total!) | 0.1 mΩ | **10 W** (shared) |

The shared bus at 4 × 80 A = 320 A is the dominant copper loss. Use stacked inner planes, at least 4 layers paralleled, and place the bus capacitors at the battery pads.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWMed leg switches. The 120° LS leg is static, so per motor you analyze one hard-switched half-bridge.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard switching, inductive load)

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}
= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 35\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{1.69\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per cycle)

$$
P_{dt} = V_F\,I\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 50\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}
$$

At 80 A, Q_rr rises with di/dt. Use the datasheet curve at the actual di/dt (~1–2 A/ns), not the headline figure.

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = Q_{oss}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} \approx 40\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}
$$

### 2.5 Gate charge (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_G)

$$
P_{gate} = 2\,Q_g\,V_{GS}\,f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{ nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{ k} = 0.048\text{ W}
$$

The ×2 covers the HS and LS gates. Across three gate-driver channels per motor, this is about 0.05 W. Check that the driver package can handle it, and also account for its bootstrap/LDO loss.
