## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.2 × 1.45 = **1.74 mΩ**. Use 1.8 mΩ to cover the spread across parts.

### 1.2 Six-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification

At any instant, two phases carry I_ph. Within the active phase pair:

- The high-side FET conducts for a fraction D of the PWM period.
- The complementary low-side FET (synchronous rectifier) conducts for the fraction (1−D).
- The opposite phase's low-side FET conducts for 100 % of the period.

Each phase leg is active for 2/3 of the electrical cycle. Averaged over that cycle, the per-ESC conduction loss is:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = I_{ph}^2 \, R_{ds}\,\left[\underbrace{D + (1-D)}_{\text{PWM leg}} + \underbrace{1}_{\text{return leg}}\right] = 2\,I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}$$

The result is independent of D. This is why synchronous rectification fixes the conduction loss regardless of throttle.

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.8\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{23.0\ W}$$

Spread over 6 FETs, this averages **3.84 W per FET**.

### 1.3 FOC / sinusoidal check (if AM32 sine mode is used)

For sinusoidal currents of peak $\hat I$, the RMS current per phase is $\hat I/\sqrt2$. Total loss across the three legs is $3\,(\hat I/\sqrt2)^2 R_{ds} = 1.5\,\hat I^2 R_{ds}$.

With $\hat I$ = 80 A, P = 17.3 W. Six-step (23 W) is therefore the design worst case.

### 1.4 Copper and interconnect conduction loss

| Path | Model | R | P at 80 A |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phase pad to motor wire, per phase | 2 oz, 4 mm wide, 6 mm long, 2 layers in parallel | ≈ 0.18 mΩ | 1.15 W (2 phases active) |
| Shared battery DC bus | 4 layers × 2 oz | ≈ 0.25 mΩ | See below |

The DC bus current at full burst is $I_{bat} = \sum D_k I_k$. At D = 0.9 for all four motors, I_bat ≈ 288 A. This gives:

$$P_{bus} = 288^2 \times 0.25\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{20.7\ W}$$

**Design finding:** a 20×20 board cannot realistically carry 288 A of battery current. The XT60 rating, the pigtail (12 AWG ≈ 5.2 mΩ/m), and the pad copper all violate limits. A per-motor "80 A burst" is credible only if the four motors are not saturated simultaneously. Section 4 quantifies the time limit.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Per ESC in six-step operation, one leg is hard-switching at any time.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

The transition time follows from the gate charge and driver current:

$$t_{r,f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}+Q_{gs2}}{I_g}$$

With Q_gd + Q_gs2 ≈ 12 nC, I_g ≈ 1.2 A effective (after R_g = 2.2 Ω), t ≈ 10 ns, so t_r + t_f ≈ 20 ns:

$$P_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\times10^{-9} \times 48\times10^3 = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per period:

$$P_{dt} = V_{sd}\, I_{ph}\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 600\times10^{-9} \times 48\times10^3 = \mathbf{1.84\ W}$$

This is the largest switching-related term. Reducing t_dt from 300 ns to 150 ns (the AM32 dead-time setting) halves it. Validate the shorter dead time against shoot-through on the bench.

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\times10^{-9} \times 25.2 \times 48\times10^3 = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} \times 2 = 1.1\times10^{-9} \times 635 \times 48\times10^3 = 0.034\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

Two FETs switch per active leg:

$$P_g = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} \times 2 = 38\times10^{-9} \times 10 \times 48\times10^3 \times 2 = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g, not in the FET.

### 2.6 Loss budget at 80 A burst

| Term | Per ESC (W) | 4-in-1 total (W) |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 23.0 | 92.0 |
| Overlap switching | 0.97 | 3.9 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.84 | 7.4 |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.11 | 0.4 |
| Phase copper | 1.15 | 4.6 |
| **Subtotal, power stage** | **27.1** | **108.3** |
| DC bus copper (all motors saturated) | n/a | 20.7 |
| FC logic (H743, IMU, OSD, regulators) | n/a | ~1.5 |
| **Board total** | n/a | **≈ 130 W** |

**Efficiency check:** output power ≈ 4 × 0.9 × 25.2 × 80 ≈ 7.26 kW. Losses of 130 W give η ≈ 98.2 %, which is plausible.

The problem is not efficiency. It is that **130 W must be absorbed by roughly 10 cm² of PCB**.
