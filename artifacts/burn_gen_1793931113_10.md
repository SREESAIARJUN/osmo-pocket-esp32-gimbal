## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Assumptions

**Partitioning.** The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. It runs ArduPilot and issues DShot600. Each of the four ESC channels has its own commutation MCU running AM32, for example AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071. BLHeli_32 is end-of-life, so it is not recommended for a new design. The 48 kHz figure is the ESC-side PWM frequency, which is an AM32 setting.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 0.70 × 1.45 = **1.015 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification

In each 60° sector:
- The high-side FET of phase A is PWMed at duty D.
- The complementary low-side FET of phase A conducts for (1−D).
- The low-side FET of phase B conducts continuously.

Two FETs therefore always carry the phase current:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2\,[D R_{ds} + (1-D) R_{ds} + R_{ds}] = 2\,I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This total is independent of D.

### 1.3 Distribution across the six FETs

Each FET conducts during 120° of every 360° electrical cycle. The electrical frequency (≈3.5 kHz at 30k RPM with 14 poles) is much faster than the die thermal time constant (≈1–5 ms). The thermally averaged loss per FET is therefore:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6}$$

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A

| Term | Expression | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|---|
| MOSFET conduction, hot | 2 × 80² × 1.015 mΩ | **12.99 W** | 51.97 W |
| Copper conduction | 80² × 0.30 mΩ | 1.92 W | 7.68 W |
| Shunt (if used, 0.25 mΩ) | 80² × 0.25 mΩ | 1.60 W | 6.40 W |

The shunt loss shows why phase or bus shunts should be ≤0.25 mΩ, or replaced with a Hall-effect sensor. A common choice is a single bus shunt with a per-channel current estimate.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWMed (hard-switched) FET has V·I overlap loss. The synchronous FET turns on after its body diode is already conducting, which is near zero-voltage switching.

### 2.1 V–I overlap

$$E_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) = \tfrac12(25.2)(80)(25\,\text{ns}) = 25.2\,\mu\text{J}$$

$$P_{VI} = E_{sw} f_{sw} = 25.2\,\mu\text{J}\times48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.21\,W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}=40\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k}=\mathbf{48\,mW}$$

Q_rr grows with di/dt and current. At 80 A, verify Q_rr against the datasheet curve; it may be 2–3× the headline value.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss}=Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}=25\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k}=\mathbf{30\,mW}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period)

$$P_{dt}=V_{SD}\,I_{ph}\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw}=0.8\times80\times100\text{n}\times48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.31\,W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

$$P_g = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw}\times 2 = 40\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k}\times2 = \mathbf{38\,mW}$$

Most of this is dissipated in the gate driver and gate resistors, not in the FET.
