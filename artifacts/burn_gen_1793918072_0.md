# STM32H743 FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC (Nano): Power Stage Losses, DShot Timing, and DMA Allocation

## 0. Scope and Corrections

Three points change the design before any math:

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In a 4-in-1 stack, each ESC channel normally has its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to those four MCUs. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs, not by the H743. The model below covers both sides of that link.
2. **80 A per channel × 4 on a nano (20×20 mm) board is not thermally sustainable.** The math in §3 shows roughly 90 W of loss into about 2 J/K of thermal mass. "80A burst" must be defined as a time-limited rating of about 1 to 2 s, or the board must grow to 30×30 with paralleled FETs.
3. **Device parameters are assumptions.** Values marked [A] are representative of a 40 V, 5×6 mm trench MOSFET class. Replace them with your actual datasheet values before release.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

In 6-step drive, two phases conduct at any instant:
- Leg A: the high-side FET is PWM'd at duty D, and its synchronous low-side FET conducts for 1−D.
- Leg B: the low-side FET is held fully on.

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{hot} = 2\,I^2 R_{hot}$$

With complementary PWM, conduction loss is independent of duty.

$$R_{hot} = 1.0 \text{ mΩ} \times 1.6 = 1.6 \text{ mΩ}$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.6\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{20.5\ W}$$

The hottest single device is the continuously-on low-side FET:

$$80^2 \times 1.6 \text{ mΩ} = \mathbf{10.2\ W}$$

Its position rotates every 60° electrical, which helps averaging at high eRPM but not at stall or low speed.

### 2.2 Hard-Switching Loss

Only one leg switches at a time. Transition times come from the gate charge and the driver current at the Miller plateau:

$$t_r = \frac{Q_{gd}+Q_{gs2}}{(V_{drv}-V_{pl})/R_g} = \frac{13\text{ nC}}{1.63\text{ A}} = 8.0\text{ ns}$$

$$t_f = \frac{13\text{ nC}}{V_{pl}/R_g} = \frac{13\text{ nC}}{0.875\text{ A}} = 14.9\text{ ns}$$

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 22.9\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{1.11\ W}$$

### 2.3 Secondary Losses

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Output capacitance (C_oss) | Q_oss · V · f | 40 n × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.05 W** |
| Reverse recovery (Q_rr) | Q_rr · V · f | 50 n × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.06 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f · I · 2t_dead · f | 0.8 × 80 × 400 n × 48 k = **1.23 W** |
| Gate drive (both FETs in leg) | 2 · Q_g · V_drv · f | **0.04 W** (dissipated in driver/Rg) |

Dead-time loss is about equal to switching loss. Trimming dead time to 100 ns, if the gate driver propagation matching allows it, saves about 0.6 W per channel.

### 2.4 Totals

| | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 20.5 W | 82.0 W |
| Switching + secondary | 2.5 W | 10.0 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 23 W** | **≈ 92 W** |

Add about 1 to 3 W of copper loss in 2 oz traces and the battery pads at 320 A aggregate. A 0.2 mΩ total path gives I²R ≈ 20 W at 320 A, so in practice this is where the battery pad and bus design matters most.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Junction Rise (fast time constant)

For the hottest FET, with Z_th,jc(t ≥ 100 ms) ≈ R_θjc ≈ 1.2 K/W [A]:

$$\Delta T_{jc} = 10.2\text{ W} \times 1.2 = \mathbf{12\ K}$$

The junction-to-case rise is not the limiting factor.

### 3.2 Board Bulk (slow time constant), 2-node Cauer Model

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| C_board (20×20 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz, plus FETs and caps) | ≈ 2.0 J/K |
| R_ba (board to air, in prop wash) | ≈ 4 K/W |
| τ = R·C | 8 s |

Steady state at 92 W would be ΔT = 368 K, which is impossible. So the rating must be time-limited:

$$T_b(t) = T_0 + P R_{ba}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right)$$

Solving for the time to reach a board limit of 110 °C from 40 °C (ΔT = 70 K):

$$t = -\tau \ln\left(1 - \frac{70}{368}\right) = 8 \times 0.211 = \mathbf{1.7\ s}$$

The resulting junction temperature is about 110 + 12 + 5 (spreading) ≈ 127 °C. That is consistent with the k_T = 1.6 assumption.

### 3.3 Deliverable Rating

The defensible specifications are:
- **80 A per channel, at most 1.5 s burst, starting from a board temperature at or below 40 °C.**
- **Continuous rating:** set P_total ≤ (110 − 40)/4 ≈ 17.5 W. Since conduction dominates, P_cond ∝ I², so I_cont ≈ 80 × √(17.5/92) ≈ **35 A per channel, all four at once.**

To make "80A" honest, use one of these options:
- 30×30 mm format.
- 2 paralleled FETs per switch, which halves conduction loss to 10 W per channel.
- A copper-coin or aluminium heatsink to cut R_
