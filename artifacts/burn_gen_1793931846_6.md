## 0. Feasibility Summary

These conclusions are derived in the sections below. They set the scope of the rest of the document.

1. **80 A per motor is a time-limited burst on a nano board, not a continuous rating.**
   - The derived figures are about 23 W of loss per ESC channel and about 95 W total.
   - A 20×20 mm or 30.5×30.5 mm board has a lumped thermal capacitance of roughly 5–6 J/K.
   - With all four motors at 80 A, the safe burst window is **about 2–4 s**, depending on airflow and starting temperature.
   - The datasheet "80 A burst" figure has to be specified with a duration and duty cycle. Firmware has to enforce it through current and temperature limiting.
2. **The STM32H743 does not drive the gate drivers directly** in this architecture.
   - Each motor channel uses a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Examples are the AT32F421 and the STM32G071.
   - The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to the ESC MCUs over bi-directional DShot600.
   - The 48 kHz PWM and the commutation are generated by the ESC MCUs.
3. **Assumed operating point.** 6S LiPo (25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal), 40 V N-channel MOSFETs in 3.3×3.3 mm packages, trapezoidal (6-step) commutation with synchronous rectification.

All component values are representative. **Replace them with datasheet values for the parts you actually select**, then re-run the equations. They are written to be parametric.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A burst)

### 2.1 Current paths in 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification

In each 60° electrical sector, two legs are active:
- **Leg A (PWM leg):** the high-side FET conducts for duty fraction *D*. The complementary low-side FET conducts for *(1−D)*, minus dead time.
- **Leg B (return leg):** the low-side FET is held ON for the whole sector.

So the phase current always flows through exactly **one FET in leg A and one FET in leg B**, apart from dead-time intervals. That gives:

$$
P_{cond,ESC} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot \left[ R_{DS(on)}(T_j)_{A} + R_{DS(on)}(T_j)_{B} \right] + I_{rms}^2 R_{Cu}
$$

### 2.2 Temperature dependence of R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25} \cdot \left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0065\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

### 2.3 Ripple correction

DC-link and phase ripple are set by motor inductance *L_ph* at f_sw = 48 kHz:

$$
\Delta I_{pp} \approx \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf}) \cdot D}{2 L_{ph} f_{sw}}, \qquad I_{rms} = I_{avg}\sqrt{1 + \frac{1}{12}\left(\frac{\Delta I_{pp}}{I_{avg}}\right)^2}
$$

For a typical 5" motor, L_ph ≈ 15 µH phase-to-phase. Taking V_bus − V_bemf ≈ 4 V at D ≈ 0.85:
- ΔI_pp ≈ 4·0.85 / (2·15e-6·48e3) ≈ 2.4 A
- The ripple correction factor is ≈ 1.00004, which is negligible.
- Therefore **I_rms ≈ 80 A**.

### 2.4 Numeric evaluation

Representative values:

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| R_DS(on),25 @ V_GS = 10 V | 1.0 mΩ |
| T_j (hot, design point) | 125 °C → factor 1.65 → **1.65 mΩ** |
| R_Cu (pad, trace and via path per phase pair) | 0.35 mΩ |

$$
P_{cond} = 80^2 \cdot (2 \times 1.65\text{m} + 0.35\text{m}) = 6400 \times 3.65\text{m} = \mathbf{23.4\ W}\ (\text{hot})
$$

At T_j = 25 °C (cold start), the result is 6400 × 2.35 mΩ = **15.0 W**.

**Per-FET average.** Conduction rotates across the 6 FETs every 60° electrical. At the eRPMs seen at 80 A (more than 100k eRPM), the electrical period is under 1 ms, so averaging across FETs is valid:

$$
\bar P_{FET} \approx \frac{P_{cond}}{6} \cdot 2 \cdot \frac{1}{2}\ \text{(each FET duty)} \approx 3.3–3.9\ \text{W}
$$

## 3. Switching Losses at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches hard. The return leg changes state only at commutation, which is about 6× per electrical cycle and negligible compared with 48 kHz.

### 3.1 V–I overlap (hard switching)

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I \,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}
$$

The switching times are set by the Miller plateau:

$$
t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}},\quad I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{DRV} - V_{plateau}}{R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}}
$$

Example values: Q_gd = 9 nC, V_DRV = 10 V, V_plateau = 4.5 V, R_g = 10 Ω + 1.2 Ω.
- I_g ≈ 0.49 A, which gives t_r ≈ 18 ns
