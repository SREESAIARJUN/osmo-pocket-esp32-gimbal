## 0. Executive Findings

Read these before the derivations.

1. **The STM32H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC MCU.** Each 4-in-1 ESC channel (or one shared MCU per pair) needs its own commutation controller running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical choices are the AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 talks to them over bi-directional DShot600. The netlist reflects this two-tier split.

2. **"80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is not thermally credible for more than about 1 to 2 s with all four motors loaded simultaneously.**
   - Section 3 shows roughly 115 W of total dissipation at 4 × 80 A.
   - The board has only about 2.5 J/K of thermal mass.
   - The resulting heating rate is about 45 K/s, adiabatic.
   - **Recommendation:** rate it as 80 A per channel for ≤1 s, single-channel, or move to a 25.5×25.5 / 30.5×30.5 mm form factor.

3. **Fitting 24 MOSFETs, 4 ESC MCUs, gate drivers, bulk capacitance and an H743 on 20×20 mm is at or beyond the routing limit.** That is true even with a TFBGA100 H743 and an 8-layer HDI stack. The 30.5 mm AIO layout is the realistic target.

4. **Bi-directional DShot600 on the H743 needs 2 timers and 6 DMA streams** (via DMAMUX). This is comfortably within the H743's 16 DMA1/DMA2 streams. Section 5 gives the allocation.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

Temperature-adjusted on-resistance:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + k_T (T_j - 25)\right]$$

At T_j = 115 °C:

$$R_{ds} = 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega \times (1 + 0.0045 \times 90) = 2.11\,\text{m}\Omega$$

With synchronous rectification, the current path always runs through exactly two channel resistances, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond}(80\,\text{A}) = 2 \times 6400 \times 2.11\times10^{-3} = 27.0\,\text{W}$$

$$P_{cond}(45\,\text{A}) = 2 \times 2025 \times 1.85\times10^{-3} \approx 7.5\,\text{W} \quad (T_j \approx 75\,°\text{C})$$

**Per-FET distribution.** Each FET is in the conduction path 1/3 of the electrical cycle:

$$P_{FET,cond} = \frac{I^2 R_{ds}}{3} \approx 4.5\,\text{W} \;(@\,80\,\text{A})$$

**PCB copper loss (often ignored, but significant).**
- Assume the bus plus phase path is about 0.25 mΩ equivalent: 4 oz inner layers, ~15 mm path.
- That gives P_Cu = 80² × 0.25 mΩ = **1.6 W**.

### 2.2 Switching Losses (hard-switched leg)

Only the PWM-ing leg hard-switches. Using the linear V–I overlap approximation:

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I\, (t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}$$

$$P_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\times10^{-9} \times 48\,000 = 0.97\,\text{W}$$

### 2.3 Output Capacitance Loss

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25\times10^{-9} \times 25.2 \times 48\,000 = 0.015\,\text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-Time / Body-Diode Conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM period:

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2\, t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\times10^{-9} \times 48\,000 = 0.61\,\text{W}$$

### 2.5 Reverse Recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 20\times10^{-9} \times 25.2 \times 48\,000 = 0.024\,\text{W}$$

(This ignores the I_rr-induced added turn-on loss. Add about 10–20 % to P_sw if Q_rr is high.)

### 2.6 Gate Drive Loss

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs. Both FETs of the PWM leg switch every period:

$$P_{gate} = 2\, Q_g\, V_{gs}\, f_{sw} = 2 \times 30\times10^{-9} \times 10 \times 48\,000 = 0.029\,\text{W}$$

### 2.7 Loss Summary (per ESC channel, 6S)

| Loss term | 80 A burst | 45 A continuous |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 27.0 W | 7.5 W |
| Copper | 1.6 W | 0.5 W |
| Switching | 0.97 W | 0.55 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W | 0.35 W |
| C_oss + Q_rr + gate | 0.07 W | 0.07 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 30.3 W** | **≈ 9.0 W** |
| Efficiency (P_out ≈ V·I·D, D ≈ 0.9) | ≈ 98.3 % | ≈ 99.1 % |

**Key insight: conduction dominates (about 90 %) at burst current.** Raising f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz costs only about 0.8 W at 80 A. You buy lower motor current ripple and quieter operation at negligible thermal penalty. R_ds(on) is the lever that matters.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Junction-to-Case Rise (per FET, worst case: high-side FET on PWM leg)

$$P_{FET,max} = 4.5\,(\text{cond}) + 0.97 + 0.61/2 \approx 5.8\,\text{W}$$

$$\Delta T_{jc} = 5.8 \times 1.5 = 8.7\,\text{K}$$

**The die is not the bottleneck. The board is.**

### 3.2 Board Thermal Mass (30.5 × 30.5 mm vs 20 × 20 mm, 8-layer)

The stack-up assumes 2 × 4 oz power layers, 6 × 1 oz signal/plane layers, and 1.6 mm FR4.
