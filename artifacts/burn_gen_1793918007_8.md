# STM32H743 Flight Controller + 4-in-1 80A ESC: Engineering Model

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Risks

No component selections or board constraints were provided, so this model rests on the assumptions below. Every equation is parametric. Substitute your actual datasheet values before release.

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller running ArduPilot. It sends bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs (one per motor, e.g. AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32). Each ESC MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 does not drive gates directly.

| Parameter | Value assumed |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus,max = 25.2 V |
| Burst current | 80 A **phase current per motor**, all 4 motors simultaneously (worst case) |
| MOSFET | 40 V, DFN3.3×3.3, R_DS(on) = 1.2 mΩ @ 25 °C, 10 V V_GS; Q_g = 50 nC; Q_rr = 30 nC; Q_oss = 40 nC; t_r + t_f = 30 ns; V_SD = 0.8 V; R_θJC = 1.5 K/W |
| FET count | 24 (6 per motor), single FET per switch position |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal, complementary (synchronous) PWM, dead time t_d = 200 ns |
| Board | 30.5 × 30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz copper all layers, 1.6 mm FR-4 |
| T_amb | 40 °C; T_j derating limit = 125 °C |

**Key risks:**

* **80A burst per motor on a nano/20×20 board is not physically credible.** This model assumes a 30.5×30.5 mm footprint. On 20×20 mm the copper area cannot carry the current. 80A on 20×20 should be rejected at design review.
* "80A burst" on a 4-in-1 ESC commonly means battery current, not phase current. If your spec means 80A battery current, losses scale by roughly (I_ph,actual/80)². Confirm the definition with the customer.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.2 mΩ × 1.5 = **1.8 mΩ**. Use the hot value for the burst calculation.

### 1.2 Six-step with synchronous rectification

In each 60° sector, one high-side and one low-side FET carry the phase current. With complementary PWM, the PWM leg alternates current between its high-side FET (duty D) and low-side FET (1−D). Total conducting resistance in the loop is always 2·R_DS(on).

Losses per FET (each switch position is active 120° = 1/3 of the electrical period):

$$P_{cond,HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} I_{ph}^2 R_{DS(on)} \cdot D_{eff},\qquad P_{cond,LS} = \tfrac{1}{3} I_{ph}^2 R_{DS(on)}\cdot(2 - D_{eff})$$

The low-side expression assumes the PWM is applied to the high side. The FETs are not equally loaded, which matters for layout. The total per motor is independent of D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2 R_{DS(on)} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.8\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{23.0\ W}$$

The worst single FET is a low side at D → 0.5:

$$P_{LS,max} = \tfrac{1}{3}(6400)(1.8\text{m})(1.5) = \mathbf{5.76\ W}$$

At D = 1 every FET carries **3.84 W** while active.

### 1.3 Ripple correction

The phase inductor ripple adds a small term:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12},\quad \Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

For example, with L_ph = 15 µH at 48 kHz and V_bus − V_bemf ≈ 5 V near full throttle, ΔI ≈ 7 A. The correction is +0.06 %, which is negligible. At low throttle with low-inductance motors (< 5 µH), it is not negligible.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg hard-switches. Per motor, one leg is switching at any time.

| Term | Equation | Value per motor |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap (hard switch) | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = 0.5·25.2·80·30n·48k | **1.45 W** |
| Body-diode conduction in dead time | V_SD·I·2t_d·f_sw = 0.8·80·400n·48k | **1.23 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 30n·25.2·48k | 0.036 W |
| Output capacitance | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw = 0.5·40n·25.2·48k | 0.024 W |
| Gate charge (2 FETs/leg) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw = 2·50n·10·48k | 0.048 W* |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.8 W** |

*Gate-charge loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs.

Two findings from this table:

* **Dead-time loss is about as large as the hard-switching loss.** Reducing t_d from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per motor. That is the cheapest optimization available, but verify shoot-through margin against the driver's propagation skew.
* Going from 48 kHz to 24 kHz halves P_sw (saving about 1.4 W per motor). The cost is roughly 2× current ripple and audible/ripple torque effects.
