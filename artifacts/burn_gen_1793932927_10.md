## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Stated Assumptions

Before the math, three things need to be flagged, because they change the design.

1. **The H743 is not the ESC controller.** On every production AIO of this class, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. Each motor bridge is commutated by a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071 (×4, or ×1 for some 4-in-1 parts). The 48 kHz PWM is generated by those ESC MCUs. The H743 only emits DShot600 frames and captures the eRPM telemetry. This package models both domains with that split.

2. **80 A per channel on a nano (20×20 mm) board is thermally feasible only as a short burst.** The model below quantifies this as roughly 2 s with all four motors at 80 A. Treat "80 A" as a transient rating, never a continuous one.

3. **All component values below are assumptions, not datasheet-verified selections.** Substitute your actual BOM values into the equations. They are parametric for exactly this reason.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.45) = **1.45 mΩ** (use 1.5 mΩ for margin).

### 1.2 Six-step (trapezoidal) commutation with synchronous rectification

At any instant, two phases carry I_ph:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side conducts for D. The low-side carries the freewheel current for (1−D), in complementary mode.
- **Return leg:** the low-side is statically on.

The current therefore always traverses exactly two channels, independent of D (dead time is neglected here and handled in §2.4).

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each FET is active for 1/3 of the electrical cycle (120° conduction):

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} = \frac{P_{cond,bridge}}{6} \approx 3.2\ \text{W (average)}, \qquad P_{inst} = I^2 R_{ds} = 9.6\ \text{W while conducting}$$

The instantaneous-vs-average distinction matters for junction ripple (§4.3).

### 1.4 Copper and shunt conduction

These are often bigger than people expect.

- **Phase/bus copper.** Assume 2 oz copper, 4 parallel layers, 3 mm × 10 mm effective path:
  - R_sheet ≈ 0.25 mΩ/□ per layer, so ≈ 0.0625 mΩ/□ for four layers.
  - Path ≈ 3.3 □, so R_cu ≈ 0.2 mΩ.
  - **P_cu = 80² × 0.2 m = 1.3 W per channel.**
- **Current shunt** (if fitted, 0.25 mΩ on the shared bus): it carries the summed current. At 4 × 80 = 320 A, **P = 25.6 W**.

> **Design action:** Do not use a single bus shunt at 320 A burst. Use per-ESC shunts, or a Hall sensor (e.g., ACS7xx / TMCS11xx class) or a 100 µΩ shunt.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (f_sw = 48 kHz)

Only the PWM leg switches hard. The return leg is static.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard-switched turn-on and turn-off)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

The edge time can be estimated from the gate driver: t ≈ Q_gd / I_g,plateau with I_g = (V_drv − V_plateau)/(R_g,ext + R_g,int).

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (low-side body diode, at high-side turn-on)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

There is also a recovery-induced current overshoot that adds to the turn-on V–I overlap. Fold this in by raising t_r if you measure it.

### 2.3 Output capacitance (two FETs per leg)

$$P_{oss} = 2 \times \tfrac12 C_{oss(er)} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = 0.030\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per cycle)

$$P_{dt} = 2\, V_f\, I_{ph}\, t_{dt}\, f_{sw} = 2 \times 0.8 \times 80 \times 50\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated in the driver and R_g, not the FET.

$$P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2 \times 30\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.029\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Switching total per bridge

$$P_{sw,bridge} \approx 1.94 + 0.036 + 0.030 + 0.31 + 0.029 \approx \mathbf{2.35\ W}$$
