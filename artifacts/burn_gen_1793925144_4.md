## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Paths

In 6-step commutation, exactly two phases are active in each 60° sector.

- **PWM-on (fraction D):** current flows through the high-side FET of phase A, the motor, and the low-side FET of phase B. Two FETs conduct.
- **PWM-off (fraction 1−D), synchronous rectification:** current freewheels through the low-side FET of phase A and the low-side FET of phase B. Two FETs conduct.

Motor (phase) current I_m relates to bus current by:

$$I_{bus} = D \cdot I_m \quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{at } D\to 1,\ I_m \approx I_{bus}$$

### 1.2 Per-Motor Conduction Loss

Two FETs carry I_m at all times, so:

$$\boxed{P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_m^2\, R_{ds,hot}}$$

Each FET is assigned to a phase for 2/3 of the electrical cycle, split between high and low sides by D. Averaged over time, each of the 6 FETs dissipates:

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} = \frac{P_{cond,motor}}{6}$$

The worst-case FET is the low side at low D (it carries the freewheel current), but the average is the right figure for board thermal mass.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A Burst

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.2\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{28.2\ W}$$

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} = 4.7\ \text{W}, \qquad P_{cond,4\,motors} = 112.6\ \text{W}$$

### 1.4 Additional Copper Losses (Often Ignored, Not Negligible)

**Bus and phase copper.** Consider a 2 oz trace, 4 mm wide, 15 mm long, plus vias. Its resistance is roughly 0.3 mΩ per path, giving 80² × 0.3 m ≈ **1.9 W per motor**. Use paralleled inner planes to drive this toward ~0.1 mΩ.
