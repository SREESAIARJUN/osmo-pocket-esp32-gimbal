# STM32H743 AIO + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #22): Power-Stage Thermal Model and Bi-Directional DShot600 Architecture

## 0. Architecture Clarifications

Three points shape the rest of the analysis.

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design, the H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU, for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32. The 48 kHz PWM is generated on those ESC MCUs. The H743's job is DShot600 command output and bi-directional eRPM capture. The document is partitioned accordingly.
2. **"80 A burst" on a 20×20 mm nano board is a transient rating, not a thermal steady state.** The model in §3 shows it is sustainable for about 1–2 s, not continuously. Any spec sheet should state the burst duration.
3. **Some numbers are representative, not measured.** Parameters such as R_DS(on), Q_g and R_th are typical values for 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm PDFN MOSFETs. Replace them with your selected part's datasheet values and validate with thermocouple and IR testing. The netlists are reference-design level and need DRC/ERC and layout review before fab.

## 2. Loss Model (per motor channel, at the 80 A burst)

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

**Current path.** In 6-step trapezoidal control with complementary (synchronous) PWM, the current path at any instant contains exactly two conducting MOSFETs:

- the PWM leg's high-side or low-side device (alternating with duty cycle D), and
- the static leg's low-side device.

Over a full PWM period, the path resistance is therefore always 2·R_DS(on), independent of D, provided dead time is ignored:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I^2 R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

**Temperature dependence.**

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right]$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

$$R = 1.2\,\text{mΩ}\times(1+0.005\times85) = 1.71\,\text{mΩ}$$

$$P_{cond} = 2\times 80^2\times1.71\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{21.9\ W}$$

**Per-FET distribution.** Each FET conducts for 120° of the electrical cycle. At motor electrical frequencies of 1–3 kHz, junction thermal time constants (ms range) average this out. So:

- average per FET ≈ 21.9 / 6 ≈ **3.65 W**
- instantaneous peak while conducting ≈ **11 W**

**Thermal runaway check.** Because R_DS(on) rises with temperature, loss and temperature feed back on each other. Solve the loop self-consistently:

$$P(T_j) = 2I^2R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)], \qquad T_j = T_{ref} + R_{th}\,P(T_j)$$

$$T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + R_{th}\cdot 2I^2R_{25}(1-25\alpha)}{1 - R_{th}\cdot 2I^2R_{25}\,\alpha}$$

A stable solution requires:

$$R_{th}\cdot 2I^2R_{25}\,\alpha < 1$$

Here 2I²R₂₅α = 0.077 W/K. The effective per-channel R_th must therefore be below about 13 K/W, which is satisfied (see §3).

**Copper conduction.** A 2 oz outer layer has about 0.25 mΩ/sq. Assume roughly 1.5 squares of phase and bus copper per path, paralleled across 4 layers at an effective ~0.15 mΩ:

$$P_{Cu} \approx 80^2 \times 0.15\times10^{-3} \approx \mathbf{1.0\ W}$$

### 2.2 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches.

**Transition time** (Miller plateau ≈ 4 V, driver at 10 V, R_g,total ≈ 4 Ω):

$$t_{r,f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g} \approx \frac{9\,\text{nC}}{\sim0.9\,\text{A}} \approx 10\ \text{ns}$$

Add the current-rise portion (Q_gs2) and allow about 15 ns per edge.

**V–I overlap loss:**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\times25.2\times80\times30\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

**Coss loss:**

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 30\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery** (body diode conducts during dead time):

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

**Dead-time diode conduction** (two dead-time events per period):

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**Gate drive.** This is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors. Two FETs switch per period:

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\times45\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.043\ W}$$

### 2.3 Capacitor Ripple Loss

Input RMS ripple for a buck-like PWM leg:

$$I_{C,rms} = I\sqrt{D(1-D)} \Rightarrow \text{max } 40\ \text{A}_{rms} \text{ at } D=0.5$$

The four channels are uncorrelated, but in the worst case they are aligned. Assume a shared bank of about 8× 10 µF X7R 0805 caps at roughly 3 mΩ effective ESR each, plus a bulk electrolytic off-board.

Per channel:

$$P_{ESR} \approx 40^2\times 0.4\,\text{mΩ} \approx \mathbf{0.6\ W}$$

At D = 0.5 and 48 kHz with I = 80 A, the MLCC bank voltage ripple is:

$$\Delta V \approx \frac{I\,D(1-D)}{C f} = \frac{80 \times 0.25}{80\,\mu\text{F}\times48\text{k}} \approx 5.2\ \text{V}_{pp}$$
