# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #45): Power-Stage, Thermal, and Bidirectional DShot600 Architecture

## 0. Feasibility Findings

Two points change the architecture, so they come first.

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In any ArduPilot-compatible stack, the H743 is the flight controller. Each motor needs a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071, one per motor. The H743 talks to those MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. All loss math below concerns the ESC power stage, which runs at a 48 kHz PWM carrier set in ESC firmware.
2. **80 A per motor on a "nano" (20×20 mm) footprint is a seconds-scale burst rating, not a thermal design point.** Section 3 shows that steady-state dissipation at 80 A cannot be rejected from a 20×20 board. Treat 80 A as a burst rating of ≤2–5 s with duty-cycle limits, or move to a 30.5×30.5 mm or larger form factor. I designed to the requirement but flag the margins explicitly.

## 2. Loss Model (per motor, 6 MOSFETs, 3 half-bridges)

### 2.1 Conduction Topology

With 6-step trapezoidal commutation and complementary (synchronous) PWM, current flows through two phases at all times:

- During the on-time (fraction D), current flows through the high-side FET of phase A and the low-side FET of phase B.
- During the off-time (fraction 1−D), current freewheels through the low-side FET of phase A (synchronous rectification) and the low-side FET of phase B.

So at any instant exactly **two MOSFET channels** carry I, apart from dead time.

### 2.2 Conduction Loss

R_DS(on) depends on temperature:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

Conduction loss per motor, with dead-time intervals excluded:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)\,(1 - 2t_{dt}f_{sw})$$

The diode term below adds the dead-time portion back:

$$P_{dt} = 2\,V_F\,I\,t_{dt}\,f_{sw}$$

**Numbers at T_j = 125 °C:**

- R_DS = 0.9 mΩ × (1 + 0.0045 × 100) = **1.305 mΩ**
- 2·t_dt·f_sw = 2 × 100 ns × 48 kHz = 0.0096
- P_cond = 2 × 80² × 1.305e-3 × 0.9904 = **16.54 W**
- P_dt = 2 × 0.85 × 80 × 100e-9 × 48e3 = **0.65 W**

**Copper and interconnect loss.** Assume 2 oz copper, about 0.25 mΩ effective per phase path, plus solder pads and motor-wire joint:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{path} \approx 6400 \times 0.5\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

This term is not negligible. On a nano board it is often the hot spot.

### 2.3 Switching Loss

These terms apply to the one hard-switched leg (the PWM leg); the low side mostly soft-commutates. Each term below uses I = 80 A and V = 25.2 V.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Hard-switching V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 18e-9 × 48e3 | 0.871 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 45e-9 × 25.2 × 48e3 | 0.054 W |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss·V·f_sw (approximation) = 40e-9 × 25.2 × 48e3 | 0.048 W |
| Gate drive (2 switching FETs, mostly in driver and R_g) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw = 2 × 45e-9 × 10 × 48e3 | 0.043 W |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 1.02 W** |

At 48 kHz switching loss is only about 5% of total. **Conduction dominates by 16:1.** For 80 A, the R_DS(on) budget matters far more than the carrier frequency. Dropping to 24 kHz saves only about 0.5 W per motor and costs acoustic noise and current ripple.

### 2.4 Total Burst Dissipation

$$P_{motor} = P_{cond}+P_{dt}+P_{sw}+P_{Cu} \approx 16.54+0.65+1.02+3.2 = \mathbf{21.4\ W}$$

$$P_{4\text{-in-}1} = 4 \times 21.4 \approx \mathbf{85.6\ W\ at\ simultaneous\ 80\ A\ burst}$$

Add about 0.5 W for the ESC MCUs, the 10 V and 5 V LDO/buck, and current sensing.

Efficiency check: output power is about 25.2 × 80 × 4 ≈ 8 kW, so η ≈ 98.9%. The efficiency is excellent; the problem is the heat **density**.

### 2.5 Bus Capacitor Ripple

Capacitor ripple current per ESC:

$$I_{C,rms} = I\sqrt{D(1-D)} \;\le\; 0.5\,I = \mathbf{40\ A_{rms}}\ \text{at } D = 0.5$$

The 4 ESCs are not phase-interleaved across MCUs, so the worst-case aggregate is about 80 A_rms if summed coherently. In practice it is about √4 × 40 = 80 A_rms only if correlated, and the realistic value is about 40–60 A_rms.

- **Design value:** 1× 35 V / 1000 µF low-ESR electrolytic at the pads, plus 4× (4×10 µF X7R 50 V 1206) local to each half-bridge group.
- **Pad voltage ripple** with ESR ≈ 15 mΩ: ΔV ≈ 40 A × 15 mΩ = 0.6 V. Acceptable.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Thermal Network (per FET → ambient)

```
Tj ─[Rθjc 1.2 K/W]─ Tc ─[Rθ solder+via 3.0 K/W]─ PCB ─[Rθ PCB→air]─ Ta
 │                    │                                │
Cth,j (~0.02 J/K)   Cth,pkg (~0.1 J/K)              Cth,board+heatsink
```

### 3.2 Steady-State Check (shows the burst limit)

**Board convection.** Area is A = 2 × (0.020 m)² = 8×10⁻⁴ m². In propwash, h ≈ 100 W/m²K, so:

$$R_{\theta,board\to air} = \frac{1}{hA} = \frac{1}{100 \times 8\times10^{-4}} = 12.5\ \text{K/W}$$

$$\Delta T_{ss} = 85.6 \times 12.5 \approx 1070\ \text{K}$$

**Physically impossible: continuous 80 A is not supported.**

**Sustainable continuous current** for a 70 K board rise:

- P_max = 70 / 12.5 = 5.6 W total, i.e. 1.4 W per motor.
- Solving 2·I²·R_hot + I²·R_Cu ≈ 1.4 gives I²·(2.6 + 0.5) mΩ ≈ 1.4.
- Result: **I_cont ≈ 21 A per motor** at a 20×20 footprint.

This matches the real-world ratings of nano 4-in-1 boards.

### 3.3 Burst (Adiabatic Lumped-Mass) Model

Thermal mass:

- PCB with 2 oz copper (6-layer): ≈ 4 g × 0.9 J/gK (FR4 + Cu mix) ≈ 3.6 J/K
- Bonded aluminium heat spreader, 8 g × 0.9 J/gK = 7.2 J/K
- C_th ≈ **10.8 J/K** total

Temperature rise over a burst:

$$\Delta T(t) = P\,R_\theta\left(1-e^{-t/\tau}\right),\quad \tau = R_\theta C_{th} = 12.5 \times 10.8 = 135\ \text{s}$$

Because t ≪ τ, the adiabatic approximation holds: ΔT ≈ P·t / C_th.

| Burst duration | ΔT board (all 4 motors at 80 A) |
|---|---|
| 1 s | 85.6 / 10.8 = **7.9 K** |
| 3 s | **23.8 K** |
| 5 s | **39.6 K** |
| 8 s | **63.4 K**, hits the 105 °C board limit from 40 °C ambient |
