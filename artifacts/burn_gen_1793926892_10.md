# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC (Job #42): Loss, Thermal and Bi-Directional DShot Design Package

## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

### 0.1 System architecture

The STM32H743 cannot practically commutate 12 half-bridges with hardware dead-time while also running ArduPilot. Counting all complementary-capable outputs:

- TIM1: 3 complementary pairs
- TIM8: 3 complementary pairs
- HRTIM: 5 pairs
- TIM15/16/17: 1 pair each

That totals 14 pairs, but consuming them would starve the flight stack and the DShot timers. The design is therefore split:

| Block | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIH6 (TFBGA100) or VIT6 (LQFP100), 480 MHz | ArduPilot, bi-directional DShot600 master |
| ESC MCUs (×4) | AT32F421K8U7 (or STM32G071) | AM32 firmware, 48 kHz complementary PWM, sync rectification |
| Gate drivers (×4) | FD6288Q (3-phase, bootstrap) | 12 V gate rail |
| Power stage | 24× N-MOSFET, 40 V, 3.3×3.3 PQFN | 6 per motor |

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{ds} = 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.45 = 2.18\,\text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification

In each of the 6 sectors, current flows through exactly **two** conducting FETs at any instant:

- The PWM leg alternates between its high-side FET (for duty D) and its low-side FET (for 1−D).
- The return leg's low-side FET stays on.

The per-channel conduction loss is therefore duty-independent:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 1.3 Ripple correction

$$\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{2L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

Worst case occurs at D ≈ 0.5, with V_bemf ≈ V_bus·D:

$$\Delta I = \frac{12.6 \times 0.5}{30\,\mu\text{H} \times 48\,\text{kHz}} \approx 4.4\,\text{A}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2\left(1+\frac{(\Delta I/I)^2}{12}\right) \approx 80^2 \times 1.00025$$

The ripple term is negligible at 48 kHz.

### 1.4 Per-channel conduction loss

$$P_{cond,ch} = 6400 \times 2 \times 2.18\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{27.9\ W}$$

### 1.5 Per-FET distribution

Each leg is "active" for 2/3 of the electrical cycle. Over a full cycle:

| FET | Expression | At D = 0.3 |
|---|---|---|
| High side | P_HS = I²·R_ds·(1/3)·D | 1.40 W |
| Low side, PWM leg | I²·R_ds·(1/3)·(1−D) | 3.26 W |
| Low side, return leg | I²·R_ds·(1/3) | 4.65 W |

Total low-side loss is 7.9 W, versus 1.4 W for the high side.

**Design consequence:** low-side FETs run hottest at low/mid throttle with high current, which is exactly the punch-out-from-hover condition. Place low-side FETs on the best thermal copper.

### 1.6 Copper and interconnect conduction

A 2 oz layer has sheet resistance R_□ ≈ 0.245 mΩ/□. Phase pad to motor wire, roughly 4 squares spread across 3 parallel layers, gives about 0.33 mΩ:

- **Phase interconnect:** P_Cu,phase = 80² × 0.33 mΩ × 2 ≈ 4.2 W per channel
- **Bus distribution:** I_bus,avg = D·I_ph; at D = 0.5 that is 40 A per channel, or 160 A total. Through ~0.2 mΩ: P ≈ 5.1 W total
