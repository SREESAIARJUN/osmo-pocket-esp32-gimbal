## 0. Architecture Decisions and Assumptions

A few points must be settled before the mathematics is meaningful.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** On this class of board the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot. Each motor bridge is driven by a dedicated ESC MCU, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32, or one MCU per two channels. The 48 kHz PWM lives on the ESC MCUs. The H743 talks to them over internal bidirectional DShot600 traces. If you intend the H743 to drive gate drivers directly, the DShot section becomes irrelevant and the timer budget changes entirely. Confirm this with the customer.

**2. "80A burst" on a nano form factor is the critical risk.** A 20×20 mm board cannot physically hold 24 MOSFETs (12 per phase-leg pair × 2 sides) of the size needed to carry 80 A. The analysis below shows that even a 30.5×30.5 mm board survives 80 A per channel only for roughly 1–2 s. Treat 80 A as a **per-channel, single-motor, ≤1 s burst** rating, not a simultaneous 4×80 A (320 A) rating.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step complementary PWM

At any commutation step, two phases conduct (say A+ and B−):

- **Phase A leg (PWM'd):** the high side conducts during D·T. The low side conducts synchronously during (1−D)·T, minus dead time.
- **Phase B leg:** the low side is on continuously for the step.
- **Phase C:** floating, used for BEMF sensing.

The motor current therefore always flows through **exactly two channel-resistances in series**, regardless of duty cycle. This is the key simplification:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{rms}^2 \, R_{DS(on)}(T_j) \;+\; I_{rms}^2\,R_{Cu}$$

### 1.2 RMS current including ripple

For a triangular ripple ΔI on top of DC current I:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

Ripple is set by the motor phase-to-phase inductance:

$$\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph\text{-}ph}\, f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5″ motor (L ≈ 15 µH ph-ph) at D = 0.8 with V_bemf ≈ 18 V:
ΔI ≈ (7.2 × 0.8)/(15e-6 × 48e3) ≈ **8 A**.
The correction is 8²/12 = 5.3 A², which is under 0.1 % of 6400 A². **Ripple is negligible at burst current**, so I_rms ≈ 80 A.

### 1.3 Temperature dependence of R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left(\frac{T_j + 273}{298}\right)^{\kappa},\quad \kappa \approx 2.0\text{–}2.3$$

At T_j = 110 °C with κ = 2.2: R ≈ 1.0 × (383/298)^2.2 = **1.74 mΩ**.

Because R depends on T_j and T_j depends on P, the problem is a fixed-point iteration (§4.3).

### 1.4 Copper and interconnect resistance

| Path | Resistance |
|---|---|
| Phase pad → motor wire solder joint and pour (2 oz, multilayer) | ≈0.3 mΩ |
| Bus plane + ground return share | ≈0.2 mΩ |
| Shunt (if per-channel; usually only a single bus shunt) | 0 |
| **R_Cu** | **≈0.5 mΩ** |

### 1.5 Result at 80 A, T_j = 110 °C

$$P_{cond,FET} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.74\text{ mΩ} = \mathbf{22.3\ W}$$
$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.5\text{ mΩ} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

### 1.6 Per-device split (needed for junction temperature)

Averaged over a full electrical cycle, each leg spends 1/3 of the time PWM'd, 1/3 statically low, and 1/3 floating. For D = 0.8:

| Device | Conducting fraction | Avg loss |
|---|---|---|
| High side (A+) | D/3 = 0.267 | 0.267 × I²R = 2.97 W |
| Low side, sync (A−, PWM step) | (1−D)/3 = 0.067 | 0.74 W |
| Low side, static (B−) | 1/3 = 0.333 | 3.71 W |
| **Worst LS (sum per device)** | 0.40 | **≈4.45 W** |

Here I²R = 6400 × 1.74e-3 = 11.1 W instantaneous. **Peak instantaneous dissipation in a single conducting FET is 11.1 W.** This number matters for transient junction rise within a commutation step (§4.4).
