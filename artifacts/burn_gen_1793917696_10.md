## 0. Architecture Review: Read Before the Math

Four issues shape every number below.

| # | Issue | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | **The H743 does not commutate motors.** ArduPilot outputs DShot only. The 48 kHz gate PWM, dead-time and back-EMF commutation run on four dedicated ESC MCUs (AT32F421 running AM32 is assumed). | The design has two digital domains: FC (H743/ArduPilot) and ESC (4× AT32F421/AM32). They share only DShot lines, GND and an optional telemetry UART. |
| R2 | **"80 A burst" on a nano board is a transient rating, not a thermal rating.** As derived in §2, each channel dissipates about 30 W at 80 A. Four channels dissipate about 120 W on roughly 6.5 cm² of PCB. | The rating must be specified as **80 A for ≤ 2 s, 30 s cool-down, 25 °C ambient, ≥ 10 m/s airflow**. A continuous rating of about 35 to 40 A/ch is realistic. |
| R3 | **Footprint.** 24 power FETs plus 4 drivers plus 4 MCUs do not fit with the H743 on a 20×20 board. | A 30.5×30.5 mm stack (FC board + ESC board) or a 25.5×25.5 mm single 8-layer board with ESC on the bottom side. This package assumes a **30.5×30.5 two-board stack**. ESC board: 8-layer, 2 oz copper on all layers. |
| R4 | **Bidirectional DShot on ArduPilot pairs channels.** On a 4-channel timer, ArduPilot shares input-capture DMA within channel pairs. | Each motor's eRPM refresh rate is about half the DShot loop rate. Verify the final allocation with ArduPilot's `dma_resolver.py` at build time. |

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Current paths in 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification

At any commutation step, one phase is PWM-switched, one is held low, and one floats. Over a PWM period with duty D:

- The PWM leg's high-side FET (HS_pwm) conducts I_ph for time D·T.
- The PWM leg's low-side FET (LS_pwm, synchronous rectifier) conducts I_ph for time (1−D)·T.
- The return leg's low-side FET (LS_ret) conducts I_ph for the full period T.

So **exactly two FETs carry I_ph at every instant**, independent of D. Per-channel conduction loss is:

$$
P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 \, R_{ds}(T_j)\,\big[D + (1-D) + 1\big] = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

Phase current relates to battery current by $I_{ph} = I_{batt}/D$. Near full throttle (D ≈ 0.95 to 1), I_ph ≈ I_batt.
