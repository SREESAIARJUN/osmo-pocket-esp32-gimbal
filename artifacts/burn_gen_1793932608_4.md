# STM32H743 AIO 4-in-1 ESC Nano FC (Job #17): Thermal, Switching, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Design Model

## 0. Architecture Clarifications

These points constrain the rest of the model.

1. **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz motor PWM.** In any ArduPilot-compatible design, the H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bi-directional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs (one per motor). Typical choices are the AT32F421 or STM32G071, running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Those MCUs generate the 48 kHz complementary PWM. ArduPilot has no firmware path for driving gate drivers directly.
2. **80 A on a nano (20×20 mm) footprint is a burst-only rating.** Section 3 shows it is thermally sustainable for roughly 1–3 s, not continuously. A realistic continuous rating is about 25–35 A per channel. Any datasheet claim should state "80 A burst (≤2 s, Tamb 25 °C, prop-wash airflow)."
3. **All component parameters below are assumptions.** They are representative of 40 V PQFN 3.3×3.3 MOSFETs and FD6288-class drivers. Substitute your actual BOM values into the equations before sign-off.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A burst)

### 1.1 Commutation topology
In 6-step trapezoidal (and approximately in FOC) operation, exactly one high-side and one low-side FET carry the phase current at any instant. With synchronous rectification enabled (AM32 "complementary PWM"), the freewheel current flows through the opposite FET's channel rather than its body diode. So the current path always contains two R_ds(on) in series, regardless of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = I^2 \cdot 2 R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)
$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.3 mΩ × 1.5 = **1.95 mΩ**

### 1.3 Numbers
| | 25 °C (cold start) | 125 °C (hot) |
|---|---|---|
| P_cond per ESC | 6400 × 2.6 mΩ = **16.6 W** | 6400 × 3.9 mΩ = **25.0 W** |
| Instantaneous per conducting FET | 8.3 W | 12.5 W |
| Average per FET (each conducts ⅓ electrical cycle) | 2.8 W | 4.2 W |

### 1.4 Parasitic copper loss (often ignored, but significant)
Assume 2 oz copper, 0.35 mΩ effective path resistance per channel, plus a 0.25 mΩ shunt:

$$P_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.6\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.8\ W/channel}$$

### 1.5 Thermal-runaway check
Because R_ds(on) rises with temperature, loss feeds back into temperature. Solve self-consistently:

$$T_j = T_b + P(T_j)\,R_{\theta JB}, \qquad P(T_j) = I_{FET,rms}^2 R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

$$T_j = \frac{T_b + I^2 R_{25}(1 - 25\alpha)\,R_{\theta JB}}{1 - I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\, R_{\theta JB}}$$

Per FET, I²_rms = 6400/3 ≈ 2133 A² and R_θJB = 7.5 K/W. The runaway denominator is:

1 − 2133 × 1.3e-3 × 0.005 × 7.5 = 1 − 0.104 = **0.896**

This is stable (runaway occurs as the denominator approaches 0). With T_b = 90 °C, T_j ≈ 128 °C, which is consistent with the assumed k_T.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Per channel, only one half-bridge is PWM-switching at a time.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap
$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction
$$P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dead} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 2.4 Output capacitance
$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.030\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive
This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET.

$$P_g = Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} \times 2 = 30\text{nC}\times10\times48\text{k}\times2 = \mathbf{0.029\ W}$$

### 2.6 Switching-frequency trade-off
Total switching-related loss is about 2.1 W/channel at 48 kHz and scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it falls to about 1.05 W, but current ripple doubles. The ripple is:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

For L_ph = 15 µH and D = 0.5, ΔI_pp = 8.75 A at 48 kHz. 48 kHz is a reasonable choice for low-inductance 5"–7" motors.
