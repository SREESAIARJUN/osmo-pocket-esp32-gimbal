# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal & Bi-Directional DShot Architecture

**Job #0, Revision A (Architecture Baseline)**

---

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Corrections

These points need resolving before the mathematics, because they change the design.

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production AIO design, the H743 runs ArduPilot and emits DShot. Each ESC channel uses its own small MCU, either one per motor or one per pair. Typical parts are the AT32F421, STM32G071 or GD32E230, running AM32 or BLHeli_32. This document models that topology. Direct H743 FOC of four motors is possible in principle, but ArduPilot does not support it.
2. **80 A per motor on a "nano" (20×20 mm mount, ~25×25 mm board) is beyond its steady-state thermal limit.** The math below shows 80 A is a **burst rating of roughly 1–3 s**, bounded by board thermal mass rather than by the MOSFET datasheet. I treat "80 A burst" as the design point and derive the burst duration.
3. **Component parameters are representative assumptions.** I use a 40 V N-MOSFET in DFN3.3×3.3 with R_DS(on) ≈ 1.0 mΩ at 25 °C and V_GS = 10 V. Substitute your selected part's datasheet values into the equations. All equations are parametric.
4. **Bus voltage:** 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V maximum, which sets 40 V FETs as the minimum. 8S would require 60 V FETs with roughly 2× R_DS(on).

## 1. Power Stage Topology

Each motor channel uses:
- A three-phase bridge with 6 MOSFETs: high-side Q_H{A,B,C} and low-side Q_L{A,B,C}.
- Trapezoidal 6-step commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM at f_sw = 48 kHz.
- In any commutation sector, one high-side FET is PWM'd with its complementary low-side FET (synchronous rectification), and one opposite low-side FET is held ON.

**The current path is therefore always two series FET conductions**, either HS + LS(static) or LS(sync) + LS(static).

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ /^\circ\text{C}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.0 mΩ × 1.45 = **1.45 mΩ**.

### 2.2 Per-channel conduction loss

Because two FET resistances are always in series with the phase current I_ph:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j)$$

With trapezoidal drive and low ripple (high-inductance-ratio assumption), I_ph,rms ≈ I_DC,phase:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.45\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{18.6\ W}$$

### 2.3 Per-FET distribution

- Each phase leg conducts 2/3 of the electrical cycle, split between its HS and LS.
- The HS conducts for D (the PWM duty); its sync LS conducts for (1−D).

Worst-case HS loss occurs at D → 1 (full throttle, burst):

$$P_{Q,HS} \approx I^2 R_{DS}\cdot\tfrac{1}{3}\cdot D \;+\; (\text{static-LS share on the other sectors})$$

Instantaneous dissipation in a conducting FET is 80² × 1.45 mΩ = **9.3 W**. The cycle-averaged value is ≈ 18.6 W / 6 ≈ **3.1 W per FET**. At 80 A the commutation frequency is in the kHz range, far shorter than the die thermal time constant (~ms), so the average dominates for die temperature.

### 2.4 PCB copper loss (often ignored, but significant here)

The phase and bus path is about 15 mm of 2 oz copper on multiple layers.

- Sheet resistance of 2 oz Cu ≈ 0.25 mΩ/□.
- Assume 4 paralleled layers and an effective 3 squares.

$$R_{Cu} \approx \frac{0.25 \times 3}{4} = 0.19\ \text{m}\Omega \;\Rightarrow\; P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 0.19\text{m} \approx \mathbf{2.4\ W}$$

The factor 2 accounts for the in and out paths.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per channel, only the PWM'd HS/LS pair switches. Assumptions:
- V = 25.2 V, I = 80 A, f = 48 kHz.
- t_r + t_f = 20 ns, set by the gate driver at ~1 A source/sink and Q_gd ≈ 8 nC.

| Mechanism | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap (hard-switched HS) | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 20 ns × 48 k = **0.97 W** |
| Output capacitance (Q_oss ≈ 40 nC) | ½ · Q_oss · V · f × 2 FETs | **0.05 W** |
| Body-diode reverse recovery (Q_rr ≈ 50 nC) | Q_rr · V · f | **0.06 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction (t_dt = 100 ns, V_f = 0.8 V) | V_f · I · 2t_dt · f | 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (Q_g = 30 nC, V_GS = 10 V) | Q_g · V_GS · f × 2 | **0.03 W** (dissipated in driver/R_g) |
| **Total switching, per channel** | | **≈ 1.72 W** |

**Design insight:** at 48 kHz, dead-time loss is about 60% of the overlap loss. Reducing t_dt from 100 ns to 50 ns (AM32 allows tuning) saves about 0.3 W per channel. Do not go below the driver's propagation skew plus margin, roughly 40 ns for the FD6288Q class.

### 3.1 Total loss budget at 80 A burst

| Component | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (T_j = 125 °C) | 18.6 W | 74.4 W |
| PCB copper | 2.4 W | 9.6 W |
| Switching | 1.7 W | 6.9 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, if low-side current sense) | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| **Total** | **24.3 W** | **≈ 97 W** |

For comparison, the delivered power is 4 × 80 A × ~24 V ≈ 7.7 kW, so efficiency is about 98.7%. This ratio is excellent, but 97 W into a 25 mm board is the problem.
