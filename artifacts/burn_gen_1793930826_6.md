## 0. Architecture Corrections

Two scope issues need to be settled before the math, because they change the results.

1. **The STM32H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC MCU.** A production-grade 4-in-1 ESC uses four dedicated ESC MCUs, typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Each one handles commutation, back-EMF/zero-cross detection, and 48 kHz PWM. The H743 runs ArduPilot and drives the ESCs over bidirectional DShot600. Running sensorless commutation for four motors on the H743 alongside the ArduPilot scheduler is not supported by ArduPilot and is not recommended. The rest of this document assumes the split architecture.

2. **"80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is thermally limited to roughly 1 second.** It is not a sustained capability. Section 2.5 derives this. The realistic continuous rating is about 15–20 A per motor. If marketing needs "80 A," it must be specified as **80 A burst, ≤1 s, at a starting board temperature ≤50 °C**.

## 2. Thermal Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Conduction topology

Assume trapezoidal six-step commutation with synchronous (complementary) PWM. During each 60° sector:

- **Phase A high-side (Q_AH)** is PWM'd with duty D.
- **Phase A low-side (Q_AL)** conducts synchronously for (1 − D).
- **Phase B low-side (Q_BL)** is on continuously.

### 2.2 Conduction loss per ESC

Hot on-resistance:

$$R_{hot} = k_T \cdot R_{25} = 1.5 \times 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega = 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega$$

Loss in each conducting device:

$$P_{AH} = I^2 R_{hot} D,\quad P_{AL} = I^2 R_{hot}(1-D),\quad P_{BL} = I^2 R_{hot}$$

Summing the three:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2 R_{hot} \quad (\text{independent of } D)$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ ESC}$$

**Peak single-device dissipation.** Q_BL carries the full current for the whole sector:

$$P_{BL} = 80^2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 9.6\ \text{W}$$

Commutation rotates this load across all six FETs, so the time-averaged loss per FET is P_cond / 6 ≈ 3.2 W. At low eRPM, however, the instantaneous 9.6 W sets the junction transient.

**Ripple correction.** With phase inductance around 10 µH and f_sw = 48 kHz, ripple is ΔI ≈ 10–15 A peak-to-peak. RMS current rises by a factor of √(1 + (ΔI/I)²/12). That is about 0.1 % here, so it is negligible.

### 2.3 Shunt and copper losses

Shunt loss is shared across the board, with all four channels summing on the battery return:

$$P_{sh} = (4 \times 80)^2 \times 0.2\,\text{m}\Omega \times \bar{D}$$

At full throttle ($\bar{D} \approx 1$, worst case) this is about **20 W**. That is not acceptable. Use **one shunt per ESC phase return**, or place a single shunt in the battery path rated as follows:

- Per-channel shunt: 80² × 0.2 mΩ = **1.28 W**.

Copper trace loss is estimated with 2 oz inner layers × 4 paralleled, about 0.15 mΩ effective per phase path:

$$P_{Cu} \approx 80^2 \times 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega \approx \mathbf{1.9\ W}\ \text{per ESC (in and out)}$$

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

These losses are counted per ESC. One FET hard-switches per PWM period; the complementary FET soft-switches through dead time.

### 3.1 Hard-switching overlap loss

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 3.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 3.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 3.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 30\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.018\ W}$$

### 3.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated in the driver and R_G, not the FET. Two FETs switch per period:

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 3.6 Switching subtotal

$$P_{sw,total} \approx 1.94 + 0.61 + 0.05 + 0.02 + 0.05 = \mathbf{2.67\ W\ per\ ESC}$$

**Sensitivity to switching frequency.** P_sw,total scales linearly with f_sw.

| f_sw | P_sw,total per ESC |
|---|---|
| 24 kHz | 1.34 W |
| 48 kHz | 2.67 W |
| 96 kHz | 5.3 W |

At 80 A, 48 kHz is acceptable because conduction loss dominates about 7:1.
