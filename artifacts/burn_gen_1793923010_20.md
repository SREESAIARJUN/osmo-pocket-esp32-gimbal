# STM32H743 4-in-1 AIO Nano FC (Job #8): Systems Architecture Model

## 0. Architectural Ground Rules

These decisions shape every calculation that follows:

1. **The STM32H743 is not the ESC controller.** A 4-in-1 ESC needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, such as an AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071. That MCU handles BEMF zero-cross detection and 48 kHz complementary PWM. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to it over bidirectional DShot600. Merging the two roles is not supported by ArduPilot.
2. **"80 A burst" is interpreted as per channel**, which is how the industry rates it. That means up to 320 A aggregate. On a 20×20 mm board this is only achievable as a short transient. Section 3 quantifies how short.
3. **Component parameters are representative placeholders.** Every value marked ⚠ must be replaced with the datasheet value for the parts you actually procure, and all pin and alternate-function assignments must be checked against the H743 datasheet (DS12110) AF tables before layout.

**System baseline**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max (22.2 V nominal) |
| PWM frequency | f_sw = 48 kHz, center-aligned, complementary (synchronous rectification) |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal; at any instant two phase legs carry the full motor current I |
| MOSFETs | 24 total (6 per channel), 40 V, DFN 3.3×3.3 ⚠ |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

With R_DS,25 = 1.5 mΩ ⚠ at V_GS = 10 V and T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{DS}(125) = 1.5 \times 1.45 = 2.18\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Per-channel conduction loss

With synchronous rectification, the PWM'd leg always has either its high-side or its low-side FET conducting. The current therefore always flows through exactly two FET channels:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Phase-current ripple adds a small term:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12},\qquad \Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5″-class motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH line-to-line, D = 0.5, V_bus − V_bemf ≈ 6 V), ΔI_pp ≈ 4.2 A. This contributes under 0.03 % and is negligible at 80 A.

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.00218 = \mathbf{27.9\ W}$$

### 1.3 Self-consistent junction temperature

R_DS depends on T_j, and T_j depends on loss, so solve iteratively:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_{ref} + P(T_j^{(n)})\cdot Z_{th}(t)$$

For bursts, use the transient Z_th from Section 3, not the steady-state R_th.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the PWM'd leg switches; the other active leg is static. Per channel, per switching event:

### 2.1 V–I overlap

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_g},\qquad I_g = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}{R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}}$$

Assumptions: Q_gs2 + Q_gd = 12 nC ⚠, V_drv = 10 V, V_plateau = 3.5 V, R_g,total = 6.5 Ω. This gives I_g ≈ 1.0 A, so t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 12 ns.

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(24\text{ ns})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.16\ W}$$

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{ ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$
