# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: System Model

## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Assumptions

Two points shape the rest of this document.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production AIO design (and in ArduPilot), the STM32H743 is the flight controller. Each ESC channel has its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, such as an AT32F421, STM32G071 or GD32E230. That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM and closes the commutation loop. The H743 talks to the ESC MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. Running 4 × 6-step commutation plus ArduPilot on one H743 is not supported and is not modelled here.

**2. "80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 or 25×25 mm) board is a marketing number.** The model below shows it is only survivable for a few seconds. I treat **80 A per motor channel** as the worst case and derive the allowable burst duration.

**Assumed parameters.** These are representative, not tied to a specific datasheet. Substitute your actual BOM values.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S, full) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Phase current (burst) | I | 80 A |
| MOSFET R_DS(on) @ 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | R₂₅ | 0.80 mΩ |
| R_DS(on) temperature multiplier @ ~100 °C | k_T | 1.40 |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM. In each of the 6 sectors, two phases conduct:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for duty D. The low-side FET of the same leg conducts for 1 − D (synchronous rectification).
- **Static leg:** the low-side FET is continuously on.

The motor current therefore always passes through exactly **two R_DS(on) in series**, whatever the duty cycle.

### 1.2 Equations

Hot on-resistance:

$$R_{hot} = k_T \cdot R_{25} = 1.40 \times 0.80\,\text{m}\Omega = 1.12\,\text{m}\Omega$$

Conduction loss per channel:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{hot} = 80^2 \times 2.24\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{14.3\ W}$$

For sinusoidal/FOC drive, replace I² with I_rms² summed over three legs. The result is similar in magnitude.

### 1.3 Temperature feedback

R_DS(on) rises with junction temperature, so conduction loss and temperature have to be solved together:

$$R(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ /\text{K}$$

$$T_j = T_a + P(T_j)\cdot Z_{th}(t)$$

Iterate this pair until it converges. If α · P · Z_th approaches 1, the system has no stable solution and you have **thermal runaway**.

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

Each leg is active for 4 of the 6 sectors. Averaged over an electrical cycle, the 6 FETs share the channel loss roughly equally: ≈ 2.4 W each from conduction. The instantaneous dissipation of a conducting FET is higher: I²R_hot ≈ 7.2 W.

### 1.5 PCB copper losses

These are often larger than the FET losses and are frequently ignored.

- Battery bus burst current = 4 × 80 A = **320 A**.
- A 2 oz (70 µm) copper pour, 15 mm wide and 20 mm long:

$$R = \frac{\rho L}{wt} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8} \times 0.02}{0.015 \times 70\times10^{-6}} \approx 0.33\,\text{m}\Omega$$

$$P = 320^2 \times 0.33\,\text{m}\Omega \approx \mathbf{34\ W}$$

This is distributed over the pour, and the current splits progressively along it, so the real figure is lower. It still dominates unless the board uses 3–4 oz copper or soldered bus bars. **This is the realistic limiter of the 80 A claim.**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Hard switching occurs only in the PWM leg: one turn-on and one turn-off per period.

| Loss term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V·I overlap | ½ · V_bus · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 30 ns × 48 kHz | 1.45 W |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss · V_bus · f_sw = 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.05 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw | 0.05 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f · I · 2t_dt · f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 100 ns × 48 k | 0.31 W |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2 · Q_g · V_GS · f_sw | 0.05 W (dissipated in the driver/gate resistors) |
