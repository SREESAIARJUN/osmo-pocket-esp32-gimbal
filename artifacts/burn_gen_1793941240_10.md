# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Engineering Model

This document covers the loss model, thermal limits, bidirectional DShot timer and DMA allocation, and a reference netlist for the design.

## 0. Architecture Corrections

Three points in the request need correcting before the numbers mean anything.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** On every production 4-in-1 design, each ESC channel (or a pair of channels) has its own MCU, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 commands to the ESC MCUs. The model below follows this split.
2. **80 A on all four channels at once does not survive on a nano board.** In a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm format, that load cannot be sustained. Section 3 shows the burst is time-limited to a few seconds. Treat "80 A burst" as a per-channel peak with a duration limit, not a continuous operating point.
3. **The device values are placeholders.** They represent a 40 V, ~1 mΩ PDFN3.3 class MOSFET. Replace every parameter with the datasheet values of the parts you actually select, and validate on a thermal rig before release.

### Operating Assumptions

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 25.2 V | 6S battery, fully charged |
| I | 80 A | Phase current during burst |
| f_sw | 48 kHz | ESC PWM frequency |
| R_ds(on),25 | 1.0 mΩ | MOSFET on-resistance at 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V |
| α | 0.006 /K | Temperature coefficient of R_ds(on) |
| t_r, t_f | 20 ns each | Rise and fall times with the gate driver used |
| t_dead | 50 ns | Dead time |
| Q_rr | 40 nC | Body-diode reverse-recovery charge |
| Q_g | 50 nC | Gate charge |
| V_f | 0.8 V | Body-diode forward voltage |
| C_oss,eff | 1 nF | Effective output capacitance |
| R_θJC | 1.5 K/W | Junction-to-case thermal resistance, per FET |
| R_pcb | 0.5 mΩ | Copper path resistance, per channel |

The model also assumes 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM). Under that scheme, current always flows through one high-side path and one low-side path, regardless of duty cycle.

## 1. Conduction Loss

### Temperature-dependent on-resistance

R_ds(on)(T_j) = R_25 · [1 + α (T_j − 25)]

At T_j = 125 °C, R_ds(on) = 1.0 × 1.6 = **1.6 mΩ**.

### Loss per channel

With synchronous rectification, there are two devices in the conduction path at all times:

P_cond,ch = 2 · I² · R_ds(on)(T_j) + I² · R_pcb

| Term | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Per conducting FET | 6400 × 1.6 mΩ | 10.24 W |
| Two FETs in the path | 2 × 10.24 W | 20.48 W |
| PCB copper | 6400 × 0.5 mΩ | 3.20 W |
| **Conduction total, per channel** | | **≈ 23.7 W** |

### Thermal runaway check

Conduction loss rises with temperature, and temperature rises with loss. The loop is stable only if:

dP/dT_j · R_θ,j-amb < 1, where dP/dT_j = I² · R_25 · α

At 80 A, dP/dT_j = 6400 × 1e-3 × 0.006 = **0.0384 W/K per FET**.

That gives a maximum allowable R_θ,j-amb of about 26 K/W. The steady-state path is well below that, so the loop is stable. The real constraint is the heat-capacity limit, covered in Section 3.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz (per channel)

Only one FET is hard-switched per PWM edge pair in 6-step commutation.

| Mechanism | Formula | Result |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 kHz | 1.935 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw = 40 nC × 25.2 × 48k | 0.048 W |
| Output capacitance | 2 × ½ · C_oss · V² · f_sw | 0.030 W |
| Dead-time body diode | V_f · I · 2 · t_dead · f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 100 ns × 48k | 0.307 W |
| Gate drive (6 FETs) | 6 · Q_g · V_GS · f_sw = 6 × 50 nC × 10 × 48k | 0.144 W (dissipated mostly in the driver) |
| **Switching total** | | **≈ 2.46 W** |

### Totals

- **Per channel:** P_ch = 23.7 + 2.46 ≈ **26.2 W**
- **Board, all four channels at 80 A:** P_tot ≈ **105 W**

Note that conduction loss dominates by roughly 10:1. Raising f_sw from 24 kHz to 48 kHz costs only about 1.2 W per channel, while the gain is smoother low-throttle operation and lower acoustic noise.

### Capacitor ripple

Input capacitor RMS ripple current is:

I_C,rms ≈ I · √(D(1 − D))

This peaks at D = 0.5, giving **40 A RMS per channel**. That load requires all of the following:

- MLCC banks of at least 4 × 10 µF 50 V X7R per channel, placed close to the half-bridges.
- A bulk low-ESR electrolytic of at least 1000 µF / 35 V at the battery pads.
- A TVS diode, for example SMBJ33A, sized to clamp regenerative braking spikes.

## 3. Thermal Model

### Junction-to-case rise

Each conducting FET dissipates about 10.24 W of conduction loss plus about 1.2 W of shared switching loss, roughly **11.5 W**.

ΔT_jc = 11.5 × 1.5 ≈ **17 K**

Add about 8 K for spreading through the 6-layer, 2 oz board under the FET pad. Total junction-above-board rise is **≈ 25 K**.

### Board as a lumped thermal mass

C_th · dT_b/dt = P_tot − (T_b − T_amb) / R_θ,b-a

**Heat capacity.** The board plus FETs contains about 6 g of copper (0.385 J/g·K) and about 3 g of FR4 (1.1 J/g·K):

C_th ≈ 2.3 + 3.3 ≈ **6 J/K**

**Convection to air.** With propwash, h ≈ 100 W/m²K over A ≈ 2 × (0.03 m)²:

R_θ,b-a ≈ 1 / (100 × 0.0018) ≈ **5.6 K/W**

**Time constant.** τ = R · C ≈ **33 s**

**Steady state** at 105 W would be T_b − T_amb ≈ 590 K. The 4 × 80 A case is therefore transient-only by definition.

### Burst time limit

Solving the lumped equation for the time to reach a given board temperature:

t_burst = −τ · ln[1 − (ΔT_b,allow) / (P_tot · R_θ)]

Inputs:

- T_j,max = 150 °C, derated to 140 °C.
- T_amb = 40 °C.
- Pre-burst board temperature at hover: 60 °C.
- Junction margin of 25 K over the board, so the board limit is 115 °C.
- Allowed board rise: ΔT_b,allow = 55 K from the 60 °C starting point.

Because the burst is short compared with τ, the rise is close to linear:

t ≈ C · ΔT / P = 6 × 55 / 105 ≈ **3.1 s**

### Design actions

- **Firmware current limit.** Set the AM32 current limit to around 45 to 50 A per channel. Leave burst headroom to the protection logic.
- **Thermal sensing.** Place an NTC under the FET cluster and feed it to the ESC MCU's temperature cutback.
- **Heat-capacity improvements.** A heatsink plate adding 5 g of aluminium (about +4.5 J/K) extends the burst time by roughly 75%.
- **Verify the model.** Measure with an IR camera and thermocouples, then refit C_th and R_θ to real data.
