# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Model, DShot Allocation, and Netlists

## 0. Corrections and Assumptions

I don't have prior context for "Job #74", so every assumption is stated and parameterized. Replace the representative values with your actual BOM datasheet values.

Three points change the design before any math is done:

1. **The H743 cannot commutate the motors under ArduPilot.** ArduPilot only outputs DShot commands. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. AM32 natively supports 24/48 kHz PWM and EDT/bidirectional telemetry. The architecture is therefore:
   - STM32H743 as the flight controller.
   - 4 × ESC MCUs, for example AT32F421 or STM32G071.
   - 4 × three-phase gate drivers and 24 MOSFETs.
2. **"Nano" size and 80 A burst conflict.** A 20×20 mm board cannot carry 4 × 80 A thermally. The model below assumes a 30.5×30.5 mm power board, possibly stacked under a 20×20 FC. Even at that size, 80 A is a **seconds-scale transient rating**, not a continuous one (see §1.6).
3. **Battery: 6S**, V_bus,max = 25.2 V, nominal 22.2 V. MOSFETs should be rated 40 V.

## 1. Thermal and Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 1.1 Conduction topology

The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. AM32 defaults to this mode.

During any 60° sector, two legs carry current:
- **The PWM leg**: the high-side FET conducts for duty D, and its low-side FET synchronously rectifies for (1 − D).
- **The return leg**: the low-side FET conducts continuously.

The current therefore always flows through **exactly two R_ds(on)**, independent of D:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Per-FET distribution, averaged over the electrical cycle (each FET is active in 1/3 of sectors in each role):

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{2 I^2 R_{ds}}{6}\cdot\text{(role weighting)} \;\Rightarrow\; \text{peak instantaneous worst FET} = I^2 R_{ds}$$

**Copper conduction.** Copper resistance must be added. For 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner layers, the phase plus bus path is roughly 0.3–0.5 mΩ per channel:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu}$$

### 1.2 Switching losses at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Each term is:

| Loss | Equation |
|---|---|
| V–I overlap | $P_{ov} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}$ |
| Reverse recovery | $P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw}$ |
| Output capacitance | $P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw}$ |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | $P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2t_{dt})\, f_{sw}$ |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver/R_g, not the FET) | $P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw}$ |

The overlap time can be checked from the gate charge: $t_{sw}\approx Q_{gd}/I_{g}$. With roughly 0.6 A of driver current, 12 nC / 0.6 A ≈ 20 ns, which is consistent with the assumed rise/fall times.

### 1.3 Worked numbers: 80 A, V_bus = 25.2 V, T_j = 125 °C

At 125 °C, R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.006 × 100) = **1.6 mΩ**.

| Term | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| P_cond (FETs) | 2 × 80² × 1.6 m | **20.5 W** |
| P_Cu (0.4 mΩ) | 80² × 0.4 m | 2.6 W |
| P_ov | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40 n × 48 k | 1.94 W |
| P_dt | 0.8 × 80 × 400 n × 48 k | 1.23 W |
| P_rr | 50 n × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.06 W |
| P_oss | 0.5 × 60 n × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.04 W |
| **Total per channel** | | **≈ 26.4 W** |
| Gate drive (driver IC) | 2 × 50 n × 10 × 48 k | 0.05 W |

**Four channels at simultaneous 80 A: ≈ 106 W.**

Conclusion: **conduction dominates at about 78% of total loss.** Moving from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves only about 1.6 W per channel. The 48 kHz choice is justified for motor current ripple and acoustics; the thermal fight is entirely about R_ds(on) and copper.

### 1.4 Temperature-dependent closed form

Because R_ds rises with temperature, loss and temperature are coupled. Steady state requires:

$$T_j = T_a + R_{\theta,tot}\left[2I^2R_{ds,25}(1+\alpha(T_j-25)) + P_{sw}\right]$$

Solving for T_j:

$$T_j=\frac{T_a + R_{\theta}\,[2I^2R_{ds,25}(1-25\alpha)+P_{sw}]}{1-2\alpha I^2 R_{ds,25} R_{\theta}}$$

**Thermal runaway** occurs when the denominator reaches zero:

$$2\alpha I^2 R_{ds,25}R_\theta \ge 1$$

At 80 A the runaway threshold is $R_\theta \ge 1/(2\cdot0.006\cdot6400\cdot0.001) = \mathbf{13\ K/W}$. The board-to-air path is much worse than that (§1.6), so **80 A has no steady state. It is purely a transient rating.**

### 1.5 Junction-to-board gradient (worst-case FET)

The return-leg low-side FET carries the full 80 A for a 120° block. Over sub-second time scales, its R_θJC + R_θCB matters:

$$\Delta T_{j-b} = I^2 R_{ds}\,(R_{\theta JC}+R_{\theta CB}) = 80^2\times1.6\text{m}\times(1.0+2.5) \approx \mathbf{36\ K}$$

R_θCB ≈ 2.5 K/W assumes a via-stitched pad with 12+ thermal vias of 0.3 mm diameter into internal 2 oz planes.
