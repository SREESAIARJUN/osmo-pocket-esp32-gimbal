## 0. Architecture Corrections

**0.1 The STM32H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC MCU.**
A 4-in-1 ESC runs 4 independent sensorless BLDC controllers. Each one does 6-step commutation with BEMF zero-cross detection at up to ~48 kHz PWM. That work belongs on 4 dedicated ESC MCUs running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, such as the AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to the 4 ESC MCUs over bidirectional DShot600.

Running 12 half-bridges, 4 BEMF comparator sets, and ArduPilot on one H743 is not viable:
- There are not enough advanced-timer complementary outputs with dead-time.
- ArduPilot has no ESC firmware.

**0.2 "80 A burst" on a nano form factor (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) is thermally marginal.**
§3 shows that 4×80 A at the same time is roughly a 2–4 second event, set by the board's heat capacity. It is not sustainable. Treat 80 A as a per-channel transient rating. All-motor sustained capability will be roughly 25–35 A per channel, depending on airflow.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Topology

In 6-step synchronous-rectified commutation, one phase is PWM'd (HS for duty D, LS for 1−D). A second phase has its LS held on, and the third phase floats. The motor current I therefore flows through:

- one switching leg: HS during D, LS during 1−D, so effectively one R_DS(on) the whole time, plus dead-time diode intervals;
- one static LS FET, conducting continuously.

$$P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At 80 A, with ripple neglected (I_rms ≈ I_dc for motor inductance >> ripple):

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond per channel |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.20 mΩ | 2·6400·0.0012 = **15.4 W** |
| 100 °C | 1.74 mΩ | **22.3 W** |
| 125 °C | 1.92 mΩ | **24.6 W** |

**Electro-thermal feedback.** Because R_DS(on) rises with T_j, the loss and temperature are coupled:

$$T_j = T_a + R_{\theta}\cdot P(T_j) \;\Rightarrow\; T_j = \frac{T_a + R_\theta P_0(1-25\alpha)}{1 - R_\theta P_0 \alpha}$$

where P_0 = 2I²R₂₅. This has a stable solution only if **R_θ·P_0·α < 1**. Otherwise you get thermal runaway. At 80 A, P_0 = 15.4 W, so runaway sets in at R_θ > 10.8 K/W (effective, channel to ambient). That is a hard steady-state ceiling, and a nano board in still air exceeds it.

**PCB copper loss (often ignored, but significant):**

$$R_{trace} = \frac{\rho\, l}{w\,t},\quad \rho_{Cu}(25\,°C)=1.72\times10^{-8}\,\Omega\text{m}$$

Example: a 2 oz (70 µm) plane, 8 mm wide, 12 mm long gives 0.37 mΩ.
- Doubled on two layers: 0.18 mΩ.
- Allow for battery pad, phase pad, and return paths: ≈ 0.5 mΩ total per channel.
- Loss: 80²·0.5 m = **3.2 W per channel**.
- Shared battery input copper at 4×80 = 320 A through 0.1 mΩ = **10 W**.

This is why multiple stitched inner layers on the bus are mandatory.

### 2.2 Switching Losses at 48 kHz (only the PWM'd leg switches)

**(a) V–I overlap (hard switching, inductive load).**

Rise and fall times are set by the Miller charge and gate current:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,src}} = \frac{8\text{ nC}}{1.5\text{ A}}\approx 5.3\text{ ns}$$

$$t_f \approx \frac{8\text{ nC}}{1.8\text{ A}} \approx 4.4\text{ ns}$$

In practice, gate resistors (needed for EMI and ringing control) push these to ~12 ns each. Using that design value:

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot24\text{ ns}\cdot48\text{ k} = \mathbf{1.16\ W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery of the LS body diode** (it conducts during dead time before the HS turns on):

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

The HS also sees additional turn-on current I_rr. A practical approximation is to add +20 % to the turn-on part of P_VI, giving about +0.12 W.

**(c) Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \cdot 2 = 40\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (2 edges per period):

$$P_{dt} = V_f\,I\,(2t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.85\cdot80\cdot200\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.65\ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive** (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET):

$$P_g = Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw}\cdot 2 = 45\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k}\cdot2 = \mathbf{0.043\ W}$$
