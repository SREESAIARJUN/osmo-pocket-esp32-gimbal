# STM32H743 AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC): Power-Stage, Thermal, and Bidirectional DShot Reference Design

I don't have the Job #1 context or a Job #2 specification, so this design rests on the explicit assumptions below. Each equation is parameterized so you can substitute your actual BOM values.

> **Feasibility warning.** On a nano-class (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board, four simultaneous 80 A channels mean about **80 W of heat** into a few joules-per-kelvin of copper and FR-4. The math below shows this is a *seconds-scale* rating, not a continuous one. Define "80 A burst" contractually with a duration, duty cycle, starting temperature, and airflow. Otherwise the rating is meaningless.

---

## 0. System Architecture and Assumptions

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Flight controller MCU | STM32H743 at 480 MHz, timer kernel clock 240 MHz (APB ×2) |
| ESC MCUs | 4× AM32-class (AT32F421 / STM32G071). The H743 does **not** commutate the motors; it sends DShot600. |
| Bus | 6S LiPo: 22.2 V nominal, **25.2 V max**, 30 V transient design limit |
| MOSFET (representative 40 V, 5×6 DFN) | R_ds(on),25 = 1.0 mΩ; α = 0.0045 K⁻¹; Q_g = 60 nC; Q_gd = 10 nC; Q_rr = 50 nC; C_oss = 1.5 nF; R_θJC = 1.0 K/W; V_SD = 0.8 V |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal; high-side PWM with synchronous low side (AM32 complementary mode); f_sw = 48 kHz |
| Dead time | 50 ns per edge |
| Burst definition | 80 A per motor, all four motors simultaneously (worst case) |

> Nano-size boards usually use 3.3×3.3 mm FETs with 2–3 mΩ. If that is your case, **double or triple every conduction-loss number below**.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance
$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0045 × 85) = **1.38 mΩ**

### 1.2 Per-FET current-conduction fractions (6-step, sync PWM)

Each leg is "high" for 120°, "low" for 120°, and floating for 60°. Let D be the PWM duty.

| FET | Conduction weight over one electrical cycle |
|---|---|
| High side (HS) | D/3 |
| Low side (LS), complementary in own "high" interval | (1−D)/3 |
| Low side (LS), fully on in own "low" interval | 1/3 |

Per leg:
$$P_{leg} = I^2R\left[\tfrac{D}{3}+\tfrac{1-D}{3}+\tfrac{1}{3}\right]=\tfrac{2}{3}I^2R$$

Per ESC channel (3 legs). Two FETs are always in the current path:
$$\boxed{P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)}$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A
- P_cond,ch = 2 × 6400 × 1.38 mΩ = **17.7 W per motor channel**
- Worst single FET (LS at low D): (2/3) I²R → up to **5.9 W**
- Hottest FET at burst (D → 0.95): LS ≈ 0.35 I²R ≈ 3.1 W; HS ≈ 0.32 I²R + switching ≈ 2.8 + 2.4 ≈ **5.2 W**

### 1.4 Additional I²R paths (often forgotten)

| Element | Value | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Current shunt (per channel) | 0.2 mΩ | 1.28 W |
| PCB phase/bus copper (per channel, 2 oz, short paths) | ~0.3 mΩ | 1.9 W |
| Battery pad → bus (shared, 320 A) | 0.1 mΩ | 10.2 W |

The shared battery entry is critical. Use multilayer stitched bus planes, and solder pads sized for 10–12 AWG.
