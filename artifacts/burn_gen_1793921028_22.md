**Three architectural flags you should resolve before layout:**

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** It sends DShot600 to four ESC MCUs. The 48 kHz switching frequency is an ESC firmware setting, not an H743 timer. If Job #19 really intends the H743 to run FOC/six-step directly, that is a different architecture. It would need 12 complementary PWM outputs plus BEMF/current ADC and would conflict with the DShot allocation below.
2. **4 × 80 A = 320 A of input current** is not credible through a nano board's battery pads and an XT60. Treat 80 A as a **single-channel, seconds-scale** rating. Section 2 quantifies this.
3. **Steady-state 80 A on all four channels is thermally impossible** in this form factor (≈ 100 W into a ~6 g board). The rating is only defensible as a time-limited burst, derived in Section 3.

## 1. Loss Model per Channel

### 1.1 Commutation Model (Six-Step Trapezoidal, Complementary PWM)

In each 60° sector, one phase is PWM'd (HS on for D, synchronous LS on for 1−D), one phase is held low (LS on 100%), and one phase floats (BEMF sense). Over a full electrical cycle, each leg:

- PWMs for 1/3 of the time,
- returns current (LS solid on) for 1/3,
- floats for 1/3.

Average conduction duty per device:

$$
\delta_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}D,\qquad \delta_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D) + \tfrac{1}{3}
$$

At any instant, phase current flows through exactly **two FETs in series**.

### 1.2 Conduction Loss

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]
$$

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) \quad \text{(independent of } D \text{ with synchronous rectification)}
$$

Worked values at I = 80 A, with T_j iterated to a ~115 °C fixed point, so R_ds = 1.2 × 1.45 = **1.74 mΩ**:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.74\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{22.3\ W}
$$

Worst single FET (low side at full throttle, δ = 1/3 + small):

$$
P_{cond,FET} \approx 6400 \times 1.74\text{ m}\Omega \times \tfrac{1}{3} \approx \mathbf{3.7\ W}
$$

The fixed point must be iterated, because P depends on T_j and T_j depends on P:

$$
T_j^{(k+1)} = T_{ref} + Z_{th}\,P\!\left(R_{ds}(T_j^{(k)})\right)
$$

This converges if $Z_{th}\cdot\partial P/\partial T_j < 1$. Here $\partial P/\partial T \approx 2I^2R_{25}\alpha = 0.077$ W/K, which is stable for any Z_th < 13 K/W. A board-level runaway risk exists only near steady state.

### 1.3 Switching Loss at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg hard-switches. The HS turns on into the LS body diode.

| Term | Formula | Value @ 25.2 V, 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | ½·25.2·80·30 ns·48 k = **1.45 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 40 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.05 W** |
| C_oss (2 FETs) | 2·½·C_oss·V²·f_sw | **0.05 W** |
| Dead-time diode | V_f·I·2·t_dt·f_sw | 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (mostly in driver) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw | **0.06 W** |
| **Total** | | **≈ 2.2 W / channel** |

**Design levers:**

- **Dead-time diode loss** is your largest controllable term after overlap. Cutting dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel.
- **Overlap loss** scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it is about 0.73 W; at 96 kHz it is about 2.9 W.
- The cost of lowering f_sw is higher current ripple and audible noise on low-inductance motors.
