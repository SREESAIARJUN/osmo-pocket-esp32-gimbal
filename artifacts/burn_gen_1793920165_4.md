There are four findings up front:

1. **80 A per channel is not a continuous rating on 20×20 mm.** Section 3 shows that 4×80 A is thermally limited to about **1.3–1.8 s** from a 45 °C board. Continuous capability is about **35–40 A per channel**. Market "80 A" labels on this size class are transient figures.
2. **The H743 must be thermally decoupled from the power stage.** At 480 MHz (VOS0), the H743 is specified only up to **T<sub>J</sub> = 105 °C**. Use a copper moat and a separate ground pour joined at a single point. Otherwise derate to 400 MHz (VOS1, T<sub>J</sub> = 125 °C).
3. **Bus capacitance is the hidden killer.** Worst-case ripple is 40 A<sub>RMS</sub> per channel (Section 2.6). An external 1000 µF / 35 V low-ESR cap at the pads is mandatory.
4. **BDShot is a timer-plus-DMA problem, not a CPU problem.** One advanced timer with 4 channels and one UP stream is sufficient. Input-capture DMA streams are the scarce resource (Section 4).

## 1. Electrical Operating Model (6-Step, Complementary PWM)

During each 60° commutation sector, one phase is PWM'd (HS with duty D, LS complementary with 1−D), one phase has its LS held on, and one phase floats (BEMF sense).

The current path at any instant is therefore exactly **two conducting FETs in series**:

- During D: HS<sub>A</sub> + LS<sub>B</sub>
- During 1−D: LS<sub>A</sub> (sync rectifier) + LS<sub>B</sub>

Each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical cycle (HS: D·⅓, LS: ⅓ + (1−D)·⅓ split across sectors). At electrical frequencies above about 500 Hz, the die thermal time constant (~1–5 ms for 3.3×3.3 DFN) averages this. Per-FET average power is therefore valid for burst analysis above roughly 6 krpm mechanical on 14-pole motors. Below that, apply the transient Z<sub>th</sub> correction in Section 3.4.
