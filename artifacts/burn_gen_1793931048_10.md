# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Thermal, Switching-Loss, and Bi-Directional DShot Design Package

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Up-Front Design Risks

I don't have the Job #28 requirements document, so this package states every assumption explicitly. Swap in your real values; every equation is parameterized.

### 0.1 Architecture assumed

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. It is the flight controller running ArduPilot, and it emits bi-directional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs on the same stack or AIO. The ESC MCUs (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071) run AM32 or BLHeli_32 at 48 kHz PWM.

This split is standard because commutation and FOC are hard real-time. You do not want ArduPilot's scheduler in that loop.

### 0.2 Two red flags to resolve before layout

1. **"Nano" vs. 80 A.** A 20×20 mm board cannot physically dissipate the roughly 85 W this design generates at 80 A on four motors (Section 1.6). The numbers below assume a **30.5×30.5 mm mount / ~36×36 mm outline**. Even then, 80 A is a **≤ 2–3 s burst rating**, not continuous.
2. **Bulk capacitor ripple current reaches ~40 A RMS** at 80 A per motor (Section 1.7). This is frequently what actually fails, before the FETs do.

## 1. Power-Stage Loss Model (per motor, then ×4)

### 1.1 Conduction topology

The ESC uses six-step (trapezoidal) commutation with **synchronous complementary PWM**, which AM32 and BLHeli_32 both default to.

At any instant, two half-bridge legs carry the full phase current I. In each leg, exactly one FET conducts: the high-side during t_on, the low-side during t_off (outside dead time). So regardless of duty D, there are always **two R_ds(on) in series** in the current path.

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I^2\, R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = 2\, I^2\, k_T\, R_{ds(on),25}$$

Check that this is duty-independent. In the PWM leg, the high-side carries D·I²R and the low-side carries (1−D)·I²R, which sum to I²R. The static leg carries I²R. So the total is 2I²R.

**At 80 A:**
$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5 \times 10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ motor}$$

Per-FET peak (for junction transients) is the case where the static-leg FET carries the full current:
$$P_{FET,max} = I^2 R_{hot} = 6400 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{9.6\ W}$$

### 1.2 Hard-switching (V–I overlap) loss

Only the PWM leg switches. With an inductive load (motor winding), the active FET sees roughly the full V_bus × I during the transitions:

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I\, (t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}$$

$$P_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\times10^{-9} \times 48\,000 = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

This is concentrated in the high-side FET of the PWM leg. That FET is the hottest device: **≈ 9.6·D + 1.94 W**, approaching ~11.5 W at high duty.

### 1.3 Capacitive (C_oss) loss

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 1.4 Reverse recovery and dead time

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$

During dead time, current freewheels through the low-side body diode, twice per PWM period:

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.307\ W}$$

### 1.5 Gate drive

Two FETs (the HS/LS pair of the PWM leg) are charged per cycle. The commutation-rate contribution (eRPM-dependent, a few kHz) is negligible by comparison.

$$P_{gate} = 2\, Q_g\, V_{drv}\, f_{sw} = 2\times60\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.058\ W}$$

Most of this dissipates in the driver IC and gate resistors, not the FETs.

### 1.6 Totals

| Loss term | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.20 W | 76.8 W |
| Hard switching | 1.94 W | 7.8 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.31 W | 1.2 W |
| C_oss + Q_rr | 0.11 W | 0.4 W |
| Gate drive | 0.06 W | 0.2 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 21.6 W** | **≈ 86 W** |

Add PCB copper I²R. For a 2 oz (70 µm) inner/outer stack, 4 mm wide × 15 mm long phase path:

$$R = \rho L/(wt) = 1.72\times10^{-8}\times0.015/(0.004\times70\times10^{-6}) \approx 0.92\ \text{m}\Omega$$

That is ≈ 5.9 W per phase path at 80 A. **This is why you must parallel the phase copper across ≥ 4 layers.** Doing so reduces it to ~1.5 W per motor. Budget **~92 W total** at a 4×80 A burst.

**Key insight:** at 48 kHz with fast FETs, conduction dominates switching by roughly 10:1. Lowering f_sw buys little thermally. Lowering R_ds(on) (paralleling FETs, larger packages) is the lever.

### 1.7 Bulk capacitor ripple current

The input capacitor ripple for a buck-like PWM leg is:

$$I_{C,rms} = I\sqrt{D(1-D)} \;\le\; 0.5\, I$$

At 80 A and D = 0.5, this gives **40 A RMS per motor**. The four motors' PWM is unsynchronized, so the shared bank sees roughly √4 × 40 ≈ 80 A worst case if lumped. Realistically it is per-ESC-local.

**Requirement:** low-ESR polymer plus MLCC per motor, rated ≥ 15–20 A RMS each, plus a ≥ 1000 µF / 35 V low-ESR electrolytic at the battery pads. Keep battery-lead inductance low.

$$\Delta T_{jc} = P_{FET} \cdot Z_{\theta jc}(t)$$

For bursts longer than ~100 ms, Z_θjc reaches R_θjc ≈ 1.5 K/W:

$$\Delta T_{jc} = 11.5 \times 1.5 \approx \mathbf{17\ K}$$
