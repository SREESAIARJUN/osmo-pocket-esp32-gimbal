# STM32H743 AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC): 80 A Burst Power-Stage and Bidirectional DShot600 Architecture

**Design Review Package, Job #33, Rev A (preliminary)**

---

## 0. Executive Summary and Feasibility Flags

Before you commit to layout, three findings need decisions:

| # | Finding | Severity | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | 80 A burst per channel on a 20×20 mm "nano" board dissipates about **30 W per channel** (about 120 W total) at 6S. Board thermal capacitance is about 1.8 J/K, so heating is about **65 K/s** with all four channels in burst. | **Critical** | Define "burst" as ≤ 1 s, single channel, with ≥ 10 s recovery. Otherwise move to a 30.5×30.5 mm board or 5×6 mm FETs. |
| F2 | 80 A in 2 oz copper needs about **5–6 mm effective width spread over all layers** (IPC-2152, ΔT = 30 K). A 20×20 mm board cannot route this per phase without stacking all four layers. | High | Use a 6-layer, 2 oz board with phase pours on L1/L3/L4/L6 and via stitching of ≥ 20 vias per phase node. |
| F3 | The H743 does **not** commutate motors. The 48 kHz PWM is set in the ESC firmware (AM32/BLHeli_32) running on four ESC MCUs. The H743 only sources DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry. | Info | The architecture below uses 4× AT32F421 plus a 3-phase gate driver per channel. |

All numbers below are parameterized. Swap in your actual FET datasheet values (table 1.1) and the model recomputes.

### 1.2 Commutation topology

The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM:

- In each 60° sector, one high-side FET (HS) and one low-side FET (LS) on different phases carry current.
- The PWM'd leg alternates between its HS (fraction D) and its complementary LS (fraction 1−D).
- Because motor inductance makes the phase current essentially continuous, **two FET channels are always conducting the full phase current I_ph, regardless of D.**

Note: I_ph is phase current. Battery current is I_bat ≈ D·I_ph. An "80 A burst" spec must state which one it means. This document assumes **80 A phase current**, the thermally relevant quantity.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Per-channel total

Two conducting switch positions, each at I_ph:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds,25}\, \alpha_R(T_j)$$

Temperature coupling: let α_R(T_j) ≈ 1 + k(T_j − 25), with k ≈ 0.0059 /K (fits 1.5 at 110 °C).

### 2.2 Numeric result at 80 A

$$R_{ds,hot} = 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.5 = 2.25\,\text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 6400 \times 2.25\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{28.8\ W}$$

### 2.3 Per-FET distribution

Each phase leg conducts for 240° electrical: 120° as the "fixed" side and 120° as the PWM'd side. Averaged over an electrical revolution, the six FETs share the loss as follows:

- **Fixed (non-PWM) FET of a sector:** I²R for 1/3 of the time, giving 4.8 W average.
- **PWM'd HS/LS pair:** D and (1−D) of 1/3 of the time each, so they sum to 4.8 W per leg.
- **Worst-case single FET** (fixed LS with 120° full conduction plus complementary conduction when D is low): **≈ 6–8 W average, 14.4 W instantaneous.**

Size the thermal design for **14.4 W instantaneous per FET**. At low eRPM (motor stall or punch-out from hover) a single sector can persist for milliseconds, so averaging over commutation is not safe.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In each PWM period, one hard-switched transition pair occurs per channel. The incoming FET hard-switches against the opposite FET's body diode after dead time.

### 3.1 V–I overlap loss

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.968\ W}$$

### 3.2 Reverse-recovery loss

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}$$

Q_rr scales with I_ph and di/dt, so treat this as a lower bound. Multiply by 2–3 if your gate drive is aggressive.

### 3.3 Output capacitance loss

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss,eff} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.023\ W}$$

### 3.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I_{ph}\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.307\ W}$$

### 3.5 Gate-drive loss

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET.

$$P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \times 40\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.038\ W}$$

### 3.6 Switching subtotal

| Term | W per channel |
|---|---|
| V–I overlap | 0.968 |
| Q_rr | 0.036 |
| C_oss | 0.023 |
| Dead time | 0.307 |
| **FET switching total** | **1.33** |
| Gate drive (driver IC) | 0.038 |

### 3.7 Frequency sensitivity

$$P_{sw}(f) = 1.33\,\text{W} \times f/48\,\text{kHz}$$

- At 24 kHz: 0.67 W.
- At 96 kHz: 2.67 W.

Switching loss is under 5% of conduction loss at this current. **Conduction dominates, so f_sw is not your thermal lever; R_DS(on) and copper are.** 48 kHz is justified by acoustic and current-ripple considerations, not efficiency.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Steady-state budget per channel (80 A, all terms)

$$P_{ch} = 28.8 + 1.33 + \text{shunt/trace} \approx \mathbf{31.5\ W}$$

The shunt and trace term is about 1.4 W, assuming 0.5 mΩ shunt (if fitted) plus about 0.5 mΩ of phase copper.

All four channels give **P_total ≈ 126 W** (worst case, simultaneous).

### 4.2 Steady-state is not achievable

The board-to-ambient thermal resistance for a 20×20 mm board in prop wash is about 8–12 K
