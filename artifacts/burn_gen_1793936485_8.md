# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #22): Power-Stage Thermal Model, Switching Losses, and Bidirectional DShot600 Allocation

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

This is a stacked or integrated design. The **STM32H743 is the flight controller**. Each ESC channel runs its own MCU (AT32F421 running AM32 assumed), which generates the 48 kHz motor PWM. The H743 only produces the DShot600 commands and decodes the bidirectional eRPM telemetry.

All numbers below come from the assumed parameters in this table. Substitute datasheet values for your chosen parts before you sign anything off.

| Parameter | Symbol | Assumed value |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | V_bus | 6S, 25.2 V full charge (worst case) |
| Burst phase current per channel | I_ph | 80 A |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| MOSFET (40 V, 3.3×3.3 or 5×6 package) | R_ds(on),25°C | 1.0 mΩ at V_gs = 10 V |
| R_ds(on) temperature factor at T_j ≈ 125 °C | k_T | 1.5 |
| Effective rise + fall time | t_r + t_f | 20 ns + 20 ns |
| Reverse recovery charge | Q_rr | 40 nC |
| Output charge | Q_oss | 30 nC |
| Gate charge | Q_g | 50 nC at 10 V |
| Dead time | t_dt | 100 ns |
| Body-diode forward voltage | V_f | 0.8 V |
| Copper path resistance (phase pad → FET → bus), per conduction path | R_cu | 0.25 mΩ |
| Commutation | — | 6-step trapezoidal, complementary (synchronous) PWM |

**Feasibility warning.** 80 A per channel on a "nano" (20×20 mm) 4-in-1 is a **burst-only** rating. The model below shows it is thermally limited to roughly 5 seconds. The realistic continuous rating is about 30 A per channel.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths
In 6-step synchronous commutation, phase current always flows through **exactly two switches in series**:
- one in the PWM-active leg (the high side during D, the low side during 1−D, since synchronous rectification keeps one of them on), and
- one static switch in the return leg.

So, ignoring the short dead-time intervals:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,(k_T R_{ds,25} + R_{cu})
$$

### 1.2 At 80 A burst

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot (1.5\,\text{mΩ} + 0.25\,\text{mΩ}) = 2 \cdot 6400 \cdot 1.75\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{22.4\ W}
$$

This splits into:
- **FETs:** 19.2 W
- **Copper:** 3.2 W

### 1.3 Per-FET share
Over an electrical cycle, six FETs share the FET loss equally:

$$
P_{cond,FET} = \frac{2 I^2 R_{ds,hot}}{6} = \frac{19.2}{6} = \mathbf{3.2\ W\ (average)}
$$

Within any 60° interval, though, one FET carries up to the full 9.6 W. Use this instantaneous value for the junction ripple check in section 3.4.

### 1.4 Battery current versus phase current
Battery current is approximately D·I_ph. At D = 0.5, an 80 A phase current draws only about 40 A per channel from the battery. Size the current sensor and the XT60/XT90 connector on battery current. Size the FETs and phase pads on phase current.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-active leg switches. The static return-leg FET contributes conduction loss only.

| Term | Formula | Value per channel |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = ½ · 25.2 · 80 · 40 ns · 48 k | **1.94 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw = 40 nC · 25.2 · 48 k | 0.048 W |
| Output capacitance (2 edges) | Q_oss · V · f_sw = 30 nC · 25.2 · 48 k | 0.036 W |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_f · I · 2 t_dt · f_sw = 0.8 · 80 · 200 ns · 48 k | **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (HS + LS) | 2 · Q_g · V_gs · f_sw = 2 · 50 nC · 10 · 48 k | 0.048 W* |
| **Total** | | **≈ 2.68 W** |

*Gate drive loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET.

**Design levers:**
- Dead-time loss is the second-largest term. Reducing t_dt to 50 ns, if the driver propagation-delay matching allows it, saves about 0.3 W per channel.
- At 24 kHz, total switching loss drops to about 1.3 W. 48 kHz is a deliberate trade: smoother low-throttle behaviour and lower motor ripple, paid for in loss.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Total burst dissipation

$$
P_{ch} = 22.4 + 2.68 \approx 25.1\ \text{W}, \qquad P_{board} = 4 \times 25.1 \approx \mathbf{100\ W}
$$

### 3.2 Lumped RC thermal model (board level)
- **Thermal capacitance:** 20×20 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz board with components and FETs, about 10 g at an effective 0.7 J/(g·K). This gives **C_th ≈ 7 J/K**.
- **Board-to-ambient resistance in prop wash:** **R_ba ≈ 5 K/W** (estimate; validate in a wind tunnel or on a hover log).
- **Time constant:** τ = R·C = **35 s**.

$$
\Delta T(t) = P R_{ba}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right)
$$

### 3.3 Burst duration limit
Start at 40 °C ambient. The board limit is 110 °C (FR4 and solder-joint margin), so the allowed rise is ΔT_max = 70 K. The asymptotic rise at full burst would be P·R = 500 K.

$$
t_{burst} = -\tau \ln\!\left(1 - \frac{70}{500}\right) = 35 \times 0.151 \approx \mathbf{5.3\ s}
$$

For a quick check, the initial slope is P/C ≈ 14 K/s.

### 3.4 Junction rise above the board
Per-FET dissipation is about 3.2 W conduction plus about 0.9 W switching share, roughly 4.1 W average. The thermal path is:

$$
R_{jc} \approx 1.5\ \text{K/W} + R_{c\text{-}board}\ (\text{solder + via array}) \approx 3\ \text{K/W}
\;\Rightarrow\; \Delta T_{jb} \approx 4.1 \times 4.5 \approx 18\ \text{K}
$$

The 60° instantaneous peak of about 9.6 W lasts only about 100 µs at typical eRPM. That is far shorter than the die thermal time constant (milliseconds), so it adds only about 2–3 K of ripple.

**Result:** T_j ≈ T_board + ~20 K. When the board reaches 110 °C, T_j ≈ 130 °C, which is below the 150–175 °C rating. **The board and solder are the limiting element, not the silicon.**
