# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC with 4-in-1 ESC: Systems Architecture Model (Job #36)

This document is a **reference design with stated assumptions**, not a production release. Every number derives from the parameters in §0. Substitute your actual MOSFET datasheet values, re-run the equations, and validate on hardware with thermocouples or IR before freezing the design.

**Key conclusion up front:** 80 A per motor on a nano-format board (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) is a **burst-only rating of about 2–4 s**, set by thermal capacitance, not by steady-state conduction. At 80 A the board dissipates about 85 W, which cannot be shed continuously through copper of this size.

---

## 0. Architectural Clarification and Assumptions

**Partitioning.** The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs. In every viable AIO architecture:

- **FC MCU (STM32H743)** runs ArduPilot and sends bi-directional DShot600.
- **The 4-in-1 ESC section** uses one small MCU per motor (or one per pair). Examples are the AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or BLHeli_32-class firmware. These MCUs perform commutation at f_sw = 48 kHz.

**Design parameters**

| Symbol | Parameter | Assumed value |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | Battery, 6S | 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal |
| I_ph | Burst phase current | 80 A (≤ 3 s) |
| f_sw | PWM frequency | 48 kHz |
| MOSFET | 40 V N-ch, 3.3×3.3 DFN class | 1 per switch, 6 per motor, 24 total |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, two phases are energized at any instant. The current path is:

- During D·T_sw: HS(A) → motor → LS(B)
- During (1−D)·T_sw: LS(A) (synchronous freewheel) → motor → LS(B)

In both sub-intervals exactly **two channels carry I_ph**. Conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle, provided synchronous rectification is used and dead time is neglected:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0053/\text{K} \Rightarrow k_T(110^\circ\text{C}) \approx 1.45
$$

$$
R_{hot} = 1.45\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

### 1.3 At 80 A

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.45\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{18.56\ W}
$$

### 1.4 Distribution across the six FETs

Each phase leg conducts for 2/3 of the electrical period. Assuming equal HS/LS utilization over a revolution:

- Average loss per FET: P̄_FET ≈ 18.56 / 6 ≈ **3.1 W**
- Instantaneous peak per conducting FET: I²R = 80² × 1.45 m = **9.28 W**

The peak value drives the junction transient analysis in §4.3.

### 1.5 Copper and shunt conduction (frequently ignored, not negligible)

**Phase traces.** Assume 2 oz outer layers plus internal planes, an effective 6 mm × 10 mm path, and about 0.2 mΩ per phase path:

$$
P_{Cu,phase} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.2\text{m} = 2.56\ \text{W/motor}
$$

**Battery input.** Total burst current is 4 × 80 × D_avg. At full throttle, D approaches 1, so I_bat approaches 320 A. Assuming the pad, solder, and plane total 0.15 mΩ:

$$
P_{in} = 320^2 \times 0.15\text{m} \approx 15.4\ \text{W}
$$

This is a strong reason to **use thick XT60/XT90 leads soldered directly to large pads, and 6 oz-equivalent copper (stacked inner layers)**.

**Current shunt.** For example, 0.25 mΩ with 320 A gives 25.6 W. This is **not acceptable at burst current.** Use a 0.1 mΩ shunt (10.2 W), or a Hall sensor or per-motor sensing instead.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In 6-step operation only **one leg is PWM-switching** at a time. The second energized leg is held statically on.

### 2.1 V-I overlap (hard-switched commutation edges)

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period)

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I_{ph}\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}
$$

Reducing dead time to 100 ns halves this loss. The penalty is shoot-through risk; verify it with a double-pulse test.

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = V_{bus} Q_{rr} f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 50\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}
$$

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} \approx V_{bus} Q_{oss} f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (two complementary FETs switching)

$$
P_{gate} = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

The gate-drive loss is dissipated mainly in the driver and gate resistors.

### 2.6 Switching total

$$
P_{sw,motor} = 1.45 + 1.23 + 0.06 + 0.048 + 0.048 \approx \mathbf{2.84\ W}
$$

Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it falls to about 1.42 W, at the cost of more audible noise and current ripple.
