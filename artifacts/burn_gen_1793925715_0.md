## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Findings

Three findings shape the rest of this document.

### 0.1 The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs

ArduPilot does not commutate motors. A real "H743 4-in-1" AIO board has this structure:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──bidir DShot600 ×4──► ESC MCU(s) (AM32 / BLHeli_32)
                                              e.g. 4× AT32F421 or STM32G071
                                              ──► 3-phase gate driver ×4 ──► 24 MOSFETs
```

- The bidirectional DShot section (§4) applies to the H743.
- The loss model (§1–3) applies to the ESC power stage.

### 0.2 80 A burst on a true "nano" (20×20 mm) board is not physically achievable

The numbers below show why.

- **20×20 mm board:** Real products use 3.3×3.3 mm FETs at roughly 3–5 mΩ and are rated about 25–40 A burst.
- **30.5×30.5 mm board:** The model shows 80 A per motor is possible only as a burst of about 1–2 s.
- **Continuous rating:** Even at 30.5×30.5 mm, the board sustains only about 25 A per motor.

I present both cases so the decision is data-driven.

### 0.3 Device parameters are design assumptions

Device values are representative assumptions, not a specific vendor's datasheet. Replace them with your selected FET's datasheet values. Every equation is parameterized for that purpose.
