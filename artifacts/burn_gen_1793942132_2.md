## 1. Conduction Loss Model at 80 A Burst

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, phase current flows through exactly **two MOSFET channels** at every instant:

- **PWM ON:** HS(A) → winding → LS(B)
- **PWM OFF:** LS(A) (synchronous freewheel) → winding → LS(B)

So whatever the duty cycle D, the conducted I²R per channel is:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)\quad(\text{minus dead-time intervals, handled in }\S2)$$

The loss is split differently among devices depending on D. The highest per-device stress is on the "return" low-side FET (LS(B)). It carries the full current for its 120° electrical window, so its RMS current over a full electrical cycle is:

$$I_{rms,LS(B)} = I_{ph}\sqrt{1/3} = 46.2\text{ A}\ (\text{at }80\text{ A})$$

### 1.2 Temperature-coupled solution

R_DS(on) rises with temperature, and temperature rises with loss. The fixed point is:

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + R_{\theta,jb}\,P_{FET}(T_j),\qquad P_{FET}=I_{rms}^2 R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Solving for T_j in closed form:

$$T_j = \frac{T_{pcb} + R_{\theta jb} I_{rms}^2 R_{25}(1-25\alpha)}{1 - R_{\theta jb} I_{rms}^2 R_{25}\alpha}$$

**Thermal runaway criterion.** The denominator must stay positive:

$$R_{\theta jb}\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{25}\,\alpha < 1$$

With R_θjb = 1.5 K/W and I_rms = 46.2 A, the left side is 1.5 × 2134 × 0.00085 × 0.0055 = **0.015**. This is far from runaway. The device is stable, and the limit is the board's heat capacity, not the die.

### 1.3 Numbers per channel at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond per channel = 2·I²·R |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 0.85 mΩ | 10.9 W |
| 75 °C | 1.08 mΩ | 13.9 W |
| 100 °C | 1.20 mΩ | 15.3 W |
| 115 °C | 1.27 mΩ | **16.3 W** (design point) |

**Copper and trace loss (do not ignore).** Assume roughly 0.25 mΩ of bus and phase copper path per channel at 80 A, including the phase pad and the solder-to-motor-wire joint:

$$P_{Cu}=80^2\times0.25\text{ m}\Omega = 1.6\text{ W per channel}$$

Shunt (if using per-channel 0.5 mΩ low-side current sense):

$$P_{sh}=80^2\times 0.5\text{ m}\Omega\times\tfrac{1}{1}\approx 3.2\text{ W}$$

Many nano AIOs instead use a single DC-bus shunt. If so, it carries the total bus current, not the phase current. That is about 4 × 80 A × D. At D = 0.8 this is 256 A, giving 256² × 0.1 mΩ = **6.6 W** in a 0.1 mΩ shunt. That is a significant hotspot.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Per hard-switched leg (the PWM-ing phase), per switching period:

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard switching on the HS FET)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}V_{bus}I_{ph}(t_r+t_f)f_{sw} = 0.5\times25.2\times80\times24\text{ns}\times48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.16\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

The LS body diode conducts during dead time and is then hard-commutated:

$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 45\text{nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.054\ W}$$

Q_rr increases with di/dt and current. Derate ×2–3 at 80 A, giving about 0.15 W.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss}=Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}\ (\text{HS charging LS }C_{oss}+\text{own}) \approx 40\text{nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time windows per period:

$$P_{dt}=V_{SD}\,I_{ph}\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw}=0.8\times80\times600\text{ns}\times48\text{k}=\mathbf{1.84\ W}$$

This is the largest switching-related term. It argues for reducing dead time to the minimum that is safe for your gate driver's propagation-delay mismatch.

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss lands in the driver and gate resistor, not the FET. Two FETs switch per leg:

$$P_g = 2\,Q_gV_{GS}f_{sw}=2\times55\text{nC}\times10\times48\text{k}=0.053\text{ W}$$

### 2.6 Switching total per channel

$$P_{sw,ch}=1.16+0.15+0.05+1.84+0.05\approx \mathbf{3.25\ W}$$

**Frequency scaling.** Every term is linear in f_sw. At 24 kHz this drops to about 1.6 W. At 96 kHz it rises to about 6.5 W.
