# STM32H743 Nano AIO: 4-in-1 ESC Power Stage and ArduPilot BDShot600 Design Package (Job #78)

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Feasibility Flags

Before the math, three architectural points shape everything else.

**1. Partitioning.** ArduPilot on the H743 does not commutate motors directly. The architecture assumed here is:

- **H743 (FC):** ArduPilot, sending DShot600 bidirectional to the ESC MCU(s).
- **ESC MCU:** AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32. Either one MCU per channel (4×) or one shared MCU per pair. These generate the 48 kHz complementary PWM.
- **Power stage:** 3× half-bridge per channel, 24 MOSFETs total.

**2. 80 A per channel on a nano (20×20 mm) board is burst-only and thermally adiabatic.** The math below shows roughly a 1.8 s limit with all four channels at 80 A. Treat "80 A" as a firmware-enforced burst rating, not continuous.

**3. Bus current.** 4 × 80 A = 320 A from the battery. That exceeds what XT30 connectors, 14 AWG leads, or 20×20 copper pours can carry without severe drop and heating. A realistic aggregate design target is about 120–160 A burst. The model below still computes the full 80 A/channel case as requested.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (6-Step Trapezoidal, Complementary PWM)

In any commutation sector, one phase is PWM'd (high-side/low-side complementary), one phase is held low (return path), and one floats.

- **PWM phase:** the high-side conducts for duty D, and the synchronous low-side conducts for 1–D. Together they carry I for the whole period.
- **Return phase:** the low-side conducts I continuously.

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{ds}\,D + I^2 R_{ds}(1-D) + I^2 R_{ds} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This is independent of D, which is a key property of synchronous rectification.

**At 80 A:**

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.8\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{23.04\ W}$$

**Per-FET average.** Each FET's role rotates over 6 sectors, so:

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx P_{cond,ch}/6 = 3.84\ \text{W}$$

**Peak instantaneous FET** (return LS during its sector): 80² × 1.8 mΩ = 11.5 W.

**Electrothermal self-consistency.** Iterate using

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\quad \alpha\approx0.006/\text{K}$$

The 1.5× factor corresponds to T_j ≈ 108 °C.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM phase's leg switches hard (one hard turn-on and one hard turn-off per period).

**3.1 V–I overlap**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(20\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.968\ W}$$

**3.2 Output capacitance**

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

**3.3 Reverse recovery of the sync FET's body diode**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 30\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

**3.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction**

$$P_{dt} = V_f I\,(2t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.614\ W}$$

This is the second-largest switching term. Reducing dead time to 50 ns halves it, but verify shoot-through margin with the actual driver propagation skew.

**3.5 Gate drive** (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g)

$$P_g = 2\,Q_gV_{gs}f_{sw} = 2\cdot40\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k} = 0.038\ \text{W}$$

**3.6 Per-channel total at 80 A**

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction | 23.04 |
| V–I switching | 0.968 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.614 |
| Coss | 0.048 |
| Qrr | 0.036 |
| Gate | 0.038 |
| **P_ch** | **≈ 24.74 W** |
| **4 channels** | **≈ 99.0 W** |

**3.7 Scaling law.** The general-current form is:

$$P_{ch}(I) = 2R_{ds}I^2 + \big[\tfrac12V(t_r+t_f)f + V_f 2t_{dt}f\big]I + (Q_{oss}+Q_{rr})Vf + 2Q_gV_{gs}f$$

$$P_{ch}(I) \approx 3.6\times10^{-3}I^2 + 0.0198\,I + 0.122\ \text{[W]}$$

Example values:

- **Hover (≈10 A):** P_ch ≈ 0.68 W, so about 2.7 W total.
- **30 A:** P_ch ≈ 3.96 W.

Above about 6 A, conduction dominates. Lowering f_sw buys little at burst current; R_ds(on) and copper are the levers.
