## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation topology

In six-step trapezoidal commutation (AM32 / BLHeli_32), two phases conduct in each 60° electrical sector:

- **Phase A high side (HS):** PWM at duty D.
- **Phase A low side (LS):** synchronous rectification, conducts for (1 − D).
- **Phase B low side:** held on for the whole sector.

The motor current I therefore always flows through exactly two channels in series:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

This total is **independent of duty**, which is an important consequence of synchronous rectification.

### 1.2 Temperature coefficient

For modern trench MOSFETs, Rds(on) rises by about 1.5× from 25 °C to 110 °C:

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

At Tj = 110 °C with the maximum datasheet value:

$$
R_{ds} = 1.3\ \text{m}\Omega \times (1 + 0.006 \times 85) = 1.96\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

$$
P_{cond,FET} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.96\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{25.1\ W/channel}
$$

### 1.4 Copper loss

Assume a 0.5 mΩ effective path per channel (battery pad → bus → phase pad, 2 oz, short runs):

$$
P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{3.2\ W/channel}
$$

The positive and negative battery bus carries the full 320 A. With a bus resistance of 0.1 mΩ:

$$
P_{bus} = 320^2 \times 0.1\times10^{-3} = 10.2\ \text{W}
$$

> Bus copper is a dominant hotspot. Use solder-filled bus bars or thick pads, not traces.

### 1.5 Thermal runaway check

Because conduction loss depends on Tj and Tj depends on loss, the junction temperature must satisfy:

$$
T_j = T_{case} + R_{\theta jc}\, P_{FET}(T_j)
$$

This converges (no runaway) when:

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\, R_{\theta} < 1
$$

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j} = 2 I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.3\times10^{-3} \times 0.006 = 0.10\ \text{W/K per channel}
$$

Spread across the FETs, the per-FET value is about 0.017 W/K. Multiplied by Rθjc = 1.2 K/W this gives 0.02, far below 1. **The junction is stable.** Any runaway risk lives at the board level, governed by Rθ(board–ambient), and is handled by the time limit in Section 3.
