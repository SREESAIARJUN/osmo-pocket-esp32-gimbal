## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Up-Front Feasibility Flags

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Form factor | 25.5 × 25.5 mm "nano/whoop-class" AIO, 8-layer stack, 2 oz outer and 1 oz inner copper (outer layers upgradable to 3 oz) |
| Flight controller (FC) | STM32H743VIH6 (rev V), SYSCLK 400 MHz, APB1/APB2 timer clock 200 MHz |
| ESC | 4 independent channels, each with its own MCU running AM32 (AT32F421 or STM32G071), PWM at 48 kHz, complementary PWM with synchronous rectification |
| Bus | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V maximum, 22.2 V nominal |
| FETs | 40 V N-channel in a 3.3 × 3.3 mm package, 6 per channel (24 total) |
| Burst rating | 80 A phase current per channel, short-duration burst |

**Feasibility flags. Read these first:**

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** It sends bi-directional DShot600 commands to four ESC MCUs. A single MCU running four sensorless field-oriented-control or 6-step loops plus a flight stack is not a supportable ArduPilot configuration.

2. **"80 A burst" must be defined.** The model below shows that 4 × 80 A simultaneously dissipates about 135 W in the power stage. A 25 mm board survives that for only about 2–3 s.
   - A credible datasheet rating would be **80 A for ≤1 s per channel** and **35–40 A continuous with airflow**.
   - Anything stronger is marketing, not engineering.

3. **Battery current is not the sum of phase currents.**
   - I_batt ≈ Σ(D_k · I_phase,k), where D is the duty cycle.
   - At full throttle (D ≈ 1), 4 × 80 A would mean about 320 A through the battery pads. That is not achievable through an XT30/XT60 pigtail on this board size.
   - The battery-entry copper should therefore be sized for about 120 A peak. Design the burst for transient, partial-duty events.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

With 6-step trapezoidal commutation and synchronous rectification, two FETs carry the phase current at all times:

- During the on-time (D): high-side (HS) of phase A and low-side (LS) of phase B.
- During the off-time (1−D): synchronous-rectifier LS of phase A and LS of phase B.

Integrated over a PWM cycle:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \left[ D\,(R_{HS}+R_{LS}) + (1-D)\,(R_{LS}+R_{LS}) \right] \approx 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

This holds when the HS and LS FETs are the same part. Each FET conducts for 120° electrical, so **per-FET average** loss is P_cond,ch / 3 per position pair. The **instantaneous** peak per FET, however, is I²R_DS(on). The peak is what drives transient junction temperature, because the commutation period (about 0.1–1 ms) is far shorter than the die time constant.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence (positive feedback)

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ /\text{K}
$$

A typical 40 V 3.3 × 3.3 mm part has R₂₅ ≈ 1.5 mΩ at V_GS = 10 V.

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond,ch at 80 A | Per conducting FET (instantaneous) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.50 mΩ | 19.2 W | 9.6 W |
| 100 °C | 2.18 mΩ | 27.9 W | 14.0 W |
| 125 °C | 2.40 mΩ | **30.7 W** | **15.4 W** |

### 1.3 Copper conduction loss

The phase pour plus the via field between the FETs and the motor pad is roughly 6 mm long and 3 mm wide, on parallel 2 oz layers (top and bottom), plus via arrays.

- Copper sheet resistance at 2 oz ≈ 0.25 mΩ/□.
- 6 mm / 3 mm = 2 squares, giving 0.5 mΩ per layer.
- Two layers in parallel give 0.25 mΩ. Add about 0.1 mΩ for vias.
- Result: R_cu ≈ 0.35 mΩ per phase leg.
- Two legs are in the current path, so 0.7 mΩ total, giving **P_cu ≈ 80² × 0.7 mΩ ≈ 4.5 W per channel**.

This loss is significant and is deposited directly into the board.

### 1.4 Thermal-runaway stability check

Conduction loss and junction temperature feed each other. The loop is stable if:

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\,R_{th} = 2I^2R_{25}\alpha\,R_{th} < 1
$$

- With 2I²R₂₅α = 0.115 W/K and R_th(j-a, effective) ≈ 6 K/W, the loop gain is **0.69**.
- That is stable, but with little margin. Any increase in R_th (conformal coat, no airflow) pushes it toward runaway.
- This is a second reason to keep 80 A strictly a burst rating.
