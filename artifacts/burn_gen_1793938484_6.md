The model below assumes a 30.5×30.5 mm AIO. Even then, 80 A is a burst rating of about 2–3 s, not a continuous rating (see §3).

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C with the max datasheet value: R_ds = 1.3 × (1 + 0.0045 × 85) = **1.80 mΩ**.
For a typical part at 110 °C: **1.38 mΩ**. This analysis uses **1.4 mΩ** (typical hot) and gives the worst case separately.

### 1.2 Six-step current path

In every commutation sector, the motor current flows through exactly two conducting devices:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET during t_on (duty D), or the complementary low-side FET during t_off (synchronous rectification).
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET, which stays on for the whole sector.

So, ignoring dead time:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\, I^2_{rms}\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

The phase current ripple is ΔI ≈ V_bus·D(1−D)/(L·f_sw). It adds ΔI²/12 to I_rms². With L_phase ≈ 20 µH and D = 0.5, ΔI ≈ 6.6 A. The ripple term is then 3.6 A², which is less than 0.1 % of 6400 A² and can be neglected.

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.4\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{17.9\ W}\quad(\text{worst case } 1.8\,\text{m}\Omega:\ 23.0\ \text{W})$$

**Per-FET average:** each phase leg conducts for 4 of 6 sectors. Over an electrical cycle, losses are roughly even across the 6 FETs:

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx 17.9/6 \approx 3.0\ \text{W}$$

At low eRPM, instantaneous loss can sit on one pair for milliseconds. Peak per-FET loss is then 2× the average. This matters for startup and stall (§3.3).

### 1.3 Copper (I²R) losses

Loss is set by the effective path resistance: phase pad → FET → bus → shunt, plus the bus-cap loop.

$$R_{cu} = \frac{\rho_{cu}(T)\,\ell}{w\,t},\qquad \rho_{cu}(100^\circ C) = 2.26\times10^{-8}\,\Omega\text{m}$$

Example: 12 mm effective length, 6 mm width, 3 paralleled 2 oz layers (t = 3 × 70 µm):
R_cu = 2.26e-8 × 0.012 / (0.006 × 210e-6) = **0.215 mΩ**.

Add the shunt (0.25 mΩ, if it is in the phase path) and solder/via interfaces (~0.1 mΩ): **R_path ≈ 0.55 mΩ**.

$$P_{cu} = 80^2 \times 0.55\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.5\ W\ per\ channel}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the PWM leg switches. That is one HS/LS pair per channel at any time.

### 2.1 Transition (V–I overlap) loss

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g},\quad I_g \approx \frac{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}{R_g+R_{g,int}}$$

Placeholder values: Q_gd = 9 nC, V_drv = 10 V, V_plateau = 3.5 V, R_g = 4.7 Ω + 1 Ω internal.
This gives I_g ≈ 1.14 A, so t_r ≈ t_f ≈ **8 ns**. Add current-commutation time and use **12 ns** per edge.

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 24\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.16\ W}$$

### 2.2 Body-diode (dead-time) conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

> This term is as large as the overlap loss. Cutting dead time to about 100 ns, if the driver and FET delays allow it, saves ~0.6 W per channel.

### 2.3 Reverse recovery, output capacitance, gate charge

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$
$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}$$
$$P_{gate} = 2\, Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2\times 50\text{nC}\times10\times48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

The gate term is dissipated in the driver and R_g, not in the FET.

### 2.4 Switching total per channel

$$P_{sw} = 1.16 + 1.23 + 0.05 + 0.06 + 0.05 \approx \mathbf{2.55\ W}$$
