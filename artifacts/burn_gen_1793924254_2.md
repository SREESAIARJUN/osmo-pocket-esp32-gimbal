# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Thermal, Switching and DShot Design Package

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Critical Findings

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller and does not drive the MOSFETs. A 4-in-1 ESC needs a commutation MCU per channel, so this design assumes:

- **FC:** STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100) running ArduPilot. It sends bi-directional DShot600 to the ESCs.
- **ESC:** 4× AT32F421 (or STM32G071) running AM32, 4× FD6288Q 3-phase gate drivers, and 24× N-channel MOSFETs.

**Electrical assumptions** (replace these with your BOM datasheet values):

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |

> ⚠️ **Critical finding:** an 80 A burst **per channel** is physically incompatible with a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board except for about 1 s pulses. The derivation in §1–3 proves this. If "80A burst" means **80 A total (20 A per channel)**, the design is comfortably viable. Both cases are quantified below.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation topology

The model assumes AM32 six-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. Each phase spends 120° high-side active, 120° low-side active and 120° floating.

For phase A over one electrical cycle:

- **High-side FET A⁺** conducts for a fraction D/3 of the time.
- **Low-side FET A⁻** conducts for a fraction (1−D)/3 as the synchronous rectifier, plus 1/3 as the commutating low leg.

$$P_{HS} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,\frac{D}{3}, \qquad P_{LS} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,\frac{2-D}{3}$$
