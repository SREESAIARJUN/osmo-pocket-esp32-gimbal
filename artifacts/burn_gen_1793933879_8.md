# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage and DShot Architecture

I don't have prior documentation for Job #14, so every input below is an explicit assumption. Replace the parameter table with your actual BOM and the equations re-evaluate directly. The most important conclusion comes first.

> **Bottom line:** On a 20×20 mm (or even 30.5×30.5 mm) stack, a 4×80 A burst dissipates about **110 W**. With realistic copper thermal mass, the board reaches its thermal limit in **about 1–3 seconds**. "80 A burst" is achievable only as a short transient, and firmware must enforce it with current and temperature limiting. Treat 80 A as a 1-second rating, not a continuous one.

## 1. System Partitioning

```
 6S LiPo ──► 4-in-1 ESC board ──────────────────────────────┐
            ├─ 4× ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32 FW)   │
            ├─ 4× 3-phase gate driver                       │
            ├─ 24× N-MOSFET (4 ESC × 3 legs × 2)            │
            ├─ shunt/current sense, Vbat divider, NTC       │
            └─ 8-pin JST-SH ─► FC: M1–M4, CUR, VBAT, GND, TLM│
 STM32H743 FC ◄── bidirectional DShot600 (eRPM telemetry) ──┘
```

The H743 does not drive the gates. It commands four ESC MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. The thermal model applies to the ESC board, and the DMA model applies to the FC.

## 3. Conduction Loss Model

### 3.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C, this gives 1.6 mΩ (a factor of 1.6).

### 3.2 Current path

With 6-step commutation and synchronous rectification, the current always flows through exactly two FETs:
- **On-state:** high-side of phase A, then low-side of phase B.
- **Off-state:** low-side of phase A (synchronous), then low-side of phase B.

Sinusoidal FOC gives nearly the same result, within about 10%.

$$P_{cond,ESC}=I_{ph}^2\,(2R_{ds}(T_j)+R_{cu})$$

### 3.3 Evaluation at 80 A

| T_j | R_ds | P_FET (2 FETs) | P_Cu | **P_cond per ESC** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.0 mΩ | 12.8 W | 3.2 W | **16.0 W** |
| 125 °C | 1.6 mΩ | 20.5 W | 3.2 W | **23.7 W** |

The instantaneous power in a single conducting FET is 80² × 1.6 mΩ = **10.2 W**. Each FET conducts about one-third of the electrical cycle, so its average is about 3.4 W.

## 4. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In 6-step operation, only one leg PWMs at a time. That leg has one hard-switched FET and one synchronous FET.

### 4.1 Gate current and transition times

$$I_{g,on}=\frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{g,on}}=\frac{6.5}{5}=1.3\text{ A}\qquad I_{g,off}=\frac{V_{pl}}{R_{g,off}}=\frac{3.5}{7}=0.5\text{ A}$$

$$t_r=\frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}}=12.3\text{ ns}\qquad t_f=\frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,off}}=32\text{ ns}$$
