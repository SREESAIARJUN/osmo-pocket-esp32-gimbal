# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #41): Architecture, Loss Model, DShot Timers & Netlists

## 0. Architecture Corrections

Three assumptions in the brief need correcting before the math is useful.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot is flight-control firmware. It does not run BLDC commutation, and bidirectional DShot is a protocol *between* the flight controller (FC) and separate ESC MCUs. The correct topology is:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──DShot600 bidir (×4)──► 4× ESC MCU (AM32 or BLHeli_32-class,
                                               e.g. AT32F421 / STM32G071)
                                               └─► gate driver ─► 6 FETs per motor
```

The 48 kHz switching frequency is set in the ESC firmware, not in ArduPilot. The FC and ESC can share a PCB (an AIO design), but they are separate firmware domains.

**2. "80 A per motor" on a nano board does not close at the system level.** At full throttle (duty ≈ 1), battery current is about 4 × 80 = **320 A, roughly 8 kW at 6S**. That exceeds what any XT30/XT60 pad, 20×20 or 30×30 copper, or a typical battery can deliver. The analysis below treats 80 A as a **short per-motor phase-current transient at partial duty**, which is what product labels actually mean. Section 2.6 derives the time limit.

**3. Component parameters below are representative placeholders.** They describe a typical 40 V, ~1 mΩ, 3.3×3.3 or 5×6 mm DFN FET. Substitute datasheet values for the selected parts. All equations are parametric for that reason.

## 1. Electrical Operating Point

| Symbol | Meaning | Value used |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 6S nominal / full charge | 22.2 V / 25.2 V (25.2 used, worst case) |
| I_ph | burst phase current | 80 A |
| f_sw | ESC PWM frequency | 48 kHz |
| R_ds(on),25 | FET on-resistance at 25 °C, V_gs = 10 V | 1.0 mΩ |
| k_T | R_ds(on) temperature factor at T_j ≈ 110 °C | 1.5 |
| t_r, t_f | switching edges at the chosen gate resistance | 20 ns each |
| Q_g | total gate charge | 60 nC |
| Q_rr | body-diode reverse recovery charge | 50 nC |
| Q_oss | output charge | 60 nC |
| V_SD | body-diode forward voltage | 0.8 V |
| t_dead | dead time per edge | 200 ns |
| L_ph-ph | motor line-to-line inductance | 20 µH |

**Commutation scheme.** Six-step trapezoidal (AM32 default) with complementary (synchronous) PWM. At any instant two phase legs carry I_ph: one high-side and one low-side path. The PWM'd leg alternates between its HS and LS FET.

## 2. Loss Model (per motor, then per board)

### 2.1 Ripple-corrected RMS current

The peak-to-peak current ripple is:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph\text{-}ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

Worst case is D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{25.2 \cdot 0.25}{20\,\mu H \cdot 48\,kHz} = 6.6\ \text{A}$$

The RMS current including ripple is:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.6 \approx 6404\ \text{A}^2$$

The ripple contribution is negligible at 48 kHz. Raising f_sw buys no conduction-loss benefit here; it only increases switching loss.

### 2.2 Conduction loss

With synchronous rectification, one FET conducts in the PWM'd leg at every instant, regardless of duty. One FET also always conducts in the static leg. Therefore:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{ds(on)}\, k_T = 2 \cdot 6404 \cdot 1.0\,m\Omega \cdot 1.5 = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

Averaged over the electrical cycle, this spreads across 6 FETs at **~3.2 W each**. Instantaneously, during a 60° sector, the two conducting positions carry about 9.6 W each. This instantaneous figure sets the peak junction rise (section 2.5).

### 2.3 Switching loss (hard-switched PWM leg)

Each period has one turn-on and one turn-off edge under full current.

**V–I overlap:**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\,ns \cdot 48\,kHz = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery** (the complementary FET's body diode conducts during dead time):

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\,nC \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\,kHz = 0.06\ W$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 60\,nC \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\,kHz = 0.07\ W$$

**Dead-time diode conduction** (2 dead-time intervals per period):

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I_{ph}\, 2 t_{dead}\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 400\,ns \cdot 48\,kHz = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

**Gate drive** (2 switching FETs, dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistor, not the FET):

$$P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 60\,nC \cdot 10\,V \cdot 48\,kHz = 0.06\ W$$

Two design notes follow from these numbers:

- **Dead time is the second-largest dynamic term.** Trimming it from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per motor. Tune it on the scope, not by feel.
- **Faster edges cut P_sw but cost EMI and ringing.** At 80 A, the commutation-loop inductance must stay below about 2 nH to tolerate 20 ns edges without exceeding a 40 V FET rating (V = L·di/dt ≈ 2 nH × 4 A/ns = 8 V of overshoot).
