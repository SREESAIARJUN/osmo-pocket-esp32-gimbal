# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #44): Power, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility Checks

These conditions affect every number in this document. No MOSFET, motor, or board stack-up was specified, so I chose a parametric reference design. Every equation is written so you can substitute your datasheet values.

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does **not** commutate the motors. Each of the four ESC channels has its own MCU (STM32G071 or AT32F421 class, running AM32 or BLHeli_32) at 48 kHz complementary PWM. ArduPilot cannot run 6-step BEMF commutation, so a single-H743 design is not viable on that firmware. The H743 talks to the ESC MCUs over bidirectional DShot600.

**"80A burst" on a nano form factor.** This means 4 × 80 A of phase current. At high throttle, battery current can approach about 250–320 A. A 20×20 or 30×30 mm board can survive this only for **seconds** (see §3). Treat 80 A as a time-limited rating, not a continuous one.

**Reference parameters (substitute your own):**

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 25.2 V | 6S, fully charged |
| R_ds,25 | 1.0 mΩ | 40 V, PDFN 5×6 class |
| α | 0.0055 /K | Temperature coefficient of R_ds |
| t_r, t_f | 15 ns each | Switching edges at 80 A, ~1.5 A gate drive |
| t_dt | 100 ns | Dead time per edge |
| V_SD | 0.85 V | Body-diode drop at 80 A |
| Q_rr | 50 nC | Reverse-recovery charge |
| Q_oss | 60 nC | Output charge |
| Q_g | 80 nC | Total gate charge |
| V_GS | 10 V | Gate drive voltage |
| L_ph-ph | 20 µH | Motor phase-to-phase inductance |
| f_sw | 48 kHz | PWM frequency |

**Voltage margin.** 40 V FETs on 6S leave about 15 V for switching overshoot. This requires low-ESR bulk capacitance plus local 100 nF–1 µF ceramics at every half-bridge. If you cannot measure ringing below about 34 V, use 60 V parts and accept a higher R_ds.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.0 × 1.55 = **1.55 mΩ**.

### 1.2 RMS current including PWM ripple

Ripple at the worst-case duty cycle (D = 0.5):

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L\,f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\,\mu\text{H} \times 48\,\text{kHz}} = 6.56\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{dc}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.6 \approx 6404\ \text{A}^2$$

Ripple adds only 0.06% at this current, so I_rms ≈ 80 A. The ripple term does matter at light load and with low-inductance motors.

### 1.3 Current path in 6-step synchronous commutation

At every instant, exactly two FETs carry the phase current:
- PWM on: high side of phase X and low side of phase Y.
- PWM off: low side of phase X (synchronous freewheel) and low side of phase Y.

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j) = 2 \times 6404 \times 1.55\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.9\ W\ per\ channel}$$

Board copper adds its own loss. Assume 0.3 mΩ for the phase path plus 0.15 mΩ for the bus share:

$$P_{Cu} \approx I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.45\,\text{m}\Omega = 2.9\ \text{W per channel}$$

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution (hotspot)

The hottest device is the low side of the "Y" phase. Over its 120° sector it conducts continuously, and its duty is (1 − D) when it also acts as freewheel. Its sector-averaged share is about 1/3 of the time at the full I²R:

$$P_{FET,hot} \approx \tfrac{1}{3} \times 6404 \times 1.55\,\text{m}\Omega \times (1 + 0.5) \approx \mathbf{5.0\ W}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In 6-step operation, only one half-bridge per channel is PWM-switching at any time. The terms below are per channel.

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Hard-switching overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 30 ns × 48 k = **1.45 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_SD · I · 2t_dt · f | 0.85 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k = **0.65 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f | 50 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.06 W** |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss · V · f (upper bound) | **0.07 W** |
| Gate charge (dissipated in driver) | 2 · Q_g · V_GS · f | **0.08 W** |
| **Total P_sw** | | **≈ 2.3 W** |

**Design levers:**
- Overlap loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it is about 0.7 W, but ripple and acoustic noise double.
- Dead-time loss is the second-largest term. AM32's dead-time register should be the minimum that is safe with your driver's propagation skew, measured on a scope.
