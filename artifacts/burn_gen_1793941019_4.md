### Architecture flags to resolve first

1. **The H743 does not run the 48 kHz bridge.** In an AIO board the H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot600 to **four separate ESC MCUs** (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32). The 48 kHz PWM, dead time, and commutation all live on those ESC MCUs. Section 3 covers only the H743 side.
2. **"Nano" and "80 A" conflict.** On a 20×20 mm board, 80 A per channel is not physically supportable even as a burst; the thermal math in Section 2 shows why. This analysis assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm** stack board with 5×6 mm FETs. Treat 80 A as a **≤ 10 s burst rating**, not a continuous one.
3. **48 kHz is a high PWM frequency** at this current. It gives smooth, quiet motors, but switching loss scales linearly with f_sw (≈1.8 W per channel here; ≈0.9 W at 24 kHz).

## 1. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 1.1 Commutation model
The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM, which is the AM32 default. In each 60° commutation sector:

- **Phase A high-side (HS):** switches at duty D.
- **Phase A low-side (LS):** synchronous rectifier, conducts for (1−D), minus dead time.
- **Phase B low-side:** held fully on.

### 1.2 Conduction loss
The instantaneous current path always passes through exactly **two conducting FETs**, so the D-dependence cancels:

$$
P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds,hot}\,\big[D + (1-D) + 1\big] = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds,hot}
$$

$$
R_{ds,hot} = k_T \cdot R_{ds,25} = 1.5 \times 0.90\ \text{m}\Omega = 1.35\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.35\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{17.28\ W}
$$

**Electro-thermal coupling.** R_ds depends on T_j. Iterate the following until T_j converges (2–3 passes is typical):

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.0059\ /\text{K}
$$

### 1.3 Dead-time (body-diode) loss
$$
P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I_{ph} \cdot 2\,t_{dt}\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 2(100\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

### 1.4 Switching loss (hard-switched high-side)
Gate currents at the Miller plateau:

$$
I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{drv,src}+R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}} = \frac{12-3}{6} = 1.5\ \text{A}
\qquad
I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{drv,snk}+R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}} = \frac{3}{5} = 0.6\ \text{A}
$$

Transition times use the switching charge Q_sw = Q_gs2 + Q_gd = 15 nC:

$$
t_{on} = \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{g,on}} = 10.0\ \text{ns}, \qquad t_{off} = \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{g,off}} = 25.0\ \text{ns}
$$

The V–I overlap loss is then:

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_{on}+t_{off}) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(35\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.69\ W}
$$

*Design note:* turn-off dominates. A 1 Ω R_g,ext with a schottky bypass diode cuts t_off to about 15 ns and saves roughly 0.5 W per channel. The cost is higher dV/dt, so verify ringing.

### 1.5 Recovery, capacitive, and gate losses
$$
P_{rr} = V_{bus} Q_{rr} f_{sw} = 25.2(40\text{n})(48\text{k}) = 0.048\ \text{W}
$$
$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} Q_{oss} f_{sw} \times 2 = 0.060\ \text{W}
$$
$$
P_{gate} = 2\, Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2(60\text{n})(12)(48\text{k}) = 0.069\ \text{W}
$$

P_gate is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FETs.
