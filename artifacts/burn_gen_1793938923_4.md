# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage, Thermal, and Bidirectional DShot Design Package

**Job #53, Revision A (architecture baseline)**

I have no prior context for Job #53, so this package states its assumptions explicitly (§0). Every result is derived symbolically first and then evaluated, so you can substitute your actual BOM values. The numbers lead to one conclusion you should take to the design review: **80 A per channel on a nano-class board is only feasible as a short burst of a few seconds, and the burst limit must be enforced in firmware.**

## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

### 0.1 System partitioning

An STM32H743 does not commutate the motors directly. The standard, and only sensible, partition is:

```
 ┌────────────────────── FC board (STM32H743, ArduPilot) ─────────────────────┐
 │ TIM1 CH1..CH4 ──► DShot600 bidir (half-duplex, 1 wire/motor) ──────────────┼──► ESC board
 └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
 ┌────────────────────── 4-in-1 ESC board ────────────────────────────────────┐
 │ 4 × ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32 firmware, 48 kHz PWM)              │
 │ 4 × 3-phase gate driver (FD6288Q-class) → 24 × N-MOSFET (PDFN 3.3×3.3)      │
 └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

AM32 supports a fixed 48 kHz PWM frequency and bidirectional DShot (eRPM telemetry). BLHeli_32 is end-of-life and is not recommended for new designs.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (Trapezoidal 6-Step, Complementary PWM)

### 1.1 Current paths

During any 60° commutation sector, phase A is PWM-driven and phase B is held low:

| PWM state | Conducting devices | Count |
|---|---|---|
| ON (fraction D) | Q_AH → motor → Q_BL | 2 |
| OFF (1 − D), synchronous rectification | Q_AL → motor → Q_BL | 2 |

So **exactly two MOSFETs carry the full phase current at all times**, independent of duty cycle (dead time is handled in §2.3). Assuming a flat-top trapezoidal current, I_rms ≈ I_pk:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{pk}^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence and self-consistency

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds(on),25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.2 × 1.45 ≈ **1.74 mΩ** (use 1.8 mΩ for margin).

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.8\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{23.0\ W\ per\ channel}$$

Because T_j depends on P and P depends on T_j, the equations must be solved together:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + R_{\theta}\,P(T_j) \;\Rightarrow\; T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + R_\theta P_{25}(1-25\alpha)}{1 - R_\theta P_{25}\alpha}$$

**Thermal runaway criterion:** a stable solution exists only if R_θ · P_25 · α < 1.

- With P_25 = 15.4 W per channel (at 1.2 mΩ), runaway occurs above R_θ = 14.4 K/W.
- This is why the steady-state thermal path in §4 cannot sustain 80 A at all.

### 1.3 Per-device distribution

Over one electrical cycle, each low-side FET conducts continuously for 120° (as the "held" leg) and for an additional (1 − D) share during its PWM sectors. Under worst-case low-duty, high-current conditions (punch-out at low throttle with high current is rare, so D ≈ 0.85–1.0 is typical at 80 A):

| Quantity | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Instantaneous per-FET loss | I²R | 6400 × 1.8 mΩ = **11.5 W** |
| Average per FET (½ of the six FETs active at any instant → 2 of 6) | 23.0 W / 6 | ≈ **3.8 W** |

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. The held leg commutates at the electrical frequency (≈ 1–3 kHz), which is negligible.
