# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #15): Power, Thermal & DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope and Ground Rules

**Partitioning.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller. It runs ArduPilot and generates bidirectional DShot600. The 4-in-1 ESC runs its own MCU, assumed here to be an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 (or BLHeli_32-class firmware). That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The H743 never drives the gates directly.

**Assumptions.** Device parameters are flagged as `[ASSUMED]`. They represent a typical 40 V, ~1 mΩ, 5×6 mm or 3.3×3.3 mm class MOSFET. Substitute values from the actual BOM's datasheet before releasing the design.

**Key finding up front.** An 80 A per-motor burst on a nano-format (20×20 or 30×30) 4-in-1 is a short transient rating only, a few seconds at most. The math below shows the board's continuous capability is roughly 30–35 A per motor even with good prop-wash cooling. Section 3.4 quantifies this.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{DS} = 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega \times (1 + 0.005 \times 100) = 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega$$

### 2.2 Current path in 6-step synchronous PWM

In every commutation sector, two phases are active:

- **PWM leg:** current alternates between the high-side FET (duty D) and the synchronous low-side FET (1 − D). Because of the complementary drive, exactly one FET of this leg conducts at any instant, apart from dead time.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET is held fully on.

The motor current I therefore always passes through **exactly two channel resistances in series**, independent of D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j) + I_{rms}^2 \cdot R_{Cu} + I_{rms}^2 \cdot R_{shunt}$$

### 2.3 Parasitic copper and shunt

- **Copper path, R_Cu.** Assume 2 oz (70 µm) outer layers with about 15 mm of effective path per phase, 4 mm wide. Copper resistivity is ρ_Cu = 1.72×10⁻⁸ Ω·m, rising to about 2.4×10⁻⁸ Ω·m at 125 °C.

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{w t} = \frac{2.4\times10^{-8} \times 0.015}{0.004 \times 70\times10^{-6}} \approx 1.29\,\text{m}\Omega \;(\text{single layer})$$

  With two paralleled internal and external layers and two traversed path segments, the effective value is about **1.0 mΩ**. This is comparable to the FETs themselves, which is why heavy copper and via stitching matter as much as MOSFET choice.
- **Shunt.** R_shunt = 0.25 mΩ, shared on the bus return or per motor.

### 2.4 Conduction loss at 80 A burst

$$P_{FET,cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 19.2\,\text{W per motor}$$

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega = 6.4\,\text{W}, \qquad P_{shunt} = 80^2 \times 0.25\,\text{m}\Omega = 1.6\,\text{W}$$

Each FET in the always-on return position dissipates the most:

$$P_{FET,max} = 80^2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 9.6\,\text{W}$$

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Losses are hard-switched on the high-side turn-on edge, with the low-side body diode recovering.

### 3.1 Loss components (per motor)

**V–I overlap (hard switching):**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\times10^{-9} \times 48000 = 0.97\,\text{W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period):**

$$P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\times10^{-9} \times 48000 = 0.61\,\text{W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\times10^{-9} \times 25.2 \times 48000 = 0.06\,\text{W}$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\times10^{-9}\times 25.2\times48000 = 0.05\,\text{W}$$

**Gate drive (2 switching FETs, dissipated mostly in the driver):**

$$P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\,\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48000 = 0.058\,\text{W}$$

$$\boxed{P_{sw,motor} \approx 1.75\,\text{W}}$$

**Design insight.** At 48 kHz, switching loss is only about 8% of conduction loss at 80 A. The 48 kHz choice costs roughly 0.9 W per motor versus 24 kHz, in exchange for lower current ripple and quieter motors. Dead time is the largest controllable switching term: tuning it from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per motor.

### 3.2 Total burst dissipation

| Term | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Copper | 6.4 W | 25.6 W |
| Shunt | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| Switching | 1.75 W | 7.0 W |
| **Total** | **28.95 W** | **≈116 W** |

Efficiency at the burst point: if the output is about 25.2 V × 80 A × D_eff with D_eff ≈ 0.9, that is roughly 1.8 kW per motor, so the ~29 W loss is about 1.6% per motor. The electrical efficiency is excellent; **the limit is purely thermal density**.

### 3.3 Junction temperature at the hottest FET

The constant-on return FET dissipates 9.6 W, plus about 0.5 W allocated from switching. Assume:

- R_th,JC = 1.2 K/W `[ASSUMED]`
- R_th,C-PCB (solder plus thermal vias) ≈ 2.0 K/W

$$T_j = T_{PCB} + P(R_{JC} + R_{C-PCB}) = T_{PCB} + 10.1 \times 3.2 = T_{PCB} + 32\,\text{K}$$

The PCB must stay below **118 °C** to keep T_j under 150 °C (absolute max is typically 175 °C). The recommended design limit is T_PCB ≤ 100 °C, which keeps T_j at or below 132 °C.
