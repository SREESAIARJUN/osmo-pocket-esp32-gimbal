# FOC Motor Control and Dual-IMU Fusion for a 3-Axis Gimbal

I don't have the specification for Job #48, so this design states its assumptions explicitly. Every parameter is a named constant, so you can substitute your real values.

## 0. Assumptions and Timing Architecture

| Item | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Motors | Gimbal BLDC, 12N14P (p = 7 pole pairs), R ≈ 5 Ω, L ≈ 2 mH, K_t ≈ 0.1 N·m/A |
| Position sensor | 14-bit absolute magnetic encoder per axis (e.g., AS5048A or MT6701) |
| MCU | STM32G4/H7-class with FPU, center-aligned PWM, ADC triggered at the PWM valley |
| IMUs | Two ICM-42688-P on the camera plate, mounted symmetrically about the payload centroid (r₂ ≈ −r₁), sharing a common CLKIN |

The loops are rate-separated as follows:

| Loop | Rate | Bandwidth target |
|---|---|---|
| Current (FOC d/q PI) | 20 kHz | ≈ 1.5–2 kHz |
| Velocity PI, cogging feedforward, encoder PLL | 4 kHz | ≈ 150–250 Hz |
| Attitude (angle) loop, IMU fusion | 1–2 kHz | ≈ 30–50 Hz |
| IMU sampling (FIFO burst) | 8 kHz ODR, decimated | n/a |

Rule of thumb: each outer loop's bandwidth should be at most 1/5 to 1/10 of the inner loop's bandwidth.

## 1. FOC Current Loop

### 1.1 Transforms

Use the amplitude-invariant Clarke transform with i_a + i_b + i_c = 0:

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}(i_a + 2 i_b)
$$

Park transform, with electrical angle θ_e = p·θ_m − θ_offset:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix}=
\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}
$$

The inverse Park transform is the transpose of that matrix.

### 1.2 Plant and PI Tuning

The surface-PM machine model in the dq frame is:

$$
v_d = R i_d + L\dot i_d - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad
v_q = R i_q + L\dot i_q + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e \lambda_m
$$

The cross-coupling and back-EMF terms are removed by feedforward decoupling:

$$
v_d^{ff} = -\omega_e L i_q,\qquad v_q^{ff} = \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e\lambda_m,\qquad \lambda_m = \tfrac{2K_t}{3p}
$$

After decoupling, each axis reduces to G(s) = 1/(Ls + R
