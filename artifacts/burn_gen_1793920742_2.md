## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Flags

Several brief items need settling before the math, because they change the results.

1. **The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In every production 4-in-1 stack, the H743 runs ArduPilot and emits DShot. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071 / EFM8) running AM32 or BLHeli. **The 48 kHz PWM is generated by those ESC MCUs**, not the H743. The model below follows this split.
2. **"80A" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is a transient rating only.** The thermal section shows a 20×20 board reaches its limit in about 1 s at 80 A per channel. 80 A ratings are physically credible only on 30.5×30.5 mm boards with paralleled or large-die FETs, and even then only as bursts.
3. **Assumed operating point.** Every number below derives from this list. Swap values to re-run.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | V_bus | 6S, 25.2 V max (worst case for switching loss) |
| Burst phase current | I | 80 A per channel |
| MOSFET (40 V class, e.g. 3.3×3.3 PQFN) | R_ds(on),25 | 1.1 mΩ @ V_gs = 10 V |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance
$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$
At T_j = 110 °C: R = 1.1 mΩ × 1.4675 ≈ **1.61 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Instantaneous loss per channel
In each 60° commutation step, current flows through one phase's high-side/low-side pair (PWM leg, duty D) and the opposite phase's low-side FET (on continuously):

$$P_{cond} = I^2\Big[\underbrace{D\,R_{HS} + (1-D)\,R_{LS}}_{\text{PWM leg}} + \underbrace{R_{LS}}_{\text{return leg}}\Big] + I^2 R_{par}$$

R_par is the parasitic path resistance: shunt, copper pours, and solder joints. A typical value is about 0.5 mΩ.

With R_HS = R_LS = R:
$$P_{cond} = I^2\,(2R + R_{par})$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 110 °C
- MOSFETs: 6400 × 2 × 1.61 mΩ = **20.6 W**
- Parasitics: 6400 × 0.5 mΩ = **3.2 W**
- **Total per channel: ≈ 23.8 W**
- **Four channels: ≈ 95 W**

### 1.4 Per-FET average
Over a full electrical cycle, each of the 6 FETs conducts for 120°:

$$\bar P_{FET} = \frac{2 I^2 R}{6} = \frac{I^2R}{3} \approx 3.4\ \text{W}$$

The die thermal time constant (~ms) averages this at typical eRPM.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In 6-step drive, one leg switches at a time. Per channel:

$$P_{sw} = \underbrace{\tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f}_{V\text{-}I\ overlap} + \underbrace{\tfrac12 C_{oss} V^2 f}_{C_{oss}} + \underbrace{Q_{rr} V f}_{recovery} + \underbrace{2 V_f I\, t_{dt} f}_{dead\text{-}time\ diode} + \underbrace{2 Q_g V_{gs} f}_{gate\ (HS+LS)}$$

| Term | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 20 ns × 48 k | 0.968 W |
| C_oss | 0.5 × 1.5 nF × 25.2² × 48 k | 0.023 W |
| Q_rr | 60 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.073 W |
| Dead-time body diode | 2 × 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k | **1.229 W** |
| Gate drive (lost in driver, not FET) | 2 × 50 nC × 10 × 48 k | 0.048 W |
| **Total** | | **≈ 2.34 W per channel** |

**Key insight:** dead-time diode conduction is the largest switching-related term. Cutting t_dt from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per channel. This is AM32's dead-time setting, but it must not drop below the driver propagation mismatch plus FET t_d(off).

### 2.1 Frequency scaling
$$P_{sw}(f) \approx 2.34\,\text{W} \times \frac{f}{48\,\text{kHz}}$$
- 24 kHz: about 1.2 W
- 96 kHz: about 4.7 W

At 80 A, conduction dominates by roughly 10:1, so 48 kHz is an acceptable trade for low-inductance motors.
