# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC: Reference Architecture (Job #33)

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Corrections

Several points in the brief need clarifying before the math, because they change it.

| Topic | Brief | Engineering position |
|---|---|---|
| ESC MCU | "STM32H743 4-in-1 ESC" | The **H743 is the flight controller**. Each ESC channel needs its own small commutation MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32). The H743 cannot do 4× sensorless 6-step commutation at 48 kHz *and* run ArduPilot's EKF3 reliably. The design below is an AIO stack or two-board stack: H743 FC plus 4× G071 ESC. |
| "Nano" + 80A | 20×20 mm | 4×80A burst on 20×20 mm is not thermally credible (see §3.4). **I've specified 30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz.** 80A is treated as a **burst (≤5 s) phase current per channel**, not continuous. |
| Bus voltage | not given | **6S LiPo, V_bus,max = 25.2 V.** 40 V FETs with RC snubbers. For 8S, move to 60 V FETs and redo §2 with the higher R_DS(on). |
| 48 kHz | not specified | Treated as the **ESC PWM frequency**, set via AM32 `PWM_FREQ`. It is unrelated to DShot. |

Every number below is derived from stated parameters. Swap in your actual datasheet values and the model still holds.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Commutation topology

In 6-step trapezoidal drive, two phases conduct per 60° sector:
- **Phase A (PWM phase):** HS and LS alternate at duty D (complementary).
- **Phase B (sink phase):** LS is held on.

So at any instant, current flows through exactly **two FETs in series**.

### 2.2 Conduction loss

Including PWM ripple (motor L_ph ≈ 15 µH):

$$
\Delta I_{pp}=\frac{(V_{bus}-E)\,D}{2L_{ph}f_{sw}}, \qquad
I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2+\frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}
$$

At D = 0.75 with back-EMF E ≈ 0.7·V_bus:

$$
\Delta I_{pp}=\frac{7.56\cdot0.75}{2\cdot15\mu\cdot48k}=3.9\text{ A}
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
I_{rms}^2=6400+1.3\approx6401\text{ A}^2
$$

Ripple is negligible at 48 kHz, which is one benefit of the high PWM frequency.

Channel conduction loss:

$$
P_{cond}=I_{rms}^2\,R(T_j)\,[\underbrace{D+(1-D)}_{\text{PWM leg}}+\underbrace{1}_{\text{sink leg}}]=2\,I_{rms}^2\,R(T_j)
$$

| T_j | R(T_j) | P_cond / channel |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W |
| 100 °C | 1.41 mΩ | 18.1 W |
| 125 °C | 1.55 mΩ | **19.8 W** |

Add PCB copper resistance. A 2 oz, 8-layer phase/bus path is about 0.25 mΩ effective:

$$
P_{Cu}=2\cdot6400\cdot0.25\text{ m}\Omega=3.2\text{ W/channel}
$$

Note that P_Cu heats the board, not the junctions.

**Per-FET split over one electrical cycle** (each FET is sink-LS for 1/3, PWM-leg for 1/3, idle for 1/3):

- HS FET: $\tfrac13 D\,I^2R = \tfrac13(0.75)(6400)(1.55\text{m}) = 2.48$ W
- LS FET: $\tfrac13\big[(1-D)+1\big]I^2R = \tfrac13(1.25)(9.92) = 4.13$ W

**The LS FETs run hottest.** Prioritise their thermal vias.

### 2.3 Switching loss at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-leg HS switches hard. The LS commutates softly via the body diode.

**Gate current at the Miller plateau:**

$$
I_{g,on}=\frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}+R_{drv}}=\frac{12-3.2}{2.2+1.0+4}=1.22\text{ A}
\qquad
I_{g,off}=\frac{3.2}{2.2+1.0+2}=0.62\text{ A}
$$

**Transition times:**

$$
t_r=\frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}}=\frac{18n}{1.22}=14.8\text{ ns},\qquad
t_f=\frac{18n}{0.62}=29\text{ ns}
$$
