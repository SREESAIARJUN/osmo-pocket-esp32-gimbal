## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.2 × (1 + 0.006 × 100) = **1.92 mΩ**

### 2.2 Current path in a 6-step bridge with synchronous rectification

At every instant, exactly two FETs carry the full phase current I:
- During t_on: high-side (HS) of phase A plus low-side (LS) of phase B.
- During t_off: sync LS of phase A plus LS of phase B.

Therefore the per-channel conduction loss is independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 2.3 Per-FET distribution

Over one electrical cycle, each phase leg is active for 120° (1/3 of the time) in each current direction.

| FET role | RMS² current | Loss at 80 A, 125 °C |
|---|---|---|
| "Sink" LS (on for the full 1/3 period) | I²/3 | 6400 × 1.92 m / 3 = **4.10 W** |
| PWM HS (fraction D of the 1/3 period) | D·I²/3 | 4.10·D W |
| Sync LS of the PWM leg | (1−D)·I²/3 | 4.10·(1−D) W |
| Peak single FET | — | ≈ **4.1 W** (plus switching loss, see §3) |

### 2.4 Totals

$$P_{cond,ch} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.92\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{24.6\ W}$$

$$P_{cond,board} = 4 \times 24.6 = \mathbf{98.3\ W}$$

### 2.5 Copper path losses (often ignored, and that is a mistake)

Phase plus bus trace resistance, estimated at about 0.3 mΩ per channel:

80² × 0.3 m ≈ **1.9 W/ch → 7.7 W total**

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Losses below are per channel.

**3.1 Voltage–current overlap (hard switching, inductive load)**

$$P_{ov} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(27\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = \mathbf{1.31\ W}$$

**3.2 Body-diode reverse recovery**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

The recovery event also adds turn-on current overshoot to the HS FET. Budget roughly +10 % on P_ov, which gives ~0.13 W.

**3.3 Output capacitance (C_oss) discharge**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,eff}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5(1.2\text{n})(635)(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.018\ W}$$

**3.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction**

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{n}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

This term is significant. Reducing dead time to 50 ns halves it, but shoot-through margin must be verified on the bench.

**3.5 Gate drive loss (dissipated in the driver and Rg, not the FET)**

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2(50\text{n})(10)(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 3.6 Switching loss summary

| Term | Per channel | Board (×4) |
|---|---|---|
| Overlap + recovery overshoot | 1.44 W | 5.76 W |
| Reverse recovery | 0.05 W | 0.19 W |
| C_oss | 0.02 W | 0.07 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W | 2.46 W |
| Gate drive | 0.05 W | 0.19 W |
| **Total switching** | **2.17 W** | **8.7 W** |

### 3.7 Frequency sensitivity

Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw: P_sw(f) ≈ 2.17 × (f / 48 kHz) W per channel. At 24 kHz this is about 1.1 W.

At 80 A, conduction loss dominates. Lowering f_sw buys little here, but it increases current ripple and acoustic noise.

### 3.8 Bulk capacitor ripple heating

$$I_{C,rms} \approx I\sqrt{D(1-D)} \le 0.5I = 40\text{ A per channel (worst case at } D=0.5)$$

Capacitor loss is I²·ESR. With 4 × 470 µF low-ESR polymer caps sharing the load (~10 mΩ effective), this is about 4 W per channel at worst case. You must add MLCC decoupling at each bridge (≥ 4 × 10 µF/50 V X7R per channel) and an external low-ESR bulk electrolytic on the battery leads.
