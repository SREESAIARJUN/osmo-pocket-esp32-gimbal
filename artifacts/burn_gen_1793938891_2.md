# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80 A-Class 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model (Job #41)

**Scope.** This is a first-pass engineering model. It is not a release-ready design package. The MOSFET, gate-driver and board parameters below are representative assumptions, chosen so the math is traceable. Every boxed parameter must be replaced with your selected parts' datasheet values, and the result must be validated with thermocouple/IR testing under load. The netlists are reference-level and must be captured, ERC/DRC-checked and reviewed before fabrication.

## 0. Architecture Decisions and Red Flags

| Item | Decision / Finding |
|---|---|
| Partitioning | The H743 runs ArduPilot. The 4-in-1 ESC has its own MCU (e.g., AT32F421 / STM32G071 running AM32, or BLHeli_32-class). The **48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCU, not the H743.** The H743 only sends bidirectional DShot600. |
| Topology | 4 × three-phase bridges, 24 MOSFETs, synchronous (complementary) 6-step PWM. |
| Bus | 6S LiPo: 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal. 40 V MOSFETs are the minimum; 60 V is preferred if spikes exceed ~32 V. |
| **Red flag 1** | "80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 / 25.5×25.5) footprint is not physically sustainable beyond a few seconds (see §3). Treat 80 A as a **per-motor, single-digit-second burst**, not a rating. |
| **Red flag 2** | 4 × 80 A simultaneously means 320 A from the pack. The battery pads, XT60/XT90 connector and bus copper are the limit, not the FETs. A realistic simultaneous-burst budget is about 4 × 45–55 A. |
| **Red flag 3** | Bus-capacitor ripple current reaches ~40 A RMS per bridge (§2.6). Low-ESR polymer plus a bulk electrolytic at the pads is mandatory. |
