## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.0 mΩ × 1.60 = **1.6 mΩ**

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step synchronous drive

In any commutation sector, two phases are active:
- **PWM phase:** the high-side (on-time D) and the complementary low-side (1−D) carry current alternately. That makes one FET-equivalent conducting 100% of the time.
- **Return phase:** the low-side is on continuously.

So the current always flows through exactly **two R_DS(on)**, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.6\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{20.5\ W\ per\ motor}$$

Averaged over the 6 FETs per channel through commutation, this is about **3.4 W per FET**. The instantaneous peak in one FET while it conducts is 10.2 W.

### 1.3 Copper / PCB trace loss

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{trace}, \quad R_{trace} = \frac{\rho\,L}{w\,t\,n_{layers}}$$

Assume a 15 mm phase path, 4 mm width, 4 paralleled 70 µm layers, ρ_Cu(100 °C) ≈ 2.2×10⁻⁸ Ω·m:

$$R = \frac{2.2\times10^{-8}\times0.015}{0.004\times70\times10^{-6}\times4} \approx 0.29\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{Cu} \approx 80^2 \times 0.29\text{m}\Omega \times 2\ \text{(in+out path)} \approx \mathbf{3.7\ W\ per\ motor}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

### 2.1 Hard-switching V-I overlap (one switching FET per PWM cycle)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I\, (t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.024\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Body-diode conduction during dead time (two dead-time events per cycle)

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2\,t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g)

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2 = 50\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k}\times2 = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Loss summary

| Loss | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 20.5 W | 82.0 W |
| Copper | 3.7 W | 14.8 W |
| Switching overlap | 1.45 W | 5.8 W |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 W | 4.9 W |
| Qrr + Coss + gate | 0.13 W | 0.5 W |
| **Total** | **≈27 W** | **≈108 W** |

**Efficiency at 80 A burst:** P_out ≈ 4 × 80 × 22.2 ≈ 7.1 kW, so η ≈ 98.5%. The losses are small relative to throughput, but large relative to the tiny thermal mass.

**Design insight:** Conduction dominates at about 76%. Dropping to 48 kHz from higher frequencies helps little. The real levers are:
- Lower R_DS(on) (paralleled FETs halve P_cond)
- Shorter dead time (≈100 ns saves about 0.6 W/motor)

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network (per FET, Cauer simplified)

```
P_FET ──[R_jc=1.2 K/W]──┬──[R_cb=2 K/W solder+vias]──┬──[R_ba ≈ 8 K/W board→air, shared]── T_amb
                        │                            │
                    C_j ≈0.05 J/K              C_board ≈ 4.5 J/K (shared)
```

### 3.2 Board thermal capacitance

- Copper: 8 layers × 70 µm × 9.3 cm² × 60% fill ≈ 0.31 cm³ → 2.8 g × 0.385 J/g·K ≈ **1.1 J/K**
- FR-4: ≈1.5 cm³ × 1.85 g/cm³ × 1.1 J/g·K ≈ **3.0 J/K**
- FETs + caps: ≈ **0.4 J/K**
- **C_board ≈ 4.5 J/K**

### 3.3 Adiabatic burst (props generate airflow, but assume worst case)

$$\frac{dT_{board}}{dt} = \frac{P_{total}}{C_{board}} = \frac{108}{4.5} \approx \mathbf{24\ K/s}$$

### 3.4 Junction above board

$$\Delta T_{jb} = P_{FET,peak}(R_{jc}+R_{cb}) = 10.2 \times 3.2 \approx 33\ \text{K}$$

This is the worst case while a FET carries continuous current within a sector. The junction time constant is milliseconds and the commutation period is about 0.1–1 ms, so the average is closer to 3.4 W × 3.2 ≈ 11 K.

### 3.5 Allowed burst duration

Constraints: T_amb = 40 °C, T_j,max = 150 °C, derated target 125 °C.

$$t_{burst} = \frac{(125 - 33) - 40}{24} \approx \mathbf{2.2\ s}\quad(\text{worst case}),\qquad \approx 3\ \text{s}\ (\text{averaged } \Delta T_{jb})$$

With forced prop-wash convection (h ≈ 50–100 W/m²K over about 20 cm² of exposed area), steady-state removal is only about 0.1–0.2 W/K. Convection barely affects a 2–3 s burst.
