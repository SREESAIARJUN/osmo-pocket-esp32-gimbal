# STM32H743 AIO + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #7): Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and Bidirectional DShot600 DMA Allocation

## 0. Feasibility Findings

These findings constrain the rest of the design.

| # | Finding | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | **80 A on all four channels at once draws about 320 A from the battery** (about 7 kW at 6S). | This exceeds XT60 (60 A continuous) and XT90 ratings. The burst rating must be defined as per-channel, short-duration, asymmetric. |
| F2 | A "nano" form factor (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) cannot hold 80 A per channel thermally, even for bursts longer than about 1 s. | The model below assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm** board. On 20×20 mm, derate to roughly 35–45 A burst. |
| F3 | The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. | Each ESC channel runs on its own MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071 running AM32, or EFM8 running BLHeli_S/Bluejay). The H743 only generates DShot and captures eRPM telemetry. |
| F4 | All MOSFET parameters below are **representative placeholders** for a 40 V, ~1 mΩ, 3.3×3.3 or 5×6 mm DFN part. | Rerun the model with values from the datasheet of the part you actually choose. |
| F5 | Co-locating the H743 (about 0.5–0.8 W at 480 MHz) with the power stage reduces IMU thermal stability. | Use a split stack, or thermally isolate the IMU with a heater-stabilized pad. |

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

In 6-step drive, two phases conduct at any instant: one high-side FET (HS) and one low-side FET (LS).

- **During t_on (D·T):** current flows through HS(a) and LS(b).
- **During t_off ((1–D)·T), with synchronous rectification:** current freewheels through LS(a) and LS(b).

So **two R_DS(on) are always in series with the phase current**, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12},\qquad \Delta I_{pp} \approx \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

**Ripple check.** Take L_ph = 15 µH (typical 2207-class motor, line-to-line ÷ 2) at D = 0.5:
ΔI = 25.2 × 0.25 / (15e-6 × 48e3) = 8.75 A_pp, which gives ΔI²/12 = 6.4 A². This is negligible against 6400 A².

**At T_j = 110 °C:** R = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0045 × 85) = 1.38 mΩ

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 6406 \times 1.38\text{e-}3 = \mathbf{17.7\ W}$$

**Per-FET share.** Over one electrical revolution each of the 6 FETs carries roughly 1/3 (HS) to 2/3 (LS, including freewheel) of the conduction time. The LS FETs therefore run hotter: about 4 W versus about 2 W for HS.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one FET per commutation sector hard-switches: the PWM'd high-side. The LS partner commutates at zero voltage through its body diode.

**Hard-switching overlap**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 50\text{e-}9 \times 48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{2.42\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery** (body diode of the sync FET)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{e-}9 \times 25.2 \times 48\text{e}3 = 0.048\ W$$

Add induced HS turn-on loss of about 0.5 × I_rr × V × t_rr, which is roughly 0.05 W.

**Output capacitance**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{e-}9 \times 635 \times 48\text{e}3 = 0.023\ W$$
