# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Reference Engineering Package

**Scope:** loss model, thermal model, DShot DMA allocation, and reference netlists.
**Status:** This is a pre-layout reference design. Every number derives from the stated assumptions. Replace them with your selected-part datasheet values and validate on hardware before release.

---

## 0. Architecture Correction (Read First)

The STM32H743 should **not** commutate the motors directly. For bi-directional DShot, ArduPilot expects each ESC channel to run its own firmware: AM32 or BLHeli_32 on an AT32F421 or STM32G071, for example. Each channel then returns eRPM telemetry.

The correct partition is:

```
[H743 FC] --DShot600 bidir (x4)--> [ESC MCU] --> [3-ph gate driver] --> [6x MOSFET] --> motor
```

The "48 kHz" PWM frequency is therefore an **ESC firmware setting**. In AM32 it is configurable (24/48/96 kHz). It is not an H743 timer.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j)=R_0\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 0.9 × 1.40 ≈ **1.26 mΩ**. Use 1.35 mΩ as the design value to cover datasheet spread.

### 2.2 Current path

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, current always flows through **one high-side and one low-side device in series**. During the PWM off-time, the complementary FET carries the current instead of the body diode, so two R_DS(on) values are always in the path:

$$P_{cond}=I^2\,(2R_{DS}+R_{pcb})$$

### 2.3 Per channel at 80 A

- FETs: 6400 × 2 × 1.35 mΩ = **17.3 W**
- Copper and shunt: 6400 × 0.5 mΩ = **3.2 W**

Conduction is distributed across the six FETs over one electrical cycle. Each FET conducts about 1/3 of the cycle in 120° commutation, split with its complement by duty.

- Average per FET: ≈ 17.3 / 6 ≈ **2.9 W**
- Peak instantaneous while conducting: ≈ 8.6 W

## 3. Switching Loss Model @ 48 kHz

In 6-step operation only one half-bridge is PWM-switching at a time.

### 3.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap

$$P_{sw}=\tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}=0.5(25.2)(80)(30\text{n})(48\text{k})=\mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 3.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt}=V_f\,I\,(2t_d)\,f_{sw}=0.8(80)(400\text{n})(48\text{k})=\mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

### 3.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}=60\text{n}(25.2)(48\text{k})=\mathbf{0.073\ W}$$

### 3.4 Output capacitance (two devices per leg)

$$P_{oss}=2\cdot\tfrac12 C_{oss}V^2 f=1.5\text{n}(635)(48\text{k})=\mathbf{0.046\ W}$$

### 3.5 Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors)

$$P_g=2\,Q_gV_{GS}f=2(80\text{n})(10)(48\text{k})=\mathbf{0.077\ W}$$

### 3.6 Frequency sensitivity

Switching-related losses scale linearly with frequency. They are about 2.8 W at 48 kHz.

| f_sw | Switching-related loss | Per channel total |
|---|---|---|
| 24 kHz | 1.4 W | 21.9 W |
| 48 kHz | 2.8 W | 23.3 W |
| 96 kHz | 5.6 W | 26.1 W |

**Insight:** at 80 A the design is conduction-dominated (about 88%). Lowering R_DS(on) and copper resistance matters more than lowering frequency. Cutting dead time to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per channel, provided the driver's propagation matching allows it.

## 4. Total Loss Budget at 80 A Burst

| Component | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 17.3 W | 69.2 W |
| Copper / shunt | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| Switching + dead time + Q_rr + C_oss | 2.8 W | 11.2 W |
| Gate drive | 0.08 W | 0.3 W |
| **Total** | **≈23.4 W** | **≈93.5 W** |

Efficiency check per channel: P_out ≈ 25.2 V × 80 A × duty. At full throttle, about 2 kW in and 23 W lost gives roughly 98.8%. The efficiency is good. The problem is power density.

## 5. Thermal Model

### 5.1 Network (per FET, lumped)

```
P_fet ─► Tj ─[Rθjc ≈1.0 K/W]─► Tc ─[Rθcb ≈1.5 K/W (vias+solder)]─► T_board ─[Rθba]─► T_amb
              (Cj ~ ms τ)                                          (C_board)
```

### 5.2 Steady-state check (shows why 80 A is burst-only)

Assume a 30.5 × 30.5 mm nano 4-in-1 at about 7 g with 2 oz copper.

- With prop-wash convection, the whole board's R_θba is about 2.5–4 K/W.
- At 93.5 W, ΔT_steady = 93.5 × 3 ≈ **280 K**.

That is physically impossible, so **80 A per motor cannot be continuous** on a nano-format board. The realistic continuous rating is about 25–35 A per channel. See 5.5.

### 5.3 Burst (adiabatic board heating)

Board heat capacity: C_b ≈ m·c_p,eff ≈ 7 g × 0.75 J/(g·K) ≈ **5.3 J/K**. Copper raises this slightly, FR-4 lowers it.

Solving the first-order RC:

$$T_b(t)=T_{amb}+P\,R_{\theta ba}\left(1-e^{-t/(R_{\theta ba}C_b)}\right)$$

With τ = 3 × 5.3 ≈ 16 s, the heating rate is roughly P/C_b ≈ 17.6 K/s for t ≪ τ.

| Burst duration | Board ΔT | T_board (from 40 °C) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 s | 17 K | 57 °C |
| 2 s | 33 K | 73 °C |
| 3 s | 47 K | 87 °C |
| 5 s | 71 K | 111 °C |

### 5.4 Junction rise above board

Use peak instantaneous conduction of about 8.6 W per FET, plus switching loss on the PWM'd device.

- With R_θjb ≈ 2.5 K/W: ΔT_jb ≈ 8.6 × 2.5 ≈ 22 K.
- The die responds within milliseconds, so use the steady R_θjb.

### 5.5 Electrothermal iteration (R_DS rises with T_j)

Iterate: T_j → R_DS → P → T_j. At a 3 s burst:

| Iteration | T_j | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 87 + 22 | 109 °C |
| 2 | R_DS ≈ 1.20 mΩ | P_cond lower than the 1.35 mΩ design value, so converges at T_j ≈ 105 °C |

**Burst limit:** about **3 s at 80 A** from a 40 °C start keeps T_j at or below 125 °C with derating margin, where T_j,max = 150–175 °C.

**Continuous limit:** solving P(I)·R_θba ≤ 60 K gives P ≤ 20 W total, which is about **≤ 30 A per channel continuous**.

**Mitigations:**
- Enable ESC temperature protection, with AM32 or the ESC's temperature limit around 110 °C.
- Enable ArduPilot current limiting (`BATT_CURR_LIM`).
- Use an exposed-pad top-side heatsink.
