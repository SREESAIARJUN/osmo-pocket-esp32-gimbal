# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Reference Design Analysis (Job #1)

**Status:** Engineering model and reference netlist. It needs EVT bench validation, thermal imaging and DShot scope captures before release.
**Scope:** 6S (25.2 V max), 4-in-1 ESC rated 80 A burst per channel, 48 kHz PWM, ArduPilot with bidirectional DShot600.

---

## 0. Architecture Decisions

Three findings change the design before any calculation applies.

| # | Finding | Decision |
|---|---------|----------|
| A1 | The H743 cannot commutate 4 BLDC motors directly. Twenty-four complementary gate outputs would be needed, but only TIM1 and TIM8 are advanced timers (2 motors' worth). HRTIM adds 10 outputs that cannot cleanly form 2 more 3-phase bridges with BEMF zero-crossing. | Use **4 dedicated ESC MCUs** (e.g., AT32F421 running AM32). The H743 acts only as the flight controller and talks to them over bidirectional DShot600. |
| A2 | The "nano" (20×20 mm) footprint cannot hold 80 A per channel. Section 3 shows about 120 W of total dissipation at burst, which is several hundred watts per square inch. | Use a **30.5×30.5 mm ESC board** stacked under a 20×20 FC, or derate to roughly 40 A burst on 20×20. |
| A3 | 80 A is thermally a **burst** rating, not a continuous one. | The firmware must enforce a time-limited current (I²t limit, Section 3.4). |

Temperature-dependent resistance:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_ds = 1.5 × 1.51 = **2.27 mΩ**. Adding R_pcb gives an effective **R = 2.57 mΩ**.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

The commutation scheme is 6-step trapezoidal with high-side PWM and synchronous rectification (complementary low side).

Each leg spends one-third of the electrical period in each state:

- **High interval:** the high-side (HS) switch conducts for fraction D; the low-side (LS) switch carries the freewheel current for (1–D).
- **Low interval:** the LS switch is held on for the full interval.
- **Floating interval:** no current flows.

Conduction loss per switch:

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I^2 R,\qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(2-D)\, I^2 R$$

Summed over the leg, the duty terms cancel:

$$P_{leg} = \tfrac{1}{3} D I^2R + \tfrac{1}{3}(2-D) I^2R = \tfrac{2}{3} I^2 R$$

Over three legs:

$$\boxed{P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R(T_j)}$$

This matches the physical picture: at every instant, the current passes through exactly two conducting switch positions.

At 80 A and R = 2.57 mΩ:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 6400 \times 0.00257 = \mathbf{32.9\ W}$$

Worst single FET at D → 1 (full-throttle burst):

- LS: P = (1/3) × 6400 × 0.00257 = **5.5 W**
- HS: the same 5.5 W, plus switching loss.

### 2.2 Switching Loss

Only one leg switches at a time, and only its HS device hard-switches.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 30 ns × 48 k | 1.452 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw = 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.048 W |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss · V · f_sw (conservative) | 0.036 W |
| Dead-time diode | V_f · I · 2 · t_dt · f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 100 ns × 48 k | 0.307 W |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver/R_g) | 2 · Q_g · V
