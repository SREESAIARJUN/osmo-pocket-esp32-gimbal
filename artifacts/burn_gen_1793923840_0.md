# STM32H743 Nano AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC): Power-Stage Loss, Thermal, and Bidirectional DShot600 Design Package

**Document:** Job #0, Rev A (engineering baseline)
**Scope:** 80 A burst conduction loss, 48 kHz switching loss, transient thermal model, ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 timer and DMA allocation, and a reference netlist.

---

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

Two points need correcting before any numbers are meaningful.

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 AIO, the H743 runs ArduPilot. Commutation runs on separate ESC MCUs (AM32 or BLHeli_32 class, for example an STM32G071, AT32F421, or GD32E230), one per motor. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by those ESC MCUs. The H743's job is the bidirectional DShot600 link to them.
2. **80 A burst on a nano (20×20 mm) board is a seconds-scale transient, not a rating.** The thermal section shows the board's thermal mass supports roughly 1 s of 4×80 A, while continuous capability is roughly 20 A per motor. Treat "80 A" as a burst spec with an explicit duration, and enforce it in firmware.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current Path

In six-step commutation with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM, the AM32 default), current always flows through exactly two MOSFETs per motor:

- **During t_on:** the high-side FET of phase A, then the motor, then the low-side FET of phase B.
- **During t_off:** the low-side FET of phase A (synchronous), then the motor, then the low-side FET of phase B.

Within the active leg, the duty cycle D only shifts loss between the high-side and low-side FETs. The total does not change:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 \left[ D\,R_{HS} + (1-D)\,R_{LS,A} + R_{LS,B} \right] \approx 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds,on}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature Dependence (Electro-Thermal Coupling)

$$R_{ds,on}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha_R (T_j - 25)\right]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0065 × 100) = **1.65 mΩ**.

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.65\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{21.1\ W\ per\ motor}$$

Because R rises with temperature, the system is positive-feedback. Solve it iteratively:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_{board} + P(T_j^{(k)})\cdot R_{\theta JB},\qquad \text{stable iff } \frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j} R_{\theta JB} < 1$$

Here ∂P/∂T = 2I²R₂₅α = 2(6400)(0.001)(0.0065) = 0.083 W/K per motor, split across two FETs. With R_θJB = 2 K/W per FET, the loop gain is about 0.083 < 1. **It is stable, with no thermal runaway at the junction level.** Runaway can still occur at the board level if the burst duration is not limited (Section 4).

### 1.3 Copper and Shunt Conduction

| Element | R | Current | Loss |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phase trace/pad, 2 oz, ~8 mm, multilayer parallel | ~0.3 mΩ | 80 A | 1.9 W per motor |
| Current shunt (shared, 0.25 mΩ) | 0.25 mΩ | 320 A peak burst | 25.6 W at full 4×80 A |
| Battery pad to bus, 4 layers, 2 oz | ~0.2 mΩ | 320 A | 20.5 W |

**Design finding:** at 4×80 A, the shared bus copper and shunt dissipate as much as one full motor's FETs. Recommended changes:

- Use a 0.1–0.15 mΩ shunt (or Hall sensing) and accept lower ADC resolution.
- Stitch the bus across all inner layers with via arrays (≥ 40 vias per amp-node).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Each motor has one hard-switched leg (the PWM leg). The low-side FET of the conducting phase is static.

### 2.1 Overlap (V·I) Loss, Hard-Switched FET

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$
