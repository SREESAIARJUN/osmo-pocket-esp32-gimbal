# STM32H743 Nano AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC): Power-Stage, Thermal and DShot Architecture, Job #28

## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

### 0.1 Architectural correction

The STM32H743 cannot commutate the motors itself. It has no spare timer, ADC or comparator budget for four sensorless BLDC loops alongside ArduPilot. A production-correct partition is:

| Block | Device class | Role |
|---|---|---|
| FC MCU | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100), 480 MHz | ArduPilot, sends DShot600 to the ESCs, captures bidirectional (EDT) eRPM |
| ESC MCUs (×4) | STM32G071 / AT32F421 class | AM32 or BLHeli_32 firmware, 48 kHz complementary PWM, back-EMF commutation |
| Gate drivers (×4) | 3-phase half-bridge driver, about 1–2 A source/sink | One per ESC channel |
| MOSFETs (×24) | 40 V, 5×6 mm DFN class | 6 per channel |

### 0.2 Model parameters

Substitute datasheet values from the chosen MOSFET before release. Every equation below is parametric.

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 25.2 V | 6S, fully charged (worst case for switching loss) |
| I_pk | 80 A | Burst phase current per channel |
| f_sw | 48 kHz | PWM frequency |

### 0.3 Feasibility statement

On a 20×20 or 30.5×30.5 mm board, 80 A per channel is a burst rating (≤ 1–2 s), not a continuous one. Section 3 quantifies this.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

In six-step commutation with synchronous (complementary) PWM, two legs are active at any instant. Current always flows through exactly two channels in series:

- **PWM-on interval:** the high-side FET of phase A, then the motor, then the low-side FET of phase B.
- **PWM-off interval:** the low-side FET of phase A (synchronous freewheel), then the motor, then the low-side FET of phase B.

So, independent of duty D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ /^\circ C \Rightarrow k_T \approx 1.6 \text{ at } 125^\circ C$$

With the 120° trapezoidal approximation and burst current treated as flat-top, I_rms ≈ I_pk = 80 A for the conducting pair.

### 1.2 Numbers

| Term | Equation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| R_ds hot | 0.8 mΩ × 1.6 | 1.28 mΩ |
| P_cond (FETs) | 80² × 2 × 1.28 mΩ | **16.4 W / channel** |
| P_Cu (copper) | 80² × 0.3 mΩ | 1.9 W / channel |

### 1.3 Electrothermal positive feedback

Because R_ds rises with T_j, solve self-consistently:

$$T_j = T_a + P(T_j)\cdot Z_{th}(t), \qquad P(T_j) = I^2 \cdot 2R_{25}\left[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\right] + P_{sw}$$

Closed form for the steady state:

$$T_j = \frac{T_a + Z_{th}\left[2I^2R_{25}(1-25\alpha) + P_{sw}\right]}{1 - 2I^2R_{25}\alpha Z_{th}}$$

Thermal runaway occurs when the denominator reaches 0:

$$Z_{th,crit} = \frac{1}{2I^2R_{25}\alpha} = \frac{1}{2\cdot 6400 \cdot 0.0008 \cdot 0.006} \approx 16\ ^\circ C/W$$

The board-to-air thermal resistance per channel must therefore stay well below 16 K/W at 80 A, or the channel runs away even in steady state.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switched leg incurs hard-switching losses: one hard-switched transition pair per PWM period.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard-switching)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 35\,\text{ns} \cdot 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.69\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period)

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD} \cdot I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 400\,\text{ns} \cdot 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

This term is large. Dropping dead time to 100 ns halves it, but requires verified driver propagation-delay matching.

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\,\text{nC}\cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\,\text{kHz} = 0.06\ W$$

Recovery also adds roughly Q_rr·V of extra turn-on energy in the opposite FET. This is already folded into the effective t_r.

### 2.4 Output capacitance (both FETs in the leg)

$$P_{oss} = 2 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss(er)} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1.5\,\text{nF}\cdot 635 \cdot 48\,\text{kHz} = 0.05\ W$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g, not the FET)

$$P_g = Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} \cdot 2 = 60\,\text{nC}\cdot 10 \cdot 48\,\text{kHz}\cdot 2 = 0.06\ W$$

### 2.6 Loss budget at the 80 A burst

| Loss | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 16.4 W | 65.6 W |
| Copper | 1.9 W | 7.6 W |
| V–I switching | 1.69 W | 6.8 W |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 W | 4.9 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.17 W | 0.7 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 21.4 W** | **≈ 85.6 W** |

Efficiency check for one channel at D = 0.8: P_out ≈ 25.2 × 0.8 × 80 ≈ 1.6 kW, so 21.4 W is about 1.3 % loss. The efficiency is fine; the heat density on a nano PCB is the problem.

### 2.7 Frequency trade-off

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial f_{sw}} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) + 2V_{SD} I t_{dt} + Q_{rr}V + C_{oss}V^2 + 2Q_gV_{drv} \approx 61\ \mu W/Hz$$

Moving 24 → 48 kHz therefore costs about 1.5 W per channel. That is acceptable for the reduced current ripple and lower acoustic noise with low-inductance motors.
