## 0. Assumptions and Timing Architecture

| Item | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Motors | 3× gimbal BLDC (e.g. GBM2804), 12N14P → pole pairs `P = 7` |
| Winding | R = 5.6 Ω, L = 2.0 mH (phase-to-neutral), Kt ≈ 0.045 N·m/A |
| Encoder | 14-bit absolute magnetic encoder (MT6701/AS5048A) per axis |
| MCU | STM32G474 (FPU, CORDIC), Vbus = 12 V |
| IMUs | 2× ICM-42688-P on the camera plate, each on its own SPI, both driven from a shared external CLKIN (32 kHz) |

### Loop rates (cascade bandwidth separation of about 4–5× per stage)

```
 20 kHz  ── Current loop (FOC, PI on id/iq)          ωc ≈ 2π·1500 rad/s
  4 kHz  ── Velocity loop (PI, gyro feedback)        ωv ≈ 2π·60   rad/s
  4 kHz  ── IMU read (ODR 8 kHz, FIFO, decimated)
  1 kHz  ── Angle loop (P + small I, IMU attitude)   ωθ ≈ 2π·12   rad/s
  1 kHz  ── Attitude estimator (Mahony on fused IMU)
 100 Hz  ── Cogging LMS adaptation, notch re-tune, diagnostics
```

## 1. FOC Current Loop

### 1.1 Transforms

**Clarke transform** (amplitude-invariant, with `i_a + i_b + i_c = 0`):

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \frac{1}{\sqrt3}(i_a + 2 i_b)
$$

**Park transform** with electrical angle $\theta_e = P\,\theta_m - \theta_{\text{off}}$:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix}=
\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}
$$

### 1.2 Plant (PMSM in the dq frame)

$$
v_d = R i_d + L\frac{di_d}{dt} - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad
v_q = R i_q + L\frac{di_q}{dt} + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e\lambda_m
$$

Torque is $\tau = \tfrac32 P \lambda_m i_q = K_t i_q$. For a surface-mount motor, $L_d = L_q$ and reluctance torque is zero.

### 1.3 PI controller with decoupling and back-EMF feedforward

Pole-zero cancellation of the RL plant, $G(s) = 1/(Ls+R)$, gives:

$$
K_{p,i} = L\,\omega_c,\qquad K_{i,i} = R\,\omega_c
\;\;\Rightarrow\;\; \frac{i}{i^*} = \frac{\omega_c}{s+\omega_c}
$$

The resulting control law is:

$$
v_d^* = \text{PI}_d(e_d) - \omega_e L\, i_q,\qquad
v_q^* = \text{PI}_q(e_q) + \omega_e L\, i_d + \omega_e \lambda_m
$$

### 1.4 Voltage limiting and anti-windup

The voltage vector is limited to the linear SVPWM circle:

$$
|v_{dq}| \le V_{\max} = \frac{V_{\text{bus}}}{\sqrt3}\cdot 0.95
$$

The d-axis has priority:

$$
v_d \leftarrow \text{clamp}(v_d,\pm V_{\max}),\qquad
v_q \leftarrow \text{clamp}\!\left(v_q,\pm\sqrt{V_{\max}^2 - v_d^2}\right)
$$

Integrators use back-calculation:

$$
x_I \mathrel{+}= T_s\left[K_i e + K_{aw}(v_{\text{sat}} - v_{\text{unsat}})\right],\qquad K_{aw} = K_i/K_p
$$

### 1.5 Inverse Park and SVPWM (min-max zero-sequence injection)

First compute the three phase voltages $v_a, v_b, v_c$ via the inverse Park and inverse Clarke transforms. Then inject the zero-sequence offset:

$$
v_0 = -\tfrac12\left(\max(v_{abc}) + \min(v_{abc})\right),\qquad
d_x = \tfrac12 + \frac{v_x + v_0}{V_{\text{bus}}}
$$

This is mathematically identical to sector-based SVPWM. It gives 15.5 % more voltage than sine PWM.

## 2. Cascaded Angle → Velocity → Current Control

For a gimbal, the velocity loop must be closed on the **IMU gyro** (inertial rate), not the encoder. Otherwise base motion leaks straight through to the camera. The encoder provides the commutation angle, plus a rate fallback during startup and fault.

$$
J\dot\omega = K_t i_q - \tau_{\text{cog}}(\theta) - b\,\omega - \tau_{\text{dist}}
\;\;\Rightarrow\;\;
G_v(s) = \frac{K_t}{Js + b}
$$
