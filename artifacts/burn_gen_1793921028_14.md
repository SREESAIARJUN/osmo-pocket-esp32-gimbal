# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Systems Architecture Package (Job #35)

**Scope:** 6S (25.2 V max), 30.5 × 30.5 mm "nano" AIO board, 48 kHz PWM, 80 A burst target, ArduPilot with bidirectional DShot600

**Status:** Engineering model. Device parameters are representative, so substitute the values from your selected parts' datasheets before freezing the design.

---

## 0. Architectural Corrections

Two points in the brief need resolving before the math is meaningful.

**1. The STM32H743 cannot be the ESC controller.** ArduPilot runs on the H743 and emits DShot. No supported firmware does 4× sensorless commutation on the H743 alongside ArduPilot. The ESC section therefore needs **four dedicated ESC MCUs**, either AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 (or BLHeli_32-class firmware). The H743 sees the ESC as four DShot endpoints.

**2. 80 A per motor continuous is physically impossible on a 30.5 mm board.** Section 3 shows that 4 × 80 A produces about 120 W of FET loss into a board with roughly 5 J/K of heat capacity. The model below quantifies this as a **burst-time limit**, so that "80 A burst" receives a defensible definition in seconds rather than a marketing number.

## 2. Loss Model

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM, as in AM32's default). At every instant the motor current passes through **two** conducting channels: the high-side (or its complementary low-side) of the PWM'd phase, plus the low-side of the return phase.

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Because R_DS depends on T_j and T_j depends on loss, the model must be solved iteratively. At T_j = 100 °C with typical R₂₅:

$$R_{DS}(100) = 1.6\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.3375 = 2.14\,\text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.14\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{27.4\ W\ per\ ESC}$$

Add the copper path. With 2 oz copper, approximately 0.3 mΩ of trace/plane resistance per current path gives 2 × 6400 × 0.3e-3 ≈ **3.8 W** (dissipated in the PCB, not the FETs).

### 2.2 Switching Loss

Only the PWM'd leg hard-switches. The return-phase low-side stays static during a commutation sector.

Gate currents during the plateau:

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{pl}}{R_{drv}+R_g} = \frac{7}{15} = 0.47\,\text{A} \qquad I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{drv}+R_g} = \frac{3}{15} = 0.20\,\text{A}$$

Voltage/current crossover times, using Q_sw = Q_gd + Q_gs2 = 8.5 nC:

$$t_{on} = \frac{8.5\,\text{nC}}{0.47} = 18\,\text{ns} \qquad t_{off} = \frac{8.5\,\text{nC}}{0.20} = 43\,\text{ns}$$

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_{on}+t_{off}) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 61\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{2.95\ W}$$

Turn-off dominates. Using a split turn-off path (a diode plus 2 Ω in parallel with R_g) cuts t_off to about 20 ns and P_sw to about **1.8 W**.

### 2.3 Secondary Losses

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse recovery | V·Q_rr·f | 25.2 × 40n × 48k = **0.048 W** |
| C_oss (per switching event) | ½·C_oss·V²·f | 0.5 × 900p × 635 × 48k = **0.014 W** |
| Body-diode dead time | V_f·I·2t_dt·f | 0.8 × 80 × 200n × 48k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (in driver, not FET) | Q_g·V_drv·f·2 | 30n × 10 × 48k × 2 = **0.029 W** |
