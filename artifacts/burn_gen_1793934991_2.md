## 0. Corrections and Assumptions

Two points change the design, so they come first.

**1. The H743 must not commutate the motors directly.** It has only two advanced timers with complementary outputs (TIM1 and TIM8), which is 6 complementary pairs, enough for 2 motors. Four motors need 12 half‑bridges, so HRTIM plus TIM1/TIM8 would require heroic workarounds. ArduPilot also does not run ESC firmware. The correct architecture is:

> **H743 (ArduPilot) → 4× bidirectional DShot600 → 4× AT32F421 ESC MCUs (AM32 firmware, 48 kHz PWM) → 4× 3‑phase gate driver → 24 MOSFETs**

The 48 kHz switching frequency is therefore an AM32 setting (`PWM frequency = 48 kHz`), not an H743 timer.

**2. 80 A on a nano form factor is a burst rating only.** The math below shows roughly 100 W of dissipation with all four channels at 80 A. That is survivable for about 0.5–1.5 s, never continuously. Any marketing of "80 A" must be tied to a stated pulse duration.

**Parameters.** All are design assumptions. Replace them with your chosen datasheet values; every equation is parametric.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

AM32 uses complementary (synchronous) PWM. In trapezoidal six‑step drive, at any instant two phases carry current:

- The high‑side leg alternates between the HS FET (on‑time D) and the LS FET in synchronous freewheel (1−D).
- The low‑side leg's LS FET is on for the full interval.

So at any instant **exactly two MOSFET channels are in series** with the motor current, independent of duty cycle:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds(on)}(T_j)=R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

With α ≈ 0.0055 /K, R(125 °C) = 1.2 × 1.55 = **1.86 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Ripple correction

The phase inductor ripple adds to the rms current:

$$
I_{rms}=\sqrt{I_{DC}^2+\frac{\Delta I^2}{12}},\qquad 
\Delta I=\frac{(V_{bus}-E)\,D}{L_{ph} f_{sw}}
$$

Worst case at D = 0.5, with E ≈ V_bus/2 and L_ph = 8 µH (typical 2207 motor, phase‑to‑phase):

$$
\Delta I=\frac{12.6\cdot0.5}{8\mu\cdot48k}=16.4\,\text{A}\Rightarrow I_{rms}=\sqrt{80^2+22.4}=80.14\,\text{A}
$$

The ripple is negligible (0.2 %). This is one benefit of 48 kHz.

### 1.3 Result

$$
P_{cond,ch}=80.14^2\cdot2\cdot1.86\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{23.9\ W\ per\ motor}
$$

At a cold start (T_j = 25 °C) the same calculation gives 15.4 W. Conduction loss therefore rises as the part heats, which is positive feedback. Include it in the transient sim (§3.3).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM‑switching leg switches; the commutation leg changes only 6× per electrical revolution and is negligible.

### 2.1 Hard‑switching overlap (V–I crossover)

$$
P_{VI}=\tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}=0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot33\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k}= \mathbf{1.60\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}=40\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

Q_rr scales with di/dt and I, so derate it ×2 at 80 A, giving about 0.1 W.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss}=Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}\cdot\tfrac12\cdot2 \approx 30\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k}= \mathbf{0.036\ W}
$$

### 2.4 Dead‑time body‑diode conduction

$$
P_{dt}=V_f\,I\,(2t_{dead})f_{sw}=0.8\cdot80\cdot300\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.92\ W}
$$

This is significant. Use the minimum safe dead time (verify against shoot‑through with a scope). Each 50 ns removed saves 0.15 W.

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in the driver and R_g, not the FET)

$$
P_g = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw}\times 2_{(HS+LS)} = 45\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k}\cdot2 = 0.043\text{ W}
$$

### 2.6 Per‑channel and total budget at 80 A burst (T_j = 125 °C)

| Loss term | W / motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 23.9 | 95.6 |
| V–I switching | 1.60 | 6.4 |
| Dead‑time diode | 0.92 | 3.7 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.14 | 0.6 |
| Gate drive | 0.04 | 0.2 |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, 1 per motor) | 1.60 | 6.4 |
| Bus copper (≈0.3 mΩ effective share) | ~1.9 | ~7.7 |
| **Total** | **≈30 W** | **≈121 W** |

**Efficiency at 80 A × 4:** P_out ≈ 25.2 × 80 × 4 × D. At D ≈ 0.9 that is about 7.3 kW, so η ≈ 98.4 %. The issue is not efficiency; it is power density, about 120 W into roughly 10 cm².

### 2.7 Frequency trade

$$
P_{sw}(f)=(P_{VI}+P_{dt}+P_{rr}+P_{oss})\cdot\frac{f}{48\text{k}}\approx 2.66\text{ W}\cdot\frac{f}{48k}
$$

Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.3 W per motor (≈5 %). At 80 A, conduction dominates by 9:1, so 48 kHz is justified for acoustic and ripple reasons.

## 3. Thermal Calculations

### 3.1 Steady state: shows why continuous 80 A is impossible

Thermal path is junction → case → PCB → air (forced, prop wash ≈ 5–8 m/s).

| Element | Value |
|---|---|
| R_θJC (3.3×3.3 DFN, bottom pad) | 1.2 K/W per FET |
| R_θCB (solder + via array, 12 × 0.3 mm vias) | 2.5 K/W per FET |
| R_θBA (whole board, 8‑layer, forced air ~6 m/s, h ≈ 60 W/m²K, A ≈ 2 × 10 cm² + components) | ≈ 6–8 K/W total |

At 121 W steady state:

$$
\Delta T_{board}=121\cdot7 \approx 850\text{ K}
$$

Impossible. The maximum continuous rating comes from limiting the board rise to ΔT_BA ≤ 60 K at T_amb = 35 °C (board at 95 °C, T_j ≈ 115 °C):

$$
P_{max,cont}=60/7\approx 8.6\text{ W}\ \text{total} \Rightarrow \text{per motor } \approx 2.1\text{ W}
$$

$$
I_{cont}=\sqrt{\frac{2.1}{2\cdot1.86\text{m}+0.25\text{m}+\ldots}}\approx \mathbf{\sim 22\ A\ continuous\ per\ motor}
$$

**Honest rating: about 20–25 A continuous, 80 A burst.** That is typical of real nano‑class 4‑in‑1
