# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #39): Power, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

## 0. Feasibility Review

Before the math, the requirements contain three conflicts that should be resolved at design review.

| # | Issue | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **"Nano" vs. 80 A burst.** On a 20×20 mm board, 24 MOSFETs in 3.3×3.3 packages cannot pass 80 A/channel. Copper cross-section and heat capacity are far too small. | The baseline below uses **30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz outer/inner power planes, 5×6 mm FETs**. "Nano" is only viable at about 35–45 A burst. |
| 2 | **System current.** 4 × 80 A = 320 A at 6S is roughly 7 kW. An XT60 is rated about 60 A continuous, and battery pads and leads saturate long before 320 A. | Treat 80 A as a **per-channel, sub-5-second burst rating**, not simultaneous. Size the system for about 4 × 45 A continuous. |
| 3 | **Who switches at 48 kHz.** The H743 does not commutate motors. Each ESC needs its own MCU (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32) and a 3-phase gate driver. | The H743 only emits bidirectional DShot600. The 48 kHz PWM is an ESC-firmware parameter. |

All MOSFET parameters below are **representative assumptions** for a 40 V, 5×6 mm trench FET class. Replace them with datasheet values for the selected part.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 General form (per motor channel)

With synchronous rectification, current always flows through exactly one FET on the PWM leg and one on the static leg:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{DS,hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS,hot} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS,hot}$$

Conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle, but the **phase** current depends on duty. At partial throttle, I_phase ≈ I_batt / D. This is why low-throttle punch-outs from hover can have higher phase current than battery current suggests.

**Temperature dependence:**

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

### 2.2 Numbers at 80 A

- Per motor, FETs: P_cond = 2 × 80² × 0.0015 = **19.2 W** (9.6 W per conducting FET)
- PCB copper, assuming about 0.3 mΩ in the loop: P_Cu = 80² × 0.0003 = **1.9 W**

This is a thermal runaway check: as R_DS rises with T_j, loss rises, which raises T_j further. The loop converges only if

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\cdot R_{\theta JA} < 1$$

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Hard-switching V-I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 20e-9 × 48e3 | **0.97 W** |
| Reverse recovery | V · Q_rr · f_sw = 25.2 × 50e-9 × 48e3 | **0.06 W** |
| C_oss charge | Q_oss · V · f_sw = 25e-9 × 25.2 × 48e3 | **0.03 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f · I · 2 · t_dt · f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 2 × 100e-9 × 48e3 | **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs, dissipated mostly in driver and R_g) | 2 · Q_g · V_drv · f_sw = 2 × 60e-9 × 10 × 48e3 | **0.06 W** |
| **Total switching, per motor** | | **≈ 1.73 W** |

**Design insights:**

- **Dead time is the second-largest switching term.** Trimming it from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per motor. Verify shoot-through margin on the scope first.
- **Choice of frequency.** At 48 kHz, switching loss is only about 8% of the total at 80 A. Going to 24 kHz saves about 0.85 W per motor but adds audible noise and current ripple. 48 kHz is the right choice; conduction dominates.

## 5. Thermal Model

### 5.1 Junction rise above the board

For the hottest FET, dissipating about 9.6 W conduction plus about 0.9 W switching share (≈ 10.5 W):

$$\Delta T_{JC} = 10.5 \times 1.0 = 10.5\ \text{K}$$

Add about 5 K across the solder joint and thermal vias, giving **ΔT_J-board ≈ 15 K**.

### 5.2 Board as a lumped thermal capacitance

| Element | Mass | c_p | C_th |
|---|---|---|---|
| FR4 | ~5 g | 1.1 J/g·K | 5.5 J/K |
| Copper (8 layers) | ~5 g | 0.385 J/g·K | 1.9 J/K |
| FETs, caps, solder | ~2 g | ~0.7 J/g·K | 1.4 J/K |
| **Total** | | | **C_th ≈ 8.8 J/K** |

### 5.3 Convective path (prop wash)

- Heat transfer coefficient: h ≈ 100 W/m²·K
- Area: A ≈ 2 × (0.0305)² ≈ 1.86 × 10⁻³ m²
- Board-to-air resistance: R_θBA = 1 / (hA) ≈ **5.4 K/W**
- Time constant: τ = R · C ≈ 5.4 × 8.8 ≈ **47 s**

### 5.4 Transient solution

$$T_{board}(t) = T_a + P R_{\theta BA}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right)$$

Constraint: T_j = T_board + 15 ≤ 125 °C, so **T_board ≤ 110 °C**.
