**Architectural note.** In a standard 4-in-1 design, the 48 kHz switching happens on the ESC MCUs, not the H743. The H743's job is the DShot600 command stream and eRPM telemetry capture. That is why the thermal model (ESC side) and the DMA model (FC side) are separate subsystems.

## 1. Electrical Operating Point

For 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary PWM at duty D, one phase's high side and another phase's low side conduct. During the off-time, current freewheels through the active-phase low-side FET.

- DC link current: I_DC ≈ D · I_ph
- Phase current (flat-top approximation): I_ph ≈ 80 A at burst
- Each conducting FET pair carries I_ph for 1/3 of the electrical cycle (120° conduction)

At D → 1 (full-throttle burst), the two series FETs conduct I_ph continuously, and switching stops at exactly 100% duty. The worst combined case is D ≈ 0.90–0.95, where both conduction and switching losses are near maximum. The calculations below use D = 0.95.

## 2. Loss Model (Per Channel)

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0065\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 100 °C: R_DS ≈ 1.0 × 1.49 ≈ **1.5 mΩ**. All calculations use this hot value.

### 2.2 Conduction loss

Over one electrical cycle, two FETs are in the current path at any time:

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{DS}(T_j) \cdot 2 = 80^2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega \times 2 = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

These 19.2 W are spread across 6 FETs by 120° rotation. Each FET's average loss is therefore ≈ 6.4 W.

Within each 48 kHz period, the conducting phase leg splits its loss as D (high side) and 1−D (low side). Peak instantaneous loss in one FET is 80² × 1.5 mΩ = 9.6 W. That 9.6 W figure drives the junction transient in §3.

### 2.3 Hard-switching (V–I overlap) loss

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12\, V_{bus}\, I_{ph}\,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}$$

With t_r + t_f = 20 ns (≈ 2 A gate drive, Qgd-limited):

$$P_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 2.4 Secondary switching terms

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Output capacitance | Q_oss · V_bus · f_sw | 30 nC × 25.2 × 48k = 0.036 W |
| Body-diode reverse recovery | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw | 40 nC × 25.2 × 48k = 0.048 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_F · I_ph · 2 t_dt · f_sw | 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (2 switching FETs) | 2 · Q_g · V_GS · f_sw | 2 × 50 nC × 10 × 48k = 0.048 W (dissipated mostly in driver and R_g) |

Dead-time loss is non-trivial at 80 A. Trimming dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel. Only do this after you have verified shoot-through margin on the real layout.

### 2.5 Copper and interconnect loss

Consider a 5 mm wide, 15 mm long trace in 70 µm copper:

$$R = \frac{\rho L}{w t} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\times0.015}{0.005\times70\times10^{-6}} = 0.74\ \text{m}\Omega$$

With 2 layers in parallel plus stitching vias, R ≈ 0.37 mΩ per path. A phase current loop includes about 2 such paths:

$$P_{Cu} \approx 80^2 \times 0.74\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 4.7\ \text{W per channel}$$

On a 30.5 mm board, copper loss is comparable to FET loss. It is usually the most underestimated term.

### 2.6 Loss totals

| | 1 FET per switch | 2 FETs in parallel per switch |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.2 W | 9.6 W |
| Switching (V·I) | 0.97 W | ≈ 1.1 W (more Coss, slightly slower edges) |
| Dead-time / Qrr / Coss | 0.70 W | ≈ 0.9 W |
| Copper | 4.7 W | 4.7 W |
| **Per channel** | **≈ 25.6 W** | **≈ 16.3 W** |
| **Board total (4 ch)** | **≈ 102 W** | **≈ 65 W** |

Efficiency at burst: P_out ≈ 22.2 V × 80 A × 4 ≈ 7.1 kW, so the ESC is about 98.6% efficient. The challenge is not efficiency; it is roughly 100 W of heat in about 10 cm² of board.
