## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Design Risks

Three points shape everything that follows.

1. **The STM32H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC commutation MCU.** Production 4-in-1 ESCs use one small MCU per motor, typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32. The H743 cannot run four sensorless BLDC commutation loops alongside ArduPilot. I have modelled the board as an all-in-one (AIO) design: H743 FC plus 4× AT32F421 (AM32) on a shared 20×20 mm PCB. The AM32 firmware sets the 48 kHz PWM frequency.

2. **"80 A burst" is interpreted as per motor, for a short duration (≤2 s).** Four motors at 80 A is 320 A of battery current. No nano-class power entry (XT30, 12 AWG) can carry that. Section 3 shows that the thermal capacitance of a 20×20 board, not the MOSFET rating, sets the burst limit.

3. **All device parameters are assumed representative values, not datasheet-verified.** Substitute values from your chosen MOSFET's datasheet. Treat the netlist in Section 5 as a reference topology to be captured in your EDA tool, not a fab-ready output.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

R_ds = 1.5 mΩ × 1.45 ≈ **2.2 mΩ**

### 1.2 Six-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification

At any instant, the current path runs through one high-side and one low-side device. With complementary (synchronous) PWM, the off-time current flows through the low-side channel rather than the body diode, so the path resistance is always 2·R_ds:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_b^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Each of the 6 FETs conducts for 1/3 of the electrical period, so the average per FET is:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \tfrac{1}{6}P_{cond,motor} = \tfrac{1}{3} I_b^2 R_{ds}$$

### 1.3 Numerical results

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per motor | P_cond per FET (avg) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.5 mΩ | 19.2 W | 3.2 W |
| 125 °C | 2.2 mΩ | **28.2 W** | **4.7 W** |

### 1.4 Additional series losses (copper is not free)

**Phase traces/planes.** A 2 oz (70 µm) copper path of length 8 mm and width 4 mm:

R = ρL/(wt) = (1.72×10⁻⁸ × 0.008)/(0.004 × 70×10⁻⁶) ≈ 0.49 mΩ per layer.

Stitching 3 layers gives ≈0.16 mΩ. Two path segments add 2 × 80² × 0.16 mΩ ≈ **2.1 W per motor**.

**Shunt (if used).** At 0.25 mΩ: 80² × 0.25 mΩ = **1.6 W**. This is why phase-current sensing is usually omitted on nano boards. Use a single DC-bus shunt instead (Section 5).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM-active leg hard-switches; the other leg is static during each 60° sector.

### 2.1 V-I overlap loss (hard switching, inductive clamp)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_b (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = 0.97\text{ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

Recovery is minimized by synchronous rectification, but the diode still conducts during dead time:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{ k} = 0.048\text{ W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{ k} = 0.036\text{ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f I_b (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ k} = 0.61\text{ W}$$

Reducing dead time to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per motor. Verify shoot-through margin first.

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in the driver, not the FETs)

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \times 30\text{ nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{ k} = 0.029\text{ W}$$

### 2.6 Switching summary per motor

| Loss term | Power |
|---|---|
| V-I overlap | 0.97 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | 0.61 W |
| Reverse recovery + Coss | 0.08 W |
| Gate drive | 0.03 W |
| **Total switching** | **≈1.7 W** |
