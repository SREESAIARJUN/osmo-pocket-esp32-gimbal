# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #43): System Architecture & Mathematical Model

**Document scope:** Power-stage loss model (80 A burst, 48 kHz), transient thermal model, bus capacitor sizing, bidirectional DShot600 timer/DMA allocation for ArduPilot on STM32H743, and representative netlists.

**Assumption policy:** Component parameters marked **[A]** are representative design-entry values. They are not vendor-verified. Replace them with datasheet values from your chosen parts before release. The equations remain valid either way.

---

## 0. Architectural Decision: Read This First

An STM32H743 running ArduPilot **should not** also perform commutation for 4 motors. ArduPilot's scheduler and ChibiOS RTOS are not built for per-motor ~µs zero-cross/BEMF loops. "Bidirectional DShot600" also implies a separate ESC endpoint by definition.

**Adopted architecture:**

| Block | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIH6 (TFBGA100, 8×8 mm) **[A]** | ArduPilot, sensors, DShot master |
| ESC MCUs (×4) | STM32G071 / AT32F421 class **[A]**, AM32 firmware | 6-step commutation, BDShot slave, 48 kHz PWM |
| Gate drivers (×4) | FD6288Q-class 3-phase bootstrap driver **[A]** | 12 V gate drive |
| Power FETs (×24) | 40 V N-ch, 3.3×3.3 DFN, R_DS(on)=1.0 mΩ @25 °C, V_GS=10 V **[A]** | 4 motors × 6 switches |
| Board | 2-board stack, 30.5×30.5 mm mounting, 8-layer 2 oz power stack **[A]** | — |

Two consequences drive everything below:

1. **"80 A burst" on a nano/30.5 footprint is a short-duration rating only.** The thermal model in §3 quantifies this as seconds, not tens of seconds.
2. **IMU co-location with ~80 W of dissipation is the dominant integration risk** (§3.5).

## 2. Loss Model (per motor)

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

With α ≈ 0.0045 K⁻¹ **[A]** (typical for trench FETs; read the normalized R_DS vs T_j curve):

| T_j | R_DS(on) |
|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 1.34 mΩ |
| 125 °C | **1.45 mΩ** (design value) |

### 2.2 Conduction loss

In 6-step operation with complementary PWM, motor current always flows through exactly **two** channel-conducting FETs:

- During the on-time: the high-side of phase X and the low-side of phase Y.
- During the off-time: the sync-rectifying low-side of X and the low-side of Y.

This holds independently of D, apart from the dead time:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)\,(1-2t_d f_{sw})$$

The correction factor for t_d = 100 ns is (1 − 0.0096) ≈ 0.99, which can be neglected:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.45\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{18.56\ W}$$

**Per-FET average.** Each phase leg conducts 2/3 of the electrical cycle, and the loss is shared between the high and low switch. A good approximation is P_FET ≈ 18.56/6 ≈ **3.1 W**. The worst case for a single FET is the low-side at low D, which carries current ~2/3 of the cycle × (1 − D) plus its "Y-side" role: up to ≈ 2·I²R/3 ≈ 6.2 W instantaneous-average. Use **6.2 W for single-FET junction checks**.
