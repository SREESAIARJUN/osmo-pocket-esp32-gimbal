# Job #27: STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC, 80 A Burst Power-Stage and DShot Architecture

## 0. Feasibility Findings

Some of the requirements conflict physically. These findings come before the derivations because they change what the derivations mean.

| # | Finding | Severity |
|---|---------|----------|
| F1 | 80 A burst per motor × 4 on a "nano" (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board means about 84 W of dissipation at full burst. A nano board's thermal mass limits this to a burst of roughly 1–3 s. It cannot be a continuous rating. | **Critical** |
| F2 | DC-link capacitor ripple current reaches about 40 A RMS per motor at D = 0.5. A nano footprint cannot hold enough MLCC or polymer capacitance for that. A bulk low-ESR electrolytic on pigtails is mandatory. | **Critical** |
| F3 | The STM32H743 cannot commutate 4 motors directly in a credible design. The H743 acts as the FC and sends bidirectional DShot600 to 4 ESC MCUs (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32), or to one quad-ESC MCU. This document assumes 4 × AT32F421. | Architecture |
| F4 | 48 kHz PWM is fine for small motors. It roughly doubles switching loss versus 24 kHz. Section 2 quantifies this. | Design trade |

All component values below are **design assumptions**. Replace them with datasheet values for the parts actually selected.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Assumed MOSFET (40 V N-ch, PQFN 3.3×3.3)

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |
|--------|-------|---------|
| R_ds(on),25 | 1.0 mΩ | at V_gs = 10 V |
| α | 0.0045 K⁻¹ | R_ds(on) temperature coefficient |
| R_θJC | 1.5 K/W | junction to case |
| Q_g | 45 nC | total gate charge |
| Q_rr | 40 nC | body-diode reverse-recovery charge |
| Q_oss | 30 nC | output charge |
| V_f | 0.8 V | body-diode forward voltage |

Bus voltage V_bus = 25.2 V (6S, full charge).

### 1.2 Current path (trapezoidal / 6-step, synchronous complementary PWM)

During each commutation sector, current I flows through exactly two legs in series. One leg's half-bridge chops: the high-side (HS) conducts for duty D and the low-side (LS) synchronous-rectifies for 1−D. The return leg's LS is held on.

Losses on the chopping leg:

$$P_{cond,chop} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Losses on the return leg:

$$P_{cond,ret} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Total per motor:

$$\boxed{P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]}$$

Dead-time intervals are excluded here and counted in 2.3.

### 1.3 Electro-thermal fixed point

Let one FET dissipate P = I²R(T) with an effective junction-to-ambient path R_θ. Then:

$$T_j = T_a + I^2 R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]\,R_\theta$$

Solving for T_j:

$$T_j = \frac{T_a + I^2R_{25}R_\theta(1-25\alpha)}{1-\alpha I^2 R_{25} R_\theta}$$

Thermal runaway occurs when the denominator reaches zero:

$$\boxed{I_{crit} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{\alpha R_{25} R_\theta}}}$$

Example: R_θ = 40 K/W (steady state, nano PCB, still air) gives I_crit = √(1/(0.0045·0.001·40)) = **74.5 A**.

**80 A is therefore not a steady-state operating point.** It is only valid as a transient (Section 3).

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

- R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0045·100) = **1.45 mΩ**
- P_cond,motor = 2 × 6400 × 1.45 m = **18.6 W**
- Per conducting FET (time-averaged over a sector): about 9.3 W

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the chopping half-bridge switches. Each term below is per motor.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap (HS turn-on and turn-off)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}$$

Assuming t_r + t_f = 20 ns (FD6288-class driver, R_g ≈ 10 Ω):

0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 20 ns × 48 k = **0.97 W**

### 2.2 Reverse recovery and output capacitance

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}·25.2·48\text{k} = 0.048\text{ W}$$

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.036\text{ W}$$

### 2.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw}$$

With t_dt = 100 ns: 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k = **0.61 W**

### 2.4 Gate drive (6 FETs, only 2 switching per sector)

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2·45\text{n}·10·48\text{k} = 0.043\text{ W}$$

This loss lands mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs.

### 2.5 Total per motor at 80 A

| Term | W |
|------|---|
| Conduction | 18.6 |
| V·I switching | 0.97 |
| Dead time | 0.61 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.08 |
| Gate | 0.04 |
| **Total** | **≈ 20.3 W** |

All 4 motors at 80 A give about **81 W**. Add copper loss: a 0.3 mΩ path × 80² × 4 ≈ 7.7 W, for **≈ 89 W** in total.

**Frequency sensitivity:**

$$\partial P/\partial f_{sw} \approx 34\ \mu\text{J per cycle}$$

At 24 kHz the total saves about 0.8 W per motor. Conduction dominates (91 %), so 48 kHz is acceptable. R_ds(on) and copper are the levers that matter.

### 2.6 DC-link ripple (Finding F2)

$$I_{C,rms} = I\sqrt{D(1-D)} \le 0.5\,I = 40\text{ A per motor}$$

Phase-staggering is not available because each ESC MCU runs its own PWM clock, so ripple currents add partially. Use the following:

- 1 × 470 µF / 35 V low-ESR electrolytic (≥ 3 A rated ripple) on short leads
- 4 × 10 µF X7R 50 V 1210 per motor, placed at the HS drains

The MLCC bank shunts the high-frequency content. Measure capacitor temperature on the prototype.
