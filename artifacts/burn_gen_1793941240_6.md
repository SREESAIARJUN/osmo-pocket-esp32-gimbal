# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and Bidirectional DShot600 DMA Allocation (Job #40)

## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Feasibility Flags

Three issues should be resolved before layout.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.**
- The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller.
- Each motor needs its own small ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical choices are the AT32F421 or STM32G071, either four separate parts or one per motor channel.
- The 48 kHz PWM and the 80 A currents live on the ESC side. The H743 only sends bidirectional DShot600 to the ESC MCUs.
- This model assumes that split. Commutating 4×3 phases with field-oriented or trapezoidal control from the H743 while also running ArduPilot is not something ArduPilot supports.

**2. 80 A per motor on a nano (20×20 mm) board is not physically achievable.**
- Section 2 shows ≈100 W of dissipation for 4 motors at 80 A, landing on roughly 5 J/K of thermal mass.
- My recommendation is a 30.5×30.5 mm minimum, or better a stacked FC (20×20) on top of a separate ESC (30×30). Treat "80 A" as a ≤1–2 s burst rating.

**3. Assumed operating point.** All numbers below rest on these assumptions. Swap in your actual MOSFET datasheet values.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

**Commutation assumption.** With trapezoidal 6-step commutation and synchronous (complementary) PWM, two phases conduct at any instant. The current path is:

- one high-side position, carried by the HS FET during the on-time and by the LS synchronous rectifier during the off-time; plus
- one fully-on low-side FET.

So two R_DS(on) are always in series with the motor current:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_b^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + I_b^2\,R_{cu}$$

**Temperature dependence of R_DS(on):**

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ /\mathrm{K}$$

At T_j = 125 °C this gives R = 0.9 × 1.6 = 1.44 mΩ.

**Results:**

| Term | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (hot) | 2 × 80² × 1.44 mΩ | **18.4 W** |
| FET conduction (cold, 25 °C) | 2 × 80² × 0.9 mΩ | 11.5 W |
| Copper | 80² × 0.5 mΩ | 3.2 W |

**Per-device distribution (duty D).**
- Phase HS FET: D·I²R.
- Its LS sync partner: (1−D)·I²R.
- The static LS FET: I²R for its full 120° conduction interval.

Averaged over an electrical revolution, each FET conducts 1/3 of the time. The hottest FET at any instant still sees ≈9.2 W, which matters for the transient junction rise.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

These losses apply per switching leg, i.e. one hard-switched HS/LS pair per motor at a time.

| Loss term | Formula | Value per motor |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = 0.5·25.2·80·40 ns·48 k | **1.94 W** |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_f·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·600 ns·48 k | **1.84 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 50 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.06 W |
| C_oss charge/discharge | 2·½·C_oss·V²·f_sw | 0.05 W |
| Gate drive (6 FETs, only 2 switching) | 2·Q_g·V_drv·f_sw | 0.06 W |
| **Switching subtotal** | | **≈3.95 W** |

**Design lever:** dead time is now as costly as the overlap loss itself. Reducing t_dt from 300 ns to 100 ns (AM32 `dead_time` setting, validated against shoot-through on a scope) saves ≈1.2 W per motor.

## 3. Total Burst Dissipation

| | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET, hot) | 18.4 W | 73.7 W |
| Copper | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| Switching | 3.95 W | 15.8 W |
| **Total** | **25.6 W** | **≈102 W** |

At 25.2 V × 80 A × 4 = 8.06 kW input, the ESC efficiency in burst is ≈98.7%. That is good efficiency, but 102 W in a small board is the problem.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Lumped board heat capacity

Case: 30×30 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz copper, 1.6 mm thick.

| Component | Mass | c_p | C_th |

| FR4: 1.44 cm³ × 1.85 g/cm³ | 2.66 g | 1.1 J/g·K | 2.9 J/K |

For a 20×20 nano board this drops to roughly 2.4 J/K.
