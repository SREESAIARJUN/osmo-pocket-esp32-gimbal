## 0. Architecture Clarification

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. A 4-in-1 ESC needs per-motor sensorless BEMF commutation with zero-crossing comparators and complementary PWM. That requires dedicated ESC MCUs. The architecture is therefore:

| Block | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| FC MCU | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100) | ArduPilot, sensors, DShot600 master (bidirectional) |
| ESC MCU ×4 | AT32F421K8U7 (QFN32), AM32 firmware | 6-step commutation, 48 kHz PWM, eRPM telemetry |
| Gate driver ×4 | FD6288Q-class 3-phase half-bridge driver | 12 V bootstrap gate drive |
| Power stage ×4 | 6× 40 V N-MOSFET, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN | Three-phase bridge |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-Dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At the T_j = 125 °C design point:

$$R_{hot} = 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.6 = 2.4\,\text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Current Path

With 6-step trapezoidal commutation and synchronous (complementary) PWM, exactly two MOSFETs carry the phase current at every instant:

- Active high side or its synchronous low side (same leg), plus
- The low side of the return phase.

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{hot} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.4\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{30.7\ W}$$

Each FET conducts for roughly 1/3 of the electrical cycle. Averaged across the 6 FETs:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} \approx 30.7/6 = 5.1\ \text{W}$$

**Use the instantaneous figure for junction checks.** The die thermal time constant (~1–5 ms) is comparable to the electrical period at low eRPM. The worst case is a single FET at **15.4 W**.

### 1.3 Interconnect Copper Loss

Estimated phase-path copper, vias, and solder resistance is about 0.5 mΩ:

$$P_{Cu,ch} = 80^2 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

### 2.1 Gate Currents During the Plateau

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{src}+R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}} = \frac{12-3}{10.7} = 0.84\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{snk}+R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}} = \frac{3}{8.7} = 0.345\ \text{A}$$

### 2.2 Transition Times

$$t_{on} = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = \frac{11\,\text{nC}}{0.84} = 13.1\ \text{ns}$$

$$t_{off} = \frac{11\,\text{nC}}{0.345} = 31.9\ \text{ns}$$

### 2.3 Hard-Switching Overlap Loss (one switching FET per channel)

$$E_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus}I_{ph}(t_{on}+t_{off}) = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 45\,\text{ns} = 45.4\ \mu\text{J}$$

$$P_{sw} = E_{sw}f_{sw} = \mathbf{2.18\ W}$$

### 2.4 Secondary Switching Terms

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw | 0.036 W |
| Output capacitance (2 FETs) | 2 · ½ C_oss V_bus² f_sw | 0.037 W |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_f · I_ph · 2t_dt · f_sw | **1.54 W** |
| Gate drive (driver-side loss) | 2 Q_g V_drv f_sw | 0.046 W |
| **Total switching-related, P_sw,tot** | | **≈ 3.84 W** |

> **Optimization:** Dead-time loss is about 40 % of switching loss. Reducing AM32 dead time from 250 ns to 120 ns saves about 0.8 W per channel. Confirm shoot-through margin first with V_DS/V_GS double-pulse testing.
