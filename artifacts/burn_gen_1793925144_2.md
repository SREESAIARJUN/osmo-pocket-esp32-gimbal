# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model (Job #7)

**Scope:** 80 A burst conduction loss, 48 kHz switching loss, coupled thermal model, ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 timer/DMA allocation, and a reference netlist.

---

## 0. Assumptions and Critical Flags

**1. The H743 does not switch the bridge.** In a 4-in-1 stack, the STM32H743 is the flight controller. It sends DShot600 to four ESC MCUs (e.g., AM32 on AT32F421/STM32G071, or BLHeli_32), and those MCUs generate the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The model is therefore split into two domains:
- **ESC power stage** (Sections 1–3)
- **FC DShot/DMA** (Sections 4–5)

**2. No MOSFET part number was given.** All numbers use a clearly labelled hypothetical 40 V, 5×6 mm DFN FET. Substitute your datasheet values. The equations are general.

| Param | Symbol | Example value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S full) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Burst phase current | I | 80 A |
| R_DS(on) at 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | R₂₅ | 1.1 mΩ |
| R_DS(on) temperature coefficient | α | 0.0045 /K |
| Gate–drain + post-threshold charge | Q_sw = Q_gs2 + Q_gd | 12 nC |
| Total gate charge | Q_g | 40 nC |
| Reverse recovery charge | Q_rr | 40 nC |
| Output charge | Q_oss | 30 nC |
| Body diode forward voltage | V_f | 0.8 V |
| Gate drive current (avg, plateau) | I_g | 1.0 A |
| Dead time | t_dt | 200 ns |
| Switching frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |

**3. Feasibility warning.** 80 A per motor on a 20×20 mm board is a **burst-only** rating. Sustaining it is not possible, and Section 3.3 proves this mathematically. Treat 80 A as a 1–3 s envelope with active current limiting.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (Six-Step, Synchronous Rectification)

In trapezoidal commutation, two legs are active at any instant. With complementary PWM, the phase current flows continuously through exactly **one FET in each active leg**:
- the high-side or its complementary low-side in the PWM leg, and
- the low-side in the "sink" leg.

So the current always traverses two R_DS(on) paths. Over a full electrical cycle each FET conducts for 1/3 of the time on average, but the instantaneous dissipation for a channel is:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS}(T_j)=R_{25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

Per FET, averaged over the electrical cycle, with duty D in the PWM leg:

$$P_{cond,HS}=\tfrac{1}{3}D\,I^2R_{DS},\quad P_{cond,LS,PWM\,leg}=\tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)I^2R_{DS},\quad P_{cond,LS,sink}=\tfrac{1}{3}I^2R_{DS}$$

The worst-case FET is the one acting as a fixed sink: 1/3 of I²R continuously, or the full I²R for its 120° window. That window is short relative to the die thermal time constant at eRPM ≫ 1 kHz, so averaging is valid.

**Worked example at T_j = 100 °C:**

$$R_{DS}(100) = 1.1 \times (1 + 0.0045 \times 75) = 1.471\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.471\times10^{-3} = 18.83\ \text{W}$$

**PCB copper and solder path.** Allow about 0.4 mΩ round trip per channel:

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.4\ \text{m}\Omega = 2.56\ \text{W}$$

This loss heats the board, not the junctions.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The sink leg is static within a commutation sector.

**(a) V–I overlap (hard switching)**

$$t_r \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_g}=12\ \text{ns}$$

$$P_{VI}=\tfrac12 V_{bus} I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw}=\tfrac12(25.2)(80)(24\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz})=1.16\ \text{W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery** (the body diode recovers at each HS turn-on)

$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}=40\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k}=0.048\ \text{W}$$

**(c) Output capacitance**

$$P_{oss}\approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}=0.018\ \text{W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead times per PWM period)

$$P_{dt}=V_f\, I\, (2t_{dt})\, f_{sw}=0.8\times80\times400\text{n}\times48\text{k}=1.23\ \text{W}$$

> **Lever:** Dead time is the largest switching-related term. Reducing it from 200 ns to 100 ns saves 0.6 W per channel. On AM32 this is the `DEAD_TIME` setting; validate shoot-through margin with a scope first.

**(e) Gate drive** (dissipated in the driver and R_g, not the FET die)

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw}\times 2 = 40\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k}\times2=0.038\ \text{W}$$
