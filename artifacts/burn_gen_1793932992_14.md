# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #59): Power-Stage Model, Thermal Budget, and ArduPilot BDShot Allocation

## 0. Architectural Ground Rules and Assumptions

Two points need to be fixed before any math, because they change the answers.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot has no on-FC ESC firmware. The board is therefore:

- **FC domain:** STM32H743VIT6 running ArduPilot.
- **ESC domain:** 4× independent ESC MCUs (reference: AT32F421K8U7 running AM32) driving 4× three-phase bridges.
- **Link:** on-board bi-directional DShot600 traces, one per motor.

**2. "80A burst" on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board is a transient rating, not a thermal operating point.** Section 3 shows the board survives roughly 1.5–2.5 s at 4×80A before the hottest junction reaches the limit. That figure must appear in the spec sheet as a duration-qualified rating. Otherwise it is misleading.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation, exactly two legs conduct at any instant: the PWM'd leg and the low-side "sink" leg.

- **ON-time (D):** current flows through HS_a, then the motor, then LS_b.
- **OFF-time (1−D):** current flows through LS_a (synchronous rectification), then the motor, then LS_b.

So the current always flows through two FET channels:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 RMS with ripple

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}-E_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}$$

Example: L_ph = 10 µH line-line, worst case D = 0.5 at E = 12.6 V.

ΔI = 12.6 × 0.5 / (10 µH × 48 kHz) ≈ 13 A, so ΔI²/12 ≈ 14 A². Against I² = 6400 A², ripple adds about 0.2%. It is negligible at 48 kH
