# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80A-Burst 4-in-1 ESC: Engineering Model

This model uses explicit parametric assumptions, because I don't have a Job #68 spec sheet or BOM. Every number below traces back to a stated assumption. Substitute your actual MOSFET datasheet values and the equations still hold.

---

## 0. Architecture Clarification (Read First)

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. In any ArduPilot-compatible 4-in-1 design:

```
[STM32H743 FC] --4x bidirectional DShot600 (single wire, open-drain-ish, 3V3)--> 
[4x ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32 or BLHeli_32)] --> [3-phase gate driver] --> [6x N-MOSFET per motor]
```

- The **H743** handles DShot generation and eRPM telemetry decode (DMA/timers, Section 3).
- The **ESC MCUs** generate the 48 kHz PWM and own the switching-loss domain (Section 2).

These two domains are coupled only through the DShot wire and thermal proximity on the stack.

## 2. Power-Stage Loss Model (Per Motor)

### 2.1 Commutation topology

Assume 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification (AM32/BLHeli_32 default "complementary PWM"):

- At any instant, one phase is **PWM-switched**: HS on for D, LS on for (1−D).
- A second phase is **held low**: LS on 100%.
- The third phase floats (BEMF sensing).

### 2.2 Conduction loss

Hot R_ds(on):

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1 + k_T (T_j - 25)] = 0.8\,\text{mΩ} \times 1.47 = 1.18\,\text{mΩ} \quad (T_j = 110\,°\text{C})$$

The switched leg conducts I²R_ds for the full period (D through HS plus 1−D through LS). The return leg conducts I²R_ds continuously. The total is therefore independent of duty:

$$P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}$$

Ripple term (assumed L_phase-phase = 10 µH, worst case D = 0.5):

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{10\,\mu\text{H} \times 48\,\text{kHz}} = 13.1\,\text{A} \;\Rightarrow\; \Delta I^2/12 = 14.3\,\text{A}^2 \;(\text{negligible vs } 6400)$$

$$P_{cond} = 6414 \times 2 \times 1.18\,\text{mΩ} = \mathbf{15.1\,W}$$

Copper loss:

$$P_{cu} = I^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\,\text{mΩ} = \mathbf{3.2\,W}$$

### 2.3 Switching losses at 48 kHz (only the PWM'd leg hard-switches)

**V–I overlap (hard turn-on and turn-off of HS):**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 50\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{2.42\,W}$$

**Reverse recovery (LS body diode, Q_rr dumped into HS turn-on):**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.06\,W}$$

Note that at 80 A, Q_rr rises with dI/dt. Derate ×2–3 if using a datasheet value taken at low current.

**Output capacitance (C_oss) energy:**

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 60\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.04\,W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period):**

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.23\,W}$$

**Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g, not the FETs):**

$$P_g = n_{FET,switching} \cdot Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\,\text{nC} \times 10\,\text{V} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = 0.06\,\text{W}$$

### 2.4 Per-motor and board totals at 80 A burst

| Loss term | W / motor | W / board (×4) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 15.1 | 60.4 |
| Copper | 3.2 | 12.8 |
| V–I switching | 2.42 | 9.7 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 | 4.9 |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.16 | 0.6 |
| Current-shunt (0.25 mΩ on bus, shared) | — | ~1.6 (at 320 A total) |
| **Total** | **≈ 22.1** | **≈ 90 W** |

Efficiency check: P_out ≈ 25.2 V × 80 A × 4 ≈ 8 kW, so losses are ~1.1%. That is plausible, and it confirms the problem is thermal **density**, not efficiency.

### 2.5 Sensitivity: the 48 kHz cost

The switching-related terms (P_VI + P_dt + P_rr + P_oss) scale linearly with f_sw, at ≈ 3.75 W / motor at 48 kHz:

| f_sw | Switching W / motor | Board total |
|---|---|---|
| 24 kHz | 1.9 | ~82 W |
| 48 kHz | 3.75 | ~90 W |
| 96 kHz | 7.5 | ~105 W |

48 kHz costs ~8 W board-wide versus 24 kHz. In exchange, it gives lower ripple and quieter motors. This is a reasonable trade-off, but conduction loss dominates.
