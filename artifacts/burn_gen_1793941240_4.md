# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC Stack: Architecture and Design Package (Job #7)

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Design Basis

On a 4-in-1 ESC, the STM32H743 does not commutate the MOSFETs. ArduPilot runs on the H743 flight controller. Each of the four ESC channels has its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, typically an STM32G071 or AT32F421. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 commands to those MCUs and receives eRPM telemetry back. The system therefore splits into two domains:

| Domain | Silicon | Concern addressed |
|---|---|---|
| FC (ArduPilot) | STM32H743VIT6 @ 480 MHz | DShot600 bidir timers, DMA, eRPM notch filtering |
| ESC power stage (×4) | AM32 MCU + 3-phase gate driver + 6× N-FET | Conduction and switching losses, thermals |

**Design assumptions** (substitute your actual BOM values; every equation is parametric):

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S, full charge) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Burst phase current per ESC | I_ph | 80 A (≤10 s) |
| Continuous current per ESC | I_cont | 45 A |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| MOSFET (40 V, PDFN 5×6 class) | R_DS(on),25°C | 1.0 mΩ |
| R_DS(on) temperature coefficient | α | ≈0.0045 /K (×1.5 at 135 °C) |
| Rise + fall (effective) | t_r + t_f | 20 ns total at 80 A |
| Total gate charge | Q_g | 50 nC @ V_GS = 10 V |
| Body-diode reverse recovery charge | Q_rr | 40 nC |
| Output charge | Q_oss | 35 nC |
| Dead time | t_dt | 100 ns per edge |
| Body diode forward voltage | V_f | 0.8 V |
| Phase copper path resistance | R_cu | 0.4 mΩ per conduction path |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Conduction topology

In six-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM), the current path at any instant always passes through two MOSFETs:

- During the on-time: the high-side FET of phase A and the low-side FET of phase B.
- During the off-time: the low-side FET of phase A (freewheeling) and the low-side FET of phase B.

Duty cycle D only moves loss between the high-side and low-side FET of the PWM leg. The total stays the same:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \left[ D\,R_{HS} + (1-D)R_{LS} + R_{LS,B} \right] = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

This assumes matched FETs. Current ripple adds a small correction:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}-E_b)\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

With L_ph = 15 µH (typical 5" motor), D = 0.85, and back-EMF E_b ≈ 19 V:

ΔI ≈ (6.2 × 0.85)/(15e-6 × 48e3) ≈ 7.3 A, so ΔI²/12 ≈ 4.4 A².

This is negligible against 6400 A².

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

R_DS(T_j) = R_DS,25 · [1 + α(T_j − 25)]

R_DS(T_j) and T_j are coupled, so the solution is iterated (Section 3). The three reference points are:

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond per ESC: 2·I²·R | Plus copper I²·R_cu (2.56 W) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W | 15.4 W |
| 100 °C | 1.34 mΩ | 17.1 W | 19.7 W |
| 135 °C | 1.50 mΩ | 19.2 W | 21.8 W |

**At 80 A burst on all four channels, conduction alone is 62–87 W.** This number drives everything else in the design.

### 1.3 Per-device distribution (worst case)

The PWM-leg low-side FET carries I² · R · (1 − D) + I² · R when it doubles as the "B-leg" sink in the next sector. Averaged over the six sectors, each of the 6 FETs runs at roughly 1/3 duty:

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{2 I^2 R_{DS}}{6} \times 1 \;\to\; \approx 2.9\text{–}3.2\ \text{W per FET at } T_j=100\text{–}135^\circ C$$

**Peak instantaneous dissipation per FET is 9.6 W** (80² × 1.5 mΩ). At low eRPM (large sector dwell times), the junction tracks this peak rather than the average. This makes stall and low-speed high-torque the worst thermal case, not top speed.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ed leg switches hard. Each term below is per ESC channel.

### 2.1 V-I overlap (hard switching)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{e-}9 \times 48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (low-side body diode, at HS turn-on)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{e-}9 \times 25.2 \times 48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 35\text{e-}9 \times 25.2 \times 48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{0.042\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two dead-times per period)

$$P_{dt} = 2\,V_f\,I_{ph}\,t_{dt}\,f_{sw} = 2 \times 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{e-}9 \times 48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (HS + LS of the PWM leg; dissipated in driver and R_g, not the FET)

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{e-}9 \times 10 \times 48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

The gate driver bootstrap/LDO must supply I_g = 2·Q_g·f_sw × 3 legs ≈ 14.4 mA worst case.

### 2.6 Switching total

| Term | W per ESC | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Overlap | 0.97 | Scales with t_r + t_f. R_g sizing trades this against ringing. |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 | Reduce t_dt to about 50 ns if the driver allows it. |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.09 | |
| Gate | 0.05 | |
| **Σ switching** | **≈1.72 W** | |

**Frequency sensitivity:** P_sw ∝ f_sw. At 24 kHz this drops to 0.86 W; at 96 kHz it rises to 3.4 W. At 80 A, conduction dominates switching by about 11:1. Lowering f_sw saves little; lowering R_DS(on) and R_cu saves a lot.

### 2.7 DC-link capacitor ripple current

$$I_{C,rms} = I_{ph}\sqrt{D(1-D)} \;\xrightarrow{D=0.5}\; 40\ \text{A}_{rms}\ \text{per ESC (worst case)}$$

Interleaving between the four ESCs is uncorrelated, so it gives no reliable cancellation. Size per channel:

- Local MLCCs: ≥4 × 10 µF 50 V X7R 1210 per channel, at about 3 A each.
- Bulk at the pad: a 35 V low-ESR electrolytic (1000 µF) carries the remainder.

Without the bulk capacitor, the 80 A burst on 6S will produce LC spikes beyond the 40 V FET rating.
