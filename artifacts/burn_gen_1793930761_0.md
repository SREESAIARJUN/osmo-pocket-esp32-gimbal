## 0. Architecture Clarification (Read First)

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. In any viable "H743 4-in-1" stack, the work is split as follows:

| Function | Device | Firmware |
|---|---|---|
| Flight control, DShot600 generation, eRPM telemetry decode | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100), 400 MHz | ArduPilot |
| 6-step/FOC commutation, 48 kHz PWM, dead-time | 4× ESC MCU (e.g. AT32F421 / STM32G071) | AM32 / BLHeli_32-class |
| Gate drive | 4× 3-phase half-bridge driver (FD6288Q-class) | n/a |
| Power stage | 24× N-MOSFET (6 per channel) | n/a |

The loss model in §1–§3 applies to the ESC power stage. The DMA/timer model in §4 applies to the H743.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Commutation geometry

In 6-step operation with synchronous rectification, exactly two phase legs carry I_ph at any instant.

- During the on-time (D), current flows through the high side of leg A and the low side of leg B.
- During the off-time (1−D), it freewheels through the low side of leg A (sync) and the low side of leg B.

The current path therefore always contains **two R_DS(on) in series**, independent of D. This assumes the dead-time is excluded; dead-time is handled in §3.

**Per channel:**

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right]$$

### 2.2 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond / channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W | 51.2 W |
| 100 °C | 1.34 mΩ | 17.1 W | 68.4 W |
| 125 °C | 1.45 mΩ | 18.6 W | 74.2 W |

### 2.3 Thermal runaway check

Losses rise with temperature and temperature rises with losses. The loop is stable only if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\cdot R_\theta < 1$$

- Per FET: ∂P/∂T = I²·R₂₅·α ≈ 6400 × 1e-3 × 0.0045 = 0.029 W/K.
- Loop gain = 0.029 W/K × 3 K/W = 0.086, which is stable at the junction level.

The **board level** is not stable in steady state at 80 A. See §5.

### 2.4 Copper loss (frequently forgotten)

- 2 oz copper has a sheet resistance of ≈ 0.25 mΩ/□.
- A phase path of about 4 squares, paralleled over 4 layers, gives ≈ 0.25 mΩ.
- Add about 0.3 mΩ for pads and vias.

$$P_{Cu,ch} \approx 80^2 \times 0.55\text{ m}\Omega \approx 3.5\text{ W/channel}$$

This is significant. Use paralleled inner planes and solder-wicked or exposed copper on the phase pads.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the actively switched (PWM'd) leg incurs hard-switching loss.

### 3.1 V–I overlap (hard switching)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 0.97\text{ W}$$

### 3.2 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} \times 2 = 0.5 \times 1\text{nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = 0.03\text{ W}$$

### 3.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 30\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.036\text{ W}$$

The diode-recovery-induced turn-on loss in the opposing FET adds about the same again, so P_rr,total ≈ 0.07 W.

### 3.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}I_{ph}\cdot 2t_{dt}\cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 0.61\text{ W}$$

Dead-time is the second-largest switching term. Tune it down to about 50 ns if the driver and layout allow, which saves about 0.3 W per channel.

### 3.5 Gate drive

The gate drive loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs. It counts toward the board total, though.

$$P_g = Q_gV_{GS}f_{sw}\times 2 = 50\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = 0.048\text{ W/channel}$$

### 3.6 Switching subtotal

**≈ 1.7 W per channel at 80 A.**

At 48 kHz, conduction dominates switching by about 10:1. Raising the PWM frequency to 96 kHz would add only about 1.7 W per channel. **The thermal design is an R_DS(on) and copper problem, not a switching problem.**
