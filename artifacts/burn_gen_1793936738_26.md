# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #44): Electro-Thermal Model, DShot600 Timer/DMA Allocation, and Reference Netlist

## 0. Feasibility Findings

These issues shape every number below.

1. **80 A burst on a nano (20×20 mm) 4-in-1 is not thermally credible.** The model shows ~100 W of total stack loss at 4×80 A. A 20×20 board has roughly 1.5 J/K of thermal mass and reaches FET T_j limits in under 2 s. **I baseline a 30×30 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz board.** If the nano form factor is fixed, the burst rating must be about 50–55 A for 2 s.
2. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In a 4-in-1 stack, each motor has its own ESC MCU (e.g., AT32F421 running AM32, or a BLHeli_32-class target). The ESC MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 generates bidirectional DShot600 commands and decodes eRPM telemetry. I model both sides.
3. **"80 A" is defined here as per-motor RMS phase current**, not battery current. Battery current is roughly D·I_phase.
4. **The netlist is a reference design.** It is not a verified layout. Part values are representative classes, so substitute datasheet parameters into the equations before release.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology accounting

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM. In each 60° sector:

- **PWM'd leg:** the high side conducts for D, and the synchronous low side conducts for (1−D).
- **Return leg:** the low side conducts for 100% of the sector.

So at every instant exactly two FET channels carry I_ph, independent of D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

Ripple correction (triangular ripple ΔI):

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{avg}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

With typical motor inductance L_ph ≈ 15 µH:

$$\Delta I \approx \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{2 L_{ph} f_{sw}} \approx \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{2 \times 15\mu \times 48k} \approx 4.4\ \text{A}$$

The correction is +0.03% and can be neglected.

### 1.2 Electro-thermal fixed point

T_j depends on P, and P depends on T_j:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + R_\theta\, P_0\, [1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

$$\Rightarrow\quad T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + R_\theta P_0 (1 - 25\alpha)}{1 - \alpha R_\theta P_0}$$

**Runaway criterion:** the system is stable only if α·R_θ·P_0 < 1.

At burst with T_j = 125 °C, R_ds = 1.2 × 1.45 = **1.74 mΩ**:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.74\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{22.3\ W}$$

### 1.3 Distribution per FET

The electrical frequency (≥ 1 kHz) is far faster than the FET thermal time constants. Each FET therefore sees its 1/3 electrical-cycle share averaged.

- **High side:** 1/3 × D × I²R. At D = 0.5 this is ≈ 1.86 W.
- **Low side:** 1/3 × (2 − D) × I²R. That is the return leg plus synchronous rectification, ≈ 5.6 W.

Low-side FETs dominate conduction loss at partial throttle. At D → 1 the split equalizes to ≈ 3.7 W each.
