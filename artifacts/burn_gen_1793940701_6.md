# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage and DShot Architecture (Job #19)

## 0. Feasibility Warnings and Assumptions

### Warnings to review before committing

1. **80 A per channel on a nano form factor is a burst rating, not a continuous one.**
   - At 80 A, one channel dissipates about 30 W in the FETs alone. Four channels dissipate about 130 W.
   - A 20 × 20 mm mounting-pattern board cannot reject that heat in steady state. It only survives by storing heat for a few seconds (§2.6).
   - The marketing figure should be stated as **"80 A burst ≤ X s at T_amb = Y °C"**, with X taken from §2.6.
2. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.**
   - In this architecture, the H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 commands.
   - Four dedicated ESC MCUs (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32-class firmware) perform commutation and the 48 kHz PWM.
   - Putting six-step or FOC commutation for four motors on the H743 would be a different design with different firmware.
3. **All numbers below come from a stated parameter set.**
   - Substitute your chosen MOSFET's datasheet values before release.
   - The netlist is a reference topology for schematic capture. It is not fab-ready output.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

R_DS(on)(T_j) = R_25 · [1 + α(T_j − 25)]

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.6 mΩ × 1.45 = **2.32 mΩ**

### 1.2 Six-step commutation with synchronous rectification

In any 60° commutation sector, two phase legs are active:
- **PWM leg:** the high side conducts for duty D. The complementary low side conducts for (1 − D).
- **Static leg:** the low side conducts 100% of the time.

Instantaneous conduction loss for one ESC channel:

P_cond,ch = I²R(D) + I²R(1 − D) + I²R = **2·I²·R_DS(on)(T_j)**

This result is **independent of duty cycle** when synchronous rectification is used.

At 80 A and T_j = 125 °C:

P_cond,ch = 2 × 6400 × 2.32 × 10⁻³ = **29.7 W**

Adding copper loss: P_Cu = 2 × 6400 × 0.4 × 10⁻³ = **5.1 W**. This heats the board, not the junctions.

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each of the six FETs carries the load for about one third of the electrical cycle. Their average loss is P_FET ≈ P_cond,ch / 6 ≈ **5.0 W**.

The ripple in that loss sits at the electrical frequency (hundreds of Hz to kHz). This is faster than the FET's thermal time constant, so the average value is valid for junction-temperature estimates.

## 2. Switching and Ancillary Losses at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Gate current during the Miller plateau

| Edge | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Turn-on | I_g,on = (V_drv − V_pl)/R_g | (10 − 3.5)/4 = 1.63 A |
| Turn-off | I_g,off = V_pl/R_g | 3.5/4 = 0.875 A |
| Turn-on time | t_on = Q_sw/I_g,on | 9 nC / 1.63 A = 5.5 ns |
| Turn-off time | t_off = Q_sw/I_g,off | 9 nC / 0.875 A = 10.3 ns |

These are intrinsic times. Layout inductance typically stretches them by 2–3×. A **×2.5 derating** is used below.

### 2.2 Hard-switching loss

Only the PWM leg's high side hard-switches.

P_sw = ½·V·I·(t_on + t_off)·f_sw·k
= 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 15.8 ns × 48 kHz × 2.5
= **1.91 W**

### 2.3 Other loss terms

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 0.05 W |
| C_oss discharge | ½·C_oss·V²·f_sw | 0.03 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_F·I·2t_dead·f_sw | **1.23 W** |
| Gate drive | 2·Q_g·V_drv·f_sw (driver loss, not FET) | 0.03 W |

Dead-time conduction is significant. Each 50 ns of dead time removed saves about 0.3 W per channel at 80 A. Tune dead time down in AM32 settings, but only after verifying there is no shoot-through.

### 2.4 Total loss budget at 80 A, 48 kHz, 6S

| Loss | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs, T_j = 125 °C) | 29.7 W | 118.8 W |
| Switching (hard, derated) | 1.9 W | 7.6 W |
| Dead time + Q_rr + C_oss | 1.3 W | 5.2 W |
| Copper / PCB | 5.1 W | 20.4 W |
| **Total** | **38.0 W** | **152 W** |

Electrical efficiency at full burst: P_out ≈ 25.2 V × 80 A × D. At D ≈ 0.9 this is about 1.8 kW per channel, so η ≈ 98%. Efficiency is fine. **Heat density is the limit.**
