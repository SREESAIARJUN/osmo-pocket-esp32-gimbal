## 0. Architecture Decision (Read First)

ArduPilot does not commutate motors. It outputs DShot and reads eRPM telemetry. The 4-in-1 ESC therefore needs its own commutation MCU per channel (or a dual/quad-channel MCU) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 is the flight controller only.

| Block | Assumed part | Role |
|---|---|---|
| FC MCU | STM32H743VIH6 (LQFP100 or TFBGA100), 400 MHz in ArduPilot | Flight control, DShot600 TX, eRPM RX |
| ESC MCU | 4× AT32F421K8U7 (AM32) | Sensorless commutation, 48 kHz PWM |
| Gate driver | 4× FD6288Q (3-phase, bootstrap) | 6 gates per channel |
| MOSFETs | 24× 40 V N-ch PQFN 5×6, R_DS(on),25 = 0.80 mΩ @ V_GS = 10 V | Power stage |
| Bus | 6S LiPo, V_max = 25.2 V, nominal 22.2 V | |
| Form factor | 30.5×30.5 mm mounting (≈36×36 mm board) | |

> **Feasibility warning:** On a true 20×20 "nano" board, 80 A per channel is not achievable. 5×6 FETs do not fit at 24-off. The model below assumes a 30.5 mm stack. At 20×20 mm you are limited to 3.3×3.3 mm FETs (R_DS(on) around 1.5–2.5 mΩ). In that case, rescale with §2 and expect roughly 2–3× the conduction loss.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Commutation geometry

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM. At any instant, current flows through exactly two legs:

- **The PWM leg:** current alternates between the high-side (HS) FET (duty D) and the low-side (LS) FET (1−D). The LS conducts in the third quadrant instead of the body diode.
- **The return leg:** its LS FET is held fully on.

In either sub-interval, two channels of R_DS(on) are in series with the load. Therefore:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) \quad\text{(independent of D)}
$$

Each FET carries current for 120° of the electrical period (1/3 of the time) in the worst case:

$$
I_{FET,rms} = \frac{I_{ph}}{\sqrt{3}} = 46.2\ \text{A}, \qquad P_{cond,FET} = \frac{I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}(T_j)}{3}
$$

### 2.2 Temperature dependence

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]
$$

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond per FET | P_cond per channel |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 0.80 mΩ | 1.71 W | 10.24 W |
| 90 °C | 1.06 mΩ | 2.26 W | 13.57 W |
| 110 °C (design) | 1.14 mΩ | 2.43 W | 14.59 W |

### 2.3 Copper loss

$$
P_{cu,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{cu,ph} = 2 \cdot 6400 \cdot 0.3\text{ mΩ} = 3.84\ \text{W}
$$

This is not negligible. Copper loss is about 25% of the FET conduction loss. Mitigations:

- Use 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner power planes.
- Stitch with at least 20× 0.3 mm vias per FET pad.
- Solder phase wires directly to plated pads.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. One HS turn-on/turn-off pair and one LS pair occur per cycle. The LS switches at near-zero V_DS (it is clamped by the body diode during dead time). So hard-switching loss lands on the HS FET.

### 3.1 V–I overlap (HS FET)

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = \tfrac12 (25.2)(80)(22\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = 1.06\ \text{W}
$$

### 3.2 Reverse recovery

The LS body diode recovers at each HS turn-on. The loss appears mostly in the HS FET.

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 60\,\text{nC} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\,\text{k} = 0.073\ \text{W}
$$

### 3.3 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\,\text{nC}\cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\,\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}
$$

This counts both FETs' charge/discharge in the leg.
