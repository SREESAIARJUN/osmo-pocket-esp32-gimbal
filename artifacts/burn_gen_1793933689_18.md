# 3-Axis Gimbal: FOC Control Loop and Dual-IMU Sensor Fusion Design

## 0. Assumptions and Loop Architecture

I have no prior specification for Job #60, so this design rests on the assumptions below. All of them are exposed as parameters in the code, so you can change them without touching the algorithms.

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| MCU | Cortex-M4F/M7 (STM32G4/H7 class), single-precision FPU |
| Motors | Gimbal BLDC, 12N14P (p = 7 pole pairs), R ≈ 5–15 Ω, L ≈ 1–3 mH |
| Rotor sensor | 14-bit absolute magnetic encoder (AS5048A / MT6701), one per axis |
| IMUs | 2 × ICM-42688-P on the camera plate, SPI, 8 kHz ODR, shared CLKIN |
| Current sense | 2- or 3-shunt, sampled at the PWM center |

**Loop rates**

```
 ┌───────────── 1 kHz ─────────────┐ ┌──── 4 kHz ────┐ ┌──── 20 kHz ────┐
 θ_ref ─►(Σ)─► Angle P(+I) ─►(Σ)─► Velocity PI ─►(Σ)─► Current PI (d,q) ─► SVPWM ─► Motor
          ▲      ω_ff ───────┘ ▲     i_ff(accel) ┘ ▲  i_cog (anti-cogging)
          │                    │                   │
     Attitude (AHRS)     Gyro rate (dual IMU,   Encoder θ_e, ω_m
     → joint angles      notched + fused)→Jacobian
```

The outer loops are closed on **inertial** quantities, which is what makes the gimbal stabilize. The angle loop uses fused attitude, and the velocity loop uses fused gyro rate mapped into joint space. The encoder supplies electrical commutation angle, the cogging phase, and the motor-speed estimate for the dynamic notch.

## 1. FOC Inner Current Loop

### 1.1 Transforms

Clarke transform (amplitude-invariant, with $i_a+i_b+i_c=0$):
$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \frac{1}{\sqrt3}\,(i_a + 2 i_b)
$$

Park transform, with $\theta_e = p\,\theta_m - \theta_{\text{offset}}$:
$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix}=
\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}
$$

Inverse Park transform:
$$
v_\alpha = v_d\cos\theta_e - v_q\sin\theta_e,\qquad v_\beta = v_d\sin\theta_e + v_q\cos\theta_e
$$

### 1.2 Delay-compensated commutation angle

There is a 1.5-sample transport delay (one sample for computation, half a sample for PWM). The commutation angle is advanced to cover it:
$$
\hat\theta_e = \theta_e + \omega_e\,(1.5\,T_s)
$$

### 1.3 Plant and PI design (pole-zero cancellation)

The dq-frame plant is:
$$
L\frac{di_d}{dt} = v_d - R i_d + \omega_e L i_q,\qquad
L\frac{di_q}{dt} = v_q - R i_q - \omega_e L i_d - \omega_e\lambda_m
$$

Choose the current bandwidth $\omega_c$ (rad/s), typically $\omega_c \le 2\pi f_{pwm}/20$. Then:
$$
K_{p,i} = L\,\omega_c,\qquad K_{i,i} = R\,\omega_c
\quad\Rightarrow\quad \frac{i}{i^*} = \frac{\omega_c}{s+\omega_c}
$$

### 1.4 Discrete PI with decoupling and back-calculation anti-windup

The control law is:
$$
\begin{aligned}
e_k &= i^*_k - i_k\\
u_k &= K_p e_k + I_k + u_{ff,k}\\
\bar u_k &= \text{sat}(u_k)\\
I_{k+1} &= I_k + K_i T_s e_k + K_{aw}\,(\bar u_k - u_k),\qquad K_{aw}=1/K_p\cdot K_i T_s
\end{aligned}
$$

The decoupling feedforward terms are:
$$
u_{ff,d} = -\omega_e L i_q,\qquad u_{ff,q} = \omega_e (L i_d + \lambda_m)
$$

### 1.5 Voltage limit (circular, q-priority)

$$
|v_{dq}| \le \frac{V_{dc}}{\sqrt3}\cdot m_{max},\qquad v_q^{max} = \sqrt{V_{max}^2 - v_d^2}
$$

### 1.6 SVPWM (min-max zero-sequence injection)

$$
v_a = v_\alpha,\quad v_b = -\tfrac12 v_\alpha + \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}v_\beta,\quad v_c = -\tfrac12 v_\alpha - \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}v_\beta
$$
$$
v_0 = -\tfrac12\left(\max(v_{abc}) + \min(v_{abc})\right),\qquad
D_x = \tfrac12 + \frac{v_x + v_0}{V_{dc}}
$$

> **High-R gimbal motors.** With R > 10 Ω, a voltage-mode loop ($v_q = R\,i_q^* + \omega_e\lambda_m$) is often adequate. The code exposes a `voltage_mode` flag.
