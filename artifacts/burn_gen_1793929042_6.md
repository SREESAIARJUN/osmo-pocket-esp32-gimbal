## 0. Baseline Assumptions & Architecture Decision

### 0.1 Architecture: the H743 should not commutate the motors

The STM32H743 has only **two advanced timers with complementary outputs and dead-time** (TIM1 and TIM8). That covers two 3-phase bridges. HRTIM can do more, but sensorless BEMF commutation of four motors on the flight-control MCU is not a supportable design.

The industry-standard partition is:

| Block | Device (example) | Firmware |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100) or VIH6 (TFBGA100) | ArduPilot |
| ESC ×4 | AT32F421 / STM32G071 | AM32 (supports bidirectional DShot + EDT) |
| Gate drivers ×4 | 3-phase driver, e.g. FD6288Q class | — |
| Power stage | 24× N-ch MOSFET, 40 V, PDFN3.3×3 | — |

The H743 sends DShot600 over short on-board traces to each ESC MCU and receives eRPM telemetry back on the same wire.

### 0.3 Sanity flag: 80 A × 4 on a nano board

Four ESCs at 80 A means **320 A from the battery**. That exceeds what an XT60 or XT90 and nano-class power pads can carry. "80 A burst" on a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 board only makes sense as a **per-channel, ≤1–2 s, non-simultaneous** rating. The model in §3 shows this quantitatively.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path (six-step with synchronous rectification)

At every instant in trapezoidal commutation, two phases carry current I:

- **PWM-ON:** HS of phase X, then LS of phase Y.
- **PWM-OFF (complementary):** LS of phase X, then LS of phase Y.

In both states, exactly two FET channels carry I. The conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

where

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \qquad R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

### 1.2 Phase-current ripple

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{2 L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}$$

The factor of 2 appears because current flows through two windings in series.

For a 2207-class motor, L_ph ≈ 15 µH. Worst case is D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{25.2 \cdot 0.25}{2\cdot 15\mu \cdot 48k} \approx 4.4\text{ A}$$

The ripple adds only $4.4^2/12 = 1.6\ \text{A}^2$ to $6400\ \text{A}^2$, which is negligible at 80 A. You can drop it at burst current, but keep it for low-current efficiency work.

### 1.3 Numbers

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond per ESC |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.50 mΩ | 2 × 6400 × 0.0015 = **19.2 W** |
| 100 °C | 2.06 mΩ | **26.4 W** |
| 125 °C | 2.25 mΩ | **28.8 W** |

### 1.4 Copper and interconnect losses (do not omit)

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 (R_{phase\,trace} + R_{bus\,share})$$

For 2 oz outer and inner layers, a 4 mm × 8 mm phase pour gives ≈ 0.12 mΩ per segment, and the per-ESC share of the bus is ≈ 0.25 mΩ. At 80 A:

$$P_{Cu} \approx 6400 \times (2 \times 0.12 + 0.25)\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 3.1\ \text{W}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg hard-switches per PWM cycle: the PWM'd high-side and its complementary low-side.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

The LS body diode conducts during dead time, then the HS turns on into it:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

The real effect is larger. Q_rr also increases the HS turn-on current spike and therefore P_VI. A practical multiplier is ×1.2 on the turn-on half of P_VI.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss,eff} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 1.5\text{n}\cdot 635 \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.023\ W}$$
