# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #9): Power-Stage, Thermal and BDShot Timer/DMA Architecture

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Feasibility Warning

The H743 is the flight controller. It does not commutate motors. Each motor gets its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. ArduPilot does not run ESC commutation, so the H743 talks to the ESCs over bidirectional DShot600 (BDShot600).

| Parameter | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Form factor | 20×20 mm, M2 mounting, 2-board stack (FC + ESC) |
| Bus voltage | 6S. Design point 25.2 V; 22.2 V nominal |
| Burst | 80 A per motor phase-current RMS, ≤10 s, all four motors worst case |
| MOSFET (×24) | 40 V N-ch, PDFN 3.3×3.3. R_ds(on) = 1.5 mΩ at 25 °C, 10 V V_gs. Q_g = 40 nC, Q_rr = 40 nC, Q_oss = 30 nC, V_SD = 0.8 V, R_θJC = 1.2 K/W |
| Gate driver | FD6288Q-class 3-phase, ~1.2 A source / 1.5 A sink, external R_g = 2.2 Ω |
| ESC MCU (×4) | STM32G071 or AT32F421, AM32 firmware |
| PWM | 48 kHz, center-aligned, complementary (synchronous rectification), dead-time 200 ns |

**Feasibility warning:** the numbers below show that four motors at 80 A at the same time on a 20×20 board cannot be sustained even for the full 10 s window. Per-motor 80 A bursts while the others are partially loaded are feasible, as is a ~1–2 s all-motor burst. A sustainable all-motor 80 A design needs a 30×30 board with 2 FETs in parallel per switch.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths

**Six-step (trapezoidal) mode:**
- At any instant, exactly two phase legs conduct.
- In the PWM leg, current alternates between the high-side (HS) and low-side (LS) switch according to duty *D*. The LS carries current through its channel (synchronous rectification), not its body diode, except during dead-time.
- The other leg is held on one switch continuously.

Therefore, outside dead-time, motor current always flows through exactly **two channel resistances in series**:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

**Sinusoidal/FOC mode:** the equivalent result uses the phase-current RMS across three legs. For balanced sinusoidal currents (one switch per leg conducting at all times):

$$P_{cond} = 3 \cdot I_{ph,rms}^2 R_{ds}$$

With $I_{ph,rms} = I_{dc,eq}\sqrt{2/3}$ this gives the same $2I^2R$ form. Six-step is used below.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ /K$$

Typical for 40 V trench FETs; check the normalized R_ds curve in the datasheet.

| T_j | R_ds(on) |
|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.50 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 2.12 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 2.33 mΩ |
| 150 °C | 2.54 mΩ |

Add **0.3 mΩ per conduction path** for PCB copper, solder and the shunt. This is a 2 oz, 8-layer ESC with a 0.5 mΩ shared low-side shunt per ESC.

### 1.3 Per-motor conduction loss at 80 A (T_j = 125 °C)

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \times (2\times 2.33 + 0.3)\,\text{m}\Omega = 6400 \times 4.96\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{31.7\ W}$$

Of this, FET-only loss is 6400 × 4.66 mΩ = 29.8 W. Copper and shunt account for 1.9 W.

### 1.4 Distribution across the six FETs

Each leg is active 2/3 of the electrical cycle. Within that window, it is PWM'd for half the time and solid-on for the other half. At duty D (the HS share of the PWM half):

| Switch role | Average conduction fraction |
|---|---|
| HS, per leg | $\tfrac{1}{3}D + \tfrac{1}{3}\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot$… |

In practice, AM32 rotates sources, so the losses average to:

$$P_{FET,avg} = \frac{29.8}{6} \approx \mathbf{5.0\ W\ per\ FET}$$

Worst-case low-rpm imbalance (D ≈ 0.3, LS-heavy): **≈6.3 W** on the LS FETs. Use 6.3 W for junction sizing.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only one leg is hard-switching at a time in six-step. The constant-on leg contributes no switching loss.

### 2.1 Gate drive timing

Gate plateau V_pl ≈ 4.5 V; gate-drain charge Q_gd ≈ 9 nC; total gate resistance R_g,tot = 2.2 Ω external + 1.0 Ω internal + driver resistance.

$$I_{g,on} \approx \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{g,tot}+R_{drv,src}} = \frac{10-4.5}{3.2+5} \approx 0.67\ \text{A}\ \Rightarrow\ t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}+Q_{gs2}}{I_g} \approx \frac{9+4}{0.67} \approx 19\ \text{ns}$$

$$I_{g,off} \approx \frac{4.5}{3.2+3.5} \approx 0.67\ \text{A}\Rightarrow t_f \approx 19\ \text{ns}$$

The driver output resistance dominates. FD6288-class drivers are weak, so budget **t_r + t_f ≈ 40 ns**. Use 30 ns only if you move to a stronger driver.

### 2.2 Loss components (V = 25.2 V, I = 80 A, f = 48 kHz)

| Component | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | $\tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f$ = 0.5·25.2·80·40 ns·48k | **1.94 W** |
| Reverse recovery | $Q_{rr} V f$ = 40 nC·25.2·48k | 0.05 W |
| Output capacitance | $\tfrac12 Q_{oss} V f \times 2$ = 0.5·30 nC·25.2·48k·2 | 0.04 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | $V_{SD} I \cdot 2t_{dt} f$ = 0.8·80·400 ns·48k | **1.23 W** |
| Gate charge (driver/LDO side) | $6 Q_g V_{gs} f$ = 6·40 nC·10·48k | 0.12 W (not in FETs) |
| **Total switching-related (FETs)** | | **≈3.26 W/motor** |

Reverse-recovery current also adds about 15–20 % turn-on overlap. Include it in the 40 ns figure.

### 2.3 Design levers

- **Dead-time:** cutting it from 200 ns to 100 ns saves 0.6 W/motor. This is only safe if the driver propagation-delay mismatch is under 50 ns.
- **PWM frequency:** dropping to 24 kHz halves the switching terms (saves ~1.6 W/motor), at the cost of more audible noise and current ripple on low-inductance motors.

## 3. Total Loss Budget

| | Per motor | ×4 (all at 80 A) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 29.8 W | 119.2 W |
| Copper / shunt | 1.9 W | 7.6 W |
| Switching + dead-time | 3.3 W | 13.0 W |
| Gate drive + LDO + MCU | 0.3 W | 1.2 W |
| **Total** | **35.3 W** | **≈141 W** |

**Efficiency at 80 A:** P_out ≈ 25.2 V × 80 A × D_eff. At full throttle (D ≈ 0.95) that is ~1915 W/motor, so ESC efficiency is ≈98.2 %. Efficiency is fine; heat **density** is the problem.
