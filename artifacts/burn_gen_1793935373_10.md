## 0. Architecture Assumptions

The requirements need clarifying before any math, because two of them change the design.

1. **The STM32H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC.** The 48 kHz PWM and commutation run on four dedicated ESC MCUs. This design uses the AT32F421K8U7 with AM32 firmware. The H743 runs ArduPilot and drives four bidirectional DShot600 lines to those MCUs. No production 4‑in‑1 runs BLDC commutation on the FC's H7, and ArduPilot does not support it.

2. **"Nano" form factor and 80A burst conflict.** A 20×20 mm board cannot carry 4×80A. This design assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm mount pattern on a 36×36 mm PCB**, which matches the market label "nano AIO 80A". Section 3.6 shows that 80A is a roughly 5 second thermal burst rating, not a continuous one.

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal |
| Burst current | 80 A per channel (320 A aggregate theoretical) |
| PWM frequency | f_sw = 48 kHz, center‑aligned, synchronous rectification |
| MOSFET (generic 40V, 3.3×3.3 DFN) | R_ds(on),25 = 1.0 mΩ @ V_gs = 10 V; Q_g = 50 nC; Q_gd = 10 nC; Q_rr = 30 nC; Q_oss = 25 nC; R_θJC = 1.0 K/W |
| Gate driver | FD6288Q (3 half‑bridges, internal bootstrap), ~1.2 A source / 1.5 A sink, V_drv = 10 V |
| Dead time | t_dt = 100 ns |
| PCB | 8‑layer, 2 oz copper on all layers, 1.6 mm, 36×36 mm |
