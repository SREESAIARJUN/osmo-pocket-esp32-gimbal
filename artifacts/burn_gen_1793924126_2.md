# STM32H743 Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: 80 A Burst Thermal, 48 kHz Switching, and Bidirectional DShot600 Allocation

## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

### 0.1 Architecture

The H743 does not switch the MOSFETs. In every production 4-in-1 stack, the work is split across two kinds of processor:

- **Flight controller (H743, ArduPilot).** It generates bidirectional DShot600 and captures the eRPM telemetry that comes back.
- **ESC (four channels).** Each channel has its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071. That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM, a 3-phase gate driver (e.g. FD6288Q) drives the gates, and six N-channel MOSFETs form the bridge.

The model therefore splits cleanly along the same line:

- Sections 1–3 cover the ESC power stage.
- Section 4 covers the FC timers and DMA.
- Section 5 covers the interconnect and power-stage netlists.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Path

The ESC uses six-step (trapezoidal) commutation with synchronous rectification. At any instant two phases conduct. Current flows through:

- one FET on the PWM leg: the high side (HS) during the D portion, the low side (LS) during the 1−D portion;
- the continuously-on FET of the opposite leg.

There are therefore always two R_DS(on) in series, independent of duty cycle D.

### 1.2 Temperature-Dependent Resistance and RMS Current

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp}=\frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5″–7″ motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH, worst case D = 0.5, V_bemf ≈ V_bus/2), ΔI_pp ≈ 8.75 A. The ripple term adds only 0.2 %, so I_rms ≈ I_ph.

### 1.3 Total Conduction Loss per Motor

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) + I_{rms}^2 R_{Cu,path}$$
