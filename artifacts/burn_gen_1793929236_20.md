## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Feasibility Flags

No component list or design baseline came with Job #22. Every parameter below is a **stated assumption**. Replace each `[A]` value with datasheet numbers from your selected parts and re-run the equations. All numbers are typical/max engineering estimates and are not validated part data.

**Feasibility flags:**

1. **The H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC MCU.** The H743 runs ArduPilot and generates bidirectional DShot600. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU: AM32 on AT32F421/STM32G071, or BLHeli_32-class firmware. One H743 cannot run ArduPilot and 4× sensorless FOC/6-step at 48 kHz in a certifiable way. The architecture below reflects this.
2. **"Nano" form factor vs. 80 A burst.** At 80 A per motor, each channel dissipates about 30 W (derived below), or about 120 W for all four. A 20×20 mm board cannot sustain this. I assume a **30.5×30.5 mm stack with 2 MOSFETs paralleled per switch**. The single-FET case is also computed to show why it fails.
3. **"Burst" definition [A]:** 80 A phase current for ≤ 5 s on one motor, or ≤ 2 s on all four. Continuous rating is 45 A. Section 3 derives the duration limits.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step complementary PWM

At any moment, two legs carry phase current I.

- **PWM leg:** current flows through the HS FET for duration D·T and through the LS FET (synchronous rectification) for (1−D)·T.
- **Static leg:** the opposite LS (or HS) FET conducts continuously.

Because of synchronous rectification, **two switch positions always carry I**, regardless of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,\frac{R_{ds}(T_j)}{N_p} + I^2 R_{cu}$$

Each switch position conducts for 1/3 of the electrical cycle. At full throttle (D → 1), the six positions share the loss equally:

$$P_{cond,pos} = \frac{P_{cond,ch,FET}}{6}\cdot 2 \;\Rightarrow\; P_{pos} = \frac{I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)}{3N_p}\ \text{per position (each 120° conduction)}$$

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent resistance (self-consistent)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}\,R_{\theta,jb}$$

This is a positive feedback loop. Solve it in closed form:

$$P_{FET} = \frac{k\,R_{ds25}[1+\alpha(T_{pcb}-25)]}{1-k\,R_{ds25}\,\alpha\,R_{\theta,jb}},\quad k=\frac{I^2}{3N_p^2}$$

The denominator is the **thermal-runaway criterion**. It must remain > 0 with margin; target > 0.9.

### 1.3 Numbers

Inputs: R_θ,jb = 1.8 K/W (junction to board, through via array) [A], T_pcb = 90 °C at end of burst.

**Case A: single FET per switch (N_p = 1)**

- k = 6400/3 = 2133 A²
- R_ds(90 °C) = 0.9 × (1 + 0.0045 × 65) = 1.163 mΩ
- Denominator = 1 − 2133 × 0.9e-3 × 0.0045 × 1.8 = 0.984
- P_FET = 2133 × 1.163e-3 / 0.984 = **2.52 W per FET**
- T_j = 90 + 2.52 × 1.8 = 94.5 °C, then iterate once more → ≈ 95 °C
- Per-channel FET conduction = 2 × 6400 × 1.18e-3 ≈ **15.1 W**

**Case B: N_p = 2 (recommended)**

- Per-channel FET conduction = 2 × 6400 × 1.18e-3 / 2 ≈ **7.6 W**
- Per FET (12 per channel) ≈ 0.63 W
- Junction rise ≈ 1.1 K above the PCB
