**Job #24, Rev A (engineering baseline)**

## 0. Architecture Decisions and Assumptions

Two decisions shape the rest of the design.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.**
- ArduPilot has no native sensorless BLDC/FOC commutation. The H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bi-directional DShot600.
- Each motor is driven by a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32: 4× AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071GBU6.
- This is the only architecture that meets "ArduPilot + bi-directional DShot" without custom firmware.

**2. "80A burst" is defined as 80A per motor phase current, all four motors simultaneously, for a bounded time.**
- That is a 320A aggregate phase current.
- Battery current is lower: I_batt ≈ Σ D·I_phase.
- Section 3 shows this is thermally limited to about 5 s on a 30.5×30.5 mm board.

**Form factor reality check.** 24 power FETs plus 4 ESC MCUs plus an H743 cannot credibly be placed on a 20×20 mm "nano" board at 80A. The baseline is therefore:
- A 30.5×30.5 mm stack: an FC board and an ESC board, joined by an 8-pin board-to-board connector.
- If 20×20 is mandatory, derate to about 35–40A burst. The equations below scale directly.

**Topology.** 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM, which is the AM32 default. One switch per position, so there are 6 FETs per motor and 24 in total.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation with complementary PWM, two FETs are always in the conduction path:
- **During t_on:** the high-side (HS) FET of phase A and the low-side (LS) FET of phase B conduct.
- **During t_off:** the LS FET of phase A (synchronous) and the LS FET of phase B conduct.

Each phase is active for 120° (1/3) of the electrical period.

### 1.2 Hot resistance

$$R_{hot} = R_{25}\cdot k_T = 0.75 \cdot 1.50 = 1.125\ \text{m}\Omega$$

Equivalently, using the linear form $R(T_j) = R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$ with α ≈ 0.0059 /K.

### 1.3 Per-device conduction loss

$$P_{cond,HS} = I^2 R_{hot}\cdot\frac{D}{3}$$

$$P_{cond,LS} = I^2 R_{hot}\cdot\frac{1}{3}\;+\;I^2 R_{hot}\cdot\frac{1-D}{3}$$

The first LS term is the static LS of the "return" phase. The second is synchronous rectification. The 100 ns dead-time intervals are carried by the body diode and are counted in Section 2.

$$I^2 R_{hot} = 6400 \cdot 1.125\times10^{-3} = 7.20\ \text{W}$$
