# STM32H743 AIO + 4-in-1 ESC (Nano Format): Electrothermal Model, 48 kHz Switching Loss Model, and ArduPilot Bi-Directional DShot600 Timer/DMA Architecture

## 0. Scope, Architecture, and Stated Assumptions

I don't have the Job #34 requirements document. Every parameter below is an explicit assumption. Replace them with your BOM values and the equations will carry through.

**Architecture.** On every practical design, the H743 does not commutate the motors. It sends DShot600 to four ESC MCUs, and those MCUs run AM32 or BLHeli_32 and generate the 48 kHz PWM. The model is split accordingly:

```
 6S LiPo ──┬── Bulk caps ── 4× [ESC MCU ─ 3φ gate driver ─ 6× N-MOSFET ─ motor]
           │                      ▲ DShot600 + bidir eRPM (single wire, open-drain-ish)
           └── 5V/3V3 BEC ── STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ── IMU/Baro/OSD/UARTs
```

| Symbol | Assumption | Note |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 25.2 V (6S full) | worst case for switching loss |
| I_burst | 80 A **per motor channel** | phase current, six-step |
| f_sw | 48 kHz | set in the ESC firmware, not ArduPilot |
| MOSFET | 40 V, 3.3×3.3 DFN, R_DS(on) = 1.4 mΩ @ 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | e.g. a typical 40 V "SGT" part |
| R_DS(on) tempco | ×1.5 at T_j = 125 °C, ×1.6 at 150 °C | read from the datasheet curve |
| t_r / t_f | 15 ns / 15 ns at 80 A | depends on gate driver and R_G |
| Q_oss, Q_rr, Q_g | 40 nC, 50 nC, 40 nC | |
| Dead time | 200 ns per edge | |
| V_SD (body diode) | 0.8 V @ 80 A | |
| PCB | 20×20 mm, 8 layers, 2 oz (70 µm) all layers | nano / AIO class |

> **Up-front engineering caveat:** 80 A per channel × 4 on a 20×20 mm board is **burst-only and seconds-limited**. Section 3 shows this quantitatively. Treat the analysis as a design-limit study, not a validation of the rating.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Topology current paths

With six-step (trapezoidal) commutation and synchronous (complementary) rectification, at any instant:
- one high-side FET carries I_ph, either switching or its low-side complement is on, and
- one low-side FET of another phase carries I_ph continuously.

During the PWM ON interval, current flows through HS_a and LS_b. During OFF, it flows through LS_a (synchronous) and LS_b. **Either way, two R_DS(on) are in series.** Conduction loss is therefore essentially independent of duty, apart from the dead-time intervals:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)\,(1 - 2 t_{dt} f_{sw})
$$

### 1.2 Temperature-coupled R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

This is consistent with the ×1.5 factor at 125 °C.

At T_j = 125 °C, R = 2.1 mΩ, and dead-time factor = 1 − 2·200 ns·48 kHz = 0.981:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 2.1\text{ m}\Omega \cdot 0.981 = \mathbf{26.4\ W}
$$

### 1.3 Copper (PCB) conduction loss

Phase and bus paths are about 8 mm long. Assume an effective 4-layer parallel, 3 mm wide, 70 µm copper path:

$$
R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{A} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot 0.008}{4 \cdot 3\times10^{-3}\cdot 70\times10^{-6}} \approx 0.16\text{ m}\Omega\ \ (+0.39\%/\text{K})
$$

There are two such paths (bus+ and bus−/phase), so:

$$
P_{Cu,ch} \approx 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 0.2\text{ m}\Omega \approx \mathbf{2.6\ W}
$$

Solder joints, motor-pad interfaces, and the current shunt (if per-channel; typically 0.25–0.5 mΩ) add another 1–3 W. Use **≈4 W** for the channel.

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

Electrically, each FET conducts for about 1/3 of the electrical cycle (120°). The peak instantaneous dissipation in a conducting FET is:

$$
P_{FET,peak} = I^2 R = 80^2 \cdot 2.1\text{ m} = 13.4\ \text{W}
$$

The cycle-averaged dissipation per FET is about 26.4/6 ≈ 4.4 W, plus switching loss on the active leg. At motor electrical frequencies (> 100 Hz, so periods < 10 ms) the die thermal time constant (~1–5 ms) partially follows the 13.4 W pulses. Budget a **ripple of +5…10 K** on T_j above the average-based estimate.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-modulated leg switches hard. In synchronous mode, that is one HS/LS pair per channel at any time.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap (linear approximation)

$$
P_{sw,VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 30\text{ ns} \cdot 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}
$$

To derive t_r and t_f from the driver instead (Miller plateau method):

$$
t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{(V_{DRV} - V_{pl})/(R_{G,ext}+R_{G,int}+R_{drv,src})},\qquad
t_{f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{V_{pl}/(R_{G,ext}+R_{G,int}+R_{drv,sink})}
$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance loss

$$
P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{ nC}\cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{ kHz} = 0.048\ \text{W}
$$

The ½ factor is commonly applied when using E_oss directly. Using Q·V gives the conservative bound.
