# STM32H743 AIO + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #47): Power, Thermal, DShot and Netlist Design Package

**Rev A: Engineering baseline.** All component values are design assumptions. Verify them against the vendor datasheets before layout freeze.

---

## 0. Executive Summary

**An 80 A burst on all four channels is not thermally achievable on a 20×20 mm "nano" PCB.** The derivation below shows about **122 W of dissipation** at that operating point. Adiabatically, that heats the board about **34 K per second**. Sustained, it would mean a steady-state rise of more than 1,000 K.

The same model predicts a realistic rating of **about 12–15 A continuous and 35–40 A burst (≤5 s) per channel**. That is consistent with shipping nano AIO products, which gives a useful sanity check on the model.

Recommendations:
- Re-spec the 80 A figure as a **single-channel, ≤1 s, phase-current transient**, or
- Move to a 30.5×30.5 mm two-board stack with 5×6 mm FETs.

**Definition of "80 A".** Here it means the *motor phase current* I_ph.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current Path

In 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary PWM, current flows through two phases at any instant:

- **During the on-interval D:** high-side FET of phase A → motor → low-side FET of phase B.
- **During the off-interval (1−D):** synchronous low-side FET of phase A → motor → low-side FET of phase B.

So the current path always contains exactly **two channels' R_DS(on)**:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 2.2 Temperature Correction

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right]^{\,\beta}\;\approx\;R_{25}\,(1+0.005\,(T_j-25))$$

At T_j = 125 °C, this gives R ≈ 1.5 × 1.5 = **2.25 mΩ**.

### 2.3 Results

| I_ph | P_cond @ 25 °C | P_cond @ 125 °C (per ch) | ×4 channels @ 125 °C |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15 A | 0.68 W | 1.01 W | 4.1 W |
| 30 A | 2.70 W | 4.05 W | 16.2 W |
| 40 A | 4.80 W | 7.20 W | 28.8 W |
| **80 A** | **19.2 W** | **28.8 W** | **115 W** |

**Shunt loss.** P_shunt = I_bat² · R_sh = 80² × 0.2 mΩ = 1.28 W per channel. The shunt sits in the DC bus, so use I_bat, not I_ph.

**Copper loss.** Bus plus phase traces add about 0.5 mΩ of effective resistance at 2 oz, roughly 3.2 W at 80 A. This term is frequently ignored, but it is significant at this current.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Per PWM cycle, only the high-side FET of the PWM-ing phase hard-switches. The synchronous FET switches at near-zero V_DS, after its body diode has conducted during the dead time.

**(a) V–I overlap, hard-switched FET:**
$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f)f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(20\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = \mathbf{0.968\ W}$$

**(b) Output capacitance** (two FETs per switching node):
$$P_{oss} = 2\cdot\tfrac12 C_{oss(er)}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = (1\,\text{nF})(635)(48\,\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.030\ W}$$

**(c) Reverse recovery** of the synchronous FET body diode:
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = (30\,\text{nC})(25.2)(48\,\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.036\ W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction:**
$$P_{dt} = V_F\,I_{ph}\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(200\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.614\ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive** (two FETs; dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs):
$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2(30\,\text{nC})(10)(48\,\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.029\ W}$$

**Totals:**

| Scope | Value |
|---|---|
| Switching, per channel @ 80 A | **≈ 1.68 W** |
| Switching, ×4 channels | **≈ 6.7 W** |

**Design insight.** At 48 kHz the switching loss is only about 5% of the conduction loss. Dead-time loss (d) is the largest switching term.

- Cutting dead time to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel.
- Dropping to 24 kHz halves all switching terms, but increases current ripple and audible noise.
- 48 kHz is justified for low-inductance racing motors. Conduction loss is the real limit.

## 5. Thermal Model

### 5.1 Lumped Steady-State Network

$$T_j = T_a + P_{FET}\,R_{\theta jc} + P_{board}\,R_{\theta b\text{-}a}$$

**Board-to-ambient resistance:**
- Effective area, both sides: A = 2 × (0.02)² = 8×10⁻⁴ m².
- Convection coefficient in prop wash: h ≈ 50–100 W/m²K. Use 80.

$$R_{\theta b\text{-}a} = \frac{1}{hA} = \frac{1}{80\times 8\times10^{-4}} \approx \mathbf{15.6\ K/W}$$
