## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Flags

Two points need to be settled before the math.

**0.1 Where the H743 sits.** The STM32H743 runs **ArduPilot as the flight controller**. It does not commutate motors. Each of the four motor channels needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, for example an AT32F421 or STM32G071. That ESC MCU generates the **48 kHz PWM**, while the H743 generates **bidirectional DShot600**. This split drives the whole netlist.

```
            ┌───────────────── STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ─────────────────┐
 IMU/Baro ──┤ SPI1/SPI4/I2C       TIM3/TIM5 + DMAMUX → DShot600 BIDIR ├──┐
 GPS/RC  ───┤ UARTs                ADC: VBAT, CURR                     │  │
            └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘  │
   ┌──────────────┬──────────────┬──────────────┬──────────────┐         │ ×4 single-wire
   │ ESC MCU 1    │ ESC MCU 2    │ ESC MCU 3    │ ESC MCU 4    │ ◄───────┘
   │ AM32 48 kHz  │              │              │              │
   │ 3φ driver    │ 3φ driver    │ 3φ driver    │ 3φ driver    │
   │ 6× NMOS      │ 6× NMOS      │ 6× NMOS      │ 6× NMOS      │  = 24 FETs
   └──────────────┴──────────────┴──────────────┴──────────────┘
```

**0.2 The "80A burst" on a nano form factor is not credible as a sustained or simultaneous rating.** Section 3 derives this quantitatively. The model below therefore defines "80A burst" as **single-motor, ≤1–2 s, with airflow**. Marketing-grade "80A" on 20×20 or 30.5×30.5 boards is rarely backed by a thermal model.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{ds} = 1.2\,(1+0.45) = 1.74\text{ mΩ}$$

### 1.2 Six-step (trapezoidal) bridge with synchronous rectification

At any instant, current flows through exactly two conducting devices:

- the high-side FET of phase X during D, or its synchronous low-side during (1−D);
- the low-side FET of phase Y for the full 60° sector.

With complementary PWM, the path resistance is always 2·R_ds, independent of duty:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

The duty split determines the loss per device within the commutating leg:

$$P_{HS} = D\,I^2R_{ds}\cdot\tfrac{1}{3},\qquad P_{LS,sync} = (1-D)\,I^2R_{ds}\cdot\tfrac13,\qquad P_{LS,static}=I^2R_{ds}\cdot\tfrac13$$

The factor 1/3 is each leg's share of the electrical cycle.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2(80)^2(1.74\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{22.3\ W\ per\ motor}$$

**Hot-spot device.** The instantaneous loss in a conducting FET is I²R_ds = 11.1 W. This value, not the cycle-average of about 3.7 W per FET, matters for thermal transients at low eRPM.

**At low eRPM** (sector time much longer than the FET thermal time constant, a few ms), use the instantaneous value. **At high eRPM**, use the average.

### 1.4 Parasitic copper and shunt (add to budget)

$$P_{Cu} = I^2\left(R_{trace}+R_{shunt}\cdot\tfrac{I_{shunt}}{I}\right)$$

Example: 0.3 mΩ of effective phase-path copper gives 80² × 0.3 m = **1.9 W**. A common 0.25 mΩ shunt carrying the summed current is evaluated at the system level.
