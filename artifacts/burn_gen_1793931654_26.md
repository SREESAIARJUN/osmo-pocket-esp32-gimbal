## 0. Architecture Assumptions

These assumptions shape every number below.

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate motors.** ArduPilot runs on the H743 as the flight controller. Each of the four ESC channels has its own small MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071) running AM32. The H743 talks to them over bidirectional DShot600. One H7 cannot run four sensorless BLDC control loops alongside ArduPilot, and ArduPilot has no ESC firmware path.
2. **Battery:** 6S LiPo. V_nom = 22.2 V, V_max = 25.2 V. All loss calculations use **25.2 V** (worst case).
3. **Form factor:** "Nano" means a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm stack with **one MOSFET per switch** (24 FETs total, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN-class).
4. **Control scheme:** AM32 trapezoidal control with complementary (synchronous) PWM at f_sw = 48 kHz.

> ⚠️ **Headline finding:** "80 A burst" on a nano board with single 3×3 FETs is a **transient rating only, valid for about 1–3 seconds**. The math in Section 3 shows that the board cannot reach thermal steady state at 80 A on all four channels without forced airflow. Specify the burst as **80 A for ≤ 2 s at a 10% duty cycle** (or similar), not as an open-ended rating.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation with synchronous PWM, during any 60° sector:

- **PWM-on:** HS(phase A) → motor → LS(phase B)
- **PWM-off (synchronous freewheel):** LS(phase A) → motor → LS(phase B)

So, apart from dead time, **the phase current always flows through exactly two FETs in series**, independent of duty cycle D:

```
P_cond,ch = I_rms² · 2 · R_ds(T_j)
```
