# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC: Electrical, Thermal and DShot Timing Model

This is an engineering model built on stated assumptions. The MOSFET, PCB and copper values are representative placeholders. Replace them with datasheet and stack-up values before using any number for sign-off. Several requirements as stated push past what a "nano" form factor can physically sustain. Those conflicts are flagged in §0 rather than hidden in the math.

---

## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Red Flags

| Item | Reality | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Where 48 kHz PWM lives | On the **ESC MCU** (e.g. AM32 on AT32F421 or STM32G071), not on the H743. | The H743 only generates DShot600 and captures eRPM telemetry. |
| "80A burst" | Assumed per motor, ≤5 s. | Peak battery current can reach ~320 A (see §2.5). A nano board's power entry, pads and XT60 cannot carry this. |
| Thermal mass | Nano boards are ~20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm. | 80 A is only feasible as a seconds-scale burst with airflow (§3). |
| FC + ESC on one PCB | High di/dt and ground bounce near the IMU and H743. | Requires split planes, star ground, and IMU isolation (§5). |

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

During any commutation step, current flows through two legs:

- **Leg A (non-PWM leg):** the low-side FET is on continuously, so P = I²R.
- **Leg B (PWM leg):** the high-side FET conducts for a fraction D of each period, and the synchronous low-side for 1−D. The sum is I²R regardless of duty.

The hot on-resistance is:

$$R_{hot} = k_T R_{ds(on),25} = 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega$$

Total conduction loss per ESC is therefore independent of D:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{ph}^2 R_{hot} = 2(80)^2(1.5\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

The worst single FET is the static low-side of Leg A:

$$P_{FET,max} = I^2R_{hot} = 9.6\ W$$

This holds for 1/3 of each electrical revolution. Each low-side FET is the static leg in 2 of the 6 steps, so its time-averaged loss is about 3.2 W, plus its synchronous-rectifier share.

Because R_hot depends on T_j, the model must be iterated to a self-consistent temperature:

$$R(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

### 2.2 Switching Loss at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches hard.

| Term | Formula | Result |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·20 ns·48 kHz | **0.968 W** |
| C_oss | Q_oss·V·f_sw = 50 nC·25.2·48 kHz | 0.060 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 40 nC·25.2·48 kHz | 0.048 W |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_SD·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·200 ns·48 kHz | **0.614 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs switching, dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw = 2·60 nC·10·48 kHz | 0.058 W |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 1.75 W** |

### 2.3 Copper and Shunt Loss (per ESC)

Assume a phase path of 2 oz internal copper, 4 mm wide and 15 mm long:

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{w t} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot 0.015}{0.004\cdot 70\times10^{-6}} \approx 0.92\ \text{m}\Omega$$

Two paths in series, at 80 A:

$$P_{Cu} \approx 2\cdot80^2\cdot0.92\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 11.8\ \text{W}$$

**This is comparable to the FET conduction loss and is the dominant layout risk.** Mitigations:

- Parallel the path across several 2 oz layers. Four layers gives about 3 W.
- Use via arrays of roughly 1 A per 0.3 mm via under transient conditions.
- Place the motor pads directly at the half-bridge.

A 0.25 mΩ current shunt adds 80²·0.25 m ≈ 1.6 W. Many 4-in-1 designs share one shunt on the battery side, which avoids carrying this per channel.

### 2.5 Bus Current and Input Capacitor Ripple

Battery current per ESC is I_bat ≈ D·I_ph. At full throttle (D → 1) that is 80 A each, or **320 A total**. This is a hard red flag for the battery connector and the power-entry copper.

Input capacitor RMS ripple per ESC is worst at D = 0.5:

$$I_{C,rms} = I_{ph}\sqrt{D(1-D)} = 80\cdot0.5 = \mathbf{40\ A_{rms}}$$

Interleaving the four ESC PWM phases (0°, 90°, 180°, 270°, synchronized carriers) reduces the aggregate ripple substantially.

Recommended capacitor fit: per ESC, about 4× 10 µF/50 V X7R 1210 MLCC placed under 3 mm from the half-bridges. On the main input, a 1000 µF/35 V low-ESR electrolytic plus a TVS rated around 33 V standoff.

Voltage ripple with 40 µF of effective capacitance (after DC-bias derating to roughly 40%):

$$\Delta V \approx \frac{I D(1-D)}{C f_{sw}} = \frac{80\cdot0.25}{40\,\mu\text{F}\cdot48\,\text{kHz}} \approx 10.4\ \text{V}_{pp}$$

**This is unacceptable.** It forces either:

- far more local capacitance (≥200 µF effective per ESC, using polymer capacitors), or
- interleaving plus a short, low-inductance path to the bulk capacitor.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Junction Rise (fast time scale)

The worst FET dissipates 9.6 W instantaneously:

$$\Delta T_{JC} = 9.6\cdot0.8 \approx 7.7\ \text{K}$$

The junction-to-case rise is not the limiter. The board is.

### 3.2 Board Lumped-Capacitance Model (burst)

Model the board as a single thermal capacitance with a convective path to ambient:

$$C_{th}\frac{dT}{dt} = P - \frac{T - T_a}{R_{th,BA}}
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
T(t) = T_a + P R_{th}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right), \quad \tau = R_{th} C_{th}$$

Estimated values for a 25.5 mm, 8-layer board:

- **Heat capacity:** about 9 g of PCB, copper, FETs and caps at an effective c_p ≈ 0.7 J/g·K gives C_th ≈ 6.3 J/K.
- **Thermal resistance to ambient:**
  - Still air: R_th ≈ 25 K/W.
  - Prop wash at roughly 10 m/s: R_th ≈ 6 K/W.

Results at P = 96 W, T_a = 35 °C:

| Condition | τ | ΔT after 2 s | ΔT after 5 s | Steady state |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Still air | 158 s | 30 K | 75 K | not viable |
| Prop wash | 38 s | 30 K | 72 K | 576 K (not viable) |
