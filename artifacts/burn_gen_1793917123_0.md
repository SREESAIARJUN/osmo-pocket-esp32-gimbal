# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #7): Power Stage, Thermal, and Bi-Directional DShot Architecture

## 0. Architecture Assumptions

The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs directly. It runs ArduPilot as the flight controller and sends DShot600 frames to four ESC MCUs. Those MCUs run AM32 (or BLHeli_32) firmware and perform 6-step commutation with 48 kHz complementary PWM.

> **Reality check up front:** 80 A per channel on a nano-format board is a burst rating lasting a few seconds at most. It is not a continuous rating. The model below shows why quantitatively.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step complementary PWM

At any instant exactly two phase legs conduct:

- **PWM'd leg:** the high-side FET (HS) conducts for duty D. The low-side FET of the same leg conducts synchronously for (1 − D), so it carries freewheel current.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET is on for the whole 60° sector.

With synchronous rectification, the current therefore always flows through two FET channels (dead-time intervals are handled in §2). The instantaneous conduction loss per motor channel is:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds,on}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{ds,on}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)]$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each of the 6 FETs in a bridge is active in 2 of the 6 sectors (1/3 duty over an electrical cycle). The time-averaged loss per FET is therefore:

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{P_{cond}}{3}$$

The split between HS and LS shifts with D. At high throttle, the HS carries more of the loss.

**Is averaging over an electrical cycle valid?**

- Example motor: 2207, 1950 KV, 6S, 14 poles (7 pole pairs). That gives ≈ 45 krpm mechanical, so f_e ≈ 5.3 kHz and T_e ≈ 190 µs.
- Each FET's on-interval is ≈ 63 µs.
- Die thermal time constants are on the order of 1–10 ms, so averaging over the electrical cycle is valid.

### 1.4 Self-consistent junction-temperature solution

The coupled equations are:

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}(T_j)\cdot R_{\theta JC}, \qquad P_{FET}(T_j)=\tfrac{2}{3}I^2R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Solving in closed form:

$$T_j = \frac{T_{pcb} + k\,R_{\theta JC}\,(1-25\alpha)}{1 - k\,\alpha\,R_{\theta JC}},\qquad k=\tfrac{2}{3}I^2R_{25}$$

Numerical evaluation at I = 80 A:

- k = (2/3)(6400)(0.0015) = 6.4 W
- k·α·R_θJC = 6.4 × 0.005 × 1.2 = 0.0384, which is much less than 1, so the solution is stable (no thermal runaway at the FET level).

### 1.5 Copper and shunt losses (often larger than designers expect)

**Phase pads and traces**

- Assume about 0.3 mΩ effective resistance per phase path (2 oz copper, multilayer pour plus pad).
- Two phases carry current at any time: P = 2 × 80² × 0.3 mΩ ≈ 3.8 W per channel.

**Current shunt**

- If one shared 0.25 mΩ shunt is used in the battery return: I_bus ≈ I_phase × D.
- At D = 0.8 the bus current is 64 A, so a single shared shunt dissipates (4 × 64)² × 0.25 mΩ ≈ 16 W.
- That is why per-channel or Hall-based sensing is preferred. At 64 A per channel, a per-channel 0.25 mΩ shunt dissipates ≈ 1.0 W.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches. Per channel, per PWM period:

### 2.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap (HS turn-on and turn-off)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(30\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges)

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(400\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

This term is significant. Reducing dead time to 100 ns halves it, but requires verified gate-driver propagation matching.

### 2.3 Reverse recovery (the LS body diode, recovered by HS turn-on)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}(25.2)(48\text{k}) = 0.048\ W$$

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}\ (\text{energy-equivalent approx.}) \approx 0.036\ W$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (mostly dissipated in the driver and gate resistors)

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2(50\text{n})(10)(48\text{k}) = 0.048\ W$$

### 2.6 Switching total

$$P_{sw} \approx 2.8\ W \text{ per channel}$$

**Important nuance:** at 100% throttle, AM32 stops PWM switching on the active leg. Only commutation edges at f_e ≈ 5 kHz remain, so P_sw drops by about 10×. The worst case for switching loss is therefore high current at partial duty (D ≈ 0.85–0.95), such as punch-outs and prop-wash recovery.
