# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #37): Power-Stage, Thermal & Bidirectional DShot600 Design Package

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

Three points must be settled before any of the math means anything.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In a 4-in-1 stack, the H743 runs ArduPilot and emits DShot600. Each ESC channel has its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071. A single H743 doing 4× sensorless FOC/6-step plus flight control is not supported by ArduPilot. The netlists below use the standard split architecture.
2. **"80A burst" × 4 on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board is thermally marginal.** The model below shows it is a burst of a few seconds, not a continuous rating. That conclusion matters more than any single number.
3. **The MOSFET parameters are a representative placeholder.** They describe a 40 V, PQFN 3.3×3.3 class device. Replace them with your selected part's datasheet values and the equations carry through.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation topology
AM32/BLHeli_32 runs 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM. At any instant two phase legs carry current:

- **PWM leg:** current alternates between the high-side (HS) switch during D and the low-side (LS) switch during 1−D. The leg always has exactly one switch conducting.
- **Return leg:** the LS switch is held on.

So at every instant **two channels of R_DS(on) are in series with the motor current**, regardless of duty cycle.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent resistance
$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At a design junction temperature of 100 °C, the factor is 1 + 0.0045·75 = 1.3375:
- Typical: R = 1.34 mΩ
- Worst case: R = 1.74 mΩ

### 1.3 RMS current including PWM ripple
$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12},\qquad \Delta I_{pp}=\frac{(V_{bus}-E)\,D}{L_{\phi\phi}\,f_{sw}}$$

Take L_φφ = 20 µH (typical 2207–2812 class motor), worst case at D = 0.5 with E ≈ V_bus/2:
- ΔI_pp = (12.6 · 0.5)/(20 µH · 48 kHz) = 6.6 A
- Ripple contribution: ΔI²/12 = 3.6 A²

This is negligible against 6400 A². Low-inductance motors (<5 µH) are different: the ripple becomes significant and should not be ignored.

### 1.4 Per-channel and board totals
$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

| Case | Per channel | Per FET (peak instantaneous) | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|---|
| Typical | 2·6404·1.34 mΩ = **17.2 W** | 8.6 W | 68.6 W |
| Worst case | 2·6404·1.74 mΩ = **22.3 W** | 11.1 W | 89.1 W |

Per-switch time-averaged dissipation over an electrical cycle is lower because of rotation (each LS conducts about 1/3 of the time). The table uses the peak instantaneous value deliberately: thermal time constants of the die (~ms) are comparable to the commutation period at low eRPM, so peak governs T_j.

### 1.5 Copper (PCB) conduction loss
$$P_{Cu} = I^2 \cdot N_{sq}\cdot R_{sq}(T),\qquad R_{sq,2oz}\approx 0.25\ \text{m}\Omega/\text{sq}\ @25^\circ\text{C}$$

Assume a phase path of 4 squares on each of 4 parallel 2 oz layers, giving an effective 1 square, so R ≈ 0.25 mΩ, or 0.33 mΩ at 100 °C (copper α = 0.0039).
- Per phase: P_Cu = 6400 · 0.33 mΩ = **2.1 W**
- Two active phases plus shared bus copper: **about 5 W per channel**

**This is not negligible.** It is why nano ESCs need at least 6–8 layers of 2 oz copper.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The HS turn-on is a hard commutation from the LS body diode.

### 2.1 Gate current and transition times
$$Q_{sw} = Q_{gd} + \tfrac{1}{2}Q_{gs} = 9\ \text{nC}$$

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{src}+R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}} = \frac{7}{10.7} = 0.65\ \text{A} \Rightarrow t_r = \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{g,on}} \approx 14\ \text{ns}$$
