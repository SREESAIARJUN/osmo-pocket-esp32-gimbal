# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: System Architecture Package (Job #1)

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

This architecture is built on four premises:

1. **The H743 is the flight controller (FC), not the ESC controller.** Each motor channel needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, for example an AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 commands those four MCUs over bidirectional DShot600 (BDShot600). It does not commutate the motors itself. ArduPilot has no on-board BLDC commutation path, and running commutation for four motors alongside the attitude loop would be a poor partition.
2. **"Nano" form factor.** 80 A burst per motor is not credible on a 20×20 mm board. This package assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz power-plane ESC stack** with a separate 20×20 or 30.5 FC board. Section 2.6 calculates the 20×20 case to show why it fails.
3. **Supply is 6S** (25.2 V maximum, 22.2 V nominal), with a 48 kHz complementary (synchronous) PWM at the ESC.
4. **Device parameters are representative 40 V DFN3.3×3.3 MOSFET values, not a specific part.** Substitute datasheet values for your selected FET and re-run the equations. Every result below is parametric.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

The ESC uses six-step trapezoidal drive with complementary PWM. In each commutation sector, current flows from the PWM leg (high side **or** synchronous low side, alternating with duty D) through the motor and into the fully-on low side of the return leg. At any instant, exactly two MOSFET channels carry the phase current:

$$
P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2\left[D\,R_{HS} + (1-D)\,R_{LS} + R_{LS,ret}\right] \approx 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_J)
$$

When high-side and low-side FETs are identical, this is **independent of duty cycle**. This is the main benefit of synchronous rectification.
