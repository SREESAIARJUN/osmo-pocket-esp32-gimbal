## 0. Architecture Decisions and Assumptions

Several premises in the brief need correcting before any numbers are meaningful.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot outputs DShot. Each motor needs its own ESC MCU running sensorless commutation, for example an AT32F421 running AM32, which supports bidirectional DShot and EDT. The architecture is therefore:

> H743 (ArduPilot) → 4× DShot600 bidirectional → 4× AT32F421 (AM32) → 4× 3-phase gate driver → 24 N-channel MOSFETs

**2. "80A burst" on a nano form factor is a transient rating, not a thermal steady state.** The model below shows that a 20×20 or 30×30 board can hold 80 A per motor for only about 2–3 s. Continuous capability is roughly 25–30 A per motor with prop wash. I recommend specifying the product as **"80 A burst (≤2 s), 30 A continuous"** and stating the airflow condition.

**3. Co-locating an H743 with 24 power FETs on one PCB is thermally hostile.** The H743 is rated to Tj = 125 °C (industrial grade). I recommend either:
- a stacked design: 30×30 ESC board plus FC board on 8-pin harness or board-to-board connector, or
- a single board with a slot and copper keep-out isolating the MCU/IMU region.

The IMU also suffers thermally induced gyro bias drift if it sits on the power copper.
