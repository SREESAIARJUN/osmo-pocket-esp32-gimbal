## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path per commutation step

With six-step commutation and complementary PWM, two phases conduct at once:

- **Switched leg:**
  - The high-side FET conducts for duty fraction D.
  - The low-side FET synchronously rectifies for (1 − D).
- **Static leg:** the low-side FET conducts 100 % of the step.

### 1.2 Equations

$$R_{hot} = k_T \cdot R_{DS(on),25} = 1.5 \times 0.70\,\text{m}\Omega = 1.05\,\text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond,switched} = I^2 R_{hot}\,[D + (1-D)] = I^2 R_{hot}$$

$$P_{cond,static} = I^2 R_{hot}$$

$$\boxed{P_{cond,ch} = 2 I^2 R_{hot}} \quad \text{(independent of D under synchronous rectification)}$$

### 1.3 At 80 A

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.05\times10^{-3} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.05\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{13.44\ W}$$

### 1.4 Copper and shunt additions

| Element | Resistance | Loss @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase pad + trace (2 oz, ~8 mm, wide pour) | ~0.15 mΩ × 2 legs | 1.92 W |
| Per-ESC current shunt (if fitted) | 0.25 mΩ | 1.60 W |

Board-level copper is computed separately in §3. It heats the board but not the junctions directly.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap (high-side FET)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.935\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (low-side body diode)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$

### 2.3 Output-capacitance energy (both FETs in the leg)

$$E_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} = 0.756\,\mu\text{J}$$

$$P_{oss} = 2E_{oss}f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.073\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period)

$$P_{dt} = V_f \, I \, (2 t_d) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.229\ W}$$

> ⚠ Dead-time loss is about 64 % as large as overlap loss. Cutting t_d from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per channel. Characterize shoot-through margin on the bench before doing this.

### 2.5 Gate drive (mostly dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET)

$$P_g = Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 60\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.029\ \text{W per FET}$$

That is 0.17 W across 6 FETs.
