## 0. Scope and Critical Design Notes

Three issues frame everything that follows.

1. **Partitioning.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller MCU. It does not commutate the motors. Each ESC channel runs its own MCU, either 4× STM32G071 or AT32F421 on AM32, or a single-die 4-channel BLHeli_32 part. The 48 kHz PWM is generated on the ESC side. The H743 only generates DShot600 and decodes the bidirectional eRPM telemetry.
2. **80 A per channel on a nano form factor is a burst rating, not a continuous one.** The math below shows that roughly 95 W of total stage dissipation cannot be held in steady state on a 30×30 mm board. The deliverable is therefore a **time-limited burst rating (≈5 s)** with a defined starting temperature.
3. **Supplier data must be confirmed.** All MOSFET and driver parameters are representative values. They must be replaced with the selected supplier datasheet values, and the calculations are written parametrically so this is a substitution exercise.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.0 × (1 + 0.006 × 100) = **1.6 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Per-channel conduction (trapezoidal, synchronous)

During any 60° commutation sector, two legs are active:

- **PWM leg:** high side conducts for D, low side (synchronous) conducts for 1−D. Combined loss ≈ I²R, independent of D.
- **Return leg:** low side conducts 100% of the sector, giving I²R.
