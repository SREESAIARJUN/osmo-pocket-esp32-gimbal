## 0. Architecture Assumptions

A 4-in-1 ESC is normally not commutated by the H743. It needs four independent closed-loop BLDC controllers. This note therefore uses the standard AIO split:

| Domain | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100), 480 MHz | ArduPilot, sensors, bidirectional DShot600 master |
| ESC MCU ×4 | AT32F421K8U7 (or STM32G071) | AM32 firmware, six-step commutation, 48 kHz PWM, eRPM telemetry |
| Gate driver ×4 | FD6288Q (3-phase, bootstrapped) | 1.0 A source / 1.5 A sink |
| Power stage | 24× 40 V N-MOSFET, DFN3.3×3.3 | One FET per switch position |
| Bus | 6S LiPo: V_bus = 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal | |

**Design check (read first):** "80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board is a time-limited rating, not a continuous one. Section 3 shows it is defensible only for about 1–3 s windows, with airflow and a pre-burst temperature below 60 °C. Datasheet and marketing claims must state the burst duration.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current Path in Six-Step with Synchronous Rectification

In each 60° commutation sector, one phase is driven high, one is held low, and one floats. AM32 uses complementary PWM, so:

- **During the PWM ON-time (D):** current flows through HS(phase A), the motor, and LS(phase B).
- **During the PWM OFF-time (1−D):** current flows through LS(phase A), the motor, and LS(phase B). This is the synchronous freewheel, and the body diode carries current only during dead time.

At every instant the motor current therefore passes through exactly two conducting channels. The instantaneous conduction loss per ESC channel is:

$$
P_{cond,ESC} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2 \cdot R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

This holds independent of duty cycle. Ripple adds a small correction, covered in 1.4.

### 1.2 Temperature-Dependent R_ds(on)

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right]
$$

| T_j | R_ds |
|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.50 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 2.12 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 2.33 mΩ |

### 1.3 Burst Conduction Loss at I_ph = 80 A

Evaluated at T_j = 125 °C (worst case):

$$
P_{cond,ESC} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 2.33\,\text{m}\Omega = 6400 \times 4.66\,\text{m} = \mathbf{29.8\ W}
$$

**Per-FET distribution:** A high-side FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical period at duty D. A low-side FET conducts for 1/3 as the return path, plus (1−D)·1/3 as the freewheel.

At full-throttle burst (D ≈ 0.95):

$$
P_{HS} \approx \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I^2 R = \tfrac{1}{3}(0.95)(6400)(2.33\text{m}) = 4.72\ \text{W}
$$

$$
P_{LS} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}\left[1 + (1-D)\right] I^2 R = \tfrac{1}{3}(1.05)(14.91) = 5.22\ \text{W}
$$

The check closes: 3 × (4.72 + 5.22) = 29.8 W. **The low-side FETs are the hottest devices** and should get the most copper.

### 1.4 PWM Ripple Correction

Motor phase inductance is L ≈ 15 µH (typical 2207 at 1900 KV). Worst-case ripple occurs at D = 0.5:

$$
\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L\,f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{15\mu \times 48\text{k}} = 8.75\ \text{A}
$$

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I_{dc}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 6.4
$$

This is a +0.1 % change, so it is negligible at 80 A.

### 1.5 Copper and Shunt Losses

| Element | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| Shunt, 0.25 mΩ (bus return) | 80² × 0.25 m | 1.6 W |
| PCB copper, 2 oz, ~0.3 mΩ effective path | 80² × 0.3 m | 1.9 W |

The shunt power can halve if you use 0.125 mΩ with a gain-50 amplifier.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switched positions incur hard-switching loss. With complementary PWM, the HS FET hard-switches. The LS FET in the same leg soft-switches (ZVS via body diode) but generates the Q_rr event.

### 2.1 Gate Drive Timing

FD6288Q: V_drv = 12 V, R_g,ext = 4.7 Ω (turn-on), with a diode bypass for turn-off.

$$
I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}+R_{drv,on}} = \frac{12-3.2}{4.7+1.2+8} \approx 0.63\ \text{A}
$$

$$
t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd} + Q_{gs}/2}{I_{g,on}} = \frac{16\text{nC}}{0.63} \approx 25\ \text{ns}
$$
