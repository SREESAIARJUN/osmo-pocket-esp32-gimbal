## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0045 × 100) = **1.45 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current path

**Trapezoidal (6-step) drive with synchronous rectification:**
- During any commutation sector, two phase legs are active.
- One leg has its high-side FET PWM'd at duty D. Its complementary low-side FET conducts during the remaining (1−D) of the period.
- The return leg's low-side FET conducts continuously.

So exactly two FET channels carry I at every instant:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\, I^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

**FOC / sinusoidal drive:** with phase RMS current I_ph,rms, the loss is P = 3·I_ph,rms²·R_DS. Because I_ph,rms = I_pk/√2 for 80 A peak sinusoidal phase current, the result is lower: 3 × 3200 × 1.45 mΩ = 13.9 W. The 6-step case below is the worst case.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.45\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{18.6\ W}$$

### 1.4 Self-consistent junction temperature

T_j depends on R_DS, and R_DS depends on T_j, so iterate:

$$T_j = T_{board} + P_{FET}\,(R_{\theta JC} + R_{\theta CB}), \qquad P_{FET} = I^2 R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]\cdot k_{share}$$

Here k_share is the fraction of the sector during which that FET conducts. For a continuously conducting low-side FET, k_share = 1/3 averaged over the electrical cycle. Instantaneous loss is still I²R, and that is what drives die temperature on short timescales.

**Instantaneous worst-case FET** (I²R = 9.3 W while conducting):
- ΔT_jc ≈ 9.3 × 1.2 = 11 K.
- ΔT across the solder and vias adds about 28 K, but board thermal capacitance filters this at commutation rates.

This is why cycle-averaged values are used in §3.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd high-side FET hard-switches. Its complementary low-side FET commutates through its body diode during dead time.

### 2.1 V-I overlap loss

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance loss

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.03\ W$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery loss

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.06\ W$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

Two dead-time intervals occur per period:

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive loss

This is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET. Two FETs switch per active PWM leg:

$$P_{gate} = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.06\ W$$

### 2.6 Per-ESC total at 80 A

| Component | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (125 °C) | 18.6 |
| V-I switching | 0.97 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.09 |
| Gate drive | 0.06 |
| **Total per ESC** | **≈ 20.3 W** |
| **4-in-1 total (all motors at 80 A)** | **≈ 81 W** |

**Key insight.** At 48 kHz, conduction is 92 % of the loss. Raising f_sw is cheap. Every 0.1 mΩ of R_DS(on) or of copper is worth about 1.3 W per ESC. **PCB copper resistance matters as much as the FET.** About 0.5 mΩ of phase and bus trace on a nano board adds roughly 6.4 W per motor (I²R in the copper, 80² × 0.5 m × 2 paths).

## 3. Thermal Model: How Long Can "80 A Burst" Last?

### 3.1 Lumped capacitance (adiabatic, worst case)

Assume a 30.5 × 30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz board:

| Mass | Contribution |
|---|---|
| FR4 ≈ 4 g × 1.1 J/g·K | 4.4 J/K |
| Copper ≈ 3 g × 0.385 J/g·K | 1.2 J/K |
| 24 FETs + components | ≈ 0.6 J/K |
| **C_th** | **≈ 6.2 J/K** |

### 3.2 Temperature budget

- Averaged junction-to-board rise per FET: 81 W / 24 FETs = 3.4 W, times (1.2 + 3.0) K/W ≈ **14 K**.
- Board budget: 125 − 14 − 40 = **71 K**.
