### 0.2 Architecture Facts That Shape the Design

1. **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz PWM.** On a 4-in-1 AIO, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU (typically AT32F421 or STM32G071) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 only sends DShot600 and receives bidirectional eRPM telemetry. The 48 kHz switching model therefore lives in the ESC firmware domain.
2. **Package fit is a gating risk.** The H743 in LQFP100 (14×14 mm) is very difficult to fit on a 20×20-hole AIO alongside four ESC MCUs and 24 FETs. Use the TFBGA100 (8×8 mm) package. That choice forces at least 6 layers, via-in-pad or 0.4 mm-pitch-capable fab rules, and BGA escape routing.
3. **Headline finding: 80 A is a short-burst rating, not a thermal rating.** On this footprint, full four-motor 80 A burst produces about 130 W of loss. The board can absorb that for roughly 1 s before the PCB passes 100 °C (§3). The spec sheet must define "burst" as a duration, for example "80 A / 1 s, 1 motor" or "65 A / 5 s". Otherwise the product will be returned for burnt FETs.

### 1.1 Current Paths in 6-Step Commutation with Synchronous PWM

In each 60° commutation sector, one phase PWMs high, one phase is held low, and one phase floats. Taking the A+ / B− sector as an example:

- **PWM on-time (fraction D):** HS_A carries I, and LS_B carries I.
- **PWM off-time (1−D), synchronous rectification:** LS_A carries I, and LS_B carries I.
