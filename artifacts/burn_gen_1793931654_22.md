# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage, Thermal & DShot Architecture

**Document:** Job #5 Technical Design Basis, Rev A

---

## 0. Feasibility Findings

These findings change the design brief, so they come first.

| # | Finding | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | The STM32H743 should **not** commutate the motors. Each ESC channel needs its own low-latency 6-step/FOC loop with complementary PWM, dead-time, BEMF comparators and ADC sync. ArduPilot cannot do this, and one H743 cannot do it for 4 motors alongside the flight stack. | **Architecture:** H743 = flight controller (ArduPilot). 4× AT32F421K8U7 (or STM32G071) = ESC MCUs running AM32 with bidirectional DShot600. |
| F2 | **80 A burst on a 20×20 mm ("nano") board is thermally impossible as a sustained rating.** Section 3 shows ~143 W of loss with all four motors at 80 A, against a board heat capacity of ~1.5 J/K. | Burst duration is **≈0.4–0.7 s** before the junction limit. Realistic 20×20 ratings: **~18 A continuous / ~40 A burst** (Sec. 3.5). |
| F3 | A true 80 A burst / 50 A continuous rating needs a **30.5×30.5 mm** board, DFN5×6 FETs (or 2× paralleled DFN3.3), and ≥8-layer 2 oz copper. | The netlist (Sec. 5) is written so the **same schematic** fits either footprint. Only the FET package and BOM quantities change. |

All device parameters below are **representative values for a 40 V, ~1.4 mΩ-class DFN MOSFET**. Before committing the BOM, replace them with the selected part's datasheet values (R_DS(on) vs. T_j curve, Q_gd, Q_rr, Coss).
