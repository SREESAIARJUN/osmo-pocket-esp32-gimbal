**Feasibility flag: confirm before layout.**

- Twelve 5×6 mm FETs plus drivers, an MCU and bulk capacitance do not fit a 20×20 mm "nano" footprint at 80 A.
- Commercial 20×20 4-in-1 boards top out around 40–55 A burst using 3.3×3.3 FETs.
- For 80 A burst you need one of these:
  - a 30.5×30.5 mm board or larger, or
  - a split stack (FC 20×20 over an ESC at 30×30 or larger).

**Architecture assumed in this document.**

- The H743 runs ArduPilot.
- Each ESC channel has its own small MCU (for example an AT32F421 running AM32) driving a 3-phase gate driver.
- The H743 does not commutate motors. It only generates DShot.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

The model assumes trapezoidal (6-step) commutation with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM). At any instant, motor current flows through exactly two MOSFETs in series:

- one device in the PWM leg (high side during t_on, low side during t_off), and
- the static low side of the return leg.

Therefore, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

(For FOC/sinusoidal drive, replace with $P = 3\,I_{ph,rms}^2 R_{ds}$, where the peak current is √2·I_rms.)

### 1.2 Temperature-coupled iteration

Because R_ds depends on T_j, solve self-consistently:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_{board} + P_{FET}\big(T_j^{(k)}\big)\cdot R_{th,jb}$$

Take T_board = 90 °C, the late-burst board temperature (see §3).

| Iteration | T_j (°C) | R_ds (mΩ) | P_cond,ch (W) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 100 | 1.204 | 15.41 |
| 1 | 98.6 | 1.198 | 15.33 |
| converged | ≈98.5 | 1.198 | **15.3 W** |

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Over one electrical revolution, each FET carries current for one third of the cycle, so the average is:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6}\cdot 3 \cdot \tfrac{1}{3}\cdot 2 = \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6}\cdot... \approx 2.6\ \text{W}$$

**Worst case to design against.** The thermal time constant of the junction to its pad is about 10–50 ms. Below roughly 2,000 eRPM (startup or stall), a single FET carries the full current for long stretches:

$$P_{FET,peak} = I^2 R_{ds} = 6400 \times 1.198\,\text{m}\Omega = 7.7\ \text{W}$$
