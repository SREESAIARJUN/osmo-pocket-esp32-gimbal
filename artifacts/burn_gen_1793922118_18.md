# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Package

## 0. Architecture Correction and Assumptions

**Clarification:** The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs directly. On an AIO design, the H743 is the flight controller MCU. It sends DShot to **four dedicated ESC MCUs** (or one quad-ESC MCU), which run AM32 or BLHeli_32 and handle commutation. This document models that architecture.

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V max (worst case), 22.2 V nominal |
| Board | 30.5 × 30.5 mm mounting (≈36 × 36 mm outline), 8-layer, 2 oz copper all layers |
| ESC MCU | AT32F421 ×4, AM32 firmware |
| Gate driver | FD6288Q (3-phase, bootstrap, 10 V gate drive) |
| MOSFET | 40 V, 5×6 DFN class: R_DS(on) = 1.0 mΩ max at 25 °C, Q_g = 60 nC, C_oss = 1.5 nF, Q_rr = 40 nC, V_SD = 0.8 V, R_θJC = 1.2 K/W |
| PWM | 48 kHz center-aligned, complementary with synchronous rectification, dead time t_dt = 100 ns |
| Commutation | Trapezoidal six-step |

**Feasibility warning:** An "80 A burst" rating on a 20×20 "nano" board is not physically credible, and the thermal analysis below shows why. I have sized this design at 30.5 mm. If the job truly requires 20×20, the realistic figure is about 40–45 A burst. Replace the MOSFET parameters above with the values from your actual BOM part. The equations are general.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (per motor, 80 A burst)

In six-step commutation, two phase legs conduct at any instant: one high-side path (PWM'd, with complementary low-side synchronous rectification) and one low-side return. Because synchronous rectification puts every conduction interval through a channel R_DS(on), the current path always sees exactly 2 × R_DS(on), regardless of duty cycle.

**Hot R_DS(on):** At T_J ≈ 125 °C, the typical temperature coefficient gives
R_DS,hot ≈ 1.6 × R_DS,25 = **1.6 mΩ**

**Total conduction loss per motor:**
P_cond = I² · 2 · R_DS,hot = 80² × 2 × 1.6 mΩ = **20.5 W**

**Phase RMS current** (each phase conducts 2/3 of the electrical period):
I_ph,rms = I · √(2/3) = 65.3 A

**Per half-bridge leg:**
P_leg = I_ph,rms² · R_DS,hot = 4267 × 1.6 mΩ = 6.83 W
Three legs × 6.83 W = 20.5 W, which matches the total above.

**Per FET (average):** 20.5 W / 6 ≈ **3.4 W**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

At any instant, only one leg is hard-switching: the PWM'd high side, with its complementary low side.

### 2.1 Voltage/current overlap loss

The edge times come from the gate charge and driver current:
t_r ≈ t_f ≈ Q_gd / I_g,plateau ≈ 15 ns, using FD6288 at about 1 A with R_g chosen accordingly.

P_ov = ½ · V_bus · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw
= 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 30 ns × 48 kHz = **1.45 W**

### 2.2 Output capacitance loss

P_oss = ½ · C_oss · V² · f_sw
= 0.5 × 1.5 nF × 25.2² × 48 kHz = **0.023 W**

### 2.3 Body-diode reverse recovery

P_rr = Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw
= 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 kHz = **0.048 W**

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM cycle.

P_dt = V_SD · I · 2 · t_dt · f_sw
= 0.8 × 80 × 2 × 100 ns × 48 kHz = **0.61 W**

### 2.5 Gate drive loss

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FETs. Two FETs switch per cycle.

P_g = Q_g · V_GS · f_sw · 2
= 60 nC × 10 × 48 kHz × 2 = **0.058 W**

### 2.6 Loss totals at 80 A

| Loss | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 20.50 W | 82.0 W |
| V/I overlap | 1.45 W | 5.8 W |
| Dead time | 0.61 W | 2.4 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.07 W | 0.3 W |
| Gate drive | 0.06 W | 0.2 W |
| **Total** | **≈22.7 W** | **≈90.8 W** |

At 80 A, conduction loss is about 90% of the total. The 48 kHz switching frequency is not the thermal limiter.

The switching-related terms (P_ov + P_dt) scale linearly with f_sw:
- At 24 kHz: −1.0 W per motor
- At 96 kHz: +2.1 W per motor

48 kHz is a reasonable compromise between motor current ripple and acoustic noise on one side, and switching loss on the other.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Junction-to-case rise (worst-case FET)

The worst-case FET dissipates about 3.4 W of conduction loss plus about 0.7 W of switching share, for **P_FET ≈ 4.1 W**.

ΔT_JC = P_FET × R_θJC = 4.1 × 1.2 = **4.9 K**

Because ΔT_JC is small, the board temperature dominates. The design problem is therefore spreading heat through copper, not the FET package.
