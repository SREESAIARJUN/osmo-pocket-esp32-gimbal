## 0. Architecture Premises and Feasibility

Three points shape the rest of this document.

1. **Partitioning.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller running ArduPilot. The 4-in-1 ESC uses four dedicated ESC MCUs (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32), one per motor. The H743 does not commutate motors. It only sources DShot600 and decodes the bidirectional eRPM telemetry.
2. **The 80A rating is a burst rating.** The thermal model below shows a 20×20 mm stack can absorb 80A on all four channels for roughly 1 to 2 s, limited by board heat capacity. It cannot sustain that. A continuous rating of about 25–35A per channel is the honest figure for this form factor. The 80A spec should state duty (for example, 1 s on, 10 s off).
3. **Datasheet parameters are placeholders.** The model is fully parametric. The values in the tables are representative of a 40V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN MOSFET class. Replace them with the selected part's datasheet values before release.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology Basis

The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal drive with **complementary (synchronous) PWM**, which is the AM32/BLHeli_32 default.

In each commutation sector:
- One phase is PWM-modulated. The current alternates between its high-side (HS) FET (fraction D of the period) and its low-side (LS) FET (fraction 1−D, synchronous rectification).
- A second phase holds its LS FET on for the full 120° sector.
- The third phase floats and is used for back-EMF sensing.

### 1.2 Per-Channel Equations

Hot on-resistance:

$$R_{ds,hot} = k_T \cdot R_{ds,25} = 1.4\ \text{m}\Omega$$

Conduction loss in the PWM'd leg (ignoring ripple):

$$P_{cond,PWM} = I^2 R_{ds,hot}\,[D + (1-D)] = I^2 R_{ds,hot}$$

This is independent of D because synchronous rectification always provides a channel path.

Conduction loss in the static leg:

$$P_{cond,static} = I^2 R_{ds,hot}$$

Total per channel, including copper:

$$P_{cond} = I^2\,(2R_{ds,hot} + R_{phase,eff})$$

Optional ripple correction. Replace I² with I_rms²:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \quad \Delta I = \frac{V_{bus} D (1-D)}{L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

For a motor with L_ph ≈ 10 µH at D = 0.5:
- ΔI ≈ 13 A
- ΔI²/12 ≈ 14 A², which is less than 0.3% of 6400 A². This term is negligible.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| Term | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| PWM leg | 80² × 1.4 mΩ | 8.96 W |
| Static leg | 80² × 1.4 mΩ | 8.96 W |
| Phase copper (2 conducting phases) | 80² × 2 × 0.2 mΩ | 2.56 W |
| **P_cond per channel** | | **≈ 20.5 W** |

### 1.4 Shared Battery Bus (Critical)

All four channels draw through the shared battery bus. With every channel at 80 A, the average battery current is:

$$I_{bat} \approx \sum D \cdot I_{ph}$$

At full throttle (D → 1), I_bat approaches 320 A, giving:

$$P_{bus} = 320^2 \times 0.3\ \text{m}\Omega \approx 30.7\ \text{W}$$

**Design consequence:** a 320 A instantaneous draw exceeds XT60 (≈60 A continuous) and typical 6S pack C-ratings. In practice, the system-level burst is bounded by the battery. The 4×80 A case is therefore a worst-case thermal envelope, not a realistic sustained flight state.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

These losses are per channel. Only one leg switches at a time.

### 2.1 Hard-Switching V·I Overlap (HS FET)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = 0.97\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction (Two Edges per Period)

$$P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2t_d \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = 1.23\ \text{W}$$

This is the largest switching term. Two ways to reduce it:
- Cut dead time to about 100 ns, which the FD6288-class driver allows if gate loops are tight. This halves the term.
- Choose a FET with a lower-V_f integrated Schottky.

### 2.3 Reverse Recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{kHz} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Output Capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \approx 40\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{kHz} \approx 0.05\ \text{W}$$

This is the charge-based upper bound.

### 2.5 Gate Drive

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FETs.

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\,\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\,\text{kHz} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$
