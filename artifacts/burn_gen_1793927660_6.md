# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #49): Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, DShot DMA Plan, and Reference Netlist

## 0. Architecture and Feasibility Findings

Two points shape everything below.

**1. The H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC.** The H743 runs ArduPilot and generates bidirectional DShot600. Each motor channel (or one quad-channel MCU) runs ESC firmware (AM32 on AT32F421 or STM32G071) and does the 48 kHz power-stage PWM. The loss model applies to the ESC power stage. The DMA/timer model applies to the H743.

**2. "80A burst" on a nano-form-factor board is thermally limited by heat capacity, not by the MOSFETs.** The math in Section 3 shows:
- All four motors at 80A can be sustained for only about 2 s on a 20×20 mm board.
- One motor at 80A can be sustained for about 8–9 s.

The rating must therefore be specified as **per-motor, single-channel, ≤5 s burst**, or the board must move to a 30.5 mm footprint. I recommend publishing the rating with explicit conditions (Section 3.5).

All component parameters below are representative values for a 40V DFN5×6 MOSFET class. Replace them with datasheet maxima from the selected part before release.

| I_g | Effective gate current (FD6288-class driver, R_g = 4.7 Ω) | ≈1.0 A source / 1.5 A sink |
| Q_rr | Body-diode reverse-recovery charge | 40 nC |
| Q_oss | Output charge at 25 V | 50 nC |
| V_f | Body-diode forward voltage at 80 A | 0.8 V |
| t_dt | Dead time per edge | 200 ns |
| R_cu | PCB copper per conduction path (bus + phase pad) | 0.5 mΩ |

**Commutation model:** 6-step trapezoidal with complementary (synchronous) PWM, as used by AM32. In any sector:
- The active leg alternates high-side (duty D) and low-side (1 − D).
- One low-side MOSFET in the return leg conducts continuously.

So at every instant, exactly two R_ds are in series with the phase current.

## 2. Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

Temperature-corrected resistance:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)] = 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.51 = 1.81\,\text{m}\Omega$$

RMS current including PWM ripple ΔI:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

With ΔI ≈ 8 A (typical for a low-inductance 2306-class motor at 48 kHz), the ripple adds only 0.07 %. It is negligible.

Conduction loss:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j) = 6400 \times 3.62\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{23.2\ W}$$

Copper loss:

$$P_{cu} = I^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

This loss is spread over the board, which helps the thermal picture.

### 2.2 Switching losses (hard-switched edges on the active leg)

Transition time per edge:

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_g}$$

- Rise: t_r ≈ 18 nC / 1.0 A = 18 ns
- Fall: t_f ≈ 18 nC / 1.5 A = 12 ns

V–I overlap loss:

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

Reverse recovery (high-side turn-on into the conducting low-side body diode):

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

The reverse-recovery current also adds roughly 10–15 % to the turn-on loss. That brings P_VI to about **1.65 W**.

Output-capacitance loss:

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss}V_{bus} f_{sw}\times 2 = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

Dead-time body-diode conduction (two dead times per period):

$$P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times400\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

Gate drive (2 FETs × 1 switching leg):

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2\times60\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$
