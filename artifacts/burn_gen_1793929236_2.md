## 1. Conduction-Path Model (per ESC channel)

In 6-step commutation, two phase legs carry the motor current at any instant:
- **PWM leg:** HS conducts for duty *D*, LS (sync rect) for 1−*D*
- **Return leg:** LS conducts continuously during the 60° sector

So at any instant exactly **two MOSFET channels** carry *I*, independent of *D* (ignoring dead time). That gives the governing equation:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + I^2 R_{Cu} + I^2 R_{shunt}$$

### 1.1 Temperature-corrected R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.004\ /\text{K}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.0 × 1.40 = **1.40 mΩ**

### 1.2 Copper and shunt

- Shunt: 0.5 mΩ (2512 size, one per channel or one common)
- Trace and plane path (phase pad → FET → bus): ~0.4 mΩ, from R = ρL/(wt) with 3 oz copper, 6 mm wide, about 15 mm path, two layers in parallel

### 1.3 Conduction loss at 80 A

$$P_{FET,cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.40\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{17.9\ W}$$
$$P_{shunt} = 80^2 \times 0.5\times10^{-3} = 3.2\ \text{W}$$
$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.4\times10^{-3} = 2.6\ \text{W}$$

**Conduction subtotal: 23.7 W per channel at 80 A.**

Because the 3.2 W shunt loss is significant, consider sensing current through a single 0.25 mΩ common shunt, or use R_DS(on) sensing.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg commutates hard. Its HS switches against the LS body diode or channel.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

Rise and fall times come from the Miller plateau:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,R_{g,tot}}{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}},\qquad t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,R_{g,tot}}{V_{plateau}}$$

With Q_gd = 10 nC, R_g,tot = 5 Ω (driver + 2.2 Ω external + internal), V_drv = 10 V, and V_pl = 4.5 V: t_r ≈ 9 ns and t_f ≈ 11 ns. Add di/dt-limited current transition (~5 ns each) to get **t_r + t_f ≈ 30 ns**.

$$P_{VI} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{e-}9 \times 48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{e-}9 \times 25.2 \times 48\text{e}3 = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \times 2 = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{e-}9 \times 48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

This assumes 100 ns dead time per edge, which is FD6288Q internal (~200 ns) trimmed in firmware. Dead time is the dominant secondary loss: every 50 ns removed saves 0.15 W.

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in driver and R_g, not the FET)

$$P_g = Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} \times 2 = 50\text{e-}9 \times 10 \times 48\text{e}3 \times 2 = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

**Switching subtotal: about 2.2 W per channel.**

### 2.6 Frequency sensitivity

$$P_{sw}(f) \approx 2.2\ \text{W} \times \frac{f}{48\ \text{kHz}}$$

| f_sw | P_sw per channel |
|---|---|
| 24 kHz | 1.1 W |
| 48 kHz | 2.2 W |
| 96 kHz | 4.4 W |

At 80 A, 48 kHz costs about 9% of total loss. The benefit is lower current ripple and quieter low-inductance
