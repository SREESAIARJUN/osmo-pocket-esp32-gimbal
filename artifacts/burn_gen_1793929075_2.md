## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Flags

The STM32H743 is the flight controller MCU. It does not commutate motors. A 4-in-1 ESC at 48 kHz needs dedicated ESC MCUs. The reference architecture is:

| Block | Device | Firmware |
|---|---|---|
| FC MCU | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100), 480 MHz | ArduPilot (ChibiOS) |
| ESC MCU ×4 | STM32G071CBU6 or AT32F421K8U7 | AM32 (bi-directional DShot, 48 kHz PWM) |
| Gate driver ×4 | 3-phase half-bridge driver, 1 A / 1.5 A class (e.g., FD6288Q) | n/a |
| Power stage | 24× N-MOSFET, 40 V, 3.3×3.3 PQFN | n/a |

The original spec has three feasibility problems:

1. **"Nano" vs. 80 A.** A 20×20 mm board cannot sustain 80 A per motor. This model assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm** board with 6-layer, 2 oz copper. 80 A is treated as a **≤2 s burst per motor**, which Section 3 derives.
2. **Total battery current.** 4 × 80 A = 320 A simultaneous exceeds the rating of an XT60 (60 A continuous, about 180 A peak) and of the board's input copper and shunt. Use XT90 or direct-soldered 10 AWG leads, and specify a realistic simultaneous burst (e.g., 4 × 80 A for ≤1 s, or 2 × 80 A + 2 × 40 A).
3. **Supply voltage.** The model uses 6S, with V_bus up to 25.2 V. With 40 V FETs, keep inductive overshoot below 35 V. This needs a bulk capacitor plus local MLCCs per leg (Section 5).

## 2. Loss Model

### 2.1 Conduction Losses (6-step synchronous, AM32 complementary PWM)

In 120° trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification, phase current always flows through **exactly two FETs in series**:

- the sourcing leg, through either the HS FET (during D) or the LS sync FET (during 1−D), and
- the sinking leg, through its LS FET (on continuously).
