## 1. Conduction-Loss Model (80 A burst)

### 1.1 Per-bridge conduction

With synchronous rectification (complementary PWM), the phase current always flows through exactly **one high-side and one low-side FET** in series. This holds in 6-step, ignoring dead time.

$$
P_{cond,ESC} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2 \cdot R_{DS(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = k_T \cdot R_{25}
$$

$$
P_{cond,ESC} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 6400 \times 3.0\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W}
$$

**Per-FET average.** In 6-step commutation each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical period. At realistic eRPM, the electrical period is microseconds to milliseconds, far shorter than the die thermal time constant, so the average governs:

$$
\bar P_{FET} = \frac{19.2}{6} = 3.2\ \text{W}, \qquad \hat P_{FET} = 9.6\ \text{W (instantaneous, while conducting)}
$$

### 1.2 Copper / interconnect conduction

$$
R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho_{Cu}(T)\, L}{w\, t}, \qquad \rho_{Cu}(T) = 1.72\times10^{-8}\,[1 + 0.00393\,(T-20)]
$$

Example: a 10 mm phase path, 3 mm wide, 3 oz (105 µm), at 100 °C:

$$
R_{Cu} = \frac{2.26\times10^{-8} \times 0.010}{0.003 \times 105\times10^{-6}} = 0.72\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{Cu,phase} = 80^2 \times 0.72\,\text{m}\Omega = 4.6\ \text{W}
$$

Add the bus return path, vias, and solder joints (~0.1–0.2 mΩ per via cluster). A realistic total is **≈ 0.5–1.5 mΩ per current loop**, so **3–10 W per ESC**. On a nano board, the copper loss is the same order as the FET loss and is frequently underestimated.

### 1.3 Shunt (if per-ESC current sensing)

$$
P_{shunt} = I_{bus}^2 R_{sh}, \quad I_{bus} \approx D \cdot I_{ph}
$$

With a 0.25 mΩ shunt at D = 1: 80² × 0.25 m = **1.6 W**. Most 4-in-1 designs use a single shared shunt on the bus; that shunt then sees up to **4 × 80 = 320 A**, which is not realistic on a nano board (see Section 6).

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per bridge, one leg is actively PWM-switching at a time.

**(a) V–I overlap (hard switching)**

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 0.968\ \text{W}
$$

**(b) Output-capacitance loss**

$$
P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}
$$

(Q_oss·V is used rather than ½·C·V² because C_oss is strongly nonlinear.)

**(c) Reverse recovery (body diode conducts during dead time)**

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 60\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.073\ \text{W}
$$

**(d) Dead-time diode conduction (two dead times per period)**

$$
P_{dt} = V_F I_{ph} \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{n}\times48\text{k} = 0.614\ \text{W}
$$

**(e) Gate drive (dissipated in driver and R_g, not the FET)**

$$
P_{g} = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times N_{\text{switching FETs}} = 50\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k}\times2 = 0.048\ \text{W}
$$

**Switching subtotal per ESC ≈ 1.76 W.**

At 48 kHz, switching loss is under 10 % of conduction loss at 80 A. **The burst problem is entirely I²R.** Raising f_sw from 24 kHz to 48 kHz roughly doubles terms (a) through (e) but buys lower current ripple and quieter motors. That is a reasonable trade.
