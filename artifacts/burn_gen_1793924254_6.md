## 0. Architectural Clarifications

Three design points must be settled before the math is meaningful.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot is flight-controller firmware. It outputs DShot and does not run BLDC commutation or FOC. The 4-in-1 ESC section therefore uses **four dedicated ESC MCUs** running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. The H743 talks to them over bidirectional DShot600. This is the standard AIO topology.
2. **"80A" is defined here as per-channel motor phase current (burst, ≤ ~3 s).** Battery-side current is lower: I_bat ≈ D·I_ph. Even so, four channels at 80 A phase means roughly 270 A of battery current. That exceeds any XT60/XT90 pigtail and any nano-board power entry. See §4.4.
3. **"Nano" form factor.** On a 20×20 mm footprint, 80 A burst is not physically achievable with acceptable junction temperatures. The calculations below assume a **30.5×30.5 mm board (~36×36 mm outline), 6-layer, 2 oz copper on all layers**. That is the smallest realistic platform for this rating.

```
             ┌────────────────────── 36 x 36 mm, 6L 2oz ─────────────────────┐
 VBAT ──┬──► │ Bulk + MLCC bank ──► 4x [ESC MCU + 3φ driver + 6x N-FET]      │──► M1..M4
        │    │                         ▲ DShot600 bidir (1-wire each)         │
        └──► │ 5V/3.3V BEC ──► STM32H743VIT6 (ArduPilot) ── IMU/Baro/OSD/SD  │
             └───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```
