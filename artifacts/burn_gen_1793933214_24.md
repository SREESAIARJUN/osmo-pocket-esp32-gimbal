## 0. Architecture Clarification and Key Findings

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 is the **flight controller** MCU. It does not commutate motors. The architecture is:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──DShot600 bidir──► 4× ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32 firmware)
                                              └─► 3-phase gate driver ─► 6× N-MOSFET ─► motor
```

**Key findings.** These are stated up front because they drive the design.

1. **80 A per channel on a 20×20 mm (nano) board is a sub-second rating, not a sustained one.** The model below gives about **25 W dissipated per channel** at 80 A. With roughly 1 J/K of effective thermal mass, the board reaches MOSFET limits in about **0.5–1 s**. Any "80 A / 10 s" figure on a nano 4-in-1 is not physically supportable without external heatsinking.
2. **Conduction loss dominates**, at about 85% of the total. Switching loss at 48 kHz is about 2 W per channel. Lowering the PWM frequency barely helps; lowering R_DS(on) does.
3. **Input capacitor ripple current reaches about 40 A RMS per channel** at 50% duty. A low-ESR external bulk capacitor is mandatory.
4. **DShot on the H743 is DMA-unconstrained** because DMAMUX1 lets any request use any stream. The real constraints are:
   - timer/pin selection;
   - avoiding CHxN outputs, which cannot do input capture for the bidir telemetry.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current path

The ESC uses 6-step commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. At every instant, current flows through **two** MOSFETs in series:

- The **PWM leg**: high-side (HS) device during the on-time D, low-side (LS) device during the off-time (1−D).
- The **return leg**: LS device on for the full commutation step.
