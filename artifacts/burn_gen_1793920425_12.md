# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #12): Engineering Model and Reference Design

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Two Critical Findings

No component selections, stack-up, or battery voltage were specified, so this document uses explicitly labeled assumptions. **Every number marked [A] must be replaced with datasheet values from your actual parts before release.** The equations are general; the numbers illustrate the method.

### Finding 1: Architecture

ArduPilot does not commutate motors. Even on an "AIO nano" board, the system has two separate firmware domains:

- **STM32H743 (ArduPilot):** sends DShot600 commands and receives bidirectional eRPM telemetry.
- **4× ESC MCUs (e.g., AT32F421 running AM32, or EFM8/BLHeli_S):** each runs 6-step commutation with PWM at 48 kHz.

A single H743 running both ArduPilot and four-motor sensorless FOC/6-step is not a supported configuration. The design below uses the standard split architecture.

### Finding 2: Thermal feasibility

An "80 A burst" rating is not physically sustainable on a 20×20 mm nano board for more than a few seconds per channel. The math in §3 shows:

- Realistic continuous capability is about **12–15 A per channel** (all four channels loaded).
- 80 A on one channel is survivable for roughly **2–4 s adiabatic**, before adding margin.

I recommend either rating the board honestly (e.g., 20 A continuous / 35 A for 10 s) or moving to a 30.5×30.5 mm layout with a heat spreader.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation geometry (6-step trapezoidal, complementary PWM)

At any instant two phases conduct: one high-side leg and one low-side leg, carrying current I.

With complementary (synchronous) PWM at duty D on the high side:

- **Switching leg:** the high-side FET conducts for D, and the low-side FET of the same leg conducts for 1−D.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET conducts 100 %.

So there are always exactly two FET channels in series in the current path, independent of D:

$$P_{cond,FET} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds,on}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,Cu} = 2\,I^2\,R_{cu}(T_{pcb})$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{ds,on}(T_j) = R_{25}\left(\frac{T_j + 273}{298}\right)^{\kappa},\quad \kappa \approx 2.3 \text{ (Si trench, fit from datasheet curve)}$$

At T_j = 125 °C, R_ds,on ≈ 1.0 × (398/298)^2.3 ≈ **1.95 mΩ**. A linear fit of 1.7–1.8× is common; I use 1.75 mΩ below.

$$R_{cu}(T) = R_{cu,20}\,[1 + 0.00393\,(T-20)]$$

At 100 °C, R_cu ≈ 0.5 × 1.31 = **0.66 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Results at 80 A

| Term | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 2 × 6400 × 1.75 mΩ | **22.4 W** |
| Copper conduction | 2 × 6400 × 0.66 mΩ | **8.4 W** |

Per-leg RMS current, for FET sizing over an electrical cycle (each leg conducts 2/3 of the time):

$$I_{leg,rms} = I\sqrt{2/3} = 65.3\text{ A}$$

### 1.4 Electrothermal coupling (positive feedback)

Because R_ds,on rises with T_j, the junction temperature must be solved self-consistently:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + P(T_j)\,Z_{th}(t)$$

Iterate, or solve the quadratic in the linear-R approximation. Thermal runaway occurs when

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\,Z_{th} \ge 1$$

Check this explicitly for the burst case.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing leg hard-switches; the return-leg low-side FET is static. The load is inductive with clamped current, so the standard piecewise-linear model applies.

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drive} - V_{pl}}{R_{g,ext} + R_{g,int} + R_{drv,src}} = \frac{10 - 3}{4.7 + 1 + 0.3} \approx 1.17\text{ A}$$
