# STM32H743 Nano Flight Stack with 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

Two premises in the brief need correcting before any numbers will hold up.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.**
- ArduPilot is flight-controller firmware. It has no BLDC commutation, current loop, or gate-drive code.
- A real "H743 4-in-1" product is either:
  - an FC/ESC stack, or
  - an AIO board where the H743 sends DShot to **four ESC MCUs**, or one MCU per motor pair. Typical parts are the AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32.
- The 48 kHz PWM, dead-time, and commutation all live on the ESC MCU. The H743's job is DShot600 output, eRPM capture, and telemetry.
- The model below is partitioned this way.

**2. "80A burst" on a 20×20 mm nano board must be defined.**
- **Case A:** 80 A per motor. Section 2 shows this is limited to about 1 s adiabatically. It is not a credible rating.
- **Case B:** 80 A total, about 20 A per motor. This is feasible.
- Both cases are computed. Treat any marketing "80A nano" number skeptically.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation structure

The model assumes:
- 6-step trapezoidal drive (AM32 and BLHeli_32 default),
- complementary PWM (synchronous rectification),
- PWM on the high-side leg.

Over one electrical cycle, each phase spends:
- 120° high-state (PWM),
- 120° low-state (LS FET fully on),
- 60° floating.

Per-FET conduction duty (D = PWM duty):

$$
\delta_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}D,\qquad
\delta_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D) + \tfrac{1}{3} = \tfrac{1}{3}(2-D)
$$

At every instant, exactly two FETs carry motor current I per motor channel. That gives:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j),\qquad
I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}
$$

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

### 1.2 Hot R_ds(on)

At T_j = 110 °C, R = 1.4 × 1.51 = **2.11 mΩ**. Ripple is neglected here, since low-inductance racing motors at 48 kHz add a few percent.

| Case | I per motor | P_cond per motor | P_cond, 4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 80 A | 2 × 6400 × 2.11 m = **27.0 W** | **108 W** |
| B | 20 A | 2 × 400 × 2.11 m = **1.69 W** | **6.8 W** |

**Per-FET distribution (Case A):**
- The instantaneous dissipation is 13.5 W in a conducting FET.
- The average over an electrical cycle is about 27/6 = **4.5 W per FET**.
- Commutation runs at several kHz electrical, which is far faster than the case thermal time constant. The average is therefore the valid number for T_case.
- Junction rise over case: ΔT_jc ≈ 4.5 × 1.5 ≈ **6.8 K**.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only one leg per motor channel switches at a time (the PWM leg).

**Hard-switching overlap:**

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{in} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}
$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{in} f_{sw}
$$

**Output capacitance (approximation):**

$$
P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss} V_{in} f_{sw}
$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction:**

$$
P_{dt} = 2\, V_f\, I\, t_{dt}\, f_{sw}
$$

**Gate drive (dissipated mostly in driver and gate resistors):**

$$
P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw}
$$
