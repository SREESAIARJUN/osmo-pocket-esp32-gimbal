## 0. Architecture Assumptions

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. In every production 4-in-1 design, each ESC channel has its own small MCU that generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to those MCUs over bidirectional DShot600.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation, two phase legs conduct at any instant:
- The PWM'd high-side (HS) of one leg.
- The low-side (LS) of another leg.

With synchronous rectification, during PWM off-time the freewheel current flows through the complementary LS FET of the PWM leg instead of its body diode. The motor current therefore always traverses exactly **two channels** (excluding dead time).

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

At T<sub>j</sub> = 125 °C: R = 1.0 mΩ × 1.5 = **1.5 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Per-ESC-channel conduction loss

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W}
$$

Ripple correction: $I_{rms}^2 = I_{avg}^2 + \Delta I_{pp}^2/12$. Low-inductance racing motors (L ≈ 15–30 µH) at 48 kHz have ripple of a few amps, which adds less than 1 %. It is neglected here.

### 1.4 Shunt and copper losses

$$
P_{shunt} = 80^2 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 3.2\ \text{W per channel}
$$

Copper: a 2 oz trace has about 0.25 mΩ/sq. With roughly 2 squares in the power path, P<sub>Cu</sub> ≈ 80² × 0.5 mΩ ≈ **3.2 W per channel**. This term is often forgotten, and it is why phase pads must be poured and stitched on all layers.

### 1.5 Per-device split

Each FET conducts for about 1/3 of the electrical period:

$$
\bar P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6}\cdot 2 = 6.4\ \text{W average}
$$

The instantaneous loss while conducting is 9.6 W.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f<sub>sw</sub> = 48 kHz

All terms are per ESC channel. Only one leg switches at a time.

### 2.1 V-I overlap (hard-switched HS)

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I_{ph}\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}
$$

The transition times come from the gate charge:

$$
t_{r,f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}+Q_{gs2}}{I_{g}}, \quad I_g = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}{R_g+R_{g,int}}
$$

With V<sub>drv</sub> = 12 V, V<sub>plat</sub> ≈ 3.5 V, R<sub>g</sub> = 10 Ω, and Q<sub>sw</sub> ≈ 10 nC:
- I<sub>g</sub> ≈ 0.75 A
- t ≈ 13 ns
- Use **t<sub>r</sub> + t<sub>f</sub> = 40 ns**, a conservative figure that includes layout inductance.

$$
P_{VI} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (LS body diode, after dead time)

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 50\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = C_{oss,er}\,V_{bus}^2\,f_{sw} = 1.5\text{ nF}\times 635 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}
$$

This counts both FETs of the switching leg, each charged or discharged once per cycle.

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

Two dead-time intervals of t<sub>dt</sub> = 100 ns occur per PWM period:

$$
P_{dt} = V_F\,I_{ph}\,2\,t_{dt}\,f_{sw} = 0.8\times 80\times 200\text{ ns}\times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

> **Design lever:** In AM32, setting dead time to the minimum safe value for the FET and driver combination saves more power than a lower-R<sub>DS(on)</sub> FET would at 48 kHz.
