## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Scope

**Architecture.** I assume the conventional split architecture. The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and generates DShot. Each ESC channel has its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, on an AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 does not run four sensorless FOC/6-step loops alongside ArduPilot, so it does not drive gate drivers directly.

**Status of numbers.** Every numeric value below is a design-model estimate built from representative datasheet-class parameters. Before layout freeze, you must substitute the parameters of the specific MOSFET, gate driver, and capacitors you select. The netlists are a reference topology, not a fabrication-verified design.

**Key finding up front.** On a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 nano board, 80 A per channel is thermally sustainable only as a burst of a few seconds. The math in §3 shows why. If marketing wants "80 A," define it as **80 A / 5 s burst, 45–55 A continuous with airflow**.

### Operating Point Definitions

| Symbol | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 25.2 V (6S full), 22.2 V nominal | 40 V-rated FETs required |
| I_ph,burst | 80 A per channel | Phase current at near-full throttle |
| f_sw | 48 kHz | ESC PWM frequency |
| Topology | 3-phase bridge, complementary (synchronous) PWM, 6-step trapezoidal | AM32/BLHeli_32 default |

| Gate drive | 10 V, ~2 A peak source/sink | Integrated 3-phase driver (e.g., FD6288-class) |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Distribution in 6-Step Commutation

In each electrical cycle, each phase conducts high-side for 120°, low-side for 120°, and floats for 120° (twice for 60°).

At duty D, with synchronous rectification:
- The **active (PWM'd) FET** in a conducting phase carries I_ph for a fraction D of its 1/3 window.
- The **complementary FET** carries I_ph for a fraction (1−D) of the same window. The exception is the dead time, during which the body diode carries it.

At burst (D → ~0.95), per-FET RMS current, worst case:

$$I_{FET,rms} = I_{ph}\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{3}} = 80/\sqrt{3} = 46.2\ \text{A}$$

### 1.2 Temperature-Corrected R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\left[1+\alpha (T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0045/\text{K}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS ≈ 0.7 mΩ × 1.45 ≈ **1.02 mΩ** (use datasheet max, not typical, for sign-off).

### 1.3 Conduction Loss

Per FET:
$$P_{cond,FET} = I_{FET,rms}^2 \cdot R_{DS}(T_j) = 46.2^2 \times 1.02\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{2.18\ W}$$

Per channel (6 FETs; at any instant two FETs carry I_ph, so this is equivalent to):
$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph}^2 R_{DS} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.02\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{13.1\ W}$$

**Copper and trace loss.** Assume 2 oz outer layers plus 2 oz inner layers, with an effective phase path of ~0.25 mΩ, which is typical for a nano board:
$$P_{Cu,ch} \approx 80^2 \times 0.25\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{1.6\ W}$$

**Current-sense shunt.** One shunt on the shared battery path, 0.25 mΩ, carrying total battery current. At 4×80 A × D ≈ 300 A:
$$P_{shunt} = 300^2 \times 0.25\,\text{m}\Omega = 22.5\ \text{W}$$

> ⚠ This is unacceptable. Use **≤ 0.1 mΩ** (9 W, still large) or a **Hall/magnetic sensor** (e.g., TMCS1123-class, ~0.1–0.6 mΩ internal path; check its burst rating). On nano boards, Hall sensing is strongly recommended.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd FET in each active phase hard-switches. The complementary FET soft-switches (ZVS) after dead time.

### 2.1 V-I Overlap (Hard-Switching) Loss

$$E_{on} + E_{off} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_{r} + t_{f})$$

Gate-driven transition times are derived from Q_gd and driver current. With Q_gd ≈ 15 nC, a plateau of ~3 V, a 10 V drive, and R_g,total ≈ 4 Ω:

$$t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}R_g}{V_{drv}-V_{pl}} \approx \frac{15\text{n}\times 4}{7} \approx 9\ \text{ns},\quad t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}R_g}{V_{pl}} \approx 20\ \text{ns}$$

Use **t_r + t_f ≈ 30 ns**. This includes a margin for layout inductance.

$$P_{sw,VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}(25.2)(80)(30\text{n})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.45\ W}\ \text{per channel}$$

### 2.2 Output Capacitance Loss

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 2 \approx 60\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.07\ W}$$

### 2.3 Body-Diode Dead-Time Conduction and Reverse Recovery

Dead time t_dt ≈ 200 ns at each of two edges, with V_f ≈ 0.8 V at 80 A:
$$P_{dt} = V_f I_{ph}(2t_{dt})f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times400\text{n}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

Reverse recovery, with Q_rr ≈ 50 nC at high di/dt:
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

> Dead time is your second-largest dynamic loss. Trimming to ~100 ns (AM32 allows this) halves it, provided you have verified shoot-through margin with a scope at T_j,max.

### 2.4 Gate Drive Loss

This is dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g, not in the FET.

$$P_{gate} = Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} \times 2\ \text{(active pair)} = 90\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k}\times2 = \mathbf{0.086\ W}$$

Bootstrap/driver quiescent losses add ~0.05 W per channel.
