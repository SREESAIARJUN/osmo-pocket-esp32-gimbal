## 0. Feasibility Findings

These results drive every later design choice.

| Finding | Consequence |
|---|---|
| 80 A per motor gives ≈ 31 W of conduction loss per motor channel, ≈ 125 W for the board. | A nano (20×20 / 25.5×25.5 mm) board cannot hold this steady-state. "80 A" is valid only as a **≤ ~1.5–2 s burst** rating, set by the board's thermal capacitance (§3). |
| 4 × 80 A = 320 A of peak battery current. | No nano-class power entry (pads, XT30/XT60, 2 oz copper) handles this. The **continuous** rating should be set at 25–35 A per motor. |
| The H743 does not commutate motors in a production 4-in-1. | Architecture: H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs (e.g. AT32F421 running AM32). Each ESC MCU runs 48 kHz PWM. |
| Conduction loss is about 10× switching loss at 80 A. | Spend effort on R_DS(on), copper, and thermal mass first. Edge-rate tuning comes second. |

The scheme is six-step trapezoidal (AM32/BLHeli) with complementary (synchronous) PWM.

### 2.2 Conduction Loss

In six-step operation, two phases conduct at any instant. With complementary PWM:

- **On-time:** current flows HS_x → motor → LS_y.
- **Off-time:** current flows LS_x (sync rectification) → motor → LS_y.

Two FETs are therefore in the current path 100 % of the time, independent of duty cycle:

$$
P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j)=R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond per motor |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.5 mΩ | 2·6400·0.0015 = **19.2 W** |
| 125 °C (design point, ×1.6) | 2.4 mΩ | 2·6400·0.0024 = **30.7 W** |

**Per-FET distribution.** Over an electrical cycle each of the six FETs carries ~1/3 of the path-time, so the average is P_cond/6 ≈ **5.1 W per FET**. At low duty (D → 0), low-side FETs carry more because they also conduct during freewheeling. The worst-case LS FET is ≈ I²R·(1/3 + (1−D)/3), up to ~2× the average.

**PCB copper loss.** This is not negligible. For 2 oz copper, R_□ ≈ 0.25 mΩ/sq at 25 °C (+0.39 %/K). A phase loop of ~4 squares gives:

$$
P_{Cu}= I^2 R = 6400 \cdot 0.0013 \approx 8\ \text{W at }100\,^\circ\text{C}
$$

Use 3 oz outer / 2 oz inner copper, stitch layers in parallel, and keep phase loops under 3 squares.

### 2.3 Switching Loss at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches.

**(a) V–I overlap (hard turn-on and turn-off):**

$$
P_{VI}=\tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot40\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

**(b) Reverse recovery of the LS body diode at HS turn-on:**

$$
P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}=40\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k}= \mathbf{0.05\ W}
$$

**(c) Output capacitance (Q_oss approximation):**

$$
P_{oss}\approx Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}= \mathbf{0.06\ W}
$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period):**

$$
P_{dt}=V_F\,I\,(2t_d)\,f_{sw}=0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{n}\cdot48\text{k}= \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

**(e) Gate drive (two complementary FETs switching):**

$$
P_g=2\,Q_gV_{GS}f_{sw}=2\cdot60\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k}= \mathbf{0.06\ W}
$$

This power is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors.

$$
P_{sw,total}\approx \mathbf{2.7\ W\ per\ motor}
$$
