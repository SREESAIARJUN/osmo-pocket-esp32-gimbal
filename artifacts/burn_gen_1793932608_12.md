## 0. Architecture Clarification

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the bridges. In every production-grade 4-in-1 stack (BLHeli_32 / AM32 / Bluejay class), the partitioning is:

| Layer | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 / IIH6 | ArduPilot, attitude control, bi-directional DShot600 master |
| ESC MCU ×4 | e.g. AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071 [P] | 6-step / FOC commutation at 48 kHz PWM, DShot slave, eRPM telemetry |
| Gate driver ×4 | 3-phase half-bridge driver, e.g. FD6288Q class [P] | Level shift, bootstrap high-side |
| Power stage | 24× N-MOSFET, 40 V, ~1 mΩ class [P] | 4 × 3 half-bridges |

The thermal and switching model (§1–§3) applies to the ESC power stage. The DMA/timer model (§4) applies to the H743.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80A Burst)

### 2.1 Current path in 6-step synchronous commutation

At any commutation sector, one phase is PWM-ed (HS/LS complementary), one phase has its LS held ON, and one phase floats (BEMF sense).

- During the ON interval (duty D): current flows through HS_a and LS_b.
- During the OFF interval (1 − D): current freewheels through LS_a (synchronous) and LS_b.

Therefore, two R_DS(on) are always in series with the phase current, independent of D:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j)=R_{DS,25}\left[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\right]
$$

With α ≈ 0.006 /K, the result is k_T ≈ 1.6 at 125 °C.

### 2.2 Numeric evaluation

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.6\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{20.5\ W\ per\ motor}
$$

Add PCB copper resistance. A 2 oz, 4-layer stack with paralleled phase pours gives roughly 0.3 mΩ per phase path, which is about 0.6 mΩ round trip:

$$
P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.6\,\text{m}\Omega = 3.8\ \text{W per motor}
$$

### 2.3 Per-FET distribution (worst-case sector)

The worst-case FET is the LS held ON for the full sector:

$$
P_{FET,max}=I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)=10.2\ \text{W (during that sector)}
$$

Each FET is active in 2 of the 6 sectors in a role. The time-averaged worst-FET loss over a full electrical cycle is therefore about 10.2 × (1/3 + D/6 + …). A conservative design value is ≈ 4.5 W average per FET with peaks of 10.2 W. At eRPM > 10 kHz, the sector period is shorter than the die thermal time constant (~1–5 ms), so the average governs T_j.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ed half-bridge switches. Hard-switching occurs on the HS turn-on/off. The LS sees soft (ZVS) transitions after dead time.

### 3.1 V-I overlap

$$
P_{VI}=\tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\times25.2\times80\times40\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

### 3.2 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss}=Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}=45\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k}=0.054\ \text{W}
$$

### 3.3 Reverse recovery (LS body diode → HS turn-on)

$$
P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}=60\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k}=0.073\ \text{W}
$$

The Q_rr-induced turn-on current spike also adds roughly 10–20 % to P_VI. Budget it as **0.3 W**.

### 3.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$
P_{dt}=V_F\, I\, (2t_{dead}) f_{sw}=0.8\times80\times200\text{ns}\times48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

### 3.5 Gate drive (dissipated in driver/R_g, not FET)

$$
P_{gate}=Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw}\times N_{sw}=50\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k}\times2=0.048\ \text{W per motor}
$$

### 3.6 Total per motor and per 4-in-1 at full burst

| Loss | W per motor |
|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs, 125 °C) | 20.5 |
| Copper | 3.8 |
| V-I switching + Q_rr | 2.24 |
| Coss | 0.05 |
| Dead time | 0.61 |
| Gate | 0.05 |
| **Total** | **≈ 27.3 W** |
| **×4 (simultaneous burst)** | **≈ 109 W** |

Efficiency check: P_out ≈ 25.2 × 80 × 4 ≈ 8.06 kW, so η_stage ≈ 98.7 %. That looks good on paper, but the absolute heat is the problem.

### 3.7 Sensitivity

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial f_{sw}} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) + V(Q_{oss}+Q_{rr}) + 2V_F I t_{dead} \approx 53\ \mu\text{W/Hz}
$$

Moving from 24 → 48 kHz therefore costs ≈ 1.3 W per motor. Conduction dominates at 75 % of total loss. **R_DS(on) is the primary lever, not f_sw.**

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Network (per motor, Cauer-reduced)

```
P_motor ──[R_θJC/6 eff]──●T_c──[R_θ,PCB-spread]──●T_pcb──[R_θ,PCB-amb]──●T_amb
                         │                       │
                       C_die                   C_board (+ heatsink)
```

### 4.2 Adiabatic burst check (worst case: no prop-wash cooling credited)

Assumptions for a 30.5 × 30.5 mm 4-in-1 board:

- Board mass ≈ 12 g. Effective c_p (Cu/FR4 mix) ≈ 0.6 J/g·K, so C_board ≈ 7.2 J/K.
- Optional 3 g Al heat spreader: C_Al = 3 × 0.9 = 2.7 J/K.
- C_total ≈ 9.9 J/K.

$$
\Delta T_{board} = \frac{P\,t}{C} = \frac{109\times10}{9.9} \approx \mathbf{110\ K}
$$

**Conclusion: a simultaneous 4 × 80A, 10 s burst on a nano 30.5 mm board is NOT thermally viable.**

Viable redefinitions of the rating, pick one:

1. **Per-motor burst (one motor at 80A, others at cruise ~15A)**
   - P ≈ 27.3 + 3 × 1.2 ≈ 31 W
   - ΔT ≈ 31 W × 10 s / 9.9 J/K ≈ 31 K
   - **Passes.**
2. **Burst duration 2 s at 4 × 80A**
   - ΔT ≈ 22 K adiabatic
   - **Passes.**
3. **Require 0.5 mΩ FETs (or 2× paralleled) plus a 6 g spreader**
   - P_total ≈ 60 W, C ≈ 12.6 J/K
   - ΔT at 10 s ≈ 48 K
   - **Marginal.**

### 4.3 Junction rise above board (quasi-steady within burst)

For the worst FET at 10.2 W peak (sector-averaged 4.5 W):

$$
\Delta T_{jc} = 4.5 \times 0.8 = 3.6\ \text{K (avg)},\quad Z_{\theta JC}(1\,\text{ms})\approx0.3\ \text{K/W} \Rightarrow +3\ \text{K ripple}
$$

Then:

$$
T_j = T_{amb} + \Delta T_{board} + \Delta T_{spread} + \Delta T_{jc} = 40 + 31 + \sim10 + 7 \approx 88\,^\circ\text{C}
$$

This assumes per-motor burst (option 1) and is within the 150 °C rating with margin. Iterate R_DS(T_j) accordingly: 88 °C gives k_T ≈ 1.38, so P_cond drops to 17.7 W. The 125 °C assumption is conservative.

### 4.4 Steady-state continuous (55A, airflow h ≈ 60 W/m²K, A ≈ 2 × 0.93e-3 m²)

$$
R_{\theta,amb} = 1/(hA) \approx 9\ \text{K/W (board)}
$$

At 4 × 55A, P ≈ 4 × (2 × 55² × 1.4m + 55² × 0.6m + 1.9) ≈ 4 × 12.2 ≈ 49 W, giving ΔT ≈ 440 K.
