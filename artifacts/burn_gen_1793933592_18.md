# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #9): Power Stage Thermal Model and DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Feasibility

This document derives the physics from **stated assumptions**. Before layout freeze, replace every parameter in §0.2 with values from the selected MOSFET datasheet and measured board data. The netlists in §5 are a **reference architecture**, not a verified release netlist.

### 0.1 Feasibility warning

On a 20×20 mm "nano" stack, 80 A per channel is a **transient** rating of a few seconds, not a continuous one.

- The model below shows roughly **100 W dissipation** across four channels at 80 A.
- The board's lumped heat capacity is only a few J/K.
- The board-temperature limit, not the MOSFET junction, is the binding constraint.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

The on-resistance at temperature is approximated linearly between the 25 °C and 125 °C datasheet points:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\left[1 + \frac{k_T - 1}{100}(T_j - 25)\right]$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{hot} = 1.2 \times 1.5 = 1.8\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step commutation

In each 60° sector, current flows through exactly **two phase legs**: one sourcing and one sinking.

- With synchronous rectification, each leg always has exactly one MOSFET carrying I_pk:
  - The high-side FET during the on-time D.
  - The low-side FET during the off-time 1 − D.
- Dead time is ignored here and treated separately in §2.

The duty-cycle terms therefore cancel at the channel level:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{pk}^2 R_{hot} = 2(80)^2(1.8\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{23.0\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-device distribution

**High-side FET of the PWM-ing phase.** It is the source for 1/3 of the electrical cycle at duty D. At full throttle, D ≈ 0.95:

$$P_{HS} = I^2 R_{hot}\cdot\tfrac{1}{3}\cdot D = 6400 \times 0.0018 \times 0.317 = 3.65\ \text{W}$$

**Low-side FET.** It conducts 1/3 of the cycle as the return path, plus (1 − D) as the synchronous freewheel path:

$$P_{LS} \approx 3.84 + 0.19 \approx 4.0\ \text{W}$$

This is the worst-case device under the stated scheme. Exact numbers depend on whether the firmware complements the low side or the high side.

### 1.4 Copper loss

$$P_{Cu,ch} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\ \text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

This includes the battery pads, plane bottlenecks and phase pads. It is often underestimated on nano boards. Thin necks near the phase pads are the usual hotspots.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the PWM-ing leg switches hard, so there is one hard-switched commutation pair per PWM period.

### 2.1 Turn-on and turn-off transition times

Gate currents during the Miller plateau:

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{pl}}{R_{g,tot}} = \frac{10 - 4.5}{6} = 0.92\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{sink}} = \frac{4.5}{4} = 1.13\ \text{A}$$

Transition times:

$$t_{on} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2} + Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = \frac{12.5\ \text{nC}}{0.92\ \text{A}} = 13.6\ \text{ns}$$

$$t_{off} \approx \frac{12.5\ \text{nC}}{1.13\ \text{A}} = 11.1\ \text{ns}$$

### 2.2 V–I overlap loss

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{pk} (t_{on} + t_{off}) f_{sw} = \tfrac12 (25.2)(80)(24.7\ \text{ns})(48\ \text{kHz}) = \mathbf{1.19\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery loss

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\ \text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\ \text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

The additional turn-on current spike caused by recovery adds about 5–10 % to P_VI.

### 2.4 Output capacitance loss

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss(er)} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} \times 2 \approx \mathbf{0.027\ W}$$

### 2.5 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time edges per PWM period:

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD} I_{pk} (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\ \text{ns} \times 48\ \text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

This term is significant. Reducing dead time to 50 ns halves it, but only if the measured shoot-through margin allows.
