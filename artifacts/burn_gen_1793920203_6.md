## 0. Architectural Corrections and Scope Assumptions

Three issues in the brief would cause a failed design if implemented literally.

| # | Issue | Resolution |
|---|-------|-----------|
| 1 | **ArduPilot does not commutate BLDC motors.** An H743 running ArduPilot cannot be "the ESC". | The topology is **H743 flight controller + 4 dedicated ESC MCUs** (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32) on the same PCB stack. The H743 drives them with bidirectional DShot600. |
| 2 | **"80A" is ambiguous.** It could mean battery DC current or phase RMS current. | This model uses **80A as phase current per motor during burst** (worst case for conduction). Battery current is I_bat ≈ D·I_ph. |
| 3 | **80A per motor on a nano (20×20 / 25.5×25.5 mm) board is not continuously sustainable.** | The model below shows it is a **transient thermal-capacitance-limited rating** of roughly 2–5 s. This is derived in §3. |

**Operating assumptions:**

- 6S LiPo, V_bus,max = 25.2 V. Design MOSFETs are 40 V class.
- 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM at f_sw = 48 kHz.
- 3 half-bridges per motor, 24 MOSFETs total, PQFN 3.3×3.3 mm.
- Gate driver: FD6288Q-class 3-phase bootstrap driver, one per motor.

## 2. Loss Model per Motor (One 3-Phase Bridge)

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

In 6-step operation, two phases conduct at any instant:

- **Switching leg:** the high-side conducts for duty D. The low-side synchronously rectifies for (1 − D), minus dead time.
- **Return leg:** the low-side is on continuously for that 60° sector.

The phase current I_ph therefore flows through exactly two channel resistances at all times (ignoring the dead-time intervals):

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{ds}(T_j) \cdot \big[D + (1-D) + 1\big] = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This is independent of duty cycle, which is the key insight: low-throttle high-current events such as prop strikes or desync are just as lossy.

Add PCB copper and phase-pad resistance R_pcb (⚠ estimate 0.5 mΩ per current path for 2 oz inner plus 2 oz outer):

$$P_{cond,total} = 2\,I_{ph}^2 \left(R_{ds}(T_j) + R_{pcb}\right)$$

**At 80 A, T_j = 125 °C:**

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 6400 \times (2.32 + 0.5)\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{36.1\ W}$$

(MOSFETs alone: 29.7 W; copper: 6.4 W.)

### 2.2 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the switching leg's half-bridge commutates hard. The hard-switched edge losses are:

**(a) V–I overlap:**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 0.968\ W$$

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per cycle):**

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I_{ph}\, (2\,t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 0.614\ W$$

**(c) Reverse recovery of the low-side body diode at high-side turn-on:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.036\ W$$

**(d) Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \;(\text{upper bound}) = 25\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.030\ W$$

**(e) Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET):**

$$P_{g} = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \times 40\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.038\ W$$

$$P_{sw,total} \approx \mathbf{1.69\ W\ per\ motor}$$

**Design note:** at 48 kHz, switching loss is only about 4.5 % of total. Doubling to 96 kHz would add about 1.6 W per motor, which is acceptable. Raising f_sw is a viable acoustic and ripple trade on this design. Conduction loss dominates everything.

### 2.3 Loss Summary at 80 A Burst

| Term | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 29.7 W | 118.8 W |
| Conduction (PCB copper) | 6.4 W | 25.6 W |
| Switching (all terms) | 1.7 W | 6.8 W |
| **Total** | **37.8 W** | **151.2 W** |

Efficiency check: at D = 0.9, motor input ≈ 25.2 × 0.9 × 80 ≈ 1814 W per motor, so ESC efficiency ≈ 98 %. The efficiency is fine; the problem is power *density*.

### 2.4 Per-MOSFET Distribution

Over one electrical revolution, each FET carries current for 120°:

- **High-side:** conducts I²R for fraction D of its 1/3 window, and takes all switching loss.
- **Low-side:** conducts for 1/3 of the time as a continuous return path, plus about 1/3 × (1 − D) as a synchronous rectifier.

At high D (≈0.9), low-side FETs are slightly hotter per unit. Average per FET is ≈ 37.8 W / 6 ≈ **6.3 W**:

$$\Delta T_{jc} = 6.3 \times 1.5 \approx 9.5\ K$$

Junction-to-case rise is therefore not the limiter. **Board heat capacity is.**
