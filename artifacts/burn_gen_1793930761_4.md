# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #12): Design Model

## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility Notes

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 cannot practically commutate four sensorless BLDC motors on its own. It has only two advanced timers with complementary outputs (TIM1 and TIM8), and four motors need 12 complementary pairs. This design therefore uses:

- **H743** as the flight controller, running ArduPilot.
- **4× AT32F421 or STM32G071** ESC MCUs running AM32, one per channel.
- Bidirectional DShot600 links from the H743 to each ESC MCU.

**Board size.** An 80 A per-channel burst on a 20×20 mm "nano" board is not thermally credible (see §3). This model assumes:

- A 30.5×30.5 mm board with 8-layer, 2 oz copper.
- A burst of 80 A phase current for ≤ 5 s.
- 50 A continuous only with airflow.

If "nano" is a hard requirement, derate to roughly 35 A burst.

**Electrical and component parameters:**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Bus | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max |
| PWM | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous rectification) |
| Dead time (t_dt) | 100 ns |
| MOSFETs | 40 V N-ch, 3.3×3.3 mm (DFN3333) |
| R_ds(on) | 1.0 mΩ @ 25 °C, V_gs = 10 V |
| Q_g | 60 nC |
| Q_gd | 12 nC |
| Q_rr | 40 nC |
| Q_oss | 50 nC |
| R_θJC | 1.2 K/W |
| FET temperature coefficient (α) | ≈ 0.006 /K |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.006 × 85) = **1.51 mΩ**

### 1.2 Six-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary PWM

Within each 120° commutation interval:

- The high-side (HS) FET of the PWM phase conducts for fraction D.
- The low-side (LS) FET of the same phase conducts the freewheel current for fraction 1−D (synchronous rectification).
- The LS FET of the return phase conducts 100% of the interval.

Each phase spends 1/3 of the electrical cycle in each role. The per-FET average losses are:

$$P_{HS}=\frac{I^2R_{ds}D}{3},\qquad P_{LS}=\frac{I^2R_{ds}(1-D)}{3}+\frac{I^2R_{ds}}{3}$$

Summing over the bridge:

$$P_{cond,bridge}=3P_{HS}+3P_{LS}=I^2R_{ds}\,[D+(1-D)+1]=\boxed{2I^2R_{ds}}$$

This holds independent of duty, because two FETs are always in the current path.

### 1.3 Numbers at I = 80 A, T_j = 110 °C

**FETs:**

$$P_{cond,FET} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.51\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.3\ W\ per\ channel}$$

**Copper and shunt** (per channel, at 80 A):

- Phase traces + bus copper: ≈ 0.3 mΩ → 1.9 W
- Shunt: 0.25 mΩ → 1.6 W
- Subtotal: ≈ **3.5 W**

**Worst-case FET.** The LS FET at D ≈ 0.5 dissipates:

$$I^2R\,(0.5/3 + 1/3) = 9.66 \times 0.5 = \mathbf{4.8\ W}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model (f_sw = 48 kHz)

Only one half-bridge hard-switches per PWM period: the PWM-phase HS turn-on and turn-off. The LS FET switches with zero voltage (ZVS) via body-diode conduction during the dead time.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss (HS FET)

$$t_{sw}\approx\frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g}},\quad I_g\approx\frac{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}{R_g}=\frac{12-4}{10\,\Omega}=0.8\text{ A}\Rightarrow t_r\approx t_f\approx 15\text{ ns}$$

$$P_{VI}=\tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}=0.5(25.2)(80)(30\text{n})(48\text{k})=\mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per period:

$$P_{dt}=V_f\,I\,(2t_{dt})f_{sw}=0.8(80)(200\text{n})(48\text{k})=\mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V f_{sw}=40\text{n}(25.2)(48\text{k})=\mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss}=\tfrac12 Q_{oss}V f_{sw}=0.5(50\text{n})(25.2)(48\text{k})=\mathbf{0.03\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss lands in the driver and gate resistor, not the FET. Two FETs switch per period:

$$P_g=2Q_gV_{drv}f_{sw}=2(60\text{n})(12)(48\text{k})=\mathbf{0.07\ W}$$

### 2.6 Total switching loss

**P_sw ≈ 2.2 W per channel.** Most of it (≈ 1.9 W) lands on the active HS FET.

**Effect of switching frequency.** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it falls to about 1.1 W, but current ripple doubles. Ripple is computed in §4.3.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Power budget at an 80 A burst

| Component | Per channel | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 19.3 W | 77.2 W |
| Switching | 2.2 W | 8.8 W |
| Copper + shunt | 3.5 W | 14.0 W |
| Driver/MCU/LDO | 0.3 W | 1.2 W |
| **Total** | **25.3 W** | **≈ 101 W** |

Board efficiency at this load: 4 × 80 A × ~22 V (effective) ≈ 7 kW delivered, so ≈ 98.6%. The efficiency is fine; the problem is the **heat density**.

### 3.2 Junction rise (fast time constant)

For the worst-case FET dissipating 4.8 W average (≈ 9.7 W instantaneous while conducting), with R_θJC = 1.2 K/W:

$$\Delta T_{jc}\approx 9.7\times Z_{th}(1\,\text{ms}\approx0.4\,\text{K/W})\approx 4\text{ K}\ \text{(instantaneous)},\qquad \Delta T_{jc,avg}=4.8\times1.2=5.8\text{ K}$$

The junction-to-case rise is not the limiting factor. The board is.

### 3.3 Board lumped-capacitance model (burst duration limit)

The board is modeled as a single first-order thermal node:

$$C_{th}\frac{dT_b}{dt}=P-\frac{T_b-T_a}{R_{th,ba}}$$

**Model parameters:**

- **C_th:** PCB (8-layer, 2 oz, 30.5 mm, ≈ 9 g) plus FETs, capacitors, and conformal coat ≈ 12 g, with c ≈ 0.95 J/(g·K) → **C_th ≈ 11.5 J/K**.

- **Thermal time constant:** τ = R·C ≈ 104 s (bare) or 46 s (with sink).
