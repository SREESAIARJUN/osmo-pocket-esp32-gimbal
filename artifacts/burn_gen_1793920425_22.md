## 0. Baseline Assumptions and Architecture

### 0.1 Architecture

The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does **not** commutate the motors. Each ESC channel has its own commutation MCU: an AT32F421K8U7 running AM32, the common choice for 4-in-1 boards. The H743 talks to each ESC over bi-directional DShot600, one wire per motor.

### 0.2 Feasibility flag: board size

A true 20×20 mm "nano" board cannot carry 80 A per channel.

- **The FET package won't fit.** 80 A needs 5×6 mm PDFN FETs: 24 per board, about 720 mm² of silicon footprint alone.
- **The thermal mass is too small**, as shown in §3.

The model below therefore assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm stack** (FC on top, ESC below). A 20×20 build would be thermally limited to roughly 35–45 A burst.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-step current path

In 120° trapezoidal commutation with synchronous (complementary) PWM, at any instant the current flows through:

- one **static** low-side FET (fully on during its 60° sector), and
- the **switching leg**: the HS FET for duty D, and the LS FET for (1−D) acting as a synchronous rectifier.

So the motor current always passes through exactly **two conducting FETs** (ignoring dead time):

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j) + I_{rms}^2 \cdot R_{Cu}$$

### 1.2 Temperature-corrected R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At a design point of T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{DS}(110) = 1.2\,\text{mΩ} \cdot (1 + 0.0045 \cdot 85) = 1.66\,\text{mΩ}$$

### 1.3 Ripple-corrected RMS current

Phase inductance for a typical 5" motor is about L_ph ≈ 15 µH, giving 30 µH line-to-line. The worst-case ripple occurs at D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L_{LL} f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \cdot 0.25}{30\mu \cdot 48k} = 4.4\,\text{A}$$

$$I_{rms} = \sqrt{I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}} = \sqrt{6400 + 1.6} \approx 80.01\,\text{A}$$

The ripple contribution is negligible at 80 A.

### 1.4 Result per channel at 80 A

$$P_{FET,cond} = 6400 \cdot 2 \cdot 1.66\text{m} = \mathbf{21.2\ W}$$

$$P_{Cu} = 6400 \cdot 0.4\text{m} = \mathbf{2.6\ W}$$

### 1.5 Per-FET split

Each of the 6 FETs per bridge carries current for about 1/3 of the electrical cycle. Averaged over the cycle:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx 21.2/6 = 3.5\ \text{W}$$

The instantaneous loss during its own conduction sector is about 10.6 W. The commutation ripple on T_j is small because the electrical period (≈ 0.4 ms at 25k eRPM / 7 pole pairs) is far shorter than the die thermal time constant.
