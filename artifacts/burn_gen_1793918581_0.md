## 0. Architecture Note and Feasibility Summary

**The H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC MCU.** It cannot run 4× sensorless FOC/6-step commutation and fly the aircraft. A real "H743 4-in-1" board is an AIO: H743 running ArduPilot, plus 4 dedicated ESC MCUs (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32-class firmware), linked by bidirectional DShot600. This document uses that architecture.

**"80A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is thermally implausible for more than a fraction of a second.** The math in §3 shows this. The analysis below states the actual sustainable envelope rather than adopting the marketing figure.

All device parameters below are stated assumptions for a representative 40V, ~1 mΩ-class MOSFET. **Replace them with datasheet values for your selected parts before release.** The netlist in §6 is a reference topology to adapt, not a verified, DRC-clean production netlist.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

In 6-step commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM, the current path always passes through **two** MOSFETs: either the high-side or low-side device of the PWM leg (both sharing duty D and 1−D), in series with the low-side device of the return leg, which is continuously on.

$$P_{cond,PWM\,leg} = I^2 R_{hot}\,D + I^2 R_{hot}(1-D) = I^2 R_{hot}$$

$$P_{cond,return} = I^2 R_{hot}$$

$$\boxed{P_{cond} = 2\,I^2 R_{hot} = 2(80)^2(1.5\times10^{-3}) = 19.2\ \text{W}}$$

Treating the phase current as flat-topped is conservative. Including the 48 kHz ripple adds a ΔI²/12 term:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

With typical low-inductance quad motors (L_phase ≈ 10–30 µH), ΔI is about 5–15 A. That adds less than 0.3% and can be ignored.

### 2.2 Hard-Switching (V–I Overlap) Loss

Switching times:

$$t_r \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_g} = \frac{15\,\text{nC}}{1\,\text{A}} = 15\ \text{ns}$$

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(30\times10^{-9})(48\,000)$$

$$\boxed{P_{sw} = 1.45\ \text{W}}$$

### 2.3 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\,(2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 400\times10^{-9} \cdot 48\,000$$

$$\boxed{P_{dt} = 1.23\ \text{W}}$$

This loss is nearly as large as the switching loss. Trimming dead time from 200 ns to 100 ns (if the driver and FETs permit, with no shoot-through) saves about 0.6 W per channel.

### 2.4 Reverse Recovery, C_oss, and Gate Drive

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} \cdot 2 = 1\text{n}\cdot 635 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.030\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{gate} = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \cdot N_{switching} = 50\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k}\cdot 2 = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

The gate-drive loss is dissipated mostly in the driver IC and R_g, not the FET.

### 2.5 Total per Channel and per Board

| Loss term | Per channel (W) | ×4 channels (W) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.20 | 76.8 |
| Switching | 1.45 | 5.8 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 | 4.9 |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.13 | 0.5 |
| **Total** | **≈ 22.0** | **≈ 88 W** |

Conduction dominates at about 87% of the total. **Raising f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz costs about 1.3 W per channel. Halving R_DS(on) saves about 9.6 W.** Spend the design effort on FET R_DS(on) and copper, not switching speed.

### 2.6 Efficiency at Burst

Electrical output power per channel is roughly V_bus·D·I. Near full throttle that is about 25 V × 80 A ≈ 2 kW. The ESC efficiency is therefore about 99%, but 88 W of heat has to go into a board with a heat capacity of only a few J/K (see §3).

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Per-FET Steady Distribution

Averaged over an electrical cycle, each of the 6 FETs per channel carries a roughly equal share:

$$P_{FET} \approx \frac{22\ \text{W}}{6} \approx 3.7\ \text{W}$$

Assumed thermal resistances:
- R_θ(j-c) ≈ 0.8 K/W (PDFN 5×6 class; 3×3 packages are worse)
- R_θ(c-pcb) ≈ 0.5 K/W, including solder and vias

$$\Delta T_{j-pcb} = 3.7 \times 1.3 \approx 4.8\ \text{K}$$

**The junction-to-board rise is small. The binding constraint is the board's bulk heat capacity and its heat rejection to air.**
