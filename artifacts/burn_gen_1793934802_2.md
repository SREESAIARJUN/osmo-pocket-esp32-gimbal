## 0. Architecture Clarification and Stated Assumptions

**The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 stack, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The ESC MCU is typically an AT32F421, STM32G071 or GD32E230, and it generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to the four ESC MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. The model below splits the design the same way:

| Power stage thermal | ESC PCB | §3 |
| BDShot600 timing, timers, DMA | STM32H743 / ArduPilot | §4 |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.2 × 1.6 = **1.92 mΩ**

### 1.2 Current paths in 6-step trapezoidal with synchronous rectification

In each 60° electrical sector, current flows through two phase legs:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for D. The complementary low-side FET (synchronous rectification) conducts for (1−D).
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET is on for 100 % of the sector.

Instantaneous conduction loss per motor:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 R_{ds}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds}\cdot 1 = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This is **independent of duty**, provided synchronous rectification is enabled (AM32/BLHeli_32 default). Without it, the low-side portion becomes body-diode loss: V_SD·I·(1−D).

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.20 mΩ | 2·6400·1.2e-3 = **15.4 W** | 61.4 W |
| 125 °C | 1.92 mΩ | **24.6 W** | 98.3 W |

Copper loss: P_Cu = I² R_Cu = 6400 × 1.0e-3 = **6.4 W per motor** (25.6 W total). This heat is spread across the PCB, not concentrated in the FETs.

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

Each FET carries current for about 1/3 of an electrical period (120° conduction):

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{P_{cond,motor}}{6} = \frac{24.6}{6} \approx 4.1\ \text{W (at 125 °C)}$$

High-side FETs additionally take essentially all of the switching loss (§2), so **the HS FETs are the thermal hot spots**.

### 1.5 Electrothermal self-consistency (runaway criterion)

Let P₀ be the conduction loss at 25 °C and R_th the junction-to-ambient resistance. Then

$$T_j = T_a + R_{th}\,P_0\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)] + R_{th}P_{sw}$$

Solving:

$$\boxed{T_j = \frac{T_a + R_{th}(P_0 + P_{sw}) - 25\,\alpha R_{th} P_0}{1-\alpha R_{th}P_0}}$$

**Thermal runaway** occurs when α·R_th·P₀ ≥ 1. For one FET with P₀ ≈ 2.6 W, the limit is R_th < 1/(0.006·2.6) ≈ **64 K/W**. Any sane layout is far below this, so the electrothermal loop is stable. The board-level limit in §3 binds long before runaway.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz (PWM leg only)

### 2.1 V-I overlap (hard switching, inductive load)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = \tfrac12(25.2)(80)(20\text{ ns})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{DT} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2t_{dead})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 400\text{ ns}\cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

This term exceeds the overlap loss, so **dead-time tuning is your highest-leverage switching knob**
