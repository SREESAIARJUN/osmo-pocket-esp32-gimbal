## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in six-step with synchronous rectification

In each 60° commutation sector, two phases carry current and one floats. The current path alternates between two states:

- **PWM on (fraction D):** high-side FET of phase A → motor → low-side FET of phase B.
- **PWM off (fraction 1−D):** low-side FET of phase A (synchronous rectification) → motor → low-side FET of phase B.

The motor current I_m flows through exactly two conducting FETs at all times, apart from the dead-time intervals covered in §2.4. Per ESC channel:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_m^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

This is independent of duty cycle. The distribution between FETs does depend on D:

| FET | Conduction share | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| HS, phase A (PWM leg) | D × ⅓ of electrical period | I_m² R · D/3 |
| LS, phase A (sync rect) | (1−D) × ⅓ | I_m² R · (1−D)/3 |
| LS, phase B (return leg) | 1 × ⅓ | I_m² R /3 |

Averaged over all six sectors, each phase leg dissipates ⅔ I_m² R. At D → 1 (full-throttle burst), the hottest devices are the low-side return FETs and the high-side PWM FETs, each at about (I_m² R)/3 average with I_m² R instantaneous.

**Battery vs. motor current.** Battery current is I_bat = D·I_m. An "80 A" rating measured at the battery at partial throttle implies higher motor current. For example, D = 0.8 gives I_m = 100 A. This model therefore uses **I_m = 80 A at D ≈ 1** as the burst case. If your rating convention is battery-side at partial throttle, scale by 1/D².

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_0\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

| T_j (°C) | R_DS (mΩ) | P_cond,ch @ 80 A (W) | 4 channels (W) |

| 25 | 0.90 | 11.5 | 46.1 |
| 75 | 1.13 | 14.4 | 57.6 |

### 1.3 Copper path losses

Assume the effective series copper from battery pad → bus planes → FETs → phase pad → back is R_cu ≈ 0.25 mΩ per channel at 2 oz, multilayer, including vias. For copper, α_cu = 0.0039 K⁻¹.

$$
P_{cu,ch} = I_m^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.25\text{ m}\Omega = 1.6\ \text{W}
$$

The shared battery-input planes carry the sum current of 320 A. With R_in ≈ 0.05 mΩ:

$$
P_{in} = 320^2 \times 0.05\text{ m}\Omega = 5.1\ \text{W}
$$

This term is frequently overlooked, and it dominates at the XT60 pad and solder joint. Use heavy pads, stitch every copper layer at the battery
