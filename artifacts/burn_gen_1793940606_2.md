# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Thermal Model, Switching Losses, and Bidirectional DShot600 Allocation

**Document scope:** Engineering derivation, worked numbers, reference netlists and ArduPilot `hwdef` allocation.

**Status:** Every device parameter marked **[A]** is an assumption. Replace it with datasheet values for the parts you actually select before you freeze the design.

---

## 0. Architectural Clarifications

Three points shape everything that follows.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** On a combined FC + 4-in-1 stack, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and emits DShot600. Each ESC channel is driven by its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32 (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071), or by one MCU per two channels. That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The two problems are therefore decoupled:
   - **H743 side:** timer and DMA allocation for DShot.
   - **ESC side:** PWM, dead-time, and power dissipation.

2. **"80 A burst" on a nano form factor is a transient rating, not a thermal steady state.** The model in §3 shows why. A 20×20 mm board can survive 80 A per channel for roughly 1 s, not continuously.

3. **48 kHz is the ESC PWM frequency.** It has no relationship to the DShot600 bit rate (600 kbit/s).

## 2. Conduction Losses

### 2.1 Commutation topology

In six-step trapezoidal drive, two phases conduct at any instant: one sourcing, one sinking. With complementary (synchronous) PWM, the current in each active leg always flows through exactly one MOSFET channel. That is the high-side FET during the on-time and the low-side FET during freewheeling.

Instantaneous conduction loss per ESC channel:

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \cdot I^2 \cdot R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

The duty cycle cancels out to first order: the current is always in some channel, so only the current's path changes. It does not cancel during dead-time, which is handled in §2.4.

### 2.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25} \cdot k(T_j), \qquad k(T_j) \approx \left(\frac{T_j + 273}{298}\right)^{\beta},\ \beta \approx 2.0\text{–}2.3
$$

At T_j = 110 °C: k ≈ (383/298)^2.2 ≈ 1.74 by formula. The datasheet curve **[A]** gives 1.55, so use 1.55, which yields 2.33 mΩ.

**Do not use R_25 for thermal design.** Doing so underestimates loss by about 35%.

### 2.3 Parasitic copper and current path

Each conducting leg has a series path of phase pad → FET → bus plane → shunt. Assume R_path ≈ 0.3 mΩ per leg **[A]**, using 2 oz copper with a shared current-sense shunt excluded.

$$
P_{cu} = 2 \cdot I^2 \cdot R_{path}
$$

### 2.4 Worked numbers at 80 A

| Term | Expression | Value |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 2 × 80² × 2.33 mΩ | **29.8 W** |
| Copper path | 2 × 80² × 0.3 mΩ | **3.8 W** |
| Per-FET average (6 FETs, each conducts ≈1/3 of electrical cycle in its leg) | (80² × 2.33 mΩ)/3 | **≈5.0 W avg / 14.9 W peak during conduction interval** |

At electrical frequencies above about 1 kHz (high-KV motors at speed), the FET's thermal mass averages the 14.9 W pulses. At low eRPM under load (stall, prop strike), it does not: one FET may carry 14.9 W for tens of milliseconds. **The stall case is the design-limiting case.**
