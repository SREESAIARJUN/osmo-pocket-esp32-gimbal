## 0. System Architecture Clarification

An STM32H743 is not normally the commutation controller inside a 4-in-1 ESC. The standard partition, and the one modeled here, is:

| Block | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 @ 480 MHz, ArduPilot | Attitude control; generates 4× bidirectional DShot600 |
| ESC MCUs (×4) | AT32F421 / STM32G071 running AM32 | 6-step or sinusoidal commutation at 48 kHz PWM, eRPM telemetry |
| Gate drivers (×4) | FD6288Q (3-phase half-bridge driver) | 6 gate outputs per motor |
| Power stage | 24× N-MOSFET, 40V, DFN5x6 | 3 half-bridges per motor |
| Supply | 6S LiPo (25.2 V max) | Bus voltage V_bus |

If you intend the H743 to commutate the motors directly (a single-MCU "AIO"), Section 3 changes significantly: you would need 4× advanced timers with complementary outputs and dead time, and the H743 has only TIM1/TIM8 plus HRTIM. Flag this and I will redo the allocation.

## 2. Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 2.1 Conduction topology

The model assumes 6-step commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM on the active high-side phase.

- At any instant, current flows through two legs: the PWM'd phase leg (HS during t_on, LS during t_off) and the return-phase LS FET (continuously on).
- The PWM'd leg therefore always has exactly one FET conducting. The series path is always **2 × R_DS(on)**, independent of duty cycle.

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2 R_{DS(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

At T_j = 100 °C: R_DS(on) = 1.0 mΩ × (1 + 0.006·75) = **1.45 mΩ**

$$
P_{cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.45\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{18.56\ W}
$$

This splits to roughly 9.3 W in each conducting FET position. The PWM'd leg's loss is shared between its HS and LS by D and (1−D).

**Copper parasitics.** Assume about 0.5 mΩ effective trace, via, and solder resistance in the current loop **[A]**:

$$
P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 3.2\ W
$$

### 2.2 Switching transition (hard-switched PWM leg only)

Gate currents through the Miller region:

$$
I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{DRV}-V_{pl}}{R_{g}} = \frac{10-3}{5} = 1.4\ \text{A}, \qquad
I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_g} = \frac{3}{5} = 0.6\ \text{A}
$$

$$
t_r = \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{g,on}} = 8.6\ \text{ns}, \qquad t_f = \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{g,off}} = 20\ \text{ns}
$$

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 28.6\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.38\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Second-order switching terms

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse recovery | V_bus · Q_rr · f_sw | 25.2 × 50 nC × 48k = **0.060 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | 2 · V_F · I · t_dt · f_sw | 2 × 0.8 × 80 × 100 ns × 48k = **0.614 W** |
| C_oss discharge | ½ C_oss V_bus² f_sw | 0.5 × 1.5 nF × 635 × 48k = **0.023 W** |
| Gate drive (2 switching FETs) | 2 · Q_g · V_DRV · f_sw | 2 × 40 nC × 10 × 48k = **0.038 W** (dissipated in driver and R_g) |

### 2.4 Loss summary at 80 A burst, T_j = 100 °C

| Loss | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 18.56 W | 74.2 W |
| Copper | 3.20 W | 12.8 W |
| Switching (V·I overlap) | 1.38 W | 5.5 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W | 2.5 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.12 W | 0.5 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 23.9 W** | **≈ 95.5 W** |

**Efficiency at burst.** Each channel delivers about 25.2 V × 80 A ≈ 2016 W at D → 1, so the stage is about **98.8 % efficient**. Conduction dominates at roughly 78 % of the losses. Two levers follow from this:

- Lowering R_DS(on) (for example, paralleling FETs) is far more valuable than raising gate drive speed.
- Raising f_sw from 24 kHz to 48 kHz costs only about 1 W per channel.
