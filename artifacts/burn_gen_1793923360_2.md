# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC: Design Package

No component datasheets or mechanical constraints were specified, so this package uses the baseline below. Every equation is parametric, so you can substitute real datasheet values.

| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal (AM32/BLHeli_32-class firmware) | |
| Burst profile | 80 A per motor, ≤ 5 s | |

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 is the *flight controller*. Each ESC channel has its own small MCU (e.g., AT32F421 running AM32) that receives DShot600 and returns eRPM over the same wire. The H743 does not commutate motors directly. This split is how ArduPilot bidirectional DShot works.

**Feasibility warning.** 4 × 80 A simultaneous at 6S is about 8 kW. On a 20×20 or 30×30 board this is a **thermally transient-only** rating. Section 2.6 shows that the board saturates in seconds and that steady-state 80 A is physically impossible without a heatsink. Treat "80A" as a per-channel, short-duration burst rating. Validate it with thermocouple and IR testing before publishing the spec.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous commutation

In any 60° commutation sector, phase X is PWM-driven high, phase Y is held low (low side ON), and phase Z floats.

- **Leg X (switching):** the high side conducts for fraction D. The complementary low side conducts for (1−D) as a synchronous rectifier.
- **Leg Y (static):** the low side conducts 100% of the sector.

The conduction loss per motor channel is therefore:

$$P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{rms}^2 R_{DS}(T_j) = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

This result is independent of duty cycle. Dead-time diode conduction is handled separately in §2.3.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.0 mΩ × 1.5 = **1.5 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Ripple correction

The RMS current includes phase-current ripple:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{2 L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}, \qquad I_{rms}^2 = I_{dc}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}$$

Two phases are in series, hence 2L_ph. For a typical 5" motor with L_ph ≈ 15 µH at D = 0.5:

ΔI_pp = 25.2 × 0.25 / (2 × 15 µH × 48 kHz) ≈ 4.4 A → I_rms² = 6400 + 1.6. The correction is negligible at 80 A.

### 1.4 Results at 80 A

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ channel}$$

Per-FET instantaneous loss in the static low side is I²R = **9.6 W**. Each FET carries current for 120° of every 360° electrical cycle. Electrical frequency is about 1–3 kHz, which is far faster than the junction thermal time constant (ms), so the per-FET average is:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx \frac{19.2}{6} = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

### 1.5 PCB copper and shunt losses (frequently forgotten)

| Element | Resistance | Loss @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase/bus copper path (2 oz, 6-layer, per channel) | ~0.4 mΩ | 2.6 W |
| Per-channel current shunt | 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| Shared battery-pad copper (320 A total) | ~0.1 mΩ | 10.2 W (board total) |

A single shared shunt on the total bus is not viable: 0.25 mΩ × 320² = 25.6 W. Use per-channel sensing or a hall sensor on the bus.
