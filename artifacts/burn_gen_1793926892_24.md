## 0. Executive Summary and Feasibility Verdict

You should know the key results before reading the derivations.

| Item | Result |
|---|---|
| Conduction loss per ESC channel @ 80 A, Tj ≈ 100 °C | **≈ 27 W** (FETs) + **≈ 3 W** (copper) |
| Switching and dead-time loss per channel @ 48 kHz, 6S | **≈ 2.6 W** |
| Total per channel @ 80 A | **≈ 33 W** |
| All 4 channels @ 80 A simultaneously | **≈ 130 W** |
| Burst time to Tj = 125 °C, 20×20 mm board, all 4 motors @ 80 A | **≈ 1 s** |
| Burst time, single motor @ 80 A | **≈ 3–4 s** |
| Bidirectional DShot600 transaction time | **≈ 90 µs**, so loop rates up to roughly 4–8 kHz are supported on a single timer |

**Architectural verdict:** An "80 A burst" rating on a 20×20 mm nano form factor is valid only for bursts under about 1 s with all four motors loaded. Marketing will want to print 80 A, but the realistic rating is 80 A for about 1 s, or about 35–45 A continuous with prop-wash cooling. If sustained 80 A matters, move to a 30.5×30.5 mm board. That roughly doubles thermal mass and dissipating area (see §3.6).

**Partitioning decision:** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and does *not* commutate the motors. Each ESC channel has its own commutation MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32), which generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 talks to the four ESC MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. This is the only architecture ArduPilot supports for bidirectional DShot RPM telemetry, and it keeps hard-real-time commutation off the flight-control core.
