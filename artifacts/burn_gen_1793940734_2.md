## 0. Architectural Correction

The H743 cannot run the motors directly. An STM32H743 does not commutate MOSFETs on a 4-in-1 ESC. The correct partitioning is:

| Function | Silicon | Firmware |
|---|---|---|
| Flight control, DShot master, telemetry decode | STM32H743VIT6 (480 MHz) | ArduPilot |
| Per-motor commutation, 48 kHz PWM, eRPM report | 4× STM32G071 or AT32F421 (one per motor) | AM32 (or BLHeli_32-class) |
| Gate drive | 4× 3-phase half-bridge driver (FD6288Q-class) | none |
| Power stage | 24× N-ch MOSFET (6 per motor) | none |

The 48 kHz switching analysis applies to the ESC MCUs. The DShot/DMA analysis applies to the H743.

**Reality check on the "nano" form factor and "80A burst":**
- A 20×20 mm board cannot support 4×80 A even briefly with adequate copper.
- This package assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm AIO** with an 8-layer, 2 oz outer / 2 oz inner copper stack.
- The "80 A burst" rating is defined as a **time-limited** rating and is derived quantitatively in Section 3.

## 2. Loss Model per Motor Channel

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

With 6-step trapezoidal commutation and **synchronous rectification** (complementary PWM, which AM32 enables by default):
- At any instant, two phase legs carry the motor current.
- During the PWM on-time, the path is HS(A) → motor → LS(B).
- During the off-time, current freewheels through LS(A) (synchronous) → motor → LS(B).

So the current always sees two R_ds(on) in series, independent of duty cycle:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds(on)}(T_j) + I^2 R_{Cu}
$$

Temperature-corrected resistance:

$$
R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha_R (T_j-25)]
$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

$$
R_{ds(on)} = 1.2\,\text{mΩ}\times(1+0.005\times85) = 1.71\,\text{mΩ}
$$

Copper path resistance (phase pad + bus pour + vias): R_Cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ per channel loop **[extract from layout]**.

$$
P_{cond} = 80^2(2\times1.71\text{m} + 0.3\text{m}) = 6400 \times 3.72\text{m} = \mathbf{23.8\ W}
$$

At 45 A continuous (T_j ≈ 90 °C, R_ds = 1.59 mΩ):

$$
P_{cond} = 2025\times(3.18+0.3)\text{m} = \mathbf{7.0\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Switching Loss at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switched high-side FET has hard-switching overlap loss. The terms are:

$$
P_{sw} = \underbrace{\tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}}_{\text{V-I overlap}}
+ \underbrace{Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw}}_{\text{reverse recovery}}
+ \underbrace{Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw}}_{\text{C}_{oss}}
+ \underbrace{2 V_f I t_{dt} f_{sw}}_{\text{dead-time diode}}
$$

At 80 A, 25.2 V, 48 kHz:

| Term | Expression | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| Overlap | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 30 ns × 48 k | 1.45 W |
| Q_rr | 60 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.073 W |
| Q_oss | 50 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.060 W |
| Dead time | 2 × 0.8 × 80 × 100 ns × 48 k | 0.614 W |
| **P_sw total** | | **2.20 W** |

Gate-drive loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g, not the FETs:

$$
P_{gate} = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2\times60\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k} = 0.058\ \text{W per active leg}
$$

**Scaling with frequency.** Switching loss is linear in f_sw. Moving from 24 kHz to 48 kHz roughly doubles it, from about 1.1 W to 2.2 W at 80 A. The benefit is lower current ripple and acoustic noise. Conduction loss dominates (~11:1), so 48 kHz is acceptable.
