# STM32H743 FC + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC: Loss Model, Thermal Budget, and Bidirectional DShot600 Allocation

**Job #65 · Architecture Reference Rev A**

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

**The STM32H743 is not the commutation controller.** In production 4-in-1 designs, each motor is commutated by its own ESC MCU, typically an STM32G071 or AT32F421 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Those MCUs generate the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to them.

The H743 could in principle run FOC for four motors, but ArduPilot does not support that. Everything below assumes the split architecture.

**All device parameters are assumed.** Replace them with datasheet values for the parts you actually select before signing off.

**Feasibility warning.** An 80 A burst on a 30×30 mm board is at the edge of physical feasibility. The math below shows it is burst-only, limited to a few seconds.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C the multiplier is 1.45:

- Typical: 1.45 mΩ
- Worst case: **1.885 mΩ**

### 1.2 Six-step with synchronous rectification

In any commutation sector, exactly two phase legs carry I_ph. With complementary switching, the current always flows through one FET in each of the two active legs. This is the high-side or the low-side depending on the PWM state.

The total series resistance is therefore 2·R_ds regardless of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j) + I_{ph}^2 R_{pcb}$$

| Case | FET loss | Copper loss | **Per motor** | ×4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Typical, 125 °C | 6400·2.9 mΩ = 18.6 W | 3.2 W | 21.8 W | 87 W |
| Worst, 125 °C | 6400·3.77 mΩ = **24.1 W** | 3.2 W | **27.3 W** | **109 W** |

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each phase conducts for 2/3 of the electrical cycle, split between its high-side and low-side FET according to duty. Averaged over the six FETs:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{24.1}{6} = 4.0\ \text{W}$$

The instantaneous loss in a conducting FET is 12.1 W. That is the figure to use against Z_th at low eRPM, where commutation periods exceed about 5 ms.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In six-step, one leg is PWM-switched at a time. With a hard-switched inductive load:

$$P_{sw} = \underbrace{\tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw}}_{V\text{–}I\ overlap} + \underbrace{Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}}_{C_{oss}} + \underbrace{Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}}_{recovery} + \underbrace{V_f I_{ph}\,2t_{dt}\, f_{sw}}_{dead\text{-}time\ diode}$$

| Term | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | 0.5·25.2·80·35 ns·48 kHz | 1.69 W |
| C_oss | 60 nC·25.2·48 kHz | 0.07 W |
| Q_rr | 50 nC·25.2·48 kHz | 0.06 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.8·80·400 ns·48 kHz | 1.23 W |
| **Total per motor** | | **3.05 W** |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver) | 2·60 nC·10 V·48 kHz | 0.06 W |

Two sensitivities matter:

- **Dead-time loss is about 40 % of switching loss.** Cutting dead time from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per motor. Verify shoot-through margin with a double-pulse test first.
- **Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw.** At 24 kHz it falls to about 1.5 W, but current ripple rises.

### 2.1 Total burst dissipation

$$P_{motor} = 27.3 + 3.05 \approx 30.4\ \text{W}, \qquad P_{board} \approx \mathbf{121.5\ W}\ (\text{worst case, all four at 80 A})$$

### 3.2 First-order RC network

Each FET couples to the board through Z_th(j-b), which feeds the board capacitance C_board. The board sheds heat to ambient through R_th(b-a). Assumed values:

- **R_th(b-a) ≈ 2.5 K/W** with prop-wash airflow. Without airflow it is about 8 K/W.
- **R_th(j-b) ≈ 3 K/W** per FET. This combines R_th(jc) of about 1 K/W with via-array spreading.

The board time constant is:

$$\tau_{board}=R_{th(b\text{-}a)}C_{board}= 2.5 \times 4.3 \approx 10.8\ \text{s
