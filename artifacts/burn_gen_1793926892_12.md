## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation-State Analysis

In each of the 6 commutation sectors, two phases carry current I:

- **Phase X (PWM leg):** the high-side conducts for D·T_sw. The low-side (synchronous) conducts for (1−D)·T_sw.
- **Phase Y (return leg):** the low-side is fully on.

The loss in one sector is:

$$P_{sector} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Synchronous rectification makes conduction loss independent of duty cycle, apart from the dead-time intervals handled in §2.

Each FET is active for 1/3 of the electrical cycle, which gives:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 I^2 R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad P_{cond,FET} = \frac{I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)}{3}$$

### 1.2 Ripple Correction

Phase inductance ripple ΔI_pp adds an RMS term:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus}-E_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

Example: L_ph = 10 µH, V_bus − E = 6 V, D = 0.8 gives ΔI_pp = 10 A. The correction is 8.3 A², which is under 0.2 % at 80 A and is neglected below.
