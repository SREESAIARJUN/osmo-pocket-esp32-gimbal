# STM32H743 Nano AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Systems Architecture Package (Job #36)

## 0. Architectural Reality Checks

These constraints shape the rest of the design:

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every practical 4-in-1 design, each ESC channel has its own commutation MCU (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to the ESC MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. This document models the system that way.
2. **"80A burst" on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board is only achievable as a short transient (about 1–2 s), and not on all four channels at once.** Four channels at 80 A phase current near full duty means about 320 A of battery current. No nano-scale input copper or XT30/XT60 connector carries that. The thermal model below quantifies the real burst window.
3. **All component values are design assumptions.** They are representative of current 40 V, ~1 mΩ, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN MOSFETs and FD6288-class drivers. Substitute datasheet values before release and validate on hardware with thermocouples and an IR camera.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current path in 6-step synchronous PWM

At any instant, two of the three phases are energised. Current flows through exactly **one FET in each active leg**:
- During the on-time: the high-side FET in the PWM leg plus the low-side FET in the return leg.
- During the off-time: the low-side FET (synchronous rectifier) in the PWM leg plus the low-side FET in the return leg.

So the motor current always sees **two R_DS(on) in series**, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 2.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond per ESC @ 80 A | P_cond per ESC @ 45 A |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W | 4.05 W |
| 75 °C | 1.23 mΩ | 15.7 W | 4.97 W |
| 100 °C | 1.34 mΩ | 17.1 W | 5.42 W |
| 125 °C | 1.45 mΩ | 18.6 W | 5.87 W |

### 2.3 Electrothermal feedback (stability check)

Conduction loss rises with temperature, which raises temperature further. The steady state is stable only if

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T} \cdot R_{th} < 1 \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; 2 I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\, R_{th,ja,eff} < 1$$

At 80 A: 2·6400·0.001·0.0045 = 0.0576 W/K. Runaway occurs if R_th,eff > ~17 K/W per ESC. The nano board's effective R_th to ambient is roughly 3–6 K/W per quadrant under prop wash, so there is no thermal runaway. However, the steady state at 80 A is far above T_j,max (see §5), so **80 A is transient-only**.

### 2.4 Copper (PCB) conduction losses

Assumptions for phase copper: 2 oz (70 µm) on 4 of 6 layers in parallel, effective trace length 8 mm, width 4 mm.

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho\, L}{w\, t\, n} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot 0.008}{0.004\cdot 70\times10^{-6}\cdot 4} = 0.123\ \text{m}\Omega$$

- Two phase paths in series, plus DC bus/ground share ≈ 0.35 mΩ effective per ESC.
- P_Cu ≈ 6400 · 0.35 mΩ ≈ **2.2 W per ESC** at 80 A.
- Copper resistivity rises about 0.39 %/K, so this term is roughly ×1.3 at 100 °C.

**Shunt (if a per-ESC low-side shunt is used):** 0.25 mΩ × 6400 = **1.6 W** at 80 A. Many nano designs instead use a single bus shunt or rely on R_DS(on) sensing. Budget it here.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the **PWM leg** hard-switches each period. The return-leg low-side FET is static within a 60° commutation sector.

### 3.1 Hard-switching (V–I overlap)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}\, V_{bus}\, I_{ph}\,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}$$

$$= 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 3.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I_{ph}\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 400\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

This term scales linearly with dead time. Reducing t_dt to 100 ns (if driver propagation-delay matching permits) saves about 0.6 W per ESC.

### 3.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 50\text{nC}\cdot 25.2\cdot 48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

(The induced extra high-side turn-on loss is roughly the same magnitude, so budget 0.12 W.)

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \cdot 2 = 60\text{nC}\cdot 25.2\cdot 48\text{k} = 0.073\ \text{W}$$
