# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Engineering Reference (Job #25)

## 0. Scope and Critical Design Flags

These items determine whether the design is viable. Review them before the math.

| # | Flag | Implication |
|---|------|-------------|
| F1 | **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** A 4-in-1 ESC needs its own MCU per channel (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32/Bluejay), with a 3-phase gate driver and 6 MOSFETs per channel. | The H743 handles flight control and DShot generation only. The 48 kHz PWM is an ESC-MCU parameter, not an H743 timer. |
| F2 | **80 A per motor on a "nano" (20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm) board is not sustainable.** Section 2 shows a heat input of about 90–100 W into roughly 2–3 J/K of thermal mass. | 80 A is a **≤1–2 s burst** rating at best, and only if FETs, copper weight and current sense are sized for it. Treat any continuous rating above about 35–45 A on this footprint as suspect. |
| F3 | Part values below are **representative parameters**, not a validated BOM. | Replace every parameter with datasheet values for your chosen MOSFET and driver, then verify with thermal imaging and a thermocouple on the FET tab. |
| F4 | The ArduPilot hwdef syntax and BDShot rules described here follow ArduPilot's ChibiOS hwdef conventions. | Verify them against the current ArduPilot `libraries/AP_HAL_ChibiOS/hwdef` documentation for your firmware version. |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + k_T (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0055 × 100) = **1.55 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current path (trapezoidal 6-step, synchronous rectification)

At any instant two phases conduct:

- **PWM leg:** current flows through the high-side FET for D·T_sw and the low-side FET (synchronous rectifier) for (1−D)·T_sw. Its RMS conduction is therefore always one R_DS.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET is fully on for that 60° sector, adding one R_DS.

$$P_{cond,ESC} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j) + I^2 R_{pcb} + I^2 R_{shunt}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET RMS (for junction temperature)

Each FET conducts 1/3 of the electrical cycle, split by duty across HS and LS roles. For the worst-case FET (LS in the return role plus LS during freewheel):

$$I_{RMS,LS} \approx I\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{3}\left[1 + (1-D)\right]/2}$$

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A

| Term | Value | P @ 25 °C | P @ 125 °C |
|---|---|---|---|
| FETs (2 × R_DS) | 2.0 / 3.1 mΩ | 12.8 W | **19.8 W** |
| PCB copper (phase + bus, est.) | 0.4 mΩ | 2.6 W | 3.4 W (Cu +0.39 %/K) |
| Shunt (if per-ESC sense) | 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W | 1.6 W |
| **Per ESC channel** | | **17.0 W** | **24.8 W** |

**Battery vs. phase current:** I_batt ≈ D × I_phase. At D = 0.85, 80 A phase gives 68 A per motor and 272 A battery for all four. Pack, XT60/XT90 and pad sizing must reflect that. An XT60 cannot carry 272 A even as a burst.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches in 6-step. With FOC/sinusoidal drive all three legs switch, so multiply by about 3 and derate I.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V-I overlap

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

$$= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 22\times10^{-9} \times 48\times10^{3} = \mathbf{1.06\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (LS body diode during dead time)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

Reverse recovery also inflates turn-on current. Add $I_{rr}\,t_{rr}$ overlap if the datasheet shows t_rr > 20 ns.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 35\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.021\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver IC and gate resistors)

$$P_g = Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} \times N_{switching\ FETs} = 30\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = \mathbf{0.029\ W}$$

### 2.6 Switching total

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| V-I overlap | 1.06 |
| Q_rr | 0.05 |
| Q_oss | 0.02 |
| Dead time | 0.61 |
| Gate | 0.03 |
| **P_sw per ESC** | **≈ 1.77 W** |

Losses scale as $P_{sw} \propto f_{sw}$. Going from 24 to 48 kHz doubles these roughly 1.8 W. That is small next to the 25 W of conduction loss at 80 A, but it dominates at cruise currents of about 10 A, where conduction is only about 0.3 W.

### 2.7 Total dissipation at 80 A

$$P_{ESC} = 24.8 + 1.8 \approx \mathbf{26.6\ W\ per\ channel}$$

$$P_{board} \approx 4 \times 26.6 + P_{ESC\ MCUs+LDO} (\sim 0.5) \approx \mathbf{107\ W}$$

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Two-node network per FET plus board lumped mass

```
P_FET ──► [Zth_jc(t)] ──► T_case ──► [R_th,cb] ──► T_board ──► [R_th,ba] ──► T_amb
                                                     │
                                                 C_board
```

### 3.2 Board thermal capacitance (36×36 mm, 6-layer 2 oz)

| Material | Volume | Mass | c_p | C |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Copper (6 × 70 µm, ~85 % fill) | 12.96 cm² × 0.042 cm × 0.85 = 0.46 cm³ | 4.1 g | 0.385 J/g·K | 1.59 J/K |
| FR4 (1.6 mm) | 2.07 cm³ | 3.8 g | 1.1 J/g·K | 4.2 J/K |
| 24 FETs + passives + solder | — | ~2.5 g | ~0.5 | 1.25 J/K |
| **Total** | | | | **≈ 7.0 J/K** |

FR4 is a poor lateral conductor, so the *effective* capacitance near the FETs over 1 s is lower. Use C_eff ≈ 4 J/K.

### 3.3 Adiabatic burst heating

$$\frac{dT_{board}}{dt} = \frac{P_{board}}{C_{eff}} = \frac{107}{4} \approx \mathbf{27\ K/s}$$

Starting at 50 °C (hot from cruise), with a board limit of about 110 °C (FR4 Tg margin and solder reliability):

$$t_{burst} \approx \frac{110 - 50}{27} \approx \mathbf{2.2\ s}$$
