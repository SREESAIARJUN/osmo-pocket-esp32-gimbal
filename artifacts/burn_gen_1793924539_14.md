# STM32H743 Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Nano AIO): Architecture, Loss Models, and DShot Allocation

## 0. Assumptions

### 0.1 Architectural correction

The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. In every ArduPilot-compatible 4-in-1 design, the system is split as follows:

| Domain | MCU | Firmware | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIH6 (480 MHz rev V) | ArduPilot | Sends bidirectional DShot600 and receives eRPM |
| ESC (×4 channels) | 4× AT32F421 or STM32G071 (one per motor) | AM32 | 48 kHz complementary PWM, BEMF commutation |
| Gate drive | 4× FD6288Q (3-phase, 20 V) | n/a | High-side bootstrap and low-side drive |

The 48 kHz switching frequency is an ESC-firmware parameter (AM32 `PWM_FREQ`). It is not an H743 timer function.

### 0.2 Design point

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Bus voltage | 6S, V_bus = 25.2 V max (22.2 V nominal) |
| Burst current | 80 A per motor, 10 s max |
| f_sw | 48 kHz, synchronous (complementary) PWM |
| MOSFET (example class) | 40 V, R_ds(on),25°C = 0.70 mΩ, Q_g = 60 nC, Q_rr ≈ 50 nC, Q_oss ≈ 40 nC, PDFN 5×6 |
| Dead time t_d | 200 ns |
| Board | 36 × 36 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner, 30.5 mm mount |

### 0.3 Feasibility warning

The model below shows that 4 × 80 A is thermally possible only as a short transient on a nano-sized board. It cannot be sustained. Treat 80 A as a burst rating with a firmware-enforced duration limit.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step trapezoidal commutation, two phase legs are active in each 60° sector:
- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for duty D, and the low-side FET conducts (synchronous rectification) for 1 − D.
- **Static leg:** the low-side FET (or high-side, depending on scheme) conducts 100 % of the sector.

So with synchronous rectification, exactly two R_ds(on) are in series with the phase current at every instant, independent of D:

$$P_{cond,FET} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ /K$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 0.70 × 1.6 = **1.12 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Ripple contribution

Phase current ripple:

$$\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}$$

With L_ph = 15 µH (typical 2207 motor), V_bus − V_bemf ≈ 4 V, and D ≈ 0.9:

ΔI ≈ 4 × 0.9 / (15e-6 × 48e3) ≈ 5 A p-p

$$I_{rms} = \sqrt{I_{dc}^2 + \Delta I^2/12} = \sqrt{6400 + 2.1} \approx 80.01\ \text{A}$$

The ripple contribution is negligible.

### 1.4 Copper loss

Path: battery pad → bus planes → FET → phase pad, with a total estimated R_Cu ≈ 0.5 mΩ per motor current path. Copper tempco is 0.0039/K.

### 1.5 Results per motor at 80 A

| Term | Expression | Value |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (T_j = 125 °C) | 2 · 80² · 1.12 mΩ | **14.3 W** |
| FET conduction (T_j = 25 °C, start of burst) | 2 · 80² · 0.70 mΩ | 9.0 W |
| PCB copper | 80² · 0.5 mΩ | 3.2 W |

Per-FET average is ≈ P_cond / 6 ≈ 2.4 W at hot R_ds(on), assuming symmetric commutation.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM half-bridge switches in each sector, so there is one hard-switched commutation pair per motor.

### 2.1 Voltage–current overlap

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

Gate current comes from the FD6288Q (sources about 1.5 A peak and sinks about 1.8 A), with 4.7 Ω gate resistors. The Miller plateau gives t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 20 ns.

P_VI = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40e-9 × 48e3 = **1.94 W**

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

The body diode conducts during dead time, then recovers:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{e-9} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{e3} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

Q_rr rises with di/dt and T_j, so derate by 2× and use **0.12 W**.
