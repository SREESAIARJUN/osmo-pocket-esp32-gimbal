## 1. Conduction Loss Model (6-Step Complementary PWM)

In any 60° sector, current flows through two legs:
- **Leg A (PWM leg):** the high side conducts for D·T. The low side conducts for (1−D)·T, either via synchronous rectification or the body diode during dead-time.
- **Leg B (static leg):** the low side is fully on.

At every instant, exactly two channels are in the current path:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This is independent of duty cycle, which is the key result for burst rating.

Temperature dependence:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per bridge at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W |
| 110 °C | 1.38 mΩ | 17.7 W |
| 125 °C | 1.45 mΩ | 18.6 W |

**Additional copper loss.** A 2 oz inner/outer 4-layer path with about 0.3 mΩ per phase loop adds I²R_cu = 6400 × 0.0003 = 1.9 W per motor. This heat is distributed in the board, not in the junctions.

**Per-FET distribution.** At 30k rpm with 14 poles, the electrical frequency is f_e = 3.5 kHz, so each sector lasts about 48 µs. That is far shorter than the die thermal time constant (on the order of milliseconds), so each FET sees the electrical-cycle average:

$$\bar{P}_{FET} \approx \frac{P_{bridge,total}}{6}$$
