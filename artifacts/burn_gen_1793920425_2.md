# STM32H743 AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC): Power-Stage, Thermal & DShot Architecture

## 0. Architectural Ground Rules and Assumptions

**Correction to the premise.** The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. A production-grade AIO uses a split architecture:

| Domain | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743 (400 MHz in ArduPilot) | Attitude control, DShot600 master, bidirectional eRPM capture |
| ESC (×4) | 4× STM32G071 or AT32F421 running AM32/BLHeli_32 | Six-step or FOC-lite commutation, 48 kHz PWM, eRPM GCR reply |
| Gate drive (×4) | 3-phase half-bridge driver (FD6288-class) | Bootstrap high-side drive, VCC ≈ 10–12 V |
| Power stage | 24 N-MOSFETs (6 per ESC) | 3 half-bridges per motor |

**The "nano + 80 A" constraint.** 80 A per channel on a 20×20 mm board cannot be sustained, and it is marginal even on 30.5×30.5 mm. Treat 80 A as a **≤2–3 s burst**. The model below quantifies exactly how long that burst can last.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in six-step with synchronous rectification

At any commutation state, exactly two half-bridges conduct:
- **PWM leg:** the high-side during D·T_sw, and the low-side (synchronous rectification) during (1−D)·T_sw.
- **Sink leg:** the low-side, on continuously for the 60° sector.

At every instant, therefore, **two FETs are in series with the motor current**, independent of duty cycle (neglecting dead-time):

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I_m^2\,R_{DS(on)}\,k_T$$

With ripple, use the RMS motor current:

$$I_{m,RMS}^2 = I_m^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph,ll}\,f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5″–7″ motor, L_ll ≈ 15–30 µH. At D = 0.5, V_bus − V_bemf ≈ 12 V and L = 20 µH, ΔI ≈ 6.25 A, which adds only about 0.5 % to I². It is negligible at 80 A.

### 1.2 Numbers at 80 A

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.5 = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

**Per-FET distribution.** Each FET conducts for one third of the electrical period. The PWM-leg pair splits its sector by D, but the sector average is symmetric. This gives:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx \frac{19.2}{6} = 3.2\ \text{W (average)}, \qquad \hat P_{FET} = I_m^2 R k_T = 9.6\ \text{W (instantaneous during conduction)}$$

### 1.3 PCB copper loss (frequently ignored, and significant)

A 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner copper trace has a sheet resistance of about 0.25 mΩ/□. A phase path of roughly 6 squares, paralleled across 4 layers, gives about 0.4 mΩ. Counting the effective path pair:

$$P_{Cu} \approx 80^2 \times 2 \times 0.4\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 5.1\ \text{W}$$

Bus copper adds more on top of this. **Budget about 5 W per ESC** unless the board is 8-layer with heavy copper.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The high-side FET sees hard turn-on and turn-off; the low-side switches at near-zero voltage after the dead-time.

**2.1 Overlap (V–I crossover) loss**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_m (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

**2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction**

$$P_{DT} = V_{SD}\,I_m\,(2t_{dead})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**2.3 Reverse recovery**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

The real effect is larger because the recovery current spike adds to the turn-on overlap. Apply a ×1.5 factor to P_VI turn-on if no Schottky-like body diode is present.

**2.4 Output capacitance**

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

**2.5 Gate drive** (dissipated in the driver and R_g, not in the FET)

$$P_{g} = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.058\ \text{W}$$

**2.6 Summary per ESC at 80 A, 25.2 V, 48 kHz**

| Loss term | W |
|---|---|
| MOSFET conduction | 19.2 |
| Switching (VI + DT + Qrr + Qoss) | 1.69 |
| PCB copper | ~5.0 |
| Gate drive / driver / MCU / LDO | ~0.3 |
| **Total per ESC** | **≈ 26 W** |
| **All four at 80 A** | **≈ 105 W** |
