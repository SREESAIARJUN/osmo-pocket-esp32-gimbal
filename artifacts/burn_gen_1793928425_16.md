### Three findings that must be resolved before layout

1. **80 A per channel on four channels at once is not thermally survivable on a 30.5 mm board for 10 s.** The adiabatic estimate in §3.4 reaches about 110–130 K of board temperature rise. 80 A is defensible only as:
   - a per-channel rating, *or*
   - a shorter burst (≤ 2–3 s) with 4-channel derating.

   On a 20 × 20 mm "nano" footprint, 80 A is not credible at all. **Recommendation:** spec "80 A burst (single channel, 5 s), 55–60 A continuous-equivalent aggregate."
2. **The STM32H743 does not switch the MOSFETs.** In every production AIO/4-in-1 design, each ESC channel has its own MCU, for example:
   - AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32
   - EFM8BB21 running BLHeli_S / Bluejay

   The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to those MCUs over bi-directional DShot600. The 48 kHz PWM lives in ESC firmware, not in ArduPilot. The architecture below follows that split.
3. **DShot600 bidirectional framing bounds the motor update rate** to roughly 8–10 kHz maximum (§4.2). ArduPilot runs DShot at a multiple of its loop rate, so the 1–8 kHz range is the realistic one.

## 1. Electrical Operating Model (one ESC channel, 6-step, complementary PWM)

During any 60° commutation sector, exactly two phases conduct. Take the example of phase A sourcing and phase B sinking:

- **Leg A (PWM leg):**
  - High-side FET Q_AH is on for duty D.
  - Low-side FET Q_AL synchronously rectifies for (1 − D), minus dead time.
- **Leg B (sink leg):** Q_BL is on for the whole sector.
- **Leg C:** floating, used for BEMF sensing.

The phase current I is approximately constant over a PWM period, because L_phase / R_phase ≫ 1/f_sw. Mean-to-RMS ripple correction is in §2.1.

At any instant, the current flows through **one** FET in leg A and **one** FET in leg B. That gives two R_ds(on) in series.

Duty at 80 A: on a high-thrust burst the duty runs 0.85–1.0. **Use D = 0.9** for the loss split. **Use D = 0.5** for the capacitor RMS worst case.

## 2. Loss Model

### 2.1 Conduction losses

Temperature-dependent on-resistance:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

Phase current RMS, including PWM-frequency ripple ΔI_pp:

$$I_{rms} = \sqrt{I^2 + \tfrac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}}$$

Ripple magnitude:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus} - E_{bemf})\,D}{2 L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5-inch-class motor with L_ph ≈ 15 µH and V_bus − E ≈ 3 V at D = 0.9, ΔI_pp ≈ 1.9 A. This is negligible against 80 A: I_rms ≈ 80.002 A. Drop the term for this motor class. Keep it for low-inductance cinewhoop motors (≤ 5 µH).

Per-FET conduction loss over the sector, averaged over a full electrical cycle:

| FET | On-fraction in sector | Electrical-cycle occupancy | Average conduction loss |
|---|---|---|---|
| High-side (PWM leg) | D | sources during 1/3 of cycle | $\tfrac{1}{3} D I^2 R_{ds}$ |
| Low-side, sync rectifier | (1 − D) − 2t_dt f_sw | 1/3 | $\tfrac{1}{3}(1-D-2t_{dt}f_{sw}) I^2 R_{ds}$ |
| Low-side, sink leg | 1 | 1/3 | $\tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{ds}$ |

Total for one ESC channel (six FETs), excluding the dead-time window:

$$P_{cond,ESC} \approx I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,(2 - 2 t_{dt} f_{sw})$$

**Numerical example.** Solved iteratively, T_j converges near 105 °C (§3):

- R_ds(105 °C) = 0.80 × [1 + 0.0045 × 80] = **1.09 mΩ**
- 2 t_dt f_sw = 2 × 300 ns × 48 kHz = 0.0288
- P_cond = 80² × 1.09e-3 × (2 − 0.0288) = **13.75 W per ESC channel**

Worst single FET is the sink-leg low-side, which is fully on through its 120°:

- Peak (in-sector) loss: P = I² R = 80² × 1.09e-3 = **6.98 W**
- Cycle-averaged loss: 2.33 W

**Use the in-sector value for transient junction analysis** (§3.2). Electrical sector time at a 2 kHz electrical frequency is 167 µs. That is shorter than the die thermal time constant, so cycle averaging is acceptable for the steady-state board model.

### 2.2 Copper (PCB) conduction loss

80 A must travel from the battery pad, through the bus planes, through the FETs, and out to the motor pad.

Plane resistance:

$$R_{cu} = \rho_{cu}(T)\,\frac{\ell}{w\,t}$$

with ρ_cu(80 °C) = 2.06e-8 Ω·m.

- 2 oz copper: t = 70 µm.
- Effective path: ℓ ≈ 15 mm, w ≈ 6 mm, with 3 paralleled 2 oz layers.
- R_cu ≈ 2.06e-8 × 0.015 / (0.006 × 3 × 70e-6) = **0.245 mΩ** each way. Count the supply and return paths, so about 0.49 mΩ.
- Add motor-pad and solder joints, about 0.15 mΩ, for a total of **R_cu ≈ 0.64 mΩ**.

$$P_{cu} = I^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.64\text{e-}3 = \mathbf{4.1\ W\ per\ channel}$$

This is often the forgotten 25% of the heat budget, and it lands directly in the board.
