# STM32H743 Nano AIO: 4-in-1 ESC Design Package (Job #74)

## 0. Architectural Clarifications

Three points shape everything below:

1. **The STM32H743 is the flight-control MCU, not the ESC controller.** ArduPilot does not run motor commutation. A 4-in-1 ESC needs four ESC MCUs (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware) and three-phase gate drivers. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to those four MCUs. I've modeled it that way.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm mount, ~25×25 mm PCB) is thermally a seconds-scale rating, not a sustained one.** The math in §3 shows roughly 1–2 s with all four channels at 80 A. Treat that as a quantified result, not a flaw in the analysis.
3. **Device parameters are representative placeholders.** They are typical of 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN MOSFETs. Substitute your selected part's datasheet values into the equations. All formulas are parametric.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Paths in a Commutation Sector

In each 60° sector, two phases conduct:

- **PWM leg:** The HS FET conducts for duty D. The LS FET of the same leg (synchronous rectifier) conducts for 1−D.
- **Return leg:** The LS FET is fully on.

Per-ESC conduction loss:

$$P_{cond} = \frac{I^2 R_{hot}}{n}\Big[D + (1-D) + 1\Big] = \frac{2\,I^2 R_{hot}}{n}$$

This is independent of D, which is the benefit of synchronous rectification.

### 1.2 Hot Resistance and Electro-Thermal Coupling

$$R_{hot} = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1} \Rightarrow k_T = 1.5 \text{ at } 125\,°\text{C}$$

$$R_{hot} = 1.3 \times 1.5 = 1.95\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Numerical Result

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.95\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{24.96\ W\ per\ ESC}$$

### 1.4 Copper Path Loss

Assume the PCB current path from bus input through the phase pad, on 2 oz inner layers, totals about 0.4 mΩ:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.4\times10^{-3} = 2.56\ \text{W per ESC}$$

### 1.5 Thermal Runaway Check

Loss rises with T_j through R_hot. The system is stable if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j} \cdot R_{th,ja} < 1 \;\Rightarrow\; 2 I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\, R_{th,ja} < 1$$

With 80 A, the stability limit is:

$$R_{th,ja} < \frac{1}{2\times6400\times1.3\times10^{-3}\times0.005} = 12\ \text{K/W (per ESC aggregate)}$$

A nano board in prop wash is roughly 5–8 K/W per ESC quadrant. It is therefore stable, but with modest margin. Do not reduce copper.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches.

### 2.1 V–I Overlap (Hard-Switching, Inductive Clamp)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\times10^{-9} \times 48\times10^3 = \mathbf{1.935\ W}$$

The rise and fall times are set by the Miller plateau:

$$t_{r,f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g},\qquad I_g = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}{R_g + R_{drv}}$$

Example: Q_gd = 8 nC, V_drv = 10 V, V_pl = 3.5 V, R_g + R_drv = 33 Ω. Then I_g ≈ 0.2 A and t_r ≈ 40 ns per edge. To reach the assumed 20 ns per edge, reduce R_g to about 10–15 Ω. This trades EMI and ringing for efficiency.

### 2.2 Output Capacitance Loss

$$P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Body Diode Reverse Recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-Time Diode Conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\,(2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{n}\times48\text{k} = 0.614\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate Drive

This loss is dissipated in the driver, not the FET:

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\times30\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k} = 0.029\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Switching Total

$$P_{sw,total} = 1.935 + 0.048 + 0.036 + 0.614 = \mathbf{2.63\ W\ per\ ESC}$$

**Sensitivity to frequency.** All switching terms are linear in f_sw. At 24 kHz this loss halves to about 1.3 W. At 96 kHz it doubles to about 5.3 W. At 80 A, conduction dominates by roughly 10:1, so 48 kHz is a reasonable choice for current ripple and smoothness.
