## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Flags

**Architecture correction.** The STM32H743 is not the ESC MCU. It runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. Each motor channel needs its own commutation MCU running AM32, BLHeli_32, or Bluejay. The correct architecture is:

```
H743 (ArduPilot) ──DShot600 bidir ×4──► 4× ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32)
                                          └─► 4× 3-phase driver ─► 24× N-MOSFET
```

**Feasibility flags.** These must be accepted by the program before layout.

| # | Issue | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | "Nano" (20×20 mm) at 80 A × 4 = **320 A battery burst** | 24 FETs plus a 320 A bus on about 400 mm² is at the edge of physical possibility. 30×30 is strongly recommended. |
| F2 | 80 A is a **burst** rating only | Section 3 shows continuous is about 30 A/ch and burst is about 5 s. |
| F3 | 48 kHz PWM on 6S | Viable, but dead-time diode loss becomes comparable to switching loss (Section 2). |
| F4 | AIO noise coupling into IMU | Mandatory split ground, IMU on a soft-mounted daughterboard or opposite corner. |

All numerical values below use the assumptions in Section 1. **Verify every device parameter against the datasheet of the part actually procured.**

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.2 mΩ × 1.55 = **1.86 mΩ**. Use 1.8 mΩ as the hot design value.

### 2.2 Conduction loss

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, exactly two FETs carry the phase current at every instant. The high-side FET of the PWM leg carries it during D, its complementary low-side carries it during (1−D), and the low-side of the return phase carries it continuously. Ripple is neglected because I_ripple ≪ I_m at 48 kHz with motor L ≈ 10–30 µH.

$$P_{cond}=I_m^2\cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)\quad(\text{independent of }D)$$

$$P_{cond}=80^2\times 2\times 1.8\,\text{m}\Omega=\mathbf{23.0\ W}$$

The hottest single FET is the always-on return low-side: 80² × 1.8 mΩ = **11.5 W**. Commutation rotates this duty among all six FETs, so the time-averaged load is about 11.5 × 2/6 ≈ 3.8 W per FET. However, at low eRPM each FET carries the full 11.5 W for milliseconds. The design must use the **peak**, not the average.

### 2.3 Switching loss (hard-switched edge, one per PWM transition)

Gate current at the Miller plateau (V_pl ≈ 3 V). Driver R_on ≈ 2 Ω, R_off ≈ 1 Ω, internal R_g ≈ 1 Ω:

$$I_{g,on}=\frac{10-3}{2+2.2+1}=1.35\,\text{A},\qquad I_{g,off}=\frac{3}{1+2.2+1}=0.71\,\text{A}$$

$$t_{on}=\frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}}=8.9\,\text{ns},\qquad t_{off}=\frac{12\,\text{nC}}{0.71}=16.9\,\text{ns}$$

$$P_{sw}=\tfrac12 V_{bus} I_m (t_{on}+t_{off}) f_{sw}=\tfrac12(25.2)(80)(25.8\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{k})=\mathbf{1.25\ W}$$

The turn-off edge dominates. A turn-off bypass diode across R_g (or a PNP pull-down) cuts t_off to about 7 ns, giving P_sw ≈ 0.77 W. This is recommended.
