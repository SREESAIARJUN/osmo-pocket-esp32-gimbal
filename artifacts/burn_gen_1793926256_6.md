# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC "Nano" Stack: Systems Design Package

**Scope note:** I have no access to a "Job #42" spec, so every input is stated as an assumption (marked **[A]**). Substitute your actual MOSFET datasheet values, board dimensions and battery config. The equations are general, and the numbers recompute directly. Two architectural points apply throughout:

1. **The H743 does not drive the power stage.** In a 4-in-1 ESC, each motor's (or the shared) ESC MCU, typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32, generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 only sends DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry. The losses below are ESC-side physics; the DMA plan is FC-side firmware.
2. **80 A burst on a nano-class (20×20 / 30×30) board is thermally marginal.** The math below quantifies how marginal. Treat it as a time-limited rating, not a continuous one.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_0\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At the T_j = 125 °C design point:

$$R_{DS} = 1.2\,(1 + 0.45) = 1.74\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 2.2 Per-motor conduction in a 6-step sector

In each sector, current flows from leg A (PWM leg) to leg B (low-side held ON):

| Switch | Conduction fraction |
|---|---|
| A high-side | D |
| A low-side (synchronous rectifier) | 1 − D |
| B low-side | 1 |

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}$$

With synchronous rectification, conduction loss is **independent of duty cycle**. That is the key simplification.

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.74\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{22.3\ W}$$

Ripple correction: $I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \Delta I^2/12$. For a typical ΔI of ~10 A at 48 kHz on a low-inductance motor, this adds about 0.13 %, which is negligible.

### 2.3 Per-FET average

Each of the 6 FETs per motor rotates through the roles over one electrical revolution:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} = 22.3 / 6 \approx 3.7\ \text{W}$$

The instantaneous peak in the held-ON role is $80^2 \times 1.74\text{m} = 11.1$ W. The electrical period is about 1 ms at high eRPM, which is far shorter than the die thermal time constant (~10–50 ms). The die therefore sees close to the average with a small ripple of a few K.

### 2.4 Copper / interconnect

Assume 0.5 mΩ total per-motor path (pads, traces, motor wires to pads) [A]:

$$P_{Cu,motor} = 80^2 \times 0.5\text{m} = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The high-side turn-on and turn-off are hard; the synchronous low-side switches ZVS-ish after the dead time.

### 3.1 Transition times

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{pl}}{R_{g,tot}} = \frac{10 - 3.5}{6} = 1.08\ \text{A} \quad\Rightarrow\quad t_r = \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{g,on}} = 7.4\ \text{ns}$$

$$I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{g,tot}} = 0.58\ \text{A} \quad\Rightarrow\quad t_f = 13.8\ \text{ns}$$

Real layouts with source inductance typically yield 2–3× these values. A 2× derating gives t_r + t_f ≈ 42 ns.
