## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, two phases are active at any instant:
- During PWM-on time (D), the high-side FET of phase A and the low-side FET of phase B conduct.
- During PWM-off time (1−D), both the low-side of A (sync-rect) and the low-side of B conduct.

So there are always **two FETs in series** carrying the phase current. The undriven third phase carries no current.

$$
P_{cond,motor} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2 \cdot R_{ds(on)}(T_j)
$$

The R_ds(on) temperature dependence is modeled as:

$$
R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds(on)25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ /^\circ C
$$

### 1.2 Ripple correction

The motor phase inductance L_ph creates a peak-to-peak current ripple:

$$
\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\, D}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}
$$

Typical values for a 5" class motor: L_ph ≈ 15 µH, D = 0.9, V_bemf ≈ 21 V.

$$
\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(25.2 - 21)(0.9)}{15\times10^{-6} \cdot 48000} \approx 5.25\ A
$$

The RMS current including ripple is:

$$
I_{rms} = \sqrt{I_{dc}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}} = \sqrt{6400 + 2.3} \approx 80.01\ A
$$

The ripple contribution is negligible, so 80 A is used directly.

### 1.3 Numerical result (T_j = 125 °C)

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 80^2 \times 2 \times (1.2\,m\Omega \times 1.5) = 6400 \times 3.6\,m\Omega = \mathbf{23.0\ W}
$$

**Per FET:** ≈ 11.5 W (time-averaged across the two-FET path).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Hard-switching V-I overlap

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 (25.2)(80)(40\,ns)(48\,kHz) = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

### 2.2 C_oss loss

C_oss is charged and discharged on the switched node each cycle:

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} \cdot 2 = 0.5(1\,nF)(635)(48\,kHz)(2) = \mathbf{0.03\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_f \, I \, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 (80)(200\,ns)(48\,kHz) = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

### 2.4 Reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\,nC (25.2)(48\,kHz) = \mathbf{0.06\ W}
$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated in the driver and R_g, not in the FET. It covers the two switching FETs per motor:

$$
P_g = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} \times 2 = 40\,nC (10)(48\,kHz)(2) = \mathbf{0.04\ W}
$$

### 2.6 Loss budget at 80 A burst

| Loss | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 23.00 W | 92.0 W |
| Switching (V·I overlap) | 1.94 W | 7.8 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W | 2.4 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.09 W | 0.4 W |
| Gate drive | 0.04 W | 0.2 W |
| **Total** | **25.7 W** | **≈ 103 W** |

**Efficiency check:**
- Output power ≈ 25.2 × 80 × 0.9 ≈ 1.81 kW per motor.
- Loss is 1.4 % of output power, which is electrically fine.
- **Thermally, it is not fine:** that 103 W is concentrated in a 4 cm² board.

### 2.7 Frequency sensitivity

Only the switching-related terms (§2.1 to §2.5) scale with f_sw:

$$
\frac{\partial P_{total}}{\partial f_{sw}} \approx 2.68\ W\ /\ 48\,kHz \approx 56\ mW/kHz \ \text{per motor}
$$

- Going to 24 kHz saves about 1.3 W per motor.
- Going to 96 kHz adds about 2.7 W per motor.

Conduction loss dominates, so **FET R_ds(on) and copper are the levers, not f_sw.**

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Steady-state network (per FET)

The thermal path is:

T_j → R_θJC (1.5 K/W) → solder/thermal vias (R_θCB ≈ 2 K/W) → PCB spreading (R_θBA)

- **Junction-to-board rise:** ΔT_jb = 11.5 W × 3.5 K/W = **40 K**. This is acceptable if the board itself stays cool.
- **Board-to-ambient** for a 20×20 board in prop wash (h ≈ 80 W/m²K, A ≈ 2×4 cm² + edges ≈ 9 cm²):

$$
R_{\theta BA} = \frac{1}{hA} = \frac{1}{80 \times 9\times10^{-4}} \approx 14\ K/W
$$

$$
\text{Steady-state } \Delta T_{board} = 103\,W \times 14\,K/W \approx 1440\ K
$$

This is physically impossible. **The board cannot sustain 80 A × 4 continuously.**

### 3.2 Transient (adiabatic burst) model

The board is lumped as a thermal capacitance:

| Copper (6L, 2 oz, ~70 % fill) | ≈ 2.4 g × 0.385 J/gK | ≈ 0.92 J/K |
| FR4 | ≈ 1.2 g × 1.1 J/gK | ≈ 1.3 J/K |
| 24 FETs + capacitors + solder | ≈ 1.5 g × 0.5 J
