# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #20): Power-Stage, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Assumptions

Two points need to be settled before the math, because they change the design.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot outputs DShot. It does not run motor commutation. A "4-in-1 ESC" therefore needs four ESC MCUs (or one per pair), for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 firmware.
- The **48 kHz** figure is the AM32 PWM frequency setting on those MCUs.
- The H743 only generates bi-directional DShot600 and decodes the eRPM telemetry it receives back.

**2. 80 A per channel on a nano (20×20 mm) board is a burst-only rating.** It cannot be sustained, and the math below quantifies exactly how long it can last.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path (6-step trapezoidal with synchronous rectification)

At any instant, two phases conduct:
- **Leg A is PWM'd.** The high-side (HS) conducts for duty D. The complementary low-side (LS) conducts for 1−D (synchronous rectification).
- **Leg B's LS is held on** for 100% of the 60° commutation sector.

The motor current I therefore always flows through exactly **two MOSFET channels in series**:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2 I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha_R (T_j - 25)]
$$

### 1.2 Temperature-coupled solution

R_ds depends on T_j, and T_j depends on loss. Solve by fixed-point iteration, assuming T_j = 110 °C at the end of the burst:

$$
R_{ds}(110) = 0.80 \times [1 + 0.0045 \times 85] = 1.106\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.106\times10^{-3} = 14.16\ \text{W}
$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Over one electrical revolution, each of the 6 FETs in a channel carries current for 1/3 of the time. At burst, the electrical frequency is about 1–3 kHz. The die thermal time constant is about 1–5 ms. Average loss per FET is therefore the right quantity:

$$
\bar P_{cond,FET} = \frac{14.16}{6} \approx 2.36\ \text{W}
$$

**Ripple check.** Instantaneous loss in a conducting LS FET is I²R = 7.08 W. Using Z_th(1 ms) ≈ 0.15 K/W, ΔT_ripple ≈ 1 K. This is negligible.

### 1.4 Parasitic conduction (copper + shunt)

$$
P_{shunt} = I^2 R_{shunt} = 6400 \times 0.25\times10^{-3} = 1.60\ \text{W}
$$

$$
P_{cu} = I^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 1.0\times10^{-3} = 6.40\ \text{W}
$$

Copper loss is larger than you would expect. On a nano board, **copper is the second-largest heat source**. This is why you need 2 oz outer copper, stacked internal planes, and via-stitched phase pads.
