## 0. Assumptions and Timing Architecture

The job spec does not give hardware details, so the design rests on the parameters below. All tuning formulas are written symbolically. Substitute your measured values.

| Item | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Motor | Gimbal BLDC, 12N14P (pole pairs `p = 7`), `R_s ≈ 5–10 Ω`, `L_s ≈ 1–3 mH` |
| Position sensor | 14-bit magnetic encoder (AS5047P/MT6701-class), read every PWM cycle |
| IMUs | 2× ICM-42688-P on SPI at 24 MHz, ODR 8 kHz, ±2000 dps, ±16 g |
| MCU | Cortex-M4F/M7 with single-precision FPU |
| Bus | `V_bus` = 12–16 V |

**Loop rates** (each loop is roughly 5–10× faster than the bandwidth of the loop outside it):

| Loop | Rate | Target bandwidth | Trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| Current (FOC d/q PI) | 20 kHz | 1.5–2 kHz | ADC end-of-conversion ISR |
| IMU filtering and fusion | 8 kHz | n/a | IMU INT1 → SPI DMA |
| Velocity PI(D) | 4 kHz | 150–300 Hz | Every 5th FOC tick |
| Angle P/PI + feedforward | 1 kHz | 20–50 Hz | Every 4th velocity tick |

Gimbal-specific choices:
- The **angle loop** closes on the IMU-estimated camera attitude, expressed in each joint's coordinates.
- The **velocity loop** closes on the filtered IMU gyro rate projected onto the joint axis.
- The encoder is used for **commutation**, the **cogging map lookup**, and **kinematic projection**.

This arrangement rejects base motion directly at the rate level, which is what stabilizes the camera.

## 1. Field-Oriented Control (Current Loop)

### 1.1 Transforms

Electrical angle from the mechanical encoder angle:

$$\theta_e = p\,(\theta_m - \theta_{\text{offset}}) \bmod 2\pi$$

**Clarke transform** (amplitude-invariant, assuming `i_a + i_b + i_c = 0`):

$$i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \frac{1}{\sqrt3}(i_a + 2 i_b)$$

**Park transform:**

$$\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}$$

**Inverse Park transform:**

$$v_\alpha = v_d\cos\theta_e - v_q\sin\theta_e,\qquad v_\beta = v_d\sin\theta_e + v_q\cos\theta_e$$

### 1.2 Plant Model (SPMSM, `L_d = L_q = L_s`)

$$v_d = R_s i_d + L_s\dot i_d - \omega_e L_s i_q$$
$$v_q = R_s i_q + L_s\dot i_q + \omega_e L_s i_d + \omega_e\lambda_m$$
$$\tau_e = \tfrac32 p\,\lambda_m\, i_q = K_t\, i_q$$

### 1.3 Current PI with Decoupling (Pole-Zero Cancellation)

Choose the PI zero to cancel the electrical pole `R_s/L_s`. With target bandwidth `ω_c`:

$$K_{p,i} = L_s\,\omega_c,\qquad K_{i,i} = R_s\,\omega_c$$

The closed loop then becomes first order: `i/i* = ω_c/(s+ω_c)`.

Decoupling and back-EMF feedforward terms:

$$v_d^* = \text{PI}_d(i_d^*-i_d) - \omega_e L_s i_q$$
$$v_q^* = \text{PI}_q(i_q^*-i_q) + \omega_e L_s i_d + \omega_e\lambda_m$$

Delay limit: with `T_s = 50 µs` and about 1.5 `T_s` of total delay (computation plus PWM), phase margin stays above 45° for `ω_c ≲ 1/(4·1.5 T_s) ≈ 2π·530 Hz` (conservative). Gimbal motors with large `L/R` usually tolerate `ω_c = 2π·1–2 kHz`. Verify by step test.

### 1.4 Voltage Limiting and Anti-Windup

The output is limited to the linear SVPWM circle, `|v_dq| ≤ V_bus/√3`.

- **d-axis priority:** `v_d` is clamped to `±V_max`.
- **q-axis remainder:** `v_q` is clamped to `±√(V_max² − v_d²)`.
- **Anti-windup:** back-calculation on each integrator, using the gain below.

$$x_I \leftarrow x_I + T_s\left[K_i e + K_{aw}(v_{\text{sat}} - v_{\text{unsat}})\right],\qquad K_{aw}=\frac{K_i}{K_p}$$
