# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Engineering Model (Job #7)

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Architecture Corrections

This document gives a parametric model. Every numeric result depends on the assumption table below. Before sign-off, replace the assumed device values with datasheet values for the parts you actually select.

**Architecture correction.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller. It does not drive the MOSFET gates. Each ESC channel, or the 4-in-1 as a group, has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical ESC MCUs are the STM32G071, STM32F051, or AT32F421. The resulting split of responsibilities is:

| Domain | Owner | This document |
|---|---|---|
| 48 kHz PWM, commutation, dead-time | ESC MCU | §1–§3 (power stage physics) |
| Bidirectional DShot600 out, eRPM telemetry in | STM32H743 (ArduPilot) | §4 |
| Interconnect | Signal harness / board-to-board | §5 |

**Feasibility flag.** 80A per channel on a "nano" form factor (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) is not thermally sustainable. §3 shows that it is viable only as a burst of a few seconds. I treat "80A burst" as per-channel phase current and show the time limit.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path (trapezoidal 6-step, complementary/synchronous PWM)

At any commutation step, exactly two phases conduct.

- **PWM ON:** HS(x) → motor → LS(y)
- **PWM OFF:** freewheel through LS(x) and LS(y) (synchronous rectification)

So in both states, current flows through **two FET channels in series**. This holds regardless of duty cycle D. With non-synchronous PWM, the OFF state uses the body diode instead, which is far worse.

### 1.2 Equation

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j) \;+\; I_{rms}^2\, R_{Cu}$$

- For trapezoidal drive with flat-top current: I_rms ≈ I_pk.
- R_Cu is the PCB trace and solder resistance in the loop. Budget about 0.3 mΩ for 2 oz inner/outer layers on a nano board.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80A

| T_j | R_ds | P per FET (I²R) | 2 FETs | + Copper (80²·0.3 mΩ) | **P_cond per channel** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 0.90 mΩ | 5.76 W | 11.5 W | 1.92 W | **13.4 W** |
| 100 °C | 1.24 mΩ | 7.92 W | 15.8 W | 1.92 W | **17.8 W** |
| 125 °C | 1.35 mΩ | 8.64 W | 17.3 W | 1.92 W | **19.2 W** |

**Thermal runaway check.** Positive α means losses rise with T_j. Stability requires:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\,R_{th} < 1 \;\Rightarrow\; 2 I^2 R_{ds25}\,\alpha\, R_{th,ja} < 1$$

At 80A: 2·6400·0.9 mΩ·0.005 = 0.0576 W/K. The loop therefore stays stable only if R_th,ja < 17 K/W per channel pair. That is easily met transiently but marginal in steady state on a small board.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switched half-bridge switches. That is one hard-switched FET plus one synchronous FET per channel.

### 2.1 Equations

**V–I overlap (hard-switched HS FET):**
$$P_{ov} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

**Reverse recovery (LS body diode recovers when HS turns on):**
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw}$$

**Output capacitance (C_oss energy dumped at turn-on):**
$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (2 edges per period):**
$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_{dead})\, f_{sw}$$

**Gate drive.** This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET.
$$P_{g} = Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw}\quad\text{(per switching FET)}$$

### 2.2 Numbers (80A, 25.2V, 48 kHz)

| Term | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| P_ov | 0.5·25.2·80·40e-9·48e3 | **1.94 W** |
| P_rr | 50e-9·25.2·48e3 | 0.06 W |
| P_oss | 0.5·1.5e-9·25.2²·48e3 | 0.02 W |
| P_dt | 0.8·80·200e-9·48e3 | **0.61 W** |
| P_g (2 FETs) | 2·60e-9·10·48e3 | 0.06 W (driver) |
| **P_sw per channel** | | **≈ 2.7 W** |

**Frequency sensitivity.** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it is about 1.35 W; at 96 kHz it is about 5.4 W. At 80A, conduction loss dominates by roughly 7:1. Dropping f_sw therefore gives little thermal benefit at burst current and costs current ripple. Keep 48 kHz.

### 2.3 Total power-stage loss

| Condition | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| 80A, T_j = 100 °C | 17.8 + 2.7 = **20.5 W** | **82 W** |
| 80A, T_j = 125 °C | 19.2 + 2.7 = **21.9 W** | **88 W** |
| 40A continuous, 80 °C (for reference) | ≈ 4.0 + 1.4 = **5.4 W** | **22 W** |

**Efficiency at burst:** P_out ≈ 25.2 V × 80 A × 4 ≈ 8 kW (electrical, ignoring D). Total loss is 88 W, so η ≈ 98.9%. The efficiency is good; the problem is power density.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network topology (per FET)

```
Tj ──[Rth_jc 0.8 K/W]── Tc ──[Rth_solder+via 2.0 K/W]── T_pcb ──┬──[Rth_pcb-air]── T_amb
                                                                 │
                                                           C_board (lumped)
```

With heatsink pads and prop-wash airflow, model the board as a single thermal mass with a convective sink.

### 3.2 Transient (burst) model, lumped board

$$C_{th}\frac{dT}{dt} = P - \frac{T - T_{amb}}{R_{th,ba}}
\;\Rightarrow\;
T(t) = T_{amb} + P R_{th,ba}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right),\quad \tau = R_{th,ba} C_{th}$$

**Nano board estimate (30×30 mm ESC, 6-layer 2 oz, with FETs and Al heat spreader):**

- m ≈ 12 g, effective c_p ≈ 0.85 J/g·K → **C_th ≈ 10 J/K**
- R_th,ba with prop airflow ≈ **3 K/W** → τ ≈ 30 s

**Case: all four channels at 80A, P = 85 W, T_amb = 35 °C.**

- Steady-state asymptote: 35 + 85·3 = **290 °C**. This is not survivable, which confirms that 80A can only be a burst rating.
