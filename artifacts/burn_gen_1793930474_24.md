# STM32H743 Nano FC with Integrated 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

## 0. Assumptions and Architectural Scope

Several points need stating before the derivations, because they change the numbers.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each ESC channel (or all four) runs its own MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to the ESC MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. This document models both layers.
2. **Component values are representative, not a qualified BOM.** I use a generic 60 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN MOSFET (Rds(on) 2.5 mΩ at 25 °C, 10 V Vgs). Before release, substitute your actual datasheet parameters into the equations. All formulas are written so the parameters can be swapped directly.
3. **Operating point:** 6S (25.2 V full charge), 80 A burst **per channel**, 48 kHz center-aligned PWM, synchronous (complementary) rectification, one FET per switch position.
4. **Board:** 30.5×30.5 mm footprint (larger nano class), 8-layer stack with 2 oz outer layers.

**Headline result:** a nano-class 4-in-1 cannot sustain 80 A per channel thermally. It can deliver 80 A as a short burst of roughly 1–3 s on all four channels simultaneously. Continuous capability is about 18–25 A per channel. The math in Section 3 shows why, and you should rate the product accordingly.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step BLDC with synchronous rectification

At any instant in a commutation sector, one phase is driven high and one is driven low:
- **On-time (D):** HS FET of phase A and LS FET of phase B conduct.
- **Off-time (1−D):** the LS FET of phase A (synchronous freewheel) and the LS FET of phase B conduct.

In both intervals the motor current flows through exactly **two** channels. Therefore:

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds(on)}(T_j) \quad \text{(independent of D)}$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds(on),25}\,\big[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\big], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\,/\text{K}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 2.5 mΩ × 1.6 = **4.0 mΩ**.

Add PCB copper and solder parasitics, about 0.5 mΩ per path (2 oz copper, short pours): R_path ≈ 8.5 mΩ.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| Condition | R_path | P_cond per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cold (25 °C) | 5.5 mΩ | 35.2 W | 141 W |
| Hot (125 °C) | 8.5 mΩ | **54.4 W** | **218 W** |

There is positive feedback: losses raise T_j, and higher T_j raises R. Solve iteratively with the thermal model in Section 3. Thermal runaway is bounded only because the burst duration is finite.

### 1.4 Paralleling option

Paralleling two FETs per position halves the conduction loss (about 27 W per channel hot). However, it needs 24 FETs on a 30.5 mm board, which is generally not routable at nano scale while preserving gate-loop symmetry. The model accepts N_par:

$$P_{cond} = \frac{2 I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)}{N_{par}} + I^2 R_{pcb}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

The hard-switched transition occurs in the PWM-ed half-bridge. Per channel:

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard switching)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

With t_r + t_f = 35 ns (gate driver at about 1.5 A peak, R_g ≈ 4.7 Ω):

$$P_{VI} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 35\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.69\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 600\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.84\ W}$$

This term is larger than the switching loss itself. Tighten dead time in AM32 (the `dead_time` setting) to the minimum that is safe for your driver/FET propagation skew. Going from 300 ns to 150 ns per edge saves about 0.9 W per channel.

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{ nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.048\text{ W}$$

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2}Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw} \approx 0.5\times50\text{ nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.030\text{ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g, not the FET)

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2\times40\text{ nC}\times10\times48\text{k} = 0.038\text{ W}$$

### 2.6 Per-channel total at 80 A, 125 °C

| Term | Loss |
|---|---|
| Conduction | 54.4 W |
| V–I switching | 1.69 W |
| Dead-time diode | 1.84 W |
| Q_rr + Q_oss + gate | 0.12 W |
| **Total per channel** | **≈ 58.0 W** |
| **Total, 4 channels** | **≈ 232 W** |

**Interpretation:** at 80 A, more than 93 % of the loss is conduction. Raising f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz costs about 1.8 W per channel. This is negligible against conduction losses at burst, but at hover (about 10 A) switching plus dead time is a much larger fraction. That is where 48 kHz buys smoother low-throttle behavior and lower motor ripple current.

Note also that at 80 A the throttle is usually near 100 %. Near D → 1, PWM transitions vanish and switching loss falls toward zero, so the 1.7 + 1.8 W figure is a conservative upper bound.

### 2.7 Input capacitor ripple (often the hidden failure)

$$I_{C,rms} = I\sqrt{D(1-D)} \;\Rightarrow\; 40\text{ A}_{rms} \text{ worst case per channel at } D=0.5$$

With 4 channels interleaved poorly (independent ESC MCUs are not phase-locked), plan for 60–80 A_rms aggregate ripple at mid-throttle bursts:
- **Local bypass:** at least 4× 10 µF/50 V X7R 1206 per channel, plus
- **Bulk:** 1000 µF/35 V low-ESR external electrolytic on the pigtail. This is mandatory, and the product manual must say so.
