# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: 80A Burst Thermal, 48 kHz Switching, and Bi-directional DShot600 Design Package (Job #67)

---

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Key Findings

**Architecture.** In a "4-in-1 ESC nano flight controller" the STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. The architecture is:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──DShot600 bidir──► 4× ESC MCU (AM32 / BLHeli_32 class)
                                           └─► 3-phase gate driver ─► 6× N-FET per motor
```

The 48 kHz PWM frequency is an ESC-firmware parameter. The H743 owns DShot generation, eRPM capture, and the harmonic notch.

**Key findings:**

1. **80A per channel continuous is not feasible on a nano board.** It yields about 134 W of total dissipation. That is about 2–3 s from the start temperature to Tj ≈ 120 °C, without prop wash.
2. **80A total (20A per motor) is trivial,** at about 8.5 W total.
3. **The spec must state which current is meant.** The model below covers both interpretations.
4. **The DMA plan fits in the H743's 16 DMA1/DMA2 streams,** but only if some UARTs run without DMA.

## 2. Loss Model

### 2.1 Conduction model (trapezoidal 6-step, synchronous)

During any 60° sector, one leg is PWM'd (high side at duty D, complementary low side at 1−D) and one low side is fully on. The current path therefore always contains exactly two FETs in series:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j) + I^2 \cdot 2R_{Cu}$$

Hot resistance at T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{DS}(125) = 1.6\,\text{m}\Omega\,[1 + 0.0045 \cdot 100] = 2.32\,\text{m}\Omega$$

Per motor at 80 A:

$$P_{cond} = 6400 \cdot (2 \cdot 2.32 + 2 \cdot 0.3)\,\text{m}\Omega = 6400 \cdot 5.24\,\text{m}\Omega = 33.5\,\text{W}$$

- FET share: 29.7 W.
- Copper share: 3.8 W.

**Per-FET averaging.** Each FET carries current for 120° of each electrical cycle. Within that window, the high-side/low-side pair splits it as D / (1−D). The averaged FET loss is therefore about 29.7 / 6 ≈ 5 W per FET.

**Instantaneous peak.** The always-on low side carries the full 6400 × 2.32 mΩ = 14.8 W. The die thermal mass (τ_die ≈ 1–5 ms) filters this only once the electrical frequency exceeds about 300 Hz, which is true at any flying RPM.

### 2.2 Switching loss at 48 kHz

The switching node is hard-switched on the PWM leg only.

**Linear V–I overlap:**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 20\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.97\,\text{W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (2 edges per period):**

$$P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 400\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = 1.23\,\text{W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = V_{bus} Q_{rr} f_{sw} = 25.2 \cdot 50\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.06\,\text{W}$$

**Output capacitance** (Q_oss ≈ 30 nC):

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \approx 0.02\,\text{W}$$

**Gate drive** (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g, not the FET):

$$P_g = 2 \cdot Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 40\text{n} \cdot 10 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.04\,\text{W}$$

**Switching subtotal ≈ 2.3 W per motor.** Dead time is the largest term at 48 kHz. Reducing t_dt to 100 ns saves 0.6 W per motor.
