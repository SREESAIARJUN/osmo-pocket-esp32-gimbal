## 0. Assumptions and Key Finding

No component datasheet was supplied, so the analysis rests on the stated parameters below. **Every number scales with these values, so substitute your actual BOM before using any result.**

| Parameter | Assumed value | Rationale |
|---|---|---|

| MOSFET | 40 V, 3.3×3.3 DFN, R_ds(on) = 1.5 mΩ @ 25 °C, Q_g = 40 nC, Q_rr = 30 nC, V_SD = 0.8 V | Typical for nano 4-in-1 boards |
| R_ds(on) temperature factor | ×1.5 at T_j = 125 °C, giving 2.25 mΩ | Typical silicon behaviour |
| Gate driver | 3-phase half-bridge driver (FD6288Q class), 10 V gate drive | Industry standard |

| Dead time | 200 ns per edge | Typical AM32/BLHeli_32 setting |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM) | AM32 default |
| Board | 20×20 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz copper, FC and ESC on separate stacked boards | "Nano" form factor |
| Burst profile | 80 A **phase current per motor, all four motors simultaneously** | Worst case |

> **Architectural correction:** The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. Each motor channel runs on its own ESC MCU, such as an AT32F421 running AM32. The H743 runs ArduPilot and generates bidirectional DShot600. The design below reflects that split.

> **Key finding:** At 80 A on all four channels, the power stage dissipates about **130 W** on a board with about **2 J/K** of thermal mass. "80 A burst" on a 20×20 board is sustainable for roughly **one second**, not the 10 s commonly implied. Section 3 derives this.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

In 6-step operation with synchronous rectification, current flows at any instant through one high-side FET of the energized phase and one low-side FET of the return phase. During PWM off-time, the complementary low-side FET takes over from the high-side FET. In both cases, two channels carry the full phase current, so conduction loss is independent of duty cycle to first order:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At I_ph = 80 A and T_j = 125 °C:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2 (80)^2 (2.25\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{28.8\ W}$$

Additional resistive paths, assuming about 0.3 mΩ for traces and vias plus 0.2 mΩ for solder and the motor pad:

$$P_{Cu} = 2\,I^2 R_{Cu} \approx 2(6400)(0.25\times10^{-3}) \approx \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

> **Electro-thermal feedback:** Because R_ds(on) rises with T_j, conduction loss is self-reinforcing. A stability check is that $\partial P/\partial T \cdot R_{th} < 1$. Here 0.005 × 28.8 × R_th stays below 1 only if R_th < 7 K/W. That condition holds per FET but is marginal for the whole board in still air.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only one FET per ESC hard-switches at any time: the high side in high-side PWM, with the low side commutating softly through the body diode during dead time.

**(a) V–I overlap (hard switching)**
$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(60\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = \mathbf{2.90\ W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery of the complementary body diode**
$$P_{rr} = V_{bus} Q_{rr} f_{sw} = 25.2 (30\,\text{nC})(48\,\text{kHz}) = \mathbf{0.036\ W}$$
(At 80 A, Q_rr increases with di/dt. Budget 3× this value, giving about 0.11 W.)

**(c) Dead-time body-diode conduction**
$$P_{dt} = 2\, V_{SD} I_{ph} t_{dt} f_{sw} = 2(0.8)(80)(200\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

**(d) Gate drive (dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET)**
$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2(40\,\text{nC})(10)(48\,\text{kHz}) = \mathbf{0.038\ W}$$

**(e) Output capacitance**
$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss,eff} V^2 f \approx 0.5(1.5\,\text{nF})(25.2^2)(48\,\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.023\ W}$$

### Loss summary at 80 A, 25.2 V, 48 kHz

| Component | Per ESC (W) | Whole 4-in-1 (W) |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (hot) | 28.80 | 115.2 |
| Copper and solder | 3.20 | 12.8 |
| Switching overlap | 2.90 | 11.6 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 | 4.9 |
| Q_rr (×3 margin) | 0.11 | 0.4 |
| Gate and C_oss | 0.06 | 0.2 |
| **Total** | **≈36.3** | **≈145** |

Conduction loss accounts for about 80% of the total. Reducing f_sw saves little; R_ds(on) and the copper path dominate.

**Bus-side current check:**
$$I_{batt} \approx \frac{\sum P_{out} + P_{loss}}{V_{bus}}$$
At about 80% duty with 4 × 80 A phase current, I_batt reaches roughly 250 A or more. That exceeds what an XT60 and 14 AWG leads can carry. **The pack and harness, not just the ESC, are limiting factors.**

**Bulk capacitor ripple (per ESC):**
$$I_{C,rms} = I_{ph}\sqrt{D(1-D)} = 80\sqrt{0.25} = 40\ \text{A}_{rms}\ \text{at } D = 0.5$$
This requires distributed low-ESR MLCCs (for example, 6 × 10 µF/50 V X7R per channel) plus one board-level 35 V/1000 µF low-ESR electrolytic on the pigtail.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network (Foster ladder, lumped)

```
P_FET ──[Rjc 1.2K/W]──┬──[Rcb 0.8K/W]──┬── board node (C_b) ──[Rba]── T_amb
                      C_j (~0.02 J/K)  C_case (~0.05 J/K)
```

### 3.2 Per-FET steady-state rise above the board

Averaged over a commutation cycle, each of the 6 FETs carries about 1/6 of the per-ESC FET loss:

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{28.8 + 2.9 + 1.23 + 0.11}{6} \approx 5.5\ \text{W}$$

During its active 120° interval, the instantaneous loss in the conducting high-side FET is $I^2R + P_{sw} \approx 14.4 + 2.9 = 17.3$ W.

$$\Delta T_{j-b} \approx P_{FET,avg}(R_{jc} + R_{cb}) = 5.5 \times 2.0 = 11\ \text{K}$$

Peak ripple within a commutation is negligible because the period (about 0.1–1 ms) is far shorter than τ_jc (about 20 ms).

| 24 FETs, caps, solder | 0.5 g | ~0.5 | 0.25 J/K |

Board-to-ambient resistance, with prop wash over a frame-mounted stack: R_ba ≈ 3–5 K/W. Use 4 K/W.
