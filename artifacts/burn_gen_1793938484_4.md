## 0. Architectural Assumptions and an Early Feasibility Flag

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. In every production 4-in-1 design, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot. The architecture is therefore:

```
            ┌───────────────── STM32H743 (ArduPilot, 400 MHz) ─────────────────┐
            │ TIM3_CH1..CH4 ── bidir DShot600 ── 4× ESC MCU (AT32F421, AM32)   │
            └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
ESC MCU ─► 3-phase gate driver (FD6288Q-class) ─► 6× N-MOSFET ─► motor phases A/B/C
```

**⚠ Feasibility flag: 80 A per channel versus the "nano" form factor**

- **20×20 mm:** 24 MOSFETs in PDFN5×6 cannot physically fit. Even PDFN3.3×3 parts at 80 A will exceed safe Tj within about 1 s (see §3).
- **30.5×30.5 mm:** this is the minimum realistic footprint for 80 A burst.
- **Recommendation:** rate the design at 80 A burst for ≤ 2 s, single channel, and 50–55 A burst for 4-channel simultaneous loading. The derivation below makes this quantitative.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation, the current at any instant flows through exactly **two** phase legs in series:

- the driven phase, through either its high-side switch (PWM on) or its low-side switch (synchronous freewheel), and
- the low-side switch of the return phase.

Because the PWM is synchronous, the driven leg always has one FET conducting. The series resistance is therefore always 2·R_ds, independent of duty cycle D:

$$
P_{cond} = 2\, I^2\, R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]
$$

### 1.2 Copper and shunt losses

| Element | Model | Value at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Copper path (bus → FETs → motor pad, 2 oz × 4 layers paralleled) | R_cu ≈ 0.4 mΩ ⚠ | P_cu = 80² × 0.4 mΩ ≈ 2.6 W |
| Current shunt | R_sh = 0.25 mΩ | P_sh = 80² × 0.25 mΩ = 1.6 W |

The current shunt can be omitted if per-channel current is estimated from the bus.

### 1.3 Evaluation at 80 A

| T_j (°C) | R_ds (mΩ) | P_cond (W) |
|---|---|---|
| 25 | 1.00 | 12.8 |
| 100 | 1.49 | 19.0 |
| 125 | 1.65 | 21.1 |
| 150 | 1.81 | 23.2 |

### 1.4 Electro-thermal stability (runaway criterion)

The junction temperature depends on loss, and loss depends on junction temperature:

$$
T_j = T_a + R_\theta\, P(T_j)
$$

Differentiating, the operating point is stable only if the loop gain is below unity:

$$
R_\theta \frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j} = R_\theta \cdot 2 I^2 R_{ds,25}\,\alpha < 1
$$

At 80 A: 2 · 6400 · 1e-3 · 0.0065 = 0.083 W/K.

**Result:** R_θ (channel to ambient) < 12 K/W.

- A bare nano board with modest airflow sits at roughly 15–25 K/W per channel. It therefore has **no stable continuous operating point at 80 A**.
- 80 A is strictly a transient rating. This is the mathematical reason the burst-time limit in §3 must be enforced.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In 6-step operation only **one** FET per channel is hard-switching at any time: the PWM high side. Its complementary low side commutates through the body diode during dead time.

### 2.1 Loss terms

| Term | Formula | Value (25.2 V, 80 A, 48 kHz) |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw | ½ · 25.2 · 80 · 40 ns · 48k = **1.94 W** |
| Dead-time body diode (2 edges, t_dt = 100 ns) | V_SD · I · 2 · t_dt · f_sw | 0.8 · 80 · 200 ns · 48k = **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw | 50 nC · 25.2 · 48k = **0.06 W** |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss · V · f_sw (≈ E_oss·f, upper bound) | 40 nC · 25.2 · 48k = **0.05 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs/leg, mostly dissipated in driver and R_g) | Q_g · V_GS · f_sw
