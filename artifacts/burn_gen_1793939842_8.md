## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step synchronous PWM

During any 60° commutation sector, one phase is PWM-switched and one low-side FET is held on (static).

- **PWM-on (duty D):** current flows through HS_A, the motor, then LS_B. That is 2 FETs.
- **PWM-off (1−D):** current freewheels through LS_A (synchronous rectifier) and LS_B. That is also 2 FETs.

So with synchronous rectification, **two R_DS(on) are always in series with the phase current**, independent of duty:

$$
P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I_{ph,rms}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j) \;+\; I_{ph,rms}^2 R_{Cu}
$$

With 120° trapezoidal current, the sector RMS is approximately the flat-top value, so I_ph,rms ≈ I_ph.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

This gives about 1.47× at 110 °C, consistent with typical normalized curves.

| T_j | R_DS(on) |
|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.40 mΩ |
| 80 °C | 1.82 mΩ |
| 110 °C | 2.05 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 2.17 mΩ |

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 110 °C

Assume a copper path of 4 cm of 2 oz trace, 3 mm wide, roughly paralleled on 2 layers, giving R_Cu ≈ 0.25 mΩ.

$$
P_{cond} = 2(80^2)(2.05\times10^{-3}) + 80^2(0.25\times10^{-3}) = 26.2 + 1.6 = \mathbf{27.8\ W\ per\ ESC}
$$

Per-FET split during the sector:

| FET | Conducts | Loss over the sector |
|---|---|---|
| HS_A | D | 13.1·D W |
| LS_A | 1−D | 13.1·(1−D) W |
| LS_B | 100 % | 13.1 W (worst-case hot spot) |

The static low-side FET is the hottest device. Rotation through the 6 sectors spreads this load: each FET is static for 1/3 of the electrical cycle.

**Averaged per FET:**

$$
\bar P_{FET} \approx \frac{27.8 - 1.6}{6}\cdot 2 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} \approx 4.4\ \text{W}
$$

### 1.4 Thermal-runaway stability criterion

The loop R → P → T is stable only if:

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\,R_{\theta JA} = 2I^2R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_{\theta JA} < 1
$$

At 80 A: 2·6400·1.4e-3·0.0055 = 0.0986 W/K. This requires an effective **R_θJA < 10 K/W** for the per-ESC loss path. On a nano board the effective value exceeds that (see §3), so steady state at 80 A has **no stable operating point**. That is a second, independent proof that 80 A is burst-only.
