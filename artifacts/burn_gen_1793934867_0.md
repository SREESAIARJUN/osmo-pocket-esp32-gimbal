# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model (Job #50)

This document models loss, thermal behavior, and timer/DMA allocation for an STM32H743 flight controller with an integrated 4-in-1 ESC rated for 80 A burst per channel. The thermal result determines what the 80 A rating actually means, so read §0 and §4 before the netlists.

---

## 0. Architecture Decisions and Constraints

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** Twenty-four FETs switching at 48 kHz with BEMF zero-cross detection on 4 motors is not practical on the FC MCU. The industry-standard and ArduPilot-compatible split is:
- The **STM32H743** runs ArduPilot and outputs bidirectional DShot600.
- **4× AT32F421K8U7** run AM32 firmware. Each does six-step commutation at 48 kHz and returns eRPM telemetry.

**2. "Nano" and 80 A are in tension.**
- On a 20×20 mm board, 80 A per channel is physically unrealistic.
- This design uses a **30.5×30.5 mm board** (still "compact AIO" class).
- §4 shows that even at that size, 80 A is a **~2 s burst** rating with all four motors loaded. It is not continuous.

**3. Component parameters are representative.** The values below are typical for a 40 V PDFN5×6 ESC MOSFET class and must be replaced with the selected part's datasheet values before release:

| Symbol | Meaning | Value used |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 6S full charge | 25.2 V |
| I_burst | Phase current per ESC | 80 A |
| R_ds(on),25 | Per FET, V_gs = 10 V | 1.0 mΩ |
| k_T | R_ds(on) multiplier at T_j = 125 °C | 1.5 |
| t_r, t_f | Switching edges (with gate resistor) | 20 ns each |
| Q_rr | Body-diode reverse recovery | 50 nC |
| C_oss | Output capacitance at V_bus | 1.5 nF |
| Q_g | Total gate charge | 60 nC |
| V_f | Body diode forward voltage | 0.8 V |
| t_dead | Dead time per edge | 50 ns |
| f_sw | PWM frequency | 48 kHz |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths in six-step synchronous commutation

In any 60° sector, two phases are active:
- The **PWM leg** conducts through the high-side FET for duty D and through the complementary low-side FET for (1−D).
- The **return leg's** low-side FET conducts for 100% of the sector.

The duty terms cancel, so at any instant exactly two R_ds(on) are in series with the phase current:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = I^2 R_{ds,hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds,hot} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds,hot}$$

$$R_{ds,hot} = k_T R_{ds,25} = 1.5 \times 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega = 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

### 1.2 Per-FET distribution

Each FET conducts during roughly 1/3 of the electrical cycle:
- **Instantaneous** loss per conducting FET: I²R = **9.6 W**. This sets the junction rise.
- **Cycle-averaged** loss per FET: 19.2 / 6 ≈ **3.2 W**. This sets the board heating.

### 1.3 PCB copper and shunt loss

The path is bus → FET → phase pad, plus the shunt. Assumptions:
- Copper path: 2 oz outer + 2 oz inner paralleled, ~0.25 mΩ.
- Shunt: 0.2 mΩ.

$$P_{Cu+shunt} = 80^2 \times (0.25 + 0.2)\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{2.9\ W}$$

> If you substitute a 0.5 mΩ shunt, this rises to 4.8 W. Keep shunts at ≤0.2 mΩ, or sense battery current only.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

All switching events occur in the PWM leg: one hard turn-on and one turn-off per PWM period.

| Mechanism | Equation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f = ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 k | **1.94 W** |
| Reverse recovery | V·Q_rr·f = 25.2 × 50 nC × 48 k | 0.06 W |
| C_oss discharge | ½·C_oss·V²·f = ½ × 1.5 nF × 635 × 48 k | 0.02 W |
| Dead-time diode | V_f·I·2·t_dead·f = 0.8 × 80 × 100 ns × 48 k | 0.31 W |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f = 2 × 60 nC × 10 V × 48 k | 0.06 W* |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.39 W** |

*Gate-drive loss is dissipated in the gate driver and gate resistors, not in the FETs.

**Sensitivity of the overlap term:**
- It scales linearly with f_sw. Dropping to 24 kHz saves ~1.2 W per ESC.
- 48 kHz is retained here for acoustic and current-ripple reasons on low-inductance motors.

## 3. Total Burst Power Budget

| Item | Per ESC | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching | 2.4 W | 9.6 W |
| Copper + shunt | 2.9 W | 11.6 W |
| **ESC subtotal** | **24.5 W** | **98 W** |
| H743 at 480 MHz, IMU, OSD, flash | | ~0.8 W |
| 5 V/10 V/3.3 V regulators (η ≈ 88%, 2.5 A out) | | ~1.7 W |
| **Board total at full 4× burst** | | **≈ 100 W** |

Efficiency check:
- Electrical input ≈ 4 × 80 A × 25.2 V × D_avg (~0.85) ≈ 6.9 kW.
- Losses of ~100 W give ≈ 98.5% efficiency.
- This is plausible for synchronous FET ESCs. The problem is power density, not efficiency.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Two-node RC network

```
P_FET(inst) ──► [Rθ_j-b ≈ 3 K/W] ──► Board node (C_b) ──► [Rθ_b-a = 1/G] ──► Ambient
                 junction→board          lumped thermal mass     prop-wash convection
```

### 4.2 Board heat capacity

| Element | Mass | c_p | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| FR4, 30.5² mm × 1.6 mm | ~2.8 g | 1.1 J/g·K | 3.1 J/K |
| Copper, 8 layers mixed 1–2 oz | ~4.5 g | 0.385 J/g·K | 1.7 J/K |
| 24 FETs + capacitors + connectors | ~3 g | ~0.7 J/g·K | 2.1 J/K |
| **Total C_b** | | | **≈ 6.9 J/K** |

### 4.3 Convection

- Prop-wash heat transfer coefficient: h ≈ 100 W/m²K.
- Area: A ≈ 2 × 0.0305² ≈ 1.
