## 0. Baseline Assumptions & Architectural Clarification

**The two halves of the system are independent.** The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz motor PWM. In a 4-in-1 stack:

- **The FC (STM32H743)** sends DShot600 frames to four ESC MCUs and receives eRPM telemetry back on the same wire (bidirectional DShot).
- **Each ESC MCU** (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32) generates the 48 kHz six-step PWM and commutation.

The thermal and switching models (§1–3) therefore belong to the ESC power stage. The DMA model (§4) belongs to the FC.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in six-step with synchronous rectification

In each 60° commutation sector, one phase is PWM'd on the high side (HS), and one phase is held on the low side (LS). With complementary PWM:

| Interval | Conducting devices | Fraction of sector |
|---|---|---|
| PWM ON | HS_A + LS_B | D |
| PWM OFF | LS_A (sync rect) + LS_B | 1 − D |

The current path contains exactly two FETs at all times, independent of duty cycle D. This gives the total bridge conduction loss:

$$
P_{cond,bridge} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

with temperature-dependent resistance:

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

### 1.2 Per-device split

Each FET is active for 1/3 of the electrical period. For duty D, the per-device averages are:

$$
P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}D\,I^2R_{DS}, \qquad
P_{LS,sync} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)I^2R_{DS}, \qquad
P_{LS,static} = \tfrac{1}{3}I^2R_{DS}
$$

Since each LS FET serves both roles in different sectors:

$$
P_{LS,avg} = \tfrac{1}{3}(2-D)\,I^2R_{DS}
$$

The worst case is the low-side devices at low duty and high current. Typical examples are a punch-out from low throttle, or a stalled or blocked prop.

**Averaging validity.** The die thermal time constant is τ_die ≈ 1–5 ms. Electrical frequency is f_e = eRPM/60. Averaging over the electrical period is valid for f_e > ~1 kHz, i.e., eRPM > 60k. Below that, for example at startup or stall, use instantaneous sector power, since one LS FET carries I²R continuously for a full sector.
