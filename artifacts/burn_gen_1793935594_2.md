# STM32H743 Flight Controller + 4-in-1 80A ESC: Nano-Class Design Reference

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Scope

**Important clarification on partitioning.** The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. ArduPilot runs on the H743, which sends bidirectional DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs. These run AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware and generate the 48 kHz PWM. That split drives everything below.

```
 6S LiPo ──► Power stage (4× 3-phase bridges, 24 MOSFETs)
                 ▲ gate drive
           4× ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32)  ◄── 48 kHz complementary PWM
                 ▲ bidirectional DShot600 (1 wire each)
           STM32H743 (ArduPilot, 480 MHz) ── IMU/Baro/OSD/UARTs
```

**Baseline parameters.** These are used throughout. Swap in your chosen MOSFET's datasheet values.

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 25.2 V | 6S fully charged (worst case for switching) |
| I_burst | 80 A | per-motor phase current, burst |
| f_sw | 48 kHz | PWM frequency |
| R_ds,25 | 1.0 mΩ | MOSFET R_DS(on) at 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V (40 V class, 5×6 or 3.3×3.3 package) |
| α | 0.006 /K | R_DS(on) temperature coefficient (≈1.6× at 125 °C) |
| t_r, t_f | 20 ns, 20 ns | effective V-I overlap times with your gate resistor |
| Q_rr | 50 nC | body-diode reverse recovery |
| C_oss,er | 1.5 nF | energy-related output capacitance |
| Q_g | 60 nC | total gate charge |
| t_dt | 100 ns | dead time per edge |
| V_SD | 0.8 V | body-diode forward voltage at high current |
| R_shunt | 0.2 mΩ | per-ESC current shunt (if fitted) |

> ⚠️ **Feasibility flag (up front):** The numbers below show that 80 A × 4 on a 20×20 or 30×30 "nano" board is only achievable as a **sub-second to ~1 s burst**. It is not a continuous rating. Treat 80 A as a firmware-enforced transient limit.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification

In any 60° commutation sector, one phase is PWM'd high and one phase is held low:
- **PWM on (duty D):** current flows through HS(phase A), then the motor, then LS(phase B).
- **PWM off (1−D):** current freewheels through LS(phase A, synchronously rectified), then the motor, then LS(phase B).

At every instant, exactly **two MOSFET channels** carry the full phase current I, independent of D. The phase current is essentially DC within a PWM period because L/R is much greater than 1/f_sw.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

### 1.3 Conduction loss per ESC channel

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j) \;-\; \underbrace{2\,I^2 R_{ds}\,\frac{2t_{dt}f_{sw}}{1}}_{\text{dead-time interval, negligible}}$$

**At 80 A:**

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per ESC | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W | 51.2 W |
| 100 °C | 1.45 mΩ | 18.6 W | 74.2 W |
| 125 °C | 1.60 mΩ | 20.5 W | 81.9 W |

### 1.4 Parasitic conduction (often forgotten, significant at 80 A)

| Element | Model | At 80 A per ESC |
|---|---|---|
| Shunt | I²·R_shunt = 6400 × 0.2 mΩ | 1.28 W |
| PCB copper, 2 oz (0.25 mΩ/□ at 25 °C) | I²·0.25 mΩ·N_□; assume 3 □ effective | 4.8 W |
| Solder pads + motor wire stub | ~0.3 mΩ | 1.9 W |

> Copper loss can rival the MOSFETs. Use 3 oz outer layers, paralleled inner planes, and stitch vias at ≥ 4 vias/A on high-current nodes.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the **PWM'd high-side** hard-switches. The synchronous low-side turns on after dead time with its body diode already conducting, so it soft-switches (ZVS). Per ESC:

### 2.1 V-I overlap loss (high-side, hard-switched)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance loss (HS + LS C_oss charged each cycle)

$$P_{oss} = 2 \cdot \tfrac12 C_{oss,er} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1.5\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.046\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery (LS body diode recovered by HS turn-on)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$

The real Q_rr scales with di/dt and I_F, so derate ×2 for margin, giving ≈ 0.12 W.

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction (2 edges per cycle)

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

Tuning dead time down to 50 ns halves this. Verify shoot-through margin on a scope across the driver's propagation spread.

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated mostly in driver and R_g, not the FETs)

$$P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.058\ W}$$

### 2.6 Switching summary per ESC at 80 A

| Term | Power |
|---|---|
| V-I overlap | 1.94 W |
| Coss | 0.05 W |
| Q_rr (derated) | 0.12 W |
| Dead time | 0.61 W |
| Gate | 0.06 W |
| **Total P_sw** | **≈ 2.8 W** |

**Frequency sensitivity:** P_sw ∝ f_sw. Going from 24 kHz to 48 kHz adds about 1.4 W per ESC at 80 A. In exchange it lowers current ripple and audible noise, and improves low-throttle smoothness.
