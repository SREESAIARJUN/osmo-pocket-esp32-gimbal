## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Flags

Three constraints shape everything below.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production AIO running ArduPilot, the STM32H743 is the flight controller. Each motor is driven by a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32 firmware, for example an AT32F421 or STM32G071. That MCU does sensorless 6-step commutation at 48 kHz PWM.
   - The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to the ESC MCUs and receives eRPM telemetry back.
   - ArduPilot has no native FET-commutation driver, so this split is mandatory.
2. **"80A burst" must be defined per motor and not simultaneous.** Four motors at 80 A on 6S is about 320 A and about 8 kW from the battery. The math in §3 shows a nano-class board, 20×20 or 30×30 mm, cannot sink that for more than about 2–3 s. The design target is therefore:
   - **80 A per motor, ≤10 s**, with at most one or two motors simultaneously at peak.
   - **4 × 45 A continuous-equivalent** under prop-wash airflow.
3. **Bus voltage assumption:** 6S, V_bus = 25.2 V maximum, 22.2 V nominal. Use 40 V FETs; 30 V parts leave inadequate ringing margin on 6S.

## 2. Loss Model

### 2.1 Commutation Topology Assumed (AM32 / BLHeli_32 Style)

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM. In each 60° commutation sector:

- **Leg A (PWM leg):** the high-side switches at duty D, and the low-side conducts the complementary (1−D).
- **Leg B (return leg):** the low-side is held fully on.
- **Leg C:** floats, and its back-EMF zero crossing is sensed.

Each leg spends one third of the electrical cycle in each role (PWM, return, float). With motor phase current I:

**Per-FET RMS conduction duty**

| Device | Fraction of electrical cycle conducting |
|---|---|
| High-side (HS) | D/3 |
| Low-side (LS) | (1−D)/3 + 1/3 = (2−D)/3 |
| Leg total | 2/3 |

**Check:** three legs × 2/3 = 2. This matches the fact that exactly two FETs carry I at any instant.

### 2.2 Conduction Losses (with Temperature Feedback)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$P_{HS} = \frac{D}{3}I^2R_{DS}(T_j), \qquad P_{LS} = \frac{2-D}{3}I^2R_{DS}(T_j)$$

**Numbers at I = 80 A, T_j = 125 °C:**

- R_DS = 1.2 × (1 + 0.0045 × 100) = **1.74 mΩ**
- P_cond,motor = 2 × 6400 × 1.74×10⁻³ = **22.3 W**
- At D = 1 (full throttle):
  - P_HS = P_LS = 6400 × 1.74×10⁻³ / 3 = **3.71 W per FET**
- At D = 0.5:
  - P_HS = 1.86 W
  - P_LS = 5.57 W

At mid-throttle burst, the low-side is the hot device. Lay out LS FETs with the best via field.

**Self-consistency.** Because R_DS rises with T_j, solve for the operating point by fixed-point iteration:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_a + P(T_j^{(k)})\cdot R_{\theta JA,eff}$$

This converges if dP/dT_j · R_θJA < 1. Here that condition is α·P_cond·R_θJA < 1, which sets a thermal-runaway bound of roughly R_θJA < 1/(0.0045 × 3.7) ≈ 60 K/W per FET. The design is comfortably stable.
