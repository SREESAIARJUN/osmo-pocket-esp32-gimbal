## 0. Scope Corrections and Baseline Assumptions

Three architectural issues need settling before any numbers are meaningful.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot has no 6-step or FOC motor-control path. The correct architecture is an STM32H743 flight controller sending DShot600 to **four ESC MCUs**. Suitable choices are AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or STM32G071 running BLHeli_32. Each ESC MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM, and each motor has its own gate driver and 6 MOSFETs. The H743's job is DShot generation and eRPM capture.
2. **"80A burst" is interpreted as 80 A RMS phase current per motor, all four simultaneously, for a short duration.** The thermal math (Section 3) shows that a 20×20 mm "nano" board **cannot** sustain this beyond about 1 to 1.5 s. A 30.5×30.5 mm board or a heatsinked stack is required for meaningful burst durations. I've kept the nano target but flagged where it fails.
3. **MOSFET parameters below are representative placeholders** for a 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN part suitable for 6S. Replace them with the selected part's datasheet values and re-run the model. All equations are kept symbolic first for exactly this reason.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Derivation (6-step trapezoidal, synchronous rectification)

In 6-step commutation, current flows through exactly two phases at any instant. During the PWM on-time the path is high-side FET (phase A) → motor → low-side FET (phase B). During the off-time, synchronous rectification recirculates current through the complementary low-side FET (phase A) and the same low-side FET (phase B).

So **two channel resistances are in series 100% of the time**, regardless of duty cycle:
