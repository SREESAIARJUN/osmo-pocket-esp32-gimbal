# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal & DShot Architecture

**Document:** Job #52, Rev A (engineering estimate, pre-layout)

## 0. Design Review Flags

Two issues should be resolved before the rest of the document is used for layout.

**Flag 1: The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every viable 4-in-1 design, each motor bridge is driven by a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical choices are the STM32G071 or AT32F421, either one MCU per motor or a shared multi-motor part. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to those ESC MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. The 48 kHz PWM and all commutation timing live in the ESC MCUs, not in the H743's timers. Section 3 is written on that basis.

**Flag 2: 80 A per motor on a "nano" footprint is thermally infeasible for more than about 1–2 s.** Section 2.4 shows the board heats adiabatically at roughly 17–19 K/s at full 4×80 A. I recommend one of these:
- (a) Rate it as 80 A burst ≤ 1 s and 50–55 A continuous with airflow.
- (b) Move to a 30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz inner-copper stack.
- (c) Clarify whether "80A" means total pack current. That case is easily achievable.

All component parameters below are **stated assumptions**, representative of 40 V, ~1 mΩ, 3.3×3.3 mm or 5×6 mm DFN MOSFETs. Replace them with datasheet values for the selected part before sign-off.

## 2. Mathematical Model

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

With synchronous rectification, the commutated current always flows through exactly two channel resistances: one high-side and one low-side, whichever device in the PWM'd leg is on. Duty cycle therefore cancels out of the conduction term.

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j) + I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{Cu}$$

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\,\text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$P_{FET,cond} = 80^2 \cdot 2 \cdot (1.2\,\text{m}\Omega \cdot 1.5) = 6400 \cdot 3.6\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{23.0\ W}$$

**Copper path.** Estimate R_Cu ≈ 1.0 mΩ for B+ and GND pours plus two phase pads/traces per motor:

$$P_{Cu} = 6400 \cdot 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{6.4\ W}$$

**Per-FET average.** Each FET carries current for 1/3 of the electrical period, and the load is shared over 6 FETs:

$$\bar P_{FET,cond} \approx 23.0/6 \approx \mathbf{3.8\ W}$$

The peak instantaneous dissipation in a single device is 11.5 W while it conducts.

**Thermal runaway check.** R_ds rises with T_j, so the operating point must satisfy:

$$\frac{\partial P_{cond}}{\partial T_j}\cdot R_{\theta,ja} < 1 \;\Rightarrow\; I^2\cdot 2R_{25}\alpha\cdot R_{\theta} = 6400\cdot 2.4\text{m}\cdot0.005\cdot R_\theta = 0.077\,R_\theta < 1$$

This gives **R_θ(ja, per motor) < 13 K/W**. That limit is violated by a nano board in still air, so airflow is mandatory.

### 2.2 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only one leg is PWM'd at any time, so there is one hard-switched pair per motor.

**(a) V·I overlap, hard-switched device:**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot30\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

The rise and fall times come from the gate charge: $t_{r,f} \approx \dfrac{Q_{gd}R_{G,tot}}{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}$. With 8 nC × 2.2 Ω / (10 − 3.5) V ≈ 2.7 ns intrinsic, plus loop inductance and driver limits, 15 ns is used as a realistic figure.

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two edges per period):

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD} I_{ph}\cdot 2t_{dt}\cdot f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**(c) Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 30\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}$$

**(d) Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot1.5\text{n}\cdot635\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.023\ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive** (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g, two devices switching):

$$P_g = 2\,Q_gV_{gs}f_{sw} = 2\cdot40\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.038\ W}$$

$$P_{sw,motor} \approx 1.45+0.61+0.04+0.02+0.04 = \mathbf{2.16\ W}$$

**Sensitivity:**
- $\partial P_{VI}/\partial f_{sw}$ = 30 µW/
