# Job #22: STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 80 A ESC

## Engineering Model and Reference Design

---

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility

**Partitioning.** The STM32H743 does not switch the MOSFETs. In every viable 4-in-1 design, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU that performs 6-step commutation at 48 kHz. Suitable ESC MCUs include the AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or BLHeli_32-class firmware. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to those four MCUs.

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──DShot600 bidir x4──► 4x ESC MCU ──► 4x 3-phase gate driver ──► 24 MOSFETs ──► motors
```

**Two feasibility findings you should accept before reading the numbers:**

1. **"80 A" is a burst rating, not a continuous one.** It is thermal-capacitance-limited to roughly 1–3 s. The model below shows a realistic continuous rating of about 22–30 A per channel on a 30.5 mm board in prop wash.
2. **A true 20×20 mm "nano" form factor cannot carry 80 A bursts on four channels.** It lacks the copper cross-section, FET footprint area and thermal mass. This design therefore assumes a 30.5×30.5 mm mounting pattern on a 36×36 mm board. If 20×20 is mandatory, derate to about 35–45 A burst.

**Design assumptions:**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus,max = 25.2 V |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation topology

In 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary PWM, two phases conduct at every instant:

- **PWM leg:** current alternates between the high-side FET (duty D) and the low-side FET (1 − D, synchronous rectification). One FET always carries the current.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET is on continuously for the 120° segment.

The instantaneous conduction power per channel is therefore duty-independent:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\left(\frac{T_j + 273}{298}\right)^{\alpha}, \quad \alpha \approx 2.0\text{–}2.3
$$

Equivalently, use the datasheet normalized curve, which gives about 1.5× at 125 °C.

Because loss rises with temperature, the result is a positive feedback loop. Solve self-consistently:

$$
T_j = T_{pcb} + R_{\theta,j\text{-}pcb}\,P_{FET}(T_j)
$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.7\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{34.6\ W\ per\ channel}
$$

**Per-FET averaging.** Electrical frequency is about 5.8 kHz for 7 pole pairs at 50 k RPM. That gives roughly 57 µs per 120° segment, far below the die thermal time constant (~ms). Each of the 6 FETs therefore sees the time-average:

$$
P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{34.6}{6} \approx \mathbf{5.8\ W}
$$

The instantaneous peak on a conducting FET is 80² × 2.7 mΩ = 17.3 W.

### 1.4 PCB copper conduction loss

**Battery DC current:**

$$
I_{bat} = \sum_{k=1}^{4} D_k\,I_{ph,k}
$$

Worst-case burst at D ≈ 0.85 gives about 270 A total. Realistically, the battery lead/XT60 limits this, and full-throttle is current-limited by the ESC.

**Trace resistance:**

$$
R_{trace} = \frac{\rho_{Cu}(T)\,L}{w\,t}, \quad \rho_{Cu} = 1.72\times10^{-8}\,[1 + 0.0039(T - 20)]\ \Omega\cdot\text{m}
$$

**Example: phase output path.** L = 8 mm, w = 4 mm, three paralleled 70 µm layers (t_eff = 210 µm), at 80 °C:

$$
R \approx \frac{2.12\times10^{-8} \times 0.008}{0.004 \times 210\times10^{-6}} = 0.20\ \text{m}\Omega \;\Rightarrow\; P = 80^2 \times 0.2\,\text{m} = 1.3\ \text{W per phase path}
$$

Allow about **2 W per channel** of copper loss, including the shunt (0.25 mΩ shunt carrying I_bat,ch).
