# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 80A 4-in-1 ESC (Job #32): Architecture, Loss Model, DShot Allocation and Netlists

## 0. Architectural Corrections

Three points in the brief would lead to a bad design if taken literally.

1. **The STM32H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC.** A 4-in-1 ESC needs one commutation MCU per motor, running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical choices are the STM32G071, AT32F421 or GD32E230. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to those four ESC MCUs over bidirectional DShot600.

2. **The 48 kHz PWM is set in the ESC firmware (AM32 PWM frequency), not in ArduPilot.** ArduPilot only controls the DShot command and telemetry link.

3. **"Nano" and "80A ×4 burst" conflict thermally.** A 20×20 mm board cannot reject about 110 W. The numbers below show that 80A is a roughly 2–4 s burst rating even on a 30.5×30.5 mm board. I specify a 30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer board with 2 oz copper, and treat 80A as a burst rating with about 45–55A continuous.

**Status of the numbers:**
- MOSFET and driver figures are representative assumptions for a 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN class part. Replace them with values from your chosen datasheet. All formulas are parametric.
- Pin assignments must be validated against the STM32H743 datasheet alternate-function tables and ArduPilot's `dma_resolver.py` before layout.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80A Burst)

### 2.1 Current relationships

In 6-step drive, two phases conduct at any instant. During the PWM on-time, one high-side and one low-side FET carry the phase current. During the off-time, the synchronous low-side FET freewheels the current.

Power balance, ignoring losses, with duty cycle D:

$$I_{ph} \approx \frac{I_{dc}}{D}$$

At burst the throttle is near full, so D → 0.95–1 and I_ph ≈ 80–84 A.

Each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical cycle. Its RMS current is:

- High-side (PWM-chopped): $I_{HS,rms} = I_{ph}\sqrt{D/3}$
- Low-side (commutating, synchronous): $I_{LS,rms} = I_{ph}\sqrt{(2-D)/3}$

This is approximate. It treats the passive low-side as always on, plus the synchronous freewheel during (1−D).

### 2.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C, R_DS = 1.95 mΩ.

### 2.3 Conduction loss

Per FET:

$$P_{cond,FET} = I_{rms}^2 \, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

At D ≈ 1 and I_ph = 80 A:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{80^2}{3}(1.95\,\text{m}\Omega) = \mathbf{4.16\ W}$$

Per ESC channel (6 FETs; two always conducting):

$$P_{cond,ESC} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS} = 6400 \times 3.9\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{24.96\ W}$$

Copper loss per channel:

$$P_{cu} = I_{dc}^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{1.92\ W}$$

### 2.4 Electro-thermal fixed point

R_DS depends on T_j, and T_j depends on loss, so the junction temperature must be solved iteratively:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}(T_j^{(k)})\,(R_{\theta JC} + R_{\theta CB})$$

A stable solution exists only if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}(R_{\theta JC} + R_{\theta CB}) = I_{rms}^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\,(R_{\theta JC} + R_{\theta CB}) < 1$$

Check: 2133 × 1.3 mΩ × 0.005 × 4.5 = **0.062**, which is much less than 1. There is no thermal runaway at the device level. The limit is set by board heat capacity (Section 5).
