# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Systems Architecture Model

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

I have no record of a prior "Job #6" specification, so every parameter below is an explicit assumption. Replace the values in the parameter tables with your real BOM data and re-run the equations. The equations are the deliverable; the numbers show the method.

**Architecture assumption.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller MCU. It does not commutate motors directly. It sends bidirectional DShot600 to four on-board ESC MCUs (for example AT32F421 running AM32). Each ESC MCU drives a tri-half-bridge gate driver and six MOSFETs. This is the only practical way to build an H7 AIO, because ArduPilot does not run ESC commutation firmware.

> **Design-review flag.** "80 A per motor" on a 20×20 or 25×25 nano board is achievable only as a short adiabatic burst. It is not achievable as a continuous rating. With all four motors at 80 A, the battery current is about 320 A, which far exceeds an XT60 (about 60 A continuous) and typical nano power-input copper. The math below quantifies exactly how short the burst must be.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation topology
The model assumes six-step trapezoidal commutation (AM32/BLHeli_32) with complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM.

- During the ON interval, current flows through the HS FET of phase A and the LS FET of phase B.
- During the OFF interval, current freewheels through the LS FET of phase A (synchronous) and the LS FET of phase B.
- In both intervals, the current always passes through exactly two FETs in series.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C this gives R_ds = 1.5 mΩ.

### 1.3 Per-motor conduction loss

Because the DC-link current equals the phase current and two FETs are always in the path:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 \left(2R_{ds}(T_j) + R_{cu}\right)$$

$$P_{cond} = 80^2\,(2\times1.5 + 0.5)\times10^{-3} = 6400 \times 3.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{22.4\ W}$$

The split between FETs is 19.2 W in silicon and 3.2 W in copper.

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution (for junction-temperature analysis)

Over one electrical cycle, each phase leg conducts for 240° (120° sourcing, 120° sinking).

- The low-side FET of the sinking phase conducts the full 120°.
- The PWM leg splits its 120° between HS (duty D) and LS (1 − D).

The worst-case single FET is the LS FET during its sink interval plus its freewheel share. At low duty (D → 0), that FET carries nearly the full I² R for 2/3 of the cycle:

$$P_{FET,max} \approx \tfrac{2}{3}\,I^2 R_{ds} = \tfrac{2}{3}\times 6400 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{6.4\ W}$$

The average per FET is 19.2 W / 6 = 3.2 W.
