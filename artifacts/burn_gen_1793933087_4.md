## 0. Architecture Clarification and Governing Assumptions

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does not commutate motors. Each of the four ESC channels has its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, which performs commutation at the 48 kHz PWM rate. The H743 talks to each ESC MCU over bidirectional DShot600. This split is how every production "AIO" board works. The H743 cannot run four sensorless FOC/6-step loops under ArduPilot's scheduler with deterministic latency.

All component parameters below are **representative values for the design class**. Before layout release, substitute the datasheet values of the parts you actually select. Every equation is written so you can re-run it with real numbers.

| Symbol | Meaning | Value used |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | Battery voltage (6S, full charge) | 25.2 V |
| I_ph | Burst phase current per channel ("80 A" rating) | 80 A |
| f_sw | PWM frequency | 48 kHz |
| R_ds(25) | MOSFET on-resistance at 25 °C (30–40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm class) | 0.90 mΩ |
| k_T | R_ds temperature factor at T_j ≈ 125 °C | 1.5 |
| R_ds(hot) | k_T · R_ds(25) | 1.35 mΩ |

**Rating convention.** "80 A" here means **motor phase current**. Battery current is lower, related through duty cycle by I_dc ≈ D · I_ph. For example, 80 A battery current at D = 0.8 would correspond to 100 A phase current. Product specifications often blur this distinction; the design must not.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-step, complementary (synchronous) PWM

In each 60° commutation sector, three MOSFETs carry current:

- **FET_A:** the PWM-switched FET, conducting for fraction D.
- **FET_A′:** its synchronous-rectifier complement, conducting for fraction (1 − D).
- **FET_B:** the static FET on the return phase, conducting 100% of the sector.

The total conduction loss for the channel is therefore:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 R_{ds(hot)}\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I_{ph}^2 R_{ds(hot)}$$

This result is **independent of D**, which is the key benefit of synchronous rectification.

Each FET spends 120° as the "switched/complement" device and 120° as the "static" device. Averaged over a full electrical revolution:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{I_{ph}^2 R_{ds(hot)}}{3}\left[\,D_{role} + 1\,\right]\cdot\frac{1}{2}\cdot 2 \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; \overline{P}_{cond,FET} = \frac{2\,I_{ph}^2 R_{ds(hot)}}{6} = \frac{I_{ph}^2R_{ds(hot)}}{3}$$

The second form is the six-FET average. The worst-case individual FET is the one that is a high-side PWM device at high D. It sees up to

$$P_{FET,max} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}I_{ph}^2R_{ds}(1 + D) \;/\; \ldots$$

For thermal sizing, use the average plus a **1.15× imbalance factor**.

### 1.2 Numbers at 80 A

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.35\,\text{m}\Omega = 2 \times 6400 \times 0.00135 = \mathbf{17.28\ W}$$

$$\overline{P}_{cond,FET} = 17.28 / 6 = 2.88\ \text{W} \quad\Rightarrow\quad P_{FET,design} = 2.88 \times 1.15 = \mathbf{3.31\ W}$$

### 1.3 Self-consistency check (R_ds vs. T_j)

R_ds rises with junction temperature, and junction temperature rises with R_ds losses. Iterate:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds(25)}\,[1 + \alpha(T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005/\text{K}$$

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}(R_{ds}(T_j))\cdot Z_{th,j\text{-}pcb}$$
