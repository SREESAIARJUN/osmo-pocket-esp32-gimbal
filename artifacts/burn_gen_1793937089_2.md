# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80 A Burst 4-in-1 ESC: Reference Design Package (Job #55)

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Feasibility Flags

The brief combines several items that need clarification before any numbers are useful.

1. **The H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC controller.** A 4-in-1 ESC runs its own MCU per motor or per board (for example an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). The H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600. The 48 kHz switching frequency is set in the ESC firmware, not in ArduPilot. The model below therefore covers two coupled subsystems.
2. **"80 A burst" is interpreted as 80 A motor (phase) current per motor**, at 6S (25.2 V max), for a burst of seconds.
3. **"Nano" form factor vs. 4 × 80 A is a feasibility issue.** Four motors at 80 A means 320 A of battery current. Section 2.6 shows this is thermally a **~2–5 second burst capability only**, and the input connector and capacitor bank become the real limits. A 20×20 mm ESC cannot do this. The design below assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm ESC, 8-layer 2 oz copper**, as the minimum credible size.
4. **No MOSFET was specified.** A representative 40 V, ~1 mΩ, 5×6 mm (or 3.3×3.3 mm dual-sided cooling) part is parameterised below. **Substitute your actual datasheet values into the formulas.** The structure of the model is the deliverable; the specific numbers are illustrative.

## 1. Commutation Model (Basis for All Loss Terms)

Use six-step trapezoidal commutation with **complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM**, the AM32/BLHeli_32 default.

In each 60° electrical sector:
- **Phase A (PWM leg):** the high-side FET switches at f_sw with duty D. The low-side FET conducts the freewheel current during (1−D).
- **Phase B (static leg):** the low-side FET is fully on for the whole sector.
- **Phase C:** floating, used for BEMF sensing.

The phase current I_ph flows through **exactly two conducting channels at every instant**, ignoring dead time. Battery current is related by:

$$I_{bat} \approx D \cdot I_{ph}$$

At full throttle (D → 1), I_bat ≈ I_ph = 80 A per motor.

## 2. Loss Model at 80 A Burst

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

Hot on-resistance:

$$R_{hot} = k_T \cdot R_{25} = 1.5 \times 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega = 1.8\,\text{m}\Omega$$

The general expression accounts for the PWM leg splitting conduction between high and low side, plus the static leg:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 R_{hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{ph}^2 R_{hot} = 2\,I_{ph}^2 R_{hot}$$

This is independent of D, because synchronous rectification always presents one channel per conducting leg. Substituting:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.8\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{23.0\ W}$$

A single conducting FET dissipates I²R = **11.5 W instantaneous**. Averaged over a full electrical cycle across 6 FETs, assuming symmetric rotation:

$$\bar{P}_{cond,FET} = 23.0/6 = 3.84\ \text{W}$$

**PCB copper loss must be added.** Per conducting path, assume roughly 0.3 mΩ of trace/plane plus 0.2 mΩ of motor pad and solder:

$$P_{Cu} \approx 80^2 \times 2 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 6.4\ \text{W per motor}$$

This term is frequently ignored and is a major heat source on nano boards.
