## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the MOSFETs. In every practical 4-in-1 design, it acts as the flight controller (running ArduPilot) and sends DShot600 to **four dedicated ESC MCUs** (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32-class firmware). Each ESC MCU drives one 3-phase bridge.

```
6S LiPo ──┬── Bulk caps ── 4× [ESC MCU → 3× half-bridge driver → 6 FETs] ── M1..M4
          │                         ▲ DShot600 bidir (1 wire each)
          └── 5V/3V3 BEC ── STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ── IMU/Baro/OSD/SD
```

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Phase current vs. battery current

With synchronous rectification in 6-step commutation, motor (phase) current I_ph flows **continuously** through exactly two legs during each 60° sector. It flows through the high-side FET during the on-time D and through the complementary low-side FET during the off-time 1−D. Battery current is the average of the switched current:

$$I_{bat} \approx D \cdot I_{ph}$$

Implication: "80 A" on the battery side at D = 0.5 means 160 A phase current. Worst-case FET heating occurs at **high phase current and low duty cycle**. Examples are punch-out from low throttle and prop-strike stall. This model therefore rates on phase current.

### 1.2 Instantaneous and per-channel conduction loss

At any instant, two FETs conduct the full I_ph:

1. One FET in the PWM leg: the high-side during D, the low-side during 1−D.
2. The static low-side FET in the return leg.

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0047\ \text{K}^{-1} \ \Rightarrow\ \text{1.4}\times \text{ at } 110\,°\text{C}$$

**At 80 A:**

| | R_DS(on) | P_cond per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cold (25 °C) | 1.0 mΩ | 2 × 6400 × 0.0010 = **12.8 W** | 51.2 W |
| Hot (110 °C) | 1.4 mΩ | 2 × 6400 × 0.0014 = **17.9 W** | 71.7 W |

Because R rises with T_j, the loss feeds back on itself. Solve iteratively, or use the hot value as a conservative bound.

### 1.3 Worst-stressed individual FET

Over one electrical period, each leg spends 1/3 as the PWM'd high leg, 1/3 as the static low (return) leg, and 1/3 floating. For high-side PWM with complementary low-side:

- **High-side FET:** conducts for a fraction D/3 of the period.
- **Low-side FET:** conducts for 1/3 (return leg) plus (1−D)/3 (freewheel).

$$P_{LS} = I_{ph}^2 R_{hot}\,\frac{2-D}{3}, \qquad P_{HS} = I_{ph}^2 R_{hot}\,\frac{D}{3} + P_{sw}$$

At 80 A and D = 0.2 (low-duty, high-current worst case):

$$P_{LS} = 6400 \times 0.0014 \times 0.6 = \mathbf{5.4\ W\ per\ low\text{-}side\ FET}$$

The electrical frequency is high (e.g., 30,000 RPM × 7 pole pairs / 60 = 3.5 kHz), well above the die thermal corner. Each FET therefore sees its **average** power, not the pulse.
