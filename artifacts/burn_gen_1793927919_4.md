## 0. Architecture Decisions and Baseline Parameters

### 0.1 Partition

The H743 does **not** generate the 48 kHz motor PWM. ArduPilot has no in-tree BLDC commutation, so the design splits into two layers:

| Layer | Device | Firmware | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIH6 (TFBGA100) or VIT6 (LQFP100) | ArduPilot | Attitude control. Emits bidirectional DShot600 on 4 lines and decodes eRPM for harmonic notch. |
| ESC (×4) | AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071 | AM32 | 6-step commutation, 48 kHz complementary PWM, back-EMF sensing, GCR eRPM reply. |
| Power stage (×4) | 3× half-bridge (6 N-FETs) + 3-phase gate driver | — | 80 A burst per motor |

### 0.3 Feasibility Flag

Read this before the math. A 20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm "nano" board cannot dissipate 4×80 A continuously. §3 shows that 80 A is a **time-limited burst of about 1–3 s**, set by the board's thermal capacitance, not by the FET junctions. The rating must be published as such.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current Path

In 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification (AM32 default), two phases are active in each 60° sector:

- One leg is PWM'd. Its high side conducts for D·T and its low side for (1−D)·T.
- The other leg holds its low side on for the full sector.

At every instant exactly **two FETs carry I_phase**:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Ripple Correction

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{dc}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus}-E_{bemf})\,D}{2L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

Typical 2207-class motor: L_ph ≈ 8 µH line-to-line split 4 µH/phase. At D = 0.5 and a 12.6 V net drive voltage:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{12.6 \times 0.5}{8\times10^{-6}\times 48000} = 16.4\,\text{A}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = 6400 + 22.4 = 6422\ \text{A}^2 \quad (\text{+0.35 \%, negligible at 80 A})$$

### 1.3 Temperature-Dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1} \;\Rightarrow\; R_{125} = 1.2\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 6422 \times 1.2\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{15.4\ W}$$

### 1.4 Per-FET Distribution

Over one electrical cycle each of the 6 FETs averages 1/6 of the total, with sector-dependent peaks:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} = 2.57\ \text{W}$$

The worst instantaneous case is the static low-side FET during its 120° conduction window: 80² × 1.2 mΩ = **7.7 W** for that interval.
