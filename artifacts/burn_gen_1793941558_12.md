# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Design Package (Job #12)

**Scope:** Loss model, thermal model, ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 timer and DMA plan, and reference netlists.

---

## 0. Architectural Ground Truth (read first)

Three constraints shape everything below:

1. **The H743 does not commutate motors.** ArduPilot has no on-board ESC firmware. The H743 sends DShot600. Four dedicated ESC MCUs (AT32F421K8U7 running AM32 is the reference) do the 48 kHz PWM and commutation. Running 4-motor FOC on the H743 alongside ArduPilot's scheduler is not a supportable design.
2. **"Nano" vs. "80A".** A 20×20 mm board cannot carry 4×80A, even as a burst. Copper cross-section, FET footprint, and thermal mass all fail (see §3.4). The defensible configuration is:
   - **FC board:** 20×20 mm (M2 holes) or co-located on the stack.
   - **ESC board:** 30.5×30.5 mm (M3), 8-layer, 2 oz on all layers.
   - **Rating:** 80A per motor for ≤2 s bursts, ~45A continuous with airflow.
3. **Device assumptions** are stated wherever used. Swap in your datasheet values and the equations still hold.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path
The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM, which is AM32 default.

- During any 60° sector, exactly one phase leg is PWM-switching (high-side/low-side complementary). One other leg holds its low-side fully on.
- At every instant, the motor current flows through **two R_ds(on) in series**: the active device in the PWM leg plus the static low-side.

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\left[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\right]$$

With α ≈ 0.0055/K:

$$R_{ds}(125^\circ C) = 1.0 \times 1.55 = 1.55\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Burst value (flat-top trapezoidal current, I_rms ≈ I_pk)

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.55\text{e-}3 = \mathbf{19.8\ W}$$

**Add copper resistance.** Use phase pad to FET to bus, with a 2 oz, 8-layer stitched pour of about 0.25 mΩ per path, two paths:

$$P_{Cu} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.25\text{e-}3 = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

**Add the current shunt.** One shunt is shared on the board DC return: 0.2 mΩ at 4×80 A × duty. Worst case at D ≈ 0.85, with I_bus ≈ 4·80·0.85 = 272 A:

$$P_{shunt} = 272^2 \times 0.2\text{e-}3 \approx 14.8\ \text{W}$$

At that power the shunt must be a 2512 metal-strip part with dual footprints, or use per-phase low-side sensing instead.

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution
Each FET conducts for roughly 1/3 of the electrical period (120°). Losses split to about 1/6 of the per-motor total per FET. Low-sides carry slightly more because of freewheel time at D < 1.

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx 19.8/6 \approx 3.3\ \text{W} \quad (\text{LS: up to }\sim 4.0\ \text{W at low duty})$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model @ 48 kHz

Only one leg switches per sector, so there is one hard-switched transition pair per PWM period.

### 2.1 Transition times (Miller-plateau dominated)

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,src}} + \frac{Q_{gs2}}{I_{g,src}} \approx \frac{9\,\text{nC}}{1.5\,\text{A}} + 4 \approx 10\ \text{ns}, \qquad t_f \approx 9\ \text{ns}$$

Board practice is to add series gate resistors (4.7–10 Ω) for EMI and ringing. This pushes real-world t_r + t_f to about **30 ns**.

### 2.2 Loss terms per motor

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V·I overlap | ½·V_bus·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = 0.5·25.2·80·30e-9·48e3 | **1.45 W** |
| C_oss | C_oss,er·V_bus²·f_sw = 1.2e-9·635·48e3 | 0.037 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V_bus·f_sw = 40e-9·25.2·48e3 | 0.048 W |
| Dead-time diode | 2·V_f·I·t_dt·f_sw = 2·0.8·80·100e-9·48e3 | **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (6 FETs, only 2 switching) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw = 2·55e-9·10·48e3 | 0.053 W (dissipated in driver/R_g) |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.2 W** |

**Interpretation:** at 48 kHz, conduction loss (~20 W) dominates switching loss (~2.2 W) by roughly 9:1.
