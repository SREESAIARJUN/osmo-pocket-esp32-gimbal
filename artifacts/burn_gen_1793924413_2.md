## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j)=R_{DS,25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big],\qquad \alpha\approx 0.0045\ \mathrm{K^{-1}}$$

| T_j | R_DS(on) |
|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 1.34 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 1.45 mΩ (design value) |

### 1.2 Current path under 6‑step complementary PWM

During each 60° commutation sector, two phases conduct:

- **Phase A (static leg):** high-side switch fully ON for 120°.
- **Phase B (PWM leg):** high side ON for duty D, low side (sync‑rect) ON for 1 − D.
- **Phase C:** floating (BEMF sense).

Per‑FET loss in each role:

$$P_{static}=I^2R_{DS}(T_j),\quad P_{HS,pwm}=D\,I^2R_{DS},\quad P_{LS,pwm}=(1-D)\,I^2R_{DS}$$

Each FET spends one third of the electrical cycle in each role. The cycle-averaged loss per FET is therefore:

$$\bar P_{FET}=\tfrac{1}{3}I^2R_{DS}$$

Total ESC-channel conduction loss, because exactly two FET channels are always in the path:

$$P_{cond,ch}=2\,I^2R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

$$P_{cond,ch}=2(80^2)(1.45\times10^{-3}) = 18.6\ \mathrm{W}$$

- Average per FET: 3.1 W.
- **Instantaneous peak on the static FET: 9.3 W.**

The instantaneous peak is what matters for the junction. Each commutation sector at a 25 krpm, 14‑pole motor lasts only about 57 µs. That is far shorter than the die thermal time constant (τ ≈ 1–5 ms), so the junction sees the averaged 3.1 W plus ripple. At low eRPM (startup or stall‑like bursts), however, the sector can last milliseconds. The design case is then **9.3 W in one die**.

### 1.4 Additional conduction terms

| Term | Formula | Value @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Ripple current addition | I_rms² = I² + ΔI²/12, with ΔI = V(1−D)D/(L·f_sw). For L_motor = 15 µH, D = 0.5: ΔI ≈ 8.75 A | +0.5 % (negligible) |
| Phase-trace copper (2 oz, 5 mm wide, 10 mm long, about 0.5 mΩ) | I²R | 3.2 W |
| Current-sense shunt (one shared 0.25 mΩ on the DC return per channel) | (D·I)²·R, battery-side current | 0.4 W at D = 0.5 |
| Bus / ground planes (shared, about 0.15 mΩ effective) | I_bus²R, with I_bus = Σ D·I_phase = 160 A at D = 0.5 | 3.8 W (whole board) |

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Transition times from gate charge

$$t_r=\frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,src}}=\frac{14\,\mathrm{nC}}{1.0\,\mathrm{A}}=14\ \mathrm{ns},\qquad t_f=\frac{14\,\mathrm{nC}}{1.5\,\mathrm{A}}\approx 9.3\ \mathrm{ns}$$

Add 50 % margin for common-source inductance and the Miller plateau sag under 80 A. This gives **t_r = 21 ns** and **t_f = 14 ns**.

### 2.2 Loss components (hard-switched PWM leg only; the static leg does not switch)

| Component | Equation | Value (V = 25.2 V, I = 80 A, f = 48 kHz) |
|---|---|---|
| V‑I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f | ½·25.2·80·35 ns·48 k = **1.69 W** |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss·V·f | 40 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.048 W** |
| Reverse recovery (LS body diode → HS turn‑on) | Q_rr·V·f | 50 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.060 W** |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_SD·I·2t_dt·f | 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs switching) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f | 2·60 nC·10·48 k = **0.058 W** (mostly in the driver and R_g) |

$$P_{sw,ch}\approx 2.47\ \mathrm{W}$$

### 2.3 Scaling and trade-offs

- Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it would be about 1.2 W. At 96 kHz it would be about 4.9 W.
- 48 kHz is a good compromise for AM32: it is above audible range, keeps current ripple low, and switching is still only 12 % of total loss.
- Shortening dead time to 50 ns saves 0.3 W but risks shoot-through, given driver propagation-delay mismatch (typically ±20 ns). **Keep 100 ns.**
