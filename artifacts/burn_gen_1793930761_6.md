# STM32H743 Nano AIO with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #32): Architecture, Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, DShot DMA Plan, and Netlist

## 0. Architectural Corrections

These points change the model, so they come first.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In a 4-in-1 design the STM32H743 is the flight controller. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU (typically AT32F421, STM32G071 or GD32 class) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 sends DShot600 and receives bidirectional eRPM telemetry. The 48 kHz PWM lives on the ESC MCUs, not the H743.
2. **"80 A burst" on a 20×20 mm board is a short-time rating, not a continuous capability.** The math below shows that about 100 W of loss goes into roughly 3 J/K of thermal mass. At 80 A on all four channels, the board heats at about 30 K/s. The honest specification is about 80 A for 1–2 s on one or two channels, and much less continuously.
3. **Component values are example assumptions.** I use plausible 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN MOSFET parameters. Replace them with your selected part's datasheet values. Every equation is parameterised so you can substitute directly.

## 2. Conduction-Loss Model

### 2.1 Six-step commutation with synchronous rectification

In each 60° electrical sector, one high-side FET (PWM leg) and one low-side FET (return leg) carry current. With complementary PWM:

- **PWM leg:** current flows through the high-side switch for D·T_sw and through the synchronous low-side switch for (1−D)·T_sw.
- **Return leg:** the low-side switch conducts continuously.

So phase current always passes through exactly two conducting channels:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds(on),25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.004\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

Because R_ds(on) depends on temperature and temperature depends on loss, solve iteratively:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_{ref} + P(T_j^{(n)})\cdot R_\theta$$

Take T_j = 125 °C as the design corner. Then R_ds(on) = 1.4 mΩ (1.5 mΩ with margin):

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 6400 \times 0.003 = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

### 2.2 Per-FET distribution

Each FET is in the current path for about 1/3 of the electrical cycle (120°):

- **Average per FET:** P_avg ≈ I²·R / 3 = **3.2 W**
- **Sector peak** (D → 1, high-side in its 120° window): **9.6 W** instantaneous

The peak matters for transient junction rise at low eRPM. At startup or stall, commutation is slow and one FET may hold full current for many milliseconds.

### 2.3 Copper losses (often forgotten)

$$P_{cu} = I_{ph}^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.2\ W\ per\ channel}$$

At 80 A, inner-layer copper is not negligible. Use at least 2 oz copper and stitched parallel planes, and do not run phase current through vias without a large via array.

## 3. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. That gives one hard-switched transition pair per T_sw per channel.

### 3.1 V–I overlap (hard switching)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

$$= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 3.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = 2\, V_{SD}\, I_{ph}\, t_{dead}\, f_{sw} = 2 \times 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 3.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}$$

This term is small at 25 V. Its real cost is EMI and ringing (see the snubber in §7).

### 3.4 C_oss energy

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss,eq} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1\,\text{nF} \times 635 \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.015\ W}$$

### 3.5 Gate drive (dissipated in driver and gate resistors)

$$P_g = 2\, Q_g\, V_{drv}\, f_{sw} = 2 \times 40\,\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.038\ W}$$

### 3.6 Per-channel total at the 80 A corner

| Loss term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (2 × R_ds) | 19.20 |
| Copper path | 3.20 |
| V–I switching | 1.94 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.09 |
| **Total per channel** | **≈ 25.0 W** |
| **All four channels** | **≈ 100 W** |

**Key insight:** conduction dominates (≈ 77%). Reducing f_sw to 24 kHz saves only about 1.3 W per channel. Halving R_ds(on), or paralleling FETs if footprint allows, saves about 10 W per channel. At 48 kHz you are already switching-cheap; spend the design effort on R_ds(on) and copper.
