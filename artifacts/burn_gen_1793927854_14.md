# Job #44: STM32H743 Nano FC with Integrated 4-in-1 ESC

## System Architecture, Loss Model, Thermal Budget, and DShot Timer Allocation

## 0. Scope Corrections and Design Assumptions

Two framing corrections shape the rest of this document.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** It runs ArduPilot and emits bi-directional DShot600. Each ESC channel has its own commutation MCU running AM32 (an AT32F421 in this design), or one MCU per pair. The H743 has neither the advanced-timer count nor the latency isolation to run four sensorless BLDC loops alongside an EKF. The architecture is therefore **FC (H743) → 4× DShot lines → 4× ESC MCU → 4× 3-phase bridges.**

2. **"80A burst" on a nano board is a transient rating, not a thermal operating point.** Section 3 shows the board absorbs roughly 94 W at a simultaneous 4×80 A, and survives that for only about 2–3 s. All marketing and firmware limits must follow from this.

**Baseline parameters** (all are assumptions; substitute your own datasheet values):

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Topology assumption

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with **complementary (synchronous) PWM**, which is the AM32 default.

At any instant, the phase current I_ph flows through **exactly two switches**:
- The PWM leg conducts through its high-side FET during the on-time D and its low-side FET during 1−D.
- The return leg conducts through its low-side FET continuously.

The total conduction loss is therefore **independent of duty cycle**:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS,hot} + I_{ph}^2 R_{pcb}$$

### 1.2 At the 80 A burst

$$P_{cond,FET} = 2 (80)^2 (1.40\times10^{-3}) = 17.9\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{pcb} = (80)^2 (0.5\times10^{-3}) = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Electrical frequency is high relative to the FET die thermal constant. For example, 30k RPM on a 14-pole motor gives f_e = 3.5 kHz, against a die time constant of about 1 ms. Each FET therefore sees its cycle-average loss.

Each FET carries current for 1/3 of the electrical period:

$$P_{cond,per\,FET} \approx \frac{17.9}{6} \approx 3.0\ \text{W}$$

### 1.4 Temperature feedback (must iterate)

R_DS(on) is a function of T_j:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

The model is solved iteratively against Section 3. At T_j ≈ 120 °C it converges to the 1.40 mΩ already assumed. If T_j exceeds about 150 °C, the loop runs away, which is the classic thermal-runaway condition.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the **PWM leg** switches. Its two FETs share switching events (hard turn-on of the high side, diode-commutated turn-on of the low side).

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss (hard-switched high side)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = \tfrac12 (25.2)(80)(35\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = 1.69\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (low-side body diode → high-side turn-on)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = (40\,\text{nC})(25.2)(48\,\text{kHz}) = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = (50\,\text{nC})(25.2)(48\,\text{kHz}) = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two dead times per period)

$$P_{dt} = 2 V_F I_{ph} t_d f_{sw} = 2(0.8)(80)(100\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = 0.61\ \text{W}$$
