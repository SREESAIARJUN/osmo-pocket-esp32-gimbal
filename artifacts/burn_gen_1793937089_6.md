# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #64): Electrothermal Model, Bi-Directional DShot DMA Allocation, and Reference Netlist

## 0. Feasibility Findings

These issues affect everything below, so they come first.

1. **The H743 should not drive the power stage.** Each ESC channel needs its own small MCU, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Each one runs sensorless 6-step commutation with its own gate driver. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to the ESC MCUs only through bi-directional DShot600. ArduPilot has no motor-commutation firmware for the H743.
2. **"80 A" on a nano form factor is a burst rating only.** The analysis below shows a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 board cannot dissipate full-power losses continuously. Assume four 80 A channels for a few seconds at most. Sustained current is roughly 25–35 A per channel, depending on airflow.
3. **I don't have Job #64's BOM.** All component values below are representative assumptions:
   - 40 V MOSFET, R_DS(on) = 1.0 mΩ typ at 25 °C, in 3.3×3.3 or 5×6 PDFN.
   - 6S pack.
   - 8-layer, 2 oz board.

   Substitute your actual datasheet values into the equations. The model is fully parametric.

$$R_{on}(T_j) = R_{on,25}\left(1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right), \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At 125 °C this gives a factor of about 1.6.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

AM32 and BLHeli_32 use 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. In each 60° sector:

- One leg is PWM-switched: its high-side conducts for D, its low-side for 1−D.
- One low-side FET is held fully on.

Current therefore always flows through exactly **two** channels, whatever the duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{on} \quad \text{(independent of } D \text{ with sync rectification)}$$

Including inductor current ripple ΔI:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

The ripple term is negligible at 80 A on low-inductance motors with ΔI ≈ 5–10 A.

**Numbers:**

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.6\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{20.5\ W}$$

Each FET conducts for 120° of 360° electrical:

$$P_{cond,FET} = I^2 R_{on}\cdot\tfrac{1}{3} = 6400 \times 1.6\text{m} / 3 = \mathbf{3.41\ W}$$

PCB copper adds a further loss:

$$P_{cu} = I^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\text{m} = 3.2\ \text{W/ch}$$

Copper loss is often overlooked. On a nano board it is a major term because trace widths are tightly constrained.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 3.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap

This loss occurs in the active (high-side) switch:

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I_{ph}\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

$$= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

Rise and fall times are set by the Miller plateau:

$$t \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g}, \qquad I_g = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}{R_g}$$
