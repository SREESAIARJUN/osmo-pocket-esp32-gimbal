# STM32H743 Nano AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC) Design Package, Job #49

## 0. Architecture Clarifications

Three points shape everything below:

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot cannot run 4× three-phase BLDC commutation, and the H743 cannot generate 24 complementary gate signals with dead-time while also running the flight stack. The ESC half therefore uses **4 dedicated ESC MCUs (AT32F421K8U7, AM32 firmware)**, each with a 3-phase gate driver. The H743 talks to them over **bidirectional DShot600**. 48 kHz is the ESC PWM frequency, set in AM32.
2. **"80A burst" means per channel, for seconds, not continuous.** Four channels at 80A on 6S would draw roughly 8 kW and 320A from the pack. No nano-format PCB survives that thermally. The thermal model below quantifies the actual time limits.
3. **"Nano" here means a 30.5×30.5 mm board** (36×36 mm outline, 8-layer, 2 oz copper). A 20×20 board cannot dissipate the numbers below. If 20×20 is mandatory, derate to about 45A burst.

**Assumed power-stage parameters** (substitute datasheet values for your chosen FET):

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Bus voltage V_bus | 6S, 25.2 V max (design point) |
| MOSFET | 40 V N-ch, PDFN 5×6 (e.g. BSC010N04LS-class) |
| R_ds(on) @ 25 °C, V_gs = 10 V | 1.1 mΩ |
| R_ds(on) temperature coefficient α | 0.0045 /K |
| Q_g / Q_gd+Q_gs2 / Q_oss / Q_rr | 60 nC / 12 nC / 50 nC / 40 nC |
| Gate drive | 10 V, about 1 A effective during the Miller plateau |
| Dead-time t_dt | 100 ns |
| Body diode V_f | 0.8 V |
| f_sw | 48 kHz |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.1 mΩ × 1.45 = **1.6 mΩ**. Use this value for worst-case burst.

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step with complementary (synchronous) PWM

In any commutation sector, current flows through exactly two FETs in series:

- the PWM leg: high-side during D, low-side during (1−D);
- the static leg: low-side, 100% of the sector.

The conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.6\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{20.5\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each leg spends 1/3 of the electrical period as the PWM leg, 1/3 as the static leg, and 1/3 floating.

| FET | RMS fraction of I²R | Loss @ 80A, D = 0.9 |
|---|---|---|
| High-side | D/3 | 0.30 × 10.24 W = 3.1 W |
| Low-side | (1−D)/3 + 1/3 | 0.37 × 10.24 W = 3.8 W ← **hottest** |

The single-FET term is I²R = 6400 × 1.6 mΩ = 10.24 W.

### 1.4 Copper losses

Assume about 0.25 mΩ of trace and plane resistance per phase path, including the shunt share:

P_Cu = 80² × 0.25 mΩ × 2 ≈ **3.2 W per motor**. This heats the board, not the junctions.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Its loss components are:

**(a) V–I overlap.** Using t_sw = Q_sw / I_g = 12 nC / 1 A = 12 ns per edge:

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 24\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.16\ W}$$

**(b) Coss discharge:**

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

**(c) Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead-times per period):

$$P_{dt} = 2 V_f I t_{dt} f_{sw} = 2 \times 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**(e) Gate charge** (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistor, not the FET):

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

**Switching total ≈ 1.94 W per motor.**

Design levers:
- Cut dead-time to 50 ns: saves 0.3 W.
- Drop f_sw to 24 kHz: halves switching loss, but costs ripple and acoustic performance.

### 2.1 Loss budget summary at 80A, T_j = 125 °C

| Component | Per motor | 4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 20.5 W | 82 W |
| FET switching | 1.9 W | 7.8 W |
| Copper + shunt | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| **Total** | **25.6 W** | **102 W** |
