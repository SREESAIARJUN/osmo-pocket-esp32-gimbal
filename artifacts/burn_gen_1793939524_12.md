## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

### 0.1 Partitioning

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. In a "4-in-1 ESC + FC" stack, the work splits as follows:

| Block | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP-100) | ArduPilot, sends bidirectional DShot600, receives eRPM telemetry |
| ESC MCUs (×4) | AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071 (AM32 firmware) | 6-step commutation, 48 kHz PWM, DShot decode, eRPM encode |
| Gate drivers (×4) | FD6288Q (3-phase, 250 V, bootstrap) | Drive 6 N-FETs per motor |
| Power stage | 24 N-FETs, 40 V, DFN5×6 | 80 A burst per motor |

The 48 kHz switching analysis applies to the ESC side. The DShot/DMA analysis applies to the H743 side.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current Path in 6-Step Trapezoidal with Synchronous Rectification

During any 60° commutation sector, one leg is PWM-modulated and the other leg's low-side FET is held on:

- **PWM leg high side (HS):** conducts for duty D
- **PWM leg low side (LS):** synchronous freewheel for (1 − D)
- **Return leg low side:** conducts 100 % of the sector

Phase current is assumed flat (L/R ≫ 1/f_sw; ripple is treated separately in §1.3).

### 1.2 Equations

Hot on-resistance:

$$R_{hot} = k_T \cdot R_{25} = 1.5 \times 0.70\ \text{m}\Omega = 1.05\ \text{m}\Omega$$

Instantaneous conduction loss per motor channel:

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 R_{hot}\big[D + (1-D)\big] + I_{ph}^2 R_{hot} = 2\,I_{ph}^2 R_{hot}$$

This result is independent of D, because synchronous rectification keeps a FET in the path at all times.

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.05\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{13.44\ W\ per\ motor}$$

Copper loss:

$$P_{cu} = I_{ph}^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{3.20\ W}$$

Per-FET average: each FET is active for about 1/3 of the electrical cycle, so:

$$\bar P_{FET,cond} \approx \frac{13.44}{6} = 2.24\ \text{W}$$

### 1.3 Ripple Correction

Peak-to-peak ripple:

$$\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

The RMS conduction term becomes:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \Delta I^2/12$$

Example: L_ph = 15 µH, V_bus − V_bemf = 8 V, D = 0.7:

- ΔI = 7.8 A
- Correction term ΔI²/12 = 5.1 A², which is +0.08 % (negligible at 80 A)

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the PWM leg switches. The HS FET is hard-switched; the LS FET commutates through its body diode during dead time.

| Loss term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Hard-switching V·I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 30 ns × 48 k = **1.452 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f · I · 2t_dt · f_sw | 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k = **0.614 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw | 50 n × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.060 W** |
| Output capacitance | ½ · Q_oss · V · f_sw | 0.5 × 60 n × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.036 W** |
| Gate drive (driver/LDO side) | 2 · Q_g · V_GS · f_sw | 2 × 60 n × 10 × 48 k = **0.058 W** |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.22 W per motor** |

**Sensitivity:**

- P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it would save about 1.1 W per motor but increase audible noise and ripple.
- Reducing dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per motor. Validate shoot-through margin against FD6288 propagation mismatch (about 20–50 ns).

## 3. Total Burst Power Budget

| Item | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 13.44 W | 53.8 W |
| FET switching | 2.22 W | 8.9 W |
| PCB copper | 3.20 W | 12.8 W |
| Gate drivers, ESC MCUs, LDOs | ~0.15 W | 0.6 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 19.0 W** | **≈ 76 W** |

Efficiency check at full burst: P_out ≈ 4 × 22.2 V × 80 A × 0.85 (effective) ≈ 6 kW, so the power stage is about 98.7 % efficient. The problem is not efficiency; it is **heat density on a nano-size board**.
