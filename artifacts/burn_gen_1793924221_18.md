### Architecture

This is the important correction to the brief. The H743 does **not** generate the 48 kHz bridge PWM. The H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600. Four dedicated ESC MCUs run AM32 and generate the 48 kHz complementary PWM.

```
                ┌──────────────────────── STM32H743VIT6 (ArduPilot) ───────────────────────┐
 Gyro/Baro ───► │ SPI1/2/4        TIM1 CH1..CH4 (PE9/PE11/PE13/PE14) ─ BDShot600 ──┐       │
                └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┼───────┘
                        ┌──────────────┬──────────────┬──────────────┐              │
                        ▼              ▼              ▼              ▼     (one wire per motor)
                   AT32F421 ×4  (AM32, 48 kHz center-aligned complementary PWM)
                        ▼
                   FD6288Q 3-phase gate driver ×4
                        ▼
                   6× N-MOSFET per motor (24 total), 1 shunt per ESC
```

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous PWM

During any 60° sector, phase A is PWM'd and phase B is sunk:

- **During D·T_sw:** current flows HS_A → motor → LS_B.
- **During (1−D)·T_sw:** current freewheels through LS_A (synchronous) → motor → LS_B.

Instantaneous resistive path count per motor:

$$
P_{cond,ESC} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[\,D + (1-D) + 1\,] = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

Conduction loss is therefore **independent of duty cycle**. This is a useful result: a burst at low throttle-to-stall is as bad as one at full throttle.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

Use the standard silicon mobility fit:

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left(\frac{T_j + 273}{298}\right)^{2.3}
$$

| T_j | Factor | R_DS |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 | 1.00 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 1.69 | 1.69 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 1.94 | 1.94 mΩ |

Taking T_j = 100 °C as the design point:

$$
P_{cond,ESC} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.69\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{21.6\ W}
$$

### 1.3 PCB copper loss

Assume a phase path of 15 mm length and 4 mm width across 4 layers of 2 oz (70 µm):

$$
R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{A} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}(1+0.0039\cdot 60)\times0.015}{4\times(0.004\times70\times10^{-6})} \approx 0.28\ \text{mΩ per path}
$$

With two paths active:

$$
P_{Cu} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.28\text{ mΩ} = \mathbf{3.6\ W}
$$

Shunt (0.25 mΩ, bus-side, carries I only during D): the worst case is

$$
80^2 \times 0.25\text{ mΩ} = \mathbf{1.6\ W}
$$

The shunt must be a ≥3 W, 2512 or wide-terminal part [VERIFY].

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd high-side hard-switches. The low-side commutates softly through dead time but contributes reverse-recovery and dead-time diode losses.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap (HS)

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = \tfrac12(25.2)(80)(40\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz}) = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (LS body diode → HS turn-on)

$$
P_{rr} = V_{bus} Q_{rr} f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 50\text{ nC} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}
$$

This loss is dissipated mostly in the HS. Q_rr grows with di/dt and temperature, so derate ×2 if needed.

$$
P_{dt} = V_F I (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$
