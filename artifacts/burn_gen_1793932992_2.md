## Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Budget, Bi-Directional DShot600 DMA Plan, and Reference Netlist

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Key Findings

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. In an AIO "nano" board it runs ArduPilot and sends DShot600 to four ESC MCUs (AT32F421, STM32G071, or EFM8 running AM32 or BLHeli). Each ESC MCU drives its own 3-phase bridge. The loss model in §1–2 applies to the bridges. The DMA model in §4 applies to the H743.

**Key findings.**

| Item | Result |
|---|---|
| Loss per channel at 80 A, 6S, 48 kHz | ≈ 24–25 W (≈ 22 W conduction incl. copper, ≈ 2.4 W switching) |
| All 4 channels at 80 A simultaneously | ≈ 100 W of heat |
| Steady-state survivability on a 20×20 board | **Not survivable.** Board-to-air resistance is about 4–8 K/W. |
| Permitted burst duration (40 °C → 110 °C PCB) | ≈ 3–5 s with all four channels loaded |
| MOSFET junction margin during burst | Acceptable (ΔT_jc ≈ 15 K above the PCB) |
| DShot DMA streams required | 6 (2 timers × [1 UP + 2 capture]) out of 16 available |

**Recommendation.** An 80 A rating on a 20×20 nano 4-in-1 is a *burst* rating of a few seconds at most. For marketing and the datasheet, specify **"80 A burst (≤ 2 s, single channel), 45 A continuous with airflow."** Then validate this with thermocouple and IR testing (see §6).

**Assumptions.** All component parameters below are assumptions typical of current-generation parts. Substitute datasheet values once the BOM is frozen. Every equation is written so you can re-run it.

### 1.2 Conduction topology
Trapezoidal (6-step) commutation uses complementary PWM with synchronous rectification:

- **PWM ON:** current flows through HS(phase A) → motor → LS(phase B).
- **PWM OFF:** current freewheels through LS(phase A) → motor → LS(phase B).

So at any instant, the phase current I flows through exactly **two** conducting channels. This holds outside the dead-time intervals, which are treated in §2.

### 1.3 Equations

$$R_{DS,hot} = R_{25}\cdot\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right]\approx R_{25}\cdot k_T$$

$$P_{cond,FET} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS,hot}$$

PCB copper and solder add loss in series. For 2 oz outer layers plus 2 oz inner layers and a ~4 mm path to the phase pad, assume R_Cu ≈ 0.5 mΩ:

$$P_{cond,Cu} = I_{ph}^2\,R_{Cu}$$

### 1.4 Numbers at I = 80 A, T_j = 125 °C
- P_cond,FET = 2 × 80² × 1.5 mΩ = **19.2 W/channel**
- P_cond,Cu = 80² × 0.5 mΩ = **3.2 W/channel**
- Per-FET average: 19.2 W split across the conducting positions. The worst-case single FET is the low-side of the "fixed" phase, which conducts continuously for a 120° commutation interval. Its loss is I²·R_DS,hot = **9.6 W** during that interval. Averaged over an electrical cycle, it is ≈ 3.2 W.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing leg switches. Per switching event, model these five components:

**(a) V-I overlap (hard switching, inductive load)**
$$P_{VI}=\tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw}$$
With V_bus = 25.2 V and t_r + t_f = 20 ns (gate driver at ~2 A source/sink):
P_VI = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 20 ns × 48 k = **0.97 W**

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (2 dead-times per PWM period)
$$P_{DT}=V_F\,I\,(2\,t_{dead})\,f_{sw}$$
With t_dead = 200 ns:
P_DT = 0.8 × 80 × 400 ns × 48 k = **1.23 W**

This is the largest switching term. Reducing dead time to 100 ns saves ~0.6 W/channel. Only do so once shoot-through margin has been verified on the scope.

**(c) Reverse recovery**
$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw}=50\text{ nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k}= \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

**(d) Output capacitance**
$$P_{oss}=\tfrac12\,C_{oss}\,V_{bus}^2\,f_{sw}=0.5\times1.5\text{ nF}\times635\times48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.023\ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive** (2 FETs per switching leg; dissipated in the driver and gate resistors)
$$P_g = 2\,Q_g\,V_{GS}\,f_{sw}=2\times50\text{ nC}\times10\times48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

**Switching total ≈ 2.33 W/channel** (≈ 9.7 % of the channel loss at 80 A).

### 2.1 Channel and board totals

| Term | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Copper conduction | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| Switching (all terms) | 2.33 W | 9.3 W |

Efficiency at 80 A per channel on 6S: P_out ≈ 25.2 × 80 × D. At full throttle, η ≈ 1 − 24.7/2016 ≈ **98.8 %**. The efficiency looks excellent; the problem is heat *density*, not efficiency.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Junction rise (fast time constant)
For the worst-case FET at 9.6 W instantaneous:
$$\Delta T_{j\text{-}c}=P\cdot\theta_{jc}=9.6\times1.5=14.4\text{ K}$$

For pulses under 10 ms, use the datasheet Z_th(t) curve. This is typically ≈ 0.3–0.5 K/W, giving ≈ 3–5 K. Junction-to-PCB rise is therefore small. **The PCB bulk temperature is the limiting variable.**
