# Job #24: STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC
## Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and ArduPilot Bi-Directional DShot600 Allocation

> **Scope note:** On a 4-in-1 stack, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. Each ESC channel runs its own commutation MCU, for example an AT32F421 or STM32G071 with AM32 firmware. The H743 does not commutate the motors. Commutation needs about 1 µs of deterministic response per channel, and that would conflict with EKF and scheduler load. This document models both halves and the link between them.

**Feasibility flag.** 80 A per channel on a 30×30 mm board is a **burst-only** rating limited to seconds. Section 3 shows it is set by the board's thermal mass, not the silicon. A 3×3 DFN MOSFET cannot carry 80 A at all; it is package and bond-wire limited. PDFN 5×6 (TOLL is too large for this board) with 8 FETs per phase-pair layout is the minimum.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous commutation

In any commutation sector, two phases are active:
- **Phase A, PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for duty D, and the complementary low-side FET conducts for (1−D). Dead-time intervals are handled separately in §2.3.
- **Phase B, return leg:** the low-side FET is held on for 100% of the sector.

Instantaneous conduction loss in the channel is:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

**The result does not depend on duty cycle.** That is an advantage of synchronous rectification: loss is always two R_DS(on) drops in series.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)]
$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.2 × (1 + 0.55) = **1.86 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Copper path loss

The current path is: XT60 pad → bus planes → FET → phase pad → motor wire, out and back. Planar copper resistance is R = ρL/(w·t·n_layers).

- Assume an average path of 15 mm, 8 mm width, and 4 parallel 2-oz layers (70 µm each).
- ρ_Cu at 100 °C = 2.2×10⁻⁸ Ω·m.
- One direction: R = 2.2e-8 × 0.015 / (0.008 × 70e-6 × 4) ≈ 0.15 mΩ.
- Round trip plus vias and solder joints: **R_cu ≈ 0.45 mΩ**.

$$
P_{cu} = I^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.45\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{2.9\ W}
$$

### 1.4 Conduction result at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.86\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{23.8\ W\ per\ channel}
$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches hard. The return leg is static within a sector.

### 2.1 V·I overlap loss (high-side hard turn-on and turn-off)

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

The high-side turn-on recovers the low-side body diode:

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}
$$

The induced overlap from I_rr adds roughly 20–50% to P_sw at high di/dt. This is included in the margin below.

### 2.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM period:

$$
P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2t_d)\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 600\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.84\ W}
$$

This is nearly equal to the hard-switching loss. **Dead time is the cheapest efficiency lever.** Trimming it from 300 ns to 150 ns, if the driver propagation-delay mismatch allows, saves about 0.9 W per channel.

### 2.4 C_oss and gate-drive loss

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss} V^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = 0.023\ \text{W}
$$

$$
P_{gate} = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2 = 60\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = 0.058\ \text{W}
$$

Gate-drive loss is dissipated mostly in R_g and the driver, not in the FET.
