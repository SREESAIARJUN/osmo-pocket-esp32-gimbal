# STM32H743 Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: 80 A Burst Thermal, 48 kHz Switching, and Bidirectional DShot600 Reference Design

## 0. Scope, Architecture, and Assumptions

### What this document can and cannot be

No MOSFET, board size, battery voltage, or burst duration was specified, so I have fixed representative values for each. Every number below flows from the parameter table in §0.3. Replace those values with your actual datasheet figures and the equations still hold.

The netlists are a reference topology, not a verified layout. They need ERC/DRC, a pinmux check in STM32CubeMX, and bench validation before fabrication.

### Architecture correction

In real 4-in-1 stacks, the STM32H743 is the **flight controller (FC)** MCU, running ArduPilot. The ESC runs on separate low-cost MCUs, typically one AT32F421 or STM32G071 per motor, running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to those ESC MCUs. It does not commutate motors itself. The design below follows that architecture.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous commutation

In each 60° sector, one phase is PWM'd and one phase is held low.

- **PWM'd phase:** current flows through the high-side FET for fraction D and through the low-side FET (synchronous rectification) for fraction (1−D). Phase current is continuous through one switch of that leg.
- **Low-held phase:** current flows through the low-side FET for 100 % of the sector.
- **Third phase:** floating, no current.

So at every instant, exactly **two MOSFETs carry I_ph**, independent of D (neglecting dead time). This gives the per-motor conduction loss:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

with temperature dependence

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1} \;\Rightarrow\; k_T(125\,^\circ\text{C}) = 1.6$$

### 1.2 Numbers at 80 A

| Condition | R_DS(on) | P_cond per motor | P_cond, 4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|
| T_j = 25 °C | 1.5 mΩ | 2 × 6400 × 0.0015 = **19.2 W** | 76.8 W |
| T_j = 125 °C | 2.4 mΩ | 2 × 6400 × 0.0024 = **30.7 W** | **122.9 W** |

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Over a full electrical cycle, each leg conducts for 240° (2/3 of the cycle). Averaged over the cycle:

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} = \frac{P_{cond,motor}}{6} = 5.1\ \text{W (hot)}$$

Within a sector, the instantaneous worst case is the continuously-on low-side FET:

$$P_{inst} = I^2 R = 15.4\ \text{W}$$

This lasts one 60° sector, which is about 0.1 ms at typical eRPM. That is far shorter than the package thermal time constant (~10–50 ms), so the cycle average governs T_j.

### 1.4 Shared-path conduction (do not omit)

Copper trace, shunt, and connector resistance all carry the same current.

- **Phase path per motor:** 2 × I² × R_path. With R_path = 0.3 mΩ (2 oz copper plus pad), this adds about **3.8 W per motor**.
- **DC bus:** I_bus ≈ D·I_ph. At full throttle, 4 × 80 A = **320 A** from the battery.
  - A 0.25 mΩ shunt dissipates 320² × 0.00025 = **25.6 W**.
  - An XT60 connector (~0.8 mΩ) at 320 A dissipates about **82 W**, far beyond its rating.

**→ 4 × 80 A simultaneous is not realistic on a nano-format stack. Treat 80 A as a single-motor or short-burst rating.**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg hard-switches. Its high-side FET turns on into an inductive load, against the body-diode recovery of its complementary low-side FET.

### 2.1 Voltage–current overlap

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

The switching time follows from gate charge:

$$t_{r,f} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2} + Q_{gd}}{I_g}, \qquad I_g = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}{R_{g,ext} + R_{g,int} + R_{drv}}$$

$$P_{VI} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$
