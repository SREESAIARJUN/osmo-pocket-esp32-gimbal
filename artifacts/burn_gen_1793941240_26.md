## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_ds = 1.3 mΩ × 1.425 = **1.85 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

At every instant, exactly two switch positions carry the phase current:
- one high-side (HS) or its complementary low-side (LS) during PWM off-time, on the "source" phase;
- one LS on the "sink" phase.

The conduction path therefore always contains **2 × R_ds**, independent of duty cycle, when complementary PWM is enabled. AM32 does this by default.

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph,rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j) + I_{ph,rms}^2 R_{Cu}$$

Phase vs. battery current: I_bat ≈ D · I_ph. A burst at 80 A near full throttle (D → 1) gives I_ph ≈ 80 A. Take R_Cu (traces, vias, pads, per path) ≈ 0.4 mΩ.

$$P_{cond} = 80^2(2\times1.85 + 0.4)\,\text{m}\Omega = 6400 \times 4.1\text{ m} = \mathbf{26.2\ W\ per\ motor}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each switch position conducts about 1/3 of the electrical cycle.

- Average per FET: 6400 × 1.85 m × (1/3) ≈ **3.9 W**.
- Instantaneous while conducting: **11.8 W**.

Electrical commutation periods are under 1 ms, which is much shorter than the die-to-board thermal time constant (tens of ms). The **average** value is therefore the correct input for the T_j estimate.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switched leg incurs hard-switching loss. Per motor, per PWM period:

**(a) V–I overlap (HS hard switching)**
$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(40\text{n})(48\text{k}) = 1.94\text{ W}$$

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction (LS)**
$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2t_d)\, f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(400\text{n})(48\text{k}) = 1.23\text{ W}$$

**(c) Reverse recovery**
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}(25.2)(48\text{k}) = 0.06\text{ W}$$

**(d) Output capacitance** (Q_oss ≈ 40 nC)
$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \times 2 \approx 0.05\text{ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive** (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET)
$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2(50\text{n})(10)(48\text{k}) = 0.048\text{ W}$$

$$\boxed{P_{sw,motor} \approx 3.3\text{ W}\ \text{at 80 A, 25.2 V, 48 kHz}}$$

Rule of thumb: about 0.041 W/A at 25.2 V, roughly linear in I and V_bus.

Observations:
- At 100% duty, (a) through (c) collapse toward zero. The worst case is about 85–95% duty.
- Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.6 W per motor. The cost is more current ripple, which matters on low-inductance racing motors.
- Dead time costs 63% as much as V–I overlap. Tighten AM32 dead time to the minimum that is safe for your driver's propagation skew.
