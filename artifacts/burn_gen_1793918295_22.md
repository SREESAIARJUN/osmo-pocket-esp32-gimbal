## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Commutation topology

AM32 uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM on the active high-side phase. In each 60° sector:

- **Phase A (PWM phase):** the high-side FET conducts for duty D, and its low-side partner conducts for (1−D) as a synchronous rectifier. Current flows through exactly **one** FET of this leg at all times, ignoring dead time.
- **Phase B (sink phase):** the low-side FET is held on 100 %.
- **Phase C:** floating, used for BEMF sensing.

The motor current I therefore always flows through **two** R_DS(on) in series.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\qquad \alpha \approx \frac{k_T - 1}{100} = 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.2 × 1.6 = **1.92 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Per-channel conduction loss

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,ch}(80\,\text{A}) = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.92\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{24.6\ W}$$

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

Each of the 6 FETs carries current for 1/3 of the electrical period: high-side for D/3 and its low-side as sync for (1−D)/3, plus a full 1/3 as the sink phase.

- Instantaneous FET loss while conducting: I² R = 6400 × 1.92 m = **12.3 W**
- Cycle-averaged per FET: **≈ 24.6 / 6 = 4.1 W**

The electrical period at a 14-pole motor running 30 kRPM is 0.29 ms. That is far below the die-to-pad thermal time constant (~10–50 ms), so the cycle-averaged value is valid for T_j estimation.

### 1.5 Copper (PCB) conduction loss

The phase and bus path per channel is approximately 15 mm of effective length. It uses 2 oz outer copper, 2 oz inner, and a via array, which gives an estimated R_cu ≈ 0.4 mΩ (bus + phase out + return).

$$P_{cu,ch} = I^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.4\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{2.6\ W}$$

Copper has α_Cu = 0.0039 K⁻¹, so this rises about 30 % at 100 °C board temperature.
