## 0. Architecture Decisions and Assumptions

Two points need to be fixed before the math, because they change the design.

**1. The H743 should not commutate the motors.** Four motors need 12 complementary half-bridge pairs. The H743 has only two advanced timers with complementary outputs (TIM1 and TIM8, 3 pairs each), plus HRTIM. Back-EMF zero-cross sensing for four motors would also consume the ADC and comparator resources.

The industry-standard partition, and the one this document assumes, is:

| Block | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIH6 @ 400 MHz (ArduPilot) | Sensors, EKF, outputs 4× bidir DShot600 |
| ESC MCUs (×4) | AT32F421 or STM32G071 (AM32 firmware) | 6-step commutation, 48 kHz PWM, eRPM telemetry |
| Gate drivers (×4) | 3-phase bootstrap driver (FD6288Q class) | 12 V gate drive |
| Power stage | 24× N-MOSFET, 40 V, 3.3×3.3 DFN | 6 FETs per motor |

**2. 80 A on a 20×20 mm board is a burst rating only.** The model below shows that 80 A on all four motors simultaneously is thermally limited to about 1 second. A defensible spec is **"80 A burst (≤1 s, single motor ≤3 s), 45 A continuous with airflow."** Commercial "80 A" nano ratings typically rest on similar assumptions.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology current paths
AM32 uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. During any commutation sector:

- **Phase A (PWM leg):** high-side conducts for duty D; low-side conducts synchronously for (1−D).
- **Phase B (static leg):** low-side conducts 100% of the sector.
- **Phase C:** floating (BEMF sensing).

The phase current therefore always flows through exactly two FETs in series. The resulting conduction loss is **independent of duty cycle**:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)]$$

Dead-time intervals transfer some current to the body diode. This is accounted for in §2.

### 1.2 Per-FET loss averaged over a commutation cycle
Each FET is active in 2 of 6 sectors as the static leg and 2 of 6 as the PWM leg. Averaged over an electrical revolution:

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} = \frac{2\,I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)}{6}$$

However, the **instantaneous** sector loss on one FET, I²R_ds, sets the junction ripple at low eRPM. Use that value for the worst case: stalled or locked rotor, prop strike, or punch-out from hover.

### 1.3 Electrothermal fixed point
R_ds depends on T_j, and T_j depends on loss. Solve iteratively:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_a + Z_{th}(t)\cdot P\!\left(R_{ds}(T_j^{(k)})\right)$$

The iteration converges at T_j ≈ 110 °C, the operating point at the end of a 1 s burst (see §3):

- R_ds(110 °C) = 1.2 × (1 + 0.005 × 85) = **1.71 mΩ**
- P_cond,motor = 2 × 80² × 1.71×10⁻³ = **21.9 W**
- Instantaneous worst FET (static leg) = 80² × 1.71 mΩ = **10.9 W**
- Revolution-averaged per FET = **3.65 W**

### 1.4 Copper conduction
This loss is not negligible. Assume 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner layers, phase pads to motor tabs, with an effective path of about 0.35 mΩ per motor:

$$P_{Cu} = I_{ph}^2 R_{Cu}\,[1+0.00393(T-25)] \approx 80^2 \times 0.35\text{ mΩ} \times 1.3 = \mathbf{2.9\ W/motor}$$

Add the shared battery-input path: about 0.2 mΩ at 4 × 80 × (V_ph/V_bus)·D input current. For example, at ~150 A battery current this is **4.5 W**.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. That gives one hard-switched transition pair per period, with the high-side commutating against the low-side.

### 2.1 V-I overlap (hard switching)
$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 50\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{2.42\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction
$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I_{ph}\, (2t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery
Recovery energy is dissipated mainly in the turning-on high-side FET.
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 80\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.10\ W}$$

### 2.4 Output capacitance
$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 45\text{nC}\times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.054\ W}$$

This uses the charge-based approximation. Use E_oss from the datasheet if it is available.
