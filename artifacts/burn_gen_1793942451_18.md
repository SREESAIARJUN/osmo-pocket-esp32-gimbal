# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC: Systems Architecture Package (Job #20)

## 0. Scope and Feasibility Constraints

I don't have prior context for Job #20, so the assumptions below are stated explicitly. Every equation is parameterized, so you can substitute real datasheet values.

**Three architectural facts drive the rest of the document:**

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each motor bridge is commutated by a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, typically one MCU per channel (STM32G071, AT32F421 class). The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bi-directional DShot600 to those MCUs. The 48 kHz figure is therefore the ESC firmware's PWM frequency, not an H743 timer.
2. **"80A burst" means per channel, not simultaneous.** Four channels at 80 A simultaneously produce about 70 W of semiconductor loss plus bus losses. No nano-sized board can absorb that for more than a few seconds (see §3.4).
3. **The form factor must grow.** 80 A burst on a 20×20 mm board is not physically credible. I assume a 30.5×30.5 mm mount pattern on a roughly 38×38 mm ESC board, with the H743 FC on a separate stacked board. If Job #20 truly requires 20×20 mm, derate to about 35–40 A burst.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Path in 6-Step Commutation with Synchronous Rectification

With complementary PWM, the motor current always flows through exactly two MOSFETs in series: one in each of the two active half-bridges.

- **ON state:** HS(x) → winding → LS(y).
- **OFF state:** LS(x) (synchronous freewheel) → winding → LS(y).

The total conduction loss per channel is independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I^2\, R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Per-Device RMS Current

Each leg is active for 2/3 of the electrical period. The current is split between HS and LS according to duty cycle D:

$$I_{HS,rms} = I\sqrt{\tfrac{D}{3}}, \qquad I_{LS,rms} = I\sqrt{\tfrac{2-D}{3}}$$

The LS device carries more because it also serves as the static low-side switch.

Averaged over the six devices, I_rms ≈ I/√3 = **46.2 A per FET**.

Check: 6 × (I²/3) × R = 2I²R ✓

### 1.3 Temperature-Dependent Resistance (Iterative Solution)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

| Iteration | Assumed T_j | R_ds (mΩ) | P_cond,ch (W) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 25 °C | 0.900 | 11.52 |
| 1 | 100 °C | 1.204 | 15.41 |
| 2 (converged) | 105 °C | 1.224 | **15.67** |

**Worst-case device: LS at D → 0.**

- I_rms = 80·√(2/3) = 65.3 A
- P = 65.3² × 1.224 mΩ = **5.22 W**

This device sets the thermal limit.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In 6-step commutation, only one leg per channel hard-switches at f_sw at any given time. The commutation events themselves (about 6 × f_elec) are negligible compared to 48 kHz.

### 2.1 V–I Overlap Loss

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.935\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.307\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse Recovery Loss

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$

### 2.4 Output Capacitance Loss

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \times 2 = 60\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.073\ W}$$
