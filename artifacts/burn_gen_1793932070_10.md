## 0. Architecture and Scope Corrections

Three points need to be settled before the math, because they change the results.

1. **The H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC controller.** It generates bidirectional DShot600 and decodes eRPM. Commutation, 48 kHz PWM and current sensing run on four separate ESC MCUs, one per motor. Typical choices are AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32. No production 4-in-1 runs FOC/6-step for four motors from the FC's H743, and ArduPilot does not support that. This document models that split.

2. **"80A burst" is ambiguous, so both cases are computed.**
   - **Case A:** 80 A per motor channel (320 A total).
   - **Case B:** 80 A total (20 A per channel).

   Losses scale with I² for conduction and with I for switching, so the model is parametric.

3. **Nano form factor (20×20 mm) at 80 A per channel cannot be sustained.** Section 3 shows it is transient-limited to roughly 1–2 s from a warm start. That is a valid burst rating, but the spec sheet must state the duration.

## 2. Loss Model (Per Motor Channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

With 6-step trapezoidal commutation and synchronous rectification, current always flows through exactly two MOSFET positions in series:

- one high-side position, which is either the switching FET or the low-side FET on its complementary interval, and
- one low-side position on the opposing phase.

During PWM off-time, the switching leg's synchronous FET carries the freewheel current instead of the active FET. The series path is therefore always 2 × R_DS(on), independent of duty cycle.

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot \left(2R_{DS(on),hot} + R_{cu}\right)$$

The phase current is approximately flat-topped during the 120° conduction interval, so the RMS current in the conducting path is approximately I_DC.

**Case A (80 A per channel):**

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \times (2 \times 1.5 + 0.3)\times10^{-3} = 6400 \times 3.3\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{21.1\ W}$$

- MOSFET share: 19.2 W. Copper share: 1.9 W.
- Per-FET average (6 FETs share by rotation): 19.2 / 6 ≈ **3.2 W**.
- Instantaneous dissipation while a FET conducts: 80² × 1.5 mΩ = 9.6 W.

**Case B (20 A per channel):** P_cond = 400 × 3.3 mΩ = **1.32 W**.
