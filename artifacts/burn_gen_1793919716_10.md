## 0. Feasibility Constraints

These constraints shape the rest of the design and should be resolved first.

1. **"Nano" vs. 80 A burst.** A 20×20 mm 4-in-1 cannot survive 4 × 80 A for any useful duration. The board's copper mass, FET footprint area and connector cross-section are all too small. This document assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm ESC board stacked under a 20×20 or 30.5 FC**. The model in §3 shows that even this size only supports roughly **2–4 s** of simultaneous 4 × 80 A.
2. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** A 4-in-1 ESC uses four dedicated ESC MCUs (e.g. AT32F421 running AM32, or the equivalent for BLHeli_32/Bluejay-class firmware). The H743 sends DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry. The netlist is partitioned this way.
3. **Assumed parameters.** All component values below are **representative placeholders**. Replace them with your selected FET's datasheet values (R_DS(on)(T), Q_g, Q_rr, C_oss, R_θJC) before freezing the design. The equations are exact for the stated topology; the numbers are only as good as the inputs.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right]$$

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

During each 60° sector, current I flows through two phases. One leg is PWM'd: the high-side conducts for D and the complementary low-side for (1−D). The return leg's low-side is held on. The current path therefore always contains **exactly two conducting FETs** (ignoring dead time):

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Including inductor ripple ΔI (triangular):

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

At 48 kHz with typical 5" to 7" motor inductance, ΔI/I is small at 80 A, so ripple adds less than 1 %.

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution (worst case)

Over one electrical revolution, each switch position is active for 120°, i.e. 1/3 of the time:

- **PWM'd high-side FET:** $P = \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I^2 R_{DS}$ plus its share of switching loss
- **Synchronous low-side (same leg):** $P = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)\, I^2 R_{DS}$
- **Static low-side (return leg):** $P = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{DS}$

The hottest device is the **PWM'd high-side near D → 1**, or the return-leg low-side, depending on the commutation scheme. Use $P_{FET,max} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}I^2R_{DS} + P_{sw}$.

At short bursts below one thermal time constant of the FET die (≈ms), the *instantaneous* loss matters more. Instantaneously, the two conducting FETs each dissipate $I^2 R_{DS}$.

### 1.4 Numbers (T_j = 100 °C)

$$R_{DS}(100) = 0.70 \times 1.375 = 0.9625\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.9625\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{12.32\ W}$$

### 1.5 Copper conduction (often ignored, and significant)

The phase path runs from the battery pad, through the bus plane, FET, and phase pour, to the motor pad. Approximate it as an effective length l = 25 mm, width w = 6 mm, on 4 parallel 2 oz layers (t = 70 µm), with ρ_Cu(80 °C) ≈ 2.0×10⁻⁸ Ω·m:

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho\, l}{w\, t\, n} = \frac{2.0\times10^{-8}\times0.025}{0.006\times70\times10^{-6}\times4} \approx 0.30\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.30\times10^{-3} \approx \mathbf{1.9\ W\ per\ ESC}$$

The shared battery bus carries the sum of all four channels' DC current, so its loss scales with $(\sum I_{DC})^2$. Budget a further **3–5 W** for the bus and XT60 pads at full burst.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg is hard-switched per PWM period, so all terms are per ESC.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\, I\,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}$$

$$= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\times10^{-9}\times48\,000 = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$
