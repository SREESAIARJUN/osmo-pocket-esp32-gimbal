## 1. FOC Current Loop (20 kHz)

### 1.1 Transforms

Electrical angle from the mechanical encoder angle:

$$\theta_e = p\,(\theta_m - \theta_{\text{offset}}) \bmod 2\pi$$

**Clarke transform** (amplitude-invariant, with i_a + i_b + i_c = 0):

$$i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \frac{i_a + 2 i_b}{\sqrt3}$$

**Park transform:**

$$\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}$$

**Inverse Park transform:**

$$v_\alpha = v_d\cos\theta_e - v_q\sin\theta_e,\qquad v_\beta = v_d\sin\theta_e + v_q\cos\theta_e$$

### 1.2 Plant Model (SPM, L_d = L_q = L)

$$v_d = R i_d + L\dot i_d - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad v_q = R i_q + L\dot i_q + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e\lambda_m$$

Electromagnetic torque:

$$\tau_e = \tfrac32 p \lambda_m i_q = K_t\, i_q$$

### 1.3 Current PI with Decoupling and Back-EMF Feedforward

The controller adds feedforward terms that cancel the cross-coupling and back-EMF:

$$v_d^* = \text{PI}_d(i_d^*-i_d) - \hat\omega_e L\, i_q$$

$$v_q^* = \text{PI}_q(i_q^*-i_q) + \hat\omega_e (L i_d + \lambda_m)$$

**Gain selection by pole-zero cancellation.** With decoupling, each axis reduces to the plant 1/(Ls + R). Placing the PI zero at R/L gives a closed loop of ω_cc/(s + ω_cc):

$$K_{p,i} = L\,\omega_{cc},\qquad K_{i,i} = R\,\omega_{cc}$$

Choose ω_cc ≈ 2π·1.5 kHz, which is below f_PWM/10 and accounts for the 1.5-sample computation and PWM delay.

**Discretization** (backward Euler, T_s = 1/f_PWM):

$$x_k = x_{k-1} + K_i T_s e_k,\qquad u_k = K_p e_k + x_k$$

**Voltage limiting and anti-windup.** The output is limited to a circular vector:

$$\sqrt{v_d^2+v_q^2} \le \frac{V_{dc}}{\sqrt3}$$

The limit is enforced with d-axis priority, so v_q receives whatever voltage is left. Anti-windup uses back-calculation:

$$x_k \mathrel{+}= K_{aw}(u_{\text{sat}}-u)$$

with K_aw = 1/K_p.

### 1.4 SVPWM (Min-Max Zero-Sequence Injection)

$$v_{a,b,c} = \text{invClarke}(v_\alpha, v_\beta),\qquad v_0 = -\tfrac12\left(\max(v_{abc}) + \min(v_{abc})\right)$$

$$d_x = \tfrac12 + \frac{v_x + v_0}{V_{dc}}$$

This is mathematically equivalent to sector-based SVPWM and gives a linear range up to V_dc/√3.

## 2. Cascaded Angle and Rate Control

### 2.1 Loop Structure

```
θ* ─►(+)─► P_θ ─(+ω_ff)─► ω* ─►(+)─► PI_ω ─(+τ_ff)─► i_q* ─► Current PI ─► SVPWM
      ▲ −                          ▲ −                  ▲ +i_cog
   θ̂ (fused IMU attitude      ω̂ (filtered gyro,       anti-cogging
    projected on axis)          projected on axis)
```

**Why the gyro, not the encoder derivative, closes the rate loop.** A stabilizing gimbal must hold the *inertial* attitude of the camera. The gyro measures this directly with roughly 0.5 ms latency and no quantization noise. The encoder is still needed for:

- θ_e (commutation),
- the cogging phase,
- joint-angle limits,
- the frame transform below.

**Axis projection.** With gyro rates expressed in the camera frame, the rate of each joint is obtained from the inverse kinematic Jacobian. For a yaw–roll–pitch chain (outer to inner), with roll joint angle φ and pitch joint angle θ:

$$\begin{bmatrix}\dot\psi_j\\ \dot\phi_j\\ \dot\theta_j\end{bmatrix} = J^{-1}(\phi,\theta)\,\boldsymbol\omega_{cam}$$

This is computed at 1 kHz from encoder angles. For small roll angles, J⁻¹ ≈ I, but use the full form for a pan gimbal.
