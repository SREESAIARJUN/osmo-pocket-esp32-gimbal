# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #15): Thermal, Switching and DShot Design Model

Two points shape this design and need to be clear first:

1. **In a 4-in-1 ESC, the H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** Each motor's bridge is commutated by its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, typically an STM32G071 or AT32F421. The H743 sends the bi-directional DShot600 commands and receives the telemetry. This model treats the ESC power stage and the FC timer system as two coupled subsystems.
2. **80 A per motor on a 20×20 mm "nano" board fails thermally beyond a short burst.** The math below shows this, and it gives the burst duration limit as a design output rather than assuming the requirement can be met.

All device parameters are stated assumptions. Replace them with datasheet values for your chosen MOSFET and gate driver before sign-off.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)] = 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega\,[1+0.0045\cdot75] = 1.605\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 2.2 Six-step synchronous commutation topology

During each 60° sector, two phases conduct:

- **PWM'd phase:** the high-side (HS) FET conducts for duty D. The low-side (LS) FET conducts for 1 − D (synchronous rectification, ignoring dead time).
- **Return phase:** the LS FET conducts continuously.

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{ph}^2 R_{DS} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

This result holds regardless of duty, which matters. At low throttle with high current, for example a prop strike or locked rotor, conduction loss is just as high as at full throttle.

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.605\times10^{-3} = 20.5\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Worst single FET

The return-phase LS FET carries the full current for the whole sector:

$$P_{FET,max} = 80^2 \cdot 1.605\,\text{m}\Omega = 10.3\ \text{W}$$

$$\Delta T_{JC} = 10.3 \cdot 1.5 = 15.4\ \text{K}$$

Junction-to-case temperature rise is acceptable. The bottleneck is board-level heat removal, covered in Section 4.

### 2.4 Self-consistent junction temperature

Iterate the following until T_j converges:

$$T_j = T_{board} + P(T_j)\,R_{\theta JC}$$

Because P depends on T_j through R_DS(on), thermal runaway sets in when:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\,R_\theta \geq 1$$

Here ∂P/∂T = 80² · 1.2 mΩ · 0.0045 = 0.035 W/K, and 0.035 × 1.5 ≪ 1, so the FET is locally stable.

## 3. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd half-bridge switches. In it, one FET hard-switches and the other soft-switches through its body diode.

**Turn-on/off V-I overlap (hard-switched FET)**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot20\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = 0.968\ \text{W}$$

**Output capacitance**

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

**Reverse recovery**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

**Dead-time body diode conduction (two edges per cycle)**

$$P_{dt} = V_f I_{ph}\cdot 2t_{dt}\cdot f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = 0.614\ \text{W}$$

**Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistor)**

$$P_g = 6\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 6\cdot30\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k} = 0.086\ \text{W}$$

### 3.1 Loss summary at 80 A

| Loss term | Per motor (W) | 4 motors (W) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 20.5 | 82.2 |
| Switching overlap | 0.97 | 3.87 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 | 2.46 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.08 | 0.34 |
| Gate drive | 0.09 | 0.34 |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, if present) | 1.6 | 6.4 |
| **Total** | **~23.9** | **~95.6** |

Conduction dominates at about 86% of the total. 48 kHz is therefore not the thermal problem. Raising it to 96 kHz adds only about 6 W across all four motors. The lever that matters is R_DS(on), meaning FET choice and paralleling.

## 4. Board-Level Thermal Model

### 4.1 Lumped thermal capacitance (20×20 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz Cu)

| Element | Mass | c_p (J/g·K) | C_th (J/K) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copper: 6 × 70 µm × 4 cm² | 1.51 g | 0.385 | 0.58 |
| FR4: ~1.2 mm × 4 cm² | 0.89 g | 1.1 | 0.98 |
| 24 PDFN FETs (~0.05 g each) | 1.2 g | 0.7 | 0.84 |
| **Total** | | | **~2.4 J/K** |

### 4.2 Steady-state dissipation capability

The ESC board is assumed to run in prop-wash airflow at roughly 10 m/s, giving an effective h ≈ 60 W/m²K over about 8 cm² of exposed area:

$$R_{\theta,BA} \approx \frac{1}{hA} = \frac{1}{60\cdot 8\times10^{-4}} \approx 21\ \text{K/W}$$

$$P_{cont,max} = \frac{T_{board,max}-T_{amb}}{R_{\theta,BA}} = \frac{(105-35)\,\text{K}}{21\,\text{K/W}} \approx 3.3\ \text{W}$$

### 4.3 Burst duration limit (adiabatic first-order)

Time constant:

$$\tau = R_{\theta}C_{th} = 21\cdot2.4 \approx 50\ \text{s}$$

Since the burst is much shorter than τ, the board heats almost adiabatically:

$$\frac{dT}{dt} \approx \frac{P_{tot}}{C_{th}} = \frac{95.6}{2.4} \approx 40\ \text{K/s}$$

$$t_{burst,max} = \frac{T_{board,max}-T_{start}}{dT/dt} = \frac{105-50}{40} \approx 1.4\ \text{s}\quad(\text{all four motors at 80 A})$$

### 4.4 Design conclusions

- **Burst rating:** 80 A per motor on a 20×20 board is credible only as a burst of about 1 s on all motors, or about 5 s on one motor. Spec it that way.
- **Continuous rating:** realistically 25–35 A per motor. At 30 A, P_tot ≈ 4 × 3.2 ≈ 13 W, which is still above P_cont. Sustained operation relies on duty cycling in flight.
- **Paths to genuine 80 A:**
  - Move to 30×30 mm with 0.6 mΩ FETs. Conduction loss scales with R_DS(on), roughly halving it.
  - Add a bonded aluminium heat spreader, giving about 3× C_th and about 2× hA.
- **Firmware protections are mandatory:**
  - ESC-side current limit (AM32 `CURRENT_LIMIT`).
  - Temperature limit around 100 °C on ESC NTC telemetry.
