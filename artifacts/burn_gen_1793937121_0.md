## 0. Architecture Interpretation and Feasibility Flags

### 0.1 Architecture

An STM32H743 cannot practically commutate 12 half-bridges at 48 kHz while also running ArduPilot. This design therefore uses the standard all-in-one (AIO) partition:

| Block | Device | Firmware |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100), 480 MHz | ArduPilot |
| ESC controllers (×4) | AT32F421K8U7 (QFN32), one per motor | AM32 (supports bidir DShot and 48 kHz PWM) |
| Gate drivers (×4) | FD6288Q-class 3-phase half-bridge driver | n/a |
| Power stage | 24 × 40 V N-MOSFET, PDFN 3.3×3.3 | n/a |

### 0.2 Feasibility flags (read before layout)

These items fall out of the math below and must be resolved with the program owner.

1. **"80 A burst" must be defined as per-motor, time-limited.** At 80 A on all four motors simultaneously:
   - Battery current is 320 A.
   - That exceeds any connector usable on a nano frame (XT30 ≈ 30–40 A continuous; XT60 ≈ 60 A continuous).
   - Board dissipation is ≈100 W into ≈1.6 J/K of thermal mass, i.e. about 60 K/s of heating.
2. **Recommended rating** for a 20×20 / 25.5×25.5 board (Sections 2–3 derive these):
   - ≈25–30 A continuous per motor, with airflow.
   - 80 A for ≤1 s on one motor, or ≤0.5 s on all four.
   - If a genuine multi-second 80 A burst is a requirement, move to a 30.5×30.5 board with paralleled FETs.
