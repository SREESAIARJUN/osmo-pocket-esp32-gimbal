# STM32H743 Nano AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #64)
## Power-Stage Thermal Model, Switching Losses, and ArduPilot Bi-Directional DShot600 Allocation

---

## 0. Feasibility Findings

These findings change the design, so they come before the derivations.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot cannot run six-step or FOC commutation for 12 half-bridges. The architecture is therefore:
   - The H743 runs the flight controller and sends bi-directional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs.
   - The ESC MCUs (e.g. AT32F421 running AM32, or STM32G071) drive the gate drivers, which drive 24 MOSFETs.
   - The netlist below reflects this split.
2. **An 80 A per-motor burst on a 20×20 mm "nano" board is a transient rating, not a thermal rating.** The model in §2 shows about 85 W of dissipation at full 4×80 A burst. That is sustainable for roughly 1.5–3 s from a warm start.
   - The honest continuous rating is about 25–30 A per motor with prop-wash airflow.
   - If the spec truly requires 80 A for more than 5 s, move to a 30×30 mm form factor. Section 2.6 shows the comparison.
3. **All parameter values are representative.** Replace them with datasheet values from your selected MOSFETs before sign-off. Every equation is parametric so this is straightforward.

## 2. Mathematical Model

### 2.1 Conduction Losses (Six-Step with Synchronous Rectification)

During any commutation sector, two phases are active:

- **High-side leg:** the HS FET conducts for duty D. Its complementary LS FET conducts for (1−D).
- **Return leg:** the LS FET conducts continuously.

The sector's conduction loss is:

$$P_{cond}(T_j)=I^2R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D+(1-D)] + I^2R_{ds}(T_j) = 2I^2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This loss is independent of D because synchronous rectification removes the duty dependence. On-resistance rises with temperature:

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

**At T_j = 125 °C:** R_ds = 1.45 mΩ

$$P_{cond,FET}=2(80)^2(1.45\times10^{-3}) = 18.6\ \text{W per motor}$$

Copper and shunt losses must also be counted, because they heat the same board:

$$P_{Cu}=I^2(R_{trace}+R_{shunt}) = 6400(0.65\times10^{-3}) = 4.2\ \text{W per motor}$$

Copper resistance also rises about 0.39 %/K. At a 100 °C board temperature, P_Cu ≈ 5.3 W.

Conduction loss scales with I². At 40 A it falls to one quarter: 4.6 W in the FETs and 1.3 W in the copper.

### 2.2 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing HS FET hard-switches. The LS commutation is soft (ZVS), apart from dead-time effects.

**V–I overlap loss:**
$$P_{sw}=\tfrac12V_{bus}I(t_r+t_f)f_{sw}=\tfrac12(25.2)(80)(20\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz})=0.97\ \text{W}$$

Note: at 80 A the current-dependent Miller plateau stretches t_r and t_f. A realistic range is 15–25 ns each, giving P_sw ≈ 1.5–2.4 W. **Use 2.0 W in the budget.**

**Reverse recovery:**
$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}=30\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k}=0.036\ \text{W}$$

At high di/dt, Q_rr can double, giving about 0.07 W.

**Output capacitance:**
$$P_{oss}=\tfrac12Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}=0.024\ \text{W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two edges per period):
$$P_{dt}=V_{SD}\,I\,(2t_{dead})f_{sw}=0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{n}\cdot48\text{k}=0.61\ \text{W}$$

**Gate drive** (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g, not in the FET):
$$P_g=Q_gV_{drv}f_{sw}\times2=50\text{n}\cdot12\cdot48\text{k}\cdot2=0.058\ \text{W}$$

**Switching subtotal per motor:** about 2.7 W at 80 A.

### 2.3 Per-Motor and Board Totals at Full Burst

| Loss term | Per motor (W) | ×4 motors (W) |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (T_j = 125 °C) | 18.6 | 74.4 |
| Copper and shunt (100 °C) | 5.3 | 21.2 |
| Switching + Q_rr + Q_oss | 2.1 | 8.4 |
| Dead time | 0.6 | 2.4 |
| Gate drive | 0.06 | 0.24 |
| **Total** | **≈ 26.7** | **≈ 107** |

Two refinements bring this closer to reality:

- Conduction loss uses RMS current, not peak. With 30–40 % current ripple at 48 kHz on low-inductance motors, I_rms ≈ I_avg·√(1 + r²/12), which adds about 1 %.
- Real 4-motor bursts are rarely all at 80 A simultaneously.

The **design case of about 85 W** therefore assumes 4×80 A at T_j ≈ 100 °C.

Electrical efficiency at full burst: P_out ≈ 4 × 80 A × 25.2 V × D. At D ≈ 0.9 this is about 7.3 kW, so η ≈ 98.8 %. **High efficiency does not prevent overheating: the problem is power density, not efficiency.**

### 2.4 Steady-State Thermal Network

The board-to-ambient path is lumped:

$$T_j = T_a + P_{tot}\,R_{\theta,BA} + P_{FET}\,(R_{\theta JC}+R_{\theta,CB})$$

For a 20×20 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz board (4 cm² per side, 8 cm² total):

$$R_{\theta,BA}\approx\frac{1}{h\,A}$$

| Condition | h (W/m²K) | R_θ,BA (K/W) |
|---|---|---|
| Still air | ≈ 15 | ≈ 80 |
| Prop wash, 10–15 m/s | ≈ 150–250 | ≈ 5–8 |

**Result:** with T_a = 30 °C and prop wash (R ≈ 6 K/W), the board can sustain ΔT = 70 K. That allows P_tot ≈ 12 W, which is **≈ 25–30 A per motor continuous** (all four motors).

### 2.5 Transient (Burst) Model

#### Board level (lumped capacitance)

| Mass | Heat capacity |
|---|---|
| FR4, 20×20×1.6 mm (≈ 1.2 g) | ≈ 1.1 J/K |
| Copper, 8 × 2 oz layers at about 70 % fill (≈ 1.4 g) | ≈ 0.55 J/K |
| 24 FETs, caps, solder | ≈ 0.6 J/K |
| **Total C_th** | **≈ 2.3 J/K** (≈ 4 J/K if conformal-coupled to frame or heat spreader) |

The governing equation and time constant are:

$$C_{th}\frac{dT_b}{dt}=P_{tot}-\frac{T_b-T_a}{R_{\theta,BA}},\qquad \tau=R C\approx 6\times2.3=14\ \text{s}$$

$$t_{burst}=-\tau\ln\!\left(1-\frac{(T_{lim}-T_0)}{P R}\right)$$

**Example:** T_0 = 60 °C (warm hover), T_lim = 110 °C, P = 85 W, R = 6 K/W.

$$t = -14\ln(1-50/510) \approx \mathbf{1.45\ s}$$

From a cold start (T_0 = 35 °C): t ≈ 2.2 s.

#### Junction rise above case

This uses a 4-term Foster model. Fit the R_i and τ_i values to the datasheet Z_θJC curve.

$$Z_{\theta JC}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{4}R_i\left(1-e^{-t/\tau_i}\right)$$

Typical DFN3.3 values: R = {0.15, 0.45, 0.6, 0.3} K/W, τ = {20 µs, 0.4 ms, 4 ms, 40 ms}. At t > 0.2 s, Z ≈ R_θJC = 1.5 K/W.

Per-FET peak dissipation occurs on the always-on LS FET of the return leg: 80² × 1.45 mΩ = 9.3 W, so ΔT_jc ≈ 14 K on top of the board temper
