# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #65): Architecture, Loss Model, Thermal Budget, and DShot Allocation

## 0. Architecture Corrections

Two issues in the brief change the design, so they come first.

**A. The H743 cannot commutate the motors itself in this design.** ArduPilot runs on the H743 and sends DShot to ESCs. Bidirectional DShot (eRPM telemetry) requires separate ESC firmware (AM32 or Bluejay) on ESC microcontrollers. The correct architecture is:

```
H743 (ArduPilot) ──4× bidirectional DShot600──► 4× AT32F421 (AM32) ──► 4× 3-phase bridges (24 FETs)
```

**B. 80 A per motor on a "nano" (20×20 mm) board is not physically or thermally feasible.**

- **Board area:** 24 MOSFETs in DFN5×6 occupy about 720 mm², which exceeds the 400 mm² board. Even DFN3.3×3.3 parts need about 262 mm² before the MCUs, drivers, and capacitors.
- **Thermal:** Section 3 shows about 85 W of FET dissipation at 4×80 A. A 20×20 board cannot reject that.
- **Recommendation:** Build a stacked set on 30.5×30.5 mm:
  - an FC board (H743), and
  - an ESC board using DFN5×6 FETs, rated **55 A continuous / 80 A burst for ≤ 2 s**.

  All math below targets that configuration.

**Design assumptions (replace with datasheet values on part selection):**

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A burst)

With synchronous rectification, phase current always flows through exactly one high-side-equivalent and one low-side-equivalent FET path, whatever the duty cycle. The duty cycle only changes which FET in each leg carries the current.

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot R_{ds(on)}(T_j) \cdot 2$$

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0067/^\circ C \Rightarrow 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega\ @\ 100^\circ C$$

**Ripple correction.** Ripple adds to the RMS current:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{DC}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

With an assumed motor inductance L_ph = 15 µH (line-to-line 30 µH) at D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus} D (1-D)}{L f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \cdot 0.25}{30\mu H \cdot 48k} = 4.4\ \text{A}$$

The ripple term (4.4²/12 = 1.6 A²) is negligible against 6400 A².

**At 80 A:**

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \cdot 1.5\text{m}\Omega \cdot 2 = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ motor}$$

**Per-FET instantaneous dissipation** (this sets the junction transient):

$$P_{FET,peak} = 80^2 \cdot 1.5\text{m}\Omega = 9.6\ \text{W}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg per motor is hard-switching at any time (the PWM leg in 6-step).

| Term | Equation | Value at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f = ½·25.2·80·30 ns·48 k | **1.45 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2t_dt·f = 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k | **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f = 40 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.05 W |
| Coss charge | ½·Q_oss·V·f | 0.03 W |
| Gate drive (mostly in driver) | Q_g·V_gs·f·2 = 50 nC·10·48 k·2 | 0.05 W |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.2 W per motor** |

**Total per motor at 80 A: 19.2 + 2.2 ≈ 21.4 W.**

**Total for four motors: ≈ 85.6 W.**

**Effect of switching frequency.** Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it would be about 1.1 W per motor.

48 kHz is justified for low-inductance racing motors because it reduces current ripple and audible noise. At 80 A, however, conduction loss dominates (90 %). Lowering f_sw buys little; lowering R_ds(on) is the effective lever.

**Bulk-capacitor ripple (per ESC channel):**

$$I_{C,rms} \approx I\sqrt{D(1-D)} = 80 \cdot 0.5 = 40\ \text{A}_{rms}\ (\text{worst case at } D = 0.5)$$

- No single capacitor handles this. Distribute it across low-ESR MLCCs placed near each half-bridge: 10× 10 µF/50 V X7R 1210 per motor. The derated capacitance is about 2–3 µF each because of DC bias.
- Add a 1000 µF/35 V low-ESR electrolytic at the battery pads, external on the leads.
- Capacitor ESR loss: 40² × (ESR_eff ≈ 0.5 mΩ) ≈ 0.8 W per motor.
