# STM32H743 AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC) – Job #26 Engineering Model

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and a Feasibility Warning

The model is parameterized so you can substitute your actual MOSFET datasheet values. **Every number marked [A] is an assumption. Replace it before sign-off.**

### 0.1 Architecture clarification

The STM32H743 does **not** drive the gates. A production AIO has two layers:

- **FC layer:** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot. It emits bi-directional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs.
- **ESC layer:** There are four ESC MCUs (typically AT32F421 or STM32G071) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Each drives a 3-phase gate driver and six MOSFETs.

The DMA analysis (Section 4) applies to the H743. The loss and thermal analysis (Sections 1–3) applies to the ESC power stage.

### 0.2 Feasibility warning (read first)

"80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board needs interpretation:

| Interpretation | Battery current (all 4 at burst) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 80 A per motor channel | 320 A | Not physically credible on 20×20 mm. Connector, pads, and copper cannot carry it. Thermal runaway occurs in under 1 s (see 3.3). |
| 80 A total (≈20 A/ch) | 80 A | Credible for 1–3 s bursts. |
| 80 A per channel, single-motor transient (ms) | – | Credible only as a desync or stall spike rating. |

I derive the general model, then evaluate both the **80 A/channel** case (to show why it fails) and the **20 A/channel** case (the design point).

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

With 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation and synchronous complementary PWM, exactly one high-side and one low-side path conduct phase current at any instant:

- **During PWM on-time (duty D):** HS of phase A and LS of phase B are on.
- **During off-time (1−D):** LS of phase A (synchronous freewheel) and LS of phase B are on.

Either way, current flows through **two** MOSFET channels in series. So channel conduction loss is independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2 \cdot R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence (self-consistent)

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds(on),25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At 125 °C this gives k_T = 1.5. Because T_j itself depends on P, iterate. Note the runaway condition below.

$$T_j = T_a + P(T_j)\cdot R_{\theta JA} \;\Rightarrow\; \text{stable only if } \; \alpha \, P_{cond,25}\, R_{\theta JA} < 1$$

### 1.3 PCB copper loss (frequently forgotten, significant here)

For 2 oz (70 µm) copper, R_□ ≈ 0.245 mΩ/□ at 25 °C. A phase trace 4 mm wide and 8 mm long is 2 squares. Assume 3 parallel layers:

$$R_{Cu} \approx \frac{2 \times 0.245}{3} = 0.16\ \text{m}\Omega \text{ per segment}$$

Count about 3 segments in the path (VBAT → HS, HS → phase pad, LS → GND), giving ≈0.5 mΩ. That is comparable to a FET.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switched leg (HS of the active phase, with its LS as synchronous rectifier) contributes hard-switching loss.

### 2.1 Transition times from gate charge

$$I_g = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}{R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}+R_{drv}} = \frac{10-3.5}{4.7+1.0+0.5} \approx 1.05\ \text{A}$$

$$t_r \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g} = \frac{10\ \text{nC}}{1.05\ \text{A}} \approx 9.5\ \text{ns} \;\Rightarrow\; t_r+t_f \approx 20\ \text{ns}$$

### 2.2 Loss terms

| Term | Formula | 80 A | 20 A |
|---|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw | 0.968 W | 0.242 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 0.060 W | 0.060 W* |
| Output capacitance | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw | 0.024 W |
