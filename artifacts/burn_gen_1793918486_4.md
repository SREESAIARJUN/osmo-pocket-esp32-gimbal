# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #10): Power-Stage Physics, Thermal Envelope & Bi-Directional DShot600 Architecture

## 0. Design Assumptions and Corrections

Several baseline decisions shape everything that follows:

| Item | Assumption / Decision | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Form factor | 30.5 × 30.5 mm stack (FC + ESC), ESC board 8-layer, 2 oz on L1/L8 and power planes | A true 20 × 20 board cannot even geometrically host 24 power FETs rated for 80 A |
| Bus voltage | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal | Worst-case switching loss at full charge |
| ESC control | **4 × STM32G071 (or AT32F421) running AM32**, one per channel | The H743 sends DShot only. ArduPilot does not perform BLDC commutation, and 48 kHz six-step PWM belongs on dedicated ESC MCUs. |
| Commutation | Trapezoidal six-step, synchronous complementary PWM, 48 kHz | AM32 default-class behaviour |
| MOSFET | 40 V N-ch, 3.3 × 3.3 DFN, R_DS(on) = 1.5 mΩ @ 25 °C (V_GS = 10 V), Q_g ≈ 40 nC, Q_rr ≈ 40 nC, Q_oss ≈ 50 nC, R_θJC ≈ 1.5 K/W | Representative of a 2025-era trench FET in this package |
| Gate driver | FD6288Q-class 3-phase bootstrap driver, 10–12 V gate rail | Common on 6S ESCs |
| "80 A burst" | Interpreted as **80 A per channel, transient** | The model below shows this is a seconds-scale rating, not continuous |

**Key finding up front:** with these parameters, a 30 × 30 board dissipates about 125 W at 4 × 80 A. That allows roughly **2 seconds of burst** from a warm start and roughly **20 A per channel continuous** in prop-wash cooling. Any "80 A" marketing claim must specify duration.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in six-step synchronous PWM
In each 60° commutation sector, two phases carry the motor current I:
- **PWM leg:** the high-side conducts for fraction D, and the synchronous low-side conducts for (1 − D) (dead time excluded).
- **Return leg:** the low-side is fully ON.

So at any instant, current flows through exactly **two FET channels in series**, independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I^2 \, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence
$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.0067\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

This gives about 1.5× at 100 °C. With R₂₅ = 1.5 mΩ:

| T_j (°C) | R_DS(on) (mΩ) | P_cond @ 80 A (W/ch) |
|---|---|---|
| 25 | 1.50 | 19.2 |
| 75 | 2.00 | 25.6 |
| 100 | 2.25 | 28.8 |
| 125 | 2.50 | 32.0 |

### 1.3 Additional resistive losses (often omitted, not negligible)
| Element | R (mΩ) | P @ 80 A, path factor |
|---|---|---|
| Phase copper + pad (2 oz, ~8 mm, 6 mm wide, both layers) | ~0.3 per phase | 2 × I²R = 3.8 W |
| Shunt (if used, 0.5 mΩ low-side, in path ~2/3 of the time) | 0.5 | ≈ 2.1 W |
| Solder joints / FET clip | ~0.1 per FET | 1.3 W |

These parasitics add about **5–7 W per channel**. For this reason, current sensing on the 4-in-1 should use a **single DC-bus shunt per board (or a Hall sensor)**, not per-phase shunts.

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution
Each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical period. Averaged per FET, at T_j = 100 °C:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \tfrac{1}{3}\, I^2 R_{DS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(6400)(2.25\times10^{-3}) = 4.8\ \text{W}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the **PWM leg** hard-switches. The return leg commutates at electrical frequency (≤ ~2 kHz), so its loss is negligible.

### 2.1 V-I overlap (hard-switched high side)
$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}\, V_{bus}\, I\, (t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}$$

With t_r + t_f = 40 ns (gate resistor about 4.7 Ω with a FD6288-class 1.5 A/1.8 A driver):

$$P_{VI} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = 1.94\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Body-diode reverse recovery (sync FET diode conducts in dead time)
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 40\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{ k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

Q_rr rises with di/dt and T_j, so derate it ×2 to get **0.10 W**.
