## 0. Architecture Findings

Three issues change how the rest of this document should be read.

| # | Finding | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | **The STM32H743 is the flight controller MCU. It does not commutate the motors.** A 4-in-1 ESC needs its own commutation MCUs (one per channel, or one quad-capable MCU) running AM32 or BLHeli_32, typically on STM32G071 or AT32F421 parts. ArduPilot on the H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to those MCUs. | The netlist in §5 splits the design into an FC domain and an ESC domain. |
| F2 | **80 A burst *per channel* is not achievable on a 20×20 mm "nano" form factor.** §2 to §3 show about 86 W of dissipation at full burst on all four channels. A nano board's thermal mass gives only about 2 s before the FETs reach their junction limits. Copper ampacity is also exceeded. | Two load cases are analyzed. **Case A** is 80 A per channel, reported to show it is infeasible. **Case B** is 80 A total, or 20 A per channel, which is a realistic nano rating. |
| F3 | **ArduPilot cannot do bidirectional DShot on complementary (CHxN) timer outputs.** Input capture of the eRPM telemetry needs the non-inverted channel pin. | Motor pins in §4 use TIM1_CH1 to CH4 (PE9, PE11, PE13, PE14). TIM8_CHxN pins are avoided. |

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel = one 3-phase bridge, 6 FETs)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

In 6-step commutation, current at any instant flows through **one high-side and one low-side FET in series**. The "off" interval of the PWM'd high side is carried by the synchronous low-side FET of the same leg, so the path resistance stays 2·R_DS(on) regardless of duty cycle D:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j)=R_{25}\,k_T
$$

The phase current is roughly DC within each 60° sector, so I_rms ≈ I.

Per-FET average: each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical cycle.

$$
\bar P_{cond,FET} = \frac{I^2 R_{DS(on)}}{3}
$$

### 2.2 Switching Loss (hard-switched edges)

Only the PWM'd leg switches hard, using a linear V-I overlap approximation:

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac12\, V_{bus}\, I\, (t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}
$$

### 2.3 Dead-Time (Body-Diode) Loss

$$
P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2t_{dt})\, f_{sw}
$$

### 2.4 Reverse-Recovery Loss

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw}
$$

### 2.5 Output-Capacitance Loss

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12\, C_{oss}\, V_{bus}^2\, f_{sw}
$$

### 2.6 Gate-Drive Loss (dissipated in driver and gate resistors, not in the FET)

For the two switching FETs of the active leg:

$$
P_{gate} = 2\, Q_g\, V_{GS}\, f_{sw}
$$

Observations:
- At 80 A, conduction is about 89 % of the loss. Raising f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz costs only about 1 W per channel at 80 A, while helping current ripple and acoustic noise. **R_DS(on) is the lever, not frequency.**
- At 20 A the split shifts. Switching-related terms become about 35 % of the loss, and 48 kHz becomes a meaningful trade-off.
