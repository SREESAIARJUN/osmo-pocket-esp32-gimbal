## 0. Feasibility Findings

These findings drive the rest of the document.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot is flight-control firmware. It emits DShot commands and consumes eRPM telemetry. The 48 kHz complementary PWM, dead-time and BEMF/zero-cross handling must run on dedicated ESC MCUs, one per motor. This design uses AT32F421 running AM32, which natively supports 48 kHz PWM and bidirectional DShot. The H743's role is DShot600 generation and capture.
2. **"80A burst" on a nano-size board is a short-duration rating, not a thermal steady state.** The model in §3 shows that one 80 A channel dissipates about 35 W. The board can reject only about 6 W total in prop-wash convection at ΔT = 60 K. All four channels at 80 A would heat the board at about 53 K/s, giving roughly a 1 s burst from a 40 °C start.
   - **Recommendation:** specify the rating as **80 A, 1 s, single channel**, with a **35–40 A continuous** class.
   - Alternatively, move to a 30.5 × 30.5 mm board with 5×6 mm FETs if 80 A for 5 s or more is a requirement.
3. **The supply is 6S maximum (25.2 V).** 40 V FETs give 1.59× headroom over V_bat,max. This is the minimum acceptable headroom given the switching overshoot computed in §2.6.

### 2.2 Transition times

The voltage-current overlap time is set by the charge that must be moved through the Miller plateau region:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}},\qquad t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,off}}$$

$$t_r \approx \frac{12\,\text{nC}}{0.6\,\text{A}} = 20\,\text{ns},\qquad t_f \approx \frac{12\,\text{nC}}{0.9\,\text{A}} \approx 13\,\text{ns}$$
