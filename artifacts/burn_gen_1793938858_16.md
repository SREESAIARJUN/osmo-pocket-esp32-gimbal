# STM32H743 AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC) – Architecture, Loss Model, DShot/DMA Plan

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Key Findings

**Two-processor architecture.** The STM32H743 cannot practically commutate 4 motors and run ArduPilot at the same time, and DShot only makes sense between two processors. The board is therefore defined as:

- **FC domain:** STM32H743 running ArduPilot. It emits bi-directional DShot600 on 4 timer channels.
- **ESC domain:** 4× AT32F421 (or STM32G071) running AM32. Each drives a 3-phase gate driver and 6 MOSFETs.

**Findings that change the design:**
1. **80 A per channel on a nano footprint is a burst rating of about 1 s, not a sustained rating.** The math in §3 shows this. I recommend publishing "80 A burst ≤1 s / 45 A continuous with airflow" or moving to a 30×30 mm board.
2. **Conduction loss dominates** (~27 W per channel at 80 A, hot). Switching loss at 48 kHz is about 2.2 W.
3. **DMA is not the constraint on the H7.** The DMAMUX lets any request route to any of the 16 streams. The constraint is **timer choice**, because bidirectional capture requires channel-DMA, not only UP-DMA.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

AM32 uses six-step commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. In any 60° sector, current flows through exactly **two conducting devices** in series:
- the PWM leg (high-side for duty D, sync low-side for 1–D), and
- the static low-side of the return leg.

The duty factor cancels for the PWM leg:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) = 2 I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

This must be solved self-consistently, because T_j depends on P, which depends on R(T_j).

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond per channel @80 A | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.30 mΩ | 16.6 W | 66.6 W |
| 100 °C | 1.76 mΩ | 22.5 W | 90.1 W |
| 125 °C | 1.91 mΩ | 24.5 W | 97.9 W |
| 125 °C + 0.25 mΩ PCB/solder per path | 2.16 mΩ eff. | 27.6 W | 110 W |

**Per-device distribution.** Each FET carries current about 1/3 of the electrical cycle, so the average per FET is about 27.6/6 ≈ 4.6 W.

**Ripple correction.** I_rms² = I_dc² + ΔI²/12. With motor L ≈ 15 µH:

$$\Delta I \approx \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L f_{sw}} \le \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{15\mu \times 48k} = 8.75\ A_{pp}$$

This adds less than 0.1 % and can be neglected.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per channel, only the PWM leg switches, with one hard-switched device per edge pair.

**Hard-switching V–I overlap:**
$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 1.45\ \text{W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction:**
$$P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

**Output capacitance (hard turn-on):**
$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.024\ \text{W}$$

**Gate drive** (2 FETs per cycle, at the driver supply rather than in the FETs):
$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\times40\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k} = 0.038\ \text{W}$$

**Total switching loss ≈ 2.17 W per channel (≈8.7 W for 4 channels).**

**Sensitivity.** P_VI scales linearly with t_r + t_f. If loop inductance pushes transitions to 25 ns each, P_VI rises to 2.4 W. Cutting dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns saves 0.3 W per channel. Both are layout and firmware levers, not silicon.

**Check on driver-limited transition time:**
$$t_{Miller} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}(R_{drv}+R_g)}{V_{drv}-V_{pl}} = \frac{6\text{n}\times5\Omega}{10-3} \approx 4.3\ \text{ns}$$

The intrinsic transition is about 4 ns. The 15 ns assumption is dominated by commutation-loop inductance, which is why the netlist below places decoupling directly at each half-bridge.
