# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #15): Power-Stage, Thermal, and Bidirectional-DShot Design Model

**Scope note.** I don't have Job #15's BOM, stack-up, or MOSFET selection. Every numeric input below is a stated assumption. The equations are general, so substitute your actual datasheet values and the results scale directly. Where the numbers say a requirement is not physically achievable, I state that plainly.

**Architecture clarification.** In a "nano" stack, the STM32H743 is the flight controller, running ArduPilot. The 4-in-1 ESC runs its own MCU (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 with AM32/BLHeli_32) at 48 kHz PWM. Any schematic that puts motor commutation on the H743 should be challenged; the model below assumes the conventional split.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Phase vs. battery current

In 6-step drive, two phases conduct at any instant. With duty D, the relationship is:

$$I_{phase} \approx \frac{I_{batt}}{D}$$

"80 A burst" usually means battery-side current at or near full throttle (D → 1), so I_phase ≈ 80 A.

**Partial-throttle trap.** At D = 0.7 with 56 A from the battery, phase current is already 80 A. Size for phase current, not battery current.

### 2.2 Per-channel conduction loss

With synchronous rectification, the PWM'd leg always has either its HS or its LS FET on. The commutated leg has its LS FET on. So two R_DS(on) are always in series with the phase current:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \cdot I_{phase}^2 \cdot R_{DS(on)} \cdot k_T$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega \cdot 1.5 = 2 \cdot 6400 \cdot 2.25\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{28.8\ W}$$

### 2.3 Per-FET distribution

Each switch conducts for 120° of the 360° electrical cycle, so the cycle-averaged loss per FET is:

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{P_{cond}}{6} \cdot 2 = \frac{28.8}{3} \approx 9.6\ \text{W while active} \rightarrow \text{avg} \approx 4.8\ \text{W}$$

Instantaneous dissipation in a conducting FET is I²R = 14.4 W. At typical eRPM (> 10 krpm electrical), the commutation period is far shorter than the die thermal time constant (~1–10 ms). The average value therefore governs junction rise, with ripple of a few °C.

### 2.4 Copper / trace loss (frequently ignored, significant at 80 A)

Assume a 2 oz (70 µm) inner+outer layer phase path, 10 mm long, 4 mm wide, two layers in parallel.

- One layer: R = ρL/(wt) = 1.72e-8 · 0.01 / (0.004 · 70e-6) = 0.61 mΩ
- Two layers in parallel: ≈ 0.31 mΩ, ×1.3 hot ≈ 0.40 mΩ
- Loss per phase path: 80² · 0.40 mΩ ≈ 2.6 W
- Two paths active: **≈ 5 W per channel**, plus the battery-pad bus

Use 3 oz copper or more on a 6–8 layer board, with heavy via stitching.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 3.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap (PWM'd high-side)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 3.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I \cdot 2 t_d \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 400\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

Reducing dead time from 200 ns to 100 ns halves this term. Do so only after verifying with a scope that no shoot-through occurs at temperature.

### 3.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

### 3.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \cdot 2 = 60\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} \approx \mathbf{0.07\ W}$$

### 3.5 Gate drive (dissipated in driver and R_g, not in the FET)

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \cdot N_{switching} = 50\text{n} \cdot 10 \cdot 48\text{k} \cdot 2 \approx \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

### 3.6 Frequency sensitivity

Switching-related loss is about 3.3 W at 48 kHz, which scales linearly with frequency:

| f_sw | Switching-related loss |
|---|---|
| 24 kHz | ≈ 1.65 W |
| 96 kHz | ≈ 6.6 W |

At 80 A, conduction dominates by roughly 9:1. That is why 48 kHz is a reasonable choice: it brings smoother low-throttle response for about 1.6 W extra over 24 kHz.

## 4. Loss Budget

| Loss term | Per channel (W) | 4-in-1 total (W) |
|---|---|---|
| MOSFET conduction (hot) | 28.8 | 115.2 |
| PCB copper (phase paths) | ~5.0 | ~20.0 |
| Switching overlap | 1.94 | 7.8 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 | 4.9 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.13 | 0.5 |
| Gate drive, MCU, LDO, misc. | ~0.3 | ~1.2 |
| **Total** | **≈ 37.4** | **≈ 150 W** |

Efficiency check: 4 × 80 A × 25.2 V ≈ 8.06 kW in, and 150 W lost gives about 98.1% efficiency. The percentage looks fine, but the absolute heat in a ~6 g board is the actual problem.

## 5. Thermal Model

### 5.1 Network

```
 P_FET ──[R_θJC]── case ──[R_θCB]── board copper ──[R_θBA]── ambient/propwash
                                          │
                                       C_board
```

| Parameter | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| R_θJC (DFN3.3, bottom pad) | 1.5 K/W |
| R_θCB (solder + via field to planes) | 2.0 K/W per FET |
| Board heat capacity, C_board | ≈ 4 J/K (6–8 g of FR4/Cu, c_Cu = 0.385 J/g·K, c_FR4 ≈ 1.1 J/g·K) |
| R_θBA, whole board in prop-wash | ≈ 3 K/W optimistic (~5 m/s airflow), 8 K/W still air |

### 5.2 Junction-to-board rise per FET

Average FET loss ≈ 4.8 W conduction + ~1.6 W switching/dead-time, which attributes switching to the HS devices. Worst-case FET ≈ 6.4 W:

$$\Delta T_{J-B} = 6.4 \cdot (1.5 + 2.0) \approx \mathbf{22\ K}$$

### 5.3 Steady state: not achievable

$$\Delta T_{B-A} = 150\ \text{W} \cdot 3\ \text{K/W} = 450\ \text{K}$$

The board would delaminate long before reaching that. **"80 A" on a nano 4-in-1 can only be a short-duration burst rating.**
