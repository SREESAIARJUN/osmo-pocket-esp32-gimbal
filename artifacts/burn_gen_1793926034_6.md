# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power Stage, Thermal & Bi-Directional DShot Design Package

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Flag

Two points need to be settled before any math.

**1. The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz PWM.**
- The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and emits DShot600 frames.
- Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU (AM32 or BLHeli_32-class, e.g. STM32G071 or AT32F421). That MCU does the commutation, the 48 kHz complementary PWM, and the eRPM telemetry reply.
- The switching-loss model below applies to the ESC MCU and gate-driver domain. The DMA model applies to the H743 domain.

**2. "80 A burst" on a nano form factor is thermally marginal.**
- The numbers below show that a 4 × 80 A simultaneous burst on a single-FET-per-switch, 20×20 or 30×30 mm board is limited to about **2–4 seconds** before PCB temperature limits are hit.
- I model both a single-FET and a dual-paralleled-FET topology so the spec can be negotiated honestly.

All device parameters are **representative values for a 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm or 5×6 mm DFN MOSFET class**. Substitute the selected part's datasheet values and re-run the equations. The netlists are **reference designs to be verified in ERC/DRC, not release-ready artwork**.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Topology Derivation (6-Step Trapezoidal, Synchronous)

In any 60° commutation sector, current flows **into** one phase leg and **out of** another:

- **Sourcing leg:** PWM'd complementary pair. The high-side FET conducts during D·T_sw and the low-side FET (synchronous rectifier) conducts during (1−D)·T_sw, minus dead time.
- **Sinking leg:** low-side FET held fully on.

So, neglecting dead time, **exactly two FET channels carry the full current I at every instant**:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\, I^2\, R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

This is independent of duty cycle, which is a useful property of synchronous 6-step drive.

### 2.2 Temperature-Dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds(on),25}\,[1 + \alpha_R (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C:
$$R = 1.1\,\text{m}\Omega \times (1 + 0.0045\times85) = 1.52\,\text{m}\Omega$$

### 2.3 Per-FET Distribution

Each FET's duty depends on its role over the electrical cycle.

Low-side, sink role (120° of 360°):
$$I_{rms,LS,sink} = I\sqrt{1/3}$$

High-side, source role (120° × D):
$$I_{rms,HS} = I\sqrt{D/3}$$

Low-side, synchronous rectifier role (120° × (1−D)):
$$I_{rms,LS,SR} = I\sqrt{(1-D)/3}$$

At full burst (D → ~0.95), the **low-side FETs are the hottest**:

$$P_{LS} \approx I^2 R \cdot \frac{1 + (1-D)}{3}$$

The high-side FETs run at approximately I²R·D/3.

### 2.4 Numerical Results at 80 A, T_j = 110 °C

Per-channel totals:

| Term | Single FET per switch | 2× paralleled |
|---|---|---|
| R_eff per switch | 1.52 mΩ | 0.76 mΩ |
| Bridge conduction, 2I²R | **19.5 W** | **9.7 W** |
| Copper path, I²·R_Cu | 3.2 W | 3.2 W |
| Hottest single FET (LS, D = 0.95) | 3.42 W | 1.71 W |

Per-FET breakdown (single FET, D = 0.95):
- Low-side: 6400 × 1.52 m × (1.05/3) = **3.40 W**
- High-side: 6400 × 1.52 m × (0.95/3) = **3.08 W**
- Sum over 6 FETs: 2×3.40 + 2×3.08 ... ≈ 19.5 W, consistent with 2I²R.
