## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

### 0.1 Partitioning

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. A production-grade AIO uses two tiers:

| Function | Device | Firmware |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100, 480 MHz) | ArduPilot (ChibiOS) |
| ESC ×4 (one per motor) | AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071 (QFN32) | AM32 or BLHeli_32 |
| Gate drive ×4 | FD6288Q (3-phase, 250 mA/1.0 A typ.) or equivalent | n/a |
| Power stage ×4 | 6× N-ch 40 V MOSFET, PDFN 5×6 (or 3.3×3.3 for nano) | n/a |

The H743 talks to each ESC MCU over a single-wire bidirectional DShot600 link. Section 4 covers the timer and DMA allocation for that link.

### 0.3 Engineering Warning on the Specification

80 A per motor on a nano-class board (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) is **burst-only**. The analysis below shows roughly 95 W of dissipation for the whole board at 80 A per motor. The board can only absorb that for a few seconds before it needs airflow. The model quantifies the allowable burst duration rather than pretending the rating is continuous.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation Topology

The ESC uses six-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM on the "high" phase and a static low-side on the "low" phase. At any instant the motor current flows through exactly two channels:

- **PWM leg:** HS conducts for D·T, LS (synchronous rectifier) conducts for (1−D)·T, minus dead time.
- **Return leg:** LS fully on.

The current path therefore always contains **two R_DS(on)** values, independent of duty cycle:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{rms}^2 \, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

### 1.2 RMS Current Including Ripple

Phase-current ripple at f_sw (motor inductance L_ph, worst case D = 0.5):

$$
\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{2L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}
\qquad
I_{rms}^2 = I_{avg}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}
$$

Example: L_ph = 15 µH (a typical 2207 motor, phase-to-phase 30 µH).

$$
\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{2 \times 15\times10^{-6} \times 48\,000} = 4.38\ \text{A}
\;\Rightarrow\;
\frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 1.6\ \text{A}^2
$$

This term is negligible against 6400 A², so I_rms ≈ 80.01 A. Ripple does matter for very low-inductance motors (below 5 µH) and for bus-capacitor sizing (Section 3).

### 1.3 Temperature-Dependent R_DS(on)

Use a linear fit to the normalized datasheet curve:

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right],
\qquad
\alpha = \frac{1.6 - 1}{100} = 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$
