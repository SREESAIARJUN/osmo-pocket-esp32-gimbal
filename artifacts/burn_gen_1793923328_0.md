# STM32H743 AIO + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #15): Power, Thermal and DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

No MOSFET part number, layer stack, or burst duration was specified, so this model is **parametric**. The numbers below use representative values that should be replaced with datasheet data before release.

**Architecture clarification.** The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. It runs ArduPilot and sends bi-directional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs on the same board. Each ESC MCU runs AM32 or BLHeli_32, generates the 48 kHz PWM, and drives a gate driver and six MOSFETs.

| Parameter | Symbol | Assumed value |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | V_bus | 6S, 25.2 V max |
| Burst phase current | I_ph | 80 A per ESC (all 4 simultaneously, worst case) |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous rectification) |
| MOSFET (40 V, 5×6 PDFN class) | R_ds(on),25 | 1.2 mΩ @ V_gs = 10 V |
| R_ds tempco | k_T | ×1.5 at T_j = 100 °C |
| Rise / fall time | t_r, t_f | 20 ns / 20 ns |
| Gate charge | Q_g | 80 nC |
| Output capacitance | C_oss | 1.5 nF |
| Reverse recovery charge | Q_rr | 60 nC |
| Body diode forward voltage | V_f | 0.8 V |
| Dead time | t_dt | 100 ns per edge |
| Junction-to-case thermal resistance | R_θJC | 1.0 K/W |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A burst)

With trapezoidal 6-step commutation and synchronous rectification, current always flows through exactly two FETs in series:
- during the on-time: the high-side FET of phase A plus the low-side FET of phase B;
- during freewheel: the low-side FET of phase A plus the low-side FET of phase B.

Therefore:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 100 °C, R_ds = 1.8 mΩ:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2(80)^2(0.0018) = 23.04\text{ W}$$

Per conducting FET this is **11.52 W**. Commutation rotates the load across the six FETs (each conducts about 1/3 of the time), so the average is ~7.7 W per FET. However, the junction sees the 11.5 W peak for the full commutation interval, which is milliseconds long and comparable to the die thermal time constant. **Design to 11.5 W per FET.**

**Copper and shunt losses (not optional at 80 A):**
- Phase trace / pad loss, assuming 0.3 mΩ total path per ESC: 80² × 0.0003 = **1.9 W**
- Current shunt, assuming 0.25 mΩ: 80² × 0.00025 = **1.6 W** (use a 2512 or wider part rated for at least 3 W pulse)

**Electrothermal runaway check.** Because R_ds rises with temperature, a stable operating point requires:

$$\frac{dP}{dT}\,R_{\theta JA} < 1 \;\Rightarrow\; 2I^2R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_{\theta JA} < 1$$

With α ≈ 0.0067 /K:

$$2(6400)(0.0012)(0.0067)\,R_{\theta JA} < 1 \;\Rightarrow\; R_{\theta JA} < 9.7\text{ K/W (per ESC aggregate)}$$

An 80 A continuous rating would need far lower R_θJA than this bound to also meet temperature limits. That is why 80 A must be treated as a **burst** rating (see Section 4).

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only one leg is hard-switched per PWM period.

**(a) V–I overlap loss**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(40\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = 1.94\text{ W}$$

**(b) C_oss loss**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss}V^2 f \times 2 = 1.5\text{n}(635)(48\text{k}) = 0.046\text{ W}$$

**(c) Reverse recovery loss**

$$P_{rr} = V\,Q_{rr}\,f = 25.2(60\text{n})(48\text{k}) = 0.073\text{ W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode loss**

$$P_{dt} = V_f\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f = 0.8(80)(200\text{n})(48\text{k}) = 0.61\text{ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive loss** (two FETs per active leg; this loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET)

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f = 2(80\text{n})(10)(48\text{k}) = 0.077\text{ W}$$

**Switching subtotal:** ≈ **2.71 W per ESC**.

**Frequency sensitivity:**

| f_sw | Switching subtotal per ESC |
|---|---|
| 24 kHz | ~1.35 W |
| 48 kHz | ~2.7 W |
| 96 kHz | ~5.4 W |

At 80 A, conduction dominates (about 85% of total). The 48 kHz choice is justified by lower motor current ripple and acoustic benefit, not by efficiency.

## 3. Total Loss Budget at 80 A Burst

| Loss term | Per ESC | ×4 ESCs |
|---|---|---|
| MOSFET conduction (100 °C) | 23.0 W | 92.2 W |
| Switching + C_oss + Q_rr + dead-time | 2.63 W | 10.5 W |
| Gate drive | 0.08 W | 0.3 W |
| Copper and shunt | 3.5 W | 14.0 W |
| **Total** | **~29.2 W** | **~117 W** |

At 25.2 V × 80 A × 4 ≈ 8 kW of motor input power, this corresponds to roughly **98.5% power-stage efficiency**. That is realistic for this FET class, but the absolute heat is very large for a nano-format board.

**DC-link capacitor ripple**

$$I_{C,rms} = I_{ph}\sqrt{D(1-D)}, \text{ maximum at } D = 0.5 \;\Rightarrow\; 40\text{ A rms per ESC}$$

Requirements:
- A bulk low-ESR electrolytic at the pads (≥ 1000 µF, 35 V) shared across the board.
- At least 4 × 10 µF X7R 50 V (0805/1206) per ESC placed directly at the high-side drain.
- Account for X7R capacitance derating of about 60–70% at 25 V when sizing.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Steady-state junction rise

Per active FET at 11.5 W:

| Path | Thermal resistance | Rise |
|---|---|---|
| Junction → case | R_θJC = 1.0 K/W | +11.5 K |
| Case → PCB (solder, thermal vias, 2 oz) | R_θCB ≈ 2.5 K/W (assumed) | +28.8 K |
| **Junction above board** | | **≈ 40 K** |

### 4.2 Board-level transient (lumped RC)

Board plus heatsink parameters (estimates; measure on the prototype):
- C_th ≈ 25 J/K (about 15 g of FR4 and copper plus a 10 g aluminium plate)
- R_θBA ≈ 1.5 K/W with prop wash

$$T_B(t) = T_A + P\,R_{\theta BA}\left(1-e^{-t/\tau}\right), \qquad \tau = R_{\theta BA}C_{th} \approx 37.5\text{ s}$$

At P = 117 W, starting from T_A = 35 °C:

| Burst duration | Board temperature | Junction (T_B + 40 K) |
|---|---|---|
| 3 s | 35 + 175.5(1 − e^(−0.08)) ≈ **48.5 °C** | ~89 °C |
| 10 s | ≈ **76 °C** | ~116 °C |
| Steady state | 35 + 175.5 = **210 °C** | **Not survivable** |

**Conclusion:** rate the board **80 A for at most 10 s per motor**, with continuous current derated to

$$I_{cont} \approx 80\sqrt{50/117} \approx \textbf{52 A}$$

for a board-temperature rise of about 75 K. Enforce the limit with:
- an NTC on each ESC MCU;
- AM32 current limiting;
- ArduPilot ESC telemetry temperature monitoring (EDT, extended DShot telemetry).

## 5. Bi-directional DShot600 Timing and DMA Allocation

### 5.1 Timing

**Outbound frame:**
- Bit time = 1/600 kbit/s = **1.667 µs**
- 16-bit frame = **26.67 µs**
- Timer clock: H743 at 400 MHz gives APB1/APB2 timer clocks of 200 MHz, so one bit = 333.3 ticks. ArduPilot sets the prescaler internally.
- Bi-directional mode uses inverted signaling (idle low).

**Telemetry return:**
- The ESC replies about 30 µs after the frame.
- Format: 21 bits GCR at 5/4 × 600 kbit = 750 kbit/s, giving **1.333 µs per bit**, about 28 µs total.

**Total transaction:** ≈ 26.7 + 30 + 28 + guard ≈ **90 µs**. A 2 kHz or higher loop rate is therefore comfortably achievable.

### 5.2 DMA requirements on the H7

Each timer group needs:
- **TIMx_UP** for burst (DMAR) output of the duty buffer;
- **TIMx_CHy** input-capture DMA to receive telemetry.

The H7 DMAMUX allows any request to use any DMA1/DMA2 stream (16 total), so the binding constraint is **stream count and sharing**, not a fixed mapping table.
