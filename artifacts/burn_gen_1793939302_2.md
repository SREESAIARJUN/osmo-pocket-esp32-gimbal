## 0. Architectural Clarifications

Two items in the job statement must be resolved before any math is meaningful.

### 0.1 The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors

The H743 is the **flight controller** MCU. It runs ArduPilot and emits bi-directional DShot600. Each motor bridge is driven by its own small ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, which handles commutation, the 48 kHz PWM, and the eRPM telemetry reply.

| Function | Device | Qty |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100) | 1 |
| ESC MCU | AT32F421K8U7 (QFN32), AM32 firmware | 4 |
| Gate driver | FD6288Q (3-phase, 250 V, bootstrap) | 4 |
| Power stage | 40 V N-MOSFETs, PDFN 3.3×3.3 or 5×6 | 24 |

### 0.2 "80 A burst" on a nano form factor

This document interprets the spec as **80 A per motor, burst** (bench-tested peak). Section 3.6 shows this is thermally achievable only for roughly 1–3 s on a 20×20 or 30.5×30.5 board, and the battery path would carry **320 A**. If the real requirement is 80 A total (about 20 A per motor), every loss figure below scales by roughly (20/80)² ≈ 1/16 for conduction. The design then becomes comfortable.

**Electrical operating point (worst case):** 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V (full charge), f_sw = 48 kHz, I_phase = 80 A burst, T_ambient = 40 °C.

## 3. Loss Model per Bridge

### 3.1 Commutation topology

AM32 uses 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM. In each 60° sector:

- **Phase X (PWM leg):** the high-side switches at duty D, and the low-side conducts during (1 − D) as a synchronous rectifier.
- **Phase Y (return leg):** the low-side is continuously ON.
- **Phase Z:** floating, used for BEMF sensing.

The current path therefore always contains **exactly two conducting FETs**:

- During t_on: HS_X + LS_Y
- During t_off: LS_X + LS_Y

### 3.2 Conduction loss

The phase current contains a ripple component ΔI from the motor inductance L_ph:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{DC}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{2 L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}$$

Example: L_ph = 15 µH line-to-line, V_bus = 25.2 V, D = 0.9, V_bemf ≈ 21 V gives:

$$\Delta I = \frac{4.2 \times 0.9}{2 \cdot 15\,\mu \cdot 48\,k} \approx 2.6\text{ A}$$

The ripple term is ΔI²/12 ≈ 0.56 A², which is negligible against I² = 6400 A².

Bridge conduction loss is two FETs in series at all times:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j) = 2 \times 6400.6 \times 1.55\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.84\ W}$$

Because each FET conducts for one third of the electrical period on average:

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx P_{cond,bridge}/6 \times 2 \cdot \tfrac{?}{} \;\rightarrow\; \text{(see 3.5 for the per-FET split)}$$

### 3.3 Switching loss at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Its HS/LS pair forms one hard-switched half-bridge.

**a) V–I overlap (hard turn-on and turn-off of the HS FET):**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.968\ W}$$

**b) Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period):**

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.85 \times 80 \times 200\text{n
