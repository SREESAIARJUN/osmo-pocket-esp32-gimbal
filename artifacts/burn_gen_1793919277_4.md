# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #29): Loss Model, DShot DMA Plan and Reference Netlists

## 0. Assumptions and Architecture Issues

Two items in the requirement need resolving before layout.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In a 4-in-1 stack, ArduPilot runs on the H743 (FC) and sends bi-directional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs. These are typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Each ESC MCU generates its own 48 kHz PWM. The architecture below assumes:

```
[STM32H743 FC] --DShot600 bidir x4--> [4x AT32F421 (AM32)] --> [4x gate driver] --> [4x 3-phase bridge, 24 FETs]
```

**2. 80 A per motor on a nano (20×20 mm) form factor is not thermally sustainable.**
- 4 × 80 A is roughly 320 A of battery current.
- Section 3 shows this is about 108 W of dissipation into roughly 2 J/K of board.
- I treat 80 A as a burst rating of 0.5 s or less with a defined duty cycle. Continuous rating is derived separately.
- The 12 FETs needed for 80 A realistically require 3.3×3.3 mm or larger packages, which pushes the ESC to a 30×30 mm footprint. Specifying "nano" for the FC and "30×30" for the ESC is recommended.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

R_ds(Tj) = R_ds(25) · [1 + α(Tj − 25)], with α ≈ 0.007 /K for low-voltage trench FETs.

At Tj = 110 °C: R_ds = 1.2 · (1 + 0.007 · 85) = **1.91 mΩ**

### 1.2 Current-path analysis (6-step, synchronous)

In every commutation sector exactly two phases carry I_ph:
- **PWM on (D):** high-side A and low-side B conduct.
- **PWM off (1−D):** low-side A (synchronous rectifier) and low-side B conduct.

The conduction path is therefore always two FETs in series, independent of D:

P_cond,bridge = 2 · I² · R_ds(Tj)

Each phase is active for 2 of 6 sectors. The per-FET split is:

- P_HS = (1/3) · D · I² · R
- P_LS = (1/3) · (2 − D) · I² · R

At full throttle (D → 1), losses distribute evenly at I²R/3 per FET.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| I²·R | 6400 · 1.91e-3 = 12.2 W |
| P_cond per bridge | **24.5 W** |
| Worst FET (LS, D = 0.5) | (1.5/3) · 12.2 = **6.1 W** |
| HS FET at D = 1 | 4.1 W |

### 1.4 Parasitic copper loss (not optional at 80 A)

| Element | Resistance | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase path (2 oz, 4 parallel layers, about 8 mm × 3 mm effective) | ≈ 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W per phase |
| Shunt (if fitted, per bridge) | 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |

Path loss is about 2 phases × 1.6 W plus 1.6 W, roughly 4.8 W per bridge.
**Recommendation:** use 3 oz outer and 2 oz inner copper, 6-layer stack-up.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd high-side FET hard-switches. The low side commutates through its body diode, giving near-ZVS turn-on.

### 2.1 V-I overlap (hard switch, HS)

P_VI = ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw
= 0.5 · 25.2 · 80 · 35e-9 · 48e3 = **1.69 W**

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (LS body diode, dissipated in HS)

P_rr = Q_rr · V · f_sw = 50e-9 · 25.2 · 48e3 = **0.060 W**

At 80 A, Q_rr scales roughly with di/dt and I_F; derate by 1.5–2× and use **0.12 W**.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

P_oss = Q_oss · V · f_sw = 60e-9 · 25.2 · 48e3 = **0.073 W**

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction (LS)

P_dt = V_SD · I · (2 · t_dt) · f_sw = 0.8 · 80 · 200e-9 · 48e3 = **0.61 W**

### 2.5 Gate drive (driver dissipation, per switching FET pair)

P_g = Q_g · V_gs · f_sw = 60e-9 · 10 · 48e3 = 0.029 W per FET

Total for 2 switching FETs is about 0.06 W. This is negligible for the FET but relevant to the driver/LDO budget.

### 2.6 Total switching loss per bridge

P_sw ≈ 1.69 + 0.12 + 0.07 + 0.61 + 0.06 ≈ **2.55 W**

### 2.7 f_sw sensitivity

P_sw scales linearly with f_sw:

| f_sw | P_sw per bridge |
|---|---|
| 24 kHz | 1.3 W |
| 48 kHz | 2.55 W |
| 96 kHz | 5.1 W |

At 80 A, conduction dominates by about 10×, so 48 kHz is a reasonable choice for current ripple and acoustic noise.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Power budget at 80 A burst

| Item | Per ESC | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 24.5 W | 98 W |
| Switching | 2.55 W | 10.2 W |
| Copper/shunt | 4.8 W | 19.2 W |
| **Total** | **31.9 W** | **≈ 127 W** |

**Worst single FET:** 6.1 W conduction plus 2.4 W switching (the HS carries most of the switching loss, but take the max-stack case) gives about 7–8 W.
