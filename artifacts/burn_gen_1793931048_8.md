## 0. Architectural Clarifications and Design Assumptions

The architecture has two separate processors:

1. **Flight controller (FC):** STM32H743 running ArduPilot. It generates bi-directional DShot600 and decodes eRPM telemetry.
2. **ESC:** four independent ESC MCUs (for example STM32G071 or AT32F421 running AM32, or EFM8/BB51 running BLHeli_S/Bluejay). The ESC MCUs generate the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The H743 does **not** drive the bridges directly. Using the H7's TIM1/TIM8 for 4×3-phase FOC would exhaust its advanced timers and is not how ArduPilot works.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-Dependent On-Resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.0047\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

$$R_{DS}(110^\circ\text{C}) = 0.90\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.40 = 1.26\ \text{m}\Omega$$

Because this is an electro-thermal feedback loop, solve iteratively (Section 3.4).

### 1.2 Current Path in 6-Step Complementary PWM

At any instant two phases conduct. One phase is held low-side ON for 120° electrical. The other phase is PWM'd: its high-side FET is ON for D·T_sw, and its low-side FET is ON for (1−D)·T_sw (synchronous rectification).

The **phase current therefore always flows through exactly two channels in series**, independent of duty cycle (dead time ignored here; it is treated in 2.4):

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph,rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

With ripple included:

$$I_{ph,rms}^2 = I_{pk}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12},\qquad \Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus}-E_{bemf})\,D}{2 L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5" motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH line-line, burst at D ≈ 0.9, E ≈ 0.85·V_bus):

$$\Delta I_{pp} \approx \frac{(25.2-21.4)(0.9)}{2(15\,\mu)(48\,k)} \approx 2.4\ \text{A} \ \Rightarrow\ \text{ripple term} = 0.48\ \text{A}^2 \ (\text{negligible vs } 6400\ \text{A}^2)$$

### 1.3 Numerical Result per Motor at 80 A

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.26\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{16.1\ W}$$

### 1.4 Per-FET Distribution (Hot-Spot Identification)

Each FET is "active" for 1/3 of the electrical cycle.

| FET role (within its 120° window) | Conduction fraction | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Static low-side (held ON) | 1/3 | I²R/3 = **2.69 W** |
| PWM high-side | D/3 | 2.42 W @ D = 0.9 |
| Sync-rect low-side | (1−D)/3 | 0.27 W @ D = 0.9 |

AM32 rotates the PWM'd phase, so averaged over a mechanical revolution:

- each **high-side** FET dissipates ≈ (1/6)·I²R·(1+D) ≈ 2.55 W
- each **low-side** FET dissipates ≈ (1/6)·I²R·(2−D) ≈ 1.48 W

**The high-side FETs are the hot spots at high throttle.** Give them the best copper and via fields.

### 1.5 Copper / Interconnect Conduction

| Element | Estimate | Loss @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase pad + bridge copper (2 oz, ~6 mm × 8 mm × 2 layers) | ~0.15 mΩ | 0.96 W |
| Bus copper from battery pad to each bridge (shared) | ~0.25 mΩ effective | 1.6 W (path-dependent) |
| Solder joints / motor-wire pads | ~0.10 mΩ | 0.64 W |
| **Subtotal per motor** | **~0.5 mΩ** | **≈ 3.2 W** |

Copper resistance rises about 0.39 %/K. At a 100 °C board temperature, multiply by 1.3, giving ≈ 4.1 W.
