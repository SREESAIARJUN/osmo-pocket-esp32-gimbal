# STM32H743 AIO Nano Flight Controller with 4-in-1 80A-Burst ESC: Architecture & Derivations

## 0. Scope Corrections and Baseline Assumptions

Some parts of the request need clarifying first, because they change the math.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production AIO/4-in-1 design, the STM32H743 is the flight controller running ArduPilot. It sends bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs (one per motor), which generate the 48 kHz 6-step PWM. A suitable ESC MCU is the AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or an EFM8/BB21 running BLHeli_S/Bluejay. A single H743 driving 24 FETs directly with ArduPilot is not a supported topology. I model the standard split.
2. **"Nano" plus 80A is a burst-only rating.** It is thermally impossible to sustain on a 30×30 mm board (proven in §2.5).
3. **No datasheets are attached.** I use a representative 40V, ~1.3 mΩ PDFN3.3×3.3 MOSFET class. Replace the parameter table with your actual part's datasheet values and the equations still hold.

## 1. Electrical Topology (6-step, Synchronous Rectification)

Each motor has 3 half-bridges (6 FETs). In 6-step commutation, two phases conduct at any instant:

- **PWM leg:** HS conducts for fraction D. LS conducts for (1−D) as a synchronous rectifier (complementary PWM).
- **Return leg:** LS is held fully on.

So the phase current always flows through **two FETs in series**.

## 2. Mathematical Model

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

Instantaneous conduction loss per motor:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I^2 R_{ds,hot}$$

$$= 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 0.002 = \mathbf{25.6\ W}$$

Each FET conducts for 120° (a third of the electrical cycle) in one of these roles. The electrical period is much shorter than the junction thermal time constant (a 1 kHz electrical frequency is about 1 ms). The junction therefore sees the average:

$$\bar{P}_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{P_{cond,motor}}{6}\cdot 2 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2}\ \text{(two of six FETs active)} \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{25.6}{6} \approx 4.3\ \text{W}$$

At low duty, the LS sync-rect FET of the PWM leg dominates, which is a worst-case hot spot. Size for **5 W per FET** peak-average.

### 2.2 Switching Loss (Hard-Switched PWM Leg Only)

Overlap loss:

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

$$= 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot40\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

Rise time from the gate charge model. Assume R_g,total = 10 Ω, V_drv = 10 V, V_plateau = 3 V:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,R_g}{V_{drv}-V_{pl}} = \frac{10\text{nC}\cdot10}{7} \approx 14\ \text{ns}$$

The 20 ns assumption is conservative.
