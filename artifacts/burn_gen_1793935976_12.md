## 1. Conduction Loss Model (6-step, complementary PWM)

### 1.1 Current paths
In trapezoidal 6-step commutation, two phases conduct at any moment. Phase A HS is PWM'd and phase B LS is held on. With complementary (synchronous rectification) PWM at duty D:

- HS_A conducts I for fraction **D**.
- LS_A (synchronous rectifier) conducts I for fraction **(1−D)**.
- LS_B conducts I for the **full** sector.

The instantaneous conduction loss per motor is therefore duty-independent:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

Conduction loss and junction temperature are coupled, so solve iteratively:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + P_{FET}(T_j)\cdot Z_{\theta}(t)$$

Thermal runaway condition (no stable solution): $\;2I^2R_{DS,25}\,\alpha\,Z_\theta \geq 1$ per path. At 80 A this needs Z_θ ≳ 300 K/W, so it is not a risk. Losses are bounded by the thermal mass, not by runaway.

### 1.3 Numbers at I = 80 A, T_j = 125 °C (R_DS = 1.82 mΩ)

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.82\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{23.3\ W}$$

Per-FET average over an electrical cycle (each of 6 FETs carries 1/3 of the cycle in one of the two roles):

$$\bar P_{FET,cond} \approx \frac{23.3}{6} \approx 3.9\ \text{W (avg)}, \qquad \hat P_{FET} = I^2R_{DS} = 11.6\ \text{W (peak, during its sector)}$$

At electrical frequencies above ~200 Hz (e.g. 14-pole motor above ~1700 RPM), the die's thermal time constant (~ms) averages the sector pulsing. **Use the average for junction calcs, and use the peak only at stall or low RPM.**
