## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Flags

Two points must be settled before the mathematics, because they change the results.

### 0.1 The H743 does not commutate the motors directly

An STM32H743 running ArduPilot cannot also run 4× sensorless 48 kHz commutation loops alongside the flight stack in a supportable way. The architecture is therefore:

| Block | Device | Firmware |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100) or VIH6 (TFBGA100) | ArduPilot (ChibiOS) |
| ESC MCUs | 4× AT32F421K8U7 (QFN32) **[A]** | AM32 (supports bidirectional DShot and EDT) |
| Gate drivers | 4× FD6288Q-class 3-phase half-bridge drivers **[A]** | — |
| Power stage | 24× N-MOSFETs, or 12× dual-N in 3.3×3.3 mm packages **[A]** | — |
| Link | Bidirectional DShot600, one internal trace per motor | — |

### 0.2 The "80A burst" rating is energy-limited on a nano form factor

Section 2.5 shows that a 20×20 mm board has roughly 2 J/K of thermal mass. Four channels at 80 A dissipate more than 100 W, which raises board temperature by about 55 K per second.

- **"80A burst" must be specified as 80 A per channel for ≤1 s, at 25 °C ambient, once per thermal recovery cycle.**
- A 30.5×30.5 mm board roughly doubles that window.

This is physics, not a component-choice issue. The marketing specification should be written to match.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |

The commutation scheme is six-step trapezoidal with complementary (synchronous) PWM, which is the AM32 default. In any commutation state, current flows through one high-side and one low-side leg path.

## 2. Thermal Model: Conduction Losses

### 2.1 Junction-temperature-dependent resistance

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]
$$

At a design junction temperature of T_j = 125 °C:

$$
R_{DS} = 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.45 = 2.18\,\text{m}\Omega
$$

### 2.2 Per-channel conduction loss with synchronous rectification

The PWM-ed leg alternates between its high-side and low-side FET, so that leg always has exactly one FET conducting. The opposite leg holds one FET on continuously.

- PWM leg loss: $I^2 R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D)] = I^2 R_{DS}$
- Static leg loss: $I^2 R_{DS}$

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

| Case | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Burst, 80 A | 2 × 6400 × 2.18 mΩ | **27.9 W per channel** |
| Continuous, 45 A | 2 × 2025 × 2.18 mΩ | 8.8 W per channel |

### 2.3 Copper path loss

Assumed path resistance: phase pad + bus + ground return ≈ 0.6 mΩ per channel on 8L/2 oz copper **[A]**.

$$
P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.6\,\text{m}\Omega = 3.8\,\text{W per channel (burst)}
$$

### 2.4 Electrothermal fixed point

R_DS rises with T_j, and T_j rises with loss. Iterate:

$$
T_j^{(n+1)} = T_{ref} + P\big(T_j^{(n)}\big)\cdot Z_{th}
$$

Convergence requires:

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\,Z_{th} = 2 I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\,Z_{th} < 1
$$

At 80 A: $2 \times 6400 \times 1.5\text{m} \times 0.0045 = 0.086\ \text{W/K}$.

- **Runaway threshold:** $Z_{th} > 11.6$ K/W.
- **Implication:** a bare nano board in still air (R_th,ba ≈ 15–20 K/W) is thermally unstable at continuous 80 A. Burst-only operation is mandatory unless there is forced prop-wash cooling.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

The model is evaluated per switching (PWM-ed) leg; one leg per channel switches at any time.

### 3.1 Hard-switching V-I overlap

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2}V_{bus}\,I_{ph}\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw}
$$

$$
= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 22\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = 1.06\,\text{W}
$$

### 3.2 Output capacitance loss

$$
P_{oss} = Q_{oss}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 40\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.048\,\text{W}
$$

### 3.3 Body-diode reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 50\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.060\,\text{W}
$$

### 3.4 Dead-time diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM period.

$$
P_{dt} = 2\,V_f\,I_{ph}\,t_{dt}\,f_{sw} = 2 \times 0.8 \times 80 \times 50\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.307\,\text{W}
$$

### 3.5 Gate drive

Two FETs are driven per leg. This power is dissipated mostly in the gate resistors and the driver, not in the FETs.

$$
P_{gate} = 2\,Q_g\,V_{drv}\,f_{sw} = 2 \times 45\,\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.043\,\text{W}
$$

### 3.6 Per-channel switching total (burst)

$$
P_{sw,ch} \approx 1.06 + 0.048 + 0.060 + 0.307 + 0.043 = \mathbf{1.52\,W}
$$

### 3.7 Frequency trade-off

Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw.

| f_sw | P_sw per channel | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 24 kHz | ≈ 0.76 W | Saves ~0.76 W per channel |
| 48 kHz | ≈ 1.52 W | Baseline |

Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 0.76 W per channel but increases current ripple:

$$
\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}
$$

48 kHz is justified for low-inductance motors (L_ph < 20 µH), where ripple at 24 kHz would raise RMS copper loss in the motor and the FETs.
