# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Thermal Model, Switching Losses, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Timer/DMA Allocation

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Scope

**MCU partitioning.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot on the flight controller. It does not run the ESC firmware. Each of the 4 ESC channels needs its own motor-control MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, which is what produces the bi-directional DShot eRPM telemetry. Typical choices are an AT32F421 or STM32G071, one per motor. The model below follows that split.

**Feasibility flag.** "80A burst × 4" on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board means up to **~96 W of power-stage dissipation** and **320 A of battery current**. The model shows this is survivable only for **seconds**, not as a sustained rating. The numbers below quantify that window. Treat "80A burst" as a time-limited rating that the firmware must enforce.

**Netlist scope.** The netlists are reference designs for one power channel plus the FC-side timer interface. They are not a validated, layout-ready board. Verify the part choices against current datasheets before tape-out.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Commutation topology

With 6-step (trapezoidal) drive and synchronous rectification, two phase legs carry the current at any instant:

- one high-side FET in leg A (on for fraction D of the PWM period),
- one low-side FET in leg B,
- the complementary low-side FET in leg A (on for fraction 1−D).

The current therefore always flows through exactly **two** R_ds(on) in series.

### 2.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_ds ≈ 1.0 × (1 + 0.006 × 85) ≈ **1.5 mΩ**.

### 2.3 Conduction loss per channel

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j) = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

Copper loss:

$$P_{cu} = I^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

### 2.4 Per-FET split

| FET | Loss expression | Value |
|---|---|---|
| High-side (A) | I² R D | 9.6·D W |
| Low-side sync (A) | I² R (1−D) | 9.6·(1−D) W |
| Low-side (B) | I² R | **9.6 W** (worst case, conducts the full period) |

The hottest device is the continuously conducting low-side FET at **~9.6 W**. Commutation rotates this role among the legs every 60° electrical, which averages it toward ~6.4 W per FET over a full electrical cycle. Use 9.6 W for transient design, because at low eRPM the dwell time on one leg can be long.

### 2.5 Thermal runaway stability check

$$\frac{dP}{dT_j} \cdot R_{\theta JA} = I^2 R_{ds,25}\,\alpha \cdot R_{\theta JA} < 1$$

Per FET: 6400 × 1e-3 × 0.006 = 0.0384 W/K.

- With R_θJA of a nano board around 25 K/W, the loop gain is 0.96. This is **marginal runaway at sustained 80 A**. The board cannot hold this state.
- With a burst-limited duration plus airflow (R_θJA effective ≈ 10 K/W), the gain falls to 0.38, which is stable.

## 3. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Per PWM cycle, one leg hard-switches (one turn-on and one turn-off).

### 3.1 V–I overlap loss

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 3.2 Dead-time diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 3.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = V_{bus} Q_{rr} f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 50\text{nC} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

### 3.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = V_{bus} Q_{oss} f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 40\text{nC} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

### 3.5 Gate drive (6 FETs)

This loss lands in the driver, not in the FETs.

$$P_g = 6 \cdot Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 6 \times 50\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.14\ W}$$
