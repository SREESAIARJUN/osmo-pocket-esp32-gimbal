# STM32H743 FC + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Model, Bidirectional DShot600 DMA Plan, and Netlist

**Scope.** This document covers three things:

1. The loss model for one motor channel at an 80 A burst with 48 kHz PWM.
2. A transient thermal model for the board.
3. ArduPilot timer and DMA allocation for bidirectional DShot600 on the H743, plus reference netlists.

**Flags to resolve before layout:**

- **Form factor.** "Nano" (20×20 mm) and "80 A burst × 4" are physically incompatible. Twenty-four 5×6 FETs plus bulk capacitance will not fit, and the board has no thermal mass to absorb the burst (see §3). The model below assumes a 30.5×30.5 mm ESC stacked under a 20×20 or 30×30 FC. If the brief truly means a single nano board, realistic numbers are 35–45 A burst.
- **Architecture.** The H743 does not commutate the motors. Each ESC channel has its own MCU. "Bidirectional DShot" is the FC↔ESC link, and that is what §4 allocates.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation, two phases conduct at any instant:

- **PWM leg.** The high-side FET (HS) is on for duty D. The complementary low-side FET (LS) carries the freewheel current for (1 − D), minus dead time.
- **Return leg.** The low-side FET is on for the whole 60° sector.

The motor current I_ph therefore always flows through exactly two FET channels.

### 1.2 Equations

Temperature-dependent on-resistance:

$$R_{DS}(T_j)=R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

Conduction loss per motor, with the dead-time interval handled separately in §2:

$$P_{cond}=I_{ph}^2\,[D\,R_{HS}+(1-D)R_{LS}] + I_{ph}^2 R_{LS,ret} \approx 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Copper and shunt loss:

$$P_{Cu}=I_{ph}^2\,(R_{trace}+R_{shunt}+R_{solder})$$

Average loss per FET: each FET is active for 1/3 of the electrical cycle, so with symmetric commutation

$$\bar P_{FET}=P_{cond}/6$$

The instantaneous loss is pulsed, but the electrical period is far shorter than the package thermal time constant, so the average is what matters.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond per motor | Per FET (average) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 0.75 mΩ | 2·6400·0.75e-3 = **9.6 W** | 1.60 W |
| 100 °C | 1.00 mΩ | **12.8 W** | 2.13 W |
| 125 °C | 1.09 mΩ | **13.9 W** | 2.32 W |

**Copper and shunt.** Assume 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner layers, a 0.5 mΩ shunt (or 0 if you use phase-resistance sensing), and about 0.25 mΩ of trace per path:

$$P_{Cu}=6400\times0.75\text{e-3}= \mathbf{4.8\ W}$$

This term is larger than most designers expect. Use a 0.25 mΩ shunt or sense on the low-side DC bus only once for all four channels; that drops it to about 3.2 W.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (f_sw = 48 kHz)

Only the PWM leg switches. Per PWM period there is one turn-on and one turn-off of the HS, plus two dead-time intervals with LS body-diode conduction.

### 2.1 Components

**(a) V-I overlap (hard switching, inductive load):**

$$P_{VI}=\tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot22\text{ns}\cdot48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.06\ W}$$

**(b) Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss}=Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw}=45\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k}= \mathbf{0.054\ W}$$

**(c) Body-diode reverse recovery.** The LS diode recovers when the HS turns on:

$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}=50\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k}= \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$

The recovery current spike also adds roughly I_rr·V·t_rr/2 to the HS turn-on, which is folded into a +10 % margin on P_VI.

**(d) Dead-time diode conduction:**

$$P_{dt}=V_f I_{ph}\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw}=0.8\cdot80\cdot100\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k}= \mathbf{0.31\ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive.** Two FETs are toggled per period, and most of this is dissipated in the driver and R_g rather than the FET:

$$P_g=2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw}=2\cdot60\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k}= 0.058\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Totals per motor at 80 A

| Term | Power |
|---|---|
| P_VI (+10 % recovery margin) | 1.17 W |
| P_oss + P_rr | 0.11 W |
| P_dt | 0.31 W |
| **P_sw (FET-side)** | **≈ 1.6 W** |
| P_cond at T_j = 100 °C | 12.8 W |
| P_Cu | 4.8 W |
| **P_total per motor** | **≈ 19.2 W** |
| **Four motors simultaneous** | **≈ 77 W** |

At 48 kHz, conduction dominates switching by about 8:1. Raising f_sw to 96 kHz for quieter motors costs only about +1.6 W per motor. A lower R_DS(on) or paralleled FETs pay back far more than faster edges, which mostly cost EMI.
