# STM32H743 AIO + 4-in-1 ESC Design Package (Job #29)

## 0. Scope Corrections and Assumptions

Before the math, three architectural points shape everything that follows.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** On every production 4-in-1 AIO, each motor has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32/S. Typical parts are the STM32G071, AT32F421, or EFM8BB21. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot600 to those ESC MCUs. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs, not the H743. The design below keeps that split.
2. **80 A burst on a nano board is a transient rating, not a thermal steady state.** The math in §3 shows roughly 90 W of dissipation for all four channels at 80 A. A 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm board can tolerate that for about 1–3 s at most. The realistic continuous rating is about 30–40 A per channel.
3. **No Job #29 documentation was provided**, so the component values below are stated assumptions. Swap in your actual BOM parameters; every equation is parametric.

| Parameter | Symbol | Assumed value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S full) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Burst phase current | I | 80 A |
| MOSFET | — | 40 V, 5×6 or 3.3×3.3 DFN |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Trapezoidal (6-step) commutation with synchronous rectification

During any commutation sector, two phases carry current:

- **Phase A (PWM leg):**
  - The high side conducts for fraction D.
  - The low side conducts (synchronous rectification) for fraction 1 − D.
  - Ignoring dead time, one FET in this leg is always carrying I.
- **Phase B (return leg):** the low side is fully ON.

The conduction loss per ESC channel is therefore:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

This result is duty-independent when synchronous rectification is used.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha(T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1} \Rightarrow R_{DS}(125) \approx 1.6\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| Condition | Calculation | P_cond per channel |
|---|---|---|
| Cold | 2 · 6400 · 0.0010 | 12.8 W |
| Hot | 2 · 6400 · 0.0016 | **20.5 W** |

### 1.4 Parasitic copper and shunt losses

| Element | Assumed resistance | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copper path (2 oz, phase pad to battery pad) | ≈0.4 mΩ | 6400 · 0.0004 | 2.6 W |
| Current-sense shunt (shared across channels) | 0.25 mΩ | (4 · 80)² · 0.00025 | **25.6 W** |

The shared shunt line is the important one. A single shared shunt at a 320 A total burst is untenable. Use one of these instead:
- a 0.1 mΩ shunt, or
- a Hall-effect sensor such as the ACS772 or MCS1802.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches hard. The terms below are per channel.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard-switched high side)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance (C_oss) loss, two FETs per leg

$$P_{oss} = 2 \cdot \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{nC} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Body-diode reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction (two edges per period)

$$P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive, upper bound (all 6 FETs switching)

$$P_g = 6\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 6 \cdot 60\text{nC} \cdot 10 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.17\ \text{W}$$

This power is mostly dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FETs.

### 2.6 Total switching-related loss

$$P_{sw,total} \approx 2.84\ \text{W per channel}$$

### 2.7 Frequency sensitivity

Every switching term scales linearly with f_sw.
- At 24 kHz: about 1.4 W per channel.
- At 96 kHz: about 5.7 W per channel.

At 80 A, conduction loss dominates by roughly 7:1. Reducing f_sw therefore buys little; lowering R_DS(on) or paralleling FETs buys a lot.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Loss budget at 80 A burst, hot

| Item | Per channel | Four channels |
|---|---|---|
| MOSFET conduction | 20.5 W | 82.0 W |
| Switching / dead time / Q_rr | 2.8 W | 11.4 W |
| Copper | 2.6 W | 10.2 W |
| **Total (excluding shunt)** | **25.9 W** | **≈104 W** |

### 3.2 Hottest single FET

The commutation-sector low side is the worst case:

$$P_{FET} \approx I^2 R_{DS} \cdot \tfrac{2}{3} + \text{switching share} \approx 7\text{–}8\ \text{W average}, \quad \text{peak sector } 10.2\ \text{W}$$

$$\Delta T_{JC} = 10.2 \cdot 1.5 \approx 15\ \text{K}$$

### 3.3 Board-level lumped RC (Foster first-order)

**Heat capacity.** For a 30.5 mm 6-layer 2 oz board with FETs (≈5 g effective, c ≈ 0.8 J/g·K):

$$C_{th} \approx 4\ \text{J/K}$$

**Thermal resistance to ambient** (prop wash, about 10 m/s across a 25×25 mm area, h ≈ 80 W/m²K, two sides plus wires):

$$R_{th,BA} \approx 8\ \text{K/W}$$

**Board temperature over time:**

$$T_B(t) = T_A + P\,R_{th}\,(1 - e^{-t/\tau}), \quad \tau = R_{th}C_{th} \approx 32\ \text{s}$$

$$\frac{dT}{dt} \approx \frac{P}{C_{th}} = 26\ \text{K/s}$$
