## 0. Scope, Architecture Correction, and Assumptions

Two points need settling before the math.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In any credible AIO design, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and emits four DShot600 streams. Each ESC channel runs its own commutation MCU (AM32 firmware on an AT32F421 or STM32G071), which generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The 48 kHz switching analysis applies to the ESC MCUs. The DShot analysis applies to the H743.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board is a thermal edge case.** The math below shows it is survivable only for a few seconds. Treat it as a transient rating, never a continuous one.

I have no Job #45 BOM on file, so the baseline uses the parameters below. Substitute your actual datasheet values. Every equation is written so you can re-run it.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Path

In 6-step commutation, exactly two phase legs carry current at any instant: one high-side path and one low-side path.

- During PWM-on, current flows through the HS FET of the PWM leg and the LS FET of the return leg.
- During PWM-off (with synchronous rectification), current flows through the LS FET of the PWM leg and the LS FET of the return leg.

So regardless of duty cycle D, **two FET channels are always in series with the motor current**:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = 2 I^2 \, k_T \, R_{DS(on),25}$$

### 1.2 Distribution Per Device

Each of the 6 FETs carries current for an average of 1/3 of the electrical period. The split between HS and LS depends on D:

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I^2 R_{hot}, \qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(2-D)\, I^2 R_{hot}\ \ (\text{averaged over HS/LS of each leg})$$

At 80 A, high throttle is the worst case for HS FETs. Low throttle with high current (stall or punch-out at low D) is the worst case for LS FETs, which see up to about 2/3 of the conduction time.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

$$R_{hot} = 1.55 \times 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega = 1.55\,\text{m}\Omega$$

| Term | Expression | Per channel |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 2 · 80² · 1.55 mΩ | **19.84 W** |
| Copper paths | 80² · 0.3 mΩ | 1.92 W |
| Shunt | 80² · 0.25 mΩ | 1.60 W |
| **Conduction subtotal** | | **23.4 W** |

Worst single FET (LS, low D): (2/3) · 80² · 1.55 mΩ ≈ **6.6 W**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. That is one HS/LS pair per channel at any time.

### 2.1 V–I Overlap (Hard-Switched HS Turn-On/Off)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 20\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.968\ W}$$

The effective transition time is set by the Miller plateau: t ≈ Q_gd · R_g,total / (V_drv − V_plateau). With Q_gd = 9 nC, R_g,total ≈ 3 Ω, and (10 − 3.5) V, each edge is about 4 ns of Miller time, plus rise and fall of V_GS. 20 ns combined is a realistic, layout-limited figure.

### 2.2 Output Capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 2 = 55\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.067\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse Recovery (LS Body Diode After Dead Time)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

Q_rr scales roughly with I_F·di/dt, so derate upward by about 1.5× at 80 A: approximately **0.09 W**.

### 2.4 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I \cdot 2 t_{dead} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\text{n}\cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.614\ W}$$

This is the second-largest switching term. Reducing dead time to 50 ns halves it, but only if the gate driver's propagation mismatch allows it.

### 2.5 Gate Drive

$$P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 45\text{n}\cdot 10 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.043\ \text{W}$$

Most of this is dissipated in the driver and R_g, not in the FET.

### 2.6 Switching Subtotal

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| V–I overlap | 0.968 |
| Coss | 0.067 |
| Qrr (derated) | 0.090 |
| Dead time | 0.614 |
| Gate | 0.043 |
| **Total** | **1.78 W** |

## 3. Total Loss at 80 A Burst

| | Per channel | 4 channels simultaneously |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET + Cu + shunt) | 23.4 W | 93.4 W |
| Switching | 1.8 W | 7.1 W |
| **Total** | **25.2 W** | **≈100 W** |

Electrical efficiency at 80 A, D ≈ 1: P_out ≈ 25.2 V · 80 A = 2016 W, so η ≈ **98.8 %**. The efficiency is excellent. The problem is power density.

**Scaling law for re-use.** Use this to evaluate other burst currents or FET choices:

$$P_{ch}(I) \approx I^2(2R_{hot}+R_{Cu}+R_{sh}) + I\left[\tfrac{1}{2}V(t_r+t_f)f + 2V_{SD}t_{dead}f\right] + \text{const}$$

With the baseline parameters this gives:

$$P_{ch}(I) \approx 3.65\times10^{-3}\, I^2 + 0.0198\, I + 0.2\ \ [\text{W}]$$
