## 0. Architectural Findings (Read First)

Four findings drive the rest of the package.

| # | Finding | Consequence |
|---|---------|-------------|
| F1 | The H743 has only **two advanced timers** (TIM1, TIM8), each with 3 complementary pairs. It cannot natively drive 4 × 3-phase bridges at 48 kHz with dead-time insertion. | Use a **dedicated ESC MCU per motor** (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32). The H743 commands them over bidirectional DShot600. This is the standard AIO architecture. |
| F2 | "80 A" on a nano footprint (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) is **thermally a sub-second to ~2 s burst rating**, not a continuous one. See §3. | The spec must define burst as a duration plus a start temperature plus airflow. Otherwise it is not verifiable. |
| F3 | At 4 × 80 A phase current and high duty, battery DC current approaches **~300 A**. No nano power entry (pads/XT30/XT60) supports this. | 80 A should be a **per-motor phase-current transient**, not simultaneous on all four motors at full duty. |
| F4 | A shared battery-current shunt at 300 A dissipates tens of watts. | Use a ≤0.15 mΩ shunt or a Hall/derived current estimate. See §2.6. |

## 2. Electrical Loss Model

### 2.1 Commutation model (6-step trapezoidal, complementary PWM)

In each of the 6 commutation sectors, one phase is PWM-switched high side (HS), one phase is held low side (LS), and one phase floats.

With complementary (synchronous) PWM at duty D and phase current I:

- PWM-phase HS conducts for fraction D: P = I²R·D
- PWM-phase LS (synchronous freewheel) conducts for (1−D): P = I²R·(1−D)
- Held LS on the return phase conducts continuously: P = I²R

**Conduction loss per motor (duty-independent):**

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)]$$

Strictly, the dead-time intervals transfer some conduction to the body diode. That share is captured in §2.3.
