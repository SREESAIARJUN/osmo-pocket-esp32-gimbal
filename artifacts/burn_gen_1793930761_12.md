## 0. Architecture Decisions and Assumptions

The H743 should **not** commutate the motors directly. ArduPilot has no native BLDC commutation, and 6 × 4 = 24 complementary PWM gates with BEMF sensing at 48 kHz would consume the timers needed for DShot, LED, and buzzer functions. The standard, supportable architecture is:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──DShot600 bidir ×4──► 4 × ESC MCU (AT32F421, AM32 firmware)
                                                    └─► FD6288Q gate driver ─► 6 × N-MOSFET per motor
```

Every number below depends on these assumptions. Swap in your actual BOM values and the equations still hold.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value used | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Battery | V_bus | 25.2 V max (6S) | Use 25.2 V for switching loss (worst case) |
| Burst phase current per motor | I_ph | 80 A | Per-motor phase current, **not** battery current |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz | AM32 setting |
| MOSFET | — | 40 V, 3.3×3.3 DFN class | 30 V parts are inadequate for 6S with ringing |

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path
With 6-step trapezoidal commutation and complementary (synchronous) PWM, two phases conduct at any instant:

- **PWM leg:** current alternates between HS (duty D) and LS (duty 1−D, synchronous rectification).
- **Return leg:** LS is on for the full 120° sector.

Ignoring dead time, phase current therefore always flows through exactly **two** MOSFET channels in series.

### 1.2 Equations
Hot resistance:

$$R_{DS,hot} = k_T \cdot R_{25} = 1.5 \times 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega = 1.8\,\text{m}\Omega$$

Instantaneous conduction loss per motor:

$$P_{cond,FET} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS,hot} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.0018 = \mathbf{23.0\ W}$$

Copper loss per motor:

$$P_{cu} = I_{ph}^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.0005 = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-device distribution
Each FET conducts during 2 of 6 sectors (1/3 of the electrical period). Treat the 23 W as shared by 6 FETs:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx 23.0/6 \approx 3.8\ \text{W (average)}$$

The **peak** instantaneous loss in the return-leg LS FET during its sector is:

$$P_{pk} = 80^2 \times 0.0018 = 11.5\ \text{W}$$

That peak matters for junction ripple at low eRPM, where the sector duration becomes comparable to the die thermal time constant (≈1–5 ms). Near-stall operation at 80 A is therefore the worst case.

### 1.4 Battery vs. phase current
$$I_{bat} \approx D \cdot I_{ph}\cdot\eta^{-1}$$

At D = 0.75, 80 A phase current corresponds to ≈ 60 A battery current per motor. Make sure the "80 A burst" spec states which current it refers to. Marketing figures usually quote battery current, which implies even higher phase current.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches, so there is one hard-switched HS/LS pair per motor at any time.

### 2.1 V-I overlap (hard switching)
$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 27\text{ns} \times 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.31\ W}$$

The driver-limited transition time is estimated as t ≈ Q_gd / I_g:
- 10 nC / ~0.65 A effective ≈ 15 ns rise (source current reduced by the gate resistor and plateau voltage).

### 2.2 Body-diode conduction during dead time
$$P_{dt} = V_F I_{ph} \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{nC}\times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

Q_rr scales with di/dt and I_F, so the datasheet value at 80 A may be 2–3× higher. Budget 0.15
