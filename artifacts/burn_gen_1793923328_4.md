## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility

Two points need to be settled before any equations.

1. **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz motor PWM.** In a 4-in-1 AIO, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600 to **four dedicated ESC MCUs** (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). Each ESC MCU drives a 3-phase gate driver and 6 MOSFETs at 48 kHz. The model below is partitioned the same way.
2. **80 A burst on a nano (20×20 mm) board is not thermally credible** except for sub-second transients. The model below shows why and gives a burst-duration limit. My recommendation is **30.5×30.5 mm with 5×6 mm FETs** for an 80 A rating, or a derated **≤45 A burst** on 20×20 mm.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### Temperature-Dependent Resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R = 1.5 × 1.425 = **2.14 mΩ**.

### Current Path

In 6-step commutation with synchronous PWM, exactly two phases carry current at any time:

- **PWM'd leg:** current flows through the high-side FET for duty D and through the low-side FET for (1−D). One device in this leg is always conducting.
- **Static leg:** its low-side FET is on continuously.

So at every instant, two R_DS(on) are in series, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) = 2 \times 6400 \times 2.14\text{m} = \mathbf{27.4\ W}$$

### Thermal-Runaway Check

Loss rises with temperature through R_DS(on), which feeds back into junction temperature:

$$T_j = T_a + R_{th}\,P(T_j)$$

This has a stable solution only if

$$R_{th}\cdot 2I^2R_{25}\alpha < 1 \;\Rightarrow\; R_{th} < \frac{1}{2(6400)(1.5\text{m})(0.005)} = 10.4\ \text{K/W (per motor cluster)}$$

A 20×20 board cluster with no airflow has R_th ≈ 15–25 K/W. **It has no steady-state solution at 80 A**, so a burst limit is mandatory.

### Dead-Time Body-Diode Loss

$$P_{dt} = V_f\,I\,(2t_d)\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

### Shunt and Copper Loss (Per Motor)

**Shunt** (0.25 mΩ low-side or phase shunt):

$$P_{shunt} = 80^2 \times 0.25\text{m} = \mathbf{1.6\ W}$$

**Copper:** 2 oz (70 µm) copper has a sheet resistance of

$$R_\square = \frac{\rho}{t} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}}{70\times10^{-6}} = 0.246\ \text{mΩ/□}$$

That rises to 0.32 mΩ/□ at 100 °C.

- Phase-to-pad path ≈ 3 □ per layer, 4 layers in parallel, gives ≈ 0.24 mΩ per phase.
- Two phases are in the current path:

$$P_{Cu} \approx 2 \times 6400 \times 0.24\text{m} = \mathbf{3.1\ W}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the PWM'd leg hard-switches. Its high side switches hard; the low side switches at near-zero V_DS because the body diode is already conducting, so it is soft.

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½ V I (t_r + t_f) f_sw = ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 kHz | **1.94 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr V f_sw = 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 kHz | 0.05 W |
| C_oss discharge | ½ C_oss V² f_sw = 0.5 × 1.5 nF × 635 × 48 kHz | 0.02 W |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2 Q_g V_GS f_sw = 2 × 45 nC × 10 × 48 kHz | 0.04 W* |

\*Gate-drive power is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET.

**Edge-rate tradeoff.** Halving t_r + t_f saves about 1 W but increases dv/dt. With 1 nH of loop inductance at 80 A, the overshoot reaches

$$\Delta V = L\,\frac{di}{dt} \approx 1\text{n} \times \frac{80}{20\text{n}} = 4\ \text{V}$$

That leaves margin on a 40 V part at 25.2 V bus. Keep the power loop under 1.5 nH.

### Loss Budget at 80 A Burst, T_j = 110 °C

| Component | Per motor | ×4 (board) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 27.4 W | 109.6 W |
| Dead time | 1.2 W | 4.9 W |
| Switching (total) | 2.0 W | 8.0 W |
| Shunt | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| Copper | 3.1 W | 12.4 W |
| **Total** | **35.3 W** | **141 W** |

Electrical efficiency at full burst (4 × 80 A × 22.2 V ≈ 7.1 kW) is about 98%, which is fine. The problem is power **density**: 141 W in 4–9 cm² of board.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Per-FET Average Dissipation

Commutation rotates every 60° electrical, typically tens to hundreds of µs, which is far shorter than the FET thermal time constants. Loss is therefore averaged across all 6 devices:

$$P_{FET} \approx \frac{27.4 + 1.2 + 2.0}{6} = \mathbf{5.1\ W/FET}$$
