# STM32H743 4-in-1 AIO: 80 A Burst Power-Stage Model, DShot DMA Plan, and Netlist

I don't have the Job #41 requirements document, so this design states every assumption explicitly. The equations are parametric, so you can substitute your actual MOSFET datasheet values and board dimensions.

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Finding

Two points shape everything that follows.

1. **The H743 does not run the motors.** In any practical 4-in-1 AIO, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot. Each of the 4 ESC channels has its own small MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071) running AM32 or BLHeli_32, which generates the 48 kHz commutation PWM.
   - The H743 only emits **bidirectional DShot600** and captures the eRPM telemetry.
   - The 48 kHz switching-loss model therefore belongs to the ESC MCU domain. The DMA model belongs to the H743 domain.

2. **80 A burst on a "nano" (20×20 mm) board is not thermally viable.** Section 3 shows that the board's heat capacity limits all-motor 80 A operation to roughly 1 s before exceeding safe copper and junction temperatures. I recommend one of the following:
   - (a) Move to a 30.5×30.5 mm or larger form factor with 5×6 mm MOSFETs and a heat spreader.
   - (b) Re-rate the board to roughly 35–45 A burst and 25–30 A continuous on 20×20.

The model below is kept general so you can evaluate either path.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-Dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 115 °C:

$$R_{DS} = 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega\,(1 + 0.0045 \times 90) = 1.69\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Current Path in 6-Step with Synchronous PWM

In each 60° sector, exactly two phases conduct (A positive, B negative, C floating). With duty D:

- **Leg A:** the HS FET conducts for D·T_sw. The LS FET conducts for (1−D)·T_sw as the synchronous rectifier.
- **Leg B:** the LS FET is on for the whole sector.

So **exactly two R_DS(on) drops are in the current path at all times**, independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Including the inductor ripple, with ΔI being the peak-to-peak phase ripple:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

Ripple estimate for a typical 5-inch-class motor (L_phase-phase ≈ 20 µH, worst case at D = 0.5):

$$\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\,\mu\text{H} \times 48\,\text{kHz}} \approx 6.6\ \text{A}$$

The ripple adds only 0.07 % to I², so it is negligible at 80 A.

$$\boxed{P_{cond,ESC} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.69\,\text{m}\Omega = 21.6\ \text{W}}$$
