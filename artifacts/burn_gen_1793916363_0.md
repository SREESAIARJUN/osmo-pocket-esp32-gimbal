# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #55): Engineering Model

## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Feasibility Flags

Three issues need resolving before the math, because they change the design.

1. **The H743 does not run the ESC.** ArduPilot on the H743 produces bi-directional DShot600 frames. Each of the four motor channels needs its own ESC MCU, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 (or BLHeli_32). That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM and handles commutation. The model below treats these as two separate domains connected by four DShot nets.
2. **"80 A burst" per motor on a nano board is not feasible.** A 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm board uses 3.3×3.3 mm PDFN MOSFETs. Section 1.5 shows that 4 × 80 A drives the board copper temperature up at about 34 K/s. I model both cases:
   - **Case A:** 80 A per motor, which is what the spec literally says.
   - **Case B:** 80 A aggregate, or about 20 A per motor. This is realistic for nano class.

   Please confirm which one the spec means.
3. **The netlists are reference-level.** They cover topology, critical nets and component values. They are not a fab-ready KiCad export. Part numbers are representative and should be checked against current datasheets and availability.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Paths in 6-Step with Synchronous Rectification

At any instant, two phases are active. The PWM'd leg alternates the DC current between its high-side switch (for duty D) and its low-side switch (for 1−D). The static leg holds its low-side switch on.

- PWM'd leg loss: D·I²R + (1−D)·I²R = I²R, independent of D.
- Static low-side switch loss: I²R.

The per-ESC conduction loss is therefore:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds,hot} + I^2 R_{Cu}$$

Here R_Cu is the summed copper and solder resistance in the current path. It is about 0.5 mΩ for a well-laid nano board.

Each phase leg is active 2/3 of the electrical period, so the average dissipation per MOSFET is:

$$P_{FET,avg} = \frac{2 I^2 R_{ds,hot}}{6}$$

The two FETs in one leg do not split this evenly; the split depends on D. At D → 1 the high-side switch takes nearly all of it.

### 1.2 Numbers

| | Case A: I = 80 A | Case B: I = 20 A |
|---|---|---|
| 2·I²·R_hot | 2 · 6400 · 0.0024 = **30.7 W** | **1.92 W** |
| I²·R_Cu | 6400 · 0.0005 = **3.2 W** | **0.2 W** |
| **P_cond per ESC** | **33.9 W** | **2.12 W** |
| Average per FET | 5.1 W | 0.32 W |
| Worst-case single FET (high side, D ≈ 1, while conducting) | 15.4 W | 0.96 W |

R_ds itself depends on T_j. The self-consistent iteration is R(T) = R₂₅[1 + α(T − 25)] with α ≈ 0.005/K. Case A does not converge below the 175 °C T_j(max), which means it is thermal-runaway-limited and can only exist as a short transient (§4).

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the PWM'd leg switches. That gives one hard-switched turn-on and one turn-off per PWM period.

### 2.1 Hard-Switching (V–I Overlap)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

The overlap time is set by the Miller plateau, t ≈ Q_gd / I_g(plateau). Assume about 0.6 A effective plateau current from a FD6288-class driver with R_g = 4.7 Ω, plus some ringing. That gives t_r + t_f ≈ 30 ns.

- Case A: 0.5 · 25.2 · 80 · 30 ns · 48 k = **1.45 W**
- Case B: **0.36 W**

### 2.2 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD} \cdot I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw}$$

- Case A: 0.8 · 80 · 200 ns · 48 k = **0.61 W**
- Case B: **0.15 W**

### 2.3 Reverse Recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\,\text{nC} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\,\text{k} = \textbf{0.06 W}$$

Q_rr scales with di/dt and I_F, so in Case A it could be about 2× this.

### 2.4 Output Capacitance

$$P_{oss} = C_{oss,eff}\,V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1\,\text{nF} \cdot 635 \cdot 48\,\text{k} = \textbf{0.03 W}$$

This counts both FETs in the leg, each with an energy of ½CV².

### 2.5 Gate Drive (Taken From the Driver/LDO Rail, Not the FETs)

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 30\,\text{nC} \cdot 10 \cdot 48\,\text{k} = 0.029\ \text{W per ESC}$$

### 2.6 Per-ESC Totals

| Loss | Case A (80 A) | Case B (20 A) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 30.7 W | 1.92 W |
| Conduction (copper) | 3.2 W | 0.20 W |
| Switching overlap | 1.45 W | 0.36 W |
| Dead time | 0.61 W | 0.15 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss | ≈0.12 W | ≈0.09 W |
| **Per ESC** | **≈36.1 W** | **≈2.72 W** |
| **Board (×4)** | **≈144 W** | **≈10.9 W** |

At 80 A, conduction is about 94% of the loss. Lowering the switching frequency barely helps. The levers that matter are R_ds(on), the number of parallel FETs, and copper.

**Optimum frequency check.** Switching loss scales with f. The ripple-driven motor copper loss scales as 1/f². With low-inductance (≈10 µH) quad motors, 48 kHz is a reasonable trade-off. 24 kHz would save about 1 W per ESC in Case A but increase motor ripple and audible noise.
