# STM32H743 Flight Controller + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Architecture, Loss Model, DShot Allocation, and Netlists

## 0. Architecture Decision

The STM32H743 cannot run the four motor commutation loops directly in a credible ArduPilot design. ArduPilot does not commutate BLDC motors, and 4 × 48 kHz complementary PWM plus BEMF/FOC would consume all of TIM1/TIM8 and the ADCs. The architecture used here is:

| Domain | Silicon | Role |
|---|---|---|
| FC | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100) at 400 MHz | ArduPilot; bi-directional DShot600 master; RPM telemetry consumer |
| ESC ×4 | AT32F421K8U7 (or EFM8BB51 / STM32G071) running AM32 | 48 kHz complementary PWM; commutation; eRPM reply |
| Gate drive ×4 | FD6288Q (3-phase, 250 V, bootstrap) | High-side and low-side drive with 6 FETs per ESC |
| Power stage | 24 × 40 V N-MOSFET, 5×6 or 3.3×3.3 PQFN | 4 ESCs × 3 half-bridges |

**Assumptions.** Every number below derives from this table. Replace the values with your actual BOM datasheet values.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification (AM32 complementary PWM), current always flows through exactly two FET positions in series:

- One high-side position. This is the HS FET during the on-time D, or the complementary LS FET of the same leg during 1 − D.
- One low-side FET of the sinking phase, which is on 100% of that 60° sector.

The total conduction loss per ESC is therefore independent of duty to first order:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j) + I^2 R_{Cu}$$

### 1.2 Temperature-coupled solution

R_ds depends on T_j, so solve the two equations together:

$$P(T_j) = 2I^2R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\qquad T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}\,R_{\theta jc}$$

At 80 A:

- Cold (25 °C): 2 × 6400 × 1.0 mΩ = **12.8 W**
- Hot (125 °C, R = 1.5 mΩ): 2 × 6400 × 1.5 mΩ = **19.2 W**

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each FET position carries current for 1/3 of the electrical period. The average per FET is:

$$P_{FET,avg} = \frac{19.2}{6} = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

The instantaneous per-FET peak is I²·R = 9.6 W, sustained for one sector. At 20 krpm with 14 poles, the electrical frequency is 2.33 kHz, so a sector lasts about 71 µs. This is far shorter than the FET thermal time constant of about 10 ms, so the junction sees the average:

$$\Delta T_{jc} = 3.2 \times 1.2 = 3.8\ \text{K}$$

The junction is not the bottleneck. The board is.

### 1.4 Copper loss

Assume 2 oz copper on the phase and power planes, 8 layers, with 4 layers paralleled for power. The effective path from the battery pad through the half-bridge to the motor pad is about 0.25 mΩ.

$$P_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.25\text{ m}\Omega = 1.6\ \text{W per ESC}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches hard. The other legs are static within a sector.

### 2.1 Transition times (gate-charge method)

Turn-on:

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_g} = \frac{10-3.5}{11} = 0.59\text{ A}$$

$$t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = \frac{12\text{ nC}}{0.59} = 20.3\text{ ns}$$

Turn-off:

$$I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_g} = 0.32\text{ A}$$

$$t_f = \frac{12\text{ nC}}{0.32} = 37.7\text{ ns}$$

To balance the edges, add a turn-off diode (D + 2.2 Ω) on each gate. This gives t_f ≈ 12 nC / (3.5 / 3.2) ≈ 11 ns. The baseline below omits the diode.

### 2.2 Loss components per ESC

**V·I overlap:**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 58\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{2.81\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction:**

$$P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2t_{dead} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = V \cdot Q_{rr} \cdot f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 50\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

**C_oss charging (both FETs in the leg):**

$$P_{oss} = C_{oss}V^2 f_{sw} = 1.5\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.046\ W}$$

**Gate drive** (dissipated in the driver and R_g, not the FETs):

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 2.3 Totals per ESC at 80 A, hot

| Component | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 19.20 |
| Copper | 1.60 |
| V·I switching | 2.81 |
| Dead-time | 0.61 |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.15 |
| **Total** | **24.4 W** |

The 4-in-1 total is **97.5 W** with all four motors at 80 A.

Sensitivity: P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz, the switching component drops by about 1.8 W per ESC, but current ripple and audible noise increase. The 48 kHz choice costs about 7% extra loss at burst. It is a good trade at hover currents of 10–20 A, where conduction loss falls by I² and switching loss falls only by I.

## 3. Thermal Model and Burst Duration

### 3.1 Sanity check on "80A burst" for a nano form factor

Four ESCs at 80 A each draw 320 A × 25 V = **8 kW** from the battery. This is a system-level problem, not just an ESC problem:

- XT60 connector: rated about 60 A continuous.
- Bulk capacitor ripple current.
- Battery C-rating: 320 A requires a 1500 mAh pack rated above 200C.

Realistic spec language is **"80A per channel burst, single channel, ≤ 5 s"** plus a defined continuous rating. Size everything below for the four-channel worst case and then derate.
