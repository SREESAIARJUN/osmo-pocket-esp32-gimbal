## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Assumptions

Two points about the brief need settling before the math.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot is a flight-control stack, not a BLDC commutation firmware. The valid architecture has two parts:
- **FC:** an STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600.
- **4-in-1 ESC:** four dedicated ESC MCUs run AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical parts are the AT32F421 or STM32G071. Each drives a 3-phase gate driver such as the FD6288Q and six MOSFETs.

The 48 kHz switching frequency belongs to the ESC MCUs. The H743 owns DShot timing and telemetry decode.

**2. 80 A burst means 80 A per channel phase current.** On a nano (20×20 mm) board, 4 × 80 A at 100 % duty would draw 320 A from the battery. That is not survivable through an XT30/XT60 pigtail or a 20 mm board. Accordingly:
- The model computes per-channel losses at 80 A.
- It treats the simultaneous four-channel case as a bounded transient.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A burst)

### 1.1 Per-FET conduction

In 6-step commutation, each FET conducts for 120° of each 360° electrical cycle. With synchronous rectification, two FETs are in the current path at all times: one high-side and one low-side.

$$I_{RMS,FET} = I_{ph}\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{3}} = 80\sqrt{0.333} = 46.2\ \text{A}$$

$$R_{DS,hot} = k_T \cdot R_{25} = 1.6 \times 0.90 = 1.44\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond,FET} = I_{RMS}^2 \cdot R_{DS,hot} = 46.2^2 \times 1.44\text{e-}3 = \mathbf{3.07\ W}$$
