## 0. Architectural Clarifications

Three issues shape everything downstream.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot is flight-control firmware. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, for example 4× AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to those MCUs. "STM32H743 4-in-1 ESC" therefore means an AIO stack: an H743 FC plus a 4-channel ESC.
2. **80 A on a nano (20×20 mm) board is thermal-capacity limited, not resistance limited.** The model in §2 shows the realistic burst window is about 1 s with all four channels at 80 A. Continuous rating will be roughly 20–25 A per channel. Treat "80 A burst" as a single-channel or short-transient rating, or move to 30.5×30.5 mm.
3. **Complementary timer outputs (TIMx_CHxN) cannot do input capture.** Bidirectional DShot needs input capture on the same pin. All four motor outputs must therefore use non-N timer channels.

## 2. Loss Model

### 2.1 Conduction loss

With trapezoidal commutation and synchronous rectification, exactly two FETs carry the phase current at every instant:

- the high-side FET of the sourcing leg (or its complementary low-side during the off-time), and
- the low-side FET of the sinking leg.

Total conduction loss per motor:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C, R_DS ≈ 1.0 × 1.51 ≈ 1.5 mΩ:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.5\,\text{mΩ} = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

Each FET conducts for 120° of the electrical cycle. Per-FET average:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \tfrac{1}{3} I_{ph}^2 R_{DS} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$
