## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs. On every production AIO "nano" board, the system splits into three parts:

| Block | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VI/ZI @ 480 MHz | Runs ArduPilot. Generates bidirectional DShot600 and decodes eRPM telemetry. |
| ESC MCUs (×4, or ×1 quad-capable) | AT32F421K8U7 (AM32) or EFM8BB51 (Bluejay) | Run commutation and the 48 kHz complementary PWM. |
| Gate drivers (×4) | FD6288Q (3-phase, 250 V, bootstrap) | Level-shift MCU PWM to the gates. |

The 48 kHz switching analysis therefore applies to the ESC side, and the DShot/DMA analysis applies to the H743. ## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation topology

AM32 and Bluejay use 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous (complementary) PWM. In each 60° sector, one phase is PWM-switched, one is held low, and one floats for BEMF sensing.

- **Switched leg:** the high-side FET conducts for duty D; the low-side FET (synchronous rectifier) conducts for 1 − D.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET conducts for 100 % of the sector.

So at every instant, exactly two channels carry the phase current. The total conduction loss is independent of duty:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)\,\big[D + (1-D)\big] + I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.2 mΩ × 1.70 = **2.04 mΩ**

### 1.3 Ripple contribution

Phase-current ripple at the worst case D = 0.5, with V_BEMF ≈ D·V_bus:

$$\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{BEMF})\,D}{L\,f_{sw}} = \frac{12.6 \times 0.5}{20\,\mu\text{H} \times 48\,\text{kHz}} = 6.56\ \text{A}_{pp}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.6 = 6403.6\ \text{A}^2$$

The ripple term adds 0.06 %, so it is negligible at 48 kHz. That is one benefit of the high switching frequency.

### 1.4 Results at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond per channel | Per FET (2 conducting) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.20 mΩ | 15.4 W | 7.7 W |
| 100 °C | 1.83 mΩ | 23.4 W | 11.7 W |
| 125 °C | 2.04 mΩ | **26.1 W** | **13.1 W** |

### 1.5 PCB copper loss (often larger than designers expect)

Current path: battery pad → bus planes → FET → phase pad → motor wire pad. Using 2 oz copper, 6 conducting layers, and an estimated 0.4 mΩ effective series path per channel:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu}\,(1 + 0.0039\,\Delta T) \approx 6400 \times 0.4\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.3 \approx \mathbf{3.3\ W\ per\ channel}$$

Shared DC-bus copper carries the sum of all four channels' input currents; account for it separately in the plane design.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. Per channel, one high-side FET hard-switches and its complementary low-side FET commutates via its body diode during the dead time.

### 2.1 Gate currents and transition times

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{pl}}{R_{on}} = \frac{10 - 3.5}{10} = 0.65\ \text{A} \qquad t_{on} = \frac{Q_{gs2} + Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = \frac{15\ \text{nC}}{0.65\ \text{A}} = 23\ \text{ns}$$

$$I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{off}} = \frac{3.5}{8} = 0.44\ \text{A} \qquad t_{off} = \frac{15\ \text{nC}}{0.44\ \text{A}} = 34\ \text{ns}$$
