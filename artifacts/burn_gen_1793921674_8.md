# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal, and DShot Architecture (Job #67)

## 0. Scope and Feasibility

Before the math, two architectural facts shape everything below.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In any 4-in-1 stack, each motor channel runs on its own ESC MCU. This design uses 4× AT32F421K8U7 running AM32, which supports 48 kHz PWM and bidirectional DShot. The H743 only generates DShot600 and decodes eRPM telemetry. The ESC model (Sections 1–3) and the FC timer model (Section 4) are therefore separate domains, joined by four signal wires.
2. **"Nano" (20×20 mm) cannot carry 4×80 A, even as a burst.** Sections 2 and 3 show this quantitatively. The netlists assume a **30.5×30.5 mm ESC** with an optional 20×20 FC on top. 80 A is treated as a **per-channel, time-limited burst**.

## 1. Electrical Loss Model (Per Channel, Six-Step with Synchronous Rectification)

### 1.1 Conduction loss

During any 60° commutation sector, two phases are active:

- **Phase A (PWM leg):** the high side conducts for fraction D. The complementary low side conducts for (1 − D).
- **Phase B (sink leg):** the low side is on for the whole sector.

Current I always flows through exactly two R_DS(on) in series, so:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) \qquad \text{(independent of } D\text{, ignoring dead time)}$$

Including inductor ripple, where ΔI is peak-to-peak ripple:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I \approx \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

For L_ph ≈ 15 µH and D = 0.5, ΔI ≈ 8.75 A. This adds 6.4 A² on top of 6400 A², which is negligible (0.1 %).

At hot R_DS = 1.2 mΩ × 1.5 = **1.8 mΩ**:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.0018 = \mathbf{23.04\ W}$$

Each FET conducts 120° of the electrical cycle. The die thermal time constant (around 1 ms) is much longer than the commutation period at flight eRPM, so we can average:

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx 23.04 / 6 = \mathbf{3.84\ W}$$

### 1.2 Switching loss at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Hard switching occurs on the high side; the low side switches near zero voltage after the dead time.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·30 ns·48 kHz | 1.452 W |
| Coss charging | Q_oss·V·f_sw = 40 nC·25.2·48 kHz | 0.048 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 30 nC·25.2·48 kHz | 0.036 W |
| Dead-time body diode | V_f·I·2·t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·200 ns·48 kHz | 0.614 W |
| Gate drive (lost in driver, not FET) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw = 2·50 nC·10 V·48 kHz | 0.048 W |
| **Total P_sw (in FETs)** | | **≈ 2.15 W** |

Effect of PWM frequency:

$$\frac{\partial P_{sw}}{\partial f_{sw}} \approx 44.8\ \mu\text{W/Hz}$$

- Moving from 24 kHz to 48 kHz costs about 1.07 W per channel.
- In return, current ripple halves, which reduces motor iron and copper ripple loss and acoustic noise.
- Dead time is the largest tunable term. Reducing 100 ns to 50 ns saves 0.31 W per channel.

### 1.3 Copper and shunt loss

Assume a phase path of 0.4 mΩ (2 oz inner pours plus pads) and a 0.25 mΩ shunt carrying bus current I·D:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{trace} + (I\!\cdot\!D)^2 R_{shunt} = 6400(0.0004) + 1600(0.00025) \approx \mathbf{2.96\ W}$$

### 1.4 Totals

| | Per channel | 4 channels at simultaneous burst |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 23.04 W | 92.2 W |
| Switching | 2.15 W | 8.6 W |
| Copper / shunt | 2.96 W | 11.8 W |
| **Total** | **28.2 W** | **112.6 W** |

**Efficiency:** at D = 0.5, the battery delivers 25.2 × 40 = 1008 W per channel, so η ≈ 97.2 %.

**Temperature feedback:** because R_DS depends on T_j, the model must be iterated:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_a + P\big(T_j^{(n)}\big) \cdot Z_\theta(t)$$

Convergence fails (thermal runaway) when:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\cdot Z_\theta \geq 1, \qquad \frac{\partial P_{cond}}{\partial T} = 2I^2 R_{25}(0.0045) = 0.069\ \text{W/K per channel}$$
