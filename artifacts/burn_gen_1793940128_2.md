# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC "Nano" Stack (Job #32): Power, Thermal, and DShot Engineering Model

## 0. Scope Corrections Before the Math

These issues change the design, so they come first.

1. **The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In a 4-in-1 stack, each ESC channel runs its own motor controller MCU, for example an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or an EFM8 running BLHeli_S. The H743 flight controller sends DShot600 to those MCUs and receives eRPM telemetry from them. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCU, not by the H743. I model both sides separately below.
2. **An "80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) footprint is a transient rating, not a thermal steady state.** The numbers below show that the board dissipates about 90 W with all four channels at 80 A. That load is survivable for roughly 1–3 s, not continuously. Treat the 80 A figure as a datasheet or marketing burst number and design protection around it.
3. **Representative component values.** The device parameters below are representative of 40 V, sub-1 mΩ, 3.3×3.3 or 5×6 mm power MOSFETs. Substitute the actual datasheet values for your BOM. The netlist is a reference topology, not fab-ready output. It must go through ERC/DRC, an SI review, and a datasheet pin check.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

In 6-step commutation, two phases conduct at any instant:

- **Phase A (PWM leg):** the high-side conducts for D, and the low-side synchronous-rectifies for (1−D).
- **Phase B (return leg):** the low-side conducts continuously at 100 %.

The hot on-resistance is:

$$R_{hot} = k_T R_{25} = 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega$$

Conduction loss per ESC channel:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{hot}\cdot 1 = 2 I^2 R_{hot}$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

The loss is independent of D with synchronous rectification. Per-device loss is not uniform:

| Device | Loss @ 80 A |
|---|---|
| Return-leg low-side (100 % on) | 6400 × 1.5 m = **9.6 W** (hot spot) |
| PWM high-side | 9.6·D W |
| PWM low-side | 9.6·(1−D) W |

Each FET conducts for only one or two of the six commutation sectors per electrical cycle. Time-averaged per device, the loss is about 19.2/6 × 2 ≈ 6.4 W. The transient junction rise, however, is set by the 9.6 W instantaneous value if the electrical frequency is low, for example at startup or stall.

**Copper and trace loss (often forgotten).** Assume about 0.3 mΩ from the phase pad to the motor wire plus a share of the bus copper (2 oz, short):

$$P_{Cu} \approx 80^2 \times 0.3\text{m}\Omega \approx 1.9\ \text{W/channel}$$

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

**Hard-switching V·I overlap (high-side only; the low-side soft-switches through its body diode):**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot20\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (two transitions per period):**

$$P_{dt} = V_F I \cdot 2t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot600\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.84\ W}$$

This is larger than the V·I loss. Cutting dead time to 150 ns, if the driver propagation-delay matching allows it, saves about 0.9 W per channel.

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 60\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.073\ \text{W}$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \approx 0.048\ \text{W}$$

**Gate drive (two switching FETs):**

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET.

### Per-channel total at the 80 A burst

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.20 |
| V·I overlap | 0.97 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.84 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.12 |
| Gate | 0.05 |
| Copper | 1.90 |
| **Total per channel** | **≈ 24.1 W** |
| **4 channels** | **≈ 96 W** |

Efficiency check: output is about 25.2 × 80 × D_eff. At full throttle, roughly 2 kW per channel goes to the motor, so the ESC efficiency is about 98.8 %. The efficiency is good. The power density is the problem: 96 W in about 4 cm².

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Steady state (shows why the 80 A rating is a burst rating)

Effective board-to-ambient resistance for a 20×20 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz board in prop wash:

- Forced convection: h ≈ 60–100 W/m²K.
- Area: 2 × 4 cm² + edges ≈ 9 cm².

$$R_{\theta,BA} \approx \frac{1}{h A} = \frac{1}{80\times9\times10^{-4}} \approx 14\ \text{K/W}$$

$$\Delta T_{ss} = 96 \times 14 \approx 1300\ \text{K} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{impossible; burst-only rating confirmed}$$

**Maximum continuous rating,** with the board allowed a 60 K rise (60 K / 14 K/W ≈ 4.3 W total, about 1.07 W per channel):

$$I_{cont} \approx \sqrt{\frac{1.07}{2R_{hot} + R_{Cu} + \text{sw}}} \approx 18\text{–}20\ \text{A/channel (all four loaded)}$$

This sits in line with typical nano 4-in-1 continuous ratings, which are usually quoted optimistically at 25–35 A with good airflow.

### 4.2 Transient (adiabatic burst) model

**Board heat capacity.** Assume about 2.5 g of copper (C_p = 0.385 J/gK), 2 g of FR4 (≈ 1.1 J/gK), and 1.2 g of FETs and solder:

$$C_{th} \approx 0.96 + 2.2 + 0.6 \approx 3.8\ \text{J/K}$$

**Time to a 60 K board rise** at 96 W, starting from 40 °C and reaching 100 °C:

$$t_{burst} = \frac{C_{th}\Delta T}{P - \Delta T/R_\theta} = \frac{3.8\times60}{96-4.3} \approx \mathbf{2.5\ s}$$

**Junction-over-board rise** for the hottest FET (9.6 W). Use transient Z_θJC at 1 s ≈ 0.8 K/W and a solder/pad interface ≈ 0.7 K/W:

$$\Delta T_{j-b} \approx 9.6\times1.5 = 14.4\ \text{K}$$

**Burst limit for T_j,max = 150 °C with 25 K derating to 125 °C:**

$$T_j = T_{board}(t) + 14.4 \le 125\ ^\circ\text{C} \Rightarrow T_{board} \le 110\ ^\circ\text{C}$$

$$t_{burst,max} \approx \frac{3.8\times(110-40)}{91.7} \approx \mathbf{2.9\ s}$$

**Recovery time constant:**

$$\tau = R_\theta C_{th} \approx 14\times3.8 \approx 53\ \text{s}$$

So the burst duty is roughly 3 s on, then at least 60 s at hover current.

### 4.3 Required firmware protection

- **ESC side (AM32):** set the temperature limit to 100–110 °C on the NTC, add a current-limit PID, and enable stall protection. A stall at low eRPM concentrates 9.6 W into one FET indefinitely.
- **FC side (ArduPilot):** set `BATT_*` current limits. Use `MOT_BAT_CURR_MAX` to throttle-limit total current. For example, for a 4×40 A sustained budget, set it to about 160 A with `MOT_BAT_CURR_TC` = 5 s.

### 4.4 Bulk capacitor ripple

Worst-case RMS ripple occurs at D = 0.5:

$$I_{C,rms} = I\sqrt{D(1-D)} = 40\ \text{A per channel}$$

The four channels are not phase-interleaved (each ESC MCU runs asynchronously), so plan for roughly 40–60 A_rms total.

Implications:

- A nano board cannot host enough capacitors for this.
