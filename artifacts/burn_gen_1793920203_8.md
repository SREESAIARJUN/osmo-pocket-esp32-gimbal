## 0. Architecture Decisions and Assumptions

Two architectural points shape every calculation below.

**1. The H743 does not switch the MOSFETs.** ArduPilot has no motor-commutation firmware. The topology is therefore:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──DShot600 bidir──► 4× ESC MCU (AT32F421 / AM32) ──► 4× 3-phase driver ──► 24 MOSFETs
```

The 48 kHz PWM is an ESC-firmware setting (AM32 `PWM frequency`), not an H743 timer. The H743's job is DShot generation, eRPM capture, and the control loop.

**2. "Nano" and 80 A are in tension.** A 20×20 mm board cannot carry 24 power FETs rated for 80 A per channel. I have assumed a **30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz board using 5×6 mm FETs**. If the target really is 20×20 mm with 3×3 mm FETs, R_DS(on) roughly doubles and the burst time computed in §3 drops to about 1 s. **Please confirm the form factor.**

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation geometry

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous complementary PWM.

- At any instant, two legs carry I_ph.
- One leg is PWM'd: the HS FET conducts for duty D, and the LS FET of the same leg (synchronous rectification) for 1−D.
- The other leg's LS FET is on continuously.
- Each phase is active for 120° of every 360° of electrical rotation.

**Instantaneous bridge conduction loss** (independent of D, because sync rectification replaces the diode):

$$P_{cond,bridge}(T_j) = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

**Temperature dependence:**

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

**Per-FET average.** Each FET carries current one third of the time (worst case, D → 1, for the HS FET):

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}(T_j)}{3}$$
