# STM32H743 AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC) Reference Design Analysis

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Stated Assumptions

Three issues should be settled before the math. They change the model.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In any ArduPilot-compatible AIO, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. Typical parts are the AT32F421, STM32G071 or GD32E230. The ESC MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The model below reflects that split.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is thermally marginal.** The numbers below show this. I treat 80 A as the **per-channel phase current** and show the burst duration it can survive.
3. **Component values are assumptions, not datasheet-verified selections.** Substitute your actual BOM parameters into the equations.

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 25.2 V | 6S, fully charged (worst case for switching) |
| I_ph | 80 A | Burst phase current, per channel |
| f_sw | 48 kHz | ESC PWM frequency |
| R_ds25 | 1.5 mΩ | Per FET, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN, 30–40 V class, V_gs ≈ 10 V |
| α | 0.0045 /K | Temperature coefficient of R_ds(on) |
| t_r, t_f | 15 ns each | Effective V–I overlap times |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-step synchronous commutation

Consider one 60° commutation sector:
- **Phase A high side** switches at duty D.
- **Phase A low side** conducts the complementary (1−D) interval as a synchronous rectifier.
- **Phase B low side** is on for 100% of the sector.

The conduction loss per channel is:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R(T_j)\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I^2 R(T_j)$$

This total is independent of D. Only its split between the FETs depends on D.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 100 °C:

$$R = 1.5 \times 1.3375 = 2.006\ \text{mΩ}$$

$$I^2 R = 6400 \times 2.006\times10^{-3} = 12.84\ \text{W per conducting FET}$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 25.7\ \text{W}, \qquad P_{cond,4ch} \approx 102.7\ \text{W}$$

### 1.3 Electrothermal fixed point and runaway criterion

With $T_j = T_a + P\,R_\theta$ and $P = I^2 R(T_j)$, the self-consistent loss is:

$$P = \frac{I^2 R_{ds25}[1+\alpha(T_a-25)]}{1 - I^2 R_{ds25}\,\alpha\,R_\theta}$$

**Thermal runaway** occurs when $I^2 R_{ds25}\,\alpha\,R_\theta \ge 1$.

At 80 A this gives $R_\theta \ge 1/(6400 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} \times 0.0045) = 23\ \text{K/W}$.

A 3×3 DFN on a 20×20 board in still air sits around 40 K/W steady-state. **Continuous 80 A operation is therefore electrothermally unstable. It is a burst-only rating by physics.**

### 1.4 Ancillary copper losses (per channel)

| Path | R (est.) | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase pad and trace, 2 oz inner layers | 0.4 mΩ | 2.6 W |
| Shunt (if per-phase; often a single bus shunt) | 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |

**Battery-side warning.** At high throttle, the battery current is $I_{bat} \approx D \cdot \sum I_{ph}$. That reaches up to 320 A for four motors at 80 A each. No XT60 or nano power path survives this. In practice, the 80 A burst applies to one motor or a short transient, not all four simultaneously at D ≈ 1.
