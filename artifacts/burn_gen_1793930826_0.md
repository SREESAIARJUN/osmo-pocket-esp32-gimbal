# STM32H743 Nano AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Electrothermal Model, Bi-directional DShot DMA Plan, and Reference Netlist

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Feasibility Warnings

Three facts shape the rest of this document.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every viable design, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. Each motor is driven by its own small ESC MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The 48 kHz PWM frequency is therefore an **ESC firmware setting**, not an ArduPilot parameter. ArduPilot only controls the DShot600 link and decodes bi-directional telemetry.
2. **The 80 A burst figure must be time-bounded.** On a "nano" footprint (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm), 80 A per channel is only survivable for a few hundred milliseconds. With all four channels at 80 A, the safe window is well under one second. The model below quantifies this. I recommend publishing the rating as "80 A / 1 s single-channel burst, 35–40 A continuous with airflow."
3. **All MOSFET and driver parameters are placeholder class values.** They represent a 30–40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN part. Replace them with your chosen datasheet values (R_DS(on) versus Tj curve, Qg, Qrr, Coss, Zth(j-c) Foster coefficients) before you sign off.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-step trapezoidal drive (BLHeli/AM32 default)

Two phase legs carry the motor current at any instant. One leg is PWM'd at duty D, with synchronous rectification in the complementary FET. The other leg's low-side is held fully on.

$$P_{cond}=I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\Big[\underbrace{D+(1-D)}_{\text{PWM leg}}+\underbrace{1}_{\text{static leg}}\Big]=2\,I^2R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Add the ripple term. The phase inductance L_ph (about 10–20 µH on 5" motors) produces peak-to-peak ripple:

$$\Delta I=\frac{V_{bus}D(1-D)}{L_{ph}f_{sw}},\qquad I_{rms}^2=I^2+\frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

Taking the worst case (D = 0.5, L = 10 µH):

- ΔI = 25.2·0.25/(10e-6·48e3) = 13.1 A
- ΔI²/12 = 14.3 A², which is about 0.2 % of 6400 A² and therefore negligible.

### 1.2 Temperature coupling (iterative fixed point)

$$R_{DS}(T_j)=R_{DS,25}\,[1+k(T_j-25)],\quad k\approx0.0059\ /\mathrm{K}$$

This must be solved together with the thermal network in §3. At Tj = 110 °C:

$$P_{cond}=2(80)^2(1.5\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ motor\ channel}$$

That loss is split roughly 9.6 W in each of the two conducting FETs. The PWM-leg FETs share their portion between high-side and low-side in proportion to D.

### 1.3 Copper and shunt losses (often forgotten)

- **Phase pad and trace path:** about 0.3 mΩ per phase on 2 oz inner layers. P = 2·80²·0.3e-3 = **3.84 W**.
- **Shunt (0.25 mΩ, if per-ESC current sensing is used):** P = 80²·0.25e-3 = **1.6 W**.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches.

| Mechanism | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw | ½·25.2·80·20e-9·48e3 = **0.968 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 30e-9·25.2·48e3 = **0.036 W** |
| Coss charge | ½·C_oss·V²·f_sw ×2 FETs | 2·½·1.5e-9·635·48e3 = **0.046 W** |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_F·I·2·t_dead·f_sw | 0.8·80·200e-9·48e3 = **0.614 W** |
| Gate drive (driver/LDO, not FET) | Q_g·V_GS·f_sw ×2 | 40e-9·10·48e3·2 = **0.038 W** |

**Switching subtotal: about 1.70 W per channel.**

Dead time is the second-largest switching term. Reducing it from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel. That makes it worth tuning in AM32 (`dead_time` in the target config), provided you scope for shoot-through.

**Frequency sensitivity:**

$$P_{sw}(f)\approx 3.54\times10^{-5}\,f_{sw}\ \mathrm{W/Hz}$$

This gives 0.85 W at 24 kHz and 3.4 W at 96 kHz. At 80 A, 48 kHz is a reasonable compromise between switching loss and current ripple/noise.

## 3. Total Loss and Thermal Model

### 3.1 Loss budget at 80 A burst, Tj = 110 °C

| | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| FET switching + dead time | 1.70 W | 6.8 W |
| Copper | 3.84 W | 15.4 W |
| Shunt | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 26.3 W** | **≈ 105 W** |

In efficiency terms, 105 W lost out of about 4×80×25.2×D_eff is a few percent, which is fine. The heat density is the problem, not the efficiency.

### 3.2 Thermal network (Cauer, simplified)

```
Tj ──R_jc(1.5)──┬──R_cs(solder+via, 2.0 K/W per FET)──┬── T_board ──R_ba── T_amb
                C_j (≈0.02 J/K)                        C_board                  
```

### 3.3 Board heat capacity (25.5×25.5 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz)

- Copper: 650 mm² × 6 × 70 µm = 0.273 cm³ → 2.45 g × 0.385 = **0.94 J/K**
- FR-4: 650 mm² × 1.6 mm = 1.04 cm³ → 1.93 g × 1.1 = **2.1 J/K**
- Components (12 FETs, caps, MCUs): about **0.5 J/K**
- **C_board ≈ 3.5 J/K**
