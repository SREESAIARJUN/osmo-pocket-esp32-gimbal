# Job #68: STM32H743 AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC), 80 A Burst Power-Stage and DShot Architecture

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Flags

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 cannot run ArduPilot and also commutate four BLDC bridges. The workable topology is:

- **H743** runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600 to:
- **4× ESC MCUs** (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32), each driving:
- **4× 3-phase bridges** (6 MOSFETs each) through a gate driver (FD6288Q class).

The "48 kHz switching" applies to the ESC MCUs' PWM. The DShot timing applies to the H743.

**Feasibility flags.** These come out of the math below.

1. **Steady-state 80 A per motor is not thermally possible on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5) board.** The analysis below supports roughly **1.5–3 s bursts**, not sustained 80 A.
2. **Four motors at 80 A means about 320 A from the battery.** XT60 connectors, 12 AWG leads and the board's input copper all fail at that level. The 80 A figure is credible only as a per-motor transient with staggered demand.
3. All component values below are **representative assumptions**. Replace them with datasheet values for your selected parts before freezing the design.

## 2. Conduction-Loss Model

**Current paths.** In 6-step operation with synchronous rectification, two phase legs carry the motor current I at every instant:

- One high-side (or its complementary low-side during PWM off-time) on the PWM'd phase.
- One low-side held on in the return phase.

The current therefore always passes through two R_DS(on) in series:

$$P_{cond,ch}(T_j) = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right]$$

**At T_j = 125 °C:** R = 1.2 × 1.45 = 1.74 mΩ

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.74\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{22.3\ W}\ \text{per ESC channel}$$

**Per-FET distribution.** Each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical cycle (120°).

- Instantaneous loss during its conduction window: I²R ≈ 11.1 W.
- Cycle-averaged loss: 22.3 / 6 ≈ **3.7 W per FET**.

At typical electrical frequencies of 0.5–3 kHz, the package thermal time constant (several ms) averages these windows. The cycle average is therefore valid for T_j. Add roughly 2–3 K of ripple at low eRPM.

**Electrothermal coupling.** Because R_DS(on) rises with T_j, solve iteratively:

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + \left(\tfrac{P_{cond}(T_j)}{6} + P_{sw,FET}\right)\left(R_{\theta JC} + R_{\theta sp}\right)$$

This converges in 2–3 iterations. Thermal runaway is possible only if

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\cdot R_\theta > 1,$$

which here evaluates to 0.026 W/K × 7.5 K/W ≈ 0.19, i.e. stable.

**Copper and shunt losses (do not ignore at 80 A).**

| Element | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| Phase/bus copper, ≈ 0.3 mΩ effective per channel | 80² × 0.3 mΩ | 1.9 W |
| Current shunt, 0.25 mΩ | 80² × 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |

The shunt can be dropped if you use R_DS(on) sensing.
