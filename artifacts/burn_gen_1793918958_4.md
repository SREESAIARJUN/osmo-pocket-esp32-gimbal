# STM32H743 FC + 4‑in‑1 ESC "Nano" Stack: Power Stage, Thermal, and Bi‑Directional DShot600 Design

I don't have the prior documents for "Job #20," so this is built from stated assumptions. Every assumption is in Section 0 so you can substitute your real BOM values. Before the math, there are two issues that change the project scope.

---

## Two Issues to Resolve First

**1. "80 A burst" on a nano form factor cannot be sustained, only survived briefly.**
The model below shows about 19 W of loss per channel at 80 A. That is about 77 W for all four channels on a board weighing a few grams. Without a heatsink, that board heats roughly 15–20 K per second. "80 A burst" is only meaningful with a stated duration (≤1 s is realistic) and with the current reference defined (phase vs. battery). The model assumes 80 A phase current.

**2. The H743 should not commutate the motors directly.**
Driving 4 sensorless BLDC motors needs 12 complementary PWM pairs with dead time.

| H743 resource | Complementary pairs |
|---|---|
| TIM1 | 3 |
| TIM8 | 3 |
| HRTIM | 5 |
| **Total** | **11 (one short)** |

Driving them directly would also force custom firmware instead of ArduPilot. The architecture assumed here is the industry‑standard split:

- **H743 flight controller (ArduPilot)** → bi‑directional DShot600 →
- **4× ESC MCUs** (e.g., AT32F421 / STM32G071 running AM32), each driving one 3‑phase bridge.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6‑step drive

In each 60° commutation sector, one high‑side FET (HS, PWM‑switched) and one low‑side FET (LS, static) carry current in series. The complementary LS on the PWM leg conducts during the off time.

- **ON interval (fraction D):** current flows through HS_a, the motor, and LS_b.
- **OFF interval (fraction 1−D):** current flows through LS_a (synchronous), the motor, and LS_b.

So there are always two R_DS(on) in the current path:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{HS}\,D + I^2 R_{LS,sync}(1-D) + I^2 R_{LS,static} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

The result is independent of D when all FETs match. Battery current is lower: I_batt ≈ D · I_phase.

Each of the 6 FETs conducts about 1/3 of the electrical cycle, so the average loss per FET is approximately P_cond / 6.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence (self‑consistent)

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \qquad T_j = T_{ref} + Z_{th}(t)\,P_{tot}(T_j)
$$

Solve by fixed‑point iteration. If the loop gain 2I²R₂₅α·Z_th ≥ 1, there is no steady state: this is **thermal runaway**. Check that condition explicitly.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 100 °C

- R_DS(100 °C) = 1.0 mΩ × (1 + 0.005 × 75) = **1.375 mΩ**
- P_cond = 2 × 80² × 1.375 mΩ = **17.6 W per motor**
- Per FET (average) ≈ 2.9 W
- Per FET (instantaneous, while conducting) = 8.8 W. This is the figure that matters for short‑time transient Z_th.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches (one HS/LS pair per sector).

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V·I overlap | ½ · V_bus · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw | ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 20 ns × 48 k = **0.968 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw | 30 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.036 W** |
| C_oss (HS + LS) | 2 × ½ · C_oss · V_bus² · f_sw | 1 nF × 635 × 48 k = **0.030 W** |
| Dead‑time body‑diode conduction | V_f · I · 2t_dt · f_sw | 0.8 × 80 × 100 ns × 48 k = **0.307 W** |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver/R_g, not FET) | 2 · Q_g · V_GS · f_sw | 2 × 30 nC × 10 × 48 k = 0.029 W |

**Total switching loss per motor ≈ 1.34 W** (FET side).

Switching loss is only about 7% of conduction loss at 80 A. Raising f_sw to 96 kHz would add only about 1.3 W. **Conduction loss dominates; spend design effort on R_DS(on) and copper.**

### 2.1 Totals at 80 A

| Item | Loss |
