## 0. Assumptions and Timing Architecture

| Item | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Motor | Gimbal BLDC (e.g., GBM2804-class), 12N14P, p = 7 pole pairs, R_s ≈ 5–10 Ω, L_s ≈ 2–4 mH |
| Position sensor | 14-bit absolute magnetic encoder (AS5048A/MT6701), SPI |
| Current sense | 2-phase low-side shunts, synchronous ADC sampling at PWM center |
| MCU | Cortex-M4F/M7 with single-precision FPU (STM32G4/H7) |
| Bus | 12–16 V |
| IMUs | 2× ICM-42688-P on the camera plate, SPI @ 24 MHz, gyro/accel ODR 8 kHz |

**Loop rates (one interrupt chain per motor):**

| Loop | Rate | Ts | Target bandwidth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Current (FOC) | 20 kHz | 50 µs | ω_c ≈ 2π·1.5 kHz |
| Velocity | 4 kHz | 250 µs | ω_v ≈ 2π·120 Hz |
| Angle (stabilization) | 1 kHz | 1 ms | ω_θ ≈ 2π·20–30 Hz |
| IMU fusion | 8 kHz in, 1 kHz out | — | — |

Bandwidth separation rule: each outer loop should have **≤ 1/5 to 1/10** of the inner loop's bandwidth.

> **Note:** Many gimbal motors are high-R and run *voltage-mode* FOC without current sensing (v_q ≈ R·i_q + ω_e·λ). Section 1.5 describes that degenerate mode. The rest of the design assumes current sensing.

## 1. Field-Oriented Control (Inner Current Loop)

### 1.1 Electrical model (PMSM, dq frame)

$$
v_d = R i_d + L_d \frac{di_d}{dt} - \omega_e L_q i_q,\qquad
v_q = R i_q + L_q \frac{di_q}{dt} + \omega_e L_d i_d + \omega_e \lambda_m
$$

$$
\tau_e = \tfrac{3}{2}\, p \left[\lambda_m i_q + (L_d - L_q) i_d i_q\right] \;\xrightarrow{\;L_d\approx L_q\;}\; \tau_e = K_t\, i_q,\quad K_t = \tfrac{3}{2} p \lambda_m
$$

Electrical angle: $\theta_e = p\,(\theta_m - \theta_{\text{offset}}) \bmod 2\pi$.

### 1.2 Transforms (amplitude-invariant)

**Clarke** (with $i_a + i_b + i_c = 0$):

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \frac{i_a + 2 i_b}{\sqrt{3}}
$$

**Park:**

$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d \\ i_q \end{bmatrix} =
\begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e \\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha \\ i_\beta \end{bmatrix}
$$

**Inverse Park:**

$$
v_\alpha = v_d\cos\theta_e - v_q\sin\theta_e,\qquad
v_\beta = v_d\sin\theta_e + v_q\cos\theta_e
$$

### 1.3 Current PI with decoupling, gain design by pole-zero cancellation

Plant per axis: $G(s) = \dfrac{1}{L s + R}$. With $C(s) = K_p + K_i/s$, choosing $K_i/K_p = R/L$ cancels the plant pole. The closed loop then becomes first-order with bandwidth $\omega_c$:

$$
K_p = L\,\omega_c,\qquad K_i = R\,\omega_c
$$

Decoupling feedforward (added after the PI):

$$
v_d^{*} = \text{PI}_d(e_d) - \omega_e L_q i_q,\qquad
v_q^{*} = \text{PI}_q(e_q) + \omega_e L_d i_d + \omega_e\lambda_m
$$

**Discretization.** Use backward Euler on the integrator, with a 1.5·Ts transport delay accounted for (ADC sample, compute, PWM update). To keep phase margin ≥ 45°, require $\omega_c T_s \le 0.1$–$0.15$. At 20 kHz this gives $\omega_c \le 2\pi\cdot 2.4$ kHz.

**Voltage limiting with circular saturation.** The SVPWM linear region is $|v| \le V_{dc}/\sqrt{3}$. Priority goes to $v_d$:

$$
v_d^{sat} = \text{clamp}(v_d^*, \pm V_{max}),\qquad v_q^{sat} = \text{clamp}\!\left(v_q^*, \pm\sqrt{V_{max}^2 - (v_d^{sat})^2}\right)
$$

**Back-calculation anti-windup** with $K_{aw} = 1/K_p$, or equivalently using the saturated output directly:

$$
I_{k+1} = I_k + K_i T_s e_k + K_{aw}\,(u^{sat}_k - u_k)
$$
