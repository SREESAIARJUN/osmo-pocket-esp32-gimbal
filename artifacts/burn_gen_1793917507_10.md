## 0. Architecture Constraints

Two constraints shape the whole design.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot is a flight stack, not a BLDC commutation firmware. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to **four ESC MCUs** running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware (one per motor), and those MCUs generate the 48 kHz PWM. "Switching at 48 kHz" is therefore a property of the ESC MCUs. Trying to merge both roles into the H743 is a non-starter for ArduPilot.

2. **"Nano" and 80 A are in tension.** On a 20×20 or 30×30 board, 80 A per channel can only be a **time-limited burst** (seconds), never a continuous rating. Section 3 quantifies the limit. Assumed format for this job: **30.5×30.5 mm, 6S, 40 A continuous / 80 A burst per channel.**

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per channel)

### 2.1 Commutation topology

The ESC uses six-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification. In any 60° sector, exactly one phase is PWM'd (its high-side and low-side FETs alternate), one phase's low-side is held ON, and one phase floats.

The motor current therefore always flows through **exactly two conducting MOSFET channels**: either (HS_A + LS_B) or (LS_A + LS_B) during freewheel.
