# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #28): Engineering Model

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Feasibility

**Partitioning.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller MCU. It does not commutate motors. The 4-in-1 ESC uses four dedicated ESC MCUs (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32), each driving a 3-phase gate driver and 6 MOSFETs. The H743 talks to them over bidirectional DShot600. The 48 kHz switching therefore lives on the ESC MCUs.

**Placeholder parameters.** Every MOSFET and driver parameter below is a representative 40 V / 3.3×3.3 mm PDFN value, labeled `[P]`. Before sign-off, replace them with the chosen part's datasheet values at the operating Vgs and temperature.

**Feasibility.** On a 20×20 mm "nano" board, 80 A per motor is only a transient burst of about 1–2 s, not a continuous rating. Two numbers show why:

- Total dissipation at 4×80 A is roughly 90 W.
- Peak battery current at full duty is roughly 320 A, which exceeds any connector or pad that fits a nano frame.

The math below quantifies this. If the requirement is a genuine multi-second 80 A rating, the board must grow to 30.5×30.5 mm or larger with a heatsink.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-step synchronous commutation

In any of the six commutation sectors, exactly two phases conduct:

- **PWM leg:** the high side (HS) conducts for duty D. The complementary low side (LS, synchronous rectification) conducts for (1−D).
- **Return leg:** the LS conducts for 100% of the sector.
- **Third phase:** both FETs are off, floating for BEMF sensing.

Current also flows through the body diode during dead time. That term is counted under switching losses in §2.

The per-motor conduction loss is:

$$
P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\cdot 1 = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

The result is independent of D, which is a useful property of synchronous rectification.

### 1.2 Ripple correction

The RMS current includes the ripple term:

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}
$$

For a typical 5" or 7" motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH line-to-line, D = 0.9, V_bemf ≈ 20 V):

- ΔI = (5.2 · 0.9)/(15e-6 · 48e3) ≈ 6.5 A
- ΔI²/12 ≈ 3.5 A², which is negligible against 6400 A².

### 1.3 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on): a self-consistent loop

The on-resistance rises with junction temperature:

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

$$
T_j = T_{board} + P_{FET}\,R_{\theta,j\text{-}b}
$$

Solve this by iteration. The values below are per motor at 80 A:

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per motor |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.10 mΩ | 14.1 W |
| 100 °C | 1.47 mΩ | 18.8 W |
| 125 °C | 1.60 mΩ | **20.5 W** (design value) |

**Copper and shunt losses.** Assume 0.3 mΩ of phase/bus copper path per motor, plus a 0.25 mΩ shunt carrying battery current. Copper adds 80² × 0.3 mΩ × ~1.5 (the conduction-path factor) ≈ **2.9 W per motor**.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches hard. The LS on the return leg is static within a sector.

### 2.1 V·I overlap

The overlap time depends on the gate drive:

$$
t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}+Q_{gs2}}{I_{g,src}} \approx \frac{6+4\text{ nC}}{1.5\text{ A}} + t_{R_g} \approx 12\text{ ns}, \qquad t_f \approx 12\text{ ns}
$$

The loss is:

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 24\text{ ns} \cdot 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.16\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery of the complementary body diode

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{ nC}\cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}
$$

Q_rr scales with di/dt and current. Derate ×2 if di/dt exceeds 1 A/ns, giving about 0.1 W.

### 2.3 Output capacitance (both FETs in the leg)

$$
P_{oss} = 2 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss(er)} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 900\text{ pF}\cdot 635 \cdot 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.03\ W}
$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period)

$$
P_{dt} = V_f I_{ph}\,(2 t_{dead}) f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot 80\cdot 100\text{ ns}\cdot 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}
$$
