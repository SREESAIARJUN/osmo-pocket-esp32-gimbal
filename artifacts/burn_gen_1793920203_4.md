# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Engineering Model

The figures below rest on stated assumptions, and the netlist is a reference architecture to verify against real datasheets, not a released design. Two corrections to the brief come first, because they change the design.

## 0. Architecture Corrections

**1. The STM32H743 cannot commutate the motors under ArduPilot.**
- ArduPilot outputs DShot. It does not run 6-step or FOC commutation, back-EMF zero-cross detection, or 48 kHz complementary PWM with dead-time for 12 half-bridges.
- The correct architecture is H743 (ArduPilot) → bidirectional DShot600 → 4× ESC MCUs (e.g., AT32F421 running AM32, or EFM8/BLHeli_S) → gate drivers → 24 MOSFETs.
- The 48 kHz switching analysis below therefore belongs to the ESC MCUs. The DMA analysis belongs to the H743.

**2. 80 A burst on a "nano" board is a heat-capacity problem.**
- Section 3 shows the board heats at roughly 40 K/s with all four channels at 80 A.
- "80 A burst" must be specified as per-motor, with a duration (≤1–2 s), and with a duty limit enforced in firmware.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology current path

In 6-step trapezoidal drive, two phases conduct in each 60° sector:
- One leg is PWM-modulated (HS on for duty D, complementary LS on for 1−D).
- One leg holds its LS (or HS) on continuously.

With complementary PWM, exactly two MOSFET channels carry I at every instant, independent of D. The PWM-off interval runs through the synchronous LS rather than the body diode, except during dead-time.

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + k(T_j - 25)],\quad k \approx 0.006\,\text{K}^{-1} \Rightarrow R_{125} = 2.4\,\text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond per motor | P_cond, 4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.5 mΩ | 2·6400·0.0015 = 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| 125 °C | 2.4 mΩ | 2·6400·0.0024 = 30.7 W | 122.9 W |

Conduction loss is electrothermally coupled: rising T_j raises R_DS(on), which raises loss. Solve iteratively:

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}(T_j)\,R_{\theta,J\text{-}PCB}$$

### 1.3 Per-device distribution

Over one electrical cycle, each of the 6 FETs of a motor carries current for 1/3 of the cycle. Electrical frequency typically exceeds 500 Hz, while the die thermal time constant is about 1–10 ms, so averaging is valid.

$$P_{FET,cond} \approx \frac{P_{cond,motor}}{6} = 5.1\,\text{W at } 125\,°\text{C}$$

The HS/LS split depends on D. At high throttle (D → 1) the HS of the PWM leg carries most of it.

### 1.4 Parasitic copper (do not omit)

- 80 A through a 2 oz, 4 mm wide, 15 mm trace: R ≈ 0.5 mΩ, giving 3.2 W per trace segment.
- Mitigations: parallel all layers on phase and bus nets, use via arrays (≥ 1 A per 0.3 mm via, so ≥ 80 vias per high-current node), and use solder-pad motor wires.
- Budget about 2 W per motor in copper loss at 80 A.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-modulated leg switches. That is 1 hard-switched HS turn-on/turn-off pair per period, plus synchronous-rectifier events.

### 2.1 Gate drive currents

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{DRV} - V_{pl}}{R_{g,int}+R_{g,ext}} = \frac{10-3}{5} = 1.4\,\text{A}$$

$$I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{g,int}+R_{g,ext}} = \frac{3}{5} = 0.6\,\text{A}$$

$$t_{on} = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = \frac{9\,\text{nC}}{1.4} = 6.4\,\text{ns}$$

$$t_{off} = \frac{9\,\text{nC}}{0.6} = 15\,\text{ns}$$

### 2.2 Hard-switching V–I overlap

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I\,(t_{on}+t_{off})\,f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 21.4\,\text{ns} \cdot 48\,\text{kHz} = 1.04\,\text{W}$$
