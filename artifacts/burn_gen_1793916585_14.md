# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80A-Burst 4-in-1 ESC: Systems Architecture Package (Job #1)

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

**Split-MCU architecture.** The STM32H743 does not drive the gate drivers directly. Each 4-in-1 ESC uses its own motor-control MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to the ESCs over bidirectional DShot600.

```
[STM32H743 FC (ArduPilot)] --4x bidir DShot600--> [4x ESC MCU (AM32)] -> [3-ph gate driver] -> [6x N-FET] -> motor
```

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths in six-step trapezoidal commutation

In each 60° sector:
- One phase sources current, switching at duty D: high-side (HS) on for D, synchronous low-side (LS) on for (1–D).
- One phase sinks current: its LS is on continuously.
- The third phase floats (BEMF sensing).

At every instant, the phase current I flows through exactly **two channels in series**, ignoring dead time. So:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Each phase conducts for 240° of 360° (120° sourcing, 120° sinking). Per-FET distribution:

$$I_{rms,HS}^2 = \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I^2 ,\qquad I_{rms,LS}^2 = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)\,I^2 + \tfrac{1}{3} I^2$$

The low side therefore carries more RMS current than the high side at all duty cycles. **The worst-case FET is the low side.**

### 1.2 Ripple correction

Phase-to-phase inductance is L_pp ≈ 15 µH (5" to 7" class motors). Peak-to-peak ripple at D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L_{pp} f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{15\mu \times 48k} = 8.75\text{ A}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 6400 + 6.4 \Rightarrow \text{+0.1 \% (negligible at 80 A)}$$

### 1.3 Temperature-dependent resistance (iterative)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0045/\text{K}$$

This loop is solved together with the thermal model in §3. Converged values:

| T_j (°C) | R_DS (mΩ) | P_cond per ESC (W) | Worst LS FET at D = 1 (W) | HS FET at D = 1 (W) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 | 1.50 | 19.2 | 3.2 | 3.2 |
| 100 | 2.01 | 25.7 | 4.3 | 4.3 |
| 125 | 2.18 | 27.9 | 4.65 | 4.65 |

At D = 0.5, the LS FET carries 0.5 of the I² share versus 0.167 for the HS FET, so the LS loss is about 3× the HS loss. Thermal relief must favour the LS FETs.

### 1.4 Copper and interconnect conduction

| Path element | R (mΩ) |
|---|---|
| Battery pad → bus plane (2 oz, 2 layers in parallel) | 0.15 |
| Bus → HS drain → switch node → motor pad (per phase pair) | 2 × 0.25 |
| Shunt (if per-ESC current sense) | 0.25 |
| **Series total** | ≈ 0.9 |

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.9\text{ m}\Omega \approx 5.8\text{ W per ESC}$$

This copper loss is significant. It heats the board and is not dissipated in the FETs.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing leg switches. Its HS performs hard switching, and its LS body diode conducts during dead time.

### 2.1 V·I overlap (hard turn-on and turn-off of HS)

Gate plateau current with a 10 V drive, V_plateau ≈ 4.5 V, R_g,total ≈ 3 Ω:

$$I_{g,on} \approx \frac{10-4.5}{3} = 1.8\text{ A},\quad I_{g,off} \approx \frac{4.5}{3}=1.5\text{ A}$$

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} + t_{ri} \approx 4.4 + 10 = 14\text{ ns},\qquad t_f \approx 5.3 + 10 \approx 16\text{ ns}$$

Layout parasitics in practice give an effective t_r + t_f of about 40 ns. Use that conservative value:
