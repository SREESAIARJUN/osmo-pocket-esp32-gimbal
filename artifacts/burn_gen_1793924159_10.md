# FOC Control Loop and Dual-IMU Sensor Fusion for a 3-Axis Gimbal (Job #50)

I don't have the Job #50 specification, so the design rests on the assumptions in Section 0. Every number that depends on them is a parameter in the code. Change these first if your hardware differs:

- Motor topology
- Encoder
- MCU
- Inertia

---

## 0. Assumptions and Timing Architecture

| Item | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Motors | Gimbal BLDC, 12N14P (pole pairs `p = 7`), R ≈ 5–15 Ω, L ≈ 1–3 mH, PMSM model |
| Commutation sensor | 14-bit magnetic encoder (AS5048A / MT6701 class) on each motor shaft |
| Current sensing | 2-shunt or inline per motor. A voltage-mode fallback is provided. |
| MCU | STM32G4 / H7 class, single-precision FPU, center-aligned PWM |
| PWM | 20 kHz, current sampling synchronized to the PWM center |
| IMUs | 2 × ICM-42688-P on the camera plate, rigidly mounted, ODR 8 kHz, FIFO, common FSYNC/timer-triggered reads |

| Loop | Rate | Ts |
|---|---|---|
| Current (FOC) | 20 kHz | 50 µs |
| Velocity | 4 kHz | 250 µs |
| IMU processing | 8 kHz (FIFO drained at 4 kHz) | 125 µs |
| Angle/attitude | 1 kHz | 1 ms |

Rule of thumb: each outer loop's bandwidth should be at most about 1/4 to 1/5 of the inner loop's bandwidth.

## 1. FOC Current Loop

### 1.1 PMSM model (rotor dq frame)

$$
v_d = R i_d + L_d \frac{di_d}{dt} - \omega_e L_q i_q,\qquad
v_q = R i_q + L_q \frac{di_q}{dt} + \omega_e L_d i_d + \omega_e \lambda_m
$$

$$
\tau_e = \tfrac{3}{2} p\left[\lambda_m i_q + (L_d - L_q) i_d i_q\right] \;\approx\; K_t i_q,\quad K_t = \tfrac{3}{2}p\lambda_m
$$

The electrical angle is θ_e = p·(θ_m − θ_offset), where θ_offset is found by alignment at startup.

### 1.2 Clarke, Park and inverse transforms (amplitude-invariant)

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\quad i_\beta = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}(i_a + 2 i_b)
$$
$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix} =
\begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix},\qquad
\begin{bmatrix} v_\alpha\\ v_\beta\end{bmatrix} =
\begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta_e & -\sin\theta_e\\ \sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} v_d\\ v_q\end{bmatrix}
$$

**Delay compensation.** The voltage computed at sample k is applied over roughly the next 1.5 Ts. Use θ_e + 1.5·ω_e·Ts in the inverse Park transform.
