# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #12): Electro-Thermal Model, Bi-Directional DShot600 Timer/DMA Plan, and Reference Netlists

**Document class:** Architecture baseline, Rev A
**Scope:** Per-channel MOSFET loss model at 80 A burst and 48 kHz PWM, transient thermal budget, ArduPilot `hwdef` timer/DMA allocation for bi-directional DShot600, and reference netlists.

> **Read this first: three architectural facts drive every number below**
> 1. **The H743 does not switch the MOSFETs.** In a 4-in-1 stack, each motor channel is commutated by its own ESC MCU (typically AM32 on an AT32F421 or STM32G071). The 48 kHz PWM is an **ESC firmware setting**. The H743 only generates DShot600 and decodes eRPM telemetry.
> 2. **"80 A" is defined here as per-channel phase current (RMS ≈ DC in 6-step), burst ≤ a few seconds.** On a 20×20 / 25.5×25.5 nano footprint this is a thermal-capacitance-limited rating, not a steady-state one. The math below shows why.
> 3. **All component parameters are design assumptions.** Replace them with values from your selected parts' datasheets before release. The model is parametric for that reason.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current-path topology (6-step, synchronous/complementary PWM)

At any commutation sector, one phase is PWM-active (say A) and one is low (say B):

- **PWM ON:** Q_AH → motor → Q_BL. Two FETs in series.
- **PWM OFF (synchronous rectification):** Q_AL → motor → Q_BL. Two FETs in series.

So exactly two FET channels carry I_ph at all times, independent of duty D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\alpha_T$$

The loss distributes across six FETs over an electrical cycle:

- **High-side FET** conducts for D/3 of the electrical period.
- **Low-side FET** of the PWM'd phase conducts for (1−D)/3.
- **Low-side FET** of the "low" phase conducts for a full 1/3.

**Low-side FETs run hotter than high-side, especially at low throttle with high current** (e.g., prop-strike or punch-out from hover). Place them nearest the thermal via fields.

### 2.2 Numeric evaluation at 80 A

$$R_{ds(on),hot} = 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.6 = 2.4\,\text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.0024 = \mathbf{30.7\ W\ per\ channel}$$

### 2.3 PCB copper loss (frequently ignored, not negligible)

Phase/bus path of roughly 15 mm, 4 mm wide, on 2 oz (70 µm) copper across two paralleled layers:

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{w t\, n} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot 0.015}{0.004\cdot 70\times10^{-6}\cdot 2} \approx 0.46\,\text{m}\Omega$$

Hot copper (+0.39 %/K × 60 K) gives ≈ 0.57 mΩ. The current path crosses roughly two such segments:

$$P_{Cu,ch} \approx 80^2 \times 2 \times 0.57\,\text{m}\Omega \approx \mathbf{7.3\ W}$$

**Implication:** the copper loss is about 24 % of FET conduction loss. On a nano board, **copper is a co-equal loss element**. Use 3 oz outer layers or paralleled inner planes for phase and bus nets.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per PWM period, one FET hard-switches (the active high-side on turn-on and turn-off). Its complementary partner commutates via the body diode during dead time.

### 3.1 V–I overlap loss

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = \tfrac12(25.2)(80)(50\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz}) = \mathbf{2.42\ W}$$

**Overlap time derivation (for verification):** with Q_gd + Q_gs2 ≈ 12 nC and gate current

$$I_g \approx \frac{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}{R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}+R_{drv}} = \frac{10-3.5}{2.2+1.0+1.5} \approx 1.4\text{ A}$$

the intrinsic switching time is about 9 ns. The 25 ns used above deliberately includes common-source inductance and loop-inductance effects typical of dense 4-in-1 layouts. Measure V_DS on a bench prototype to recalibrate this term.
