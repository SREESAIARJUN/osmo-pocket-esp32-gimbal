# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC Stack (Job #29): Power-Stage, Thermal, and Bi-Directional DShot Design Package

**Document scope:** This package covers four areas:

1. Conduction-loss model for 80 A burst
2. Switching-loss model at 48 kHz PWM
3. Thermal transient and steady-state budget
4. ArduPilot BDShot600 timer and DMA allocation on the H743

It also includes reference netlists for the FC-to-ESC interface and one ESC power channel.

> **Architecture note:** In a 4-in-1 stack, the H743 does not drive the MOSFETs. The H743 runs ArduPilot and emits DShot600. Each ESC channel, or a shared multi-channel ESC MCU, runs its own firmware (e.g., AM32 or BLHeli_32) and generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The loss model below applies to the ESC board. The DMA and timer model applies to the H743.

> **Form-factor flag:** 80 A burst on four channels at the same time is about 76 W of heat (derived below). That is not realistic on a 20×20 "nano" ESC. The calculations assume a 30.5×30.5 mm mounting pattern on a roughly 36×36 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz board. If Job #29 truly requires 20×20, the burst rating must drop. Section 3.5 gives the scaling.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation with complementary PWM, two phases conduct and one floats. During on-time, current flows through HS(A) and LS(B). During off-time, it freewheels through LS(A) (synchronous rectification) and LS(B). In both intervals, **exactly two channels are in series** with the phase current, independent of duty cycle D.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At the design point T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{DS}(110) = 0.90\,\text{m}\Omega \times [1 + 0.0055 \times 85] = 1.32\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Per-motor conduction loss

$$P_{cond} = I^2\,(2R_{DS}(T_j) + R_{Cu})$$

- **MOSFET term:** 80² × 2 × 1.32 mΩ = **16.9 W**
- **Copper term:** about 0.30 mΩ total path (2 oz, 6 layers paralleled, phase pads plus bus) → 80² × 0.30 mΩ = **1.92 W**. This heat is spread across the board rather than concentrated in the FETs.
