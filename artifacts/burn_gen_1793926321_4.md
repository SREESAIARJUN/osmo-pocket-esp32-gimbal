# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC: Systems Architecture Package

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

**Architecture.** On every production 4-in-1 design, the STM32H743 is the flight controller MCU running ArduPilot. Each of the four ESC channels has its own MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, typically an STM32G071, AT32F421 or GD32E230. The H743 does not commutate the motors. It sends bidirectional DShot600 commands and receives eRPM telemetry back. This document models both domains and the link between them.

**Baseline parameters.** Replace these with your BOM's datasheet values. Every equation below is parametric.

| Parameter | Symbol | Assumed value | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage | V_bus | 25.2 V (6S full) | Requires 40 V MOSFETs |
| Burst phase current | I_ph | 80 A | Per motor, about 1–2 s |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz | ESC firmware setting |
| MOSFET R_DS(on) at 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | R_25 | 1.5 mΩ | 40 V, PDFN 5×6 or 3.3×3.3 class |
| R_DS(on) temperature coefficient | α | 0.005 /K | Gives ~1.5× at 125 °C |
| Rise + fall time at 80 A | t_r + t_f | 40 ns | Depends on gate drive strength |
| Dead time | t_dt | 200 ns | Per edge |
| Body diode forward voltage | V_F | 0.8 V | |
| Reverse recovery charge | Q_rr | 50 nC | |
| Total gate charge | Q_g | 60 nC | |
| Output capacitance | C_oss | 1.5 nF | |
| Topology | — | 6 FETs per motor (1 per switch position), 24 total | Synchronous rectification, 6-step trapezoidal |

> **Form-factor reality check.** "80 A burst" on a 20×20 mm nano board is a marketing figure. Treat it as a short transient bounded by thermal capacitance, not a sustainable operating point. Section 2 quantifies this. A 30.5×30.5 mm board with 8-layer, 2 oz copper is the minimum credible platform for this claim.

## 1. Loss Model (Per Motor Channel)

### 1.1 Conduction Losses

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, two FETs are always in the current path: one high-side and one low-side. Each FET conducts for 120° of each 360° electrical cycle.

**Temperature-dependent on-resistance:**

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

**Per-FET conduction loss** (averaged over the electrical cycle):

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{1}{3}\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

**Per-motor conduction loss:**

$$P_{cond} = 6 \cdot P_{cond,FET} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

This total is independent of duty cycle, because with synchronous rectification the freewheel path also flows through a FET.

**Numbers at T_j = 165 °C**, where R_DS = 1.5 × (1 + 0.005 × 140) = 2.55 mΩ (hot-burst case):

- P_cond,FET = (1/3)(6400)(0.00255) = **5.44 W per FET**
- P_cond = **32.6 W per motor**

At T_j = 105 °C (R_DS = 2.1 mΩ), P_cond = 26.9 W.

**Thermal-runaway stability criterion.** The conduction-loss / temperature loop is stable only if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\,R_{th,ja} = \frac{1}{3} I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\, R_{th,ja} < 1$$

- With R_th,ja = 30 K/W: 0.016 × 30 = 0.48. Stable, but 48 % loop gain means the steady-state temperature rise is amplified by 1/(1 − 0.48) ≈ 1.9×.
- With R_th,ja = 62 K/W (a poor layout): the loop gain reaches 1.0, and the device is thermally unstable at 80 A steady state.

### 1.2 Switching Losses (Only the PWM-ing Leg Switches)

**Hard-switching V·I overlap:**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(40\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz}) = 1.94\text{ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two edges per period):

$$P_{dt} = V_F I_{ph} \cdot 2t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(400\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz}) = 1.23\text{ W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{ nC}(25.2)(48\text{ kHz}) = 0.06\text{ W}$$

**C_oss discharge:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.023\text{ W}$$

**Gate drive** (two switching FETs per leg; this loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET):

$$P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 0.058\text{ W}$$

### 1.3 Ancillary Copper and Shunt Losses

**Current shunt.** For a 0.25 mΩ per-channel shunt:

$$P_{shunt} = I_{ph}^2 R_{sh} \cdot D_{eff} \approx 6400 \times 0.00025 \times 0.8 \approx 1.3\text{ W}$$

**PCB phase copper.** Using R = ρL / (w · t · n_layers), a 15 mm path that is 4 mm wide on 4 × 2 oz layers gives about 0.15 mΩ. That yields **~1 W at 80 A**.
