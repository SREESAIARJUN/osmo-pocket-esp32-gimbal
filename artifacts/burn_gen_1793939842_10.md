## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step commutation

At any instant two phases conduct:
- The **PWM leg** has either its high-side (HS) or low-side (LS) FET on. Synchronous rectification means a FET channel, not the body diode, carries the current during the off-time.
- The **static leg** has one FET continuously on.

The current therefore always flows through **exactly 2 FET channels in series**, independent of duty cycle D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}\ (\Rightarrow k_T = 1.6\ \text{at}\ 125\,°C)$$

This is a positive feedback loop. Solve it iteratively with the thermal model in §3:

$$T_j = T_{board} + P_{FET}(T_j)\,R_{\theta,j\text{-}board}$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| Case | R_DS(on) | P_cond per motor | Avg per FET (÷6) |
|---|---|---|---|
| T_j = 25 °C | 1.0 mΩ | 2 × 6400 × 1.0e-3 = **12.8 W** | 2.13 W |
| T_j = 125 °C | 1.6 mΩ | 2 × 6400 × 1.6e-3 = **20.5 W** | 3.41 W |

Each FET conducts for about 1/3 of the electrical cycle at full current, so its instantaneous loss is about 3× the average (≈10 W at 125 °C). At high eRPM the commutation period (≈ 0.1–1 ms) is much shorter than the die thermal time constant (≈ 5–20 ms), so the average value governs junction temperature. At **low-RPM / stall-ish high current** (prop strike, blocked rotor) that is no longer true, and peak losses dominate.

### 1.4 Copper and parasitic conduction (often forgotten)

| Path | Estimate | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase pad → FET → bus planes (per motor) | ~0.3 mΩ | 1.9 W |
| Shared battery entry, XT60 pads, shunt (all 4 motors) | ~0.5 mΩ at I_batt | see below |

For battery entry: at hover/cruise I_batt is smaller, but at 4 × 80 A and D ≈ 0.9, I_batt ≈ 290 A, which gives 42 W. That is impossible to sustain; another reason simultaneous 4 × 80 A is a non-goal.

**Recommendation:** spec "80 A burst" as **1 motor at 80 A, or 4 motors at 55 A**, for ≤ 5 s, with 4 × 45 A continuous only with airflow.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per motor, only the PWM leg switches. V_bus = 25.2 V and I = 80 A.

### 2.1 Overlap (V·I crossover) loss

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}$$
$$= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

With a weak driver (t_r + t_f = 40 ns) this doubles to 1.94 W. Estimate the transition time from the gate driver:

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd} + Q_{gs2}}{I_{gate}},\quad I_{gate} = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}{R_g + R_{g,int} + R_{drv}}$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance loss

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 30\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.018\ \text{W}$$

In hard switching this loss is dissipated in the turning-on FET.
