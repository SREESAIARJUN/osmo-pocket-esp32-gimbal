# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #38): Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and Bidirectional DShot600 Timer/DMA Allocation

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

### Architecture

In this design the STM32H743 is the **flight controller MCU only**.

- It runs ArduPilot.
- It generates DShot600 to the ESC section.
- It decodes the bidirectional eRPM replies.

Each ESC channel has its own small MCU running the commutation firmware, for example an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. That ESC firmware produces the 48 kHz power-stage PWM, which is a firmware setting (AM32 supports 24/48/96 kHz), not something the H743 produces.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.0 × 1.5 = **1.5 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Per-channel conduction during a 6-step sector

In each 60° sector, two phases carry I_ph:

- **PWM leg:** the high side conducts for duty D, and the synchronous low side conducts for (1 − D). Together they form one continuous conduction path through one R_ds.
- **Return leg:** the low side is on 100% of the sector.

$$P_{cond,ch}=I_{ph}^2R_{ds}\,[D+(1-D)]+I_{ph}^2R_{ds}=2\,I_{ph}^2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

The result is independent of D because synchronous rectification removes the diode path (dead-time diode loss is treated separately in §2).

At 80 A and 125 °C:

$$P_{cond,ch}=2(80)^2(1.5\times10^{-3})=\mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET average

Each of the 6 FETs conducts roughly one third of the electrical period, so averaging over a revolution gives:

$$P_{cond,FET}\approx \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6}\cdot k_{share},\quad k_{share}\approx 1 \Rightarrow \approx 3.2\ \text{W}$$

**Averaging validity check.** The die thermal time constant is about 1–5 ms. At 14 poles (7 pole pairs) and 20,000 RPM, the electrical frequency is 7 × 20,000 / 60 ≈ 2.3 kHz, a period of about 0.43 ms. The period is shorter than the die time constant, so averaging is valid at speed.

**Exception: stall or low-speed lock.** At stall or low-speed lock, one FET pair carries the full 9.6 W continuously. This is the worst case and must be covered by the ESC firmware's stall protection.
