# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage, Thermal, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Architecture

## 0. Architecture Clarifications

Three issues affect every calculation below, so they come first.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** On every production 4-in-1 / AIO design, the H743 runs ArduPilot, and four separate ESC MCUs run AM32 or BLHeli_32 commutation. Typical ESC MCUs are the AT32F421 or STM32G071. Bi-directional DShot is the link between the H743 timers and those ESC MCUs. Running FOC or 6-step for four motors plus ArduPilot on one H743 is technically possible, but ArduPilot does not support it.
2. **80 A per channel in a "nano" (20×20 mm) footprint is a burst rating that only the thermal mass of the board can support.** The math below shows that the steady-state dissipation exceeds what the board can sink. The real deliverable is therefore a **time-limited burst envelope**, not a continuous rating.
3. **All device parameters are stated assumptions** (representative 40 V DFN3.3×3 MOSFETs, FD6288Q-class driver). Substitute your BOM datasheet values into the equations. The structure of the model holds regardless of the specific parts.

## 1. Assumed Operating Point & Device Parameters

| Symbol | Meaning | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 6S, fully charged | 25.2 V |
| I_burst | Phase current per ESC | 80 A |
| f_sw | PWM frequency | 48 kHz |
| R_ds(on),25 | MOSFET at 25 °C, V_gs = 10 V | 2.0 mΩ |
| α | R_ds(on) temperature coefficient | 0.0060 /K |
| t_r, t_f | Switching edges at 80 A | 10 ns each |
| Q_rr | Body-diode reverse recovery charge | 30 nC |
| C_oss,eq | Energy-equivalent output capacitance | 1.0 nF |
| Q_g | Total gate charge | 30 nC |
| V_drv | Gate drive voltage | 10 V |
| t_dead | Dead time per edge | 50 ns |
| V_f | Body-diode forward drop | 0.8 V |
| R_shunt | Current-sense shunt (one per ESC) | 0.25 mΩ |
| R_trace | Phase + bus copper, per ESC path | 0.5 mΩ |

**Commutation scheme:** 6-step trapezoidal with complementary (synchronous) PWM, which is the AM32 default.
- At any instant, **two phase legs conduct**: one high-side and one low-side path.
- The PWM leg alternates between its high-side FET and its complementary low-side FET.
- The current-carrying path therefore always has **2 × R_ds(on)** in series, independent of duty cycle.

## 2. Conduction-Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$
R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]
$$

| T_j | R_ds(on) |
|---|---|
| 25 °C | 2.00 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 2.90 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 3.20 mΩ |
| 150 °C | 3.50 mΩ |

### 2.2 Per-ESC conduction loss

The current path is two FETs in series. Averaged over a commutation cycle, each of the 6 FETs carries current about 1/3 of the time. The total is still:

$$
P_{cond,ESC}=I^2\cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

Per-FET average (thermal spreading across 6 FETs):

$$
P_{cond,FET}\approx \tfrac{1}{3}\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

The peak instantaneous loss in a conducting FET is $I^2R_{ds}$.

**At 80 A, T_j = 125 °C:**
- $P_{cond,ESC}=6400\times 6.4\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{40.96\ W}$
- Peak per conducting FET: $6400 \times 3.2\,\text{m}\Omega = 20.5$ W
- Average per FET: ≈ 6.8 W

### 2.3 Parasitic copper and shunt losses

$$
P_{Cu}=I^2(R_{shunt}+R_{trace}) = 6400\times 0.75\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{4.8\ W}
$$

Copper resistivity rises about 0.39 %/K. At +80 K this becomes roughly 6.3 W.

### 2.4 Thermal-runaway (fixed-point) check

Conduction loss raises T_j, which raises R_ds(on), which raises loss. Steady state requires a stable fixed point of:

$$
T_j = T_{ref} + R_{\theta}\,I^2 R_{ds,25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

$$
T_j=\frac{T_{ref}+R_\theta I^2 R_{ds,25}(1-25\alpha)}{1-R_\theta I^2 R_{ds,25}\,\alpha}
$$

**Stability requires** $R_\theta I^2 R_{ds,25}\alpha<1$.

- For one FET with peak loss: $I^2R_{ds,25}\alpha = 6400 \times 0.002 \times 0.006 = 0.0768$ W/K.
- Runaway therefore occurs if the effective R_θ(j-amb) exceeds **13 K/W**.
- Practical margin: keep $R_\theta < 6$ K/W.

## 3. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches: one hard-switched FET plus its synchronous complement per ESC. The non-PWM leg is static between commutations.

### 3.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap

$$
P_{VI}=\tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}=\tfrac12(25.2)(80)(20\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz})=\mathbf{0.968\ W}
$$

Edge times depend on gate drive. With Miller charge Q_gd and driver current I_g, $t \approx Q_{gd}/I_g$. Example: Q_gd = 6 nC at 0.6 A sink gives 10 ns. This is why driver strength matters more than R_ds(on) at 48 kHz.

### 3.2 Reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}=30\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.036\ W}
$$

Recovery also adds approximately $\tfrac{1}{2} I_{rrm} V t_{rr}$ to the turn-on loss of the opposing FET. This is roughly 0.05 W here.

### 3.3 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss}=\tfrac12 C_{oss,eq}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw}= 0.5\times1\text{n}\times635\times48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.015\ W}
$$

### 3.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM period.

$$
P_{dt}=V_f\,I\,(2t_{dead})f_{sw}=0.8\times80\times100\text{n}\times48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.307\ W}
$$
