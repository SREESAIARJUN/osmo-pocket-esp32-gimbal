## 0. Architecture Decision and Critical Feasibility Flags

**Topology.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does **not** commutate motors. ArduPilot has no native ESC firmware, and BLHeli_32/AM32 do not run on the H743. Each motor channel therefore has its own ESC MCU running AM32 (AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071), and the H743 drives them over bi-directional DShot600.

```
 6S LiPo ─┬─ Bulk caps ─┬─ ESC1..ESC4 (AT32F421 + FD6288Q + 6×MOSFET each)
          │             │        ▲ BDShot600 (1 wire/motor, bidirectional)
          │             │        │
          └─ BEC 5V/3A ─┴─ 3V3 LDO ── STM32H743VIT6 (ArduPilot)
                                         ├─ SPI1: ICM-42688-P
                                         ├─ I2C2: DPS310 baro
                                         ├─ SDMMC1: microSD (logging)
                                         └─ UART1..7: RC, GPS, telem, VTX, ESC telem
```

**Feasibility flags (please review before layout):**

| # | Flag | Consequence |
|---|------|-------------|
| F1 | 80 A **per motor** on a "nano" 20×20 mm board is beyond what the copper can carry continuously. The math in §3 shows roughly 100 W of total dissipation at burst. | Treat 80 A strictly as a **time-limited burst** (≈10 s, see §4). Use a 25.5×25.5 mm or 30.5×30.5 mm ESC footprint if possible. |
| F2 | 6S operation (25.2 V max, plus transient overshoot) requires **40 V MOSFETs**, not 30 V. | Device selection below assumes 40 V parts. |
| F3 | Integrating the FC and ESC on one PCB puts a noisy 100 W power stage next to the IMU. | IMU on soft mounts, separated ground return, ≥8 mm from the switch nodes. |

## 2. Operating Model (6-Step Trapezoidal with Complementary/Synchronous PWM)

AM32 drives a 6-step trapezoidal commutation sequence with complementary PWM:

- At any instant, **two phases conduct** the phase current I_ph.
- One leg PWMs at duty D: its high-side (HS) switch conducts for D, its low-side (LS) synchronous rectifier conducts for (1 − D).
- The return leg's LS switch is held fully on.
- Each leg is active for 120° of every 360° electrical.

**Definition of the 80 A burst.** Here I_ph = 80 A of phase (motor) current. Battery current is roughly I_bat ≈ D · I_ph plus losses. At D = 1, 80 A phase current means 80 A battery current per motor.

## 3. Loss Model per ESC Channel

### 3.1 Conduction Loss with Electro-Thermal Feedback

The on-resistance rises with junction temperature:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

The current path always contains exactly two conducting MOSFET positions plus copper. In the PWM leg, the HS and LS switches time-share one position. Total conduction loss is therefore **independent of D**:

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 \left[\,2R_{ds}(T_j) + R_{cu}\right]$$

Evaluated at three junction temperatures, with I_ph = 80 A:

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond (FETs) | P_cu | **P_cond total** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.20 mΩ | 15.4 W | 3.8 W | 19.2 W |
| 100 °C | 1.61 mΩ | 20.6 W | 3.8 W | 24.4 W |
| 125 °C | 1.74 mΩ | 22.3 W | 3.8 W | **26.1 W** |

**Thermal runaway check.** The loop is stable if the loss increase caused by heating, multiplied by the thermal resistance, stays below 1:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\,R_{\theta} = 2 I^2 R_{ds25}\,\alpha \cdot R_\theta = 2(6400)(1.2\times10^{-3})(0.0045)\,R_\theta = 0.069\,R_\theta$$

The loop is stable while R_θ < 14.5 K/W per channel, which is satisfied with large margin.

**Per-FET breakdown at 125 °C** (I²R_ds = 11.1 W while a FET is conducting):

- **Steady-hold LS** (return leg): conducts for 1/3 of the electrical cycle, so its average is 3.7 W.
- **PWM-leg HS/LS pair:** shares the remaining 1/3 of the cycle. At D = 1, the HS averages 3.7 W.
- The electrical frequency is high: a 14-pole motor at 30 k RPM gives 3.5 kHz. That is far faster than the FET thermal time constant (milliseconds), so **average per-FET dissipation is the correct design value**: about 3.7 W worst case.
