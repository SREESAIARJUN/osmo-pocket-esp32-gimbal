## 0. Architectural Corrections and Assumptions

Two constraints affect everything below.

**1. The STM32H743 cannot be the ESC commutation MCU in an ArduPilot system.** ArduPilot does not commutate motors. The working architecture is:

- **FC board:** STM32H743, running ArduPilot. It emits bi-directional DShot600 on 4 timer channels.
- **ESC board:** four independent ESC MCUs running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, which commutate the motors and answer with eRPM telemetry. Typical parts are the STM32G071, AT32F421 or GD32F350.

The netlists in §5 reflect this two-board design.

**2. "Nano" (20×20 mm) and 80 A burst per motor are thermally incompatible except for sub-second bursts.** §3 derives this. The model is fully parametric, so you can substitute your actual FET and board stack-up.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-step with complementary (synchronous) PWM

In any commutation sector, two phases carry current I:

- **Static leg:** one FET is fully on, so it conducts for 100 % of the sector.
- **PWM leg:** the high side conducts for duty D, and the low side conducts during the (1−D) complement, minus dead time.

The total conduction loss per ESC is therefore independent of D (excluding dead time):

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[1] + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

The temperature dependence of on-resistance is:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds(25)}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

### 1.2 Numerical result (T_j = 110 °C)

- R_ds(110) = 1.0 × (1 + 0.005 × 85) = 1.425 mΩ
- P_cond,FET = 2 × 80² × 1.425 m = **18.24 W per ESC**
- P_cu = I² R_cu = 6400 × 0.5 m = **3.2 W per ESC**

### 1.3 Electro-thermal coupling

Because R_ds depends on T_j, solve the coupled system iteratively:

$$T_j = T_{board} + P_{FET}\,R_{th,jc}, \qquad P_{FET} = I^2 R_{ds(25)}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

This has the closed form:

$$T_j = \frac{T_b + I^2 R_{25}(1-25\alpha)R_{th}}{1 - I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_{th}}$$

**Stability condition (thermal runaway):** I² R₂₅ α R_th < 1. At 80 A: 6400 × 1e-3 × 0.005 × 0.8 = 0.0256. The device itself is stable; the board is the limiting factor (see §3).
