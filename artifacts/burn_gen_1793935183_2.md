# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage & DShot Architecture (Job #23)

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Feasibility Warnings

**System partitioning.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and generates bi-directional DShot600. Each ESC channel has its own small MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32) that performs commutation at 48 kHz. The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs directly. This is the only architecture ArduPilot supports for DShot ESCs.

**Datasheet values are placeholders.** All MOSFET and driver parameters below are representative. They are listed in §1 as symbols so you can substitute real datasheet values. Every number derived from them must be re-run once the BOM is frozen.

**Feasibility warnings (read before committing to the spec):**

1. **Total battery current.** 80 A per channel × 4 channels = **320 A from the battery**. An XT60 (≈60 A continuous) or a single 12 AWG lead cannot carry this. Even XT90 is marginal. Plan for solder pads plus 10 AWG wire or bigger.
2. **Burst only, never continuous.** As §4 shows, 80 A on all four channels at once is thermally a burst rating of roughly 1–2 s on a nano-size board. That is consistent with how the industry rates "burst" current, but it must be specified that way.
3. **Single FETs per switch are not recommended at 80 A.** §5 recommends paralleled FETs. A 20×20/30×30 board cannot reject ~130 W.

## 2. Conduction-Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

R_ds(T_j) = R_ds25 · [1 + α·(T_j − 25)]

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.5 mΩ × 1.65 = **2.475 mΩ**

Because R_ds rises with temperature, conduction loss and junction temperature are coupled. Solve them self-consistently by iterating:

T_j = T_pcb + P_FET(T_j)·R_θJ-PCB

### 2.2 Six-step commutation with synchronous rectification (AM32 complementary PWM)

During each 60° sector, current flows through:

- **The PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for D, and its complementary low-side FET conducts for (1−D). Exactly one of them is on at any instant.
- **The return leg:** one low-side FET is continuously on.

So there are always two R_DS in series with the phase current:

P_cond,ch = 2 · I_rms² · R_ds(T_j)

The RMS current includes the PWM ripple:

I_rms² = I_ph² + ΔI²/12

The ripple, with L_ph the motor line-to-line inductance (back-EMF absorbed into D), is:

ΔI = V_bus · D(1−D) / (L_ph · f_sw)

Worst case for ripple is D = 0.5. For example, with L_ph = 15 µH:

ΔI = 25.2 × 0.25 / (15e-6 × 48e3) = 8.75 A

ΔI²/12 = 6.4 A², which is less than 0.1 % of I_ph² = 6400 A². Ripple is therefore negligible at 48 kHz for typical motors. The exception is very low-inductance "pancake" stators below about 5 µH, so re-check it for those.

**Per-channel conduction loss at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C:**

P_cond,ch = 2 × 80² × 2.475e-3 = **31.7 W**
