# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #23): Power-Stage Model, Bi-Directional DShot600 Allocation, Netlists

## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

### 0.1 The H743 does not commutate the motors

An H743 running ArduPilot at a 1–4 kHz loop cannot also run four sensorless 6-step controllers at 48 kHz. Every production design uses this partition:

```
[STM32H743 FC] --4x bi-dir DShot600--> [4x ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32 fw)] --> [4x 3-phase gate driver] --> [24x MOSFET]
```

The 48 kHz figure is therefore the AM32 PWM frequency on each ESC MCU. The H743 owns only the DShot timers and DMA.

### 0.2 Design basis

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max (22.2 V nominal) |
| Burst current | 80 A per motor (phase ≈ battery-side at high duty) |
| f_sw | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous) PWM, 6-step |
| MOSFET | 40 V N-ch, PDFN 5×6. R_ds(on) = 1.0 mΩ typ @ V_gs = 10 V, 25 °C; Q_g = 40 nC, Q_gd = 8 nC, Q_rr = 30 nC, Q_oss = 40 nC, V_f(body) = 0.8 V, R_θJC = 1.5 K/W |
| Gate drive | 10 V, 1.5 A source / 2.5 A sink, R_g(ext) = 2.2 Ω |
| Dead time | 100 ns |
| Form factor | 30.5 × 30.5 mm, 8-layer 2 oz |

The 30.5 mm board is assumed deliberately. A true "nano" (20 × 20 mm) board cannot physically host 24 FETs of this class. See §2.5.

## 1. Loss Model (per motor)

### 1.1 Conduction topology

In 6-step operation with synchronous rectification, exactly two FETs carry the motor current at all times:

- **PWM ON:** HS_A + LS_B
- **PWM OFF:** LS_A (sync rectifier) + LS_B

Therefore:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\right],\ \alpha \approx 0.0055/\text{K}$$

At T_j = 115 °C, the hot-to-cold ratio is ≈ 1.5×. Adding 0.2 mΩ of copper/solder per path gives an effective R ≈ 1.5 mΩ.

$$P_{cond} = 2(80)^2(1.5\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

### 1.2 Switching (hard-switched leg only)

The Miller plateau sets the transition time. With V_pl ≈ 4.5 V and R_g,tot ≈ 2.2 Ω (external) + 1 Ω (internal) + driver R_out:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = \frac{8\,\text{nC}}{(10-4.5)/(3.2+3)\,\Omega} \approx 9\ \text{ns},\qquad t_f \approx \frac{8\,\text{nC}}{4.5/(3.2+1.5)} \approx 8.4\ \text{ns}$$

Use t_r + t_f ≈ 20 ns as a conservative figure (it includes current-rise overlap).

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = \tfrac12(25.2)(80)(20\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 1.3 Secondary terms

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f = 30 nC · 25.2 · 48 k | 0.036 W |
| C_oss | ½ · Q_oss · V · f | 0.024 W |
| Dead-time diode | V_f · I · 2t_dt · f = 0.8 · 80 · 200 ns · 48 k | 0.61 W |
| Gate drive (driver-side) | Q_g · V_gs · f · 2 FETs | 0.038 W |

### 1.4 Totals

$$P_{motor} = 19.2 + 0.97 + 0.036 + 0.024 + 0.61 + 0.04 \approx \mathbf{20.9\ W}$$

$$P_{board,80A} = 4 \times 20.9 \approx \mathbf{84\ W}$$

Conduction is 92% of the loss. At 80 A, 48 kHz costs about 1 W per motor more than 24 kHz would. Raising f_sw is not the thermal problem; R_ds(on) is.
