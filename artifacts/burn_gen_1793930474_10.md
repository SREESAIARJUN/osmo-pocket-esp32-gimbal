### 0.1 Architecture Correction

The STM32H743 does **not** generate the 48 kHz motor PWM. A practical architecture splits the work across two kinds of processor:

| Function | Processor | Firmware |
|---|---|---|
| Flight control, EKF, DShot600 bidirectional master | STM32H743VIH6 (FC) | ArduPilot ChibiOS |
| 6-step/FOC commutation, 48 kHz PWM, eRPM telemetry | 4× ESC MCU (e.g. AT32F421 or STM32G071) | AM32 or BLHeli_32 |

The H743's only motor-side job is producing four DShot600 lines and capturing the eRPM replies (§3).

### 0.2 Feasibility Flag

"Nano" (20×20 mm) and "80 A burst per motor" are physically contradictory. The analysis in §2.4 shows the copper and FET thermal mass cannot absorb roughly 95 W for a useful time.

- I model a **30.5×30.5 mm** 4-in-1 board. That is the smallest form factor where 6× PQFN3.3 FETs per channel plus bulk capacitance fit.
- The H743 FC is the 20×20 "nano" stack-mate.
- If Job #48 truly requires 20×20 for the ESC, de-rate to roughly 45–55 A burst.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Path in 6-Step Trapezoidal Commutation (Complementary PWM)

During each 60° sector, two phases carry I_ph:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for duty D. The synchronous low-side FET conducts for (1−D).
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET is on 100% of the sector.

Total conduction loss per motor is therefore:

$$P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This result is independent of D, which is why synchronous rectification matters.

### 1.2 Temperature Dependence

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{ds} = 1.2 \times 1.45 = 1.74\text{ mΩ}$$

### 1.3 Ripple Correction

For a triangular ripple of peak-to-peak ΔI:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{dc}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

The ripple is:

$$\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}-E_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

At the 50% duty worst case, with L_ph = 15 µH line-to-line and V_bus − E = 12.6 V:

- ΔI ≈ 8.75 A
- I_rms² = 6400 + 6.4 ≈ 6406 A²

The correction is negligible (0.1%) at 48 kHz. Higher f_sw is mainly an acoustic and low-L motor benefit.

### 1.4 Result

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 6406 \times 1.74\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{22.3\ W\ per\ motor}$$

Add trace and shunt resistance, with R_trace ≈ 0.3 mΩ per phase path on 2 oz × 4 layers:

$$P_{Cu} = 2 \times 6400 \times 0.3\text{ mΩ} = 3.8\text{ W}$$

This copper loss heats the board directly, not the junctions.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. The losses are taken per commutating pair.

### 2.1 Loss Terms

| Term | Expression | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·20 ns·48 k | 0.968 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 40 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.048 W |
| C_oss | ½·C_oss·V²·f_sw = ½·1.5 nF·635·48 k | 0.023 W |
| Dead-time diode | V_f·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·100 ns·48 k | 0.307 W |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw | 0.038 W (dissipated in the driver, not the FET) |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 1.35 W** |

### 2.2 Total Loss per Motor

$$P_{motor} = 22.3 + 1.35 = 23.6\text{ W}$$

Conduction dominates at 94%. The most effective optimisation lever is R_ds(on) or paralleled FETs, not f_sw.

### 2.3 Loss Distribution Among FETs

| FET | Loss | Calculation |
|---|---|---|
| Return-leg low-side (worst FET) | ≈ 11.2 W | I²R |
| PWM-leg high-side | ≈ 5.6 W + 1.0 W | D·I²R at D = 0.5, plus switching |
| PWM-leg sync low-side | ≈ 5.9 W | (1−D)·I²R plus dead-time diode |

Commutation rotates these roles, so the time-averaged loss per FET is P_motor/6 ≈ 3.9 W. Instantaneous peaks of 11 W last for 1/3 of an electrical period. At more than 300 Hz electrical, the die thermal time constant (around 1 ms) smooths this to roughly the average plus 30%. For conservatism, I use **P_FET,peak ≈ 7 W**.
