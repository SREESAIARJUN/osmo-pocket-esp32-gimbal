> **Architectural flag:** On virtually every commercial "4-in-1 ESC + H743" stack, the H743 does **not** drive the bridges directly. Each ESC channel, or a shared ESC MCU, runs its own firmware (AM32 or BLHeli_32 on an STM32G071 / AT32F421 / GD32). The H743 sends DShot and receives eRPM telemetry. The power-stage math below applies to the ESC section. The DShot math applies to the H743. The netlists reflect this split.

> **Realism flag:** On a nano board, 80 A per channel is only achievable as a **short burst** (≈1–3 s). Section 3 shows that continuous capability is roughly 30–35 A per channel without a heat-spreader. Treat any "80A" marketing rating accordingly.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step complementary PWM

At any commutation step, phase A is driven high (PWM) and phase B is held low. The paths are:

- **During D:** current flows through HS_A, the motor, then LS_B.
- **During (1−D), synchronous rectification:** current flows through LS_A, the motor, then LS_B.

The per-motor conduction loss is therefore:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[\underbrace{D}_{HS_A} + \underbrace{(1-D)}_{LS_A} + \underbrace{1}_{LS_B}] = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

This loss is **independent of duty cycle** when synchronous rectification is used. That makes it a very useful design simplification.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per motor at 80 A | Per FET (average over electrical cycle, ÷6) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W | 2.13 W |
| 100 °C | 1.34 mΩ | 17.1 W | 2.85 W |
| 135 °C | 1.50 mΩ | **19.2 W** | **3.20 W** |

Each FET conducts for about 1/3 of the electrical period, but at 2× the "average-share" density during its active window. For transients longer than one electrical period (~0.1–1 ms), the averaged value is what the thermal model sees.

### 1.3 Ripple correction

Ripple adds to the RMS current:

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp} \approx \frac{V_{bus}D(1-D)}{L_{ph} f_{sw}}
$$

For example, take L_ph = 15 µH (a typical 2207 low-kV motor) and D = 0.5:

- ΔI_pp ≈ 25.2 × 0.25 / (15 µH × 48 kHz) ≈ 8.75 A.
- The resulting loss increase is about 0.1 %, which is negligible at 48 kHz.

At 24 kHz with a low-inductance motor, this term would no longer be negligible.

### 1.4 Shunt and copper losses

| Element | Assumption | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Current-sense shunt (if per-channel) | 0.2 mΩ | I²R = 1.28 W |
| PCB copper path, bus → FETs → motor pad | ~0.5 mΩ per motor (2 oz, short pours) | 3.2 W |

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg (HS_A / LS_A) switches. LS_B is static.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(20\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.97\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time events per PWM period:

$$
P_{dt} = V_F I \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(200\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = V_{bus} Q_{rr} f_{sw} = 25.2(30\text{n})(48\text{k}) = 0.036\ W
$$

### 2.4 C_oss charging (HS and LS)

$$
P_{oss} \approx C_{oss}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1\text{n}(635)(48\text{k}) = 0.030\ W
$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated mainly in the driver and R_G, not in the FET:

$$
P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2(40\text{n})(10)(48\text{k}) = 0.038\ W
$$

### 2.6 Per-motor and board totals at an 80 A burst (T_j = 135 °C)

| Term | W per motor |
|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.20 |
| Switching overlap | 0.97 |
| Dead time | 0.61 |
| Reverse recovery + C_oss | 0.07 |
| Shunt | 1.28 |
| PCB copper | 3.20 |
| **Total** | **25.3 W** |

All four motors at burst: **≈101 W** into the board.

At 80 A, conduction dominates at about 76 % of the total. Raising f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz costs only about 0.8 W per motor, so 48 kHz is thermally acceptable. **R_DS(on) and copper are the levers that matter.**
