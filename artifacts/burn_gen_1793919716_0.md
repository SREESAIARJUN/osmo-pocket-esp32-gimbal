# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC (Job #51): Design Model

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and a Feasibility Flag

**Architecture.** An STM32H743 running ArduPilot cannot also run four 6-step or FOC motor controllers at 48 kHz in real time. This design therefore uses the standard split:

- **FC:** STM32H743VIT6 at 400 MHz, running ArduPilot. It generates bi-directional DShot600 on 4 channels.
- **ESC:** four AT32F421 (or STM32G071) MCUs running AM32 at 48 kHz PWM. Each drives a 3-phase bridge.
- **Format:** a stacked or AIO "nano" board with a 20×20 mm FC section and a 30×30 mm ESC section. The math below shows why 80A will not fit on a 20×20 ESC.

**Reference power stage (assumptions).** These values are representative of 40V PDFN3.3 FETs (e.g. JMSH0401-class). Substitute your actual datasheet values.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S full) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| R_DS(on) @25°C, V_GS = 10 V | R₂₅ | 1.0 mΩ |
| R_DS(on) temperature coefficient | α | ≈ 1.5× at T_j = 125°C |
| Rise / fall time (at driver) | t_r, t_f | 20 ns / 20 ns |
| Gate charge | Q_g | 50 nC |
| Reverse recovery charge | Q_rr | 30 nC |
| Output charge | Q_oss | 40 nC |
| Body-diode forward voltage | V_F | 0.8 V |
| Dead time | t_dt | 100 ns |
| Junction-to-case thermal resistance | R_θJC | 1.5 K/W |
| Gate drive voltage | V_drv | 10 V (e.g. FD6288Q) |
| Switching frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| Burst current per motor | I_burst | 80 A |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

R_DS(T_j) = R₂₅ · [1 + k·(T_j − 25)], with k ≈ 0.005 /K.

At T_j = 125°C: R_DS = 1.0 mΩ × 1.5 = **1.5 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current path in a 6-step bridge with synchronous rectification

In each 60° commutation sector, two phases conduct:

- The **PWM leg** alternates between its high side (fraction D) and its low side via synchronous rectification (fraction 1−D).
- The **return leg** low side conducts 100% of the sector.

So, per motor channel:

P_cond = I²·R_DS·[D + (1−D)] + I²·R_DS = **2·I²·R_DS(T_j)**

### 1.3 Numbers at 80A

| Quantity | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Per channel | 2 × 80² × 1.5 mΩ | **19.2 W** |
| Peak per single FET | 80² × 1.5 mΩ | 9.6 W |
| Average per FET | 19.2 W / 6 FETs | 3.2 W |
| PCB copper (2 oz, ~0.3 mΩ effective path) | 80² × 0.3 mΩ | 1.9 W |
| **4 channels, conduction + copper** | 4 × (19.2 + 1.9) | **≈ 84.4 W** |

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches hard (one transition pair per PWM period).

| Loss term | Formula | Result |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | P_sw = ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 kHz | 1.94 W |
| Reverse recovery | P_rr = Q_rr·V·f_sw = 30 nC × 25.2 × 48 kHz | 0.036 W |
| Output capacitance | P_oss ≈ ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw | 0.024 W |
| Dead-time body diode | P_dt = 2·V_F·I·t_dt·f_sw = 2 × 0.8 × 80 × 100 ns × 48 kHz | 0.61 W |
| Gate drive (2 FETs switching) | P_g = 2·Q_g·V_drv·f_sw | 0.048 W, dissipated in the driver |
| **Total per channel** | | **≈ 2.66 W** |

### 2.1 Frequency sensitivity

The switching terms scale linearly with frequency: P_sw ∝ f_sw.

- At 24 kHz: ≈ 1.3 W per channel.
- At 96 kHz: ≈ 5.3 W per channel.

48 kHz is a reasonable trade-off between loss and current ripple. Ripple is Δi = V_bus·D(1−D)/(L_ph·f_sw). For L_ph = 10 µH and D = 0.5, this gives ≈ 13 A peak-to-peak.

## 3. Total Loss and Efficiency at Burst

| Quantity | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Loss per channel | 19.2 + 1.9 + 2.66 | ≈ 23.8 W |
| **Total loss, 4 channels** | | **≈ 95 W** |
| Delivered power | 4 × 25.2 V × 80 A × D_eff (~0.9) | ≈ 7.26 kW |
| **Efficiency** | | **≈ 98.7%** |

The efficiency looks good, but the problem is the heat density, not the efficiency.

## 4. Thermal Calculations

### 4.1 Lumped transient model (single-node RC)

T(t) = T_amb + P·R_θ·(1 − e^(−t/τ)), where τ = R_θ·C_th.

**Thermal capacitance of a 30×30 ESC**

| Component | Mass × specific heat | C_th |
|---|---|---|
| FR4 (1.6 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz) | 2.5 g × 1.1 J/g·K | 2.75 J/K |
| Copper planes | 3.0 g × 0.385 J/g·K | 1.16 J/K |
| 24 FETs plus parts | ~1.5 g × 0.7 J/g·K | ~1.05 J/K |
| **Total** | | **≈ 5.0 J/K** |

**Board-to-air thermal resistance**

- In prop wash (~10 m/s, h ≈ 80 W/m²K), with area 2 × 9e-4 m²: R_θ ≈ 1/(80 × 0.0018) ≈ **7 K/W**.
- Still air: R_θ ≈ 40 K/W.

**Resulting time constants and rise rates**

- τ = 7 × 5 ≈ 35 s.
- Initial heating rate: dT/dt = 95 W / 5 J/K ≈ **19 K/s**, which is close to adiabatic.

### 4.2 Allowable burst duration

Take T_amb = 35°C and a board limit of 110°C (ΔT = 75 K, leaving margin for T_j):

t_max = −τ·ln(1 − ΔT/(P·R_θ)) = −35 × ln(1 − 75/665) ≈ **4.2 s**

Steady-state sustainable loss at ΔT = 75 K is P = 75/7 ≈ 10.7 W. That corresponds to roughly **27A continuous per motor**, since loss scales with I².

### 4.3 Junction rise above board (worst FET)

For a 1 s pulse, Z_θJC(1 s) ≈ 1.2 K/W.

ΔT_JC = 9.6 W × 1.2 K/W ≈ **11.5 K**

Then T_j ≈ 110 + 11.5 ≈ 122°C. This is consistent with the 1.5 mΩ hot R_DS assumption, so the model is self-consistent.

### 4.4 Verdict on burst rating

| Board | Burst rating |
|---|---|
| 30×30 | Rate as "80A for 3 s, 30A continuous" |
| 20×20 (C_th ≈ 2.2 J/K, R_θ ≈ 16 K/W) | t_max ≈ 0.9 s; **not credible**. Rate as 40A burst at most |

Firmware must enforce this limit:

- ArduPilot: `BATT_*` current limiting or a `MOT_BAT_CURR_MAX` cap.
- ESC: AM32 current limit plus an NTC thermal derate, using the NTC on the ESC.
