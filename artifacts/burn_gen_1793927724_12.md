# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Model, Thermal Analysis, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Architecture (Job #95)

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Feasibility Finding

The 80 A burst target is not achievable on a 20×20 mm nano stack. With the parameters below, the stage can sustain about **24 A/channel continuously** and **80 A/channel for about 1 second**, from a 40 °C start, before T_j reaches 125 °C. A 4×80 A simultaneous burst means 320 A from the battery, which no nano-format connector, lead (XT30 or 14 AWG), or pad set can carry.

You have three options:
- Accept 80 A as a firmware-enforced burst of 1 s or less, with a 20 to 25 A continuous rating.
- Move to a 30×30 mm format with 5×6 mm FETs.
- Lower the target.

The math is below so you can re-run it with your actual BOM.

**Architecture clarification.** The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs. The ESC side has its own MCU per channel or per board, typically AM32 on an STM32G071 or AT32F421 running a 48 kHz PWM. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

In 6-step complementary PWM, two phases conduct at any instant:
- **High-side leg:** the high FET for fraction D, and its complementary low FET for (1−D).
- **Return leg:** the low FET is on for the full 60° sector.

At every instant, exactly two channels carry the phase current I_ph. Conduction loss per ESC channel is therefore **independent of duty**:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

The phase current relates to battery-side current by I_DC = D·I_ph. An "80 A" rating at partial throttle therefore implies a higher phase current (80 A at D = 0.8 means I_ph = 100 A). The worst case used here is D ≈ 1, I_ph = 80 A.

**Per-FET RMS currents** over one electrical cycle (each FET is active for 1/3 of the cycle):

$$I_{RMS,HS}=I_{ph}\sqrt{D/3},\qquad I_{RMS,LS}=I_{ph}\sqrt{(1-D)/3+1/3}$$

**At 80 A with T_j = 125 °C (R = 2.61 mΩ):**

$$P_{cond}=2\cdot 80^2\cdot 2.61\times10^{-3}=\mathbf{33.4\ W\ per\ channel}$$

**Self-consistent junction temperature.** Because R_ds rises with temperature, iterate until T_j converges:

$$T_j^{(k+1)}=T_c+R_{\theta JC}\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}\left[P_{cond}(T_j^{(k)})+P_{sw}\right]$$

Here ½ accounts for the loss being split over the two conducting die. With T_c = 90 °C, the iteration converges at T_j ≈ 124 °C, which is marginal. Thermal runaway occurs if

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\cdot R_\theta \ge 1:\quad 2I^2R_{25}\alpha\cdot R_{\theta JA,eff}\ge1$$

At 80 A this becomes 0.104 W/K × R_θ, so runaway begins when the effective R_θJA exceeds about 9.6 K/W. That is easily reached in steady state on a nano board, which is why the 80 A rating must be time-limited.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per switching edge pair, applied to the PWM-ing high FET:

| Term | Formula | Value @ 80 A, 25.2 V |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f | ½·25.2·80·30 ns·48 k = **1.45 W** |
| Dead-time diode | V_f·I·2t_d·f | 0.8·80·300 ns·48 k = **0.92 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f | 30 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.036 W** |
| C_oss | ½·C_oss·V²·f (×2 FETs) | **0.030 W** |
