# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #10): Power, Thermal and DShot Architecture

## 0. Assumptions and Scope

Two facts shape the rest of this document.

**1. The H743 cannot commutate the motors itself.** A 4-in-1 ESC needs one dedicated commutation MCU per motor, running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. Typical parts are the STM32G071, AT32F421 or GD32E230. The H743 is the flight controller and talks to those four MCUs over bi-directional DShot600. The architecture below assumes this split.

**2. 80 A burst per motor on a nano (20×20 mm) footprint is not thermally sustainable.** The math in §3 shows the board can carry it for about 1 s, not the 5–10 s burst that ESC datasheets imply. I give a recommended re-rating at the end.

**The component parameters are representative assumptions, not a vendor-qualified BOM.** Substitute datasheet values before release.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology

The commutation scheme is 6-step trapezoidal with complementary (synchronous) PWM.

- At any instant, two switch positions carry the phase current: one high-side and one low-side, on different legs.
- During the PWM off-time, the freewheel current flows through the synchronous FET rather than the body diode. The only exception is the dead time.

Each conduction path is therefore always two R_DS(on) in series, regardless of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2 \cdot R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature Dependence

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right]^{\,}\quad \alpha\approx0.005\,\text{K}^{-1}\Rightarrow k_T\approx1.5\text{ at }125\,°C$$

$$R_{hot} = 1.2 \times 1.5 = 1.8\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Ripple Correction

The peak-to-peak current ripple at 48 kHz is worst at D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{LL}\,f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{30\mu \times 48k} = 4.4\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{rms} = I_{dc}\sqrt{1+\tfrac{1}{12}\left(\tfrac{\Delta I}{I_{dc}}\right)^2} = 80\sqrt{1+\tfrac{1}{12}(0.055)^2} \approx 80.01\ \text{A}$$

The ripple contribution is negligible at 80 A. It only matters below about 10 A.

### 1.4 Result

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Per conducting FET | 80² × 1.8 mΩ = **11.5 W** |
| Per motor (2 FETs) | **23.0 W** |
| 4 motors at 80 A simultaneously | **92.2 W** |

### 1.5 Copper and Shunt Losses

These are often forgotten, and they matter here.

- **Shunt:** A shared 0.25 mΩ shunt at 4 × 80 = 320 A dissipates 320² × 0.25 mΩ = **25.6 W**.
  - Recommendation: drop to 0.1 mΩ, or use per-channel sensing. With 0.1 mΩ the loss falls to 10.2 W.
- **Bus copper:** The bus copper (battery pads, B+ planes, 2 oz) is about 0.15 mΩ effective, giving roughly **15 W**.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In 6-step operation, only one leg switches at a time per motor, so there is one hard-switched transition pair per PWM period.

### 2.1 V-I Overlap (Hard Switching)

Gate currents during the Miller plateau:

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{DRV}-V_{pl}}{R_{on}} = \frac{7}{10}=0.70\text{ A} \qquad I_{g,off}=\frac{V_{pl}}{R_{off}}=\frac{3}{6}=0.50\text{ A}$$
