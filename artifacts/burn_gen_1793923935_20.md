# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #28): Power-Stage, Thermal and Bidirectional DShot Model

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

No project-specific MOSFET, layer stack or pinout was supplied, so every number below comes from the assumed parameters in the table. Each model is written so you can substitute your real values. Treat all results as design estimates until they are checked against thermocouple and IR data.

**Architecture clarification.** On an AIO board the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and generates DShot. Each motor's commutation and 48 kHz PWM are run by a separate ESC MCU, such as an AT32F421 running AM32 or an EFM8/BB21 running BLHeli. The H743 does not switch the FETs.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step synchronous commutation
At any instant, two phases carry I_ph and one phase floats.
- The PWM leg alternates between high-side (fraction D) and synchronous low-side (fraction 1−D).
- The return leg's low-side is on continuously.

So the current always flows through exactly **two FET channels in series**, independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2\,\big(2R_{DS}(T_j) + R_{Cu}\big)$$

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)
$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

This makes the loss and junction temperature mutually dependent, so solve iteratively. At T_j ≈ 100 °C:

$$R_{DS} = 1.2\,\text{mΩ}\times1.45 = 1.74\,\text{mΩ}$$

$$P_{FET,cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.74\text{ mΩ} = 6400 \times 3.48\text{ mΩ} = \mathbf{22.3\ W}$$

$$P_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\text{ mΩ} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

Copper resistance also rises about 0.39 %/K, adding roughly 0.9 W at 100 °C. It is lumped below.

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution
Each FET is active for 1/3 of the electrical cycle. The 22.3 W is shared across 6 FETs, but unevenly:
- Low-sides carry more at low D.
- High-sides carry more at high D.
- **Average per FET ≈ 3.7 W.** The worst FET reaches about 5–6 W at extreme duty.
- Peak instantaneous power is 11 W, but the electrical period at 2–10 kHz eRPM is far shorter than the FET's thermal time constant (ms range). The average value therefore governs.
