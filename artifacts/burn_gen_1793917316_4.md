## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in trapezoidal 6-step with synchronous rectification
At any instant, two phases conduct:
- One leg is PWM-ing. Current flows through its high-side FET during the D interval and through its low-side FET (synchronous rectification) during the 1−D interval.
- One leg has its low-side FET held on for the full 60° sector.

So phase current I_ph always passes through **exactly two R_DS(on)** in series. This holds regardless of duty cycle when synchronous rectification is used.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}\ (\text{fits }\times1.6\text{ at }125^\circ\text{C})$$

### 1.3 Losses

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j), \qquad \bar P_{cond,FET} = \frac{P_{cond,bridge}}{6}=\frac{I_{ph}^2R_{DS}}{3}$$

The 1/3 factor arises because each FET carries current for 120° of the 360° electrical period.

**At 80 A burst and T_j = 125 °C:**
- R_DS = 1.2 mΩ × 1.6 = 1.92 mΩ
- P_cond,bridge = 2 × 6400 × 1.92×10⁻³ = **24.6 W per motor**
- P_cond,FET (average) = **4.1 W**

The instantaneous peak in the always-on low-side FET is I²R = 12.3 W during its 120° window. This matters for the junction ripple at low eRPM, where the window is long relative to the thermal time constant τ_jc.

### 1.4 Parasitic copper and shunt

$$P_{Cu} = I_{ph}^2\,(R_{trace}+R_{shunt,share})$$

Assumptions: 0.3 mΩ phase trace and pad, plus a 0.25 mΩ shunt on the shared DC return.
- P_Cu ≈ 6400 × 0.3 mΩ ≈ **1.9 W per motor**
- The shunt carries summed bus current. At 4 × 80 A × D_avg ≈ 200 A, this gives **10 W**. That is unworkable on a nano board, so you would need per-motor sensing or accept no current sensing at burst.
