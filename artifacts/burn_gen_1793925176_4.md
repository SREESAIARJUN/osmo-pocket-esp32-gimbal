## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step complementary PWM
In every 60° electrical sector, two phases conduct:
- **Phase A (PWM leg):** the high side (HS) conducts for duty fraction D, and the complementary low side (LS) conducts for 1−D.
- **Phase B (return leg):** the LS is held fully on.

So **two R_ds(on) are always in series** with the phase current I_ph. Over one electrical cycle each leg spends 1/3 of the time as the PWM leg, 1/3 as the return leg, and 1/3 floating.

**Per-device average conduction loss:**

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,D$$

$$P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,\big[(1-D) + 1\big]$$

**Per-channel total:**

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Ripple and temperature correction

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12},\qquad \Delta I \approx \frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\big[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\big],\quad \alpha\approx 0.005/\text{K}$$

Ripple check: with L_ph-ph = 15 µH, mid-duty, and V_bus − V_bemf ≈ 12V, ΔI ≈ 8 A_pp. That adds only 0.08% to I² at 80A, so it is negligible at burst current.
