# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #21): Electro-Thermal Model and Timer Architecture

This document covers four things:

1. **Loss model**: 80 A burst conduction losses and 48 kHz switching losses, per FET, per channel and per board.
2. **Thermal model**: a transient model that yields the burst time limit.
3. **Timer/DMA allocation**: ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 on the H743.
4. **Netlists**: FC MCU, ESC power stage, gate drive and sensing.

The values are representative, not taken from a released datasheet. Replace the parameters in §1 with your selected parts' datasheet values before you freeze the layout.

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Flags

**Topology.** The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. In a 4-in-1 stack, the H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600. Each motor is commutated by a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware (e.g., STM32G071, AT32F421 or GD32E230). The 48 kHz PWM is generated by those ESC MCUs. The model below follows that split.

**Feasibility flag: read this first.** 80 A per channel on a "nano" (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board is not a continuous rating; it is a short burst.

- Instantaneous dissipation at full burst is about **85 W** (§1.6).
- No small board can reject that heat steady-state.
- The design must therefore be specified as:
  - a burst current,
  - a burst duration,
  - a duty-cycle envelope, and
  - a thermal derating that is enforced in firmware.

Section 2 computes that envelope. Expect roughly 5 s at 80 A from a warm start. Many commercial "80 A nano" ratings are optimistic.

### 1.2 Commutation Model

The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM).

At any instant, **two FETs carry I_ph**: one high-side (PWM) and one low-side (on, or synchronously rectifying). Each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical period (120°).

Duty cycle relates bus current to phase current:

$$I_{bat} = D\cdot I_{ph}$$

At burst (D → 0.95–1.0), I_ph ≈ 80 A.

### 1.3 Conduction Loss

Current ripple adds to the RMS value. Its peak-to-peak magnitude is approximately:

$$\Delta I \approx \frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{2L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5–10 µH phase-to-phase inductance at 48 kHz, ΔI/I_ph ≈ 0.05–0.15. The RMS correction is then:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2\left(1+\tfrac{1}{12}(\Delta I/I_{ph})^2\right) \approx 1.002\,I_{ph}^2 \;\Rightarrow\; \text{negligible}$$

Hot on-resistance:

$$R_{DS,hot}=k_T R_{DS,25}=1.5\ \text{m}\Omega$$

**Per channel (two FETs always conducting):**

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,(R_{DS,hot}+R_{cu}) = 2(6400)(1.8\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{23.0\ W}$$

This splits into 19.2 W in silicon and 3.84 W in copper.

**Per FET, averaged over an electrical cycle (1/3 conduction):**

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,I_{ph}^2 R_{DS,hot} = \tfrac{1}{3}(9.6) = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

The instantaneous value while conducting is 9.6 W. This matters for the junction ripple at low electrical frequency, because Z_th(t) at 1 ms is not negligible.

**Junction ripple check.** The electrical frequency is:

$$f_e = \frac{\text{RPM}\cdot p}{120}$$

For 2207-class motors, take RPM ≈ 30k and p = 14 poles, giving f_e = 3.5 kHz. Each conduction window is therefore about 95 µs. With Z_th,jc(100 µs) ≈ 0.1 K/W, the junction ripple is about 1 K, which is negligible.

These losses occur only on the PWM-switched leg.
