# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Electrothermal Model, DShot Timing, and Reference Netlist

**Status:** Engineering model, revision 0.

The derivations below are rigorous. The numeric values are **parametric examples** based on an assumed MOSFET class. Before release, replace them with datasheet values for your chosen parts and validate on a thermal test bench.

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and an Early Warning

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Form factor | 30.5 mm or 25.5 mm AIO, 8-layer, 2 oz copper on outer and power layers |
| Supply | 6S LiPo: V_bus = 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal |
| FC MCU | STM32H743VIT6 at 480 MHz. APB1 and APB2 timer clocks = 240 MHz |
| ESC MCUs | 4× AT32F421 (or STM32G071) running AM32 firmware, one per motor |
| Gate drivers | 4× FD6288Q (3-phase, 250 V, bootstrap) |
| MOSFETs | 24× 40 V N-channel, 5×6 mm or 3.3×3.3 mm package |
| Example FET parameters | R_DS(on),25 = 1.0 mΩ, Q_g = 50 nC, C_oss = 1.5 nF, Q_rr = 50 nC, V_SD = 0.8 V, R_θJC = 1.2 K/W |
| PWM | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous rectification), 6-step trapezoidal |
| Burst rating | 80 A **per motor** |

The H743 does not drive FETs directly. Commutation runs on the ESC MCUs. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 commands and receives eRPM telemetry.

> ⚠️ **Architecture finding.** The math in §1–§3 shows that 80 A per motor on a nano-class AIO is only a **short-burst** rating, on the order of seconds. There is **no thermal steady state** at 80 A without forced cooling. The datasheet must state a burst duration and an ambient-temperature condition.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

Example: R(100 °C) = 1.45 mΩ and R(125 °C) = 1.60 mΩ.

### 1.2 Six-step synchronous PWM, per motor

During each 60° commutation sector, one leg switches with duty D. Another leg is held with its low-side FET on. In the switching leg, the high-side FET conducts for D and the synchronous low-side FET conducts for 1−D.

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 R\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R = \boxed{2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)}$$

This result is independent of D.

The loss is not shared equally among the six FETs. Each phase spends 120° high and 120° low per electrical cycle:

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I^2 R, \qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(2-D)\, I^2 R$$

The worst case is the low-side FET at low duty (D → 0), which dissipates **(2/3)·I²R**.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A and T_j = 100 °C

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| I² | 6400 A² |
| P_cond per motor | 2 × 6400 × 1.45 mΩ = **18.6 W** |
| Worst single FET (low side, D → 0) | **6.2 W** |
| PCB copper loss (≈ 3 squares/phase at 0.25 mΩ/sq on 2 oz, ~4 paralleled layers → ≈ 0.2 mΩ per path, 2 paths) | 2 × 6400 × 0.2 mΩ ≈ **2.6 W** |
