# STM32H743 AIO 4-in-1 ESC / Flight Controller: Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, BDShot DMA Allocation, and Reference Netlist

## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

Two points about the architecture shape the rest of the analysis.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every practical 4-in-1 AIO, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot600 to four ESC MCUs (for example, AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32). The 48 kHz PWM is generated by those ESC MCUs, not by the H743. Section 3 therefore covers only the H743 side (DShot/BDShot), and Sections 1 and 2 cover the power stage.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano board (~30×30 mm, 20×20 mounting) is thermally marginal.** The model below gives a quantitative burst-duration limit rather than assuming 80 A is sustainable.

All device parameters below are representative values for a 30–40 V MOSFET in a PDFN 3.3×3.3 package. Replace them with datasheet values for your selected part. The equations are general.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-Dependent On-Resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + k_T (T_j - 25)],\quad k_T \approx 0.0045\ /\text{K}$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_ds ≈ 2.0 × 1.38 ≈ **2.8 mΩ**.

### 1.2 RMS Current Including Ripple

With peak-to-peak ripple ratio r = ΔI/I:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2\left(1 + \frac{r^2}{12}\right)$$

For a typical low-inductance drone motor at 48 kHz, r ≈ 0.2, giving a correction of +0.3%. This is negligible and is dropped below.
