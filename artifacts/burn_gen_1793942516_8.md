## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

### 0.1 System architecture

An "STM32H743 4-in-1 ESC" is built as an all-in-one (AIO) board with two processor domains. The H743 does not drive the MOSFET gates directly.

```
            ┌──────────────── FC domain (3V3) ─────────────────┐
 IMU(SPI) ──┤ STM32H743 (ArduPilot)                            │
 Baro(I2C)──┤   TIM1 CH1..CH4 ── Bi-dir DShot600 ──┐           │
 OSD/RX ────┤   ADC: VBAT, IBUS, ESC_TEMP          │           │
            └──────────────────────────────────────┼───────────┘
            ┌──────────────── ESC domain ──────────┼───────────┐
            │ 4× ESC MCU (AM32 / BLHeli_32 class)  ◄┘           │
            │ 4× 3-phase gate driver                            │
            │ 24× N-MOSFET (6 per motor, 1 per switch)          │
            │ Shared VBUS, bulk + MLCC decoupling, IBUS shunt   │
            └───────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 0.3 Up-front verdict

At 80 A, the conduction loss in a single-FET-per-switch nano board is about **23 W per motor**. If all four motors burst at once, the board dissipates about **100 W** of heat.

- **Continuous operation:** this is thermally impossible on this footprint.
- **Burst operation:** it is achievable only for about **2 s with all four motors** or about **6–8 s for one motor**.

Section 3 derives these figures. The "80 A burst" rating must therefore carry a time qualifier and be enforced in ESC firmware.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

Assume trapezoidal (6-step) commutation with synchronous rectification, which is the AM32/BLHeli_32 default.

- During the PWM on-time, current flows high-side (phase A) → motor → low-side (phase B).
- During the off-time, current freewheels through the synchronous low-side FET on phase A plus the low-side FET on phase B.

So at essentially every instant, **two FET channels** carry the phase current. The dead-time slivers are handled separately in §2.4.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\left[1 + k_T\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad k_T \approx 0.0067\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

This gives α_R ≈ 1.5 at 100 °C:

$$
R_{ds}(100^\circ C) = 1.2\,\text{mΩ} \times 1.5 = 1.8\,\text{mΩ}
$$

### 1.3 RMS current

At high throttle, current ripple is small relative to 80 A because motor inductance at 48 kHz smooths it. Take I_rms ≈ I_dc,phase = 80 A. If you want the ripple term included:

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}
$$

With ΔI_pp ≈ 8 A, the ripple term adds about 0.1 %, so it is negligible.

### 1.4 Conduction loss per motor

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j) = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.8\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{23.0\ W}
$$

The per-FET split depends on the duty cycle D:

$$
P_{HS} = D\,I^2 R_{ds},\qquad P_{LS,sync} = (1-D)\,I^2 R_{ds},\qquad P_{LS,return} = I^2 R_{ds}
$$

The **return-path low-side FET carries current 100 % of its 60° electrical interval**. That makes it the hottest device at 11.5 W instantaneous. Averaged over a full electrical cycle, each FET carries current about one third of the time. However, the electrical period at burst RPM is ≪ 1 ms, which is far shorter than the die thermal time constant (about 1–5 ms). Size for the **average per FET (≈ 3.8 W)**, and verify that the 11.5 W instantaneous peak causes no ripple overshoot (see §3.3).
