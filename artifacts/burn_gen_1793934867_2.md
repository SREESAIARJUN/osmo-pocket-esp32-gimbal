# Job #63: STM32H743 Nano FC with 80 A-Burst 4-in-1 ESC

## Design Analysis Package, Rev A

---

## 0. Architectural Ground Truth (Read First)

Three findings shape the rest of this document.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 stack, the FC sends DShot commands and each ESC channel runs its own MCU. Here that MCU is an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32. The H743's job is DShot600 generation, bidirectional eRPM capture, and filtering. The motor-control math in §1–3 applies to the ESC board. The DMA math in §4 applies to the FC.

2. **80 A burst on all four channels at once is not thermally sustainable on a nano (20×20 mm) board.** §3 quantifies this. Even on a 30.5 mm board, 4×80 A is a **≤2–3 s** event. The honest rating is "80 A burst per channel, non-simultaneous; 4×45 A continuous with prop wash." Marketing must not publish "4×80 A."

3. **Bidirectional DShot cannot use complementary (CHxN) timer outputs**, because they have no input-capture path. This eliminates several "convenient" H743 pin maps. See §4.

## 2. Loss Model (Per ESC Channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, the current path always runs through exactly **two** MOSFET channels in series:

- During the PWM on-time, the path is high-side (leg A) → motor → low-side (leg B).
- During the off-time, the path is low-side (leg A, sync rect) → motor → low-side (leg B).

So the instantaneous bridge conduction loss is independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I^2\, R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right]$$

Because R_ds(on) rises with junction temperature, the result must be solved self-consistently (see §3.4). The table evaluates it at two fixed junction temperatures:

| T_j | R_ds(on) | P_cond @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 0.85 mΩ | 2 × 6400 × 0.00085 = **10.9 W** |
| 125 °C | 1.23 mΩ | 2 × 6400 × 0.00123 = **15.7 W** |

**Copper and trace path.** Allow 0.4 mΩ for the bus, phase pad, and solder path:

$$P_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.0004 = 2.6\ \text{W}$$

**Per-FET distribution.** Each FET carries current for 1/3 of the electrical cycle. A high-side FET is on for duty D of that third. Its paired low-side FET carries current for the full third plus (1 − D) of the PWM third. At D ≈ 0.5, the hottest FET (low-side) sees about:

$$P_{FET,max} \approx I^2 R \left(\tfrac{1}{3} + \tfrac{1-D}{3}\right) = I^2 R \times 0.5$$

This gives **3.9 W** at 125 °C. Expect the low-side FETs to run hotter. Place them on the best copper.

### 2.2 Switching Losses (Hard-Switched Edges)

Switching times use the gate-charge (plateau) approximation:

$$t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,(R_{drv,src}+R_g+R_{g,int})}{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}, \qquad t_{f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,(R_{drv,snk}+R_{g,int})}{V_{pl}}$$

With R_drv,src ≈ 6.7 Ω and R_drv,snk ≈ 5.6 Ω:

- t_r ≈ 11 nC × 12.4 Ω / 7 V ≈ **19.5 ns**
- t_f ≈ 11 nC × 6.6 Ω / 3 V ≈ **24 ns**

Only the high-side PWM edges are hard-switched. The sync-rect FET turns on after dead time with its body diode already conducting, so it switches at near-zero voltage.

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 43.5\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{2.10\ W
