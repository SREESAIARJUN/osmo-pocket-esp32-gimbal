# STM32H743 Nano AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC): Systems Architecture Package, Job #41

## 0. Architecture Findings Before the Math

Three issues affect every downstream number.

**F1. The H743 does not commutate the motors directly.** Four 3-phase bridges need 12 complementary PWM pairs plus 4 BEMF/current-sense paths. The H743 has only two advanced timers (TIM1, TIM8), each with 3 complementary pairs, plus HRTIM. That is not enough for 4 motors with proper dead-time hardware. The baseline is therefore:

- **H743**: flight controller running ArduPilot. Generates bidirectional DShot600.
- **4× ESC MCUs** (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or BLHeli_32-class): each does 6-step commutation at 48 kHz.
- **3× half-bridge gate drivers per channel** (or a 3-phase driver such as the FD6288Q class).

**F2. "80A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board needs definition.** The two readings differ by 16× in I²R:

| Interpretation | Battery bus current | Conduction loss scale |
|---|---|---|
| (A) 80 A per channel | 320 A total | ~115 W of FET conduction alone. Seconds-scale burst only. |
| (B) 80 A total (20 A per channel) | 80 A | ~7 W. Realistic for a nano. |

The model below is fully parametric. I work both cases and recommend designing to (B) with a 30 A/channel burst rating.

**F3. The netlists are reference designs.** They have not been through ERC or DRC. All pin/ball assignments must be checked against the specific H743 package datasheet (likely UFBGA/TFBGA for a nano) and the AF tables before layout.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Topology-Accurate Expression

In 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM, motor current I_m always flows through exactly two switch positions in series: one high-side and one low-side. Synchronous rectification means the freewheel interval also flows through a FET channel, not a diode. The total is therefore independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_m^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j) \qquad R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha_R (T_j-25)]$$

Corrections:
- **Phase-current ripple**: I_rms² = I_avg² + ΔI²/12. With ΔI ≈ 10% of I_m, this adds about 0.1%. Negligible.
- **Board copper**: add R_cu (see §2.3).
- **Dead time**: during t_dt the body diode conducts instead of the channel. This is counted separately in §3.

### 2.2 Electro-Thermal Fixed Point

Losses raise T_j, which raises R_ds, which raises losses. The system must be solved iteratively:

$$T_j = T_{amb} + R_{\theta}\left[\sum_{ch} I_m^2\,2R_{ds,25}(1+\alpha_R(T_j-25)) + P_{sw}\right]$$

This is linear in T_j, so it has a closed form:

$$T_j = \frac{T_{amb} + R_\theta\,(K(1-25\alpha_R) + P_{sw})}{1 - R_\theta K \alpha_R},\qquad K = \sum_{ch} 2 I_m^2 R_{ds,25}$$

**Thermal runaway criterion:** R_θ·K·α_R ≥ 1 means there is no steady state.

### 2.3 Worked Numbers

**Case A: 80 A per channel, all 4 channels**
- K = 4 × 2 × 6400 × 1.5 mΩ = **76.8 W** at 25 °C.
- At T_j = 100 °C: R_ds = 1.5 × 1.5025 = 2.25 mΩ, so P_cond = **115 W**.
- Steady state with R_θ = 6 K/W: R_θ·K·α = 6 × 76.8 × 0.0067 = **3.09 > 1, thermal runaway.** No steady state exists. This case is valid only as a time-limited burst (§4).

**Case B: 20 A per channel (80 A total)**
- K = 4 × 2 × 400 × 1.5 mΩ = **4.8 W**.
- R_θKα = 0.193, which is stable.

**Board copper (both cases).** Battery to bus: 2 oz copper, 4 parallel planes, ~8 mm effective width, ~15 mm length. Assume R_cu ≈ 0.15 mΩ.
- Case A: 320² × 0.15 mΩ = **15.4 W**. Case A is not survivable on nano copper, even before counting the FETs.
