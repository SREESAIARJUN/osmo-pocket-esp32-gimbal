# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Nano Stack): Electrothermal Model and DShot Architecture

## 0. Assumptions and Key Findings

I don't have Job #51's BOM, so this model uses representative parameters, flagged as **[A]**. Every equation is parametric. Substitute the datasheet values for your selected MOSFETs and driver before sign-off.

**Architectural correction.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller MCU. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU, typically an STM32G071 or AT32F421 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 does not commutate motors.

**Engineering finding.** The model in §2–§4 shows that 80 A per motor on a 20×20/25.5×25.5 mm board is a **transient (~1–3 s) rating only**:

- Realistic continuous current is about **25 A/motor** with prop-wash cooling.
- A simultaneous 4×80 A event would draw 320 A from the battery. This exceeds what XT30/XT60 connectors and nano-class power planes can carry.
- The specification should state 80 A as a **single-motor, ≤2 s burst**. This must be enforced in firmware (§4.4).

## 1. Commutation Model (6-Step, Synchronous Rectification)

In each 60° sector:

- One high-side (HS) switch PWMs at duty D.
- Its complementary low-side (LS) switch freewheels for (1−D).
- One LS switch on another phase conducts continuously as the return path.

Each phase leg is in each role for 1/3 of the electrical cycle. This gives the per-device conduction duty:

$$
\delta_{HS} = \frac{D}{3}, \qquad \delta_{LS} = \frac{(1-D) + 1}{3} = \frac{2-D}{3}
$$

As a sanity check, summing over 3 legs gives δ_HS + δ_LS = 2. This matches the fact that exactly two switches are always in series with the motor current.
