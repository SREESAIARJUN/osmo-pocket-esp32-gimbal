# STM32H743 Nano AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC): Power Stage, Thermal, and Bidirectional DShot Architecture

## 0. Architecture Corrections Before the Math

Three issues in the brief would invalidate any model built on it.

| Issue | Reality | Resolution used here |
|---|---|---|
| "STM32H743 4-in-1 ESC" | The H743 runs the flight controller (ArduPilot). It cannot run four sensorless 6-step ESCs. ## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step complementary PWM

In any commutation sector, two phase legs are active:

- **Leg A (PWM leg):** the high side conducts for duty D. The complementary low side conducts the freewheel current for (1−D).
- **Leg B (return leg):** the low side conducts continuously (100 %).

Instantaneous conduction loss per channel:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[\underbrace{D}_{HS_A} + \underbrace{(1-D)}_{LS_A} + \underbrace{1}_{LS_B}] = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

**Result:** with synchronous rectification, conduction loss is independent of duty cycle. This is why throttle position barely changes FET heating at a given current.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

| T_j (°C) | R_DS (mΩ) | P_cond per channel @ 80 A (W) |
|---|---|---|
| 25 | 1.50 | 19.2 |
| 75 | 1.95 | 25.0 |
| 100 | 2.18 | 27.8 |
| 125 | 2.40 | **30.7** |

Add board copper. A 2 oz, 3 mm-wide, 15 mm path has about 2.5 mΩ round trip. At 80 A this adds **≈ 16 W**, plus the battery pad and solder joint resistance. On nano boards, **copper loss often rivals FET loss.**

$$
P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{trace},\quad R_{trace} = \frac{\rho_{Cu}\,L}{w\,t}\,[1+0.0039(T-25)]
$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each FET conducts 120° of every 360° electrical period. At typical electrical frequencies (0.5–3 kHz), the die thermal time constant (~1–5 ms) partially averages this. Average per FET:

$$
\bar P_{FET,cond} \approx \frac{P_{cond}}{6} \approx 5.1\text{ W } (T_j = 125^\circ\text{C})
$$

The **peak** instantaneous loss in the continuously-on low side is I²R = 15.4 W. Use this for low-RPM or stall cases, where averaging fails.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The high side switches hard; the low side commutates via its body diode, which gives zero-voltage turn-on.

### 2.1 Hard-switching overlap (high side)

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = \tfrac12(25.2)(80)(30\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = \mathbf{1.45\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction (low side, twice per period)

$$
P_{dt} = 2\,V_{SD}\,I\,t_{dt}\,f_{sw} = 2(0.8)(80)(200\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = \mathbf{1.23\ W}
$$

AM32 lets you trim dead time. Each 50 ns saved recovers ~0.3 W, but shoot-through risk rises.

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = V_{bus} Q_{rr} f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 30\,\text{nC} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = 0.036\text{ W}
$$

The low-side diode Qrr also adds turn-on current to the high side. Inflate P_sw by ~10 % for that.

### 2.4 Output capacitance and gate drive

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V^2 f_{sw} = 0.015\text{ W}, \qquad
P_{g} = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 0.038\text{ W (driver-side)}
$$

### 2.5 Switching total and frequency sensitivity

$$
P_{sw,tot} \approx 1.45 + 0.15 + 1.23 + 0.04 + 0.02 \approx \mathbf{2.9\ W/channel}
$$

| f_sw | P_sw,tot | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| 24 kHz | 1.45 W | More audible, more current ripple |
| 48 kHz | 2.9 W | Baseline |
| 96 kHz | 5.8 W | Rarely worth it at 80 A |

At 80 A, **conduction dominates roughly 10:1**. Lowering f_sw is not the lever. R_DS(on) and copper are.

## 3. Total Burst Power and Thermal Model

### 3.1 Loss budget at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

| Source | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 30.7 W | 122.8 W |
| Switching + dead time | 2.9 W | 11.6 W |
| Copper (est.) | ~10–16 W | ~40–64 W |
| **Total** | **~44–50 W** | **~175–200 W** |

Converter efficiency at full burst: P_out ≈ 4 × 25.2 × 80 ≈ 8 kW, so η ≈ 97.5 %. The efficiency looks excellent; the problem is heat **density**.

### 3.2 Why "80 A continuous" is impossible here

On a 20×20 board, steady-state still-air R_th(board→ambient) is ≈ 15–25 K/W. With prop wash this drops to roughly 4–8 K/W.

$$
\Delta T_{ss} = 180\text{ W} \times 5\ \text{K/W} = 900\ \text{K}
$$

There is no steady state. The board survives only on thermal capacitance.
