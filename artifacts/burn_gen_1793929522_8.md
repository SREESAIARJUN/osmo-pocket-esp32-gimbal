## 0. Architectural Correction (Read First)

The phrase "STM32H743 4-in-1 ESC" conflates two processors. ArduPilot does not perform BLDC commutation.

| Function | Processor | Firmware |
|---|---|---|
| Flight control, DShot600 master, eRPM consumer | STM32H743VI (FC) | ArduPilot |
| 6-step commutation, 48 kHz PWM, BEMF, telemetry | 4 separate ESC MCUs (e.g. STM32G071 / AT32F421), or a single-MCU 4-in-1 | AM32 / BLHeli_32-class |

The 48 kHz switching analysis applies to the ESC MCUs. The DShot/DMA analysis applies to the H743. If you intended the H743 to drive gate drivers directly, that is a custom firmware program outside ArduPilot.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

In 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification, the phase current always flows through exactly two MOSFETs:

- During the ON interval: high-side A → motor → low-side B.
- During the OFF interval: low-side A (synchronous freewheel) → motor → low-side B.

The conduction loss is therefore nearly independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{ph,rms}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

At full-throttle burst, I_ph ≈ I_bat ≈ 80 A (D → 1).

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.05\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{13.44\ W\ per\ channel}$$

Add copper trace and bus loss. With 6 layers of 2 oz copper, assume about 0.3 mΩ of effective path per channel:

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 1.9\ W$$

At part-throttle, phase current exceeds battery current: I_ph ≈ I_bat / D. Loss scales with I_ph², so a high-Kv motor bogged down at low duty is also a worst case. ESC current limiting must act on phase current, not battery current.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switching leg incurs these losses.

**1. Hard-switching V–I overlap:**
$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**2. Dead-time body-diode conduction:**
$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2 t_{dead}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**3. Reverse recovery:**
$$P_{rr} = V_{bus} Q_{rr} f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 30\text{nC} \times 48\text{k} = 0.036\ W$$

**4. Output capacitance:**
$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V^2 f = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = 0.023\ W$$

**5. Gate drive** (6 FETs; dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors):
$$P_g = 6\, Q_g V_{GS} f = 6 \times 40\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.115\ W$$

**Caveat:** at true 100 % duty, most firmware stops PWM, so terms 1 to 4 vanish at full throttle. The worst-case switching loss actually occurs at about 85–95 % duty with high current. The model keeps the switching terms as a conservative stack-up.

### Loss Budget per Channel at 80 A Burst

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 13.44 |
| Copper | 1.90 |
| Switching overlap | 1.94 |
| Dead time | 0.61 |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.17 |
| **Total per channel** | **≈ 18.1 W** |
| **4 channels simultaneous** | **≈ 72 W** |

**Sensitivity:** P_cond ∝ I². At 60 A the total drops to about 11 W per channel. Cutting f_sw to 24 kHz saves about 1.3 W per channel.
