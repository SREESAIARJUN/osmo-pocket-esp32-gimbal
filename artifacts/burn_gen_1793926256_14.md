## 0. Architectural Clarification and Key Assumptions

An STM32H743 is not run as the ESC commutation MCU in practice. ArduPilot runs on it as the flight controller. The architecture assumed here is the standard AIO arrangement:

| Block | Part (representative) | Role |
|---|---|---|
| FC MCU | STM32H743VIH6 (TFBGA100) or VIT6 (LQFP100) | ArduPilot, DShot600 bidir master |
| ESC MCUs | 4 × AT32F421K8U7 (or STM32G071) | AM32 / BLHeli_32 six-step commutation, 48 kHz PWM |
| Gate drivers | 4 × FD6288Q (3-phase half-bridge driver, QFN24) | 3 HS + 3 LS per channel |
| Power FETs | 24 × 40 V N-ch PQFN 3.3×3.3 | Rds(on),25°C ≈ 1.5 mΩ @ Vgs = 10 V **[VERIFY]** |
| Supply | 6S Li-ion: V_bus = 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal | |
| Form factor | 20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm mount, 8-layer, 2 oz inner / 2 oz outer | |

**Reality check, stated up front:** "80 A burst" on a nano-class board is a *transient* rating that depends on thermal capacitance. It is not a steady-state rating. Section 3 derives the actual allowable burst duration.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in six-step commutation with synchronous rectification

In each 60° commutation sector, two phases conduct and the third floats (used for BEMF sensing).

- One leg is PWM-switched with complementary HS/LS drive.
- The other leg holds its LS (or HS) static ON.

Phase current I_ph therefore always flows through exactly **two MOSFET channels in series**, whatever the duty cycle D. During the ON-time it passes through HS(a) and LS(b). During the OFF-time it passes through LS(a), synchronously rectified, and LS(b).

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent Rds(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}\ \text{(PQFN 40 V trench)}\ \textbf{[VERIFY]}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.5 mΩ × 1.5 = **2.25 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Per-channel conduction loss

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j) + I_{ph}^2 R_{Cu,ch}$$

where R_Cu,ch is the PCB copper path (bus to FET to motor pad). On a nano board this is ≈ 0.5 mΩ per channel, for 4 oz-equivalent effective copper with stitched vias **[estimate from layout]**.

At I_ph = 80 A and T_j = 125 °C:

$$P_{FET} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 2.25\text{ m}\Omega = 28.8\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.5\text{ m}\Omega = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

**Per-FET average.** Each FET conducts about one-third of the electrical period, weighted by D. To first order:

$$\bar P_{FET,i} \approx \frac{28.8}{6} = 4.8\ \text{W}$$

**Worst-case hot spot.** At low D with high current, the LS FETs (synchronous rectification plus static leg) carry most of the loss. This can reach about 1.6× the average, i.e. **≈ 7.7 W in a single LS FET**. Use this figure for junction checks.

### 1.4 Electrothermal stability (runaway criterion)

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\cdot R_{th,ja} < 1 \;\Rightarrow\; I^2\cdot R_{DS,25}\,\alpha\cdot R_{th,ja} < 1$$

Per FET: (80²/3) × 1.5 mΩ × 0.005 × R_th = 0.016·R_th. This stays stable for R_th < 62 K/W, so it is **stable but slow-converging**. Iterate T_j ↔ R_DS in the transient model (Section 3).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. That gives one commutating half-bridge per channel per PWM cycle.

| Loss term | Formula | Parameters | Value / channel |
|---|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | 25.2 V, 80 A, t_r + t_f = 20 ns **[VERIFY with driver Rg]** | **0.97 W** |
| Coss charge | Q_oss·V·f_sw | Q_oss ≈ 40 nC | 0.048 W |
| Body diode Qrr | Q_rr·V·f_sw | Q_rr ≈ 50 nC | 0.060 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_F·I·2t_dt·f_sw | V_F = 0.8 V, t_dt = 200 ns | **1.23 W** |
| Gate drive (driver dissipation) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw | Q_g = 40 nC, 10 V | 0.038 W |
| **Total switching** | | | **≈ 2.35 W** |

Design notes:

- **Dead time dominates switching loss.** Cut it to 100 ns where the FD6288 propagation matching allows (AM32 "dead time" parameter). This saves about 0.6 W per channel.
- **48 kHz versus 24 kHz:** switching loss scales linearly. At 24 kHz the total drops to about 1.2 W, but current ripple doubles: ΔI = V_bus·D(1−D)/(L_ph·f_sw). The ripple's RMS adds to conduction loss as I_rms² = I_avg² + ΔI²/12. For L_ph = 20 µH on a typical 2207 motor, ΔI at D = 0.5 and 48 kHz is 6.6 A p-p. The added conduction loss is negligible (+0.05 %).

$$P_{total} = 4\times(28.8 + 3.2 + 2.35) \approx \mathbf{137\ W}$$
