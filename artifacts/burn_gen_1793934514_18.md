## 1. Conduction-Loss Model (6-Step Trapezoidal, Complementary PWM)

### 1.1 Current paths
In any commutation sector, exactly two legs are active:
- **PWM leg:** the high-side (HS) conducts for fraction D. The synchronous low-side (LS) conducts for (1−D), excluding dead time.
- **Return leg:** the low-side conducts for 100% of the sector.

So at any instant, current passes through exactly two R_ds(on) in series:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 \, R_{ds}(T_j)\,[\,D + (1-D)\,] + I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Ripple correction
With peak-to-peak ripple ratio r = ΔI/I:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2\left(1+\frac{r^2}{12}\right)$$

For r = 0.3, the factor is 1.0075, which is negligible. Low-inductance racing motors can reach r ≈ 0.6–0.8; there, the correction is +3–5%.

### 1.3 Temperature dependence
$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha_R(T_j-25)]$$

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A
| Condition | R_ds | P_cond per channel |
|---|---|---|
| Cold (T_j = 25 °C) | 1.4 mΩ | 2·6400·0.0014 = **17.9 W** |
| Hot (T_j = 125 °C) | 2.2 mΩ | 2·6400·0.0022 = **28.2 W** |

### 1.5 Per-FET distribution
Each leg is active for 2/3 of the electrical cycle: 1/3 as the PWM leg and 1/3 as the return leg. Averaged over a full electrical period:

$$P_{HS,avg}=\tfrac{1}{3}I^2R\,D,\qquad P_{LS,avg}=\tfrac{1}{3}I^2R\,[(1-D)+1]$$

At full throttle (D ≈ 1), each LS averages I²R/3 = **4.7 W** (hot). The instantaneous LS dissipation during its return sector is **14.1 W**.

Electrical frequency matters for ripple:
- A 14-pole motor (7 pole pairs) at 30 kRPM gives f_e = 3.5 kHz, so the sector is about 95 µs.
- Die thermal time constants are in the low-ms range, so the junction sees roughly the average plus a small ripple of about +2–4 K.
