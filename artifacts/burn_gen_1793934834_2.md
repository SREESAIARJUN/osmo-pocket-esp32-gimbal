# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: 80 A Burst Thermal, 48 kHz Switching, and ArduPilot BDShot600 Design Package

## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Key Findings

Three points shape everything below.

1. **The H743 does not switch the MOSFETs.** In a 4-in-1 stack, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU (e.g., AT32F421 running AM32, or BLHeli_32 hardware). That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM and drives a 3-phase gate driver. The H743's job is to produce four bidirectional DShot600 streams and decode eRPM telemetry. The power-stage math therefore applies to the ESC board, and the DMA math applies to the FC.

2. **"80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) footprint is a transient rating, not a continuous one.** The model below shows that roughly **85 W total dissipation** occurs when all four channels burst at 80 A. That heats the board adiabatically at about 35–40 K/s. Burst duration must be limited to about 1–2 s, and continuous capability is about **15–20 A per channel**. Any datasheet claiming more on 20×20 mm is using optimistic airflow assumptions.

3. **The component parameters are representative placeholders.** They are typical of 40 V, 3×3 mm, ~1 mΩ MOSFETs and FD6288-class drivers. Substitute your selected parts' datasheet values into the equations before sign-off. The netlist is a reference topology, not a fab-ready release.
