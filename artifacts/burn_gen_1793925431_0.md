# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Timer/DMA Allocation

## 0. Scope, Architecture, and Assumptions

I don't have prior project records for "Job #4", so every parameter below is an explicit, labelled assumption. Replace them with values from your BOM and datasheets, and the equations still hold.

**Architecture assumed:** a 20×20 mm (M3 or M2 mounting) all-in-one stack.

- **Flight controller:** an STM32H743 runs ArduPilot. It sends bidirectional DShot600 to the ESCs.
- **4-in-1 ESC:** four independent ESC MCUs run AM32 or BLHeli_32.
  - Typical MCUs are the AT32F421 or STM32G071.
  - They use complementary PWM at 48 kHz with synchronous rectification.
- **The H743 does not drive the gates.** Driving 24 FETs at 48 kHz in software is not how ArduPilot works. The H743's job is the DShot timers.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

With 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation and synchronous rectification, two phases conduct at any instant.

- **During D·T_sw:** current flows through the phase-A high-side FET, the motor, and the phase-B low-side FET.
- **During (1−D)·T_sw:** current flows through the phase-A low-side FET (synchronous), the motor, and the phase-B low-side FET.

So exactly **two FET channels carry I_pk at all times**, independent of duty cycle (ignoring dead time):

$$
P_{cond}(T_j) = 2\,I_{pk}^2\,R_{DS(on),25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big] + I_{pk}^2 R_{pcb}
$$

For sinusoidal/FOC drive (AM32 does not use it by default), substitute $I_{rms}=I_{pk}/\sqrt2$ per phase, with three phases conducting.

### 1.2 Numerical evaluation (per motor)

| T_j | R_DS(on) | 2·I²·R_DS | PCB | **P_cond** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.50 mΩ | 19.2 W | 3.2 W | **22.4 W** |
| 100 °C | 2.01 mΩ | 25.7 W | 3.2 W | **28.9 W** |
| 125 °C | 2.18 mΩ | 27.8 W | 3.2 W | **31.0 W** |

### 1.3 Electro-thermal feedback

R_DS rises with temperature, so the loss is a function of T_j. The steady state is found by solving the fixed point:

$$
T_j = T_a + R_{th,ja}\,\Big(P_0 + P_1\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]\Big)
\;\Rightarrow\;
T_j = \frac{T_a + R_{th,ja}\,(P_0+P_1(1-25\alpha))}{1-\alpha\,R_{th,ja}\,P_1}
$$

Here P₁ is the temperature-dependent conduction term and P₀ is everything else.

**Runaway criterion:** α·R_th,ja·P₁ ≥ 1. With P₁ ≈ 19 W and α = 0.0045, runaway occurs when R_th,ja ≥ 11.7 K/W. A 20×20 board shares roughly 4–8 K/W between all four channels, so the fixed point exists, but only at temperatures well above the FET rating (see §3).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Each PWM period has one hard-switched node per motor: the high-side FET of the PWM'd phase. The low-side FET switches at near-zero voltage (ZVS) via its body diode.

### 2.1 Loss components

1. **Overlap (V·I crossover):**
$$P_{ov} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{pk}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(22\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz}) = \mathbf{1.06\ W}$$

2. **Reverse recovery** (LS body diode, recovered by HS turn-on):
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$
   - Q_rr scales roughly with di/dt and I. At 80 A, derate pessimistically ×2, giving about 0.1 W.

3. **Output capacitance:**
$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \approx 25\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.03\ W}$$

4. **Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead-time intervals per period):
$$P_{dt} = V_F I_{pk}\,(2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot400\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$
   - This is the largest switching-related term.
   - Reducing t_dt to 100 ns halves it, but only do so if the gate driver's propagation-delay mismatch allows.

5. **Gate drive** (two actively switched FETs, mostly dissipated in the driver and gate resistors):
$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2\cdot40\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.038\ W}$$

### 2.2 Total per motor at 80 A, T_j = 100 °C

| Term | Power |
|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 25.7 W |
| PCB copper | 3.2 W |
| Overlap switching | 1.06 W |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 W |
| Q_rr + Q_oss + gate | 0.17 W |
| **Total per motor** | **≈ 31.4 W** |
| **Efficiency** at 80 A·22 V ≈ 1.76 kW | **≈ 98.2 %** |

**Key observation:** conduction losses are about 82% of the total. Moving from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves only about 1.1 W. Lower R_DS(on) or paralleled FETs are the effective lever.
