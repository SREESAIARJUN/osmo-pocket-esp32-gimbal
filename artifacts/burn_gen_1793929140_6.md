## 1. Field-Oriented Control

### 1.1 Transforms

Electrical angle from the mechanical encoder angle θ_m, with calibrated offset θ_0:

$$\theta_e = p\,\theta_m - \theta_0 \pmod{2\pi}$$

Clarke transform (amplitude-invariant, with i_a + i_b + i_c = 0):

$$i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}(i_a + 2 i_b)$$

Park transform:

$$\begin{bmatrix}i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}$$

The inverse Park transform is the transpose of this matrix.

### 1.2 Electrical Plant and Current PI with Decoupling

In the rotor frame:

$$v_d = R i_d + L\dot i_d - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad v_q = R i_q + L\dot i_q + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e\lambda_m$$

The control law is a PI on each axis plus feedforward of the cross-coupling and back-EMF terms:

$$v_d = \text{PI}_d(i_d^\* - i_d) - \omega_e L i_q$$
$$v_q = \text{PI}_q(i_q^\* - i_q) + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e \lambda_m$$

With decoupling in place, each axis becomes 1/(Ls + R). Choosing the PI zero to cancel the electrical pole gives a first-order closed loop with bandwidth ω_c,i:

$$\boxed{K_{p,i}= L\,\omega_{c,i},\qquad K_{i,i}= R\,\omega_{c,i}}$$
