# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC: System Architecture Package (Job #39)

## 0. Scope, Architecture Correction, and Assumptions

**Architecture correction.** The STM32H743 does not generate the 48 kHz motor PWM. In every production 4-in-1 stack, each ESC channel runs its own commutation MCU (AM32 or BLHeli_32 firmware on an STM32G071, AT32F421, or similar). The split is:

- **H743 (flight controller):** ArduPilot. It sends DShot600 commands and receives bi-directional eRPM telemetry, current/voltage ADC readings, and optionally KISS/BLHeli telemetry over UART.
- **ESC MCUs (×4, or one shared MCU per pair):** 6-step or FOC commutation, 48 kHz complementary PWM, gate drive.

The model below follows that split. No MOSFET part number was specified, so the design uses a parameterized model with a representative 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN MOSFET. **Replace every parameter in the table below with datasheet values before release.**

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At 110 °C: R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0045 × 85) = **1.38 mΩ**

### 1.2 Six-step trapezoidal commutation (AM32/BLHeli default)

In any 60° sector, two half-bridge legs carry the phase current I:

- The **PWM leg** alternates between the high-side FET (fraction D) and the synchronously rectified low-side FET (fraction 1 − D). It conducts I for 100% of the period, through one FET or the other.
- The **static leg** has one low-side FET fully on.

Current therefore flows through two FET R_DS(on) values in series at all times, independent of D. Neglecting dead time:

$$P_{cond,FET} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,Cu} = 2\,I^2\,R_{Cu}$$

At 80 A:

| Term | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| P_cond,FET | 2 × 6400 × 1.38 mΩ | **17.7 W** per channel |
| P_cond,Cu | 2 × 6400 × 0.5 mΩ | **6.4 W** per channel |

### 1.3 Electro-thermal fixed point

Because R_ds depends on T_j, which depends on P, iterate:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_{ref} + R_{\theta}\,P(T_j^{(n)})$$

This converges when

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\,R_\theta = 2I^2R_{ds,25}\,\alpha\,R_\theta < 1$$

**Thermal-runaway criterion.** If 2I²R_ds,25·α·R_θ ≥ 1, there is no stable operating point. At 80 A: 2 × 6400 × 1e-3 × 0.0045 = 0.058 W/K, so runaway occurs if the effective R_θ for that channel exceeds **~17 K/W**. A nano board without airflow can reach this, which is one reason 80A is a burst rating only.

### 1.4 FOC / sinusoidal variant (if the ESC runs FOC)

With three legs conducting sinusoidal current of peak Î:

$$P_{cond} = \tfrac{3}{2}\hat I^2 R_{ds}$$

For the same RMS phase current, losses are about 12% lower than six-step.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In six-step with synchronous rectification, only the PWM leg switches. That gives one hard-switched turn-on and one turn-off per period per channel.

### 2.1 V-I overlap (hard switching, inductive clamp)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period)

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

Dead time is the second-largest loss term. Cutting t_dt from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per channel, but only after scope-verifying that no shoot-through occurs at the real gate resistances.

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = V_{bus}\,Q_{rr}\,f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 50\,\text{nC} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

Treat this as a lower bound. Q_rr rises with di/dt and temperature, so use the datasheet curve at the actual di/dt.

### 2.4 Output-capacitance energy

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,er} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} \times 2 = 1.5\,\text{nF} \times 635 \times 48\,\text{kHz} = 0.05\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

This term is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET die. Two FETs switch per period.

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g\,V_{GS}\,f_{sw} = 2 \times 55\,\text{nC} \times 10\,\text{V} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = 0.05\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Switching total and the effect of f_sw

$$P_{sw,total} \approx 3.3\ \text{W per channel at 80 A}$$

Every switching term scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz this drops to ~1.65 W. At 96 kHz it rises to ~6.6 W, which approaches 40% of conduction loss. **48 kHz is a reasonable compromise** between motor current ripple (and the resulting audible and iron losses) and FET heating.
