# Job #58: STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC Design Package

**Scope:** 80A burst thermal model, 48 kHz switching losses, ArduPilot bi-directional DShot600 timer/DMA plan, and reference netlists.

## 0. Architecture Corrections Before Design

Three points shape everything that follows.

1. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In a 4-in-1 stack, the H743 sends DShot600 to the ESC board. Each motor channel (or a single quad-capable MCU) runs AM32 or BLHeli_32 firmware on its own MCU, such as an STM32G071 or AT32F421. That ESC MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The netlists below reflect this split.
2. **80A on a 20×20 mm "nano" stack is a burst rating, not continuous.** The model below shows roughly 110–130 W of dissipation at 80A across four motors. A 20×20 board cannot sustain that. The physics supports a burst of about 1 s at most. Realistic continuous capacity is about 35–45A per motor with good airflow.
3. **Device parameters are assumptions.** No specific MOSFET was given, so I used a representative 40V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN part. Replace the values in §1 with your selected part's datasheet values. All equations remain valid.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current Path

In 6-step commutation, two phases are always active. With synchronous rectification:

- **Switching leg:** current flows through the high-side FET for duty D, then the low-side FET for (1−D).
- **Static leg:** one FET (low-side) conducts for the full 60° sector.

At any instant, exactly two FET channels carry I_ph. So instantaneous conduction loss per motor is independent of D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \cdot I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds(on),25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)] \approx 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.45 = 2.175\,\text{m}\Omega$$

### 2.2 Numbers

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.175\times10^{-3} = 27.8\ \text{W}$$

Add PCB copper and solder-pad resistance. Assume about 0.4 mΩ per current path:

$$P_{Cu,motor} = 80^2 \times 0.4\times10^{-3} \approx 2.6\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Per-FET Average

Each FET carries current for 1/3 of the electrical cycle (120°). The switching FET pair shares by D and (1−D).

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} \approx \frac{2 \times 27.8}{6} \approx 9.3\ \text{W (worst case, D≈1 for HS)}$$

Use **≈ 9–14 W peak per FET** for junction calculations. Peak occurs when one FET holds the static leg.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the switching leg's FETs see hard transitions.

### 3.1 Hard-Switching (V–I Overlap)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ns} \times 48\text{kHz} = 0.97\ \text{W}$$

### 3.2 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$P_{dt} = 2 \cdot V_f \cdot I_{ph} \cdot t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 2 \times 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{kHz} = 1.23\ \text{W}$$

### 3.3 Reverse Recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{kHz} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 3.4 Gate Drive (Dissipated in Driver and Gate Resistors)

$$P_g = 2 \cdot Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \times 30\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{kHz} = 0.029\ \text{W}$$

### 3.5 Output Capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2}C_{oss}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw}$$

With C_oss ≈ 1.5 nF, this is ≈ 0.023 W.

### 3.6 Summary per Motor at 80A Burst

| Loss Term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs, hot) | 27.8 |
| Copper / pads | 2.6 |
| Switching overlap | 0.97 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.10 |
| **Total per motor** | **≈ 32.7 W** |
| **Total, 4 motors** | **≈ 131 W** |

**Insights from the breakdown:**

- At 80A, **conduction dominates (~93%)**.
- Moving from 24 kHz to 48 kHz adds only ~1.1 W per motor. 48 kHz is justified for smoother low-throttle behavior and lower acoustic noise.
- The best lever is **dead time**. Cutting it from 200 to 100 ns saves 0.6 W per motor. Verify against shoot-through margin with the gate driver's propagation skew.

### 3.7 Efficiency Check

Electrical power per motor is about V_bus × I_ph × D. At D ≈ 0.9, that is ≈ 1.8 kW. Bridge efficiency is therefore ≈ 98.2%. Efficiency is fine; **heat density is the problem**.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Network (Cauer Approximation)

```
P_FET ──[R_θJC 1.5 K/W]──┬──[R_θCB 0.5 K/W]──┬──[R_θBA ≈ 6–10 K/W total board]── T_amb
                         C_j (~0.02 J/K)      C_board (whole stack ≈ 2.5 J/K)
```

The board heat capacity estimate covers a 6-layer, 2 oz 20×20 board, 24 FETs, and capacitors. That is about 4 g at c_eff ≈ 0.6 J/(g·K), giving C ≈ 2.5 J/K.

### 4.2 Steady State (Shows 80A Continuous Is Impossible)

$$\Delta T_{BA} = P_{tot} \cdot R_{\theta BA} = 131 \times 6 = 786\ \text{K}$$

This is not survivable, so continuous 80A is ruled out.

**Continuous rating.** Set board ΔT = 60 K with prop-wash airflow (R_θBA ≈ 6 K/W). That allows P_tot ≈ 10 W, or 2.5 W per motor. Because loss scales with I²:

$$I_{cont} \approx 80\sqrt{2.5/32.7} \approx 22\ \text{A per motor, with no heatsink}$$

With an aluminum top-plate heatsink and strong airflow (R_θBA ≈ 2 K/W), the continuous rating rises to about **38–42A**.

### 4.3 Burst (Transient) Model

During short bursts, the board's thermal mass absorbs the heat:

$$\Delta T_B(t) = P_{tot} R_{\theta BA}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right), \quad \tau = R_{\theta BA} C_{board} = 6 \times 2.5 = 15\ \text{s}$$

For t ≪ τ, this simplifies to ΔT_B ≈ P·t / C:

| Burst Duration | ΔT_board | T_board (from 40 °C) | T_j peak (+ΔT_jc ≈ 14 W × 2.0 K/W ≈ 28 K) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 s | 26 K | 66 °C | ~94 °C ✅ |
| 1.0 s | 52 K | 92 °C | ~120 °C ✅ (margin to 150 °C) |
| 2.0 s | 100 K | 140 °C | ~168 °C ❌ |

**Specification:**

- **80A burst ≤ 1 s**, at a ≤ 10% duty cycle, starting from board temperature ≤ 50 °C.
- Enforce this in firmware with an I²t limiter on the ESC MCU.
- Also enforce it on the FC via `MOT_BAT_CURR_MAX`, plus ESC telemetry temperature through EDT (Extended DShot Telemetry).

### 4.4 Per-Junction Check (Static-Leg FET, Hot Spot)

$$T_j = T_B + P_{FET}(R_{\theta JC} + R_{\theta CB}) = 92 + 13.9 \times 2.0 \approx 120\ \text{°C} \quad (T_{j,max}=150\ \text{°C} \Rightarrow 30\ \text{K margin})$$
