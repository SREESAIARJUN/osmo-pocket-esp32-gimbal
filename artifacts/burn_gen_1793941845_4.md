> **Architect's flag:** 80 A per motor on a nano-format 4-in-1 is feasible only as a **sub-2-second transient**. The model below shows the board is thermal-capacity-limited, not junction-limited. Marketing ratings must state "burst (≤1–2 s)", and firmware current limits must be part of the design, not an afterthought.

**Architectural note:** In a 4-in-1 design the H743 does not commutate the motors. Each ESC (or a shared ESC MCU) runs its own firmware, e.g. AM32 on AT32F421 or BLHeli_32. The H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bi-directional DShot600. Sections 1–4 cover the ESC power stage. Section 5 covers the H743 timer/DMA plan.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths
With 6-step trapezoidal commutation and synchronous rectification, two phases conduct at any instant:
- **Static leg:** the low-side FET is on continuously for that 60° sector.
- **PWM leg:** the high-side conducts for duty D and the low-side (sync rect) for 1−D.

Instantaneous conduction is therefore always through exactly **two FETs in series**.

### 1.2 RMS current including ripple
Ripple is worst at D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L\,f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{30\,\mu\text{H} \times 48\,\text{kHz}} = 4.38\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{rms} = \sqrt{I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}} = \sqrt{6400 + 1.6} = 80.01\ \text{A}$$

Ripple is negligible for conduction at 48 kHz. That is one benefit of the high f_sw.

### 1.3 Conduction loss per ESC channel

$$R_{DS,hot} = k_T \cdot R_{25} = 1.55 \times 1.3 = 2.0\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond,FET} = 2 \cdot I_{rms}^2 \cdot R_{DS,hot} = 2 \times 6400 \times 2.0\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{25.6\ W}$$

Per-FET distribution (worst-case device):

$$P_{static\,LS} = I^2 R = 12.8\ \text{W}, \qquad P_{HS} = D \cdot 12.8, \quad P_{LS,sync} = (1-D)\cdot 12.8$$

### 1.4 Copper and interconnect loss
Assume 0.5 mΩ per channel path (pads, vias, phase pad, solder):

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 3.2\ \text{W per channel}$$

> **Thermal-runaway coupling:** R_DS(on) rises with T_j, so loss rises with temperature. Iterate the model: P(T_j) → T_j(P) until it converges. Section 4 does this.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Its hard-switched FET sees the full V·I overlap.

| Mechanism | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V·I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = 0.5·25.2·80·30 ns·48 k | **1.45 W** |
| C_oss charge | Q_oss·V·f_sw = 40 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.048 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 30 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.036 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_F·I·2·t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·2·100 ns·48 k | **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs, dissipated in driver/R_g) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw | 0.038 W |
| **Total switching per channel** | | **≈ 2.18 W** |

### 2.1 Sensitivity

$$\frac{\partial P_{sw}}{\partial f_{sw}} \approx 45\ \text{mW/kHz} \;\;(\text{at } 80\,\text{A},\ 25.2\,\text{V})$$

- **48 kHz vs 24 kHz:** costs about 1.1 W per channel. It buys halved ripple and quieter, smoother low-throttle control.
- **Dead time:** this is the second-largest switching term. Trimming t_dt from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel. Only do it if gate-driver propagation-delay matching allows.
