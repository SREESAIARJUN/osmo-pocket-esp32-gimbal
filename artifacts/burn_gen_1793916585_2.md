# Job #41: STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (80 A Burst) Design Package

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

**Architecture correction.** The STM32H743 does not drive the power stage. It runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. This design uses 4× AT32F421K8U7 (QFN-32), the common AM32 target. The H743 sends DShot600 to the ESC MCUs and receives bidirectional eRPM telemetry from them.

**Feasibility flag.** 80 A per channel on a "nano" board (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) is only achievable as a short burst. It will not work as continuous current. Section 2.4 quantifies the burst duration. Treat 80 A as a **≤ 2–3 s transient rating**, not a continuous one.

**Design parameters (stated so you can substitute your real datasheet values):**

| Symbol | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Paths in 6-Step Commutation

In each 60° electrical step, exactly two phases conduct current I:
- **PWM'd high side (HS) phase:** HS conducts for fraction D. Its complementary low side (LS) conducts the same current I for fraction (1 − D), minus dead time.
- **Return phase:** LS is held fully on for the whole step.

Over one electrical revolution, each phase is PWM'd for 1/3 of the time, is the return leg for 1/3, and floats for 1/3.

### 1.2 Per-Device RMS Currents

$$I_{HS,rms}^2 = \frac{D}{3} I^2 \qquad I_{LS,rms}^2 = \left[\frac{(1-D)}{3} + \frac{1}{3}\right] I^2$$

### 1.3 Total Channel Conduction Loss

Sum over 3 phases. At every instant, exactly two devices carry current I:

$$\boxed{P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + I^2 R_{Cu}}$$

The result is **independent of D**, because complementary PWM always keeps one switch on in the PWM'd leg.

Temperature dependence, linearised:

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS(on) | 2·I²·R |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W |
| 100 °C | 1.41 mΩ | 18.1 W |
| 125 °C | 1.55 mΩ | 19.8 W |

**Copper loss.** Assume 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner copper. Phase pad trace to bus ≈ 0.25 mΩ effective per path → 80² × 0.25 mΩ ≈ **1.6 W per channel**. This is non-trivial; use 3–4 oz copper if possible.

**Conduction loss per channel at T_j = 125 °C: ≈ 21.4 W**

### 1.5 Electrothermal Fixed Point

Conduction loss and junction temperature depend on each other, so solve:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + P(T_j)\,Z_{th}$$

Stability requires the loop gain to be below 1:

$$2 I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\,Z_{th} < 1$$

At 80 A: $12.8\ \text{W} \times 0.0055 \times Z_{th} < 1 \Rightarrow Z_{th} < 14\ \text{K/W}$.

This is satisfied for burst durations but marginal for a free-air nano board at steady state. That is another reason 80 A cannot be continuous.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Hard switching occurs only in the PWM'd HS device. The LS device in the same leg switches with near-zero voltage after the dead-time diode conduction.

### 2.1 Loss Components (Per Channel, at Any Instant One Leg Is Switching)

**(a) V-I overlap, HS device:**
$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

A more rigorous version uses the Miller plateau: $t_{sw} \approx Q_{gd} R_{g,tot}/(V_{drv} - V_{plateau})$. With Q_gd = 9 nC, R_g,tot ≈ 3.2 Ω, and (10 − 4.5) V, t_sw ≈ 5.2 ns plus the current-rise interval. The 15 ns figure is a conservative layout-inclusive value.

**(b) Body-diode reverse recovery** (LS diode recovers when HS turns on):
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

**(c) Output capacitance:**
$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \times 2\ \text{(HS+LS)} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

**(d) Dead-time diode conduction** (two edges per period):
$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2 t_d)\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

This is the largest switching-related term after V-I overlap. Reducing dead time to 100 ns halves it, but is only safe if the driver propagation-delay mismatch is under 30 ns.

**(e) Gate drive** (dissipated mostly in driver and R_g, 2 FETs toggling):
$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 45\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.043\ W}$$

### 2.2 Switching Loss Summary per Channel (80 A, 25.2 V, 48 kHz)

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| V-I overlap | 1.45 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 |
| Q_rr | 0.05 |
| C_oss | 0.05 |
| Gate | 0.04 |
| **Total** | **≈ 2.8 W** |

### 2.3 Total Burst Dissipation

$$P_{ch} = 21.4 + 2.8 = \mathbf{24.2\ W/channel} \qquad P_{board} = \mathbf{\approx 97\ W}\ \text{(all 4 at 80 A)}$$

**Bus capacitor ripple** (per channel, worst at D = 0.5):

$$I_{C,rms} = I\sqrt{D(1-D)} = 40\ \text{A}$$

This rules out relying on onboard MLCCs alone. Specify:
- a **low-ESR bulk electrolytic** (≥ 1000 µF / 35 V, external on pigtail), plus
- **≥ 8× 10 µF / 50 V X7R 1206** per channel, placed at the HS drain–LS source loop.

### 2.4 Thermal Network (Burst Capability)

Lumped Cauer model per channel: junction → case (R_θJC) → PCB copper spreader (C_pcb, R_spread) → ambient (R_conv).

**Hottest device: return-leg LS**, which carries I for its full 1/3 interval. During its active step it dissipates I²R = 80² × 1.55 mΩ ≈ 9.9 W. Averaged over the electrical cycle, it shares duty. Peak per-device average ≈ 6.6 W (LS: on 2/3 of the time worst case at low D).

**Junction-to-case rise:**
$$\Delta T_{jc} = 6.6 \times 1.2 \approx 8\ \text{K}$$

The die is fine. **The board is the bottleneck.**

**Board thermal mass (25.5×25.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz):**
- Copper: ≈ 4.5 g × 0.385 J/gK ≈ 1.7 J/K
- FR4: ≈ 3 g × 1.1 J/gK ≈ 3.3 J/K
- FET packages and solder: ≈ 0.5 J/K
- **Total C_th ≈ 5.5 J/K** (whole ESC section)

**Adiabatic rise rate** (convection is negligible within seconds; R_conv ≈ 15 K/W in 10 m/s prop wash):
$$\frac{dT}{dt} \approx \frac{97}{5.5} \approx 17.6\ \text{K/s}$$
