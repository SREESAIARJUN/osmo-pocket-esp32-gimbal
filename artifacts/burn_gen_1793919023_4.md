## 1. Thermal and Loss Model (per motor channel, 6 FETs)

### 1.1 Conduction loss

With 6-step commutation and synchronous rectification, exactly **two FETs carry the phase current at any instant**: one high-side, one low-side. The PWM duty cycle only moves conduction between the HS FET and its complementary LS FET, so the total conduction loss is independent of D.

Temperature-dependent on-resistance:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 100 °C: R_ds = 0.9 × 1.375 = **1.24 mΩ**.

RMS current including PWM ripple:

$$\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L\,f_{sw}} \;\xrightarrow{D=0.5}\; \frac{25.2\times0.25}{20\,\mu\text{H}\times48\,\text{kHz}} = 6.56\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.6 \approx 6404\ \text{A}^2$$

Ripple is negligible at 80 A.

Conduction loss per motor:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\times6404\times1.24\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{15.9\ W}$$

Copper path loss: assume R_Cu ≈ 0.4 mΩ per phase leg (pours plus vias), with two legs active.

$$P_{Cu} = 2\times 6400 \times 0.4\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{5.1\ W}$$

This heat goes into the board, not the junctions, but it still loads the board's thermal mass.

### 1.2 Switching losses at 48 kHz (hard-switched leg)

**Turn-on/turn-off overlap.** The transition time is set by the Miller and post-threshold charge:

$$t_{r}\approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}+Q_{gs2}}{I_g} = \frac{15\,\text{nC}}{1\,\text{A}} = 15\ \text{ns}$$

$$P_{ov} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\times25.2\times80\times30\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery** (LS body diode recovered by HS turn-on):

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

The Q_rr value can be 2–3× worse at 80 A and high di/dt. A ×3 margin is budgeted in the total.

**C_oss energy:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss(er)} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5\times1.5\text{n}\times635\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.02\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction:**

$$P_{dt} = V_f\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times400\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

Reducing t_dt to 100 ns halves this term. Verify the shoot-through margin with the driver's propagation-delay mismatch first.

**Gate drive:**

$$P_g = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k} = 24\ \text{mW per switching FET}$$

This is dissipated in R_g and the driver, not the FET die.

**Electrical efficiency at burst.** Output power is roughly 25.2 V × 80 A × 4 ≈ 8.06 kW, so η ≈ 98.8%. The loss is small relative to output, but it is concentrated in about 9 cm².

**Hottest FET, worst case.** The HS FET in the PWM leg carries conduction plus switching losses:

$$P_{HS} \approx 7.95 + 1.45 + 0.18 + 0.02 = 9.6\ \text{W} \;\Rightarrow\; \Delta T_{jc} = 9.6 \times 1.0 = 9.6\ \text{K}$$

### 1.4 Transient thermal model of the board

- Copper: 9.3 cm² × 8 layers × 70 µm = 0.52 cm³ → 4.7 g × 0.385 J/gK = **1.8 J/K**
- FR-4: 9.3 cm² × 0.16 cm = 1.49 cm³ → 2.8 g × 1.1 J/gK = **3.1 J/K**
- 24 FETs plus capacitors: ≈ **0.6 J/K**
