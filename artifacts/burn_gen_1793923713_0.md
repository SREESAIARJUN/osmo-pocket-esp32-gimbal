# STM32H743 AIO Nano: 4-in-1 ESC Power Stage, Losses, Thermal Model, and Bidirectional DShot600 DMA Allocation

## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

**The H743 should not drive the power stage directly.** ArduPilot's bidirectional DShot implies a separate ESC MCU per motor. The H743 has only two advanced-control timers (TIM1/TIM8) plus HRTIM. That is insufficient for four 6-step or FOC bridges with BEMF sensing alongside the flight stack. The architecture is therefore:

```
[STM32H743 FC, ArduPilot] --4× bidir DShot600--> [4× ESC MCU, AM32 on STM32G071 / AT32F421]
                                                     └─> 4× 3-phase gate driver (FD6288Q class)
                                                          └─> 24× N-MOSFET (6 per channel)
```

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_ds = 1.2 × (1 + 0.0045 × 85) = **1.659 mΩ**.

### 1.2 6-step conduction with synchronous (complementary) PWM

At any instant, two phases carry current I:
- The PWM-ing high-side FET conducts for a fraction D of each period.
- Its complementary low-side FET conducts for the remaining fraction (1 − D).
- The low-side FET of the return phase conducts 100% of the time.

$$P_{cond,ch}=I^2R_{ds}\,[D+(1-D)+1]=2I^2R_{ds}$$

The result is **independent of duty cycle**, which is a useful design invariant.

$$P_{cond,ch}=2(80)^2(1.659\times10^{-3})=\mathbf{21.2\ W\ per\ channel}$$

**Peak dissipation in the hottest FET** (the return-phase low-side FET, conducting continuously during its 120° sector):

$$P_{FET,pk}=I^2R_{ds}=10.6\ \text{W}$$

Commutation rotates this load across all 6 FETs, but the electrical period is milliseconds. Each FET's thermal time constant is also milliseconds, so design to the **sector peak (10.6 W)**, not the averaged value (~3.5 W).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f | ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 16 ns × 48 k = **0.774 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f | 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.048 W** |
| C_oss | ½·Q_oss·V·f | **0.018 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2t_dt·f | 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k = **0.614 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_drv·f | **0.048 W** (mostly dissipated in the driver) |
| **Total switching-related** | | **≈ 1.50 W per channel** |

Two design notes:
- **Dead-time loss is about 80% as large as the overlap loss.** Trimming dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel. Verify the shoot-through margin on a scope across temperature before committing.
- **Switching loss scales linearly with frequency.** Moving from 48 kHz to 24 kHz halves this ~1.5 W. Conduction loss is unchanged.

### Per-channel and board totals at 80 A burst

$$P_{ch}\approx21.2+1.5=\mathbf{22.7\ W},\qquad P_{board}(4\times80\,A)\approx\mathbf{91\ W}$$

The four-motor case implies 320 A of battery current. An XT60 connector, the battery leads, and a nano-size bus capacitor bank cannot support that. Treat "80 A" as a **per-channel, short-burst** rating, not a simultaneous one.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Steady-state junction rise above the board

$$T_j=T_{board}+P_{FET,pk}(R_{\theta jc}+R_{spread})=T_{board}+10.6\times5=T_{board}+53\ \text{K}$$

R_ds depends on T_j, so iterate:

| Iteration | Assumed T_j | R_ds | Computed T_j (T_board = 60 °C) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 110 °C | 1.659 mΩ | 113 °C |
| 2 | 113 °C | 1.675 mΩ | 113.6 °C (converged) |

### 3.2 Board heat capacity (adiabatic burst)

Assume a 6 g board with heavy copper, giving C ≈ 4.2 J/K.

$$\frac{dT_{board}}{dt}=\frac{91}{4.2}\approx21.7\ \text{K/s}\ \ (\text{all 4 at 80 A})$$

Design limit: T_j ≤ 125 °C, so T_board ≤ 125 − 53 = 72 °C. Starting from 40 °C, that leaves 32 K of headroom.
