## 0. Architectural Clarification and Assumptions

### 0.1 System partition

The STM32H743 is the **flight controller MCU**. It runs ArduPilot and generates DShot. It does **not** commutate the motors. Each ESC channel uses a dedicated low-cost ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. This is the standard partition.

```
[6S LiPo] ──► [Bus + bulk caps] ──► 4 × [3-phase bridge, 6 MOSFETs] ──► M1..M4
                     │                      ▲ gate drive
                     │               4 × [FD6288Q-class 3-ph driver]
                     │                      ▲ 6× PWM
                     │               4 × [AT32F421 / STM32G071 ESC MCU, AM32]
                     │                      ▲ bidirectional DShot600 (1 wire each)
                     └─► [BEC 5V/10V] ──► [STM32H743VIT6, ArduPilot]
```

### 0.3 Physical reality check (read before continuing)

"80 A burst" on a nano-format board is only physically meaningful as a **per-motor transient of about 1–2 s**. The analysis below shows why. A 4 × 80 A simultaneous load (320 A battery-side at full duty) exceeds what a 20×20 or 30×30 board, its XT60/pads, and its bus copper can carry except momentarily.

If the requirement means **80 A total** (20 A per motor), the losses scale by roughly (1/4)² in conduction. The board then becomes comfortably viable. Clarify this with the customer.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

During any 60° commutation sector, two phases carry the full current I. Phase A high-side (HS) is PWM'd and phase B low-side (LS) is held ON.

- **PWM-on interval (duty D):** current path is HS_A → motor → LS_B. Two FETs are in series.
- **PWM-off interval (1−D):** current freewheels through LS_A (synchronous) → motor → LS_B. Two FETs are in series.

So **at every instant, two FETs carry I** (excluding dead time).

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1} \Rightarrow k_T(125^\circ\text{C}) = 1.5$$

For trapezoidal drive with low ripple, I_rms ≈ I_pk = 80 A.

### 1.2 Per-FET distribution

Over an electrical cycle, each bridge position conducts 1/3 of the time (120° of 360°). Within that window, the HS/LS split depends on D. The total is shared across 6 FETs:

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} = \frac{P_{cond,motor}}{6}$$

At low duty, the PWM'd leg's LS carries more of the freewheel share. The worst-case FET is the LS of the freewheeling leg at low D and high current. This corresponds to the "punch-out" from hover with a stalled or high-load prop. Design for 1.3 × average.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| Condition | R_DS(on) | P_cond,motor | Per FET (avg) | Worst FET (×1.3) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cold, T_j = 25 °C | 1.2 mΩ | 6400 × 2.4 m = **15.4 W** | 2.56 W | 3.3 W |
| Hot, T_j = 125 °C | 1.8 mΩ | 6400 × 3.6 m = **23.0 W** | 3.84 W | 5.0 W |

### 1.4 Parasitic copper (do not omit)

Assume a phase-path PCB + solder + pad resistance of 0.4 mΩ (two phase legs in series = 0.8 mΩ):

$$P_{Cu,motor} = 80^2 \times 0.8\,\text{m}\Omega = 5.1\ \text{W}$$

The bus copper carries the battery current, I_batt ≈ D · ΣI_motor. At D = 0.9 with 4 × 80 A, I_batt = 288 A. With 0.15 mΩ of bus copper:

$$P_{Cu,bus} = 288^2 \times 0.15\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 12.4\ \text{W}$$

This term is frequently what actually delaminates nano boards.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd high-side hard-switches. The LS turns on after its body diode has already been conducting, so it switches at near-zero V_DS.

### 2.1 Hard-switching (V–I overlap) loss

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

$$= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance loss

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

The LS body diode recovers when the HS turns on:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$
