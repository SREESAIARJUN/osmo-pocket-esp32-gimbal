# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #46): System Architecture

This document covers three areas:
- **Part A:** thermal model of 80 A burst conduction and 48 kHz switching losses
- **Part B:** ArduPilot bi-directional DShot600 timer and DMA allocation
- **Part C:** reference netlists and validation

There is a physical conflict in the brief, and it changes the recommendation:

> **A 20×20 mm "nano" board cannot sustain 80 A burst per motor.** The thermal model below shows the board heating at about 70 K/s with all four motors at 80 A, so it would exceed limits in under 2 s. I give the complete model so the numbers are traceable. My recommendation is either **80 A total, about 20 A per motor, on 20×20** or **80 A per motor burst on 30.5×30.5 with a heat spreader**. I calculate both cases.

**Architecture assumption:** The H743 runs ArduPilot and does not drive the MOSFETs directly. Each motor has its own ESC MCU: an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 at 48 kHz PWM, with a 3-phase gate driver and 6 MOSFETs. The H743 talks to these ESC MCUs over bi-directional DShot600. This is the only practical partition, because the H743 runs ArduPilot's scheduler and cannot also run sensorless commutation for four motors.

### A.2 Conduction Loss

The drive uses 6-step synchronous rectification with complementary PWM. In any commutation sector, the current path goes through exactly one high-side and one low-side device. The PWM phase alternates between its HS and LS switch, but one of the two always conducts. So:

$$
P_{cond} = 2\, I_{ph,rms}^2 \, R_{ds(on)}(T_j)
$$

**Phase current.** With battery current I_bat and duty D, the phase current is I_ph = I_bat / D. At burst (full throttle, D ≈ 0.95), I_ph ≈ 84 A. I use 80 A RMS as the nominal case. At partial throttle the phase current exceeds battery current, which is why ESCs often fail at mid-throttle under heavy prop load.

**Temperature correction.** R_ds(on) rises with junction temperature:

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0053\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R = 1.2 mΩ × 1.45 = **1.74 mΩ**.

**Device conduction loss:**

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.74\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{22.3\ W\ per\ motor}
$$

That is 11.1 W in each of the two conducting FETs.

**Copper conduction loss.** Each motor's current path (battery pad → bus → FET → phase pad → return) is about 0.5 mΩ on a 2 oz, 8-layer board:

$$
P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}
$$

Note that R_ds(on) depends on temperature, which creates a positive-feedback loop. Solve P_cond(T_j) and T_j(P_total) simultaneously by iterating; Section A.5 does this.

### A.3 Switching Loss at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing high-side FET hard-switches. The complementary low-side FET switches at roughly zero voltage after its body diode conducts.

**Gate current during the Miller plateau:**

$$
I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}} = \frac{7}{4.3} = 1.63\ A
\qquad
I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{4.3} = 0.70\ A
$$

**Transition times:**

$$
t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = \frac{20\,\text{nC}}{1.63} = 12.3\ \text{ns}
\qquad
t_f \approx \frac{20\,\text{nC}}{0.70} = 28.6\ \text{ns}
$$
