## 0. Architecture and Scope

The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFET bridges directly. On every viable AIO design, it runs **ArduPilot** and sends **bi-directional DShot600** to four dedicated ESC MCUs. Each ESC MCU generates the **48 kHz** complementary PWM and handles commutation.

| Block | Selection (reference) |
|---|---|
| FC MCU | STM32H743VIH6 (TFBGA100, 480 MHz) |
| ESC MCU ×4 | AT32F421K8U7 running AM32 (or STM32G071, EFM8 for BLHeli_S) |
| Gate driver ×4 | FD6288Q (3-phase, bootstrap, 250 mA / 500 mA source/sink) |
| MOSFETs ×24 | 40 V, R<sub>DS(on)</sub> 0.7 mΩ typical at 25 °C, DFN 3.3×3.3 class |
| Bus | 6S LiPo, V<sub>bus,max</sub> = 25.2 V |
| Form factor | 20×20 mm (M2 mounting), 4-layer stack (6-layer recommended) |

> ⚠ **Feasibility flag:** An 80 A *per-channel* burst on a 20×20 board is at the edge of physics. The model below quantifies this. If "80 A" means **total** battery current (≈20 A/channel), Section 3.5 shows that case is comfortably feasible.

All device parameters are representative. Replace them with your chosen MOSFET's datasheet values. The equations are general.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R<sub>DS(on)</sub>

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T<sub>j</sub> = 125 °C: R<sub>DS</sub> = 0.7 × 1.45 ≈ **1.02 mΩ**. Use 1.05 mΩ to include package and bond resistance.

### 1.2 Six-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification

At any instant, two legs carry the phase current I:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for D·T<sub>sw</sub> and the complementary low-side FET for (1−D)·T<sub>sw</sub>. Exactly one FET is always in the path.
- **Static leg:** the low-side FET is fully on.

Per channel, this gives:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Including inductor ripple ΔI (peak-to-peak), the RMS current is:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12},\qquad \Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}-E)\,D}{2L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

Example: L<sub>ph</sub> = 15 µH, V<sub>bus</sub> − E = 6 V, D = 0.8 gives ΔI ≈ 3.3 A, which adds only 0.9 A² and is negligible at 80 A.

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2(80^2)(1.05\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{13.4\ W}$$

### 1.3 Copper, shunt, and connector losses per channel

| Element | R | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase pad and pour (2 oz, ~0.15 mΩ ×2 legs) | 0.30 mΩ | 1.92 W |
| Current shunt (shared, 0.25 mΩ, sees I·D_avg) | 0.25 mΩ | ~1.1 W (at D ≈ 0.7) |
| **Subtotal** | | **≈3.0 W** |

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f<sub>sw</sub> = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg's complementary pair switches. The other legs switch only at commutation (≤ a few kHz), which is negligible.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard-switched high-side)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

With an FD6288Q and R<sub>g,ext</sub> = 4.7 Ω, Q<sub>gd</sub> ≈ 8 nC, the switching edges are t<sub>r</sub> ≈ t<sub>f</sub> ≈ 15 ns:

$$P_{VI} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(30\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction (low-side FET)

$$P_{DT} = V_F\,I\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(2\times100\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

The AM32 dead time is configurable. Reducing it from 100 ns to 50 ns halves this loss, but you must verify there is no shoot-through at worst-case driver skew.

### 2.3 Reverse recovery and output capacitance

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\,\text{nC}(25.2)(48\,\text{k}) = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\,\text{nC}(25.2)(48\,\text{k}) = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET die:

$$P_G = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2(60\,\text{nC})(10\,\text{V})(48\,\text{k}) = 0.058\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Switching total per channel

$$P_{sw,ch} = 1.45 + 0.61 + 0.05 + 0.06 = \mathbf{2.17\ W}$$

### 2.6 Total loss budget at 80 A burst

| | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 13.4 W | 53.6 W |
| FET switching | 2.17 W | 8.7 W |
| Copper and shunt | 3.0 W | 12.0 W |
| Gate drive | 0.06 W | 0.23 W |
| Bulk capacitor ESR (I<sub>rip,rms</sub> ≈ 0.5·I<sub>total</sub>, ESR 2 mΩ eff.) | — | ~2.0 W |
| **Total** | **≈18.6 W** | **≈76 W** |

Electrical efficiency at full burst: P<sub>out</sub> ≈ 4 × 80 × 25.2 × 0.8 ≈ 6.45 kW, so η ≈ 98.8%. The efficiency is fine; the heat *density* is the problem.

**Per-FET average loss.** The commutation period (≈10–100 µs at high eRPM) is far shorter than the die thermal time constant, so losses average evenly across the 6 FETs:

$$P_{FET} \approx \frac{13.4 + 2.17}{6} \approx \mathbf{2.6\ W}$$

The high-side FETs carry the switching component, so they run hotter, at about 2.6 + 0.24 ≈ 2.85 W.
