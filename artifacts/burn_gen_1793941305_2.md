# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #58): Architecture, Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and ArduPilot BDShot Allocation

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

Three points need settling before the math.

1. **The H743 cannot commutate the motors itself.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. Each of the four motor channels needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 talks to those MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. The gate drivers and MOSFETs belong to the ESC MCUs, not the H743.
2. **"80A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is thermally marginal at best.** Section 3 shows that 4× 80A simultaneously is survivable only for about 1–3 seconds. Treat 80A as a per-motor transient rating and design to a lower continuous rating.
3. **Placeholder parameters.** All MOSFET values below are representative of a 40V, 5×6 mm (or 3.3×3.3 mm) trench FET. Replace them with your selected part's datasheet values. The model is structured so you can substitute directly.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Trapezoidal 6-step with complementary PWM

In each commutation sector, two phases conduct:

- **Switching leg:** the HS FET conducts for duty D, and the synchronous LS FET conducts for 1−D.
- **Static leg:** the LS FET is on 100% of the sector.

The current path always passes through exactly two FET channels, so:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 \left[ R_{ds}(T_j)\,D + R_{ds}(T_j)(1-D) \right] + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) + I^2 R_{cu}$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 \left( 2R_{ds}(T_j) + R_{cu} \right)$$

The D terms cancel. Conduction loss is independent of throttle when synchronous rectification is used.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0059\ \text{/K} \Rightarrow k_T(110^\circ C) \approx 1.5$$

$$R_{ds}(110^\circ C) = 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 At 80A

$$P_{cond,motor} = 80^2\,(2 \times 1.5 + 0.25)\times10^{-3} = 6400 \times 3.25\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{20.8\ W}$$

The FET-only portion is 19.2 W. Copper accounts for 1.6 W, which heats the PCB directly.

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

Each FET is active for 1/3 of the electrical cycle, either as the static LS or as part of the switching pair.

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} \approx \frac{19.2}{6} = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

The instantaneous worst case, a static LS FET during its sector, is 9.6 W. The ESC electrical frequency (for example, 14 pole-pairs × 400 rev/s ≈ 5.6 kHz) is far faster than the die thermal time constant (~ms). Use the average value for thermal purposes.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Switching losses occur only in the switching leg: one HS/LS pair per motor.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss (hard-switched HS FET)

Gate currents during the Miller plateau:

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{pl}}{R_{g,tot}} = \frac{10-3}{5} = 1.4\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{g,tot}} = \frac{3}{5} = 0.6\ \text{A}$$

Transition times:

$$t_{on} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = \frac{15\,\text{nC}}{1.4\,\text{A}} = 10.7\ \text{ns}$$

$$t_{off} \approx \frac{15\,\text{nC}}{0.6\,\text{A}} = 25\ \text{ns}$$

Overlap loss:

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_{on}+t_{off}) f_{sw} = 0.5\times25.2\times80\times35.7\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.73\ W}$$

Turn-off dominates. A split gate path (for example, 10 Ω on / 2.2 Ω off via diode) cuts t_off by about 2× and P_VI by about 35%.

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (LS body diode → HS FET)

$$P_{Qrr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two dead-times per period)

$$P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2 t_{dead} f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times100\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}$$
