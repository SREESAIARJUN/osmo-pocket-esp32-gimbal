# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Engineering Design Package (Job #53)

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

Several premises in the brief need correcting before any numbers can be trusted.

1. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In a 4-in-1 AIO, the H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot600 to four (or one quad-capable) ESC MCUs. Those run AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware and generate the 48 kHz PWM. This design uses **4× AT32F421K8U7 running AM32**, one per motor. The 48 kHz switching frequency is an **AM32 parameter**, not an ArduPilot one.
2. **"Nano" and 80 A burst are incompatible.** A 20×20 mm board cannot dissipate approximately 100 W, even transiently, for longer than a fraction of a second (shown in §3). This package targets a **30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz outer and inner power layer AIO** and treats 80 A as a **≤1 s burst**, with 45–55 A continuous.
3. **Datasheet values are representative.** The FET values below are typical of current 40 V 5×6 mm (DFN5x6) parts. Substitute your exact part's R_DS(on)(T), Q_gd, Q_rr, and Z_th curves before sign-off.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation topology

AM32 uses 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM. At any instant, phase current I flows through **exactly two conducting channels**:

- one high-side (HS) FET of the PWM'd phase, during the on-time D, or its complementary low-side (LS) FET during the off-time 1−D; and
- one LS FET of the sink phase, continuously for that sector.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

The loss and temperature are mutually dependent, so they are solved together:

$$P_{cond}(T_j) = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j), \qquad T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}\,R_{th(j-pcb)}$$

### 1.3 Per-channel conduction loss

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 1.4 Per-FET conduction loss

Each phase leg spends 1/3 of the electrical period sourcing and 1/3 sinking:

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{DS}\,D + \tfrac{1}{3}I^2R_{DS}\cdot 0 \quad(\text{HS off when sinking})$$

$$P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{DS}(1-D) + \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{DS}$$

At full throttle (D → 1), the worst FET dissipates I²R_DS/3, while the LS FET carries more load at partial throttle. Use the conservative bound:

$$P_{FET,max} \approx \tfrac{2}{3}I^2R_{DS} \quad (D\to 0 \text{ worst LS case})$$

For the 80 A burst (full throttle, D ≈ 0.95), the realistic per-FET value is ≈ I²R_DS/3.

### 1.5 Numerical results at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond per channel | P_cond per FET (D≈1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W | 2.13 W |
| 100 °C | 1.34 mΩ | 17.1 W | 2.86 W |
| 125 °C | 1.45 mΩ | 18.6 W | 3.09 W |

### 1.6 PCB copper loss (frequently ignored, and significant)

Bus plus phase path ≈ 25 mm of 2×2 oz, 8 mm wide (two layers in parallel):

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{w t} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot 0.025}{0.008\cdot 140\times10^{-6}} \approx 0.38\text{ mΩ} \;\;(\times1.3 \text{ at } 100\,°C \approx 0.5\text{ mΩ})$$

$$P_{Cu,ch} \approx 80^2 \cdot 0.5\text{m} \approx 3.2 \text{ W}$$

Connector, solder, and shunt add roughly 0.5 mΩ more (~3 W). A **0.5 mΩ shunt** at 80 A also dissipates 3.2 W. Use a single shared bus shunt, or skip per-phase shunts.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg hard-switches (one HS turn-on/turn-off pair per PWM period). Its complementary LS is soft-switched, with only dead-time diode conduction.

### 2.1 V-I overlap (Miller-plateau dominated)

$$t_r \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g} = \frac{9\text{ nC}}{1.0\text{ A}} = 9\text{ ns}$$

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot18\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.87\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2t_{dead})\, f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

Q_rr scales roughly with di/dt and I; this uses the datasheet value.

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

### 2.4 Output capacitance (C_oss) loss

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss}V^2 f_{sw}\times 2 = 1.4\text{n}\cdot635\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.04\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss lands in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET.

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2 = 55\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k}\cdot2 = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

### 2.6 Switching total

**P_sw,ch ≈ 1.6 W at 80 A.** At 24 kHz this would be about 0.8 W. The 48 kHz choice costs about 0.8 W per channel at burst. That is acceptable: the benefit is lower current ripple, quieter motors, and smoother low-throttle behaviour on low-inductance motors.

Ripple check (L_phase-phase = 15 µH, worst at D = 0.5):

$$\Delta I = \frac{V D(1-D)}{L f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2\cdot0.25}{15\mu\cdot48\text{k}} \approx 8.8\text{ A}_{pp}$$

The RMS addition is ΔI²/12 ≈ 6.4 A², which is negligible against 6400 A².
