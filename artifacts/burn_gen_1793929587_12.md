# STM32H743 Flight Controller with 80A 4-in-1 ESC (20×20 Stack): Engineering Model, Job #76

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Key Findings

**Architecture.** The ESC half-bridges are not driven by the H743.

- Each 4-in-1 ESC runs its own MCU (assumed AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 firmware).
- The H743 flight controller talks to it over bidirectional DShot600 through an 8-pin harness.
- The model below covers both boards. Section 4 (DMA allocation) applies only to the H743.

**Key findings.**

1. **The 80A rating is a burst rating only.** With all four motors at 80A, the board reaches its thermal limit in about **3 s**. A single motor at 80A lasts about **11 s**.
2. **Realistic continuous rating is about 30A per channel** with propwash cooling.
3. **Bidirectional DShot600 fits comfortably** in the H743 DMA budget. One timer uses 5 streams, and at least 7 of the 16 streams remain spare.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths

The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM, which is the AM32/BLHeli_S default. In every PWM sub-interval, exactly two MOSFETs carry the full phase current I:

- **PWM on (duty D):** current flows through the high side of phase A, then the low side of phase B.
- **PWM off (1 − D):** current freewheels through the low side of phase A, then the low side of phase B. Synchronous rectification means it flows through the channel, not the body diode.

The conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) + I^2 R_{cu}, \qquad R_{DS}(T_j)=R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

### 1.2 Electrothermal fixed point

R_DS(on) depends on junction temperature, and junction temperature depends on loss. The loop is solved by iteration, with target T_j ≈ 110 °C:

$$R_{DS}(110) = 1.0\,(1+0.0045\cdot85) = 1.38\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond} = 2(80^2)(1.38\times10^{-3}) + 80^2(0.3\times10^{-3}) = 17.66 + 1.92 = \mathbf{19.6\ W/channel}$$

### 1.3 Loss per MOSFET

Each phase leg carries current for 4 of the 6 commutation sectors:

- **High-side FET:** 2 sectors at duty D.
- **Low-side FET:** 2 sectors at full on-time, plus 2 sectors at (1 − D).

At a typical burst duty of D ≈ 0.9:

$$P_{HS} \approx \tfrac{2}{6}\,D\,I^2R = 2.65\ \text{W}, \qquad P_{LS}\approx\tfrac{2}{6}(1+(1-D))\,I^2R = 3.24\ \text{W}$$

Summing over 3 legs gives 3 × (2.65 + 3.24) = 17.7 W, which matches §1.2. The **low-side FETs run hottest.**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the high-side FET hard-switches. The low-side FET turns on after its body diode is already conducting, so it switches at near-zero voltage.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ · V_bus · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw | ½ · 25.2 · 80 · 27 ns · 48 k = **1.31 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw | 40 n · 25.2 · 48 k = **0.05 W** |
| Output capacitance | ½ · Q_oss · V_bus · f_sw | **0.02 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f · I · 2 · t_dt · f_sw | 0.8 · 80 · 200 ns · 48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (driver loss, not FET) | Q_g · V_gs · f_sw · 2 | **0.04 W** |
| **Switching subtotal** | | **≈ 2.0 W/channel** |

**Totals:**

- Per channel: 19.6 + 2.0 = **21.6 W**.
- Board, all four channels at 80A: **P_tot ≈ 86 W**.

**Design levers:**

- **Dead time.** Cutting t_dt from 100 ns to 50 ns saves 0.3 W per channel. It requires verified driver propagation-delay matching.
- **Switching frequency.** Dropping to 24 kHz halves the switching subtotal (saving about 1 W per channel). The cost is higher current ripple and more acoustic noise.
