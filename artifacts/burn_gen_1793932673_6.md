## 0. Scope, Architecture, and Feasibility Flags

### 0.1 System Partition

An STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFET gates directly. In every production "AIO" design the work is split between two kinds of MCU:

| Domain | MCU | Firmware | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (480 MHz, LQFP100) | ArduPilot | Attitude control, DShot600 master, bidirectional eRPM capture |
| ESC (×4 or ×1 quad) | AT32F421 / STM32G071 per motor | AM32 (or BLHeli_32) | 6-step / FOC commutation, 48 kHz PWM, gate control |

The model below follows this split.

### 0.2 Feasibility Flags

**Flag 1: board size versus current.** "Nano" (20×20 mm mounting) combined with 80 A burst per motor is physically marginal. Section 2 shows roughly 90 W of total dissipation at full 4×80 A. A 20×20 board has about 2–3 J/K of thermal mass, so the adiabatic temperature rise is about 35–40 K/s. Treat "80 A burst" as **≤1–2 s, single-motor or partial-throttle bursts**, not simultaneous 4×80 A. Commercial 80 A-class 4-in-1 ESCs use 30.5×30.5 mm or larger with 3–4 oz copper. **Recommendation:** rate the board at 80 A burst for 1 s and about 40–45 A continuous with airflow, or move to 30×30 mm.

**Flag 2: assumed component values.** All component values are representative assumptions. Replace them with datasheet values for your selected parts before sign-off. The netlist in Section 4 is a reference topology, not a DRC-validated production netlist.

## 1. Commutation Model

Each motor uses a 3-phase bridge (6 FETs), for 24 FETs on the board. With 6-step trapezoidal commutation and synchronous rectification:

- At any instant, one high-side FET and one low-side FET carry the phase current I.
- Each FET conducts during 120° of the 360° electrical period, so its conduction duty is D_c = 1/3.
- One FET in the active leg is PWM-switched (hard switching). Its complement freewheels synchronously, with body-diode conduction only during dead time.

The RMS current per FET is:

$$I_{FET,rms} = I\sqrt{D_c} = \frac{80}{\sqrt{3}} = 46.2\ \text{A}$$

## 2. Loss Model at 80 A, 48 kHz

### 2.1 Conduction Loss (temperature-coupled)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha(T_j - 25)]$$

Per motor, two FETs are in the current path at all times:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Per FET:

$$P_{cond,FET} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\, D_c = I_{rms}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond per FET | P_cond per motor |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 0.90 mΩ | 1.92 W | 11.5 W |
| 100 °C | 1.20 mΩ | 2.56 W | 15.4 W |
| 125 °C | 1.31 mΩ | 2.79 W | 16.8 W |
| 150 °C | 1.41 mΩ | 3.01 W | 18.1 W |

Because R_DS(on) rises with temperature, conduction loss feeds back on itself. Solve the self-consistent junction temperature by iteration:

$$T_j = T_c + R_{\theta JC}\,P_{FET}(T_j)$$

Thermal runaway cannot occur as long as the loop gain stays below 1:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\, R_{\theta JC} = I_{rms}^2\, R_{DS,25}\,\alpha\, R_{\theta JC} = 2134 \times 0.9\times10^{-3}\times 0.0045 \times 1.0 = 0.0086 \ll 1$$

The FET is stable. The **board/case temperature** is the limiting quantity, not runaway.

**Copper and connection losses.** Model about 0.5 mΩ per phase path (2 oz internal plus pads plus motor wire solder joint) and 0.3 mΩ for the battery pad-to-bus path:

$$P_{Cu,motor} \approx 2 \cdot I^2 \cdot 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 6.4\ \text{W (burst)}$$

**Current shunt** (if a per-motor 0.25 mΩ low-side shunt is used): 6400 × 0.25 mΩ × (2/3 duty) ≈ 1.1 W. Many 4-in-1 boards use a single bus shunt instead. A 0.2 mΩ bus shunt at 4×80 A = 320 A gives 20 W, which is unacceptable. Use 0.1 mΩ or a Hall sensor at this current level.

### 2.2 Switching Loss (hard-switched FET)

**V–I overlap loss** (linear approximation):

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I\,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 33\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = 1.60\ \text{W}$$

A more rigorous version uses the Miller-plateau charge:

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd} + Q_{gs2}}{I_g},\qquad I_g = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}{R_g + R_{g,int}}$$

For example, with Q_gd = 12 nC, Q_gs2 = 6 nC, V_plateau = 4.5 V, R_total = 3.2 Ω: I_g = 1.72 A and t_sw ≈ 10.5 ns per edge. That is the optimistic bound. Layout stray inductance usually pushes real edges to 15–20 ns.

**Body-diode reverse recovery** (charged to the switching FET):

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 45\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.054\ \text{W}$$

**Output-capacitance loss:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss}\, V_{bus}^2\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.4\,\text{nF} \times 635 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.021\ \text{W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (in the synchronous FET; 2 edges per period):

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{k} = 1.23\ \text{W}$$

This term is significant. Cutting dead time to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per motor, but only if the gate driver's propagation-delay matching allows it.

**Gate drive** (dissipated in the driver and R_g, not the FET):

$$P_{gate} = 2\, Q_g\, V_{GS}\, f_{sw} = 2 \times 55\,\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.053\ \text{W per motor}$$
