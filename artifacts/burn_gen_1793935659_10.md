## 0. Architecture Decisions and Assumptions

Three issues must be settled before any equations are useful.

**1. The H743 does not drive the FETs.** Bi-directional DShot is a serial protocol between a flight controller and ESC microcontrollers. The architecture is therefore:

- **STM32H743** running ArduPilot. It sends DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry.
- **4× ESC MCUs** running AM32, for example STM32G071 or AT32F421. Each one generates 48 kHz complementary PWM.
- **4× 3-phase gate drivers** driving 24 FETs (2 in parallel per switch, as section 2.5 shows is required).

**2. "80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is not credible.** Four motors at 80 A each means up to 320 A battery current at full duty. The board cannot carry that current or absorb the heat. I have sized the ESC as a **30.5×30.5 mm stack** with an 8-layer, 2 oz board. The model below is parametric, so if you insist on 20×20 you can see exactly where it fails.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (6-step trapezoidal, synchronous rectification)

### 1.1 Leg states per electrical cycle

In each 60° commutation sector, one leg PWMs, one leg is held low, and one leg floats. Each leg therefore spends:

- **⅓ of the cycle as the PWM leg:**
  - HS conducts for fraction D.
  - LS conducts for fraction (1−D) as the synchronous rectifier.
- **⅓ of the cycle as the low leg:** LS conducts 100%.
- **⅓ of the cycle floating:** no conduction.
