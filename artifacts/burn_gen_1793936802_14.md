# Job #75: STM32H743 + 4-in-1 ESC Nano Stack: Power, Thermal and DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller. It does not commutate MOSFETs. A 4-in-1 ESC carries four ESC MCUs (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or BLHeli_32). Each ESC MCU drives one 3-phase gate driver and six N-channel MOSFETs. The H743 talks to them over four bi-directional DShot600 lines. The model below follows that split.

**Unknowns.** No MOSFET, PCB stackup or enclosure was specified. Every number below comes from the parameter table, and the equations are what you should re-run once parts are frozen.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology

Assume 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM, which is the AM32/BLHeli_32 default. In each 60° sector:

- **PWM leg:** current flows through the high-side (HS) MOSFET for fraction D of each period and through the low-side (LS) MOSFET for 1−D.
- **Return leg:** the LS MOSFET of the second conducting phase is on for the whole sector.

So at every instant exactly two channels are in series with the motor current:

$$
P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

This holds independent of duty cycle, which is a useful property of synchronous rectification.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

Phase-current ripple adds a correction term, $I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \Delta I_{pp}^2/12$. Inductance-dependent but typically under 2% at 48 kHz; ignored here.

### 1.3 Worked numbers (per motor, I = 80 A)

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 0.90 mΩ | 11.5 W | 46 W |
| 100 °C | 1.34 mΩ | 17.1 W | 69 W |
| 125 °C | 1.49 mΩ | 19.1 W | 76 W |

**Per-device loading.** Each MOSFET conducts for about 1/3 of the electrical cycle. Its average loss is about P_cond/6, roughly 3.2 W at 125 °C. During its conduction window the instantaneous loss is about 9.6 W.

At 30k RPM with 14 poles, f_elec ≈ 3.5 kHz. That is faster than the die thermal time constant of about 1 ms, so the average figure dominates. Expect a small superimposed ripple of about 1–2 K.
