# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC (Job #29): Systems Architecture Package

**Rev A, preliminary.** The values below come from first-principles models using stated assumptions. Validate them against the selected MOSFET datasheet and bench thermography before release.

---

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility

### 0.1 Partitioning

The STM32H743 is the **flight controller**. It does not commutate the motors. A production 4-in-1 ESC uses four dedicated ESC MCUs, one per motor (for example AT32F421, STM32G071 or EFM8BB51), running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 talks to them over bi-directional DShot600.

This document covers both boards:

| Block | Function |
|---|---|
| FC (H743) | Attitude control, DShot600 frame generation, eRPM telemetry decode, ArduPilot harmonic notch |
| ESC (4× MCU) | 6-step or FOC commutation, 48 kHz PWM, gate drive, current sense |

### 0.2 Feasibility Flag: Requirements Conflict

"Nano" (20×20 mm) combined with 80 A **per-motor** burst is not thermally credible beyond about 1 second (see §3.4). This document therefore assumes:

- **80 A per motor, ≤ 1 s burst**
- **45 A continuous**
- **6S supply** (25.2 V maximum)

If the program truly needs 80 A for 10 s, the ESC must move to a 30.5×30.5 mm footprint with a clamped heatsink. **Decision required from program management.**

## 2. Loss Model (Per Motor Channel)

Commutation is 6-step trapezoidal with complementary (synchronous) PWM. At any instant, current flows through one high-side FET and one low-side FET in series, and one leg is PWM-switching.

### 2.1 Conduction Loss with Thermal Coupling

R_DS(on) depends on junction temperature:

$$R_{DS}(T_j)=R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

**Instantaneous path loss** (two series FETs):

$$P_{cond}=2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

**Per-FET average.** Each FET conducts 1/3 of the electrical cycle. Complementary PWM splits the PWM-leg current between its high-side and low-side devices, so per-FET RMS current is approximately I/√3:

$$P_{cond,FET}=\frac{I^2R_{DS}(T_j)}{3}$$

**Evaluation at T_j = 115 °C** (R_DS = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0055 × 90) = 1.495 mΩ):

| I | P_cond (path) | P_cond per FET |
|---|---|---|
| 45 A | 2 × 2025 × 1.495 m = **6.05 W** | 1.01 W |
| 80 A | 2 × 6400 × 1.495 m = **19.14 W** | 3.19 W |

**Thermal runaway check.** Runaway occurs when the loop gain exceeds unity:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}R_{\theta}= I^2R_{25}\alpha R_\theta/3 \ge 1$$

At 80 A with an effective Rθ_JA per FET of about 25 K/W on a nano board, the loop gain is 6400 × 0.001 × 0.0055 × 25 / 3 = **0.29**. This is stable: the gain is below 1, but the coupling adds roughly 40% to the temperature rise, since the gain factor is 1/(1−0.29).

### 2.2 Switching Loss (Hard-Switched Leg, 48 kHz)

**V-I overlap:**

$$P_{sw}=\tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}$$

$$= 0.5(25.2)(80)(20\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = 0.968\text{ W}$$

**Reverse recovery** (body diode of the complementary FET):

$$P_{rr}=V Q_{rr} f_{sw} = 25.2(40\text{n})(48\text{k}) = 0.048\text{ W}$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss}= V Q_{oss} f_{sw} = 25.2(35\text{n})(48\text{k}) = 0.042\text{ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead times per period):

$$P_{dt}=V_{SD} I\,(2t_{dead}) f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(200\text{n})(48\text{k}) = 0.614\text{ W}$$

**Gate drive** (dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET; two FETs switching per leg):

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2(40\text{n})(10)(48\text{k}) = 0.038\text{ W}$$

### 2.3 Channel Loss Summary at 80 A

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (115 °C) | 19.14 |
| Switching overlap | 0.97 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.09 |
| Gate drive | 0.04 |
| Phase copper (≈0.4 mΩ path) | 2.56 |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, if used) | 1.60 |
| **Total per channel** | **≈ 25.0 W** |
| **4 channels simultaneous** | **≈ 100 W** |

At 45 A continuous, the same calculation gives about 8.6 W per channel, or **34 W total**.

**Design levers:**

- Reducing dead time to 50 ns saves 0.3 W per channel.
- Lowering f_sw to 24 kHz halves the switching terms but raises current ripple and acoustic noise.
- A 0.7 mΩ FET in a 5×6 package cuts conduction loss by 30%, but it does not fit on a nano board.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Steady-State Network (Per FET)

```
Tj ──Rθjc 1.5── Tcase ──Rθ_spread 6.0── Board ──Rθ_ba(eff)── Tamb
                                        │
                                   Cth_board
```

On a 20×20 mm board with prop-wash airflow (about 5 m/s), the board-to-air resistance is **Rθ_BA ≈ 3.5 K/W** for the whole assembly. This assumes h ≈ 60 W/m²K over 8 cm² of two-sided area: 1/(60 × 8e-4) ≈ 21 K/W, improved by component fins and leads to about 3.5–5 K/W. Treat this value as optimistic.

**Steady state at 45 A, 34 W, 35 °C ambient:**

- T_board = 35 + 34 × 3.5 ≈ **154 °C**

**This fails.** A nano board cannot sustain 45 A × 4 motors continuously. The realistic continuous rating is about 25 A per motor (≈11 W total → T_board ≈ 74 °C).
