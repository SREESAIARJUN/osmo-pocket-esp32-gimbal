# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #57): Thermal, Switching, and Bidirectional DShot Architecture

## 0. Assumptions and Corrections

I don't have the Job #57 BOM, stack-up, or MOSFET selection. Everything below is a **parametric model** with representative values. Replace the bracketed parameters with datasheet values before sign-off.

There are three architectural issues to resolve first.

1. **The H743 does not drive the power stage.** In a 4-in-1 ESC, each motor channel (or one MCU for all four) runs its own commutation firmware, such as AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical ESC MCUs are the AT32F421, STM32G071, or GD32. The H743 runs ArduPilot and only generates DShot600 frames and decodes the eRPM telemetry. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCU.
2. **"80A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is thermally implausible except for very short bursts.** Section 3 quantifies this: roughly 1 s or less at 80 A on all four channels. Production 20×20 AIOs are usually rated 35 to 45 A per channel with 5 to 10 s bursts. Treat 80 A as a **per-channel transient limit with a defined duration**, or move to a 30.5×30.5 mm board with 5×6 mm FETs.
3. **Rating definition.** I assume 80 A is per-channel phase current at 6S (25.2 V maximum). If it means total battery current, divide currents by about 2 and the losses by about 4.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal (AM32/BLHeli) commutation with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM). Two phases conduct at any time. Phase current *I* flows through:

- **PWM-on interval (D):** the high-side FET of phase A and the low-side FET of phase B.
- **PWM-off interval (1−D):** the low-side FET of phase A (synchronous freewheel) and the low-side FET of phase B.

So at every instant, current passes through **two FET channels in series**, regardless of duty cycle D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{ds,on}(T_j)$$

For trapezoidal drive with low ripple, I_rms ≈ I_dc,phase. Ripple adds a factor of (1 + r²/12), where r = ΔI/I. This is negligible at 48 kHz with typical motor inductance (r < 0.2).

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

Resistance rises with junction temperature:

$$R_{ds,on}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + k_T (T_j - 25)]$$

Junction temperature depends on the loss, so solve by fixed-point iteration:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_{ref} + P_{FET}(T_j^{(n)}) \cdot Z_{th}(t)$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

At 125 °C, R_ds = 1.5 × 1.6 = **2.4 mΩ**.

$$P_{cond,ch} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 2.4\,\text{m}\Omega = 6400 \times 4.8\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{30.7\ W}$$

Distribution between FETs over one 60° commutation sector:

- The low-side FET of the "return" phase carries 100 % of the time: 15.4 W.
- The high-side FET of the PWM phase carries D × 15.4 W.
- The low-side FET of the PWM phase carries (1−D) × 15.4 W.

Averaged over a full electrical revolution, each of the 6 FETs dissipates 30.7 / 6 ≈ **5.1 W average**. The worst FET in a sector peaks at about 15 W. The peak matters for short Z_th windows: the sector time at 30k eRPM is about 330 µs.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

With complementary PWM, only the **high-side FET of the PWM phase hard-switches**. The low side commutates through its body diode during dead time, giving zero-voltage switching.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Body-diode reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

Recovery also adds turn-on current overshoot, about I_rr × V × t_rr × f. Fold that into the measured E_on.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss(er)} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = 0.023\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction

This loss is significant at 80 A.

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2\,t_{dead})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 600\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.84\ W}$$

Dead-time loss scales linearly with f_sw × t_dead. AM32 and BLHeli_32 dead-time settings and the gate driver's minimum dead time (FD6288Q: about 100 to 300 ns internal) dominate this term. Reducing t_dead to 100 ns cuts it to 0.6 W.

### 2.5 Gate drive

Loss is taken mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET:

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W per channel}$$
