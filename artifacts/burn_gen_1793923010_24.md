# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #36): Architecture, Loss Model, DShot/DMA Allocation, Netlists

## 0. Assumptions and Key Findings

These points affect every number in this document.

1. **The H743 does not switch the MOSFETs.** Bi-directional DShot600 is a link between the flight controller and an ESC MCU. Each motor channel needs its own ESC MCU (here, 4× AT32F421 running AM32) that generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743's job is timer, DMA and telemetry decoding.
2. **"Nano" is assumed to mean a 20×20 mm mounting pattern on a ~29×29 mm board.** That size only fits DFN3.3×3.3 MOSFETs. All component values below are representative datasheet-class figures, not a qualified BOM. Replace them with your chosen parts' datasheet curves before sign-off.
3. **80 A per motor is only achievable as a short burst.** The model shows:
   - Full burst (all four motors at 80 A) is limited to about **2–3 s** from a 50 °C board.
   - Continuous capability is about **25–28 A per motor** with propwash cooling.
   - This is why commercial nano 4-in-1 boards are rated ~25–35 A. Any "80 A" label must be specified as a time-limited burst with firmware current/thermal limiting.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

**Topology.** The drive is 6-step trapezoidal with synchronous (complementary) PWM. At every instant, phase current flows through exactly one high-side path and one low-side path. Synchronous rectification means a MOSFET channel, not a body diode, carries current during the PWM off-time. Therefore, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

**Per motor at burst:**

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.0024 = \mathbf{30.7\ W}$$

**Per FET.** Each of the 6 FETs conducts for 1/3 of the electrical period:

$$P_{FET,avg} = \frac{I^2 R}{3} = \frac{6400 \times 0.0024}{3} = 5.1\ W$$
