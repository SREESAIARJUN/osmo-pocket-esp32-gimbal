### Feasibility warning

An interpretation of "80 A burst" as **80 A per motor** implies 4 × 80 A × 25 V ≈ 8 kW through a 20×20 mm PCB. The math below shows this is thermally infeasible for more than about 2–3 seconds, even at the junction-to-board level. Copper current density and connector/pad limits fail before that.

The model is therefore evaluated for two cases:

- **Case A:** 80 A per motor (stress case, demonstrates the limit).
- **Case B:** 80 A total burst, i.e. 20 A per motor. This is realistic for a nano AIO and is the recommended rating.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology and conduction states

The model assumes a 3-phase bridge with trapezoidal (6-step) commutation and synchronous rectification (complementary PWM). At any instant, phase current I_ph flows through exactly two MOSFET channels:

- **ON state (duration D·T_sw):** high-side of phase X, then winding, then low-side of phase Y.
- **Freewheel state (duration (1−D)·T_sw):** low-side of X (synchronous rectification), then winding, then low-side of Y.

So the conduction path always contains 2 × R_DS(on), independent of duty cycle (dead time excluded):

$$
P_{cond} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot 2 \cdot R_{DS(on)}(T_j) \;+\; I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot R_{PCB}
$$

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)]
$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R = 1.0 mΩ × (1 + 0.0045 × 85) = **1.38 mΩ**.

Because R_DS(on) rises with temperature, conduction loss feeds back into itself. The self-consistent junction temperature solves:

$$
T_j = T_{board} + R_{\theta jc}\cdot P_{FET}(T_j)
$$

This converges when R_θjc · ∂P/∂T < 1. That holds for all cases here, but margin shrinks rapidly at high current.

### 1.3 PCB copper resistance

Assume a phase trace of 6 mm length, 3 mm width, 2 oz copper (70 µm), on 2 parallel layers:

$$
R_{PCB} = \frac{\rho\,L}{w\,t\,n} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot 0.006}{0.003\cdot 70\times10^{-6}\cdot 2} \approx 0.25\ \text{mΩ per segment}
$$

The path contains about 2 segments, so R_PCB ≈ 0.5 mΩ. Including the copper temperature coefficient (+0.39 %/K) at 100 °C gives ≈ 0.65 mΩ.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

In each PWM period, one leg actually hard-switches. The high-side turns on against the conducting low-side body diode or channel, and turns off with current commutating back to the low side. The low-side switches at near-zero V_DS (ZVS-like), so it contributes mainly dead-time and diode losses.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I_{ph}\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}
$$

- Case A: 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 kHz = **1.94 W**
- Case B: 0.5 × 25.2 × 20 × 40 ns × 48 kHz = **0.48 W**

### 2.2 Output capacitance (C_oss) loss

Both FETs in the switching leg charge or discharge:

$$
P_{oss} = 2 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss,er} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1.5\,\text{nF}\cdot 635\cdot 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.046\ W}
$$

This is current-independent, so the value is the same for both cases.

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 50\,\text{nC}\cdot 25.2\cdot 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}
$$

Q_rr scales roughly with I_F. Treat this as the Case A value and use ≈ 0.02 W for Case B.

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per period:

$$
P_{dt} = V_F\,I_{ph}\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f_{sw}
$$

- Case A: 0.8 × 80 × 100 ns × 48 kHz = **0.31 W**
- Case B: **0.077 W**

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated mainly in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET:

$$
P_{gate} = 6 \cdot Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 6 \cdot 50\,\text{nC}\cdot 10\,\text{V}\cdot 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.144\ W/motor}
$$

In practice only 2 legs actively PWM at any time with 6-step commutation, so the real figure is about 0.05–0.1 W. The model keeps the conservative value.
