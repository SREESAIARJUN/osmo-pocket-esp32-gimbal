# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Thermal, Switching, and Bi-Directional DShot Design Package (Job #67)

## 0. Scope and Feasibility Constraints

These constraints shape every number below.

1. **The H743 is the flight controller MCU, not the ESC MCU.** A 4-in-1 ESC needs a commutation MCU on each motor channel (or one MCU per channel pair). Typical choices are AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or EFM8BB51 running Bluejay. These MCUs run the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 only generates bi-directional DShot600 and decodes eRPM telemetry. This document models both sides.
2. **"80A burst" is treated as per channel, for 10 s or less.** Four channels at 80 A means 320 A at the battery. No nano-format connector or pad survives that: XT60 is rated about 60 A continuous and XT90 about 90 A. A realistic aggregate burst is about 4 × 80 A for 1–2 s or 4 × 45 A sustained. Section 2.6 quantifies this.
3. **Assumed operating point.** Use the parameters below throughout. Replace them with your selected parts' datasheet values before sign-off.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0065\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 100 °C this gives R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0065 × 75) = **1.49 mΩ**. Use 1.5 mΩ.

### 1.2 Current paths in 6-step complementary PWM

In any 60° commutation sector, two phases carry current I:

- **Switching leg.** The high-side conducts for duty D. The low-side synchronous rectifier conducts for (1 − D), minus dead time.
- **Static leg.** One low-side FET is fully on for 100% of the sector.

The loss in each leg is:

$$P_{sw\text{-}leg} = I^2 R_{ds}\,D + I^2 R_{ds}(1-D) = I^2 R_{ds}$$

$$P_{static\text{-}leg} = I^2 R_{ds}$$

$$\boxed{P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)}$$

The result is independent of D. This is why synchronous rectification matters: without it, (1 − D) of the current flows through the body diode at V_SD × I.

**At 80 A and T_j = 100 °C:**

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ channel}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each of the 6 FETs conducts for 120° of the 360° electrical cycle. Averaged over a cycle:

$$\bar P_{FET,cond} = \frac{19.2}{6} = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

The instantaneous worst case is 9.6 W in the static low-side FET for one sector. At typical eRPM above 1 kHz, the electrical period is under 1 ms. That is far below the die thermal time constant (about 5–20 ms), so the cycle average is valid for junction temperature. Peak ripple is about 2–4 K.

### 1.4 Copper and shunt losses

| Element | Model | Value at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase/bus copper (2 oz, ~10 mm × 6 mm per path, 2 parallel layers) | R ≈ ρL/(wt) / 2 ≈ 0.20 mΩ | 2 × 80² × 0.2 m = 2.6 W |
| Current shunt (0.25 mΩ, one per channel) | I²R | 1.6 W |
| Bulk capacitor ESR (I_rms ripple ≈ 0.5·I) | (40 A)² × 1 mΩ eq. | 1.6 W (in caps, off-board if leaded) |

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only one leg switches per sector. Per switching event at V = 25.2 V and I = 80 A:

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard turn-on and turn-off of the high-side)

Plateau-limited transition times:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,src}} \approx \frac{10\,\text{nC}}{1.0\,\text{A}} = 10\,\text{ns};\quad t_f \approx \frac{10\,\text{nC}}{1.5\,\text{A}} \approx 7\,\text{ns}$$
