## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At the design point T_j = 110 °C: R_DS = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0045 × 85) = **1.38 mΩ**.

### 2.2 Current-path argument

In 120° commutation, two phase legs carry I_ph at every instant. With synchronous rectification, the current always flows through exactly **one high-side-equivalent and one low-side-equivalent channel**, during both the PWM-on and freewheel intervals. Therefore:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.38\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{17.7\ W}$$

Averaged over a full electrical cycle, by symmetry each of the 6 FETs dissipates:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}}{3} = \mathbf{2.94\ W}$$

The instantaneous worst-case FET (the low side during freewheel, at low duty) sees up to I²R ≈ 8.8 W during its 120° window. The thermal mass smooths this at electrical frequencies above about 100 Hz.

### 2.3 Parasitic copper

Add the PCB trace and solder resistance R_pcb, typically 0.2–0.4 mΩ per path on 2 oz inner planes:

$$P_{pcb} = 2 I_{ph}^2 R_{pcb} \approx 2 \times 6400 \times 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega = 3.8\ \text{W/channel}$$

This loss heats the board, not the junction. It is included in the board-level thermal budget in Section 4.
