# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #65): Electro-Thermal Model, DShot DMA Plan, and Reference Netlist

## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does not commutate the motors. ArduPilot has no motor-commutation firmware, and at 6S/80A you want one dedicated ESC MCU per motor or per pair. The ESC section uses four ESC MCUs (AT32F421 or STM32G071 class, running AM32 or BLHeli_32-compatible firmware). The H743 talks to them over bidirectional DShot600.

**Feasibility.** 80A per motor on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board is a **burst rating only**. It is thermally limited to seconds, not continuous operation. The model below makes this explicit.

**Placeholder parameters.** All MOSFET and driver values are representative placeholders, chosen as typical of 40V 3.3×3.3 or 5×6 ESC FETs. They must be replaced with the datasheet values of the selected part before sign-off.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-step synchronous (complementary) PWM

At any instant one high-side (HS) and one low-side (LS) device carry the phase current. During the PWM off-time, the freewheel path is HS-off and complementary-LS-on. That path again has exactly two FETs in series.

Therefore, independent of duty cycle D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j) \;+\; I_{ph}^2 R_{Cu}$$

The temperature-corrected on-resistance is:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)] = 1.0\,\text{mΩ}\times1.4125 = 1.41\,\text{mΩ}$$

**Calculation at 80 A:**

$$P_{cond,FET} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.41\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{18.1\ W\ per\ motor}$$

Copper adds to this. With 2 oz copper and a ~0.3 mΩ phase path (pads, vias, traces): I²R_Cu = 1.9 W.

### 1.2 Per-device distribution

The duty ratio sets how loss is shared between devices. This determines the hottest FET.

Each phase is driven high for 120°, driven low for 120°, and floating for 120°.

- **HS FET:** conducts for (1/3) × D of the electrical period.
- **LS FET of the same phase:** conducts for (1/3)(1−D) as synchronous freewheel, plus 1/3 as the active low leg.

This gives:

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}D\,I^2R_{ds},\qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(2-D)\,I^2R_{ds}$$

At burst throttle, D ≈ 0.95 and I²R_ds = 9.03 W while conducting:

| Device | Conduction loss |
|---|---|
| HS | 2.86 W |
| LS | 3.16 W (hottest device for conduction) |

### 1.3 Electrothermal feedback

R_ds depends on T_j, and T_j depends on P. This is solved by fixed-point iteration:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_{ref} + Z_{th}\,P\big(T_j^{(k)}\big)$$

The iteration converges when α · I²R_ds,25 · Z_th < 1. Above that point, thermal runaway occurs. With Z_th = 20 K/W at the device, this gives 0.0055 × 6.4 × 20 = 0.70. That converges, but the margin is thin.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

With complementary PWM, the **HS device hard-switches**. The LS device switches near zero voltage, apart from dead-time diode conduction.

### 2.1 V-I overlap loss

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(50\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{2.42\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

The LS body diode recovers when the HS turns on:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance loss

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss}V^2 f_{sw} = 0.5(1\text{n})(635)(48\text{k}) = 0.015\ \text{W}$$
