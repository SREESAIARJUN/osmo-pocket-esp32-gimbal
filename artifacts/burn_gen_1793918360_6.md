# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC "Nano" Stack: Systems Design Model (Job #61)

## 0. Architectural Corrections and Scope

These points are flagged first because they change the design.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In both ArduPilot and BLHeli/AM32 ecosystems, the H743 is the flight controller. It emits DShot to four ESC MCUs (typically AT32F421 or STM32G071, one per motor, running AM32 or BLHeli_32). The 48 kHz PWM frequency is set in ESC firmware, not ArduPilot. The model below is split accordingly.
2. **"Nano" (20×20 mm) cannot sustain 80A on four channels at once.** Section 3 shows that 4×80A is a roughly 1–2 second thermal event on a 20×20 board. 80A should be specified as a single-channel or short-transient rating. Continuous capability is roughly 25–35A per channel with propwash airflow.
3. **All component parameters below are representative assumptions** (Section 1). Replace them with your actual MOSFET datasheet values and rerun the equations. The netlist is a reference topology, not a DRC-verified production netlist.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction topology (6-step trapezoidal, synchronous rectification)

At any instant, one phase leg is PWM-active and one leg is the return path with its low side held fully on. The third leg floats.

- Active leg: high-side conducts for D·T_sw, low-side (sync rect) conducts for (1−D)·T_sw. Exactly one FET conducts in this leg at all times, apart from dead time.
- Return leg: low-side is on 100% of the time.

So at all times, two series R_DS(on) carry I_ph:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS,hot} = 2(80)^2(2.4\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{30.7\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Per-FET distribution (averaged over an electrical cycle)

The electrical period at 7 pole-pairs and 30 krpm is about 0.29 ms. This is much shorter than the die thermal time constant (about 1–5 ms), so averaging is valid.

Each leg spends 1/3 of the cycle active, 1/3 as return, and 1/3 floating:

$$
P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}D\,I^2R, \qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)\,I^2R + \tfrac{1}{3}I^2R
$$

With I²R = 15.36 W:

| D | P_HS (W) | P_LS (W) |
|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | 5.12 | 5.12 |
| 0.7 | 3.58 | 6.66 |
| 0.5 | 2.56 | 7.68 |

The low-side FETs are the thermal hot spot at partial throttle with high current. Typical cases are punch-outs at D around 0.6–0.8.

### 2.3 Switching losses at 48 kHz

**V–I overlap (high-side, hard-switched):**

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = \tfrac12(25.2)(80)(30\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = 1.45\ \text{W}
$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = 40\,\text{nC}\cdot25.2\cdot48\,\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}
$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V f_{sw} = 0.024\ \text{W}
$$
