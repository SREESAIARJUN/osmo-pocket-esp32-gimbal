# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #59): Architecture, Loss Model, Thermal Budget and DShot DMA Plan

## 0. Architectural Corrections and Stated Assumptions

Three architectural issues change the math, so they come first.

1. **The H743 cannot commutate the motors.** ArduPilot does not run motor commutation or FOC. A 4-in-1 ESC needs dedicated ESC MCUs running AM32, BLHeli_32 or Bluejay. This design uses **4× AT32F421K8U7 with AM32**, and the H743 talks to them over bidirectional DShot600.
2. **"80A burst" on a nano board is thermally a seconds-scale rating, not a continuous one.** The model below quantifies exactly how many seconds.
3. **4 × 80 A = 320 A of battery current exceeds what a nano-format power entry can carry.** Pads, XT30/XT60 connectors and 12–14 AWG leads cannot handle it. The burst rating is therefore defined per motor, with an aggregate limit set by the battery and connector.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Paths in a 6-Step Sector

In each 60° sector, phase A is PWM'd and phase B is held low:

| FET | Conducts for | Fraction of sector |
|---|---|---|
| Q_AH | D·T_sw | D |
| Q_AL (synchronous rectifier) | (1−D)·T_sw, minus dead time | (1−D) |
| Q_BL | Entire sector | 1 |

### 1.2 Bridge Conduction Loss

$$P_{cond,bridge} = I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)\,\big[D + (1-D) + 1\big] = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This result is **independent of duty cycle**, which is a key property of complementary PWM.

Temperature dependence and ripple are included as:

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\qquad I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2+\frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

The ripple current is:

$$\Delta I=\frac{V_{bus}D(1-D)}{L_{ph}f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5–10 µH phase inductance at 48 kHz, ΔI is roughly 13–25 A peak-to-peak at D = 0.5. This adds only about 1–3 % to I_rms², so it is neglected at burst. Keep it for low-inductance motors.

### 1.3 Numerical Result at T_j = 100 °C

- R_ds = 1.0 mΩ × (1 + 0.006 × 75) = **1.45 mΩ**
- P_cond = 2 × 80² × 1.45×10⁻³ = **18.6 W per ESC channel**

### 1.4 Per-FET Distribution

Each FET is active in 2 of the 6 sectors, half as the PWM device and half as the static low-side device.

- Averaged over an electrical cycle: 18.6 / 6 ≈ **3.1 W per FET**
- Instantaneous peak while static low-side: I²R = **9.3 W**

The peak is filtered by the FET's Z_th(t). At electrical frequencies above about 200 Hz, the ripple in junction temperature is under 2 K, so the average value governs.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg hard-switches. That leg sees one turn-on and one turn-off per period.

| Loss term | Equation | Value (25.2 V, 80 A, 48 kHz) |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ V I (t_r + t_f) f_sw, with t_r = t_f = Q_sw / I_g = 12 ns | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 24 ns × 48 kHz = **1.16 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr V f_sw | 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.05 W** |
| Coss | ½ Q_oss V f_sw | **0.02 W** |
| Dead-time diode | V_f I (2 t_dt) f_sw | 0.8 × 80 × 100 ns × 48 k = **0.31 W** |
| Gate drive (mostly dissipated in driver and R_g) | 2 Q_g V_drv f_sw | **0.05 W** |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 1.6 W per ESC** |

### 2.1 Total per ESC Channel at Burst

$$P_{ESC} \approx 18.6 + 1.6 + P_{shunt} + P_{copper}$$

**Shunt loss.** A 0.5 mΩ shunt carries the DC link current, which is roughly D × I_ph:
- At D = 0.8: (64 A)² × 0.5 mΩ = **2.0 W**

**Copper loss.** Assume about 0.3 mΩ of effective trace and pour resistance:
- 80² × 0.3 mΩ ≈ **1.9 W**

**Total: P_ESC ≈ 24 W per channel.** With all four channels at burst, the total is about **96 W**.
