## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

AM32 uses six-step (trapezoidal) commutation with synchronous rectification. At any instant, exactly two FETs carry the phase current I:

- **On-state:** HS(x) + LS(y)
- **Off-state (freewheel):** LS(x) + LS(y), via synchronous rectification

The current path therefore always crosses 2 × R_DS(on) regardless of PWM duty D. With PCB copper resistance R_cu added:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2\left(2R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + R_{cu}\right)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left(1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right),\quad \alpha \approx 0.006/\text{K}$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R = 1.0 × (1 + 0.51) ≈ 1.5 mΩ.

### 1.3 Copper resistance

For 2 oz copper, R_□ ≈ 0.25 mΩ/□. Each channel path (battery pad → bridge → motor pad) is about 2 squares across 4 paralleled layers:

$$R_{cu} \approx \frac{2 \times 0.25}{4} \approx 0.125\text{ m}\Omega \;\;(\text{use } 0.15\text{ m}\Omega \text{ with vias})$$

### 1.4 Result at 80 A

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 (3.0 + 0.15)\times10^{-3} = 6400 \times 3.15\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{20.2\ W/channel}$$

### 1.5 Per-FET distribution

| FET role | Conducts | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| High-side | 1/3 of the electrical cycle × D | I²R·D/3 |
| Low-side | 1/3 × 1 (its own sector) + 1/3 × (1 − D) (freewheel) | — |

At D = 1 (worst burst case), the hottest single FET dissipates:

$$P_{FET,max} = \tfrac{1}{3}I^2R = \tfrac{1}{3}(6400)(1.5\times10^{-3}) = 3.2\ \text{W (average)}$$

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

There is one hard-switched commutation pair per PWM period: the HS turn-off/on against the LS body diode or synchronous FET.

### 2.1 V–I overlap

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 (25.2)(80)(40\times10^{-9})(48\times10^3) = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period)

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD} \cdot I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 (80)(100\times10^{-9})(48\times10^3) = \mathbf{0.31\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\times10^{-9}(25.2)(48\times10^3) = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss} V^2 f_{sw} = 0.5(10^{-9})(635)(48\times10^3) = \mathbf{0.015\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (2 FETs switched per period; dissipated in the driver)

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2(50\times10^{-9})(10)(48\times10^3) = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 2.6 Channel total at 80 A

| Component | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction | 20.20 |
| V–I switching | 1.94 |
| Dead time | 0.31 |
| Q_rr | 0.06 |
| C_oss | 0.02 |
| Gate drive | 0.05 |
| **Total per channel** | **22.6 W** |
| **4 channels simultaneous** | **≈ 90 W** |

Efficiency at 80 A, 6S: P_out ≈ 25.2 × 80 ≈ 2016 W/ch, so η ≈ 98.9%. The efficiency looks good, but **90 W into ~10 cm² of PCB is the governing problem.**

### 2.7 General loss polynomial (for derating and firmware current limits)

$$P_{ch}(I) = a I^2 + b I + c$$

| Coefficient | Value | Origin |
|---|---|---|
| a | 3.15 × 10⁻³ Ω | Conduction |
| b | 0.0281 W/A | (1.94 + 0.31)/80, scales with V_bus |
| c | ≈ 0.08 W | Q_rr + C_oss + gate drive |

Note on frequency: moving to 24 kHz halves b. That saves ~1.1 W/ch at 80 A, at the cost of more current ripple and audible noise.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network (per channel, lumped Foster to Cauer conversion)

```
P_FET ──[R_θJC 1.0]──(T_c)──[R_θCB 0.8 (pad + 8 vias)]──(T_board)──[R_θBA]──(T_amb)
          │                                        │
         C_j 0.05 J/K                           C_board
```

**Board heat capacity (30.5 mm, 1.6 mm, 8-layer 2 oz):**

| Contribution | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| FR4 | 1.5 cm³ × 1.9 g/cm³ × 1.1 J/gK | 3.1 J/K |
| Cu | 8 × 70 µm × 9.3 cm² × 8.9 g/cm³ × 0.385 J/gK | 1.4 J/K |
| 24 FETs + solder | — | 0.5 J/K |
| **C_board** | | **≈ 5.0 J/K** |
| Optional 3 g Al heatsink | | +2.7 J/K → 7.7 J/K |

**Convection R_θBA**, prop-wash at ~8 m/s, h ≈ 60 W/m²K, two faces of 9.3 cm²:

$$R_{\theta BA} = \frac{1}{h A} = \frac{1}{60 \times 1.86\times10^{-3}} \approx 9\ \text{K/W bare}$$

With a finned lid, R_θBA ≈ 3 K/W.

### 3.2 Board time constant

$$\tau = R_{\theta BA} C = 9 \times 5 = 45\ \text{s (bare)}, \quad 3 \times 7.7 = 23\ \text{s (heatsink)}$$

Bursts of ≤5 s are much shorter than τ, so the burst is **near-adiabatic**.
