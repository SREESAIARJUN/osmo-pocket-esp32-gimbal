# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #32): Design Basis, Loss Models, DShot Timing, and Netlists

## 0. Scope and Feasibility Constraints

Four points shape everything below.

1. **The H743 does not switch the bridges.** In a 4-in-1 stack, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM and runs commutation. The H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600 to those four MCUs. The two halves are modelled separately.

2. **"80 A burst" on a 20×20 mm board is a marketing envelope, not a thermal operating point.**
   - 4 × 80 A = **320 A** at the battery pads, against an XT60 rated around 60 A continuous.
   - I model **80 A per channel for a time-limited burst** and derive the maximum allowable burst duration. I do not claim steady-state capability.

3. **Component values are representative examples.** They are not validated against a specific vendor datasheet revision. Every number is carried symbolically so you can substitute real values.

4. **ArduPilot hwdef syntax and DMA behaviour should be re-checked against the ArduPilot master branch you build from.** The bdshot DMA handling has changed across releases.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current paths in 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation with synchronous rectification

At any instant two phases conduct: one high-side FET and one low-side FET carry I_phase. During the PWM off-time, the PWM-ed FET's complement (sync rectifier) carries the current instead. So at any moment **exactly two FET channels** carry I in series, regardless of duty D.

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

For trapezoidal drive near full throttle, I_rms ≈ I_dc,phase = I_pk. Inductor ripple adds a small correction:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{2 L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

**Ripple check.** For a typical 5" motor, L_ph ≈ 15 µH (phase-to-phase ≈ 30 µH). At D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{2 \times 15\mu \times 48k} = 4.4\text{ A}$$

The ripple term is 1.6 A², which is negligible against 6400 A².

### 2.2 Numerical evaluation (per channel)

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond = 2·80²·R_ds |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | **12.8 W** |
| 100 °C | 1.34 mΩ | **17.1 W** |
| 125 °C | 1.45 mΩ | **18.6 W** |

### 2.3 Electro-thermal coupling (fixed point)

$$T_j = T_a + R_{\theta,ja,eff}\, P(T_j)$$

$$\Rightarrow\; T_j^* = \frac{T_a + R_\theta\,(P_0 - 25\,\alpha P_0')}{1 - R_\theta\,\alpha P_0'}, \qquad P_0' = 2I^2R_{ds,25}$$

**Thermal-runaway criterion:** if R_θ·α·P'_0 ≥ 1, no steady state exists. For one channel at 80 A, P'_0 = 12.8 W and α = 0.0045, so runaway occurs at R_θ ≥ 17.4 K/W.

This bound is per-device-pair. Section 6 shows the real constraint is board-level heat capacity, not runaway.

### 2.4 PCB copper conduction loss

The sheet resistance of 2 oz Cu is about 0.245 mΩ/□ at 25 °C, with a temperature coefficient of 0.0039 /K.

**Per channel:** phase path from FET to motor pad ≈ 3 squares, two phases in series:

$$P_{Cu,ch} \approx 80^2 \times (2 \times 3 \times 0.245\text{ mΩ}) \times 1.3_{(hot)} = 12.2\text{ W}$$

This assumes one layer. Paralleling across 3 power layers (stitched with vias) gives **≈ 4 W/channel**.

**Shared bus:** the battery pad to the four half-bridge rails carries up to 320 A. Even at 0.1 mΩ effective, the loss is 320² × 0.1 m = **10 W**. This is why real nano 4-in-1 boards cannot honour a simultaneous 4×80 A burst.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ed phase leg switches hard. In complementary PWM, the active FET hard-switches and its sync partner soft-switches (ZVS after dead time).

### 3.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 3.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period)

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I \cdot 2 t_{dt}\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

Reducing t_dt to 100 ns halves this. That is the cheapest efficiency win, but verify shoot-through margin with the actual driver's propagation mismatch.

### 3.3 Reverse recovery (hard turn-on into a conducting body diode)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

Q_rr rises with di/dt and T_j. Use a ×2–3 margin in the worst case: about 0.15 W.

### 3.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

### 3.5 Gate drive (dissipated in driver and R_g, not the FET)

$$P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\text{ W}$$

### 3.6 Per-channel burst totals (T_j = 125 °C)

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 18.6 |
| Switching overlap | 1.45 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 |
| Q_rr (with margin) | 0.15 |
| Q_oss | 0.06 |
| Gate drive | 0.05 |
| PCB copper (3-layer phase) | ~4.0 |
| **Total per channel** | **≈ 25.5 W** |
| **4 channels simultaneous** | **≈ 102 W** (+ ~10 W shared bus) ≈ **112 W** |

**Frequency sensitivity:**

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial f_{sw}} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r + t_f) + 2 V_{SD} I t_{dt} + (Q_{rr} + Q_{oss}) V + 2 Q_g V_{drv} \approx 59\ \mu\text{W/Hz}$$

So going from 24 kHz to 48 kHz costs about **1.4 W per channel** at 80 A. Conduction dominates; 48 kHz is justified for low-inductance motors, but it is not where the heat comes from.

## 4. DC-Link Capacitor Ripple

For a single-leg PWM inverter, the input capacitor RMS current is:

$$I_{C,rms} = I\sqrt{D(1-D)} \;\Rightarrow\; \max = I/2 = 40\text{ A per channel at } D = 0.5$$

**Required local MLCC bank per channel,** for ΔV ≤ 1 V:

$$C \ge \frac{I D (1-D)}{f_{sw}\,\Delta V} = \frac{80 \times 0.25}{48\text{k} \times 1} = 417\ \mu\text{F}$$

This cannot be met with MLCCs alone (X7R 35 V 10 µF 1206 derates to about 3 µF at 25 V). Practical approach:

- **Local:** 6–8 × 10 µF 1210/1206 MLCC per channel, giving about 20 µF effective, for high-frequency loop current.
- **Bulk:** an external low-ESR electrolytic of 470–1000 µF / 35 V on the battery leads. This is mandatory, and the external lead inductance is the reason why.
- **Interleaving:** offsetting the four channels' PWM phases reduces the shared RMS current. AM32 does not phase-lock channels, so assume worst-case coherent ripple.
