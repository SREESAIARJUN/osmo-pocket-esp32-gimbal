## 1. Commutation Topology Model

Each channel is a three-phase bridge (6 FETs) running six-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM.

In any 60° sector:
- **Leg A (PWM leg):** the high-side (HS) conducts for D·T_sw. The low-side (LS) synchronously rectifies for (1−D)·T_sw.
- **Leg B (return leg):** the LS is on continuously.
- **Leg C:** floating (BEMF sensing).

The motor current therefore always flows through **exactly two channels' worth of R_ds,on**: one in leg A (HS or LS) plus the leg-B LS. Hard switching occurs only on leg A.

Phase current with ripple:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_m^2\left(1+\frac{r^2}{12}\right),\quad r=\frac{\Delta I_{pp}}{I_m}$$

The ripple is

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus}-E_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 2207-class motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH line-line) at 48 kHz, r ≤ 0.15, so the ripple correction is below 0.2% and is neglected below.
