# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #33): Loss, Thermal, and DShot/DMA Design Package

## 0. Feasibility Findings

Three findings affect the scope before any detailed design:

1. **80 A burst per channel does not fit a nano (20×20 mm) 4-in-1 ESC.** Section 2 shows roughly 40 W per channel and roughly 160 W total at burst. A 20×20 board has a thermal mass of about 3 J/K, which allows less than 1.5 s of burst before the copper exceeds 110 °C. The realistic options are:
   - a 30.5×30.5 mm or larger footprint with 5×6 mm FETs, or
   - de-rating the 80 A burst to about 45 A on a 20×20 board.

   The model below is parameterised so you can evaluate either option.
2. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot does not run BLDC commutation or FOC. The architecture is:
   - STM32H743 FC sends bi-directional DShot600 to 4 ESC MCUs.
   - Each ESC MCU (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32) handles 6-step or sinusoidal commutation at 48 kHz.
3. **The netlist here is a reference-design netlist, not a released design.** Pin and AF assignments are checked against the STM32H743 datasheet AF tables. FET and driver values are representative. Before fab, run ArduPilot's `dma_resolver.py`, the ERC/DRC, and a thermal FEA.

## 2. Loss Model (Per ESC Channel, 3-Phase Bridge, 6 FETs)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

With synchronous rectification in 6-step commutation, the current path always passes through exactly two FET channels: one high-side or its complementary low-side in the PWM leg, plus one low-side in the return leg. This holds independent of duty cycle.

Temperature-dependent on-resistance:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)],\quad \alpha \approx \frac{k_T-1}{100} = 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

Conduction loss:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

For flat-top 6-step current, I_rms ≈ I_pk:

$$P_{cond} = 2(80)^2(1.8\text{ mΩ}\times1.6) = 2\cdot6400\cdot2.88\text{ mΩ} = \mathbf{36.9\ W}$$

Copper and shunt add to this. With a 0.25 mΩ shunt plus about 0.4 mΩ of trace and connector:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R = 6400 \times 0.65\text{ mΩ} = \mathbf{4.2\ W}$$

Because R_ds depends on T_j, P_cond and T_j are coupled and must be iterated (positive feedback, see §3.3).

### 2.2 Switching losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg hard-switches. Each term below uses the parameters from §1.

| Mechanism | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = 0.5·25.2·80·40 ns·48 k | 1.94 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_SD·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k | 0.61 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 50 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.06 W |
| C_oss charge (2 FETs/leg) | C_oss,er·V²·f_sw = 1 nF·635·48 k | 0.03 W |
| Gate drive (mostly in driver/R_g) | 2·Q_g·V_drv·f_sw | 0.04 W |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.7 W** |

Sensitivity: P_sw scales linearly with f_sw, I, and edge time. At 24 kHz, about 1.3 W is saved. Raising f_sw beyond 48 kHz is not worth it at this current.

### 2.3 Per-channel and board totals at 80 A burst

| Scope | Calculation | Total |
|---|---|---|
| Per channel | 36.9 + 4.2 + 2.7 | ≈ 43.8 W |
| 4-in-1 board | 4 × 43.8 | ≈ 175 W |

Efficiency at 80 A × 22.2 V ≈ 1776 W per channel: η ≈ 97.5%. This efficiency looks good, but the problem is the heat density, not the efficiency.

Average loss per FET is (36.9 + 2.7)/6 ≈ **6.6 W**. The PWM high-side carries the switching loss, so it reaches about 7.5 W.

### 2.4 Bus capacitor ripple (often the hidden limiter)

$$I_{C,rms} = I\sqrt{D(1-D)} \;\xrightarrow{D=0.5}\; 0.5\times80 = \mathbf{40\ A_{rms}}\ \text{per channel}$$

No nano-size MLCC bank survives this. Required:
- an external low-ESR electrolytic (≥ 1000 µF, 35 V) at the battery pads, and
- 4–6 × 10 µF/50 V X7R per channel within 5 mm of the half-bridges.
