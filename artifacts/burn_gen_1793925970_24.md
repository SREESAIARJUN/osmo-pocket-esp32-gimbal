# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC (Job #11): Architecture, Loss Model, DShot DMA Plan, and Netlist

## 0. Feasibility Findings

These findings affect every number below.

1. **Partitioning.** ArduPilot running on the STM32H743 does not commutate motors. The 48 kHz PWM, gate drive and commutation run on four separate ESC MCUs, for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32. The H743 generates bidirectional DShot600 and decodes eRPM telemetry. The model below follows this split.
2. **80 A per channel on a nano (20×20 mm) board is not thermally sustainable.** The model shows about 25 W of heat per motor at 80 A (§1.5). With four motors that is about 100 W on roughly 4 cm² of board. The result is a **burst rating of about 1.5–3 s**, not a continuous rating. If the requirement is "80 A for 10 s," the design needs a 30×30 mm board with paralleled FETs.
3. **Current shunt.** A single 0.25 mΩ battery shunt at 320 A dissipates 25.6 W. Use a 0.1 mΩ shunt or a Hall sensor (§1.7).
4. **All component values are representative.** Before layout, verify every value against the selected datasheets.

### 1.2 Commutation Topology

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous complementary PWM on the high side. During each 120° electrical segment:

- One high-side (HS) FET chops at duty D.
- The complementary low-side (LS) FET of the same leg conducts for the remaining (1−D) fraction.
- One LS FET in another leg is the return path and is fully on.

Each FET position averages over 3 segments, which gives the RMS currents:

$$I_{HS,rms}^2 = \frac{D}{3}I_{ph}^2,\qquad I_{LS,rms}^2 = \frac{1}{3}I_{ph}^2 + \frac{1-D}{3}I_{ph}^2$$

### 1.3 Conduction Loss

The on-resistance at junction temperature T_j is:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\left[1+\alpha (T_j-25)\right]$$

At T_j = 100 °C:

$$R_{DS} = 1.3 \times 1.45 = 1.885\ \text{m}\Omega$$

**Worst case is D → 1 (full-throttle burst):**

- Per FET: I_rms² = 6400/3 = 2133 A², so I_rms = 46.2 A.
- Per FET: P_cond = 2133 × 1.885 m = **4.02 W**.
- Per motor (6 FETs, always one HS plus one LS conducting): 2 × 80² × 1.885 m = **24.1 W**.
- Per 4-in-1 board: **96.5 W**.

The model is self-consistent: R_DS depends on T_j, so iterate T_j with §1.6. At T_j = 125 °C, R_DS = 2.08 mΩ and per-motor conduction loss rises to 26.6 W. This is the thermal-runaway sensitivity you must design around.

### 1.4 Switching Loss at 48 kHz

**Gate currents during the Miller plateau:**

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{10-3}{4} = 1.75\ \text{A},\qquad I_{g,off} = \frac{3}{4} = 0.75\ \text{A}$$

**Transition times:**

$$t_{on} = \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{g,on}} = 6.9\ \text{ns},\qquad t_{off} = \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{g,off}} = 16\ \text{ns}$$

**Loss terms:**

| Term | Equation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_on + t_off)·f = ½·25.2·80·22.9 ns·48 k | 1.108 W |
| Reverse recovery | V·Q_rr·f = 25.2·30 n·48 k | 0.036 W |
| C_oss | ½·Q_oss·V·f | 0.024 W |
| Dead-time diode (LS) | V_F·I·2t_dt·f = 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k | 0.614 W |
| Gate drive (per FET, in driver and R_g) | Q_g·V_drv·f | 0.019 W |

The ideal hard-switching subtotal is **1.17 W per motor**. Apply a parasitic-inductance factor k_L ≈ 1.5 to account for common-source inductance and loop L. The estimated switching loss is then **about 1.75 W per motor**, plus 0.61 W of dead-time loss
