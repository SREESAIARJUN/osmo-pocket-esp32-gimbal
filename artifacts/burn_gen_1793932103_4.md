# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #56): Architecture, Loss Models, DShot/DMA Allocation, and Netlists

## 0. Feasibility Findings

These findings constrain everything that follows.

1. **The H743 should not commutate the motors.** Each 6-step or FOC ESC needs a 6-output complementary timer with dead-time. The H743 has two advanced timers (TIM1, TIM8) plus HRTIM, which is not enough for four independent bridges alongside the flight stack. ArduPilot also has no on-FC ESC firmware. The architecture is therefore:
   - **H743** runs ArduPilot and drives four bidirectional DShot600 lines.
   - **Four ESC MCUs** run AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. Candidates are the STM32G071 and the AT32F421. Each ESC MCU drives a 3-phase gate driver and 6 MOSFETs.
2. **"80A burst" on a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 nano board is a transient rating, not a thermal steady state.** The model in Section 3 shows that all four channels at 80A give roughly 100 W into about 2 J/K of board. That is about 45 K/s adiabatic, so a full 4-channel burst is limited to roughly 1–1.5 s. Treat 80A as per-channel, single-motor, ≤1 s, and state that explicitly on the datasheet.
3. **The input current is impossible at full burst.** Four channels at 80A is 320A on the battery leads. An XT60 is rated about 60A continuous, and 12 AWG wire is effectively 60–80A short-term. Specify a realistic aggregate, for example 4×35A continuous and 4×55A for 10 s, with per-channel 80A transients only.

All numbers below use stated example device parameters. Replace them with your selected MOSFET's datasheet values. The equations are general.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (Per Channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

In 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification, two FETs are always in the current path: one high-side and one low-side. Each FET carries phase current for 1/3 of the electrical period, either directly (high side during on-time) or as the synchronous-rectifier (low side during off-time).
