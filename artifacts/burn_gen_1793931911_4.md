# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #55): Power-Stage, Thermal and DShot Architecture

## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

The brief combines a flight controller and a 4-in-1 ESC on one board, so two points need fixing before any modeling:

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** Bidirectional DShot is a protocol between the flight controller and separate ESC MCUs. This design therefore uses the H743 running ArduPilot, plus four ESC MCUs (AT32F421, AM32 firmware), plus 3-phase gate drivers and 24 MOSFETs.
2. **The 80 A figure is treated as a per-motor burst at 6S.** As Section 3 shows, a nano footprint (20×20 mm mount, ~26×26 mm board) can absorb this only for about 2 s with all four motors loaded. Any marketing rating must say "burst."

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Bus voltage | 6S LiPo: 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal |
| Burst current | 80 A per motor, ~1–2 s |
| PWM | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous) 6-step |
| MOSFET target | 40 V, R_DS(on) ≤ 1.0 mΩ @ 25 °C, 10 V gate, Q_g ≈ 30 nC, PDFN 3.3×3.3 mm |
| Board stack | 8-layer: 6 power layers at 2 oz, 2 signal layers at 1 oz |
| Ambient | 40 °C (outside air, prop-washed) |
| T_j limit | 150 °C absolute, 125 °C design |

All MOSFET parameters are design targets. Re-run the model with datasheet curves for the selected part.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths
In 6-step complementary PWM, two phases conduct at any time:
- **PWM leg:** the high-side is on for duty D. The synchronous low-side is on for (1−D) minus dead time.
- **Return leg:** the low-side is on for the full commutation sector.

The motor current I therefore always flows through **exactly two channels** (ignoring dead time):

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + I_{rms}^2\,R_{Cu}$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.0 × 1.5 = **1.5 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Copper path
The phase-to-pad path plus the bus-to-FET path, on 6×2 oz planes, is estimated at **R_Cu ≈ 0.4 mΩ** per conduction loop. Verify this by 4-wire measurement on the first article.

### 1.4 Results at 80 A

$$P_{FET,cond} = 2(80)^2(1.5\,\text{m}\Omega) = 19.2\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{Cu} = (80)^2(0.4\,\text{m}\Omega) = 2.56\ \text{W}$$

- Per-FET conduction peak (return-leg low-side): **9.6 W**.

### 1.5 Thermal runaway criterion
Conduction loss rises with temperature, which raises loss further. The loop is stable only if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\,R_{th,ja} < 1 \;\Rightarrow\; 2I^2R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_{th,ja} < 1$$

At 80 A, with R_th,ja(effective, per channel) ≈ 7 K/W, the left side is 2·6400·0.001·0.005·7 ≈ **0.45**. This is stable, but the loop gain multiplies the rise by 1/(1−0.45) ≈ **1.8×**. That multiplier is why steady-state 80 A is impossible on this footprint.
