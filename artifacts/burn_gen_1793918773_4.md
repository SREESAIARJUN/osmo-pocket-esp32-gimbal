# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: System Architecture & Thermal Model (Job #21)

## 0. Architectural Corrections

Three points need settling before the math, because they change the design.

1. **The STM32H743 cannot be the ESC MCU.** Each motor needs its own commutation controller with 48 kHz PWM, BEMF zero-cross comparators, and µs-level commutation timing. The H743 runs ArduPilot (the flight controller). It sends bi-directional DShot600 to **four dedicated ESC MCUs**, typically AT32F421 or STM32G071-class chips running AM32. Everything below assumes this split.

2. **80 A burst on a "nano" (20×20 mm) board is not thermally achievable.** Even on 30.5×30.5 mm, all four channels at 80 A gives a time-to-limit of about **2–3 seconds** (derived in §3.5). I model the 30.5×30.5 mm board and state the burst rating that physics supports.

3. **48 kHz is the ESC PWM frequency**, set in AM32. It is not related to the H743 or DShot timing.

## 2. Power-Stage Assumptions (Worked-Example Parameters)

Twenty-four FETs must fit on 30.5×30.5 mm, so the parts are 3.3×3.3 mm DFN, not 5×6 mm.

| Symbol | Meaning | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | Bus voltage (6S, full charge) | 25.2 V |
| I_pk | Burst phase current per motor | 80 A |
| f_sw | ESC PWM frequency | 48 kHz |
| R_ds25 | R_DS(on) at 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | 1.2 mΩ |
| α | R_DS(on) temperature coefficient | 0.005 /K (normalized ≈1.5 at 125 °C) |
| t_r, t_f | Switching transition times at gate drive ~1.5 A | 20 ns each |
| Q_g | Total gate charge | 50 nC |
| C_oss | Output capacitance (effective) | 1.2 nF |
| Q_rr | Body-diode reverse-recovery charge | 40
