## 0. Architecture Clarification and Critical Findings

**Topology assumed:** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It drives a 4-in-1 ESC in which each motor channel has its own small MCU (STM32G071 / AT32F421 class, running AM32 or BLHeli_32-compatible firmware). The H743 does not commutate motors directly. It is the wrong part for that role and has no ESC firmware ecosystem.

**Critical finding (read first):** 80 A per motor on a nano (20×20 / 25.5×25.5 mm) board is **thermally impossible in steady state**. Section 3 shows it is achievable only as a time-limited burst, roughly 2 s with all four motors at 80 A and roughly 6–8 s with a single motor at 80 A. This should be rated as **"80 A burst (≤2 s, 4-motor), 35–40 A continuous with prop-wash airflow."** Marketing it as a continuous rating would be indefensible.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Path

In 6-step commutation with complementary PWM, the phase current always flows through exactly **two** conducting MOSFET channels:

- **ON interval:** high-side (phase A) → motor → low-side (phase B)
- **OFF interval:** synchronous low-side (phase A) freewheels → motor → low-side (phase B)

This gives the per-motor conduction loss:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j) \;+\; I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{Cu}
$$

where

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{/K}
$$

At T_j = 125 °C this gives R_DS(on) = 1.5 mΩ.

### 1.2 Numbers at 80 A

**Silicon loss:**

$$
P_{Si} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 6400 \times 0.003 = \mathbf{19.2\ W}
$$

**Copper loss.** Estimate R_Cu (phase pad + bus + return, per motor path) with ρ_Cu = 1.72×10⁻⁸ Ω·m:

- 2 oz (70 µm), 3 mm wide, 15 mm effective length, 2 paralleled layers ≈ 0.6 mΩ
- Add solder/via contribution: total ≈ 0.6 mΩ

$$
P_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.6\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.8\ W}
$$

**Per-FET average (for junction-temperature calculation):**

$$
P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{P_{Si}}{6} \cdot \frac{3}{3} \approx \mathbf{3.2\ W}
$$

Each of the 6 FETs per motor carries current for about 1/3 of the electrical cycle. Two FETs are always conducting, so the total loss is divided across six FETs on average. The die thermal time constant (~ms) exceeds the electrical period at high eRPM, so averaging is valid above roughly 5 k eRPM.
