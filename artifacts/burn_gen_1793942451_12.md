# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #28): Loss, Thermal, and DShot/DMA Design Package

## 0. Scope Corrections and Assumptions

Three issues change the math, so they come first.

**1. The H743 does not drive the power stage.** In this architecture the H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600. Each ESC channel has its own MCU (assumed AT32F421 running AM32) that generates the 48 kHz PWM and six-step commutation. The DMA allocation in §4 is for the FC's DShot outputs, not the MOSFET PWM.

**2. "80 A burst" needs a definition, because it changes losses by 16×.**

| Interpretation | Battery current | Credible on a nano (20×20 / 25.5×25.5) board? |
|---|---|---|
| A: 80 A per motor, all 4 simultaneously | 320 A | No. Only for a few seconds (shown below). XT60 and 12 AWG leads cannot carry it. |
| B: 80 A per motor, single-motor transient | 80 A | Marginal. Feasible for short bursts. |
| C: 80 A total (20 A/motor) | 80 A | Yes. Typical nano-class rating. |

I model the worst case (A), then give the scaling for B and C.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (Six-Step, Complementary/Synchronous PWM)

**Current path.** In six-step drive with synchronous rectification, two phases are active at any instant:
- **PWM on (duty D):** current flows high-side (leg X) → motor → low-side (leg Y).
- **PWM off:** current freewheels through low-side X (synchronous) → motor → low-side Y.

Either way, exactly two FETs are in the current path. That gives:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 \cdot 2\,R_{ds}(T_j) \cdot (1 + \epsilon_{Cu}), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j) = k_T R_{ds,25}$$

where ε_Cu is the extra PCB copper loss. For 2 oz, 8-layer construction, ε_Cu ≈ 0.15–0.3 depending on pour geometry. I compute FET loss separately below.

**At 80 A:**

$$P_{cond,FET} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 6400 \times 3.0\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W/motor}$$

**Distribution across the six FETs.** Each phase is high-side active for 1/3 of the electrical cycle and low-side active for 1/3. Averaged over a revolution, conduction is spread evenly:

$$P_{cond,per\,FET} \approx 19.2/6 = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

At low duty, the low-side FETs carry more of the loss, because the freewheel fraction is (1−D). At D≈0.5 the per-leg low-side loss peaks at about 1.25× the average.

**Copper loss.** Assume a 0.4 mΩ effective path from pad to FET to motor pad:

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.4\,\text{m}\Omega = 2.6\ \text{W/motor}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model @ 48 kHz

Per motor, one leg is hard-switching at any time. Its high-side FET switches the full motor current.

**2.1 V–I overlap loss**

Transition time: $t_{r,f} \approx Q_{sw}/I_G = 13\text{ nC}/1\text{ A} = 13\text{ ns}$

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 26\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.26\ W}$$

**2.2 Output capacitance loss**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,er} V^2 f_{sw} \times 2 = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = 0.046\ \text{W}$$

**2.3 Reverse recovery loss** (low-side body diode, hard commutation)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

**2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction**

$$P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**2.5 Gate drive loss** (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET)

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2 = 45\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = 0.043\ \text{W}$$
