# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #35): Power, Thermal & Bidirectional DShot Architecture

## 0. Architectural Assumptions and Corrections

The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. In every production 4-in-1 design, the H743 is the flight controller. It sends DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs (one per channel, or one per pair) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. This document follows that partitioning.

| Parameter | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus,max = 25.2 V | Design point for losses |
| FET rating | 40 V, PDFN 3.3×3.3 | 6S needs ≥ 40 V for ringing margin |
| R_DS(on) @ 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | 1.5 mΩ typ / 2.0 mΩ max | Representative of best-in-class 3.3×3.3 |
| R_th,JC (bottom) | 1.5 K/W | |
| Topology | 3 half-bridges per channel, 1 FET per switch | 24 FETs total |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal, complementary (synchronous) PWM | AM32 default |
| f_sw | 48 kHz | |
| Burst | 80 A **phase current per channel** | All four channels simultaneously for the worst case |
| Board | 4-layer, 2 oz on all layers | 20×20 mm "nano" vs 30.5×30.5 mm comparison given |

**Feasibility warning (stated up front):** 80 A per channel on a 20×20 board is a **sub-second to ~1 s burst rating**, not a continuous rating. The math below shows why. A realistic continuous rating is 25–35 A per channel with prop-wash airflow.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step synchronous PWM

During any 60° commutation sector, leg A is PWM-switched (HS on for D, LS on for 1−D) and leg B is held with LS on. Leg C floats (BEMF sensing). Phase current I_ph flows continuously because of motor inductance.

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2\left[D\,R_{HS}(T_j) + (1-D)\,R_{LS}(T_j)\right] + I_{ph}^2\,R_{LS}(T_j) + I_{ph}^2 R_{PCB}$$

With matched FETs, the duty cycle drops out:

$$\boxed{P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2\,\big(2R_{DS}(T_j) + R_{PCB}\big)}$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence of R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}\ (\text{trench, 40 V})$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R = 1.5 mΩ × (1 + 0.0055 × 85) = **2.20 mΩ**

### 1.3 PCB copper resistance

A 2 oz layer has a sheet resistance of 0.25 mΩ/□ at 25 °C. The phase path runs from the battery pad to the FETs to the motor pad. On a 20×20 board this is about 4 squares, paralleled over roughly 2.5 effective layers (vias limit it):

$$R_{PCB} \approx \frac{0.25 \times 4}{2.5} \times 1.33\ (\text{at } 110\,°\text{C}) \approx 0.5\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.4 Numerical result at 80 A

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \times (2 \times 2.20 + 0.5)\times10^{-3} = 6400 \times 4.9\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{31.4\ W\ per\ channel}$$

Of this, 28.2 W is in silicon and 3.2 W is in copper.

### 1.5 Electrothermal stability (thermal runaway criterion)

Loop gain of the R_DS–temperature feedback:

$$G = \frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\cdot R_{th} = 2 I^2 R_{DS,25}\,\alpha\, R_{th,JA,eff}$$

The board is stable only if G < 1. At 80 A with R_th,eff = 4 K/W (per-channel share):

$$G = 2(6400)(1.5\times10^{-3})(0.0055)(4) = 0.42$$

The board is stable, but the closed-loop temperature rise is amplified by 1/(1−G) = **1.73×**. This amplification is why steady-state 80 A is impossible, as Section 3 shows.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd high-side/low-side pair hard-switches. The held LS on leg B does not switch.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard turn-on and turn-off of the HS)

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2} + Q_{gd}}{I_g},\quad I_g = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}{R_{g,ext} + R_{g,int}}$$
