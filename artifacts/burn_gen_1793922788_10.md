**Red flag (stated up front):** 80 A × 4 channels = **320 A of battery current**. That exceeds an XT60 (≈60 A continuous) and practically any nano-class power entry. "80 A burst" on a 20×20 board is a marketing-class number that is achievable only for **seconds**, and only per channel with the others lightly loaded. The math below quantifies exactly how many seconds.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, phase current always flows through exactly **one high-side-position and one low-side-position device** at any instant. During the PWM off-time, the complementary FET carries the current instead of the body diode. So the conduction path always contains 2 × R_DS(on), regardless of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + I_{ph}^2 R_{pcb}$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0065\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

| T_j | R_DS(on) |
|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 1.49 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 1.65 mΩ |

### 1.3 PCB copper resistance

Assume an 8-layer, 2 oz board with paralleled phase and bus pours, and an effective path of ~15 mm × 4 mm × 3 layers:

$$R_{pcb} = \frac{\rho\,L}{A} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot 0.015}{0.004\cdot 70\times10^{-6}\cdot 3} \approx 0.31\ \text{m}\Omega$$

Add ≈0.2 mΩ for vias and solder joints, giving **R_pcb ≈ 0.5 mΩ per channel**, plus motor pad joints.

### 1.4 Result at 80 A, T_j = 100 °C

$$P_{cond,FET} = 80^2 \cdot 2 \cdot 1.49\text{ m}\Omega = 19.1\ \text{W}$$
$$P_{cond,pcb} = 80^2 \cdot 0.5\text{ m}\Omega = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

**Per-FET distribution.** Each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical cycle (120°). The average per FET is therefore 19.1 / 6 ≈ **3.2 W**, while the instantaneous peak is 9.6 W.

At high eRPM the commutation interval is very short compared with the FET's thermal time constant (ms range). For example, 30,000 eRPM gives a 333 µs sector. The junction therefore sees close to the averaged value, with ripple:

$$\Delta T_{j,ripple} \approx P_{peak}\cdot Z_{th}(t_{on}) \approx 9.6\,\text{W}\cdot 0.1\,\text{K/W} \approx 1\ \text{K}$$

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switching leg hard-switches. Its complement commutates softly after the dead time. The analysis is per channel.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard turn-on and turn-off)

Plateau-limited transition times:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,R_{g,tot}}{V_{DRV}-V_{plateau}},\quad t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,R_{g,tot}}{V_{plateau}}$$

With R_g,tot = 3.3 Ω external + 1 Ω driver + 1 Ω internal ≈ 5.3 Ω, and V_plateau ≈ 3.5 V:

- t_r ≈ 6 nC · 5.3 / 6.5 ≈ 4.9 ns. This is driver-current-limited, so the realistic value is **≈10 ns**.
- t_f ≈ 6 nC · 5.3 / 3.5 ≈ 9.1 ns. The realistic value is **≈12 ns**.

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot22\text{ ns}\cdot48\text{ kHz} = 1.06\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

Synchronous rectification shortens the diode conduction, but the body diode still conducts during the dead time:

$$P_{rr} = V_{bus}\,Q_{rr}\,f_{sw} = 25.2\cdot30\,\text{nC}\cdot48\,\text{kHz} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

Add the induced turn-on overlap from I_rr, roughly 2× this value: **≈0.07 W**.
