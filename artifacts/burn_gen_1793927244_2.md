> ⚠️ **Architecture clarification.** On a 4-in-1 stack the STM32H743 is the flight controller. Commutation runs on four dedicated ESC MCUs at 48 kHz. The H743 only generates DShot600 frames and decodes eRPM telemetry. Driving 24 MOSFETs at 48 kHz from the H743 while also running ArduPilot is not a viable architecture, and ArduPilot does not support it.

> ⚠️ **Feasibility warning (summarized from §3).** On a ~28×28 mm board, 80 A/motor is physically sustainable only as a burst of roughly **1–3 s**. The realistic continuous rating is **~35–45 A/motor** with prop-wash cooling. Four motors at 80 A also draw up to ~320 A from the battery, which exceeds the XT60 rating and any practical nano battery lead. The rating should be published as "80 A burst (≤ 2 s)" or reduced.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

At any instant in trapezoidal (6-step) commutation, exactly one phase is driven high, one is low, and one floats. With complementary (synchronous) PWM:

- **PWM ON (duty D):** high-side FET of phase A → motor → low-side FET of phase B.
- **PWM OFF (1–D):** low-side FET of phase A (sync rectifier) → motor → low-side FET of phase B.

In both states **two FET channels are in series with the motor current**. Total conduction loss per motor is therefore independent of duty:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{ph}^2 \, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence of R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

At the design limit T_j = 110 °C:

$$
R_{DS(on)}(110) = 1.6\ \text{m}\Omega \times (1 + 0.0045 \times 85) = 1.6 \times 1.38 = 2.21\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A burst

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.21\times10^{-3} = 28.3\ \text{W}
$$

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

Each phase's high-side FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical cycle (scaled by D). Its low-side FET conducts for 1/3 as the return path plus (1–D)×1/3 as the sync rectifier. Averaged over a full electrical cycle with D → 1 at full throttle:

$$
P_{FET,avg} = \frac{P_{cond,motor}}{6} = \frac{28.3}{6} \approx 4.7\ \text{W per FET}
$$

At 6S and high eRPM the electrical period is tens of µs. That is far shorter than the die thermal time constant (~ms), so averaging is valid.

### 1.5 Parasitic copper loss (not negligible at 80 A)

Assume a battery-to-FET path of 2 oz copper with an effective 0.3 mΩ per motor branch, plus a 0.5 mΩ shunt (if per-motor sensing is used):

$$
P_{Cu+shunt} = 80^2 \times 0.8\times10^{-3} = 5.1\ \text{W per motor}
$$

> **Shunt choice:** At 80 A, 0.5 mΩ dissipates 3.2 W in a 2512 resistor. Rated 3 W, it is at its limit. Use **0.25 mΩ**, or a single shared bus shunt with a 20× amplifier (INA186-class) feeding the FC ADC.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only **one hard-switched leg per motor** is active at a time: the PWM'd phase. Its sync-rectifier partner commutates softly, apart from dead-time and recovery effects.

### 2.1 Hard-switching (V–I overlap) loss

$$
P_{sw,VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}\, V_{bus}\, I\,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}
$$

$$
= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\times10^{-9} \times 48\,000 = 1.45\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance (C_oss) loss

$$
P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2}\, Q_{oss}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 45\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.027\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.3 Body-diode reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}
$$

At 80 A, Q_rr rises substantially, often 2–3× the datasheet value at its rated di/dt. Budget **0.1 W**.

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

With t_dt = 100 ns per edge (two edges per PWM period) and V_F ≈ 0.8 V:

$$
P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\times10^{-9} \times 48\,000 = 0.61\ \text{W}
$$

Dead time is the single biggest lever after V–I overlap. AM
