# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #31): Engineering Model and Reference Design

This document gives the analysis first and the netlists second. Where I used an assumed component parameter, it is labeled. Swap in your actual datasheet values and the equations still hold.

## 0. Architecture Correction

The H743 cannot commutate the motors itself. ArduPilot does not run six-step or FOC motor commutation. A working "4-in-1 ESC + H743" board is therefore two systems on one PCB:

```
            ┌──────────── STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──────────────┐
            │ TIMx CH → bidirectional DShot600 (1 wire/motor) │
            └──┬─────────┬──────────┬──────────┬──────────────┘
               │M1       │M2        │M3        │M4
         AT32F421   AT32F421   AT32F421   AT32F421   (AM32 / BLHeli_32-class)
         + 3-ph gate driver + 6 MOSFETs + shunt, per channel
```

There are two physical constraints you should accept before layout.

**1. "80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 / 25.5×25.5 mm) board is a per-channel, non-simultaneous, sub-10-second rating.**
- Four channels at 80 A means 320 A through the battery pads.
- No nano-size copper stack can carry that, thermally or resistively.
- Section 3 quantifies this.

**2. The ESC MCU firmware owns the 48 kHz PWM.**
- The H743 only sends throttle and receives eRPM.

## 1. Assumed Operating Point

| Parameter | Symbol | Value (assumption) |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S, full) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Phase current, burst | I | 80 A (per channel) |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| MOSFET (40 V, 5×6 or 3.3×3.3) | R_ds(on),25°C | 0.9 mΩ @ V_GS = 10 V |
| R_ds(on) temperature coefficient | k_T | ×1.6 at T_j = 125 °C |
| Combined rise + fall time | t_r + t_f | 25 ns (strong driver, ~1 A sink/source) |
| Output charge | Q_oss | 45 nC |
| Gate charge | Q_g | 55 nC |
| Gate drive voltage | V_drv | 10 V |
| Dead time (per edge) | t_dt | 100 ns |
| Body-diode forward voltage | V_f | 0.8 V |
| Diode reverse recovery charge | Q_rr | 40 nC |
| FETs per channel | N | 6 |

The scheme is trapezoidal six-step with complementary (synchronous) PWM, as AM32 does by default. At every instant, two of the three phase legs carry the full phase current I. One FET conducts in each leg, either the active switch or the sync-rectifying FET.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

The hot on-resistance and conduction loss are:

$$R_{ds,hot} = k_T \cdot R_{ds(on),25} = 1.6 \times 0.9 = 1.44\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{ds,hot}$$

Six-step phase current is flat-topped, so I_rms ≈ I during burst:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.44\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{18.4\ W}$$

The current is shared over 6 FETs by commutation, but averaged per FET:

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx \tfrac{18.4}{6} \approx 3.1\ \text{W}$$

The worst-case FET is a high-side switch at duty near 1. It carries the full current for 1/3 of the electrical period, so it averages 2 × 80² × 1.44 mΩ / 3... more precisely 80² × 1.44 mΩ × (1/3) ≈ 3.1 W, with an instantaneous peak of 9.2 W.

PCB copper also dissipates. Assume 2 oz outer layer plus 2 oz inner, about 0.25 mΩ effective per current path:

$$P_{Cu} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.25\times10^{-3} \approx 3.2\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Switching (V·I Overlap) Loss

Only one leg is PWM-switching at a time (the active leg):

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 25\times10^{-9} \times 48\,000 = \mathbf{1.21\ W}$$

### 2.3 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\times10^{-9} \times 48\,000 = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.4 Reverse Recovery and C_oss

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 45\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.054\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate Drive

Two FETs are switched per PWM cycle, the active switch and its sync partner:

$$P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2 \times 55\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.053\ \text{W}$$

This loss lands in the driver IC, not the FET.

### 2.6 Per-Channel Total at 80 A Burst

| Term | Watts |
|---|---|
| MOSFET conduction | 18.4 |
| PCB copper | 3.2 |
| Switching overlap | 1.21 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.10 |
| Gate drive | 0.05 |
| **Total** | **≈ 23.6 W** |

Conduction dominates at 80 %. Raising f_sw from 24 kHz to 48 kHz costs only about 0.9 W extra. To shrink this budget, buy lower R_ds(on). Do not chase faster edges, which mostly worsen EMI and ringing.

### 2.7 General Formula

Use this form to re-run the numbers with real parts:

$$P_{ch}(I) = 2I^2\left(k_T R_{ds} + R_{Cu}\right) + \tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f + 2 V_f I t_{dt} f + (Q_{rr}+Q_{oss}) V f + 2 Q_g V_{drv} f$$

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Junction Rise Above the PCB

Assume per-FET R_θJC ≈ 1.2 K/W (5×6 package, bottom pad soldered to a 2 oz pour). The worst FET peaks at about 9.2 W instantaneous and averages about 3.6 W including its share of switching loss:

$$\Delta T_{j-pcb} \approx 3.6 \times 1.2 \approx 4.3\ \text{K}$$

The ripple at the commutation frequency is small, because the package thermal time constant (~ms) far exceeds the commutation period. The junction therefore tracks the board temperature, and **the board is the limit.**

### 3.2 Board Lumped-Capacitance Model

The first-order model is:

$$C_{th}\frac{dT}{dt} = P_{in} - \frac{T - T_a}{R_{th,sa}}$$

Estimates for a 30.5×30.5 board (scale down for 20×20):

**Thermal capacitance.**
- FR4: ~6 g × 1.1 J/gK ≈ 6.6 J/K
- Copper: ~4 g × 0.385 ≈ 1.5 J/K
- FETs and other parts: ~1 J/K
- Total: **C_th ≈ 9 J/K**

The quadrant around one ESC sees roughly 1/4 of this, about 2.3 J/K, if lateral spreading is weak over short bursts.

**Thermal resistance.**
- R_th,sa ≈ 4 K/W with strong prop-wash.
- R_th,sa ≈ 12 K/W in still air (hover inside a frame).

Steady-state rise for one channel at 80 A, even with prop-wash:

$$\Delta T = 23.6 \times 4 = 94\ \text{K}$$

**That is not survivable continuously.**

### 3.3 Burst Duration

Set limits of T_pcb,max = 105 °C (T_j ≈ 110–115 °C, with margin to 150 °C) and T_a = 35 °C. For short bursts the cooling term is minor relative to 23.6 W, so near-adiabatic holds.

**Single channel at 80 A, local quadrant, C ≈ 2.3 J/K:**

$$t \approx \frac{2.3 \times 70}{23.6} \approx \mathbf{6.8\ s}$$

**All four channels at 80 A**, whole board, C ≈ 9 J/K. P ≈ 94 W, plus battery-path copper loss of 320² × ~0.3 mΩ ≈ 31 W, so P ≈ 125 W:

$$t \approx \frac{9 \times 70}{125} \approx \mathbf{5\ s}$$
