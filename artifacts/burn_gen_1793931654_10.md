# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #31): System Architecture Model

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Feasibility Flags

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller (FC) MCU running ArduPilot. Each motor gets its own ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071 class) running AM32, BLHeli_32, or Bluejay. Bi-directional DShot600 links the two. The H743 does not commutate motors directly.

**Feasibility flags:**

| Issue | Assessment |
|---|---|
| 80 A on a "nano" (20×20 mm) board | Not achievable as a continuous rating. The model below supports roughly 1–4 s bursts only. Commercial 20×20 4-in-1s are typically rated 20–35 A continuous. A 30.5×30.5 mm board is the minimum for credible 60–80 A burst ratings. |
| "80 A" definition | Modeled as **phase current per motor**. Battery current is I_bat ≈ D·I_ph, which is lower. |
| MOSFET parameters | Representative 40 V PDFN3.3×3 class. **Replace with your actual BOM datasheet values.** All equations are parametric. |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R = 1.5 mΩ × (1 + 0.006 × 85) = **2.27 mΩ**

Because R depends on T_j, this is a positive-feedback loop. It is solved iteratively in Section 3.4.

### 1.2 6-step trapezoidal with synchronous complementary PWM

During each 60° sector, two phases conduct:
- The **PWM leg** alternates between the high-side FET (fraction D) and the low-side FET (fraction 1−D, synchronous rectification).
- The **static leg** has its low-side FET on continuously.

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2R\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2R = 2I^2R_{DS}(T_j)$$

The result is independent of duty cycle, which is the key insight.

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.27\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{29.0\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each FET conducts for 120° of each electrical cycle:
- **Average per FET:** 29.0 / 6 = **4.84 W**
- **Instantaneous peak** (high-side at D→1 during its window): 80² × 2.27 mΩ = **14.5 W**

The electrical period is short relative to the junction-to-case time constant (~1–3 ms for PDFN3.3). At 30,000 eRPM (500 Hz electrical, 2 ms period), junction ripple is about ±15–25% of the average-based rise. Use the peak value for T_j margin checks.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg per motor hard-switches at any instant.

**(a) V–I overlap (hard switching)**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(35\text{n})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.69\ W}$$

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction**

$$P_{DT} = V_F\,I\,(2t_{dead})\,f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(600\text{n})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.84\ W}$$

This is as large as the switching loss itself. Reducing dead time to 150 ns saves about 0.9 W per motor. This is the single cheapest optimization (AM32 dead-time setting), but you must verify shoot-through margin on a scope.
