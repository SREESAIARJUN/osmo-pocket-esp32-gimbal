# STM32H743 AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC): Power-Stage and DShot Architecture Model

## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility Warnings

**Architecture.** On every production AIO of this class, the H743 does not drive gates directly. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs (for example, AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32). Each ESC MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM. I model that split, because the H743 lacks the timer and ADC resources to run four sensorless 6-step drives alongside flight control.

**Baseline parameters (replace with your datasheet values):**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Bus voltage | 6S, V_bus = 25.2 V max. Requires 40 V FETs. |
| MOSFET | 40 V, DFN3.3×3.3. R_ds(on),25 = 1.0 mΩ @ Vgs = 10 V. Q_g = 50 nC, Q_gd = 8 nC, Q_rr = 40 nC, Q_oss = 40 nC. |
| Gate driver | ~1 A source/sink, giving t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 15 ns |
| Dead time | 100 ns. Body diode V_f = 0.8 V. |
| Drive mode | 6-step trapezoidal, synchronous rectification |
| Burst current | 80 A per motor |

> **Feasibility warning.** 80 A per motor (320 A aggregate) on a 20×20 or 25×25 mm nano board is a marketing burst figure, not a thermal rating. The math below shows it is sustainable for only seconds. Treat "80 A" as a ≤ 1–5 s transient limit and design firmware current limits accordingly.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

**Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification** (phase A high-side PWM at duty D, phase B low-side on):
- **On-time (D):** I flows through Q_AH, then Q_BL.
- **Off-time (1−D):** I flows through Q_AL (synchronous rectifier), then Q_BL.

At every instant, exactly two R_ds(on) are in series. Conduction loss is therefore independent of duty:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

**Temperature dependence:**

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0045 × 85) = **1.38 mΩ**

**Losses at 80 A:**
- Per channel: P_cond = 2 × 6400 × 1.38 m = **17.7 W**
- Four channels: **70.7 W**

**Per-FET distribution.** Each FET is the "static" low-side for 120° of the electrical cycle and the PWM or synchronous-rectifier device for another 120°. The average per FET is about 17.7 / 6 ≈ **2.95 W**. The instantaneous worst case is 80² × 1.38 m = **8.8 W** in the static low-side FET.

**Thermal runaway check.** dP/dT_j = I²R₂₅α must stay below 1/R_θ. With 8.8 W instantaneous loss and R_θ,eff ≈ 15 K/W per FET on the board, the loop gain is ≈ 0.04 × 15 × 0.45 ≈ 0.27. That is below 1, so the stage is stable, but it adds roughly 35 % to the temperature rise.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (f_sw = 48 kHz)

Only the PWM leg switches. The terms are:

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f = ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 30 ns × 48 k | 1.45 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f = 40 n × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.048 W |
| Coss | ½·Q_oss·V·f | 0.024 W |
| Dead-time diode | V_f·I·2t_dt·f = 0.8 × 80 × 200 n × 48 k | 0.61 W |
| Gate drive (driver dissipation) | Q_g·V_gs·f × 6 FETs | 0.14 W |
| **Total per channel** | | **≈ 2.3 W** |
| **Four channels** | | **≈ 9.1 W** |

**Rise and fall time estimate:**

$$t_{r,f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g} = \frac{Q_{gd}}{(V_{drv}-V_{plateau})/R_g}$$

With 8 nC and ~0.55 A, this gives about 15 ns. Doubling R_g roughly doubles the overlap term, so V–I overlap becomes about 2.9 W per channel.

**Optimization levers:**
- Dropping f_sw from 48 to 24 kHz halves all switching terms. The cost is more current ripple and acoustic noise.
- Adaptive dead time down to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel.
