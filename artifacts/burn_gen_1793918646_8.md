# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #56): Architecture, Loss Model, DShot Allocation and Netlists

## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility Constraints

Several facts constrain everything that follows.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In a 4-in-1 stack, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and generates DShot600. Each motor is commutated by its own ESC MCU. This design uses an AT32F421 running AM32, which supports 48 kHz PWM and bidirectional DShot. The "48 kHz switching" therefore belongs to the ESC MCUs, not the H743.
2. **"80 A burst" is read as per channel.** That means 320 A total from the battery, or about 7.1 kW at 22.2 V.
   - On a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board this is a short-duration rating, about 1 s.
   - The model below shows sustained capability is about 20 A per channel.
   - XT60/XT90 connectors, battery leads and the battery itself are the real limiters at 320 A.
3. **Component parameters are representative assumptions.** Substitute datasheet values for your selected parts. The equations are what matter.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model (6-step, complementary PWM)

Trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification (AM32 default) behaves as follows:

- At any instant, two phases carry current.
- In the PWM leg, the high-side (HS) conducts for duty D and the low-side (LS) conducts for 1−D (sync rectification).
- In the return leg, the LS conducts 100% of the time.

So at every instant the current flows through exactly two FET channels in series:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j) \quad \text{(independent of D under sync rect.)}$$

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\ \alpha \approx 0.005\,/\text{K}$$

At 80 A with T_j = 125 °C:

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 6400 \times 3.0\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ channel}$$

Add the trace and copper path. With 2 oz copper on 6 layers, the pad-to-motor-wire path is about 0.3 mΩ:

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega = 1.9\ \text{W}$$

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. That gives one hard-switched turn-on and one turn-off per period.

**Hard-switching V·I overlap:**
$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (2 edges per period):**
$$P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
