### 0. Architectural Assumptions

Several of the requirements conflict physically, so the baseline is fixed here and the consequences are flagged throughout. Every number below is parametric: substitute your actual MOSFET datasheet values into the equations.

> ⚠ **Key finding up front:** a nano-format board cannot dissipate 80 A × 4 continuously. The model below shows about 125 W of total loss at a 4-motor 80 A burst. That limits the burst to roughly 2–4 s from a 50 °C board, depending on airflow. The "80A" label is therefore only valid as a short-burst, per-motor rating.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step complementary PWM
At any commutation sector, two phases are active. During the PWM ON-time, current flows HS(phase A) → motor → LS(phase B). During OFF-time, synchronous rectification passes current through LS(A) → motor → LS(B).

In both cases the current passes through **exactly two conducting MOSFETs**. Conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle, to first order:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

The coefficient α is fitted to the typical ×1.5 rise at 125 °C. At T_j = 110 °C:

$$R = 1.6\,(1 + 0.005 \cdot 85) = 2.28\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Ripple contribution
The phase current ripple is set by PWM and motor inductance. For a typical 5″–7″ motor, L_ph-ph ≈ 20 µH. Worst case occurs at D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \cdot 0.25}{20\mu \cdot 48k} = 6.6\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{DC}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.6 \approx 6404\ \text{A}^2$$

The ripple term is under 0.1 %, so it is negligible. That is one benefit of 48 kHz.

### 1.4 Result at 80 A

$$P_{cond,motor} = 6404 \cdot 2 \cdot 2.28\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{29.2\ W}$$

**Per-FET distribution.** Each FET conducts about 1/3 of the electrical cycle. Low-side FETs also carry freewheel current, so the low-side FETs run hotter at low throttle.

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx 29.2/6 \approx 4.9\ \text{W}$$

**Instantaneous loss.** When a FET is conducting, its loss is I²R = 14.6 W. The die thermal time constant (≈1–5 ms) is comparable to the electrical period at low eRPM. So at **low-RPM/high-current (punch-out from hover, prop strike, stall)**, use the 14.6 W peak, not the average.

### 1.5 Copper/trace losses (often forgotten)
Assumptions:
- Phase pad and trace resistance ≈ 0.3 mΩ per path (2 oz, 4 layers paralleled)
- Bus input ≈ 0.5 mΩ
- Shunt: if a single bus shunt is used, 0.25 mΩ

$$P_{Cu,motor} \approx 80^2 \cdot (2 \cdot 0.3\text{m}) = 3.8\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{bus+shunt} = (4 \cdot 80 \cdot D_{eff})^2 \cdot 0.75\text{m}$$

With D_eff ≈ 0.85, the bus current is 272 A DC, giving **55 W**.

> ⚠ This is the real limiter. A 4×80 A simultaneous burst draws about 270 A of battery current through an XT60/XT90 pigtail and a nano PCB. Use:
> - Bus current rating of 4×80 A is unrealistic; specify **4×55 A continuous / 4×80 A single-motor burst**, OR
> - Use a sense-resistor-free design (per-motor current estimation in AM32), plus heavy bus copper (≥3 oz outer layers, solder-fill on power pads).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In 6-step complementary PWM, only **one half-bridge hard-switches** per sector. The other active phase is held static.

### 2.1 V-I overlap (hard switching)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 35\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.69\ W}$$

Here t_r and t_f are current/voltage transition times set by the gate drive. FD6288Q source/sink is about 1.5 A / 1.8 A. With R_g = 0–4.7 Ω, the dominant term is the Miller plateau:

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,(R_{drv}+R_g+R_{g,int})}{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction
Dead time t_dt = 100 ns (AM32 default range ≈ 50–150 ns). There are two transitions per period:

$$P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n}\cdot 25.2\cdot 48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive
This loss is dissipated mainly in the driver and R_g, not in the FET. There are 2 switching FETs per motor:

$$P_{gate} = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2\cdot 30\text{n}\cdot 10\cdot 48\text{k} = 0.029\ \text{W}$$
