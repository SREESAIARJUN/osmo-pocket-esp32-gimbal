# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80 A ESC (Job #25): Electro-Thermal and DShot Timing Architecture

## 0. Architecture Corrections

Three points in the brief need correcting before any math is meaningful.

1. **The H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC commutation MCU.** A 4-in-1 ESC that switches at 48 kHz uses one small motor-control MCU per channel (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32). The H743 runs ArduPilot and generates bidirectional DShot600 toward those MCUs. Bidirectional DShot only exists on the FC↔ESC link, so this split is mandatory, not optional.
2. **80 A per motor on a nano (20×20 / 25×25 mm) board is a burst rating only.** The math below shows about 108 W of total dissipation at full burst. With realistic board heat capacity, this is a burst of a few seconds, not a sustained rating.
3. **All MOSFET and driver parameters below are representative placeholders.** They must be replaced with the selected part's datasheet maximum values. The model is parametric, so substitute values and re-run it.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-Dependent Resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At the design junction temperature T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{DS}(125) = 1.2 \times 1.6 = 1.92\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Six-Step Trapezoidal with Synchronous Rectification

In every commutation state, exactly two FETs carry I_ph:

- **PWM on:** HS(A) and LS(B) conduct.
- **PWM off:** LS(A) and LS(B) conduct (freewheel).

This gives:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j) = 2(80)^2(1.92\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{24.6\ W}$$

Each FET conducts during a 120° window, so its average conduction loss is:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6} = I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}/3 \approx \mathbf{4.1\ W}$$

The instantaneous loss during that window is I²R = 12.3 W. This instantaneous figure is what drives the transient junction rise in §3.3.

### 1.3 Copper Path Loss (Do Not Ignore)

A 2 oz outer + 2 oz inner pour, 6 mm wide and 10 mm long per phase, has roughly 0.25 mΩ resistance:

$$P_{Cu} \approx 80^2 \times 0.25\text{ m}\Omega \times 2 \approx 3.2\text{ W per channel}$$

Mitigations:
- Use 3 oz or heavier copper.
- Stitch vias across multiple layers.
- Solder-tin exposed phase pads.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg PWMs in each commutation state. All of the following are per channel.

### 2.1 Hard-Switching Overlap Loss

Transition time:

$$t_r \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_g} = \frac{15\text{ nC}}{1.5\text{ A}} = 10\text{ ns}$$

Overlap loss:

$$P_{ov} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(20\text{ ns})(48\text{ k}) = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse Recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Output Capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.030\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

Two edges occur per period:

$$P_{dt} = V_f\,I_{ph}\,(2t_d)\,f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(400\text{ ns})(48\text{ k}) = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

This term is larger than the overlap loss. Cutting dead time to 100 ns (when the driver propagation-delay match allows it) halves it.

### 2.5 Gate Drive

Two FETs switch per active leg:

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2(40\text{n})(10)(48\text{k}) = 0.038\ \text{W}$$

This loss lands mostly in the gate resistors and driver, not in the FET.

### 2.6 Loss Budget at 80 A Burst

| Loss term | Per channel (W) | 4-in-1 total (W) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (T_j = 125 °C) | 24.6 | 98.3 |
| Switching overlap | 0.97 | 3.9 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 | 4.9 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.08 | 0.3 |
| Gate drive | 0.04 | 0.2 |
| **Semiconductor subtotal** | **26.9** | **107.6** |
| Copper (2 oz) | ~3.2 | ~12.8 |
| **Grand total** | **~30** |
