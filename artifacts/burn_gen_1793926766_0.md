## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous PWM

During each 60° commutation sector, two phases conduct. The current flows as follows:
- **PWM'd high-side FET** (phase A): conducts for fraction D of each PWM period.
- **Phase A low-side FET** (synchronous rectifier): conducts for 1 − D (minus dead time).
- **Phase B low-side FET**: fully on for the whole sector.

At any instant, exactly **two FETs carry I_ph**. This holds regardless of D (dead time is neglected here and handled in §2).

### 1.2 Per-channel conduction loss

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{ds}(T_j)\cdot\big[D + (1-D) + 1\big] = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds,25}\,k_T
$$

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.5 = \mathbf{19.2\ W}
$$

### 1.3 Per-FET RMS current

Each low-side FET is on for its own 120° window, plus (1 − D) of the adjacent 120° window when its phase is the PWM'd phase:

$$
I_{rms,LS} = I_{ph}\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{3} + \tfrac{1}{6}(1-D)}, \qquad I_{rms,HS} = I_{ph}\sqrt{\tfrac{D}{6}+\tfrac{1}{6}}
$$

The second expression assumes the high-side FET is either the PWM'd device or fully on, depending on the commutation scheme. With high-side-PWM-only schemes, the high side sees D/3.

At D = 0.9 (full-throttle burst):
- Worst-case low-side FET: I_rms ≈ 80·√(0.35) ≈ **47 A**.
- Loss in that FET: 47² × 1.5 mΩ ≈ **3.3 W**.

### 1.4 PCB copper losses

Take a phase trace that is 5 mm wide, 10 mm long, on 4 paralleled 2 oz layers (70 µm each):

$$
R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{A} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot 0.010}{0.005\cdot 70\times10^{-6}\cdot 4} = 0.123\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

- Effective copper path per channel (bus + phase + return) ≈ 0.3 mΩ.
- Loss: 80² × 0.3 mΩ ≈ **1.9 W per channel**.
- Raise the copper resistance by about 30% at 100 °C (α_Cu = 0.0039/K).

### 1.5 Current-sense shunt (if one is fitted per channel)

- R_sh = 0.25 mΩ, carrying battery current I_bat ≈ D·I_ph ≈ 72 A.
- Loss: 72² × 0.25 mΩ ≈ **1.3 W**.

Many 4-in-1 boards use a single shared shunt instead. In that case the shunt carries the sum of all four battery currents.
