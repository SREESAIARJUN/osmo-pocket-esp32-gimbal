# STM32H743 Nano AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC): Design Model, Job #17

**Document class:** Architecture and analysis baseline (Rev A)
**Scope:** Phase-leg power loss model at 80 A burst, 48 kHz switching loss model, thermal feasibility, ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 timer/DMA plan, and key-block netlists.

---

## 0. Executive Findings

1. **The STM32H743 is the flight controller MCU, not the ESC MCU.** In a 4-in-1 AIO, ArduPilot runs on the H743 and sends DShot600 to four (or one quad-channel) ESC MCUs, typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs, so this document models both domains separately.
2. **An 80 A per-motor burst on a nano (20×20 / 25.5×25.5 mm) board is thermally limited to about 1–3 seconds**, and only from a cool start. Steady-state 80 A is not achievable. At full 4×80 A burst, total dissipation is about 90–95 W. A nano board's heat-sinking capability is roughly 5–15 W continuous with prop-wash.
3. **Recommended labeling:** "80 A burst (≤1 s, single-motor transient) / 40 A continuous." Anything stronger would not survive design review or a customer return analysis.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Per-FET resistance at temperature

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_0\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right] \approx R_0 \cdot k_T$$

With k_T = 1.6 at 125 °C:

$$R_{DS}(125^\circ C) = 1.6\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 2.2 Phase-leg conduction under synchronous PWM

For the PWM leg at duty D, the high-side FET conducts for D and the low-side FET conducts for (1−D):

$$P_{leg} = D\,I^2 R_{DS} + (1-D)\,I^2 R_{DS} = I^2 R_{DS}$$

Synchronous rectification makes leg loss **independent of duty**. The other active leg is held statically on, also at I²R. So:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 2.3 Numbers at I = 80 A

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.6\times10^{-3} = 20.48\ \text{W}$$

Worst single FET (the statically-on leg): 80² × 1.6 mΩ = **10.24 W**.

### 2.4 Additions often missed

- **PCB copper resistance.** Use 2 oz outer layers plus 2 oz inner layers, with a phase-pad-to-motor-pad path of roughly 0.3 mΩ effective. That adds 2 × 80² × 0.3 mΩ ≈ **3.84 W per motor**.
- **Current ripple.** For a motor with L ≈ 15 µH:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus} D (1-D)}{L f_{sw}}$$

At D = 0.5 this is ≈ 8.75 A. The RMS correction is $I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \Delta I_{pp}^2/12 \Rightarrow +0.1\%$, which is negligible.

**Conduction subtotal ≈ 24.3 W per motor at 80 A, hot.**

## 3. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the PWM leg switches. Hard switching is on the high side, and the low side commutates through its body diode during dead time.

### 3.1 V–I overlap loss

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(40\times10^{-9})(48\times10^3) = 1.94\ \text{W}$$

### 3.2 Output capacitance loss

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss(er)} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} \times 2 = 0.5(1.5\times10^{-9})(635)(48\times10^3)\times 2 = 0.046\ \text{W}$$

### 3.3 Reverse-recovery loss

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\times10^{-9}(25.2)(48\times10^3) = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

Q_rr also induces extra turn-on current in the high-side FET. Budget +0.1 W.

### 3.4 Dead-time diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f I (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(200\times10^{-9})(48\times10^3) = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

### 3.5 Gate-drive loss

This loss lands in the driver, not the FET:

$$P_g = Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 60\times10^{-9}(10)(48\times10^3) = 0.029\ \text{W per switching FET}$$

Over 2 switching FETs that is ≈ 0.06 W per motor.

### 3.6 Switching subtotal

$$P_{sw,motor} \approx 1.94 + 0.05 + 0.16 + 0.61 + 0.06 \approx 2.8\ \text{W}$$

### 3.7 Frequency sensitivity

$$P_{sw} \propto f_{sw}$$

| f_sw | P_sw per motor |
|---|---|
| 24 kHz | 1.4 W |
| 48 kHz | 2.8 W |
| 96 kHz | 5.6 W |

At 80 A, conduction dominates (~90%). 48 kHz is a reasonable choice, because the gain in acoustic and current-ripple performance outweighs the ~1.4 W penalty versus 24 kHz.

## 4. Total Power Budget

| Item | Per motor @ 80 A | 4 motors @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (hot) | 20.5 W | 82.0 W |
| PCB copper | 3.8 W | 15.4 W |
| Switching (48 kHz) | 2.8 W | 11.2 W |
| Shunt/sense (0.25 mΩ shunt, if used) | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 28.7 W** | **≈ 115 W** |

Without the PCB copper and shunt, the FET-only total is **≈ 93 W**.

Efficiency check: P_out ≈ 22.2 V × 80 A × 4 ≈ 7.1 kW, so η ≈ 98.4%. The efficiency is fine. **Heat density is the problem:** 115 W over about 6.5 cm² is roughly 18 W/cm².

## 5. Thermal Model

### 5.1 Steady-state network (per FET, worst FET at 10.24 W)

```
T_j ──[R_θJC ≈ 1.2 K/W]── T_case/pad ──[R_θ,spread ≈ 6 K/W shared]── T_board ──[R_θ,BA]── T_amb
```

Board-to-ambient for a 20×20 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz board:

| Condition | R_θBA |
|---|---|
| Still air | ≈ 15 K/W |
| Prop-wash, ~5 m/s | ≈ 4 K/W |
| With 1 g Al clip heatsink, prop-wash | ≈ 2.5 K/W |

Steady state at a 115 W board loss:

$$\Delta T_{board} = 115 \times 4 = 460\ \text{K}$$

**This is impossible.** Steady 4×80 A is infeasible. The continuous rating is set by T_board ≤ 100 °C at T_amb = 35 °C:

$$P_{max,cont} = \frac{65}{4} \approx 16\ \text{W total} \;\Rightarrow\; I_{cont} \approx 80\sqrt{16/115} \approx 30\ \text{A per motor (all 4)}$$

With a heatsink clip (R_θBA = 2.5 K/W): P ≈ 26 W, giving **≈ 38 A continuous on all 4 motors**.
