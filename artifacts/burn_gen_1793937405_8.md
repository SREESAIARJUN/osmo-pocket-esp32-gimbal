# STM32H743 Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #56): Architecture, Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and DShot DMA Plan

## 0. Architecture Decisions and Feasibility Flags

Four issues change the design and should be settled first.

1. **Bidirectional DShot implies separate ESC MCUs.** DShot is a link between the flight controller and the ESC controllers. If the H743 drove the gate drivers directly, there would be no DShot link at all. This design therefore uses:
   - **H743**: ArduPilot, sends DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry.
   - **4× AT32F421K8U7**: AM32 firmware, one per motor. Each drives a 3-phase gate driver and six MOSFETs.
2. **80 A burst cannot be simultaneous on all four channels on a nano board.** At full throttle the battery current would approach 4 × 80 A × D ≈ 290 A. That exceeds any XT60/XT90 pigtail, solder pad, or 2 oz plane on a 30 × 30 mm board. The spec should be read as:
   - **80 A per channel**, for **≤ 5 s**
   - **Aggregate battery current ≤ 160 A** for ≤ 5 s
   - Enforced by the ESC current limit and ArduPilot `BATT_MAX_AMPS`-style limiting.
3. **Form factor.** "Nano" (20 × 20 mm) cannot hold 24 power FETs in 5 × 6 mm packages. The thermal model below assumes **30.5 × 30.5 mm mounting, about 36 × 36 mm board**. If the form factor must be 20 × 20, the 80 A target is not achievable and should be dropped to about 35 A.
4. **Battery voltage and MOSFET rating.** The design assumes **6S, 25.2 V maximum**. 40 V FETs work only with tight layout and snubbers (see §4.3). **60 V FETs are recommended** if Rds(on) allows.
