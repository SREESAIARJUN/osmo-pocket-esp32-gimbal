# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #10): Power-Stage, Thermal, and Bidirectional DShot Architecture

## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Feasibility Verdict

Two scope points need settling before the math.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** Bidirectional DShot is a protocol between a flight controller and separate ESC MCUs. The architecture is therefore:

- **FC:** STM32H743, running ArduPilot.
- **ESC:** 4× ESC MCUs (e.g., STM32G071 running AM32, or AT32F421 running BLHeli_32).
- **Gate drive and switching:** 4× 3-phase gate drivers and 24× MOSFETs. The ESC MCUs produce the 48 kHz PWM.

**2. 80 A burst per motor on a nano (20×20 mm) board is not thermally viable.** The model below shows why. It is valid only as a burst rating measured in tenths of a second at full current. Sustained capability on 20×20 is roughly 15–25 A per motor. The recommendation in §7 is either:

- move to a 30.5×30.5 mm form factor with 5×6 mm PowerPAK FETs, or
- derate the rating to about 45 A burst.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

In 6-step complementary PWM, the phase current always flows through exactly two conducting channels:

- During the on-time: the high-side FET of phase A and the low-side FET of phase B.
- During the off-time: the low-side FET of phase A (synchronous rectification) and the low-side FET of phase B.

The conduction loss is therefore essentially independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds,hot}$$

Temperature dependence of on-resistance:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At 80 A and 1.12 mΩ:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \cdot 6400 \cdot 1.12\text{m} = \mathbf{14.3\ W/motor}$$

**Distribution across devices.** Rotating through the 6 commutation states, each of the 6 FETs carries current for about 1/3 of the electrical period. The worst-case device is the low-side FET of the PWM'd leg during low-duty operation:

$$P_{FET,max} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}(I^2 R)(1 + (1-D)) \approx 3.6\text{–}4.8\ \text{W per device}$$

**Electro-thermal coupling.** R_ds is itself a function of temperature, so solve iteratively:

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + P(T_j)\,R_{\theta,jb}$$

This converges if

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\,R_\theta = \alpha\, P_{25}\, R_\theta < 1$$

The condition fails, causing thermal runaway, when R_θ,jb exceeds about 1/(0.006 × 4) ≈ 40 K/W. Single DFN3.3 FETs on poor via arrays approach this limit.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only one leg is hard-switched at a time. Per motor:

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·40 ns·48 k | 1.94 W |
| C_oss discharge | ½·C_oss·V²·f_sw = ½·1.5 n·635·48 k | 0.023 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 60 n·25.2·48 k | 0.073 W |
| Dead-time diode | V_f·I·2·t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·200 n·48 k | 0.61 W |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw = 2·60 n·10·48 k | 0.058 W (lost in driver/R_g) |
| **Switching total** | | **≈ 2.7 W** |

**Frequency sensitivity:**

$$\frac{\partial P_{sw}}{\partial f_{sw}} \approx 54\ \mu\text{W/Hz}$$

Moving to 24 kHz saves about 1.3 W per motor. The cost is higher current ripple. Ripple for a typical 5" motor with L_ph-ph ≈ 20 µH, at D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I = \frac{V(1-D)D}{L\, f_{sw}} \approx 6.6\ \text{A at 48 kHz}$$

## 3. Total Board Dissipation at 80 A Burst

| Source | Per motor | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 14.3 W | 57.2 W |
| Switching + dead time | 2.7 W | 10.8 W |
| Shunt (0.5 mΩ, battery-side, at D ≈ 0.8, I_bat ≈ 64 A) | 2.0 W | 8.2 W |
| Copper (phase pads + bus, ~0.3 mΩ effective) | 1.9 W | 7.7 W |
| **Total** | **~21 W** | **~84 W** |

The H743 itself (~0.5 W) and the 3.3 V/5 V/10 V regulators (~1.5 W) are negligible by comparison.
