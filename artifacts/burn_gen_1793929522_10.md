# Job #39: STM32H743 Nano AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC)
## System Architecture, Loss Model, DShot Timer Plan, and Reference Netlist

## 0. Ground Rules and Architecture Correction

**Architecture decision.** The STM32H743 should run ArduPilot only. It should **not** commutate the motors. The industry-proven split, which this design uses, is:

| Domain | Device | Firmware | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIH6 (TFBGA100, 8×8 mm) | ArduPilot (ChibiOS) | Attitude control, bidirectional DShot600 master |
| ESC ×4 | AT32F421K8U7 (QFN32, 120 MHz), one per motor | AM32 | 48 kHz complementary PWM, sensorless commutation, eRPM telemetry |

Putting four sensorless field-commutation loops and a 400 Hz+ attitude stack on one H743 would wreck ArduPilot's timing guarantees, and ArduPilot does not support it. The rest of this document assumes the split above.

**Assumed parameters.** Replace these with datasheet values before release.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | V_bus | 6S, 25.2 V max (22.2 V nominal) |
| MOSFET | — | 40 V N-ch, PQFN 3.3×3.3 class |
| On-resistance | R_ds(on),25 | 1.0 mΩ @ V_gs = 10 V |
| R_ds(on) tempco | k_T | ≈ 1.55× at T_j = 125 °C |

**Feasibility flag up front.** 80 A per motor on a 20×20 mm-hole nano board is a **seconds-scale burst rating only**. Section 3 quantifies this. Market it as "80 A / 2 s burst" or the spec is not defensible.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path
The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. At any instant, exactly one high-side FET and one low-side FET carry the phase current I. During the PWM off-time, the active high-side's complementary low-side FET takes over the freewheel current.

The conduction path therefore always contains **two R_ds(on) in series**, independent of duty cycle D (dead time is neglected here and handled in §2).

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C, R_ds = 1.55 mΩ.

### 1.3 Per-ESC conduction loss

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per ESC @ 80 A | ×4 ESCs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W | 51.2 W |
| 125 °C | 1.55 mΩ | **19.8 W** | **79.4 W** |

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution
Each phase leg conducts for 120° high-side plus 120° low-side per electrical cycle. Averaged over the cycle, each of the six FETs dissipates about P_cond/6 ≈ **3.3 W** at 125 °C.

At high eRPM the commutation period is short (around 100 µs at 100k eRPM), which is far below the FET's thermal time constant. The average value therefore governs the thermal design.

### 1.5 PCB copper loss
This loss is non-negligible at 80 A and must be included. Assume:
- 8-layer board, 2 oz (70 µm) copper
- Phase and bus current shared over 4 layers
- Effective path 15 mm long × 4 mm wide

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{w\,t\,n} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot0.015}{0.004\cdot70\times10^{-6}\cdot4} \approx 0.23\ \text{m}\Omega$$

The bus feed (+ and −) and the phase output (in and out) give roughly 3 such segments in path, about 0.7 mΩ total. This yields about **4.5 W per ESC at 80 A**.

The bus copper is shared by all four motors, so its loss scales with total current squared. Treat **≈ 10–15 W board-wide** as the realistic figure at 4×80 A.

### 1.6 Shunt loss
Assume a single 0.25 mΩ bus shunt carrying 320 A, where 320 A is the bus current at D = 1.

$$P_{shunt} = 320^2 \cdot 0.25\ \text{m}\Omega = 25.6\ \text{W}$$

**This is unacceptable.** Bus current is actually I_ph·D, so typical burst at D ≈ 0.8 is about 256 A and gives ~16 W, which is still too much.

**Decision:** use a **0.1 mΩ shunt** (2512 or a 3-terminal Kelvin metal strip). This gives about 6.5 W at 256 A, and still drops to roughly 4 W if bus current is limited to 200 A. Also add a bus-current limit (see §3.4).

## 2. Switching Loss Model @ 48 kHz

### 2.1 Hard-switching (V–I overlap) loss
Only the PWM'd high-side FET hard-switches. The synchronous low-side turns on after dead time with its body diode already conducting, which is near-ZVS.

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2}V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = \tfrac12 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 30\,\text{ns} \cdot 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

Edge-time sanity check against the driver:

$$t \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{drv}} = \frac{6\,\text{nC}}{0.4\,\text{A (Miller-plateau current)}} \approx 15\,\text{ns}\ ✔$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\,\text{ns} \cdot 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 20\,\text{nC} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\,\text{kHz} = 0.024\ \text{W}$$
