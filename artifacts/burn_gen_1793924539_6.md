# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #13): Architecture, Loss Model, DShot/DMA Allocation, Netlists

## 0. Feasibility Findings

Three findings shape the rest of this document.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** A production-grade 4-in-1 runs one dedicated ESC MCU per channel (AT32F421 running AM32 here). The H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600. The 48 kHz switching happens in the AT32s. Running four six-step or FOC loops at 48 kHz on the H743 alongside ArduPilot's EKF and scheduler is not supported by ArduPilot and would be fragile.
2. **"80 A burst per channel" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is not thermally achievable in a sustained sense.** The model below shows about 100 W of dissipation at 4×80 A. On roughly 3 J/K of thermal mass, that is safe only for bursts of about 1–2 s. A sustained rating on 20×20 is about 35–45 A per channel.
3. **Recommended configuration:**
   - 30.5×30.5 mm (or 25.5 whoop-pattern) stack
   - 80 A rating at 10 s, 55 A continuous with airflow
   - 6S maximum (25.2 V)

All numbers below are parametric. Substitute your actual MOSFET datasheet values.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Current path

With trapezoidal six-step and synchronous (complementary) PWM, at any instant the motor current I flows through exactly two conducting devices: the PWM leg (HS during on-time, LS during off-time) and the static LS of the return leg. Therefore:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph,rms}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 2.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\text{–}0.007\ \mathrm{K^{-1}}$$

**Assumed device:** 40 V, R₂₅ = 1.2 mΩ, PDFN 3.3×3.3, Q_g ≈ 30 nC, Q_gd ≈ 8 nC, Q_rr ≈ 50 nC, Q_oss ≈ 40 nC.

At T_j = 110 °C with α = 0.006: R = 1.2 × 1.51 ≈ **1.81 mΩ**.

### 2.3 Numbers at 80 A

| Quantity | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Per-FET-path loss | 80² × 1.81 mΩ | **11.6 W** |
| Per channel | ×2 | **23.2 W** |
| 4 channels | ×4 | **92.7 W** |
| Shunt (single, 320 A total) | 320² × 0.25 mΩ | **25.6 W** ⚠ |

The shunt row shows that one shared shunt is untenable at full burst. **Fix:** use 4× per-channel 0.5 mΩ shunts in the low-side returns (80² × 0.5 mΩ = 3.2 W each, distributed), or use a Hall sensor (e.g., ACS770 / TMCS1100-class, about 0.1 mΩ).

### 2.4 Electrothermal stability (runaway criterion)

$$T_j = T_a + R_{th}\cdot 2I^2R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Solving gives:

$$T_j = \frac{T_a + R_{th}\,2I^2R_{25}(1-25\alpha)}{1 - R_{th}\,2I^2R_{25}\,\alpha}$$

The system runs away when the denominator reaches zero:

$$R_{th}\cdot 2I^2R_{25}\alpha \ge 1$$

At 80 A: 2·6400·1.2e-3·0.006 = 0.092, so steady-state runaway occurs at **R_th ≥ 10.8 K/W per channel**. A nano board with airflow is about 8–15 K/W per channel. This confirms that 80 A is a **transient-only** rating on small boards.

## 3. Switching Losses at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Assumptions: V_bus = 25.2 V, I = 80 A, gate driver about 1.5 A source / 1.8 A sink, R_g,ext = 2.2 Ω.

**Transition times:**

$$t_{r,f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{gate}} \approx \frac{8\,\text{nC}}{0.8\,\text{A}} \approx 10\,\text{ns each}$$

**Loss terms per channel:**

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f = ½·25.2·80·20e-9·48e3 | 0.97 W |
| Dead-time body diode (t_dt = 100 ns, V_f = 0.8 V) | V_f·I·2t_dt·f | 0.61 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f | 0.06 W |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss·V·f | 0.05 W |
| Gate charge (2 FETs on PWM leg, 10 V drive) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f | 0.03 W* |
| **P_sw per channel** | | **≈ 1.72 W** |

*The gate-charge loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistor, not the FET.

**Per-channel and board totals at 80 A:**

$$P_{ch} = 23.2 + 1.7 \approx 24.9\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{board} \approx 99.6\ \text{W} + \text{shunts} + \text{BEC (}\sim 1.5\ \text{W)}$$

**Design levers:**
- Dead-time is the largest switching term. Trimming to 50 ns with AM32 `dead_time` saves about 0.3 W per channel. Validate shoot-through on a scope first.
- Moving from 48 kHz to 24 kHz halves P_sw but increases ripple current and acoustic noise. 48 kHz is justified for low-inductance (high-KV) motors.
