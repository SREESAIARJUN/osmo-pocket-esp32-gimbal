**Correction 1: who commutates the motors.** The H743 does not commutate the motors in ArduPilot. ArduPilot has no on-FC BLDC commutation. A "4-in-1 ESC" therefore means four dedicated ESC MCUs (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32-compatible firmware), one per channel. The H743 only emits bidirectional DShot600 to those MCUs and decodes their eRPM replies. The architecture below reflects this.

**Correction 2: feasibility of the burst rating.** 80 A per channel on a 20×20 board is at the edge of physical feasibility. The math in §3 shows it is a ~1 to 2 second burst rating, not a sustained rating. Commercial 20×20 4-in-1s rated "80 A" use the same marketing convention, so the board datasheet must state the burst duration explicitly.

Two physical options exist:
- **Stacked:** a 20×20 FC on top of a 20×20 ESC. This is recommended for thermal reasons.
- **True single-PCB AIO:** realistically requires 25.5×25.5 mm or larger to fit 24 FETs plus the H743 in LQFP100/TFBGA.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ /^\circ\mathrm{C}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.2 mΩ × 1.45 = **1.74 mΩ**.

### 2.2 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

At any instant, phase current flows through exactly two MOSFETs:
- the PWM leg, which is the high-side during D and the synchronous low-side during (1 − D);
- the low-side of the return leg, which is continuously on.

So:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph,rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$
