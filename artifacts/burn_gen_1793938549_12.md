## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step with synchronous rectification

At any instant, two phases carry current I and one floats. Each phase leg spends 120° electrical "high," 120° "low," and 120° floating.

- **"High" interval:** the HS FET conducts for duty D. The complementary LS FET conducts for (1 − D).
- **"Low" interval:** the LS FET conducts 100%.

Per-FET RMS-squared current, averaged over one electrical cycle:

$$I_{HS,rms}^2 = \frac{D}{3}I^2 \qquad I_{LS,rms}^2 = \frac{(1-D)+1}{3}I^2 = \frac{2-D}{3}I^2$$

Current always flows through exactly two conducting channels in series (source leg plus return leg). The motor-level conduction loss is therefore independent of D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j) \quad (+\,I^2 R_{Cu} + I^2 R_{shunt})$$

### 1.2 Temperature-coupled solution

R_ds depends on T_j, and T_j depends on the losses, so solve by fixed-point iteration:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)], \qquad T_j^{(n+1)} = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}(T_j^{(n)})\cdot R_{\theta,j\text{-}pcb}$$

It converges in 3–4 iterations because α·P·Rθ ≪ 1. The design point below uses T_j = 125 °C, giving R_ds = 1.4 mΩ.
