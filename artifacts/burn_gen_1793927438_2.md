## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 0.70 × 1.6 = **1.12 mΩ**.

Because R depends on T_j and T_j depends on loss, this is an implicit loop. Solve it iteratively (see §4.4).

### 1.2 Six-step trapezoidal with complementary (synchronous) PWM

During any commutation sector:
- **Switching phase:** the high side conducts for D and the low side (synchronous rectification) for 1−D.
- **Return phase:** the low side is fully on.

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{DS}\left[D + (1-D)\right] + I^2 R_{DS} = 2 I^2 R_{DS}$$

The result is independent of duty cycle. With ripple, replace I² by I_rms²:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp} \approx \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

For a low-inductance 2207-class motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH, worst case D = 0.5):
ΔI_pp ≈ 25.2 × 0.25 / (15e-6 × 48e3) ≈ 8.75 A, which adds about 6.4 A² (0.1 %). This is negligible at 80 A.

**At 80 A, T_j = 125 °C:**

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.12\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{14.3\ W}$$

### 1.3 Copper and shunt losses (per channel)

| Element | Resistance | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase pad + pours (2 oz, ~8 mm, 5 mm wide) | ~0.10 mΩ ×2 phases | 1.28 W |
| Battery pour share | ~0.05 mΩ | 0.32 W |
| Current shunt (if per-channel, 0.25 mΩ) | 0.25 mΩ | 1.60 W |

ESC firmware usually measures total current with one shunt. Charge that shunt as 0.25 mΩ × (ΣI)² = 0.25e-3 × 320² = **25.6 W** at all-motor burst. That is a serious hotspot. Either use 0.1 mΩ with a gain-of-50 to 100 amplifier, or use per-channel phase sensing.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per channel, one leg is hard-switching at any instant.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard-switched high side)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

The transition times are set by the gate driver plateau current:
$$t_{r,f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g}}, \quad I_{g} = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}{R_{g,ext} + R_{g,int} + R_{drv}}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (low-side body diode, after dead time)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

Q_rr scales with di/dt and I_F, so derate upward ×1.5 to ×2 at 80 A. That gives **~0.08 W**.
