## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Feasibility Check

The architecture assumes the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot. It sends bi-directional DShot600 to four separate ESC MCUs (AT32F421 or STM32G071 class, running AM32 or BLHeli_32-compatible firmware). Each ESC MCU drives one 3-phase bridge.

**The H743 must not drive the bridges directly.** Commutation, back-EMF zero-cross detection and 48 kHz complementary PWM belong on the per-motor ESC MCU.

| Parameter | Value used | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max | Calculations use 25.2 V as worst case |
| Burst current | 80 A per channel, phase RMS at near-full duty | ≈ battery current per channel at D→1 |
| Continuous target | Derived below (it is much lower than 80 A) | |
| f_sw | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous) PWM | |
| MOSFET | 40 V N-ch, PQFN 5×6 (or 3.3×3.3 dual-cool) | R_DS(on),25 = 0.80 mΩ @ V_GS = 10 V |
| Gate driver | FD6288Q-class 3-phase driver | |
| PCB | 30.5×30.5 or 20×20-hole board, 8 layers, 2 oz outer and inner power planes | |

**Feasibility flag (the most important finding).** 80 A per channel is only achievable on a nano-class board as a **time-limited burst** of a few seconds. The calculations in §3 show ~80 W total board dissipation at full burst against a board-to-air resistance of ~4–6 K/W.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current flow in a 6-step synchronous bridge

During any 60° commutation sector, two legs are active:
- **Fixed leg:** one FET is held fully ON (e.g., low-side of phase B).
- **PWM leg:** high-side and low-side alternate. The high-side conducts for D, the low-side (synchronous rectifier) for (1−D), with dead time between.

The motor current therefore always flows through **exactly two channels**. Ignoring dead time, this holds regardless of duty:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot \left[R_{fixed}(T) + D\,R_{HS}(T) + (1-D)\,R_{LS}(T)\right] + I_{ph,rms}^2 R_{Cu}$$

With matched FETs this reduces to:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \left(2R_{DS}(T_j) + R_{Cu}\right)$$

### 1.2 Ripple correction

Phase current ripple at 48 kHz (L_ph = phase-to-phase inductance, typically 15–40 µH for 5–7" motors):

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}, \qquad I_{rms}^2 = I_{avg}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}$$

At high throttle with a 20 µH motor, ΔI_pp ≈ 3–6 A. The ripple term adds <0.1% to I², so it is negligible at 80 A. It only matters at low throttle with low-inductance motors.

### 1.3 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on) (electro-thermal coupling)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \mathrm{K^{-1}}$$

| T_j | R_DS(on) |
|---|---|
| 25 °C | 0.80 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 1.07 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 1.16 mΩ |

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A (T_j = 125 °C, R_Cu = 0.25 mΩ)

R_Cu covers the bus plane, phase pad and via-stitching path per channel.

$$P_{cond,ch} = 80^2 \times (2 \times 1.16 + 0.25)\times10^{-3} = 6400 \times 2.57\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{16.4\ W}$$

Split by element:
- FETs: 6400 × 2.32 mΩ = **14.8 W**
- Copper: **1.6 W**

**Per-FET average.** Each FET carries the "fixed" role for 120° electrical and the PWM role for part of the cycle. Averaged over a full electrical revolution, the 14.8 W spreads across 6 FETs, giving ≈ **2.5 W average per FET**. The instantaneous peak is ≈ 7.4 W in the fixed-ON FET.

The thermal time constant of a PQFN die and pad (~10–50 ms) is long compared with the electrical period at operating eRPM (≈ 0.3–1 ms). The **average** figure therefore governs T_j, plus a small ripple component.

### 1.5 Thermal runaway stability criterion

A fixed point exists only if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\cdot R_{th,ja} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS,25}\,\alpha \cdot R_{th,ja} < 1$$

At 80 A: 6400 × 1.6e-3 × 0.0045 = 0.046 W/K per channel. Against ~20 K/W effective per-channel board-to-air resistance (four channels sharing ~5 K/W), the loop gain is ≈ 0.92.

**This is marginally stable at best.** Steady-state 80 A is not a valid operating point. It must be handled as a burst with firmware current and temperature limiting.
