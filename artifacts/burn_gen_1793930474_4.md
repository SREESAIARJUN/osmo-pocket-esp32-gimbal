# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #15): Architecture, Loss Model, DShot Timing, and Netlists

## 0. Scope and Feasibility Issues

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs directly. It is the flight controller and sends bi-directional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs. Each ESC MCU (assumed AT32F421 running AM32, the common choice) handles commutation at 48 kHz through a triple half-bridge gate driver. The H743 timer and DMA plan in §4 covers the DShot links. The loss model in §2 covers the ESC power stage.

**"80A burst" on a nano-format board.** On a 20×20 mm board, 80A is credible only as a short transient of roughly 1–2 s, set by board heat capacity. The model in §3 puts realistic continuous current at **about 25A per motor**. Any 80A marketing figure should state a burst duration.

**Assumptions.** These drive every result below. Replace them with datasheet values for the actual parts.

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Bus | 6S, 25.2 V max (worst case for switching), 22.2 V nominal |
| MOSFET | 40 V, PDFN 3.3×3.3, R<sub>DS(on)</sub> = 1.2 mΩ max @ 25 °C, Q<sub>g</sub> = 40 nC, Q<sub>gs2</sub> + Q<sub>gd</sub> = 9 nC, Q<sub>rr</sub> = 40 nC, Q<sub>oss</sub> = 30 nC, V<sub>SD</sub> = 0.8 V |
| R<sub>DS(on)</sub> temperature factor | k(T) ≈ 1 + 0.005·(T<sub>j</sub> − 25) → ×1.5 @ 125 °C |
| Commutation | Trapezoidal 120°, complementary (synchronous) PWM, f<sub>sw</sub> = 48 kHz, dead time t<sub>d</sub> = 200 ns |
| Topology | One FET per switch position, 24 FETs total |

## 1. Current Path Model (One ESC Channel)

In 120° trapezoidal drive, two phases conduct and one floats. During each PWM period:

- **On-time:** current flows through HS(A), the motor, then LS(B).
- **Off-time (synchronous):** current flows through LS(A), the motor, then LS(B).
- **Dead time:** current flows through the body diode of LS(A), then LS(B).

So the phase current I always passes through **two conducting FETs**, independent of duty cycle. Motor current ripple is neglected because phase inductance is in the µH range and 48 kHz is high, so I<sub>rms</sub> ≈ I<sub>avg</sub>.

## 2. Loss Equations

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + I^2 R_{Cu}$$

At I = 80 A and T<sub>j</sub> ≈ 125 °C (R<sub>DS</sub> = 1.8 mΩ), with copper and solder path R<sub>Cu</sub> ≈ 0.3 mΩ:

- FET term: 2 × 6400 × 0.0018 = **23.0 W**
- Copper term: 6400 × 0.0003 = 1.9 W
- **P<sub>cond</sub> ≈ 24.9 W per channel**

The two conducting FETs get about **11.5 W each**, averaged over the commutation steps that use them.

### 2.2 Switching Loss (Hard-Switched Leg Only)

**Gate drive current:**
I<sub>g</sub> = (V<sub>drv</sub> − V<sub>plateau</sub>) / (R<sub>g,ext</sub> + R<sub>g,int</sub>) = (10 − 3) / (4.7 + 1.0) ≈ 1.23 A

**Edge time:**
t<sub>edge</sub> = (Q<sub>gs2</sub> + Q<sub>gd</sub>) / I<sub>g</sub> ≈ 7.3 ns ideal. Use **15 ns** to account for layout inductance and ringing.

**V–I overlap loss:**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V\,I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V\,f_{sw} = 40\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{ k} = 0.048\ W$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,Q_{oss}\,V\,f_{sw} = 0.018\ W$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction:**

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\,I\,(2t_d)\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

**Gate charge** (dissipated mostly in the driver and R<sub>g</sub>, not the FET):

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g\,V_{drv}\,f_{sw} = 0.038\ W$$

**Total switching-related loss: P<sub>sw</sub> ≈ 2.78 W per channel.** Dead time is nearly as large as the V–I overlap term. Trimming dead time to about 100 ns, if the driver allows it, recovers about 0.6 W per channel.

### 2.3 Totals at 80A Burst

| Quantity | Per channel | 4-in-1 |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 24.9 W | 99.6 W |
| Switching + dead time | 2.8 W | 11.1 W |
| **Total** | **27.7 W** | **≈111 W** |

**Efficiency at full throttle:** P<sub>out</sub> ≈ 4 × 22.2 × 80 ≈ 7.1 kW, so η<sub>stage</sub> ≈ 98.4%. Efficiency is not the problem. Power density is: 111 W in a 4 cm² board.

- 20×20 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz copper, about 3.5 g including components
