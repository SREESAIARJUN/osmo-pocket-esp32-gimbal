### Architectural clarification

On this class of board, the STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. It runs ArduPilot and emits bi-directional DShot600. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32), and that MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The two domains are modeled separately:

- **Power stage** (§1–3): 4 × three-phase bridges, 24 MOSFETs, driven at 48 kHz.
- **Flight controller** (§4–5): H743 timer/DMA allocation for DShot600 output and eRPM telemetry capture.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

The ESC uses trapezoidal 6-step commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. In any sector, current flows through two phases. One leg is PWM-switching, alternating between HS and LS synchronous conduction. The other leg holds its LS (or HS) on statically.

At every instant, exactly **two MOSFET channels** carry the full phase current I. During dead time, one body diode replaces one channel; that case is covered in §2.

### 1.2 Equations

Temperature-dependent on-resistance:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + 0.005\,(T_j - 25)]$$

Conduction loss per motor, including ripple:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j), \qquad I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}$$

Phase ripple is:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{2 L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

With L_ph ≈ 15 µH (typical 5" motor), ΔI_pp ≈ 4–8 A. This adds less than 0.1 % to I², so it is negligible at 80 A.

### 1.3 Numbers

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond per motor | 4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C (cold start) | 1.00 mΩ | 2·6400·1.0 m = **12.8 W** | 51.2 W |
| 125 °C (hot) | 1.50 mΩ | 2·6400·1.5 m = **19.2 W** | 76.8 W |

### 1.4 Per-device distribution (hot spot)

Over a full electrical cycle, each phase conducts 2/3 of the time: 1/3 as the PWM leg and 1/3 as the static leg.

For the PWM leg at duty D:
- The HS conducts D·I².
- The LS conducts (1 − D)·I² (synchronous rectification).

Worst-case single-FET average, with a static-leg LS at high throttle:

$$P_{FET,max} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}I^2R + \tfrac{1}{3}D\,I^2R \;\xrightarrow{D\to1}\; \tfrac{2}{3}\cdot 6400 \cdot 1.5\,\text{m} = \mathbf{6.4\ W}$$

The mean per FET is 19.2 W / 6 = 3.2 W. **Design to 6.4 W for the hottest FET.**

### 1.5 Copper and interconnect conduction

| Element | R (est.) | P at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase pad + trace (per active phase, ×2) | 0.15 mΩ ea. | 1.92 W |
| Battery pads → bus plane (shared, 4 × 80 A = 320 A peak; average lower) | 0.05 mΩ | up to 5.1 W |
| Current-sense shunt (if per-ESC, 0.25 mΩ) | 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W/motor |

The bus-plane figure assumes all four motors are at 80 A simultaneously. That is a worst case of about 320 A from the pack, which most 6S packs and XT60 connectors cannot deliver. **Confirm whether "80 A burst" is per motor or total.**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Per motor, there is one hard-switched transition pair per PWM period.

### 2.1 Equations

| Term | Equation | Value per motor |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | ½·25.2·80·40 n·48 k = **1.94 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 40 n·25.2·48 k = **0.048 W** |
| C_oss | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw | 0.5·50 n·25.2·48 k = **0.030 W** |
| Dead-time diode | V_F·I·2t_dead·f_sw | 0.8·80·400 n·48 k = **1.23 W** |
| Gate drive (dissipated in the driver, not the FET) | Q_g·V_drv·f_sw·2 | 60 n·10·48 k·2 = 0.058 W |
| **Total in FETs** | | **≈ 3.25 W** |

### 2.2 Observations

- **Dead time is the second-largest switching term.** Cutting t_dead from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per motor. That reduction is safe only if the gate-driver propagation-delay skew allows it, so verify shoot-through margin on a scope.
- Overlap loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it falls to about 0.97 W, but low-throttle current ripple and audible noise rise.
