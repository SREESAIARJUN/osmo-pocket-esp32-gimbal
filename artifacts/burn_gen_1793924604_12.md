## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Finding

There are two corrections to the brief before the derivation.

**1. The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In a 4-in-1 stack, each motor is commutated by a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071. The architecture looks like this:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──DShot600 bidir x4──► 4x ESC MCU ──► 4x 3-phase gate drivers ──► 24x N-FET
         ▲                                            │
         └──────────── eRPM telemetry (GCR) ◄──────────┘
```

As a result:
- The 48 kHz switching frequency is an ESC-firmware parameter (AM32 `PWM_FREQ`).
- The H743 owns the DShot timers and DMA.
- The loss and thermal model below belongs to the ESC board.

**2. 80 A per motor on a nano (20×20 mm) 4-in-1 is not thermally credible beyond about 0.5 s.** Section 3 shows the derivation. The math converges on about 25–30 A continuous per motor for this footprint, which matches commercial nano-ESC ratings. I model the 80 A burst as specified and give the duration limit it implies.
