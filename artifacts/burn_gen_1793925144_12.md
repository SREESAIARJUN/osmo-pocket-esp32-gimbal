# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80 A ESC (Job #35): Architecture, Loss Model, DShot DMA Plan, Netlists

## 0. Feasibility Constraints

Three conditions in the brief determine every number that follows.

1. **The H743 cannot run the four motor bridges itself.** Four 3-phase bridges need 12 complementary PWM pairs plus 4 back-EMF comparator sets. The H743 has two advanced timers (TIM1, TIM8) and HRTIM, which is not enough for sensorless 6-step on four motors at 48 kHz. The architecture is therefore:
   - an **H743 FC running ArduPilot**, sending bidirectional DShot600 to
   - **4× ESC MCUs** (AT32F421K8 or STM32G071, running AM32 or BLHeli_32).
2. **"Nano" (20×20 mm) and 80 A are in tension.** A 20×20 board only fits 3×3 mm PDFN FETs. Industry 20×20 4-in-1 boards are typically rated 35–45 A continuous with short bursts higher. This design uses a **30×30 mm (M3 mounting) ESC power board stacked under a 20×20 FC** and evaluates whether 80 A is survivable as a *burst*. The verdict is: **about 1 s single-motor, under 0.5 s all-four, without a heatsink.**
3. **4 × 80 A = 320 A from the battery (≈8 kW at 6S)** exceeds any XT60/XT90 pad and wire practical on this form factor. The "80 A" rating is treated as a **per-motor transient**. The input design targets 160 A peak aggregate.

Assumed operating point: 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V (full charge, worst case for switching loss), f_sw = 48 kHz, T_amb = 40 °C.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current paths in 6-step with synchronous rectification

At any commutation sector, phase A (PWM leg) and phase B (low-side held on) conduct the motor current I_m:

- PWM on (D): Q_AH → motor → Q_BL
- PWM off (1−D): Q_AL (sync rectifier) → motor → Q_BL

The current path therefore **always passes through exactly two FETs**, independent of duty:

$$P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 2.2 Ripple-adjusted RMS

Phase inductance for a typical 2207–2808 motor is L ≈ 15–30 µH. Using L = 20 µH and D = 0.5 (worst ripple):

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \cdot 0.25}{20\,\mu H \cdot 48\,kHz} = 6.56\ A$$

$$I_{rms} = \sqrt{I_m^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}} = \sqrt{6400 + 3.59} = 80.02\ A$$

At 80 A, ripple is negligible (<0.03 %). It matters only at light load.

### 2.3 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on) (electrothermal coupling)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_0\left(\frac{T_j + 273}{298}\right)^{1.9}$$

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond per motor (2 FETs, 80 A) |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.60 mΩ | 20.5 W |
| 75 °C | 2.15 mΩ | 27.5 W |
| 100 °C | 2.45 mΩ | 31.4 W |
| 125 °C | 2.77 mΩ | 35.5 W |

**Thermal-runaway check.** Steady state requires that the loop gain satisfies dP/dT · R_θ < 1:

$$\frac{dP}{dT} = 2I^2 R_0 \frac{k}{298}\left(\frac{T}{298}\right)^{k-1} \approx 2\cdot6400\cdot1.6\text{m}\cdot\frac{1.9}{298}\cdot1.25 = 0.163\ \text{W/K}$$

With a realistic board-to-ambient R_θ(motor section) ≈ 6 K/W (prop-wash cooled, quarter of a 30×30 board), the loop gain is 0.163 × 6 ≈ 0.98. **This is marginal, which is why 80 A cannot be continuous.**

### 2.4 Additional resistive paths (per motor at 80 A)

| Element | R | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| Phase pad + trace (2 oz, 3 mm wide, 6 mm, ×2 paths) | 2×0.5 mΩ | 6.4 W |
| Shunt (if per-motor; recommended: single 0.25 mΩ bus shunt instead) | — | — |
| Shared bus share (0.25 mΩ shunt + 0.3 mΩ planes) at 80 A | 0.55 mΩ | 3.5 W |

These are board losses: they heat the PCB but not the junctions directly.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg's high-side switches hard. The low-side sync FET switches ZVS-ish after dead time.

### 3.1 V-I overlap (hard-switched HS FET)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_m (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot33\,\text{ns}\cdot48\,\text{kHz} = 1.60\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 35\,\text{nC}\cdot25.2\cdot48\,\text{k} = 0.042\ \text{W}$$
