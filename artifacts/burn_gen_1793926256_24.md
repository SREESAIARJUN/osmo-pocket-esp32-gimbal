## 0. Architectural Corrections

Three points in the brief need correcting before any math is meaningful.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot is a flight-control stack. It outputs DShot commands and does not run six-step or FOC commutation. Every working 4-in-1 design uses one ESC MCU per motor (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32). The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to those four MCUs, and each MCU runs its own 48 kHz PWM. The architecture below reflects this.

2. **"Nano" form factor and 80 A burst are in tension.** Twenty-four power MOSFETs dissipating about 107 W total (derived below) need a 30.5 × 30.5 mm, 6–8 layer, 2 oz board as a practical minimum. On a 20 × 20 mm board, the realistic burst rating drops to about 35–45 A. I model the 30.5 mm case and flag the 20 mm case.

3. **48 kHz is the ESC PWM frequency.** It is unrelated to the DShot bit rate (600 kbit/s), and the two are kept separate below.

## 3. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 3.1 Hot R_ds(on)

R_ds,hot = R_ds,25 · k_T = 1.2 mΩ × 1.6 = **1.92 mΩ**

### 3.2 Six-step with synchronous (complementary) rectification

In each 60° commutation sector, current flows through two series paths:
- the PWM leg, carried by the high side during D and the low side during 1−D (complementary), and
- the low side of the return leg, carried continuously.

At every instant, therefore, the current sees two R_ds in series, independent of duty cycle (dead time aside):

P_cond,ch = I² · 2·R_ds,hot = 80² × 2 × 1.92 mΩ = **24.6 W per ESC channel**
