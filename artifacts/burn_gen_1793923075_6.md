# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Model, Thermal Budget, and Bidirectional DShot Allocation

**Design Package, Job #59, Rev A (engineering baseline)**

---

## 0. Architecture Clarifications

Three points about the system architecture shape the rest of this package:

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In a 4-in-1 AIO, the H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600. Each motor is commutated by a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. This design uses 4× AT32F421 or STM32G071. The 48 kHz PWM is generated on the ESC side. The H743's only job toward the ESC is DShot timing and DMA.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano form factor is a short transient rating, not a thermal operating point.** Section 3 shows that four channels at 80 A dissipate about 130 W in a board with roughly 3 J/K of heat capacity. That gives about 1–2 seconds of headroom. Marketing a board as "80 A" without stating burst duration is how field failures happen. The rating must be specified as **80 A / ≤1 s / single channel**, or the derating table in §3.6 must be published.
3. **Board current is aggregate.** 4 × 80 A = 320 A on the battery pads. No nano-format pad, XT60, or 2 oz trace survives that continuously. §3.7 addresses it.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.5 mΩ × 1.6 = **2.4 mΩ**. Use the hot value for burst analysis, because the burst is what heats the die.

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step synchronous commutation

In any commutation sector, two phase legs carry the motor current I:

- **Leg A (PWM'd):** the high-side (HS) conducts for D·T_sw. The low-side (LS) conducts as a synchronous rectifier for (1−D)·T_sw, minus dead time.
- **Leg B (static):** the LS of the return phase conducts 100 % of the sector.

So at every instant, exactly two channel resistances are in series with the motor current (ignoring dead time):

$$
P_{cond,motor} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

### 1.3 Ripple correction

The phase current ripple is:

$$
\Delta i = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L\,f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\,\mu\text{H} \times 48\,\text{kHz}} = 6.56\ \text{A}_{pp}\ (\text{worst case } D = 0.5)
$$

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I_{avg}^2 + \frac{\Delta i^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.6 \approx 6404\ \text{A}^2
$$

The ripple contributes less than 0.1 % and is neglected.

### 1.4 Result at 80 A

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 2.4\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{30.7\ W}
$$

**Per-FET distribution.** Each of the 6 FETs conducts on average 1/3 of an electrical revolution:

- Instantaneous: 80² × 2.4 mΩ = **15.4 W** while conducting
- Averaged: 30.7 / 6 = **5.1 W**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg per motor is hard-switched at any time. The HS takes the hard transitions. The LS sync-rectifier switches at near-zero V_DS (ZVS) but incurs body-diode losses during dead time.

### 2.1 Voltage–current overlap

Transition times are set by gate charge and driver current:

$$
t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{source}} = \frac{9\,\text{nC}}{1.0\,\text{A}} \approx 9\ \text{ns}, \qquad t_f \approx \frac{9\,\text{nC}}{1.5\,\text{A}} \approx 6\ \text{ns}
$$

Including the plateau entry and gate-resistor slowing (R_G ≈ 4.7 Ω, chosen for EMI and ringing control), use t_r + t_f ≈ **20 ns**.

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}
$$
