# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Design Package for Job #48

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and One Architecture Correction

I don't have Job #48's requirements, so this package uses the stated baseline below. **Every number marked ⚠ must be replaced with your selected parts' datasheet values before release.**

| Parameter | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal |
| Burst current | 80 A per motor, 10 s maximum |
| Continuous current | 45 A per motor |
| PWM frequency | 48 kHz, synchronous (complementary) rectification |
| MOSFET ⚠ | 40 V, 5×6 mm DFN class, R_DS(on) = 0.7 mΩ max at 25 °C, R_θJC = 1.0 K/W, Q_g = 60 nC, Q_rr = 50 nC, Q_oss = 60 nC |
| Gate driver ⚠ | 3-phase half-bridge driver (FD6288Q class), ~1.5 A source / 1.8 A sink |
| ESC MCU ⚠ | AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 (or BLHeli_32) |
| Board stackup | 30.5 × 30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz outer / 3 oz inner copper |

**Architecture correction.** The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. ArduPilot is an autopilot. It sends bi-directional DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs, one per motor, which run the commutation firmware. The 48 kHz PWM in Sections 1 and 2 is generated by those ESC MCUs. Section 3 covers the H743 side.

If Job #48 actually requires the H743 to drive the bridges directly, that is custom firmware outside ArduPilot. It would need 4 × 3 complementary PWM pairs, i.e. 24 outputs. The H743 has only two advanced-control timers (TIM1/TIM8) with complementary outputs and dead-time, so it cannot do this cleanly. Flag this to the customer.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths

With trapezoidal 6-step commutation, exactly two phase legs conduct at any instant: one high-side path and one low-side path. Each phase is high for 1/3 of the electrical period, low for 1/3, and floating for 1/3.

Synchronous rectification works as follows within each commutation step:

- **High side, PWM leg:** the high-side FET (Q_H) conducts during duty fraction D.
- **High side, PWM leg (off-time):** the complementary low-side FET (Q_L) of the same leg freewheels during the remaining fraction (1−D).
- **Low side, non-PWM leg:** Q_L is on for the full step.

So at every instant, motor current I flows through exactly two R_DS(on), independent of D.

### 1.2 Temperature-corrected on-resistance

R_DS(on)(T_j) = R_25 · [1 + α(T_j − 25)], with α ≈ 0.0065 /K ⚠ (read from the datasheet's normalized R_DS(on) curve).

At T_j = 110 °C:

R_DS(on) = 0.7 mΩ × (1 + 0.0065 × 85) = 0.7 × 1.55 = **1.09 mΩ**

### 1.3 Losses per ESC channel

P_cond,FET = I² · 2 · R_DS(on) = 80² × 2 × 1.09e-3 = **13.9 W**

The PCB copper path from battery pad to bus to bridge to motor pad adds to this. Assume ~0.25 mΩ effective ⚠ (extract it from the layout using IR-drop simulation):

P_cu = 80² × 0.25e-3 = **1.6 W**

Current-sense shunt, if used (0.25 mΩ ⚠): P_shunt = 80² × 0.25e-3 = **1.6 W**. This shunt sits in the shared battery path, so it carries the sum of all four motor currents. Size it for the total current, not per motor.

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution, needed for junction temperature

Average over a full electrical cycle, using D ≈ 0.9 at burst. Losses are expressed as fractions of I²R:

- **Q_H:** on for 1/3 of the cycle, conducting fraction D within that.
  P = I²R · D/3 = 6976 × 0.30 = **2.09 W**
- **Q_L:** on for 1/3 of the cycle (own low step), plus the (1−D)·1/3 freewheel share.
  P = I²R · (1/3 + (1−D)/3) = 6976 × 0.367 = **2.56 W**
- **Check:** 3 legs × (2.09 + 2.56) = 13.9 W ✓

**Q_L is the hottest FET in conduction.** Place the low-side FETs nearest the thermal vias and the bottom copper pour.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. V_bus = 25.2 V worst case, and I = 80 A.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss in the high-side FET

P_ov = ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw

The transition time t_r ≈ t_f ≈ Q_gd / I_drive. With Q_gd ≈ 12 nC ⚠ and an effective drive current of ~1.2 A, each transition is ≈ 10 ns:

P_ov = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 20e-9 × 48e3 = **0.97 W**

### 2.2 Output capacitance (C_oss)

P_oss ≈ ½ · Q_oss · V · f_sw = 0.5 × 60e-9 × 25.2 × 48e3 = **0.036 W**

### 2.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction in the low-side FET

Assume t_dt = 50 ns per edge ⚠ (the FD6288 has fixed ~200 ns internal dead-time, so check your driver), with V_f = 0.8 V:

P_dt = V_f · I · 2t_dt · f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 100e-9 × 48e3 = **0.31 W**

With 200 ns dead-time, this rises to 1.23 W. This is the single biggest switching-loss lever. Use the shortest safe dead-time, as programmed in AM32.

### 2.4 Reverse recovery

P_rr = Q_rr · V · f_sw = 50e-9 × 25.2 × 48e3 = **0.06 W**

Most of this loss lands in the high-side FET at turn-on.

### 2.5 Gate charge

P_g = 2 · Q_g · V_gs · f_sw = 2 × 60e-9 × 10 × 48e3 = **0.058 W**

This is dissipated in the gate resistors and driver, not the FETs. Using V_gs = 10 V assumes a bootstrap driver supplied from a ~10–12 V rail.

### 2.6 Switching total per channel

0.97 + 0.04 + 0.31 + 0.06 = **1.38 W**, plus 0.06 W in the driver.

### 2.7 Frequency sensitivity

P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. The table below sums the switching loss with the FET conduction loss (copper excluded).

| f_sw | P_sw | P_total per channel (FETs) |
|---|---|---|
| 24 kHz | 0.69 W | 14.6 W |
| 48 kHz | 1.38 W | 15.3 W |
| 96 kHz | 2.76 W | 16.7 W |

**Conclusion:** at 80 A, conduction loss dominates (~90%). 48 kHz is a reasonable choice. The gain from going to 48 kHz is lower acoustic noise and current ripple, at a thermal cost of only ~0.7 W per channel.
