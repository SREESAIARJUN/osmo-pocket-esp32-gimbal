## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance
$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At the design point T_j = 110 °C:
$$R_{ds}(110) = 1.2\,\text{mΩ} \times 1.51 = 1.81\ \text{mΩ}$$

### 1.2 Bridge conduction
With synchronous rectification in 6-step commutation, motor current always flows through exactly **two** FETs. One is a high-side FET (PWM on) or the complementary low-side FET (PWM off). The other is the active low-side FET. Conduction loss is therefore nearly independent of duty cycle.

Neglecting current ripple (I_rms ≈ I_DC):
$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

At 80 A:
$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.81\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{23.2\ W}\ \text{per motor channel}$$

- Per FET (6 FETs share the electrical cycle): **about 3.9 W average**.
- Peak per FET while conducting: I²R = 11.6 W.

### 1.3 Ripple correction (optional)
$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{DC}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12},\qquad \Delta I_{pp} \approx \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}$$

For L_ph = 10 µH (phase-to-phase 20 µH), D = 0.5, the worst case is ΔI_pp ≈ 6.6 A. The correction is about +0.06 %, so it is negligible.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Each PWM cycle produces one hard-switched transition pair in the commutating leg.

| Term | Equation | Value per channel |
|---|---|---|
| V·I overlap | ½ V I (t_r + t_f) f_sw = 0.5·25.2·80·20 ns·48 kHz | **0.968 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr V f_sw = 50 nC·25.2·48 kHz | 0.060 W |
| Output capacitance | ½ Q_oss V f_sw | 0.024 W |
| Dead-time body diode | 2 V_f I t_dt f_sw = 2·0.8·80·100 ns·48 kHz | **0.614 W** |
| Gate drive (driver dissipation) | 2 Q_g V_GS f_sw = 2·60 nC·10·48 kHz | 0.058 W |
| **Total P_sw** | | **≈ 1.72 W** |

### 2.1 Totals at 80 A burst

| | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 23.2 W | 92.8 W |
| Switching | 1.7 W | 6.9 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, if per-channel shunt) | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 26.5 W** | **≈ 106 W** |

Efficiency at burst: P_out ≈ 4 × 25.2 × 80 × 0.95 ≈ 7.66 kW, so η_ESC ≈ 98.6 %. The efficiency looks fine. The problem is the **power density**, not the efficiency.

### 2.2 Choosing f_sw
P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it would be about 0.86 W per channel. At 96 kHz it would be about 3.4 W per channel.

Conduction dominates by about 13:1, so 48 kHz is reasonable. Its main costs are dead-time and diode loss. Reduce dead time to the minimum that is safe for the driver (about 50 ns) and you recover about 0.3 W per channel.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network (Cauer approximation)
```
P_FET ──[R_jc]──●──[R_cb]──●──[R_ba]── T_amb
               C_j        C_board
```
| Element | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| R_θjc (per FET, to PCB) | 1.5 K/W | Datasheet bottom pad |
| R_θ,spread (PCB, 2 oz, 6 L) | 4 K/W per FET | Small pad, limited spreading |
| R_θba (whole board, prop wash ≈ 5 m/s) | 2.5 K/W | 25×25 mm, both sides |
| C_board (FR4 + Cu + FETs ≈ 8 g) | ≈ 6 J/K | 0.6·1100 + 0.25·385·… (estimate) |
| τ_board = R_ba·C_board | ≈ 15 s | |

### 3.2 Steady-state (why 80 A cannot be continuous)
$$\Delta T_{ba} = P_{tot}\,R_{ba} = 106 \times 2.5 = 265\ \text{K}$$
This is physically impossible. Solder reflows at about 217 °C.

### 3.3 Burst transient (2 s)
Because t_b ≪ τ_board, the board behaves as an almost adiabatic heat sink:
$$\Delta T_{board}(t) = P R_{ba}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right) \approx \frac{P\,t}{C} = \frac{106 \times 2}{6} \approx 35\ \text{K}$$

The junction rises above the board by the per-FET drop during conduction. Use the peak conduction power with a 1/3 duty, and treat the FET thermal time constant as short:
$$\Delta T_{jb} \approx 3.9\,\text{W}\times(1.5+4) \approx 21\ \text{K}\ (\text{avg}),\quad \text{peak ripple} +\sim5\ \text{K}$$

$$T_j \approx T_{board,start} + 35 + 26$$
Starting from a 60 °C hover-equilibrium board, T_j ≈ **121 °C** after a 2 s burst. That is inside 150 °C abs-max, and it is consistent with the 110 °C R_ds assumption to within one iteration.
