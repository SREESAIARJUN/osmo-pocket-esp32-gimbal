# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #5): Electrothermal Model, DShot DMA Plan, and Netlists

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Feasibility Warning

**Architecture:** The STM32H743 is the flight controller running ArduPilot. It does not drive the gates directly. Each ESC channel has its own small MCU (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32). That MCU produces the 48 kHz PWM and answers bidirectional DShot600. This is the industry-standard partition, and ArduPilot does not include a motor commutation stack, so I assume it here.

**Baseline parameters.** No MOSFET or job specification was supplied, so all numbers below are parametric. Replace them with datasheet values for your selected parts.

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 25.2 V (6S full) | DC bus |
| I_b | 80 A | burst phase current per channel |
| f_sw | 48 kHz | PWM frequency |
| R_ds(on),25 | 1.0 mΩ | per FET, at V_GS = 10 V |
| α | 0.005 /K | R_ds(on) temperature coefficient (≈1.5× at 125 °C) |
| t_r, t_f | 10 ns each | effective V–I overlap times |
| t_dt | 100 ns | dead time per edge |
| V_SD | 0.8 V | body-diode forward voltage |
| Q_rr | 50 nC | reverse-recovery charge |
| C_oss(er) | 1.5 nF | energy-related output capacitance |
| Q_g | 50 nC | total gate charge |
| V_drv | 10 V | gate drive voltage |
| R_θJC | 1.5 K/W | junction to case (3×3 or 5×6 power package) |

**Feasibility warning.** 80 A per channel × 4 channels on a "nano" footprint (20×20 mm) is not thermally sustainable. The math in §3 shows it is a seconds-scale burst rating at best. Realistic nano 4-in-1 boards are rated 35–55 A burst. 80 A burst normally requires a 30.5×30.5 mm board with 2–3 oz copper and 6+ layers. The model below quantifies exactly how long the burst can last.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (Six-Step, Synchronous Rectification)

### 1.1 Per-FET RMS current

In six-step commutation, each phase leg conducts for 2/3 of the electrical period. With complementary PWM at duty D on the switching leg, the conduction fractions are:

- **High-side FET:** conducts for a fraction D/3 of the cycle.
- **Low-side FET:** conducts (1−D)/3 while freewheeling, plus 1/3 during its own commutation window. Total: (2−D)/3.

Summed over three legs, exactly two FETs carry I at every instant. This is a useful sanity check.

At burst, D → 1, so each FET carries a 1/3 share:

$$
I_{rms,FET}^2 = \frac{I_b^2}{3} = \frac{6400}{3} = 2133\ \text{A}^2
$$

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]
$$

### 1.3 Conduction loss

Per FET:

$$
P_{cond,FET} = \frac{I_b^2}{3}R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

Per channel (two FETs always conducting):

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2 I_b^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

| T_j | R_ds | P per FET | P per channel | P for 4 channels |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.0 mΩ | 2.13 W | 12.8 W | 51.2 W |
| 100 °C | 1.375 mΩ | 2.93 W | 17.6 W | 70.4 W |
| 125 °C | 1.5 mΩ | 3.20 W | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |

### 1.4 Copper and shunt loss (often overlooked)

Assume a 0.3 mΩ effective trace path per conducting pair and a 0.25 mΩ shunt:

$$
P_{Cu,ch} = I_b^2 (R_{trace} + R_{shunt}) = 6400 \times 0.55\text{ mΩ} = 3.5\ \text{W}
$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg per channel hard-switches at any given time.

**V–I overlap (hard switching):**

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_b (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(20\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = 0.97\ \text{W}
$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (2 edges per period):**

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD} I_b (2t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(200\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = 0.61\ \text{W}
$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$
P_{rr} = V_{bus} Q_{rr} f_{sw} = 25.2(50\text{nC})(48\text{k}) = 0.06\ \text{W}
$$

**C_oss charge and discharge:**

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.023\ \text{W}
$$

**Gate drive (HS + LS of the switching leg).** This loss lands in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs:

$$
P_g = 2 Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 0.048\ \text{W}
$$

**Switching total:**

$$
P_{sw,ch} \approx 1.71\ \text{W/channel}
$$

At 80 A, conduction dominates (about 92%). Lowering f_sw from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves only about 0.85 W per channel. Choose 48 kHz for acoustic and current-ripple reasons, not thermal ones.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Total burst dissipation (at T_j ≈ 125 °C)

$$
P_{ch} = 19.2 + 1.71 + 3.5 \approx 24.4\ \text{W}
$$

$$
P_{total} = 4 \times 24.4 \approx 97.6\ \text{W}
$$

### 3.2 Electrothermal stability (thermal runaway check)

For a single FET with lumped resistance R_θ from junction to board:

$$
T_j = T_b + R_\theta P_0[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

Solving for T_j:

$$
T_j = \frac{T_b + R_\theta P_0 (1-25\alpha)}{1-\alpha R_\theta P_0}
$$

The system is stable only if:

$$
\alpha R_\theta P_0 < 1
$$
