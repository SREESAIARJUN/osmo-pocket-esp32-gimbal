# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #50): Power-Stage Loss, Thermal, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Design Basis

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Two Early Flags

I have no Job #50 BOM or datasheets, so every device parameter below is a **declared assumption**. Each one is tagged **[A#]** so you can replace it with real datasheet values. The equations are general. The numbers are only as good as the assumptions.

**Flag 1: Architecture.** In a 4-in-1 ESC stack, the STM32H743 is normally the flight controller. Each ESC channel, or a shared ESC MCU, runs AM32 or BLHeli_32 firmware on a small Cortex-M0/M4 (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071). That ESC MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM and drives the gates. The H743 only sends and receives bi-directional DShot600. This document models the system that way. Driving 12 half-bridges directly from the H743 under ArduPilot is not a supported configuration.

**Flag 2: Feasibility.** An 80 A per-channel burst on a nano form factor (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) is thermally marginal to infeasible beyond a few seconds. Section 3 quantifies this. Treat 80 A as a ≤1–2 s transient rating, not a continuous one.

## 1. Conduction Losses at 80 A Burst

### 1.1 Current Path

In 6-step commutation, exactly two legs conduct at any instant: one high-side path and one low-side return. With synchronous rectification, the PWM leg alternates between its HS and LS FET, so a FET is always in the path on each side. The instantaneous conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle:

$$
P_{cond,ch}(t) = I_{ph}^2 \left[ 2\,R_{ds}(T_j) + R_{cu} \right]
$$

(Body-diode intervals during dead time are handled separately in §2.)

### 1.2 Thermal Self-Consistency

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \mathrm{K^{-1}}
$$

Because R_ds rises with T_j, conduction loss is positive feedback. Solve iteratively with the thermal model in §3:

$$
T_j^{(n+1)} = T_{ref} + Z_\theta(t)\cdot P_{FET}\!\left(T_j^{(n)}\right)
$$

Thermal runaway occurs if

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\,R_\theta \geq 1 \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; I^2 R_{ds,25}\,\alpha\,R_\theta \geq 1
$$

### 1.3 Numbers at T_j = 125 °C (R_ds = 1.8 mΩ)

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 80^2 \,(2 \times 1.8 + 0.4)\,\mathrm{m\Omega} = 6400 \times 4.0\,\mathrm{m\Omega} = \mathbf{25.6\ W}
$$

Of this, 23.0 W is in the FETs and 2.6 W is in the copper.

### 1.4 Per-FET Distribution

Over one electrical revolution, each leg conducts 240° (120° sourcing, 120° sinking). The worst-case FET is the low side of a leg during its PWM interval at low duty, where it carries current nearly the whole sector.

- **Average per FET:** 23.0 W / 6 = **3.84 W**
- **Worst-case low-side FET** (low duty, high current, as in a punch-out from hover): it conducts ≈(1/3) + (1/3)(1 − D) of the revolution.
  - At D = 0.3: P_LS ≈ 6400 × 1.8 m × 0.567 ≈ **6.5 W**

### 1.5 Four-Channel Total

$$
P_{cond,total} \approx 4 \times 25.6 \approx \mathbf{102\ W}
$$

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The other conducting leg is static within the commutation sector. The edge V·I terms below are for that one leg, with I = 80 A and V = 25.2 V.

### 2.1 Hard-Switching V–I Overlap

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I_{ph}\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}
$$

$$
P_{VI} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\,\mathrm{ns} \times 48\,\mathrm{kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

A refinement using Miller-plateau timing is

$$
t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,R_{G,tot}}{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}
$$

With Q_gd = 12 nC, R_G = 3 Ω + 1 Ω internal, V_drv = 10 V, and V_pl ≈ 4.5 V:

$$
t_{sw} \approx 8.7\ \mathrm{ns}
$$

Add current-rise time and parasitic inductance (L_loop ≈ 2 nH → L·di/dt ringing), and 20 ns is a realistic effective value.

### 2.2 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD}\,I_{ph}\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\,\mathrm{ns} \times 48\,\mathrm{kHz} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Reverse Recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 40\,\mathrm{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\mathrm{kHz} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

Q_rr scales roughly with I_F and di/dt. At 80 A, use 1.5–2× the datasheet value: ≈ **0.09 W**.

### 2.4 Output Capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = Q_{oss}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 45\,\mathrm{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\mathrm{kHz} = \mathbf{0.054\ W}
$$

### 2.5 Gate Drive

This loss lands in the driver and gate resistor, not in the FET die. Two FETs in the PWM leg switch each period:

$$
P_G = 2\,Q_g\,V_{drv}\,f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\,\mathrm{nC} \times 10 \times 48\,\mathrm{kHz} = \mathbf{0.058\ W}
$$

### 2.6 Switching Subtotal per Channel

| Loss component | Value |
|---|---|
| P_VI | 1.94 W |
| P_dt | 0.61 W |
| P_rr | 0.09 W |
| P_oss | 0.05 W |
| **FET-side total** | **≈ 2.70 W** |
| Gate drive (driver/resistor) | 0.06 W |

Switching loss is concentrated in the PWM-leg high-side FET (V–I overlap) and the low-side FET (diode/dead-time). It rotates among legs every 60° electrical.

> **Design note:** At 48 kHz, switching loss is about 10% of conduction loss at 80 A. Lowering f_sw to 24 kHz would save only ~1.3 W/channel. **Conduction dominates the burst case**, so the lever is R_ds(on) and copper, not frequency.
