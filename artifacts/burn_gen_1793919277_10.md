## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

### 0.1 Physical constraint

A true nano (20×20 mm) board cannot carry 80 A per motor. Twenty-four power FETs in 5×6 mm packages, plus bulk capacitance, will not fit, and the copper cannot spread the heat. This design therefore assumes:

- A **30.5×30.5 mm 4-in-1 ESC board** (8-layer, 2 oz on all layers).
- A **stacked H743 flight controller** that may be 20×20 mm.
- If the ESC must also be 20×20 mm, derate burst current to about 45–55 A and use 3.3×3.3 mm FETs.

### 0.2 Control split

- The H743 runs ArduPilot only. It does not commutate motors.
- Each ESC channel has its own MCU (STM32G071 or AT32F421 class) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. These receive bidirectional DShot600 and return eRPM telemetry.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step synchronous PWM

In any 60° commutation sector, two legs are active and one is floating:

- **PWM leg:** current flows through the high-side FET during D·T_sw and through the low-side FET (synchronous rectification) during (1−D)·T_sw.
- **Static leg:** the low-side FET is on for the whole sector.

At every instant (ignoring dead time), the phase current therefore flows through exactly two FET channels in series. This holds regardless of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot \big[D + (1-D)\big] R_{DS}(T_j) + I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{DS}(110) = 1.0 \times (1 + 0.005 \times 85) = 1.425\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Results at 80 A

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.425\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{18.24\ W}$$

### 1.4 Parasitic conduction

| Element | R | P at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| PCB copper, phase pad → FET → bus (both legs) | ≈ 0.5 mΩ | 3.20 W |
| Current shunt (per channel) | 0.25 mΩ | 1.60 W |
| Motor-lead solder joints (2 × ~0.1 mΩ) | 0.2 mΩ | 1.28 W |

### 1.5 Electro-thermal feedback

R_DS rises with T_j, and loss rises with R_DS. The loop converges only if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\, R_{th} < 1 \;\Rightarrow\; 2I^2 R_{DS,25}\,\alpha\, R_{th,j\text{-}amb} < 1$$

At 80 A this gives R_th < 7.8 K/W per channel pair. That is achievable only on a transient (burst) basis on this board size. It is the formal reason the 80 A rating must be time-limited (see §4).
