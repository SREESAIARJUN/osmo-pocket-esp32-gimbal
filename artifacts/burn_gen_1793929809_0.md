# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with Integrated 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage, Thermal, and DShot Architecture Model

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Corrections

I have no prior context for "Job #3," so every parameter below is a stated assumption. Swap in your datasheet values and the equations still hold.

**Architecture correction:** The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. ArduPilot runs on the H743 as the flight controller. Each motor channel needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071. The 48 kHz PWM is an ESC-firmware setting. DShot600 is the link between the H743 and the ESC MCUs.

**Feasibility warning:** An 80 A burst on all four channels of a nano board (20×20 mm mounting) is thermally marginal. Section 3 shows this is a sub-second capability, not a sustained rating.

| Parameter | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Bus voltage | 6S, V_bus = 25.2 V max |
| MOSFET | 40 V N-channel, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN, R_DS(on),25 = 1.2 mΩ |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

R_DS(on)(T_j) = R_25 · [1 + α (T_j − 25)]

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS(on) = 1.2 mΩ × 1.6 = **1.92 mΩ**

### 1.2 Current paths in one commutation sector

| FET | Conducts | Duty |
|---|---|---|
| Active high-side (phase A) | I | D |
| Synchronous low-side (phase A) | I | 1 − D |
| Return low-side (phase B) | I | 1 (whole sector) |

The total conduction loss per motor is therefore:

P_cond,motor = I² R (D + (1 − D) + 1) = **2 I² R_DS(on)(T_j)**

This loss is independent of duty cycle, which is a useful design invariant.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

- Per motor: P_cond = 2 × 80² × 1.92 mΩ = **24.6 W**
- Four motors: **98.3 W**

**Per-FET average.** The six FETs share the load over one electrical revolution. The commutation frequency is hundreds of Hz to kHz, which is much faster than the die thermal time constant of roughly 1–10 ms. So the per-FET average is:

P_FET,avg ≈ 24.6 / 6 ≈ **4.1 W**

The peak instantaneous per-FET loss is 80² × 1.92 mΩ = **12.3 W**. This matters at low eRPM, such as stall or startup, when averaging no longer applies.

### 1.4 Copper loss (often ignored, should not be)

Assume 25 mm of 2 oz (70 µm) copper, 5 mm wide, per phase, paralleled across 2 layers:

R_Cu = ρL / (wt) = (1.72×10⁻⁸ × 0.025) / (0.005 × 70×10⁻⁶ × 2) ≈ 0.61 mΩ at 25 °C, ≈ 0.85 mΩ hot

Two phases carry current, so per motor: 2 × 80² × 0.85 mΩ ≈ **10.9 W**, or about 44 W for four motors. Copper loss is comparable to FET loss on a nano board. Use 3 oz or thicker internal layers and stitch phase pours.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the active high-side FET hard-switches per PWM cycle. The synchronous low-side FET switches at near-zero V_DS.

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 35 ns × 48 kHz | **1.69 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw = 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 kHz | 0.048 W |
| Output capacitance | ½ · Q_oss · V · f_sw | 0.018 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f · I · 2t_dt · f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 100 ns × 48 kHz | 0.31 W |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2 · Q_g · V_gs · f_sw = 2 × 50 nC × 10 V × 48 kHz | 0.048 W (dissipated in driver/R_g) |
| **Per motor** | | **≈ 2.1 W** |
| **Four motors** | | **≈ 8.5 W** |

**Frequency sensitivity:** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw.
- At 24 kHz: ≈ 4.2 W total for four motors.
- At 96 kHz: ≈ 17 W total.

At 48 kHz, switching loss is about 8% of conduction loss, so 48 kHz is a reasonable choice for ripple and acoustics.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Total burst dissipation (all four motors at 80 A)

| Source | Power |
|---|---|
| FET conduction | 98.3 W |
| FET switching | 8.5 W |
| PCB copper | ~44 W |
| H743 + regulators + ESC MCUs | ~1.5 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 152 W** |

### 3.2 Lumped thermal capacitance (20×20 mm board area, ~4 cm²)

| Element | Calculation | C_th |
|---|---|---|
| Copper, 6 layers × 70 µm | 1.5 g × 0.385 J/g·K | 0.58 J/K |
| FR4, 1.6 mm | 1.18 g × 1.1 J/g·K | 1.30 J/K |
| 24 FETs + passives | estimate | ~0.5 J/K |
| **Total** | | **C_board ≈ 2.4 J/K** |

### 3.3 Two-node model (junction → board → ambient)

**Junction-to-board** (fast node, τ ≈ ms):

ΔT_jb = P_FET,avg × (R_th,jc + R_th,solder/via) ≈ 4.1 × (1.2 + 3.0) ≈ **17 K**

**Board-to-ambient** (slow node):

C_board · dT_b/dt = P_tot − (T_b − T_amb) / R_th,ba

R_th,ba depends on airflow. With prop wash, assume ≈ 4 K/W. This gives a time constant τ = R_th,ba · C_board ≈ 9.6 s.

T_b(t) = T_amb + P_tot R_th,ba · (1 − e^(−t/τ))

For t ≪ τ, the board heats nearly adiabatically: dT/dt ≈ 152
