## 0. Scope, Assumptions and a Feasibility Flag

The H743 cannot commutate motors directly. In every production 4-in-1 design, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32, BLHeli_32 or Bluejay. Typical choices are the AT32F421, the STM32G071 or the EFM8BB51. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to those ESC MCUs over bi-directional DShot600. This document models the system that way.

**Feasibility flag: 80 A burst on a nano (20×20 mm) board is at the edge of physical plausibility.** The model below shows that 80 A per motor is sustainable for roughly **0.5–1.0 s**, not as a continuous rating. Commercial 20×20 AIOs are rated around 25–45 A continuous. I recommend this rating language:

> **"80 A burst ≤ 1 s (single motor or diagonal pair), 35 A continuous with airflow."**

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation topology

The model assumes trapezoidal six-step commutation with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM), which is the AM32 and BLHeli_32 default.

At any instant, two phases carry current:
- In the PWM'd phase, current flows through the high-side FET during the on-time and through the complementary low-side FET during the off-time.
- In the other active phase, the low-side FET is held on.

Phase current therefore always flows through **two FET channels in series**. Each FET conducts for 120° of the electrical cycle (a fraction of 1/3).

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.4 × 1.48 ≈ **2.07 mΩ**. The design value of **2.24 mΩ** is the conservative ×1.6 datasheet curve.

### 1.3 Phase current versus battery current

Phase current is not the same as battery current at part throttle:

$$
I_{batt} = D \cdot I_{ph}
$$

"80 A burst" is defined here as **I_ph = 80 A at D ≈ 1** (full-throttle punch-out). This is the worst case for conduction loss.

At part throttle, phase current can exceed battery current. For example, 40 A from the battery at D = 0.5 means 80 A in the phase. Current limiting in the ESC firmware must therefore act on **phase current**, not battery current.

### 1.4 Per-motor and per-FET conduction loss

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2 \cdot I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{DS}(T_j) = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.24\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{28.7\ W}
$$

$$
P_{cond,FET,avg} = \frac{I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}}{3} = \frac{6400 \times 2.24\times10^{-3}}{3} = \mathbf{4.78\ W}
$$

The per-FET average is valid because the electrical frequency is much faster than the FET's thermal time constant:
- For a 14-pole motor at 30,000 RPM, f_e = 7 × 500 = 3.5 kHz.
- The FET junction thermal time constant is on the order of milliseconds.
