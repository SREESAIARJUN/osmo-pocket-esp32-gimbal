# STM32H743 Nano AIO with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #20): Thermal, Switching, and Bi-directional DShot600 Design

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Feasibility Verdict

Three issues need resolving before the math, because they change what we are designing.

**1. The H743 does not switch the bridges.** On every production 4-in-1 design, each ESC channel has its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071. That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM and does commutation. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to the ESC MCUs over bi-directional DShot600. The switching-loss model below therefore belongs to the ESC side. The DMA and timer allocation belongs to the H743.

**2. "80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5) board is a marketing number, not a thermal design point.** The model below shows:
- Each channel dissipates about 26 W at 80 A.
- The board heats at roughly 30 K/s.
- 80 A per channel is survivable for only about 1–2 s, and only if a single channel is loaded.
- Realistic continuous capability is about 25–30 A per channel.

I recommend specifying the board as 40 A continuous-rated FETs, 30 A continuous per channel, and 55 A burst for ≤5 s.

**3. Treat all part values below as stated assumptions.** Swap in your actual MOSFET datasheet values. The equations are general.
