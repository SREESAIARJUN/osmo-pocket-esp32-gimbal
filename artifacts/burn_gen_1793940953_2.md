# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC (Job #27): Systems Architecture Package

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

**Architecture partition.** On a 4-in-1 stack, the STM32H743 does not switch the MOSFETs. Each ESC channel, or one MCU per two channels, runs its own firmware: AM32 or BLHeli_32 on an AT32F421 or STM32G071. That ESC MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 generates bidirectional DShot600 and decodes the eRPM telemetry. The model below covers both sides.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 100 °C:

$$R_{DS}(100) = 1.0 \times (1 + 0.0055 \times 75) = 1.41\text{ mΩ}$$

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation

At any instant, current flows through one high-side FET and one low-side FET in series. This assumes synchronous rectification, which AM32 and BLHeli_32 both use.

On the chopping leg:
- the high-side FET conducts for duty D,
- the complementary low-side FET conducts for 1 − D,
- so the total I²R in that leg is independent of D (ignoring dead time).

Instantaneous conduction loss per channel:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j) = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.41\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{18.0\text{ W}}$$

### 1.3 Per-device loss (electrical-cycle averaged)

Each FET conducts for 120° of the 360° electrical cycle. The electrical period is under 1 ms at the eRPMs that draw 80 A, which is far shorter than the die thermal time constant (around 10 ms). Averaging is therefore valid:

$$P_{FET,avg} = \frac{18.0}{6} = 3.0\text{ W}, \qquad P_{FET,peak} = 9.0\text{ W}$$

### 1.4 Copper and interconnect conduction

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho_{Cu}\, \ell}{w\, t}\,[1 + 0.00393(T - 20)]$$

Assumptions:
- 2 oz copper (t = 70 µm) on 4 parallel layers.
- Effective path ℓ ≈ 15 mm and w ≈ 4 mm, from the battery pad through the phase leg to the motor pad, with both directions included.

This gives R_Cu ≈ 0.25 mΩ, so:

$$P_{Cu} \approx 80^2 \times 0.25\text{ mΩ} \approx 1.6\text{ W per channel}$$

The shared DC bus adds roughly 320 A total through the main pads. Budget 0.1 mΩ there, which adds about 10 W across the board during an all-motor burst. **Bus copper is a first-order loss term at 320 A, not a rounding error.**

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the chopping leg switches hard. The conducting low-side leg in 6-step is static for that sector.

### 2.1 Hard-switching overlap (V–I crossover)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 35\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.69\text{ W}}$$

### 2.2 Body-diode reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{ kHz} = 0.06\text{ W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{ nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{ kHz} = 0.023\text{ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.23\text{ W}}$$

This term is non-trivial. Reducing dead time to 100 ns (AM32 is configurable) halves it, provided the driver propagation-delay mismatch allows.

### 2.5 Gate drive

Gate drive is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET die.

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2 = 60\text{ nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{ kHz} \times 2 = 0.058\text{ W per channel}$$

### 2.6 Switching total per channel

$$P_{sw,tot} \approx 1.69 + 0.06 + 0.02 + 1.23 + 0.06 = \mathbf{3.06\text{ W}}$$

### 2.7 Frequency sensitivity

| f_sw | P_sw,tot | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| 24 kHz | 1.53 W | more audible whine, higher current ripple |
| 48 kHz | 3.06 W | baseline |
| 96 kHz | 6.1 W | eats margin; not recommended at 80 A |
