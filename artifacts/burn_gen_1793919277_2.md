## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Red Flags

These decisions frame everything below, so they come first.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In any practical 4-in-1 design, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or an EFM8 running BLHeli_S. That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to the four ESC MCUs. The switching-loss model therefore belongs to the ESC side, and the DMA model belongs to the H743 side.

2. **"80A burst" on a "nano" form factor is the dominant design risk.**
   - A true 20×20 mm board cannot carry 4×80 A, even briefly. Copper cross-section, MOSFET footprint, and capacitor volume all fail.
   - The numbers below assume a **30.5×30.5 mm stack**: FC on top, ESC below, 8-layer, 2 oz on outer and inner power layers.
   - Treat 80 A as a **≤2–3 s per-channel burst** at ≤6S. It is not a continuous rating.

3. **Assumed operating point:**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S, V_bus = 25.2 V (fully charged, worst case for switching loss) |
| PWM frequency | f_sw = 48 kHz, center-aligned, complementary (synchronous rectification) |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal, which is AM32 default behavior |
| Ambient / start-of-burst board temperature | T_a = 40 °C / T_b0 = 60 °C (in-flight, warmed up) |

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous PWM

During any commutation sector, two legs are active:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side (HS) FET conducts for duty fraction D. The low-side (LS) FET conducts for 1−D (synchronous freewheel).
- **Return leg:** the LS FET conducts 100% of the sector.
- **Third leg:** floating, used for back-EMF sensing.

With flat-top phase current I (a trapezoidal approximation), the instantaneous conduction loss of the bridge is:

$$P_{cond} = I^2\left[D\,R_{HS} + (1-D)\,R_{LS} + R_{LS}\right]$$

If HS and LS FETs are identical with resistance R:

$$\boxed{P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)}\quad \text{(independent of } D\text{)}$$

### 2.2 Numeric result at 80 A

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.0 mΩ | 12.8 W | 51.2 W |
| 100 °C | 1.45 mΩ | 18.6 W | 74.2 W |
| 125 °C | 1.6 mΩ | **20.5 W** | **81.9 W** |

### 2.3 Parasitic copper loss (often forgotten)

Assume a 4-layer parallel 2 oz phase path, 10 mm long and 4 mm wide.

- Sheet resistance of 2 oz copper is about 0.25 mΩ/□.
- 2.5 squares per layer in parallel across 4 layers gives about 0.16 mΩ.
- Hot copper (+40%) gives about 0.22 mΩ per path.
- Each conducting loop has two phase paths plus the bus path, about 0.6 mΩ total.

$$P_{Cu} \approx 80^2 \times 0.6\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 3.8\ \text{W per channel}$$

### 2.4 Per-FET distribution, needed for junction temperature

Over a full electrical cycle, each FET is "the always-on LS" for 1/3 of the time and "the PWM HS/LS" for 1/3. Worst case for the LS FET is low duty (low D), which is not the 80 A burst case. At burst, assume D ≈ 0.9.

- Average loss per FET is about P_cond/6 × 2 (two of six FETs conduct at any instant), so about **6.8 W average per FET**.
- Peak is during that FET's conduction sector: about I²R = **10.2 W instantaneous**.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. There is one hard-switched commutation pair per PWM period: HS turn-on and HS turn-off, with the LS acting as the synchronous rectifier.

### 3.1 Components

**(a) V-I overlap (hard switching, HS FET):**
$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(40\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery** (LS body diode recovers at HS turn-on):
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\,\text{nC}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$
Real Q_rr rises steeply with di/dt and temperature. Budget 2–3× this, about 0.15 W.

**(c) Output capacitance:**
$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \times 2 = 0.5(40\text{n})(25.2)(48\text{k})(2) = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead-time intervals per period):
$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot600\,\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.84\ W}$$

This is as large as the overlap loss. Cutting dead time to 150 ns, which the driver propagation matching must permit, saves about 0.9 W per channel.

**(e) Gate drive** (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET):
$$P_g = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} \times 2 = 60\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k}\cdot2 = \mathbf{0.058\ W}$$
