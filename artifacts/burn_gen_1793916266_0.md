## 0. Architecture Decisions and Feasibility Flags

Four items need to be settled before the math.

**1. The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** The H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. Each of the four ESC channels gets its own small MCU running AM32 (AT32F421K8U7 baseline; STM32G071 as an alternate). This board is therefore a "4-in-1 ESC + FC" AIO with five MCUs. Bi-directional DShot600 is the link between the H743 and the four ESC MCUs. That link is what Section 4 allocates.

**2. "80A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) footprint is thermally marginal.** The model in Section 3 shows roughly 24 W of dissipation per channel at 80 A. With all four channels at 80 A simultaneously, that is about 95 W into a board with about 2.5 J/K of thermal mass, or about 38 K/s. Based on that, this document uses the following ratings:
- 80 A per channel, single-channel burst: ≤ 2 s.
- 80 A on all four channels: ≤ ~1 s.
- Continuous rating: ~35–40 A per channel with prop-wash airflow.

If the product spec requires sustained 80 A, the board must grow to 30.5×30.5 mm or larger with 5×6 mm FETs.

**3. Bus voltage assumption.** The design assumes 6S with V_bus,max = 25.2 V. That requires 40 V MOSFETs and 35 V-rated bulk capacitance (50 V preferred).

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right]$$

$$R_{ds}(125^\circ C) = 1.0 \times (1 + 0.006 \times 100) = 1.6\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 2.2 RMS current including ripple

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{dc}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph\text{-}ph}\, f_{sw}}$$

Worked example for a typical 5" motor at burst:
- L_ph-ph ≈ 20 µH, D ≈ 0.85, V_bemf ≈ 20 V.
- ΔI_pp ≈ (5.2 × 0.85)/(20 µH × 48 kHz) ≈ 4.6 A.
- The ripple term is 4.6²/12 = 1.8 A², which is negligible against 6400 A².
- Therefore I_rms ≈ 80 A.

### 2.3 Conduction path per commutation step

In any 60° commutation sector, two phases conduct:
- **PWM leg:** high-side (HS) on for D; low-side (LS) synchronous rectifier on for (1−D), excluding dead time.
- **Return leg:** LS on for 100% of the sector.

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}\left[D + (1-D)\right]_{PWM\ leg} + I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}\Big|_{return} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.6\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{20.5\ W\ per\ channel}$$

Per-FET values:
- **Instantaneous** (while conducting): 80² × 1.6 mΩ = **10.2 W**. This number sets junction transient rise.
- **Average over an electrical cycle:** 20.5/6 ≈ **3.4 W**. Each FET conducts about 1/3 of the cycle.

### 2.4 Copper and shunt losses (do not ignore at 80 A)

| Element | Resistance | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase/bus copper per channel (2 oz, ~0.3 mΩ effective round-trip) | 0.3 mΩ | 1.9 W |
| Shunt (if per-channel; often only a common shunt), 0.25 mΩ | 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 3.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap (HS turn-on and turn-off, PWM leg)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 3.2 Reverse recovery (LS body diode, after dead time)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$

Q_rr scales with di/dt and I. Treat this value as a lower bound and verify with a double-pulse test.

### 3.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 60\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.073\ W}$$

### 3.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

This term is large. Cutting dead time from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per channel, so it is the cheapest optimization available. Achieving it requires a fast driver and low-Q_g FETs.

### 3.5 Gate drive

$$P_{g} = Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} \times 2\ \text{FETs switching} = 60\text{nC}\times10\times48\text{k}\times2 = 0.058\ \text{W}$$

Most of this dissipates in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs.

### 3.6 Per-channel loss budget at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

| Loss term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 20.48 |
| V–I switching | 1.94 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 |
| Q_rr | 0.06 |
| Q_oss | 0.07 |
| Gate drive | 0.06 |
| **FET + driver subtotal** | **23.84** |
| Copper + shunt | ~3.5 |
| **Channel total** | **~27.3 W** |
| **All four channels** | **~109 W** |

The efficiency check at 80 A × 25.2 V × D (≈ 1.7 kW per channel) gives ~98.4%. The efficiency looks good; the problem is purely heat density.
