## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_0\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.0 × (1 + 0.55) = **1.55 mΩ**.

Because R_DS rises with temperature, the loss and temperature feed back on each other. Iterate to a fixed point:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_c + R_{\theta JC}\,P\big(R_{DS}(T_j^{(n)})\big)$$

### 1.2 Six-step (trapezoidal) commutation with complementary PWM

At any instant, one phase is sourcing current (HS active), one is sinking it (LS on), and one is floating.

- **Active phase pair.** The current I flows through exactly two "effective switches":
  - one in the PWM leg, where HS conducts for D and the synchronous LS conducts for 1−D;
  - one in the static leg, where LS (or HS) is on 100%.
- **PWM leg.** Conduction is D·I²R + (1−D)·I²R = I²R, independent of D. This assumes ideal synchronous rectification and excludes dead time (§2.3).

The instantaneous conduction loss per motor is therefore:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) + I_{rms}^2\,R_{Cu}$$

### 1.3 Including ripple current

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{avg}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus} D (1-D)}{L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

Assume a typical 5–7 inch motor with L_ph ≈ 15 µH phase-to-phase, a worst case of D = 0.5, and V = 25.2 V:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{15\,\mu H \times 48\,kHz} = 8.75\ A$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = 6400 + 6.4 = 6406.4\ A^2$$

The ripple adds only 0.1%, so it is negligible at 48 kHz.

### 1.4 Per-FET averaged loss over an electrical cycle

Each FET conducts for 120° (1/3) of the electrical period:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \tfrac{1}{3}\, I^2 R_{DS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(6406)(1.55\,m\Omega) = 3.31\ W$$

### 1.5 Copper loss

Assume R_Cu ≈ 0.15 mΩ per phase-pair path (2 oz, multi-layer stitched, including pads and solder):

$$P_{Cu} = 6406 \times 0.15\,m\Omega = 0.96\ W$$

### 1.6 Conduction result at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

| Item | Per motor | 4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction, 2·I²R | 19.86 W | 79.4 W |
| Copper | 0.96 W | 3.84 W |
| **Total conduction** | **20.8 W** | **83.3 W** |

At a 25 °C cold start the FET conduction is 12.8 W per motor. The fixed-point iteration in §3.3 determines where it actually settles.

## 2. Switching Losses at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only one leg switches at a time in six-step: the PWM leg, consisting of the HS hard-switching plus the LS synchronous rectifier.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V-I overlap (HS FET)

Gate driver: FD6288Q-class at 10 V, with R_g,ext = 4.7 Ω, internal R_g = 1.5 Ω, and driver R ≈ 15 Ω source / 8 Ω sink.

Turn-on drive current:

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{dr} - V_{pl}}{R_{dr,src} + R_{g,ext} + R_{g,int}} = \frac{10-3}{15+4.7+1.5} = 0.33\ A$$

Turn-off drive current:

$$I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{8+4.7+1.5} = 0.21\ A$$

The voltage transition time is set by Q_gd:

$$t_{v,on} = \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = 36\ ns, \qquad t_{v,off} = \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,off}} = 57\ ns$$

Add the current transition (about 8 ns each). This gives t_on ≈ 44 ns and t_off ≈ 65 ns.

$$P_{sw,VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_{on}+t_{off}) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(109\,ns)(48\,kHz) = 5.27\ W$$

**Recommendation.** This is the dominant switching term. Reducing turn-on R_g,ext to 2.2 Ω, while keeping turn-off at 4.7 Ω via an anti-parallel diode, brings the total to about 4.1 W. The cost is higher dV/dt (about 0.7 V/ns), so check EMI.

### 2.2 Reverse recovery and output capacitance

$$P_{Qrr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50n \times 25.2 \times 48k = 0.060\ W$$

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 55n \times 25.2 \times 48k = 0.067\ W$$

The Q_oss term counts the HS charging the LS C_oss plus its own discharge, approximately.

### 2.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction

Assume t_dt = 100 ns per edge, with two edges per period:

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200n \times 48k = 0.61\ W$$
