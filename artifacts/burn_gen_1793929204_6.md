**Architectural note.** The STM32H743 cannot practically commutate four BLDC motors at 48 kHz while also running ArduPilot. The correct partition is:

- The H743 acts as the flight controller and sends DShot600 to the ESC MCUs.
- Four ESC MCUs each drive a 3-phase bridge, which makes 24 MOSFETs in total.

## 1. Power Stage Topology (per motor)

```
 VBAT ──┬──────────┬──────────┬───────── (bulk caps + local MLCCs)
        │          │          │
      Q_AH       Q_BH       Q_CH        High side (bootstrapped)
        │          │          │
   ─────┼─ PH_A ───┼─ PH_B ───┼─ PH_C ── to motor
        │          │          │
      Q_AL       Q_BL       Q_CL        Low side
        │          │          │
 PGND ──┴──────────┴──────────┴── R_shunt (0.25 mΩ, per motor or shared) ── GND
```

In any commutation sector:

- One high-side FET is PWM-switched at 48 kHz, with duty D.
- Its complementary low-side FET (same leg) conducts during (1 − D) for synchronous rectification.
- One low-side FET on another leg is held fully ON for the whole 60° sector.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.004\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

| T_j | R_DS(on) |
|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 1.30 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 1.40 mΩ (design value) |

### 2.2 Instantaneous bridge path

At every instant, exactly two FET channels carry the phase current I_ph in a synchronous bridge. During the PWM-on interval these are the high-side FET and the opposite-leg low-side FET. During the PWM-off interval they are the same-leg low-side FET and the opposite-leg low-side FET. Therefore:

$$
P_{cond,bridge} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) + I_{ph}^2\,R_{Cu}
$$

R_Cu is the copper and solder path from the battery pad to the motor pad. On a nano board it is about 0.4 mΩ, which is significant at this resistance scale.

### 2.3 Per-device RMS over one electrical revolution

Each leg spends 1/3 of the period sourcing current (high side), 1/3 sinking (low side), and 1/3 floating.

- High-side FET: $I_{RMS,H} = I_{ph}\sqrt{D/3}$
- Low-side FET: $I_{RMS,L} = I_{ph}\sqrt{(1 + (1-D))/3} = I_{ph}\sqrt{(2-D)/3}$

The worst case for the low side occurs at low duty with high current, such as punch-out from low throttle or a locked rotor. In that case the low-side FETs dominate.

### 2.4 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

| Term | Expression | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Bridge FETs | 2 × 80² × 1.40 mΩ | **17.9 W** |
| Copper path | 80² × 0.40 mΩ | **2.6 W** |
| Shunt | 80² × 0.25 mΩ | **1.6 W** |
| **Conduction subtotal per motor** | | **22.1 W** |
| Worst single FET (low side, D = 0.5) | 80² × 0.5 × 1.40 mΩ | 4.5 W |
| High-side FET (D = 0.9) | 80² × 0.3 × 1.40 mΩ | 2.7 W |

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Hard switching occurs only on the PWM'd high-side FET. The low-side FET commutates through its body diode during dead time, so its losses come from dead-time conduction and reverse recovery.

### 3.1 Transition times from gate charge

$$
t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{G,src}} = \frac{10\ \text{nC}}{1.0\ \text{A}} \cdot k_{eff},\qquad
t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{G,sink}}
$$

The factor k_eff ≈ 1.2–1.5 accounts for gate resistance, Miller-plateau driver droop, and common-source inductance. The design values are:

- t_r ≈ 12 ns
- t_f ≈ 8 ns
- t_r + t_f ≈ 20 ns

### 3.2 Loss terms per motor (V = 25.2 V, I = 80 A, f = 48 kHz)

| Mechanism | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 20 ns × 48k | **0.97 W** |
| Coss (E_oss ≈ ½ Q_oss V, Q_oss = 40 nC) | ½ · Q_oss · V · f | 0.024 W |
| Reverse recovery (Q_rr = 30 nC) | Q_rr · V · f | 0.036 W |
| Dead-time body diode (V_F = 0.8 V, t_dt = 50 ns, 2 edges) | V_F · I · 2 t_dt · f | **0.31 W** |
| Gate drive (Q_g = 50 nC, 10 V, 2 switching FETs) | 2 · Q_g · V_GS · f | 0.048 W (mostly in driver/R_g) |
| **Switching subtotal per motor** | | **≈ 1.39 W** |

### 3.3 Frequency sensitivity

$$
P_{sw}(f) \approx 2.9\times10^{-5}\ \text{J} \cdot f
$$

| f_sw | P_sw per motor |
|---|---|
| 24 kHz | 0.70 W |
| 48 kHz | 1.39 W |
| 96 kHz | 2.78 W |

At 80 A, conduction loss is about 94 % of the total. Switching frequency is not the thermal lever; R_DS(on) and copper resistance are. 48 kHz is justified by the lower current ripple on low-inductance racing motors.

### 3.4 Total burst dissipation

| Scope | Value |
|---|---|
| Per motor | 22.1 + 1.4 ≈ **23.5 W** |
| 4 motors at 80 A simultaneously | **≈ 94 W** |
| FC, regulators, VTX (separate) | ≈ 2–4 W |
