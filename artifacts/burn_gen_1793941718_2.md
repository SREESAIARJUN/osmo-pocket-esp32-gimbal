# STM32H743 AIO + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #15): Design Model and Feasibility Review

## 0. Architecture Clarifications

Three points affect every calculation below.

1. **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz motor PWM.** In a 4-in-1 design, the H743 runs ArduPilot and outputs DShot600. Each motor channel needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071. That ESC MCU drives the gate driver at 48 kHz. The netlist below reflects this split.
2. **An 80 A burst on a 20×20 mm board is thermally limited to roughly 1–3 seconds.** This is not a sustainable rating, as shown in §3. Commercial 20×20 boards advertising 40–60 A are quoting short-burst figures under prop wash.
3. **Component parameters are representative.** They are typical of 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm power MOSFETs. Replace them with your selected part's datasheet values before release. The model's structure is exact; the numbers are only as good as the inputs.

## 2. Loss Model (Per Motor, Six-Step Trapezoidal Commutation)

At any instant, two phase legs carry current. One leg PWMs with complementary high-side/low-side switching. The other leg's low-side FET is held on. The current path therefore always passes through two conducting channels.

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C, R_ds(on) = 1.6 mΩ:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.6\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{20.5\ W}$$

Add PCB copper resistance. A 20 mm path in 2 oz copper, 3 mm wide, is about 0.16 mΩ per layer. With 2–3 paralleled layers, that adds roughly 0.1 mΩ, or about **0.6 W per motor**.

### 2.2 Switching Loss (Hard-Switched PWM Leg Only)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.3 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$P_{dt} = 2\,V_{SD}\,I_{ph}\,t_{dt}\,f_{sw} = 2 \times 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

Dead time is a significant loss term. Reduce it to 100 ns if your driver and FET combination allows, which halves this loss.

### 2.4 Reverse Recovery, C_oss, and Gate Drive

$$P_{rr} = V_{bus}Q_{rr}f_{sw} = 0.06\text{ W}$$

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.03\text{ W}$$

$$P_{gate} = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 0.06\text{ W}$$

The gate drive loss is dissipated mostly in the driver, not the FETs.

### 2.5 Totals

| Term | Per motor | 4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 20.5 W | 82.0 W |
| Copper | 0.6 W | 2.4 W |
| Switching | 1.45 W | 5.8 W |
| Dead time | 1.23 W | 4.9 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.15 W | 0.6 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 23.9 W** | **≈ 95.7 W** |

**Conclusion: conduction dominates (~86%).** Raising f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz costs only about 1.4 W per motor. The 80 A figure is what kills the thermal budget, not the 48 kHz switching frequency.

Worst single-FET dissipation is the low-side FET of the held leg, which conducts all the time during its commutation sector:

- During its sector: I²R = 80² × 1.6 mΩ = 10.2 W
- Averaged over commutation (on 1/3 of the electrical cycle, plus synchronous rectification share): about **5–7 W average, with 10+ W peaks**

### 3.2 Two-Node Transient Model

The model has two thermal nodes:

- **Board node:** heated by the total power P_tot and cooled by convection through R_ba.
  $$C_{board}\frac{dT_b}{dt} = P_{tot} - \frac{T_b-T_a}{R_{ba}}$$
- **Junction node:** sits above the board by the FET's power times its junction-to-board resistance.
  $$T_j = T_b + P_{FET}\, Z_{th,jb}(t)$$
  Use Z_th,jb of about 2 K/W at steady state, with a time constant of about 10 ms.

The convection resistance R_ba depends on airflow:

- With prop wash at 10–15 m/s, h ≈ 80 W/m²K over about 1000 mm² of exposed board area, so R_ba ≈ 12 K/W.
- In still air, R_ba ≈ 40 K/W.

Over a burst, convection removes only about 2–5 W, so the burst is essentially **adiabatic**:

$$\frac{dT_b}{dt} \approx \frac{95.7 - 4}{2.9} \approx \mathbf{32\ K/s}$$
