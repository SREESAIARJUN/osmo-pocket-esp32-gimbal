## 0. Architecture Decisions and Assumptions

### 0.1 Partitioning

ArduPilot runs on the STM32H743 as the flight controller. It does not commutate motors. The 4-in-1 ESC therefore uses four dedicated ESC MCUs that run AM32 (or BLHeli_32-class firmware) and communicate with the H743 over bidirectional DShot600.

- **ESC MCU:** AT32F421K8U7 (QFN-32, 120 MHz). It is the common AM32 target and supports a 48 kHz PWM setting.
- **Why not one H743 for all four motors:** Driving 4×6-step commutation from the H743 would need 24 complementary PWM outputs plus BEMF comparators. That is a custom-firmware project, not an ArduPilot configuration.

### 0.2 Form-Factor Warning

A true 20×20 mm board cannot physically host 24 power MOSFETs, 4 gate drivers, 4 MCUs and bulk capacitance while also carrying 80 A per channel.

This design therefore assumes:

- a **stacked nano:** 20×20 FC board on top of a 25.5×25.5 mm (20×20 mounting) ESC board, or
- a 25.5 mm whoop AIO.

The thermal numbers in §3 show that **80 A is a sub-2-second burst rating** at this size, not a continuous one.
