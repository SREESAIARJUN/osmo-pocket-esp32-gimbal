# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Thermal Model and Bi-Directional DShot600 Allocation

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and a Feasibility Flag

I don't have the Job #43 requirements package, so this document is built on stated assumptions. Each assumption is parameterized so you can substitute values from your BOM and datasheets.

**Architecture correction.** In a 4-in-1 ESC, the STM32H743 does not drive the gates. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot600. Each of the four ESC channels has its own MCU (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32). That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM and returns eRPM telemetry. The model below follows that split.

**Feasibility flag: 80 A burst on a "nano" board.** At 80 A on all four channels, the board dissipates about 70 W. A 20×20 or 30×30 mm board can hold that for only about 2–4 s (Section 3). Sustained capability is roughly 25–35 A per channel. Market "80 A" ratings at this size are typically 10 s single-motor bench numbers. The spec should say exactly which burst it means (duration, number of channels, airflow).

| Symbol | Meaning | Assumed value |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 6S nominal | 25.2 V (22.2 V nominal) |
| I_pk | Burst phase current | 80 A |
| f_sw | PWM frequency | 48 kHz |
| R_ds,25 | MOSFET R_DS(on) at 25 °C, 40 V class | 0.8 mΩ |
| α | R_DS(on) temperature coefficient | ≈0.0045 /K (×1.4 at 115 °C) |
| t_r + t_f | Switching transition time at gate | 20 ns |

| R_cu | PCB copper per conducting path | ≈0.15 mΩ ×2 |
| FET count | 6 per ESC, 24 total | Single FET per switch |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

**Commutation model.** The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM). At any instant, two legs carry the phase current I:

- **Leg A (PWM leg):** the high-side conducts for duty D, the low-side for 1−D. The leg always dissipates I²R_ds.
- **Leg B (return leg):** the low-side is on continuously and dissipates I²R_ds.

So the conduction loss per ESC channel is independent of duty:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) + 2\,I^2 R_{cu}$$

The on-resistance rises with junction temperature:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

Because R_ds depends on T_j, and T_j depends on dissipated power, the loss and temperature must be solved iteratively. The loop is: T_j → R_ds → P → T_j.

At T_j ≈ 115 °C:

- R_ds = 0.8 × 1.405 ≈ 1.12 mΩ
- FET loss: P_FET = 2 × 80² × 1.12 mΩ = **14.3 W**
- Copper loss: P_cu = 2 × 6400 × 0.15 mΩ = **1.9 W**

The hottest single FET is the return-leg low-side, at about 7.2 W during its 120° conduction window. Averaged over one electrical cycle, the loss spreads across all six FETs: about 2.4 W per FET when evenly distributed.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Each PWM period has one hard-switched turn-on and one hard-switched turn-off, both on the PWM leg.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·20 ns·48k | 0.97 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 50 nC·25.2·48k | 0.06 W |
| Output capacitance | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw (≈ hard-switched share) | 0.04 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·100 ns·48k | 0.31 W |
| Gate drive (driver IC) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw | 0.06 W |
| **Total switching** | | **≈1.44 W** |

**Sensitivity.** Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw, while conduction loss is fixed at about 16.2 W. Dropping to 24 kHz therefore saves only about 0.7 W per ESC. At 80 A, the 48 kHz choice is not the thermal driver. Conduction loss dominates at roughly 92%.

**DC-link ripple.** Input capacitor RMS current is:

$$I_{C,rms} = I\sqrt{D(1-D)}$$

This peaks at 40 A RMS at D = 0.5. You need roughly 4–6× low-ESR polymer or MLCC capacitors per ESC side, plus a bulk 35 V electrolytic of at least 1000 µF at the pads. Capacitor loss is I²·ESR ≈ 40² × 2 mΩ ≈ 3.2 W if undersized, which is a hidden heat source.

**Total per ESC channel at 80 A:** P_ch ≈ 14.3 + 1.9 + 1.44 ≈ **17.6 W**. For all four channels: **≈70 W**.

## 3. Thermal Network

### 3.1 Static network (per FET)

```
Tj --[Rth_jc ≈ 1.0 K/W]-- Tcase --[Rth_cb ≈ 2–4 K/W via pad+vias]-- Tboard --[Rth_ba]-- Tambient
```

**Board-to-air resistance.** For a 30×30 mm board with both faces in prop-wash (h ≈ 80 W/m²K, A ≈ 1.8×10⁻³ m²):

$$R_{th,ba} = \frac{1}{hA} \approx 6.9\ \text{K/W (whole board)}$$

**Steady-state limit.** With T_board ≤ 100 °C and T_amb = 40 °C, the allowed dissipation is P_max ≈ 60/6.9 ≈ 8.7 W for the whole board, or 2.2 W per channel. Solving 2.2 = I²(2·1.0 mΩ + 0.3 mΩ) gives:

$$I_{cont} \approx 31\ \text{A per channel (all four loaded)}$$

This matches real-world ratings for 30×30 boards and confirms the model is calibrated sensibly.

### 3.2 Transient burst model

The board behaves as a lumped thermal capacitance:

- Copper (≈2 g × 0.385 J/gK) + FR4 (≈2.5 g × 1.1 J/gK) + FETs and caps ≈ **C_th ≈ 4 J/K**
- Time constant: τ = R·C ≈ 6.9 × 4 ≈ 28 s

Temperature during a burst:

$$T_b(t) = T_0 + P R_{th}\left(1-e^{-t/\tau}\right)$$

Allowable burst time:

$$t_{burst} = -\tau \ln\!\left(1 - \frac{T_{lim}-T_0}{P R_{th}}\right)$$

With P = 70 W, T₀ = 50 °C (cruise-heated), and T_lim = 100 °C:

t = −28·ln(1 − 50/483) ≈ **3.1 s** (all four motors at 80 A).

With a single motor at 80 A (others at 15 A), P ≈ 20 W, giving **≈13 s**.

**Junction margin during burst.** The FET die responds in about 10 ms, so the junction sits above the board by roughly:

ΔT_jb ≈ 7.2 W × (1.0 + 3) K/W ≈ 29 K

The board limit is therefore 100 °C, which keeps T_j ≈ 130 °C, below the 175 °C rating with margin.

**Required mitigations:**
- 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner copper; 6-layer stackup preferred
- Thermal vias at 0.3 mm diameter / 0.6 mm pitch under every FET drain pad
- An NTC per ESC side fed to AM32 temperature limiting
- The firmware current limit set to the I_cont from Section 3.1, with a burst timer based on t_burst
