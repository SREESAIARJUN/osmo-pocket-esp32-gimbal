# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Thermal, Switching, and Bidirectional DShot Design Model (Job #39)

## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

### Architecture correction

On every production AIO of this class, the STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. The architecture is:

- **H743 (ArduPilot):** generates DShot600 frames and decodes bidirectional eRPM telemetry.
- **Four ESC MCUs** (AT32F421 / STM32G071-class, running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware): run 6-step commutation at 48 kHz PWM.
- **Three-phase gate drivers and 24 MOSFETs** in the power stage.

The 48 kHz figure is an ESC-firmware parameter. The H743 sees only DShot. I model both domains below.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

During any commutation sector, current flows from the high-side of phase A, through the motor, to the low-side of phase B:

- Phase A high-side (Q_AH) conducts for duty D.
- Phase A low-side (Q_AL) freewheels synchronously for (1 − D).
- Phase B low-side (Q_BL) conducts 100 % of the time.

This means two MOSFET channels are always in series with the motor current, regardless of duty:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 R_{hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{ph}^2 R_{hot} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{hot}$$

The hot resistance is:

$$R_{hot} = R_{25}\,k_T \approx R_{25}\left(1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right),\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At 80 A:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\,\text{mΩ} = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ motor}$$

$$P_{cond,4} = \mathbf{76.8\ W}$$

The hottest single device is Q_BL. It carries the full 80 A for 1/3 of each electrical cycle, and its average dissipation is about 3.2 W. Peak instantaneous dissipation, which is what drives the junction, is 9.6 W.

### 1.2 Copper losses (often ignored, but not negligible)

2 oz copper has a sheet resistance of:

$$R_\square = \rho / t = 1.72\times10^{-8} / 70\,\mu\text{m} = 0.246\ \text{mΩ}/\square$$

A phase path of about 3 squares, paralleled over 4 layers, gives about 0.18 mΩ. The motor current crosses two such paths:

$$P_{Cu,ch} \approx 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.18\,\text{mΩ} \approx 2.3\ \text{W}$$

Battery-side current is lower:

$$I_{bat} \approx D \cdot I_{ph}$$

At D = 0.8, I_bat ≈ 64 A per motor and about 256 A total. The pads and battery leads become the next bottleneck. Use 10 AWG minimum, or 12 AWG pairs.

### 1.3 Current-sense shunt

Assume 0.25 mΩ on the battery line at 256 A:

$$P = 256^2 \times 0.25\,\text{mΩ} = 16.4\ \text{W}$$

**Recommendation:** use Hall sensing (TMCS1100-class) or per-ESC low-side estimation instead. A single shunt is not viable at this current.

## 2. Switching Loss at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd phase's high-side (hard switching) and its complementary low-side (dead-time diode conduction) incur switching losses.

| Term | Formula | Value per motor |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f | ½·25.2·80·20 ns·48 k = **0.97 W** |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss·V·f | 40 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.05 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f | 50 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.06 W** |
| Dead-time diode | V_f·I·2·t_dt·f | 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f | 2·60 nC·10·48 k = **0.06 W** (dissipated in driver) |
| **Total** | | **≈ 1.75 W** |

Across all four motors this is about 7 W. Compared with 24 kHz, the 48 kHz setting roughly doubles switching losses (about 3.5 W extra). It buys quieter, smoother low-throttle behaviour.

Switching loss is dominated by overlap and dead time. Reducing dead time to 50 ns would save about 0.3 W per motor, but only if the gate loop inductance supports it without shoot-through.

### Bus-capacitor ripple current

Per channel, the input ripple current peaks at D = 0.5:

$$I_{C,rms} = I_{ph}\sqrt{D(1-D)} = 40\ \text{A}$$

The four channels are asynchronous, so they add roughly as √4:

$$I_{C,rms,total} \approx 80\ \text{A (worst case)}$$

This requires:
- 4 × 10 µF/50 V X7R MLCCs per channel at the half-bridges.
- One external 35 V low-ESR electrolytic of at least 1000 µF on the battery pads, which is mandatory.

## 3. Total Burst Budget and Thermal Model

### 3.1 Total dissipation

| Source | Power (W) |
|---|---|
| Conduction | 76.8 |
| Switching | 7.0 |
| Copper | 9.2 |
| Gate drivers, LDOs, MCUs | ~1.5 |
| **Total** | **≈ 95 W** |

### 3.2 Board thermal mass

| Component | Mass / heat capacity |
|---|---|
| Copper: 30.5² mm × 70 µm × 6 layers ≈ 390 mm³ | 3.5 g → 1.35 J/K |
| FR4 at 1.6 mm | ~2.8 g → 3.1 J/K |
| MOSFETs and solder | ~0.3 J/K |
| **Total C_th** | **≈ 4.8 J/K** |

### 3.3 Board-average temperature rise (adiabatic)

Convection under prop wash is roughly 15–25 W at a 60 K rise and is neglected at first order:

$$\frac{dT_{board}}{dt} \approx \frac{95}{4.8} \approx 20\ \text{K/s}$$
