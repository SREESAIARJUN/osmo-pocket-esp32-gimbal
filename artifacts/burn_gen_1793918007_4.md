# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #37): Design Model

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Red Flags

Three architectural points come first, because they change the math.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot runs no ESC firmware. The architecture is therefore H743 (ArduPilot) → DShot600 bidirectional → 4× ESC MCUs (AT32F421 running AM32 assumed) → gate drivers → 24 MOSFETs. The "DMA timer allocation" applies to the FC↔ESC link only.
2. **80 A per motor on a nano board (20×20 or 30×30 mm) is a burst-only rating.** This document shows quantitatively that 80 A is survivable for seconds, not continuously. Any datasheet/marketing claim must state the burst duration and starting temperature.
3. **All component values below are design-assumption placeholders**, typical of a 40 V, ~0.9 mΩ class 5×6/3.3×3.3 MOSFET. Replace them with the actual part's datasheet values (R_DS(on) vs T_j, Q_gd, Q_rr, Z_th curve) before sign-off.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation topology
The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification, which is the AM32/BLHeli_32 default. In each 60° sector:
- One phase high-side is PWM'd at duty D, with its low-side complementary.
- One phase low-side is held on as the return path.
- One phase floats for BEMF sensing.

The motor current I therefore always flows through exactly **two conducting channels** (ignoring dead time):

- Duty-on interval: HS(A) + LS(B)
- Duty-off interval: LS(A) + LS(B)

### 1.2 Per-device RMS over one electrical cycle
Each leg conducts 2/3 of the cycle (one third sourcing, one third sinking):

- High-side: I²_rms,HS = I² · D / 3
- Low-side: I²_rms,LS = I² · [(1 − D)/3 + 1/3] = I² · (2 − D) / 3

Summed over the six FETs of one channel: Σ I²_rms = 3·[I²D/3 + I²(2−D)/3] = 2I².

This gives the per-motor conduction loss, independent of D:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond per motor | 4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 0.90 mΩ | 11.5 W | 46 W |
| 100 °C | 1.27 mΩ | 16.3 W | 65 W |
| 125 °C | 1.40 mΩ | 17.9 W | 72 W |

**Worst single device.** At low duty, the low-side FET carries I²(2−D)/3, approaching 2/3·I². At 125 °C this is 0.667·6400·1.40 mΩ ≈ **6.0 W in one FET**. That FET is the hotspot and sets the thermal limit.

### 1.4 Parasitic copper and shunt losses
- Shunt: I²·R_sh = 6400 · 0.25 mΩ = 1.6 W per motor (if the shunt is in the bus path).
- Copper: a 2 oz, 3 mm wide, 15 mm long trace has R ≈ 0.08 mΩ per layer. With four paralleled layers this is ≈0.02 mΩ, giving ≈0.13 W per path. This is negligible only if the layers are genuinely paralleled with via farms (≥ 20 × 0.3 mm vias per 80 A node).

### 1.5 Thermal-runaway check
Two quantities must be compared:
- Loss slope: dP/dT_j = 2I²R_25·α = 2·6400·0.9e-3·0.0055 ≈ 0.063 W/K.
- Dissipation slope: 1/R_θ(effective).

The design is stable if 0.063 · R_θ < 1, i.e. **R_θ,eff < 15.9 K/W per motor channel**. Section 4 shows the hotspot path is about 7–10 K/W, which is stable but with less than 2× margin.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd high-side hard-switches. Its low-side complement commutates through the body diode during dead time.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap (HS)
$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 30\,\text{ns} \cdot 48\,\text{kHz} = 1.45\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction (LS)
$$P_{dt} = V_F I \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 400\,\text{ns} \cdot 48\,\text{kHz} = 1.23\ \text{W}$$

This is the biggest lever. Cutting dead time to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per motor, but the minimum must be validated against the driver's propagation skew plus t_f and margin.

### 2.3 Reverse recovery (dissipated in HS at turn-on)
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\,\text{nC}\cdot25.2\cdot48\,\text{kHz} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Output capacitance (HS turn-on discharges the HS C_oss and charges the LS C_oss)
$$P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 60\,\text{nC}\cdot25.2\cdot48\,\text{kHz} = 0.073\ \text{W}$$
