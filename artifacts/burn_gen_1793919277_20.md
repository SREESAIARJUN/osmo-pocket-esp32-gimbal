## 0. Architecture Corrections

Three requirements as written would lead to a non-functional or unsafe design.

1. **The H743 cannot drive the ESC gates directly.** Bi-directional DShot is a protocol between the flight controller and *separate ESC MCUs*. The architecture is therefore:
   - STM32H743, running ArduPilot.
   - DShot600 on 4 lines.
   - 4 ESC MCUs (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32).
   - 4 three-phase gate drivers.
   - 24 MOSFETs.

   The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs, not the H743.

2. **80 A on a nano (20×20 mm) board is a burst-only rating of roughly 1 s.** Section 3 shows that the realistic continuous rating is about 25 A per channel. 80 A sustained requires at least a 30.5×30.5 mm board, 5×6 mm MOSFETs, and a heatsink. The model below is written so you can swap in parameters.

3. **6S bus assumed** (25.2 V maximum). 40 V MOSFETs are the correct class. 30 V parts are not acceptable on 6S once regen and ringing spikes are included.

## 2. Loss Model per ESC Channel

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

The on-resistance at temperature is:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)] = 1.0\,\text{mΩ}\times(1+0.0045\times110) \approx 1.5\,\text{mΩ}$$

In six-step operation with synchronous rectification, exactly **two devices carry I at every instant**:
- one in the PWM leg (either the high side or the complementary low side), and
- the low side of the return leg.

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.0015 = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

Each device conducts for 1/3 of the electrical period, so the average per MOSFET is:

$$P_{cond,dev} = \frac{I^2 R_{ds}}{3} = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

**Copper term.** Phase and bus traces add losses. Assume about 0.3 mΩ of trace per current path; two paths are in conduction:

$$P_{Cu} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.0003 = 3.8\ \text{W}$$

This term is frequently ignored and is significant at this current.

### 2.2 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches.

**V–I overlap (hard switching on the high side):**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 1.94\ \text{W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

**C_oss discharge:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = 0.023\ \text{W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (two dead times per cycle):**

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\,I\,(2t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

**Gate drive.** All 6 gates are counted as worst case. This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET.

$$P_g = 6\,Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 6 \times 30\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.086\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{sw,total} \approx \mathbf{2.7\ W}$$

### 2.3 Totals at the 80 A Burst

| Loss | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Copper traces | 3.8 W | 15.4 W |
| Switching + dead time | 2.7 W | 10.8 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ) | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
