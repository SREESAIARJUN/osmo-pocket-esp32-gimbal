## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does not commutate motors. Each ESC channel uses its own commutation MCU running AM32 (assumed STM32G071 here). The H743 talks to those MCUs over bi-directional DShot600. This is the only architecture ArduPilot supports. An H743 driving 12 half-bridges directly would need a custom ESC firmware port, so this design does not attempt it.

**Assumed device parameters.** These are representative of current 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm PQFN FETs. Substitute the datasheet values for your chosen part.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S, full charge) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| R_DS(on) at 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | R₂₅ | 1.0 mΩ |
| R_DS(on) temperature factor at T_j = 125 °C | k_T | 1.5 → R_hot = 1.5 mΩ |
| Total gate charge | Q_g | 40 nC |
| Switching charge (Q_gs2 + Q_gd) | Q_sw | 10 nC |
| Output charge | Q_oss | 40 nC |
| Reverse recovery charge | Q_rr | 50 nC |
| Body diode forward voltage | V_f | 0.8 V |
| Gate drive current (FD6288Q-class driver) | I_g | ≈1 A |
| Dead time | t_dt | 50 ns |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| Burst phase current, per ESC channel | I | 80 A |

**Feasibility flag.** 80 A per channel on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board is far beyond what it can sustain. The model below quantifies how long a burst can actually last. Treat any "80 A" marketing figure as an adiabatic, time-limited number. It is not a continuous rating.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

AM32 uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. At any instant, exactly two FETs are in the current path: one high-side and one low-side, in different legs. Each FET carries current for 1/3 of the electrical cycle.

**Per-FET loss during PWM-active conduction** (high-side duty D, low-side conducting 1−D):

$$P_{cond,HS} = D\,I^2 R_{DS(on)}(T_j), \qquad P_{cond,LS,sync} = (1-D)\,I^2 R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

**Bridge total.** The current always passes through two FETs, so:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2 I^2 R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

**Temperature dependence:**

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1} \Rightarrow k_T \approx 1.5 \text{ at } 125\,°\text{C}$$

**At 80 A:**

- P_cond,bridge = 2 · 80² · 1.5 mΩ = **19.2 W per ESC channel**
- Peak instantaneous per FET at D → 1: 80² · 1.5 mΩ = 9.6 W
- Thermally averaged per FET (1/3 duty over the electrical period): **≈ 3.2 W**

**Thermal runaway check.** R_DS(on) rises with T_j, which raises loss, which raises T_j. The fixed point is:

$$T_j = T_b + R_{\theta,jb}\cdot\tfrac{1}{3}I^2R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

This has a stable solution only if:

$$R_{\theta,jb}\cdot\tfrac{1}{3}I^2R_{25}\,\alpha < 1$$

With R_θ,jb = 12 K/W: 12 · 2.13 · 0.005 = 0.13. This is well below 1, so the system is stable.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg is PWM-switching at a time (the high-side PWM leg, with its complementary low side).

### 2.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap

Rise and fall times are set by the switching charge and gate current:

$$t_r \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_g} = \frac{10\,\text{nC}}{1\,\text{A}} = 10\,\text{ns}$$

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 20\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM period:

$$P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 100\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = V_{bus} Q_{rr} f_{sw} = 25.2 \cdot 50\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 V_{bus} Q_{oss} f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.024\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (two FETs per switching leg)

This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET:

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 40\text{n} \cdot 10 \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.038\ W}$$

### 2.6 Total per ESC channel at 80 A

| Loss component | Power (W) |
|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.20 |
| Switching overlap | 0.97 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.31 |
| Reverse recovery + Coss | 0.08 |
| Gate drive | 0.04 |
| **Total per channel** | **≈ 20.6 W** |
| **All four channels at 80 A** | **≈ 82 W** |

**Efficiency.** Output power is about 25.2 V × 80 A × D. At D = 1 that is roughly 2 kW, giving about 99 % electrical efficiency. The problem is not efficiency; it is the 82 W dumped into a few grams of PCB.
