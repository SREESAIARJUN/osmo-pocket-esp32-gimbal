# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #21): System Architecture, Loss Model, DShot/DMA Allocation, Netlist

## 0. Feasibility Findings

Three issues change the design before any schematic work starts.

**1. The H743 cannot run the ESCs itself.**
- Four 3-phase motors need 12 complementary half-bridge pairs, plus BEMF sensing and commutation.
- The H743 has 2 advanced timers (TIM1/TIM8, 3 complementary pairs each) and an HRTIM (5 units, about 5 pairs). That gives at most about 11 half-bridges, fewer than 12.
- It would also have to run ArduPilot's EKF and scheduler on the same core.
- **Architecture decision:** the H743 is the flight controller only. Each ESC channel gets a dedicated MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32), commanded over bidirectional DShot600. This is also the only configuration ArduPilot supports.

**2. 80 A burst on a 20×20 mm "nano" board is not thermally credible as a sustained rating.**
- The model in §3 gives about **1–1.5 s** for all four channels at 80 A before the board reaches about 110 °C.
- A single channel at 80 A lasts about 5–6 s.
- Recommendations:
  - Rate the board **45 A continuous-burst (5 s) per channel**, with 80 A as a ≤1 s single-motor peak.
  - Or move to a 30.5×30.5 mm board with 5×6 mm FETs.

**3. Battery-side current.** Four channels at 80 A is 320 A, which no 20×20 pad set or XT60 connector can carry. Battery current at partial duty is lower, but the input pads and bulk capacitors must still be sized (§3.6).

All component parameters below are **representative values for a 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN MOSFET class**. Substitute datasheet values for the selected part before release.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction loss

Temperature-dependent on-resistance:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.2 × 1.45 = **1.74 mΩ** (use 1.8 mΩ for margin).

With synchronous rectification, the current always flows through exactly two channel resistances: the high side (or its complementary low-side partner during the off-time) plus the return low side. Conduction loss is therefore **independent of duty cycle**:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j) = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.8\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{23.0\ W}$$

Each FET conducts for 120° of each electrical cycle, so the average per FET is 23.0 / 6 ≈ **3.84 W**, with an instantaneous peak of 11.5 W.

Copper loss:

$$P_{cu} = I^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{1.92\ W}$$

### 2.2 Switching losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg hard-switches. The high side switches with V-I overlap; the complementary low side switches at near-zero voltage but incurs body-diode conduction and reverse recovery.

**V-I overlap (hard-switched high side):**
$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(30\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = 40\,\text{nC}\times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$
