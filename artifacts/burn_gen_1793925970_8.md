The power-stage math below applies to the ESC board. The DMA/timer allocation applies to the H743.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

**Assumed scheme.** Six-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous (complementary) PWM. At any instant, current flows through exactly two legs in series. In each leg, either the high-side or the low-side FET conducts. Therefore two channels are always conducting, regardless of duty cycle.

**Hot on-resistance:**

$$R_{hot} = k_T \cdot R_{25} = 1.6 \times 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega = 1.6\,\text{m}\Omega$$

**Per-motor conduction loss:**

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \cdot I^2 \cdot R_{hot} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.0016 = \mathbf{20.5\ W}$$

**Per-FET average.** Each phase is active for 2/3 of the electrical period, split between its high- and low-side FETs by the duty cycle D:

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{hot} \cdot D \cdot 2,\qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{hot}(1-D)\cdot 2$$

- At D = 0.5: approximately 3.4 W per FET.
- At D close to 1 (full throttle punch-out): the high-side FETs carry nearly all of it, about 6.8 W each. **This is the design-limiting case.**

**Parasitic copper and shunt losses.** Assume 0.3 mΩ of trace plus 0.25 mΩ of shunt per current path [A]:

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.55\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 3.5\ \text{W per motor}$$

This is non-trivial at nano scale. Use 2 oz or heavier inner layers and stitched pours.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-chopped leg switches. The commutation transitions occur at the electrical frequency, which is much lower than f_sw and is neglected here.

**1. Hard-switching V·I overlap:**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(40\text{n})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**2. Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

**3. Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

**4. Dead-time body-diode conduction (two dead-time intervals per period):**

$$P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{n}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**5. Gate drive (two FETs in the switching leg):**

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 0.038\ \text{W}$$

The gate-drive loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs.

**Switching subtotal:** approximately **2.7 W per motor**.

**Loss budget at 80 A:**

| | Per motor | All 4 motors at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 20.5 W | 82.0 W |
| Copper + shunt | 3.5 W | 14.0 W |
| Switching + dead time | 2.7 W | 10.6 W |
| **Total** | **26.7 W** | **≈107 W** |

**Efficiency check:** P_out ≈ 25.2 V × 80 A × 4 ≈ 8 kW, so the electrical efficiency is about 98.7%. That figure is fine. **The absolute heat is the problem.**

### 3.2 Adiabatic Burst Heating

Convection is negligible during a short burst, so the board heats at:

$$\frac{dT}{dt} = \frac{P}{C_{th}} = \frac{107}{2.2} \approx \mathbf{49\ K/s}$$

**Allowable burst duration.** Starting at 40 °C ambient and allowing the PCB to reach 110 °C (ΔT = 70 K), which keeps T_j at or below 125 °C with margin:

$$t_{burst} = \frac{70}{49} \approx \mathbf{1.4\ s}\ \text{(all four motors at 80 A)}$$

### 3.3 Junction Transient (Per FET, Worst-Case High-Side)

**Inputs:**
- P ≈ 6.8 W (conduction) + 1.3 W (half the switching loss) ≈ 8 W.
- Transient Z_th(j-c) at 1 s ≈ 1.2 K/W; at 10 ms ≈ 0.3 K/W [A, read from datasheet Z_th curve].

**Result:**

$$\Delta T_{jc} \approx 8\ \text{W} \times 1.2\ \text{K/W} \approx 10\ \text{K above local copper}$$

This is acceptable. **The bulk board temperature is the binding constraint, not the die.**
