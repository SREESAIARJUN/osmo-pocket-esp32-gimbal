## 0. Architecture Decisions and Feasibility Flags

Several requirements conflict with physics or with common practice. These are resolved below before the math, because they change the math.

| Issue | Resolution |
|---|---|
| The STM32H743 does not commutate motors in production 4-in-1 designs. | The H743 is the **flight controller** (ArduPilot). Each motor channel has a dedicated ESC MCU (AT32F421 running AM32) that drives a 3-phase gate driver. The H743 talks to the ESCs over bi-directional DShot600. |
| "80A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board | 4 × 80A is about **100W of dissipation** (see §1). A 20×20 board cannot hold this for more than about 1–2 s. **Minimum footprint is 30.5×30.5 mm** (or a 2-board stack: FC 20×20 over ESC 30×30). 80A is treated as a **≤5 s burst rating**. Continuous rating is about 45–50A with airflow. |
| 48 kHz PWM | This is the ESC switching frequency, set in AM32 (range 24–48 kHz). It is independent of the DShot rate. |

## 1. Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 1.1 Conduction Topology

The ESC uses six-step (trapezoidal) commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. In any 60° sector, current flows through:

- **Leg A:** the high-side FET (duty D) **or** the low-side FET (duty 1−D, synchronous rectification).
- **Leg B:** the low-side FET, conducting continuously.

The current path therefore always contains **exactly two FETs**, independent of D (dead time is treated separately):

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot \big(D\,R_{HS} + (1-D)\,R_{LS}\big) + I^2 R_{LS} \approx 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

The temperature-dependent on-resistance is:

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big]
$$

At T_j = 125°C: R = 1.2 mΩ × 1.45 = **1.74 mΩ**

$$
P_{cond}(80A) = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.74\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{22.3\ W}
$$

**Per-FET average.** Each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical cycle. So 22.3 W ÷ 6 FETs ≈ **3.7 W per FET**. The low-side FETs carry more than this at low D; the high-side FETs carry more at high D.

### 1.2 Hard-Switching (V–I Overlap) Loss

Only the leg being PWM'd switches. That leg sees one turn-on and one turn-off event per PWM period:

$$
P_{sw,VI} = \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw}
= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}
$$

### 1.3 Capacitive, Reverse-Recovery and Gate Losses

| Loss term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| C_oss | Q_oss · V_bus · f_sw | 30n × 25.2 × 48k = **0.036 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw | 40n × 25.2 × 48k = **0.048 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs switching) | 2 · Q_g · V_drv · f_sw | 2 × 40n × 10 × 48k = **0.038 W** (dissipated in the driver, not the FET) |

### 1.4 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per period:

$$
P_{dt} = V_f\,I\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}
$$

This is a strong incentive to cut dead time to about 100 ns with a fast driver: it halves this term.

### 1.5 Shunt and Copper

| Element | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Shunt | I² R_sh = 6400 × 0.25 mΩ | **1.6 W** (this is why the 2512 rating is ≥3W) |
| Copper (phase + bus traces, 2 oz, ~0.3 mΩ effective) | I² R_cu | **≈1.9 W** |

### 1.6 Per-Channel Totals at 80A, 48 kHz, 6S

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction | 22.3 |
| Switching (V–I) | 1.45 |
| Dead time | 1.23 |
| C_oss + Q_rr | 0.08 |
| Shunt | 1.6 |
| Copper | 1.9 |
| **Channel total** | **≈28.6 W** |
| **4 channels simultaneously** | **≈114 W** |

**Frequency sensitivity.** The frequency-dependent part of the loss is about 2.8 W per channel at 48 kHz, i.e. ≈0.058 W/kHz. Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.4 W per channel. The cost is more audible noise and higher current ripple on low-inductance motors.

**Efficiency check.** Electrical input is 25.2 × 80 = 2016 W per channel. So η_ESC ≈ 98.6% at burst, which is consistent with good 4-in-1 ESCs.

## 2. Thermal Model

### 2.1 Network (per FET, lumped)

```
Tj ──[Rθjc 1.2 K/W]── Tcase ──[Rθc-pcb 1.5 K/W]── Tpcb ──[Cθ_board]──┬──[Rθ_conv]── Tamb
                                                                      └── (thermal mass dominates during burst)
```

- Junction-to-board: Rθ_j-pcb ≈ **2.7 K/W**. This assumes thermal vias under the drain pad: an array of ≥12 × 0.3 mm vias.
- Junction thermal time constants are milliseconds. On a 1–5 s burst, the junction is in quasi-steady-state relative to the board. The board itself is **adiabatic**.

### 2.2 Board Heat Capacity (30.5×30.5 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz outer / 1 oz inner)

| Mass | Calculation | Heat capacity |
|---|---|---|
| FR4 | ≈ 0.093 dm² × 1.6 mm → ~3.0 g × 1.1 J/gK | 3.3 J/K |
| Copper | ≈ 2.5 g × 0.385 J/gK | 1.0 J/K |
| 24 FETs + solder + caps | ≈ 4 g × 0.7 J/gK | 2.8 J/K |
| Optional Al heat-spreader plate | 6 g × 0.9 J/gK | 5.4 J/K |
| **C_θ total** | without / with plate | **7.1 / 12.5 J/K** |
