# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (80 A Burst): System Model

## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

**The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production-grade 4-in-1 design, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running BLHeli_32 or AM32. That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM, handles back-EMF zero-cross detection, and replies to bidirectional DShot (BDShot). The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to the four ESC MCUs over DShot600. The model below follows that split.

| Parameter | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal | |
| Burst current | **80 A per motor channel**, burst | Not continuous |
| PWM | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous rectification), 6-step | |
| MOSFETs | 6 per channel, 24 total, 40 V N-ch, PDFN 5×6 | |
| R_DS(on) | 0.9 mΩ at 25 °C, 10 V V_GS | Representative, not a specific part |
| Q_g / Q_rr / C_oss | 55 nC / 40 nC / 1.5 nF | Representative |
| Switching times | t_r + t_f ≈ 20 ns (driver-limited) | |
| Dead time | 200 ns per edge | |
| Body-diode V_f | 0.8 V | |

**Size reality check.** A 20×20 mm "nano" form factor cannot carry 80 A per channel with PDFN 5×6 FETs. Below, "nano" is treated as a 30.5×30.5 mm stack. The result of Section 1.5 shows that "80 A" is a burst of seconds, not a continuous rating.

## 1. Power Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 115 °C: R = 0.9 mΩ × 1.495 ≈ **1.35 mΩ**.

This is solved self-consistently: assume T_j, compute losses, compute T_j, and iterate. It converges in 2–3 iterations.

### 1.2 Conduction Loss

In 6-step operation with synchronous rectification, two FETs carry the full phase current at every instant:
- the PWM leg's high-side FET (duty D) and its low-side synchronous-rectifier FET (duty 1−D), which together give one conducting FET over the full period;
- the return leg's low-side FET, which is on continuously.

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

With I ≈ 80 A (flat-top trapezoidal current, so I_rms ≈ I_pk):

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.35\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{17.3\ W}$$

The commutation sequence rotates through the six FETs. Averaged over an electrical cycle, each FET dissipates P_cond/6 ≈ 2.9 W. However, each FET carries about 8.6 W while it is conducting. On short burst timescales, that instantaneous figure is what drives junction temperature.

### 1.3 Switching Loss at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches, so there is one hard turn-on and one hard turn-off per period.

**V–I overlap loss**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery** (synchronous FET body diode)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

**Output capacitance**

$$P_{oss} = C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1.5\text{ nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

(This counts ½CV² per FET for both FETs in the leg.)

**Dead-time body-diode conduction**

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

Dead-time loss is the largest switching term. Tuning dead time from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per channel.

**Gate drive** (dissipated in the driver, not the FETs)

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2 = 55\text{ nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{ k} \times 2 = 0.05\text{ W}$$

### 1.4 Totals at 80 A Burst

| Loss term | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 17.3 W | 69.2 W |
| V–I switching | 0.97 W | 3.9 W |
| Dead time | 1.23 W | 4.9 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.10 W | 0.4 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, 80²×0.25 m) | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| Copper traces/vias (~0.3 mΩ effective) | 1.9 W | 7.7 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 23.1 W** | **≈ 92.5 W** |

Output power at 80 A × 22 V ≈ 1760 W per channel, so electrical efficiency is about 98.7%. The difficulty is not efficiency. It is removing 92 W from roughly 9 cm² of PCB.

### 1.5 Thermal Model

**Junction rise (fast, ms scale)** uses a Foster network. Typical PDFN 5×6 values: Z_th,jc(10 ms) ≈ 0.5 K/W and R_th,jc ≈ 1.2 K/W.

$$\Delta T_{jc} = P_{FET,inst} \cdot Z_{th,jc}(t) \approx 8.6 \times 1.2 \approx 10\ \text{K}\ \text{(quasi-steady)}$$

Junction-to-case rise is not the limit.

**Board bulk rise (second scale)** is the limit. Lumped capacitance model:

$$C_{th} = \sum m_i c_i$$

| Component | Calculation | C_th |
|---|---|---|
| PCB, 8-layer, 2 oz, ~6 g | 6 g × 0.9 J/(g·K) | ≈ 5.4 J/K |
| 24 FETs | 24 × 0.15 g × 0.7 J/(g·K) | ≈ 2.5 J/K |
| Optional Al heat-spreader, 4 g | 4 g × 0.9 J/(g·K) | ≈ 3.6 J/K |

$$\frac{dT_{board}}{dt} = \frac{P_{tot} - (T_{board}-T_{amb})/R_{th,ba}}{C_{th}}$$

With prop-wash convection, h ≈ 80 W/(m²·K) over about 20 cm² of both-side area, giving R_th,ba ≈ 6 K/W:

$$\tau = R_{th,ba} C_{th} \approx 6 \times 11.5 \approx 69\ \text{s},\qquad T_{ss} = T_{amb} + 92.5 \times 6 \approx 600\ ^\circ\text{C (unreachable; burst-only)}$$

**Burst duration** from T_board,0 = 50 °C to the 105 °C board limit. The junction then sits at about 115 °C, leaving margin to the 150–175 °C rating.

$$t_{burst} = -\tau \ln\!\left(1 - \frac{\Delta T}{P R_{th}}\right) = -69 \ln\!\left(1 - \frac{55}{555}\right) \approx \mathbf{7.2\ s}\ \text{(with spreader)}$$

Without the spreader (C_th ≈ 7.9 J/K, τ ≈ 47 s): **≈ 4.9 s**.

**Specification statement:** 80 A per channel, all four channels simultaneously, for 5 s (no spreader) or 7 s (with spreader). The recommended continuous rating is about 40–45 A per channel, where conduction loss drops by 4×.

## 2. ArduPilot Bidirectional DShot600 on STM32H743

### 2.1 Protocol Timing

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Bit rate | 600 kbit/s, t_bit = 1.667 µs |
| T1H | 0.75 × t_bit = 1.25 µs |
| T0H | 0.375 × t_bit = 0.625 µs |
| Frame | 16 bits = 26.7 µs (11-bit throttle + telemetry bit + 4-bit inverted CRC for BDShot) |
| Turnaround | ESC switches the line to output after ~30 µs |
| Telemetry reply | 21-bit GCR at 5/4 × 600 k = 750 kbit/s, 1.333 µs/bit, 28 µs |
| Total transaction | ≈ 26.7 + 30 + 28 + guard ≈ **90 µs per timer group** |

### 2.2 Timer Arithmetic

At 480 MHz core, the APB2 timer clock (TIM1/TIM8) is 240 MHz. APB1 timers (TIM2–5) also run at 240 MHz when the timer clock prescaler is set.

ArduPilot programs the timer so that one bit equals N ticks:

$$PSC = \frac{f_{TIM}}{f_{bit} \cdot N} - 1,\qquad ARR = N-1,\qquad CCR_1 = \lceil 0.75N \rceil,\ CCR_0 = \lceil 0.375N\rceil$$

With N = 20: PSC = 240 M / (600 k × 20) − 1 = **19**, ARR = 19, CCR₁ = 15, CCR₀ = 7–8. The exact constants vary by ArduPilot version.

**Output** uses one DMA stream on TIMx_UP, writing burst-mode DMAR to CCR1..4. All four channels of a timer go out simultaneously in one 18-entry × 4-word buffer.
