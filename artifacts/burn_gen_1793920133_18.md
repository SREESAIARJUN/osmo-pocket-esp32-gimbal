# STM32H743 AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC) Nano Design: Electrical, Thermal, and DShot Timing Model

## 0. Assumptions and Corrections

These choices shape every number that follows.

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Architecture | The **STM32H743 is the flight controller** running ArduPilot. Each ESC channel has its own small MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071, AM32/BLHeli_32-class) with a 3-phase gate driver. The H743 does not drive gates. It is the wrong part for that job and cannot commutate four motors at 48 kHz alongside the flight stack. |
| Supply | 6S LiPo: 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal. Design to V_bus = 25.2 V plus ringing, so use 40 V FETs. |
| MOSFET | 40 V N-ch, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN. R_DS(on),25°C = 1.0 mΩ (max, V_GS = 10 V). Q_g = 30 nC, Q_gd = 5 nC, Q_oss = 25 nC, Q_rr = 20 nC, V_SD = 0.8 V, R_θJC = 1.5 K/W. **Substitute your real datasheet values; the equations remain valid.** |
| Modulation | Six-step trapezoidal with synchronous (complementary) PWM, f_sw = 48 kHz, dead time t_dt = 50 ns |
| Board | 20×20 mm ("nano") or 30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz copper |

**Feasibility finding:** an 80 A per-channel rating on a 20×20 mm board is a **sub-second burst rating only**, not a thermal operating point. Section 3 proves this. Treat "80 A burst" as a firmware-enforced current limit with a time constraint, not as a continuous capability.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path
In any six-step sector, two legs carry the phase current I:
- **PWM leg:** the high-side conducts for D·T, the low-side (synchronous rectification) conducts for (1−D)·T.
- **Return leg:** the low-side conducts for the full sector.

At every instant, exactly two FET channels are in series with the motor current. The per-channel conduction loss is therefore nearly independent of duty:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j), \qquad I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}$$

### 1.2 Ripple term
The ripple is

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\, D(1-D)}{L_{ph-ph}\, f_{sw}}$$

Worst case is D = 0.5. For a typical 5″ motor, L_ph-ph ≈ 20 µH:

ΔI_pp = 25.2·0.25 / (20e-6·48e3) = **6.6 A**, so ΔI²/12 = 3.6 A².

This is negligible next to I² = 6400 A² (0.06 %).

### 1.3 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha (T_j-25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

| T_j (°C) | R_DS(on) (mΩ) | P_cond @ 80 A per channel (W) | ×4 channels (W) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 | 1.00 | 12.8 | 51.2 |
| 100 | 1.45 | 18.6 | 74.2 |
| 125 | 1.60 | 20.5 | 81.9 |

**Per-FET distribution.** Over one electrical revolution, each of the 6 FETs carries 1/6 of the channel loss on average. At 100 °C that is about 3.1 W per FET. During its active sector, though, a static low-side dissipates the full I²R = 9.3 W.

### 1.4 Thermal-runaway (stability) criterion
The loop is stable only if the loop gain is below 1:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\cdot R_{\theta} = 2I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\, R_\theta < 1$$

At 80 A with R_θ = 12.5 K/W (board to air, per channel; see 3.3):

2·6400·1e-3·0.006·12.5 = **0.96**

This is marginal. 80 A continuous would run away thermally even before reaching T_j,max, which is further evidence that the rating must be time-limited.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Per switching leg (one hard-switched transition pair per PWM period):

| Term | Equation | Value @ 80 A, 25.2 V |
|---|---|---|
| V·I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw, with t_r = t_f = 10 ns | ½·25.2·80·20e-9·48e3 = **0.97 W** |
| Body-diode dead time | V_SD·I·2t_dt·f_sw | 0.8·80·100e-9·48e3 = **0.31 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 20e-9·25.2·48e3 = **0.024 W** |
| C_oss | Q_oss·V·f_sw | 25e-9·25.2·48e3 = **0.030 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw | 2·30e-9·10·48e3 = **0.029 W** (in driver, not FET) |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 1.33 W per channel** |
