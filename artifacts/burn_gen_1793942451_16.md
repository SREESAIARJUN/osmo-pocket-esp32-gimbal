# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #8): 80 A Burst Thermal, 48 kHz Switching, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Design Package

## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Design Basis

Three points need settling before the math.

1. **The H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC MCU.** BLHeli_32, AM32 and Bluejay commutate on a dedicated MCU per motor (AT32F421, STM32G071 or EFM8BB51). On this board the H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bi-directional DShot600. Four ESC MCUs (or one per pair) run the bridges at 48 kHz. Running FOC/commutation for four motors on the H743 alongside ArduPilot is not supported by ArduPilot.
2. **"Nano" plus 80 A burst is a transient-only rating.** The analysis below shows a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm board cannot sustain anything near 80 A per motor. 80 A must be specified with a duration, roughly 1–5 s depending on airflow. I derive that duration rather than assert it.
3. **All component values are design parameters.** Replace them with values from your actual MOSFET and gate-driver datasheets. The equations are part-agnostic.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Path

The bridge uses trapezoidal (6-step) commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM, as in AM32 and BLHeli_32 by default.

- During each 60° electrical sector, one high-side FET (HS) of phase A is PWM'd and one low-side FET (LS) of phase B is held on.
- During the off-time, the complementary LS of phase A conducts the freewheel current.

So the motor current always flows through exactly two FET channels plus copper:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2\Big[\underbrace{D\,R_{HS} + (1-D)\,R_{LS,A}}_{\text{PWM leg}} + \underbrace{R_{LS,B}}_{\text{return leg}} + R_{cu}\Big]
$$

With identical FETs this reduces to $P_{cond} = I^2\,(2R_{DS}(T_j) + R_{cu})$. It is independent of duty cycle, which is a property of synchronous rectification.

### 1.2 Temperature Dependence (Self-Consistent)

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_DS = 0.9 × 1.425 = **1.28 mΩ**.

$$
P_{cond,FET} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.28\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{16.4\ W/motor}\ (8.2\text{ W per active FET})
$$

$$
P_{cu} = 80^2 \times 0.3\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{1.9\ W/motor}
$$

There is a thermal runaway check. Because $\partial P/\partial T_j = I^2 \cdot 2R_{25}\alpha$, the loop is stable only if

$$
R_{th,j\text{-}a}\,\cdot\,2 I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha < 1
$$

At 80 A: $2 \cdot 6400 \cdot 0.9\text{e-}3 \cdot 0.005 = 0.0576$ W/K. Runaway therefore occurs if $R_{th} > 17$ K/W. A nano board's steady-state board-to-air resistance is about 5–10 K/W (see §3), so the system is stable but has a loop gain of 0.3–0.6. **This amplifies temperature rise by 1/(1−G) ≈ 1.4–2.5×.** Iterate T_j; do not use R_25.

### 1.3 Commutation-Averaged Value

In 6-step drive each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical period. The HS duty is D; its complementary LS takes the remainder. Per-FET average loss is therefore:

$$
P_{FET,avg} = \frac{1}{3}\,I^2 R_{DS}\,\big[D + (1-D)\big] + \frac{1}{3}\,I^2 R_{DS}\ \ \Rightarrow\ \ \approx \frac{2}{6}\cdot 2 I^2 R_{DS}
$$

The total stays 16.4 W per motor, spread over 6 FETs: **about 2.7 W per FET averaged**, with 8.2 W instantaneous peaks lasting one sector. At a typical 1–3 kHz electrical frequency, the sector is much shorter than the FET's thermal time constant (~ms), so the average applies at the junction.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches. Per motor, one HS/LS pair commutes at f_sw. All of the following are per motor.

### 2.1 V–I Overlap (Hard Switching, Inductive Load)

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}V_{bus} I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot30\text{e-}9\cdot48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{1.45\ W}
$$

A more exact version uses the gate charges: $t_{sw} \approx Q_{gd}\,R_{g,tot}/(V_{drv}-V_{plateau})$.

### 2.2 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

There are two dead-time windows per PWM period:

$$
P_{dt} = V_F\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot400\text{e-}9\cdot48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{1.23\ W}
$$

This term is often the second-largest switching loss on ESCs. Reducing dead time from 200 ns to 100 ns saves 0.6 W per motor, but the minimum is set by driver propagation skew.

### 2.3 Reverse Recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 40\text{e-}9\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

Q_rr also adds a current spike to the turn-on transition. It is captured in t_r if measured.

### 2.4 Output Capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2}C_{oss,eff}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw}\times 2 = 1.5\text{e-}9\cdot635\cdot48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{0.046\ W}
$$

### 2.5 Gate Drive

This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET:

$$
P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw}\times 2 = 60\text{e-}9\cdot10\cdot48\text{e}3\cdot2 = \mathbf{0.058\ W}
$$

For six FETs per motor with only two switching, the driver load is about 58 mW per motor. This is within FD6288Q-class bootstrap drivers. Check the bootstrap capacitor: $C_{bs} \gg Q_g/\Delta V_{bs}$, so with 60 nC and 0.5 V droop you need ≥ 120 nF. Use **1 µF**.

### 2.6 Per-Motor and System Totals at 80 A, T_j = 110 °C

| Loss term | W/motor |
|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 16.4 |
| Copper | 1.9 |
| V–I switching | 1.45 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.15 |
| **Total** | **≈ 21.1 W** |
| **4 motors simultaneously** | **≈ 84 W** |

Bridge efficiency at 80 A, 22.2 V, ~1.78 kW in per motor (D ≈ 1): ≈ 98.8%. The percentage is excellent, but the absolute heat is far beyond what a nano board can sustain.
