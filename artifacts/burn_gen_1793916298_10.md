## 0. Two Architectural Corrections

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot has no firmware path for an H7 to drive six-step or FOC bridges directly. A viable architecture is:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──bidir DShot600 ×4──► 4× ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32 firmware)
                                                  └─► gate driver ─► 3-phase MOSFET bridge ×4
```

Everything below assumes this split.

**2. 80 A per channel on a "nano" board is a burst-only rating.** The thermal analysis in §3 shows that a 20×20 or 30×30 mm board cannot sustain 80 A per channel. It can survive about 1–3 s bursts. Treat any continuous rating above roughly 30–35 A per channel on 30×30 mm with skepticism.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Topology

In six-step commutation, two phases conduct at any instant:

- **PWM on:** current flows through the high-side switch (HS) of phase A, the winding, and the low-side switch (LS) of phase B.
- **PWM off (synchronous):** current flows through LS of phase A, the winding, and LS of phase B.

So the current always passes through exactly two MOSFET channels plus one PCB path. This holds regardless of duty cycle D.

### 2.2 Conduction Loss

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on),hot} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

$$P_{pcb} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{pcb} = 6400 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

When computing temperature rise, iterate on R(T_j). The resistance follows

$$R(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005/\text{K}$$

and the junction temperature depends on that resistance:

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}(R(T_j))\, R_{th,j\text{-}pcb}$$

This loop converges in two or three iterations.

### 2.3 Switching Loss (hard-switched leg only)

Only the PWM-ing leg switches. Commutation events occur at electrical frequency, far below 48 kHz, so they are negligible here.

**Overlap (V–I crossover):**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery** (LS body diode recovering when HS turns on):

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss(er)} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} \times 2 = 1.5\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.046\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead-time intervals per period):

$$P_{dt} = V_f I_{ph} \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}$$

**Gate drive** (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET):

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.058\ W}$$

### 2.4 Per-Channel and Board Totals at 80 A Burst

| Loss term | Watts |
|---|---|
| FET conduction | 19.20 |
| PCB / shunt conduction | 3.20 |
| Switching overlap | 1.94 |
| Dead time | 0.31 |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.15 |
| **Per channel** | **≈ 24.8 W** |
| **Four channels** | **≈ 99 W** |

Electrical efficiency at 80 A × 25.2 V (2016 W per channel) is about 98.8%. The efficiency looks fine. The problem is the heat flux density: roughly 100 W into 9 cm².
