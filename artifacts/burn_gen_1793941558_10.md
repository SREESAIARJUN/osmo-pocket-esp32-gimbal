# STM32H743 AIO + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #34): Power-Stage Model, Thermal Budget, Bi-Dir DShot600 DMA Plan, and Reference Netlists

## 0. Architecture Note and Key Finding

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 is the **flight controller**, not the ESC controller. A 4-in-1 ESC that supports bi-directional DShot uses one dedicated ESC MCU per channel, such as an AT32F421 running AM32 or an STM32G071. The H743 drives four DShot600 lines to those MCUs and decodes the eRPM telemetry they send back. All models below assume this partition.

**Key finding.** **80 A on all four channels at once is not a thermally sustainable condition on a nano (20×20 / 25.5×25.5) board.** The model shows:

- **Single channel at 80 A:** about 2.5 s from a 40 °C board to the 110 °C board limit (adiabatic).
- **All four channels at 80 A:** about 1.2 s to the same limit.

"80 A burst" should therefore be specified as **≤1 s on all four channels or ≤2 s on one channel**, with a continuous rating around 35–40 A per channel. Section 2 derives this.

## 1. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 1.1 Conduction Losses

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, two phases conduct at any instant:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for a fraction *D* of each period and the low-side FET for (1−*D*).
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET is held on for the whole 60° sector.

The phase current therefore always passes through exactly two channels in series:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

with the temperature dependence

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

The current includes PWM ripple:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp} \approx \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{2 L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5–7" motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH line-to-line, D = 0.5, V_bemf ≈ 0.5·V_bus), ΔI_pp ≈ 4.4 A. The ripple term adds less than 0.03 % and can be neglected at 80 A.

**Copper (PCB) losses.** With 2 oz copper (70 µm), the sheet resistance is 0.246 mΩ/□. A phase path of about 3 squares, paralleled across 4 inner layers, gives about 0.18 mΩ, plus about 0.1 mΩ of shared battery-pad trunk.

$$P_{Cu} \approx I^2 (R_{path}) \approx 6400 \times 0.28\text{ m}\Omega \approx 1.8\text{ W}$$

**Conduction losses at 80 A:**

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond = 2I²R |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W |
| 100 °C | 1.375 mΩ | 17.6 W |
| 125 °C | 1.50 mΩ | 19.2 W |

### 1.2 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. During dead time the low-side body diode carries current, so the high-side turn-on is a hard turn-on against diode recovery.

**Transition times:**

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{g,src}} = 15\text{ ns}, \quad t_f \approx \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{g,snk}} = 10\text{ ns}$$

Allow about 5 ns for layout parasitics, giving t_r + t_f ≈ 30 ns.

**(a) V–I overlap loss:**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(30\text{n})(48\text{k}) = 1.45\text{ W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\cdot 25.2\cdot 48\text{k} = 0.060\text{ W}$$

**(c) Output capacitance (lost at hard turn-on):**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,er} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot 2\text{n}\cdot 635\cdot 48\text{k} = 0.030\text{ W}$$
