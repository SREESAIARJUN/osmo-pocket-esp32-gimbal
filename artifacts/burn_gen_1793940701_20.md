# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model

**Scope:** 80 A burst thermal and loss model, 48 kHz switching, and ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 timer/DMA allocation.

---

## 0. Architecture Assumptions

The job brief can be read in a way that hides a design flaw, so I am fixing the architecture first.

1. **The STM32H743 is the flight-controller MCU, not the ESC MCU.** Production 4-in-1 ESCs run one small MCU per motor, such as AT32F421 or STM32G071, with AM32 or BLHeli_32 firmware. A single H743 doing both the 400 Hz+ attitude loop and four sensorless FOC/6-step commutation loops is not supported by ArduPilot. DShot only makes sense in the H743 → ESC-MCU direction, so this design assumes **H743 FC + 4× ESC MCUs on a shared AIO or stacked board**.
2. **"Nano" with 80 A burst on all four channels is thermally marginal.** The numbers below show why. This design assumes a **30.5 × 30.5 mm** power board, 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner copper, 6-layer construction, and 6S operation (25.2 V max).
3. **Device parameters are representative placeholders** (a 40 V, ~0.8 mΩ, 3.3 × 3.3 mm class N-MOSFET). Replace them with your selected part's datasheet values before release. The model is parametric for that reason.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

With 6-step trapezoidal commutation and complementary (synchronous) PWM, two MOSFETs are always in the current path: one in the high-side leg and one in the low-side leg. During the PWM off-time, the high-side switch's current transfers to its synchronous low-side partner.

**MOSFET conduction, per channel:**

$$P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2 \cdot R_{ds(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

Including inductor ripple ΔI (peak-to-peak):

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

With motor inductance L ≈ 10 µH at 50 % duty:

$$\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L\,f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{10\,\mu\text{H} \times 48\,\text{kHz}} \approx 13\ \text{A}$$

The ripple term is 13²/12 ≈ 14 A², which is negligible against 6400 A².

$$P_{cond} = 6414 \times 2 \times 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega \approx \mathbf{15.4\ W}$$

**Copper conduction:**

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{pcb} = 6400 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

Copper resistance also rises about 0.39 %/K. At +60 K it becomes ≈ 3.95 W.

**Per-FET distribution.** The high-side FET conducts for D·T and its synchronous low-side partner for (1−D)·T. The commutation low-side FET conducts continuously during its 120° sector. The worst single FET is therefore the commutating low-side FET:

$$P_{FET,max} \approx I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 6400 \times 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega = 7.7\ \text{W (during its sector)}$$

Averaged over a full electrical cycle (1/3 duty):

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx 2.6\ \text{W}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

**V-I overlap** (hard switching on the PWM'd high side):

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery.** The synchronous FET's body diode conducts during dead time, then recovers when the high side turns on:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

The diode-current Irr also adds overlap loss in the high-side FET. Approximate it by adding ~t_rr·I to (t_r + t_f).

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \times 2 = 0.10\ \text{W}$$

**Dead-time diode conduction** (two edges per period):

$$P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dead} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

> **Lever:** cutting dead time to 100 ns saves 0.6 W per channel. This is only safe if the gate driver's propagation-delay mismatch is under ~30 ns and shoot-through is verified on a double-pulse fixture.

**Gate drive** (dissipated in the driver IC and gate resistors, not the FET):

$$P_g = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} \times 2 = 60\,\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\,\text{k} \times 2 = 0.058\ \text{W}$$

**Frequency sensitivity:**

$$\frac{\partial P_{sw}}{\partial f_{sw}} = \frac{P_{VI} + P_{rr} + P_{oss} + P_{dt}}{f_{sw}} \approx 69\ \text{mW/kHz}$$

Moving from 24 kHz to 48 kHz costs about 1.66 W per channel. In return it reduces audible noise and current ripple at low throttle.

### Loss Budget per Channel at 80 A

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| MOSFET conduction (100 °C) | 15.4 |
| PCB copper | 3.2 |
| V-I switching | 1.94 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 |
| Qrr + Coss | 0.16 |
| **Total per channel** | **≈ 21.9 W** |
| **4 channels simultaneous** | **≈ 87.7 W** |

Efficiency at 80 A × ~22 V (motor-side power ≈ 1.76 kW per channel) is about 98.8 %. The efficiency is good, but **88 W of heat on a 30 × 30 mm board cannot be sustained.**
