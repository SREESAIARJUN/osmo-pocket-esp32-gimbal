**Architecture note.** On a 4-in-1 stack, the H743 does not drive the gates at 48 kHz. Each motor channel runs a dedicated ESC MCU, for example an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 firmware, which supports 48 kHz PWM and bidirectional DShot. The H743 runs ArduPilot and produces DShot600 frames, then captures eRPM telemetry on the same wire. The model below follows this split.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Conduction topology
In 6-step trapezoidal commutation, two phases conduct in each 60° sector:
- **Active (PWM) leg:** the high-side FET conducts for D·T_sw. The complementary low-side conducts for (1−D)·T_sw, minus dead time.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET is held ON for the full sector.

At every instant, the phase current passes through **exactly two channels in series**. Per motor channel:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

This holds for any duty cycle, because synchronous rectification puts a FET channel in the path rather than a body diode.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence of R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0055\text{–}0.007\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond,ch at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 0.90 mΩ | 2·6400·0.90 m = **11.5 W** |
| 100 °C | 1.27 mΩ | **16.3 W** |
| 125 °C | 1.44 mΩ | **18.4 W** |

### 1.3 Distribution per device
Over one electrical cycle each of the 6 FETs carries current for 1/3 of the time (120°). The average conduction loss per FET is:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} = \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6}\cdot 2\cdot\frac{1}{2} \approx \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6} \;\Rightarrow\; \approx 3.1\ \text{W @ }125^\circ\text{C}$$

Within a sector the return-leg low-side FET is the hot spot, because it conducts at 100% for 120°. Use the full I²R for its instantaneous (thermal-transient) peak: 80²·1.44 mΩ = **9.2 W**.

### 1.4 PCB copper conduction (do not omit)
The phase and bus copper path is about 25 mm per phase at 2 oz (70 µm), around 8 mm wide, paralleled on 4 layers:

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{w\,t\,n} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot0.025}{0.008\cdot70\times10^{-6}\cdot 4} \approx 0.19\ \text{m}\Omega$$

The loss is P_Cu = 80²·(2·0.19 m) ≈ **2.4 W per channel**. Add the bus/battery-pad copper and the current-sense shunt, if one is used (0.25 mΩ → 1.6 W).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches in a given sector, which means one hard-switched high-side FET plus its complementary low-side.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap

$$P_{sw,VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw}$$

With t_r + t_f ≈ 40 ns (driver source/sink around 1.5/1.8 A, R_g = 4.7 Ω):

$$P_{sw,VI} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot40\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (low-side body diode during dead time)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}\ (\text{approx.}) = 45\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.054\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f\,I_{ph}\cdot 2t_{dt}\cdot f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in driver and R_g, not in the FET die)

$$P_{gate} = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2\cdot60\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k} = 0.058\ \text{W per channel}$$

The driver's 12 V LDO/buck must supply the gate charge for all 4 channels: I_avg = 4·2·Q_g·f_sw = 23 mA.

### 2.6 Per-channel and total budget (T_j = 125 °C, 80 A, 25.2 V)

| Loss term | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 18.4 W | 73.7 W |
| V·I switching | 1.94 W | 7.8 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W | 2.4 W |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.11 W | 0.46 W |
| Gate (driver) | 0.06 W | 0.23 W |
| PCB copper | 2.4 W | 9.7 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 23.5 W** | **≈ 94 W** |

At 80 A × 25.2 V ≈ 2 kW per channel input, efficiency is about 98.8%, but 94 W concentrated on a 30×30 mm board **cannot be sustained**. It is strictly a burst rating.

**Sensitivity to f_sw:** dP/df_sw ≈ (P_sw,VI + P_dt + P_rr + P_oss)/f_sw ≈ 55 µW/Hz per channel. Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.3 W per channel. Raising to 96 kHz costs about 2.7 W per channel.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network (per channel, Foster/Cauer-lumped)

```
P_FET ──[R_θJC=1.0/n_par]──┬──[R_θCB=0.5]──┬──[R_θBA]── T_amb
```
