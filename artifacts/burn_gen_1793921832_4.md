# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

I have no access to prior Job #47 documents, so every parameter below is stated explicitly. Substitute your datasheet values into the equations; the model structure stays the same.

**The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs directly.** It is the flight controller. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, and the H743 talks to those MCUs over bidirectional DShot. That split is what makes bidirectional DShot meaningful.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right]$$

At T_j = 115 °C: R = 1.2 mΩ × 1.495 ≈ **1.79 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Commutation-averaged per-FET loss

In 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous (complementary) PWM:

- Each phase leg is active for 2/3 of the electrical cycle: 120° high-side window, 120° low-side window.
- During a high-side window, the high-side FET conducts for duty D and its complementary low-side conducts for (1 − D).
- The current path always runs through exactly **2 conducting FETs** in series (ignoring dead time).

Per-channel conduction loss:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) \quad\text{(independent of D under synchronous rectification)}$$

Average per-FET loss, with each FET carrying current for about 1/3 of the cycle at D → 1:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)}{3}$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A burst

| Quantity | 25 °C | 115 °C |
|---|---|---|
| P_cond per channel | 2·6400·1.2 m = **15.4 W** | 2·6400·1.79 m = **22.9 W** |
| P_cond per FET (avg) | 2.56 W | 3.82 W |
| P_cond, 4 motors | 61.4 W | **91.6 W** |

### 1.4 Copper and shunt losses (often forgotten)

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 \left(R_{trace} + R_{shunt} + R_{pad}\right)$$

Example values:

- 2 oz outer layer plus 2× 2 oz inner pours in parallel, 5 mm wide, 10 mm path: R ≈ 0.25 mΩ.
- Shunt: 0.5 mΩ. A single low-side shunt carries the total bus current.

Per channel: 6400 × 0.25 m ≈ **1.6 W**. Bus shunt at 4 × 80 A × 0.7 (motor-to-bus current ratio at D ≈ 0.7) ≈ 224 A: **25 W**.

Conclusion: use a ≤0.2 mΩ shunt, or switch to Hall or phase-current sensing.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ed leg switches. The other conducting FET is a static commutation switch.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap (active FET)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

$$= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Body-diode reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

Q_rr also adds turn-on current overshoot. That raises P_VI by roughly (Q_rr / (I·t_r)) ≈ 2.5 % here.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12\,Q_{oss}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.018\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (two FETs switching per channel)

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g\,V_{GS}\,f_{sw} = 2 \times 40\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.038\ \text{W}$$

This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET.

### 2.6 Switching summary per channel at 80 A

| Term | Power |
|---|---|
| V·I overlap | 1.94 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W |
| Q_rr | 0.05 W |
| Q_oss | 0.02 W |
| Gate | 0.04 W |
| **Total P_sw** | **≈ 2.66 W** |

At D = 100 % (full throttle) switching losses go to about zero. The worst case is about D ≈ 0.6–0.8 at high current, which occurs during punch-outs with prop load.
