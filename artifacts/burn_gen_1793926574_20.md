**Architectural clarification.** The H743 runs ArduPilot and produces bidirectional DShot600. The 48 kHz switching happens on the four ESC MCUs, not the H743. The H743 never drives gates. This separation is assumed throughout.

**Red flag: 80 A × 4 channels on a nano/20×20 board is not thermally sustainable.** The model in §2 shows roughly 85–95 W of total loss at full simultaneous burst. A 20×20 board has about 1.5 J/K of heat capacity. That yields a temperature rise of about 60 K per second. 80 A per channel is only credible as:
- a short burst (well under 1 s), or
- one channel at a time, or
- a 30.5×30.5 board with heavier copper.

Treat the "80A" rating as a burst/marketing figure unless the thermal validation in §2.6 passes.

## 1. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\text{–}0.007\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

This gives a typical ratio of 1.5–1.7× at 125 °C. Read α from the "normalized R_DS(on) vs T_j" curve in your FET datasheet.

### 1.2 Conduction loss (6-step, synchronous rectification)

- Two phase legs carry current at any instant.
- In each conducting leg, the current flows through either the high-side FET (fraction D) or the low-side FET (fraction 1−D).
- Each phase conducts for 2/3 of the electrical period.
- Therefore each individual FET carries the current, on average, for about 1/3 of the period.

Per FET:
$$P_{cond,FET} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Per bridge (6 FETs):
$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

The bridge figure is independent of D, because synchronous rectification means some FET is always conducting.

**Worked example at 80 A, T_j = 110 °C:**
- R_DS = 1.0 × (1 + 0.006 × 85) = 1.51 mΩ
- P_cond,bridge = 2 × 6400 × 1.51 m = **19.3 W**
- P_cond,FET ≈ **3.2 W**

**Pairing FETs.** If you use two paralleled FETs per position, R_DS halves and conduction loss halves. Switching loss roughly doubles in its C_oss/Q_g terms.

### 1.3 Switching losses at f_sw = 48 kHz

In 6-step operation, only one leg is PWM-switching at a time. The other conducting leg is held statically on.

| Term | Formula | Value at 25.2 V / 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 20 ns × 48 k = **0.97 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 50 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.06 W** |
| C_oss (switching node) | ½·C_oss·V²·f_sw × 2 FETs | 2 × 0.5 × 1.2 n × 635 × 48 k = **0.04 W** |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_F·I·2·t_dt·f_sw | 0.8 × 80 × 2 × 200 ns × 48 k = **1.23 W** |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver/R_G) | Q_g·V_GS·f_sw × 2 | 2 × 50 n × 10 × 48 k = **0.05 W** |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.35 W** |

Observations:
- Dead time is the largest switching term. Minimize it in ESC firmware, using the shortest safe value verified on a scope with the real driver and FET.
- With the FET's real t_r/t_f at 80 A (often 30–50 ns once you include R_G and the driver's ~1–2 A sink/source capability), the overlap term can reach 1.5–2.5 W.

### 1.4 Other loss terms

- **Current shunt** (if present, e.g. 0.25 mΩ): I²R = 6400 × 0.25 m = **1.6 W**. This is a significant hotspot. Consider a 0.2 mΩ shunt or Hall/phase-based estimation.
- **PCB copper:** for 2 oz copper with ~1 mΩ of total path, 80² × 1 m ≈ **6.4 W**. This is shared between both planes and is often overlooked.
- **Bulk capacitor ESR:** I_cap,rms ≈ I·√(D(1−D)), which is 40 A at D = 0.5. With ESR_total = 5 mΩ, that gives **8 W**. ESR must be extremely low, and the external capacitor must carry most of the ripple (§1.6).

### 1.5 Per-channel and total budget at 80 A burst

| Component | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.3 W | 77 W |
| Switching | 2.4 W | 9.4 W |
| Shunt | 1.6 W | (one shared shunt: 1.6 W at 80 A, or 41 W at 320 A total!) |
| Copper + caps | ~3–5 W | ~15 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 27 W** | **≈ 100+ W** |

**Note on the shared shunt.** A single shared battery shunt at 4 × 80 A = 320 A is not viable. Either:
- use per-channel sensing, or
- rate total battery current at around 150 A and treat 80 A as single-motor transient only.

**Efficiency check.** Output power at 80 A ≈ 80 × 25.2 × D_eff. At D = 0.9, that is about 1.8 kW per channel, so 27 W is about 1.5% loss. The efficiency is good; the problem is power density, not efficiency.
