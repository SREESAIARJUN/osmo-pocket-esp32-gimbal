```
            6S LiPo (25.2 V max / 22.2 V nom)
                    │
        ┌───────────┴────────────────────────────────────────┐
        │  Power stage ×4 (3-phase half-bridge ×3 per motor) │
        │  ESC MCU ×4: AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32 firmware   │
        └───────────┬────────────────────────────────────────┘
                    │ 4× bidirectional DShot600 (single wire, open-drain-ish, 3.3 V)
        ┌───────────┴───────────┐
        │  STM32H743VIT6 FC     │  ArduPilot (ChibiOS)
        │  400 MHz, TIM1 CH1–4  │  IMU (SPI1), Baro (I2C2), OSD (SPI2), Flash (SPI3)
        └───────────────────────┘
```

**Key architectural point.** The H743 does not commutate the motors. Each motor has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. The H743 sends DShot600 commands and receives eRPM telemetry on the same wire. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs.

## 3. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 3.1 Commutation physics

With six-step (trapezoidal) commutation and complementary (active-freewheeling) PWM, two switch positions carry phase current at any instant:

- one high-side position, and
- one low-side position (the active switch or the synchronous-rectifier switch during the PWM off-time).

So, regardless of duty cycle D, the conducting path always contains two R_DS(on) in series:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

The duty-cycle dependence only moves loss between the high-side and low-side FETs of the PWM leg. It does not change the total.

### 3.2 Temperature-corrected R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \mathrm{K^{-1}}
$$

$$
R_{DS(on)}(125\,°C) = 1.2 \times 1.6 = 1.92\ \mathrm{m\Omega}
$$

### 3.3 Per-channel and per-FET conduction loss at 80 A

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.92\ \mathrm{m\Omega} = 6400 \times 3.84\ \mathrm{m\Omega} = \mathbf{24.6\ W}
$$

Each switch position conducts for 120° of every 360° electrical period, so the average per FET is:

$$
P_{cond,FET} = \frac{I^2 R_{DS(on)}}{3} = \frac{6400 \times 1.92\ \mathrm{m\Omega}}{3} = \mathbf{4.10\ W}
$$

Check: 6 FETs × 4.10 W = 24.6 W ✓

### 3.4 RMS refinement

For trapezoidal current with ripple ΔI_pp:

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}
$$

Phase ripple at 48 kHz is

$$
\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf}) D}{L_{LL} f_{sw}}
$$

For a typical 5" motor with L_LL ≈ 20 µH at D = 0.5:

$$
\Delta I \approx \frac{12.6 \times 0.5}{20\,\mu\text{H} \times 48\,\text{kHz}} \approx 6.6\ \mathrm{A}
$$

$$
\frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 3.6\ \mathrm{A^2}
$$

This is negligible against 6400 A² (+0.06 %). The 48 kHz switching frequency is doing its job here.
