# STM32H743 4-in-1 ESC Nano FC (Job #52): Architecture, Loss Model, DShot DMA Plan

## 0. Architecture Corrections

Three issues change the design and should be settled before layout.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot has no motor-commutation firmware. In every production H743 AIO and 4-in-1 stack, each ESC channel has its own MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM and does BEMF sensing. The H743 only sends bidirectional DShot600 over on-board traces. This document uses that partitioning.
2. **80 A burst on a nano footprint is thermally marginal.** "Nano" is taken here as a 20×20 mm mounting pattern on a ~25×25 mm board. Section 2 shows that 4 channels at 80 A dissipate about 90–100 W into roughly 2 J/K of thermal mass. That limits the burst to about 1–2 s. Commercial 20×20 4-in-1s are typically rated 40–60 A burst. Hitting 80 A realistically needs a 30.5×30.5 board, 8 FETs per phase leg, or an enforced burst-duration limit.
3. **All component parameters are assumptions.** They are representative values for 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN MOSFETs and an FD6288Q-class gate driver. Substitute datasheet values for the selected parts before release.

## 1. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 1.1 Commutation Topology

Six-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous (complementary) PWM:
- At any instant, current flows through exactly two legs.
- One leg is PWM-switched (HS during D, LS during 1−D). The other leg's LS is held on.
- During both PWM sub-intervals the motor current passes through **two FET channels in series**.

### 1.2 Conduction Loss

Temperature-dependent on-resistance:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_0\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

Bridge conduction loss, independent of duty under complementary PWM:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j) \;+\; I^2 R_{Cu}$$

Evaluated at T_j = 100 °C:

$$R_{DS} = 1.2(1 + 0.005\cdot 75) = 1.65\ \text{m}\Omega$$
$$P_{FET,cond} = 6400 \cdot 3.30\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{21.1\ W}$$

Copper path (phase pad → FET → bus), assuming R_Cu ≈ 0.5 mΩ:
$$P_{Cu} = 6400 \cdot 0.5\times10^{-3} = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

**Thermal runaway check.** Conduction loss rises with temperature. The coupling is stable only if

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j} \cdot R_{\theta,eff} < 1$$

Here ∂P/∂T = I²·2R₀α = 6400·2.4e-3·0.005 = 0.077 W/K. Steady state is therefore unstable if R_θ,eff exceeds about 13 K/W per channel. A small, unheatsinked board can approach that limit, which is another reason to enforce a burst timer.

### 1.3 Switching Loss (hard-switched PWM leg)

Transition time:
$$t_{sw} = \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_g} = \frac{12\text{ nC}}{1.5\text{ A}} = 8\text{ ns}\quad (t_r + t_f = 16\text{ ns})$$

V·I overlap loss:
$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot16\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.774\ W}$$

Output capacitance loss (both FETs of the switching leg):
$$P_{oss} = 2\cdot\tfrac12 C_{oss(er)}V^2 f_{sw} = 1.5\text{n}\cdot635\cdot48\text{k} = 0.046\ \text{W}$$

Reverse recovery (LS body diode → HS turn-on):
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

Dead-time body-diode conduction (two dead times per cycle):
$$P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2t_{dt}\cdot f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.614\ W}$$

Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs):
$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\cdot30\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k} = 0.029\ \text{W}$$
