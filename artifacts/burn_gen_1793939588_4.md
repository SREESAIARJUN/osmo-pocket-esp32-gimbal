# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC (Job #53): Electro-Thermal and Firmware Architecture Model

This document covers the system architecture and stated design assumptions, the conduction and switching loss models, the electro-thermal model, ArduPilot bi-directional DShot600 timer and DMA allocation, schematic netlists, and a verification plan.

---

## 0. Architecture and Design Basis

### 0.1 System Partitioning

The H743 does not drive the power stage directly. It runs ArduPilot and emits DShot600. Each of the four ESC channels has its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, and that firmware generates the 48 kHz PWM.

```
 6S LiPo ──┬──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
           │                                              │
     [Bulk caps + TVS]                             [ESC MCU ×4: AT32F421]
           │                                              │  6× PWM @ 48 kHz
     [VBAT bus planes] ── [3-ph gate driver ×4] ◄─────────┘
           │                    │ 6 gates/ch
     [24× N-MOSFET, 4 half-bridge triplets] ──► Motors M1..M4
           │
     [Current shunt] ──► ESC ADC / FC CURR pin
 
 STM32H743 FC ── DShot600 bidir (1 wire/motor) ──► ESC MCUs
              ◄─ GCR eRPM telemetry (same wire)
```

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation Topology

In 6-step synchronous-rectified commutation, two phases conduct at any instant:

- One leg is PWM-switched: the high-side conducts for D, and its complementary low-side conducts for (1 − D).
- One low-side is held fully ON.

The current path always crosses exactly two channels in series, regardless of D:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{DS}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]
$$

(Ripple correction: with phase-current ripple ΔI, replace I² with I² + ΔI²/12. This adds less than 1% for typical motor inductance at 48 kHz.)

### 1.2 Per-FET Average

Each of the 6 FETs conducts for one third of the electrical period on average:

$$
P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6}\cdot 2 = \frac{I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}}{3}
$$

The instantaneous peak is I²·R_DS, which is about 10 W at 80 A. This only matters thermally at very low eRPM, where the commutation period becomes comparable to the die thermal time constant. That is the stall or startup case, covered in §3.5.
