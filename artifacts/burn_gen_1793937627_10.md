## 1. Loss Model: One ESC Channel

### 1.1 Conduction topology

For 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM, at any instant:

- **PWM'd leg:** the high-side (HS) FET conducts for duty D and the low-side (LS) FET for (1 − D). Their sum is one FET-equivalent for 100% of the period, excluding dead time.
- **Return leg:** the LS FET conducts for 100% of the period.

The motor current therefore always flows through **two FET channels in series**:

$$
P_{cond} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

This expression holds for both FOC/sinusoidal and trapezoidal drive to first order when you use phase RMS current. For FOC, $P = 3 I_{ph,rms}^2 R_{DS}$ across the three legs, which is about the same at equal torque.

### 1.2 Electrothermal coupling

Because R_DS depends on T_j, which depends on P, the system must be solved self-consistently. With R_θ the junction-to-reference thermal resistance:

$$
T_j = T_{ref} + P_{FET}\,R_\theta, \quad P_{FET} = I^2 R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

$$
\boxed{T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + I^2 R_{25} R_\theta (1 - 25\alpha)}{1 - \alpha I^2 R_{25} R_\theta}}
$$

**Stability criterion (thermal runaway):**

$$
\alpha I^2 R_{25} R_\theta < 1
$$

At 80 A: $I^2 R_{25} = 6400 \times 1.2\text{ mΩ} = 7.68\text{ W}$. Runaway occurs if $R_\theta > 1/(0.005 \times 7.68) = 26\text{ K/W}$.

- **Steady state:** a 3.3×3.3 FET on a small PCB has R_θJA of roughly 30–40 K/W. **Continuous 80 A is thermally unstable on this hardware.**
- **Burst:** only the transient impedance Z_θ(t) is relevant, which is why 80 A can be a burst rating only.

### 1.3 Conduction loss at 80 A

Take T_j = 110 °C as the end-of-burst design point:

$$
R_{DS}(110) = 1.2 \times (1 + 0.005 \times 85) = 1.71\text{ mΩ}
$$

$$
P_{cond,FET} = 80^2 \times 1.71\text{ mΩ} = 10.9\text{ W (per conducting FET)}
$$

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 10.9 = \mathbf{21.9\text{ W per ESC channel}}
$$

At the 25 °C start of a burst the figure is 2 × 7.68 = 15.4 W. Losses rise about 42% over the burst.
