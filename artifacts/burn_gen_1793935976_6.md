## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

Two points must be fixed before the math.

1. **The STM32H743 does not switch the MOSFETs.** In a FC + 4-in-1 stack, the H743 runs ArduPilot and emits DShot600. Each ESC channel has its own MCU (AM32 on AT32F421 or STM32G071, or BLHeli_32), which generates the 48 kHz PWM. The loss model applies to the ESC board. The DShot/DMA model applies to the H743.
2. **"Nano" vs. 80 A.** On a 20×20 mm ESC, "80 A" can only be a **burst rating of seconds**. Section 3 quantifies this. Treat continuous capability as roughly 35–45 A per channel with good airflow.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | V_bus | 6S, 25.2 V max / 22.2 V nominal |
| Burst phase current (DC-equivalent) | I | 80 A per motor |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| MOSFET (per switch, 1 die) | R_DS(on),25 | 1.2 mΩ (30–40 V, 3.3×3.3 or 5×6 DFN class) |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths

In 6-step synchronous PWM, motor current always flows through exactly two conducting switches:

- **On-time (D):** high-side of phase A, then the motor, then low-side of phase B.
- **Off-time (1−D):** low-side of phase A (synchronous rectifier), then the motor, then low-side of phase B.

So at any instant two R_DS(on) are in series, regardless of duty:

$$P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right]$$

### 1.3 Ripple correction

For a peak-to-peak ripple ratio r = ΔI/I:

$$I_{rms} = I\sqrt{1+\frac{r^2}{12}}$$

The motor phase inductance (L_ph) sets the ripple:

$$\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{2L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

Example: 2-phase L = 20 µH, V_bus − V_bemf = 6 V, D = 0.8 gives ΔI ≈ 5 A, so r ≈ 0.06 and the correction is about 1.0003 (negligible). Low-inductance racing motors (L < 5 µH) need it.

### 1.4 Electro-thermal fixed point

R_DS(on) depends on T_j, which depends on loss. Solve iteratively:

$$T_j = T_a + P_{total}(T_j)\cdot Z_{th}(t)$$

### 1.5 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond per motor |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.20 mΩ | 80² × 2 × 1.20 m = **15.4 W** |
| 85 °C | 1.56 mΩ | **20.0 W** |
| 110 °C | 1.71 mΩ | **21.9 W** |
| 125 °C | 1.80 mΩ | **23.0 W** |

**Copper and parasitic adders per motor (often forgotten):**

- PCB trace/via spreading resistance, 2 oz copper, nano layout: ~0.3–0.5 mΩ per path, giving about 2–3 W.
- Solder joints and phase pads: ~0.2 mΩ, giving about 1.3 W.

**Design value:** P_cond ≈ 25 W per motor at T_j ≈ 110 °C.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap

Only the PWM-switched high-side hard-switches. The sync low-side switches at near-zero V_DS (ZVS-like, after body-diode conduction).

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_G} = \frac{8\,\text{nC}}{1\,\text{A}} = 8\,\text{ns per edge (ideal)}$$

Layout inductance and real drivers stretch this. Use t_r = t_f = 15 ns.

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus}\, I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(30\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 30\,\text{n}\times25.2\times48\,\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

Q_rr scales with di/dt and current. At 80 A, use 2–3× the datasheet value, giving about **0.1 W**.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 25\,\text{n}\times25.2\times48\,\text{k} = 0.03\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\,(2t_d)\,f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times100\,\text{ns}\times48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

The gate-drive loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistor, not the FET. Two PWM-active FETs switch at f_sw:

$$P_{gate} = 2\,Q_g\,V_{GS}\,f_{sw} = 2(40\,\text{n})(10)(48\,\text{k}) = 0.04\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Switching loss summary per motor (80 A, 25.2 V)

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| V–I overlap | 1.45 |
| Q_rr | 0.10 |
| Q_oss | 0.03 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.31 |
| Gate (driver side) | 0.04 |
| **Total** | **≈1.9 W** |

At 48 kHz, conduction dominates switching by about 13:1. Raising f_sw to 96 kHz (smoother low-throttle response, quieter) costs only about +1.9 W per motor. Conversely, dropping to 24 kHz saves under 1 W, while ripple worsens on low-L motors.
