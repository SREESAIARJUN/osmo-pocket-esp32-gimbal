## 0. Architectural Decision: The H743 Does Not Drive the Bridges

The H743 runs ArduPilot. It should **not** commutate the motors. Running four sensorless FOC/6-step loops at 48 kHz alongside the EKF3 scheduler would break ArduPilot's timing guarantees, and ArduPilot has no in-tree ESC firmware.

The correct partition is:

```
          ┌──────────────────────────── STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ────────────────────────────┐
          │ TIM1_CH1..CH4 → Bi-dir DShot600 (output + eRPM capture on same pin)           │
          └──────┬──────────────┬──────────────┬──────────────┬──────────────────────────┘
                 │ M1           │ M2           │ M3           │ M4      (+ shared ESC telem UART, optional)
          ┌──────▼─────┐ ┌──────▼─────┐ ┌──────▼─────┐ ┌──────▼─────┐
          │ ESC MCU #1 │ │ ESC MCU #2 │ │ ESC MCU #3 │ │ ESC MCU #4 │  e.g. STM32G071 / AT32F421
          │ AM32 fw    │ │ AM32 fw    │ │ AM32 fw    │ │ AM32 fw    │  48 kHz PWM, 6-step + BEMF ZC
          └──────┬─────┘ └──────┬─────┘ └──────┬─────┘ └──────┬─────┘
             3φ driver      3φ driver      3φ driver      3φ driver   (e.g. FD6288Q-class)
             6× NMOS        6× NMOS        6× NMOS        6× NMOS
```

Everything below assumes this partition.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous commutation

At any instant one phase is sourcing and one is sinking (the third is floating for BEMF sensing). With complementary PWM, current flows through either:

- **ON interval (D):** HS FET of phase A, then motor, then LS FET of phase B
- **OFF interval (1−D):** LS FET of phase A (synchronous freewheel), then motor, then LS FET of phase B

In both intervals the current crosses **exactly two channel resistances**. Instantaneous bridge conduction loss is therefore independent of duty:

$$
P_{cond,bridge} = 2\, I_{ph}^2 \, R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + I_{ph}^2 R_{Cu,path}
$$

### 2.2 Per-motor result at 80 A

$$
R_{DS(on)}(125^\circ C) = 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.6 = 1.6\,\text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{FET,cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.6\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{20.5\ W}
$$

**Copper path.** Assume a phase-pad-to-pad path of about 0.3 mΩ per conducting branch, so 0.6 mΩ total:

$$
P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.6\times10^{-3} = 3.8\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.3 Distribution across the six FETs

Each FET sees current for 120° of each 360° electrical cycle, with the HS/LS-sync split set by D. At full throttle the electrical frequency (several kHz) is much faster than the die thermal time constant (ms range), so the FETs see time-averaged power:

$$
\bar P_{FET} \approx \frac{P_{FET,cond}}{6}\times 2 = \frac{20.5}{3} \approx 6.8\ \text{W per FET (worst-case HS at } D\to1)
$$

For thermal sizing, use 3.4 W as the average per FET (20.5 W / 6) and **6.8 W** as the worst-case loading on a single device at high duty.

### 2.4 Battery-side current

DC-link current is not phase current:

$$
I_{bat,motor} \approx D \cdot I_{ph}
$$

At 80 A phase and D ≈ 0.9, that is about 72 A per motor. All four motors at burst gives about 290 A. **This is outside realistic XT60/XT90-on-a-nano-board territory and it drives the bus-capacitor design (§4).**

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg is hard-switching per commutation sector: the PWM'd HS FET and its complementary LS FET.

### 3.1 V-I overlap (hard-switching HS FET)

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}\,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}
= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}
$$

### 3.2 Output capacitance energy

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \times 2 = 0.5\times50\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k}\times2 = 0.06\ \text{W}
$$

### 3.3 Body-diode reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.05\ \text{W}
$$

This term also inflates the HS turn-on current spike, so in practice expect it to roughly double P_VI turn-on energy if Q_rr is poor.

### 3.4 Dead-time diode conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I_{ph}\, (2 t_{dead})\, f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

### 3.5 Gate drive (dissipated in the driver and R_G, not the FET)

$$
P_{gate} = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2 = 60\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k}\times2 = 0.058\ \text{W per motor}
$$

This sets the bootstrap and charge-pump design: 6 FETs × 4 motors × Q_g × f_sw ≈ 69 mA average from the 10–12 V gate rail at full PWM.
