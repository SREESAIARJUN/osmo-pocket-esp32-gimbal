# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #29): Power, Thermal, and DShot Timing Model

## 0. Architecture Constraints and Design Review Findings

Before the derivations, four constraints shape every result below.

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In a production-grade AIO, the H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bi-directional DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). Each ESC MCU drives a 3-phase gate driver. Driving 24 MOSFETs with 48 kHz complementary PWM, dead-time insertion, and BEMF zero-cross detection from the FC MCU is not supported by ArduPilot. It would also starve the H743 timer and DMA resources.
2. **"80 A burst" is defined here as 80 A DC battery current per ESC channel at 6S (25.2 V full, 22.2 V nominal).** At 100% throttle, phase current is approximately equal to DC current in six-step drive.
3. **Thermal verdict:** a nano-format board (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) **cannot** sustain 4 × 80 A. The math in §3 shows that board-wide simultaneous burst is limited to about 1–2 s. The realistic continuous rating is about 30–35 A per channel with prop-wash cooling. Marketing should rate the board as **"35 A continuous / 80 A burst ≤1 s, single-channel"**.
4. **Assumed device parameters.** These are representative of 40 V PDFN3.3×3 MOSFETs used in 6S nano ESCs. Substitute values from the selected part's datasheet.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Six-step synchronous commutation

At any instant, one high-side (HS) MOSFET of phase A and one low-side (LS) MOSFET of phase B carry the phase current I_ph.

With complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM at duty D:
- **On-time:** HS_A and LS_B conduct.
- **Off-time:** LS_A (synchronous freewheel) and LS_B conduct.

Two R_DS(on) are therefore always in the current path. The conduction loss is:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot 2 R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right] \approx R_{25}\cdot k_T$$

### 1.2 Phase RMS current

For 120° trapezoidal conduction, each phase conducts for 2/3 of the electrical cycle:

$$I_{ph,rms} = I_{ph}\sqrt{2/3}$$

Each phase conducts in turn, and the instantaneous path always contains two MOSFETs. Total channel loss uses the full I_ph:

$$I_{ph} \approx \frac{I_{DC}}{D} \quad\Rightarrow\quad D \to 1:\ I_{ph}\approx 80\ \text{A}$$

### 1.3 Evaluation at 80 A, T_j = 110 °C

$$P_{cond,ch} = 80^2 \times 2 \times (1.3\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.55) = 6400 \times 4.03\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{25.8\ W}$$

### 1.4 Per-MOSFET distribution

Each of the 6 MOSFETs conducts about 1/3 of the cycle as its "phase-on" device. The LS devices also carry freewheel current at D < 1. At D ≈ 1, the loss splits evenly:

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx 25.8/6 \approx \mathbf{4.3\ W}$$

### 1.5 Other conduction paths

| Path | Formula | Value @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Current shunt | I²R_sh = 6400 × 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| PCB copper, 2 oz, ~15 mm path @ ~0.4 mΩ | I²R | 2.6 W |
| Bus capacitor ESR ripple (I_rms,cap ≈ 0.5·I_DC at D = 0.5) | 40² × 2 mΩ | ~1–3 W (worst case at mid-throttle) |

**Conduction subtotal per channel: approximately 30 W.**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switching leg dissipates switching energy, since the static LS_B MOSFET does not switch. Within the switching leg, the HS MOSFET hard-switches and the LS MOSFET soft-switches through its body diode.

### 2.1 Transition times from gate charge

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,src}} = \frac{8\,\text{nC}}{1.5\,\text{A}} = 5.3\ \text{ns}$$

$$t_f \approx \frac{8\,\text{nC}}{2.5\,\text{A}} = 3.2\ \text{ns}$$

Gate-loop inductance and gate resistance (R_g ≈ 2.2 Ω) slow these in practice. Use a realistic t_r ≈ 12 ns and t_f ≈ 10 ns.
