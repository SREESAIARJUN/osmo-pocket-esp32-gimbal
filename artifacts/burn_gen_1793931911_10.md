# Job #104: STM32H743 Nano AIO with 4-in-1 ESC

## Architecture Specification and Mathematical Model, Rev A

---

## 0. Feasibility Findings

These findings constrain every number that follows.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In a production 4-in-1, each ESC channel has its own MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, and that firmware sets the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 (ArduPilot) only emits bidirectional DShot600 and decodes eRPM telemetry. The architecture is therefore **1× STM32H743 FC + 4× ESC MCU (AT32F421 class, AM32)**.
2. **"80 A" must be defined as 80 A per motor, burst (≤ ~1–2 s).** Four simultaneous 80 A motors draw **up to 320 A from the battery**. An XT60 (or even an XT90) and a nano-scale board cannot sustain that. This model treats 4 × 80 A as the worst-case thermal transient, not an operating point.
3. **Form factor:** a 20 × 20 mm board cannot physically carry 80 A phases. The model assumes a **30.5 × 30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz/layer** AIO. On 20 × 20, scale the burst duration down by about 2.3×.
4. **Device parameters are parametric assumptions, not a specific datasheet.** Substitute your selected part's datasheet values into the equations; the method holds.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Commutation Geometry

With 6-step trapezoidal drive and complementary (synchronous) PWM:

- Each switch position is active for 120° of every 360° electrical, so its conduction fraction is 1/3.
- At any instant, exactly one high-side and one low-side path carry I_ph.
- Within the HS window, the HS FET conducts for fraction D. The complementary LS FET of the same leg carries the freewheel current for (1 − D), minus dead time.

Per-FET RMS current near full throttle (D → 1):

$$I_{FET,rms} = I_{ph}\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{3}} = 80 \times 0.577 = 46.2\ \text{A}$$

### 2.2 Temperature-Dependent R_DS(on) (Iterative Solution)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

The resistance depends on junction temperature, which depends on loss. Iterating to the burst end-point T_j ≈ 110 °C:

$$R_{DS}(110) = 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega \times (1 + 0.006 \times 85) = 2.27\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 2.3 Per-FET and Per-ESC Conduction Loss

$$P_{cond,FET} = I_{FET,rms}^2 R_{DS}(T_j) = \frac{80^2 \times 2.27\text{m}}{3} = \mathbf{4.84\ W}$$

Because two FETs are always in the current path:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\, I_{ph}^2 R_{DS} = 2 \times 6400 \times 2.27\text{m} = \mathbf{29.1\ W}$$
