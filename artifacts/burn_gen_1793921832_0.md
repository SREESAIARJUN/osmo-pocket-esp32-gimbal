# STM32H743 Nano FC + Integrated 4-in-1 ESC (Job #20): Power-Stage Model, Thermal Budget, Bi-Directional DShot600 Allocation and Netlists

## 0. Scope, Architecture and Feasibility Findings

### 0.1 Architecture

**The H743 does not commutate the motors.** A 4-in-1 ESC needs a dedicated commutation MCU per motor. This design uses:

```
[STM32H743VIT6 FC] --4x bidirectional DShot600--> [4x AT32F421K8U7 running AM32] --> [4x FD6288Q] --> [24x N-MOSFET] --> 4 motors
```

The 48 kHz PWM is an **ESC firmware parameter** (AM32 `PWM_FREQUENCY`). ArduPilot does not set it. ArduPilot only sets the DShot rate, the bidirectional telemetry and the motor poles.

### 0.2 Feasibility

**80 A burst on a 20×20 mm "nano" board is not thermally credible beyond about 1 second.** Section 3 shows this quantitatively. Commercial 20×20 4-in-1 ESCs are rated about 35–45 A continuous and 45–55 A burst.

My recommendation is one of two options:
- (a) Re-rate to **45 A continuous / 60 A burst (≤5 s)** on 20×20 mm.
- (b) Keep 80 A burst and move to a **30.5×30.5 mm** form factor with dual-FET paralleling.

The model below is parametric, so either choice can be evaluated directly.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation Topology

AM32 uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous (complementary) rectification. During each 60° sector:
- One high-side FET (phase A) and one low-side FET (phase B) carry the motor current.
- The PWM leg alternates between its high-side and low-side FETs. With complementary PWM, one of the two always conducts, minus dead time.

The current path therefore always contains **two FETs in series**:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot \left(2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + R_{cu}\right)
$$

### 1.2 Temperature-Dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

At T_j = 125 °C this gives R = 1.5 mΩ, consistent with k_T = 1.5.

### 1.3 Electro-Thermal Coupling

R_DS(on) depends on T_j, and T_j depends on dissipated power. Solve iteratively:

$$
T_j^{(n+1)} = T_a + P_{FET}\!\left(T_j^{(n)}\right) \cdot Z_{th}(t)
$$

This converges if the loop gain is below 1:

$$
I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\,Z_{th} < 1
$$

At 80 A with Z_th ≈ 3 K/W, the loop gain is 6400 × 0.001 × 0.005 × 3 ≈ 0.096. The iteration converges, so there is no thermal runaway at the single-FET level.

### 1.4 Ripple Correction

Phase inductance on typical 5–7" motors is about 20–60 µH. Peak-to-peak ripple at 48 kHz is:

$$
\Delta I \approx \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L f_{sw}} \approx \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{40\,\mu\text{H} \times 48\,\text{kHz}} \approx 3.3\ \text{A}
$$

The RMS correction is:

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}
$$

This adds less than 0.1 % at 80 A, so it is negligible.

### 1.5 Result at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

$$
P_{cond} = 80^2 \times (2 \times 1.5 + 0.4)\,\text{m}\Omega = 6400 \times 3.4\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{21.8\ W\ per\ motor}
$$

The FET share is 19.2 W (about 9.6 W per conducting FET). The copper share is 2.6 W.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. The fixed (60°-sector) leg is static. Each PWM period has one hard turn-on and one hard turn-off on the active FET. The complementary FET switches with near-zero voltage (ZVS) after body-diode conduction.

### 2.1 V-I Overlap (Hard-Switched FET)

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Output-Capacitance Energy

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} \times 2 \ (\text{both FETs in leg}) = 1.5\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.046\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Reverse Recovery

This occurs when the active FET turns on into the conducting body diode:

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}
$$

The corresponding energy is dumped mostly into the hard-switched FET.

### 2.4 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}
$$

This is the **second-largest switching-related term**. Reducing dead time to 100 ns halves it. Dead time must be validated against FD6288Q propagation mismatch, which is about ±50 ns.

### 2.5 Gate Drive

This loss lands in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET.

$$
P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2 \ (\text{PWM leg}) = 80\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = \mathbf{0.077\ W}
$$
