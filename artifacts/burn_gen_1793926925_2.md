# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model (Job #50)

**Scope:** Electro-thermal model of the power stage, ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 timer and DMA plan, representative netlists, and thermal budget.

**Status:** Engineering derivation. All component values are design assumptions to be validated on hardware. They are not measured data.

---

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Key Assumptions

An STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFET gates directly in any practical ESC. Commutation timing, back-EMF sensing and 48 kHz PWM generation need a dedicated MCU per motor. The architecture assumed here is:

```
6S LiPo ──► Power stage (4 × 3-phase bridge, 24 MOSFETs)
                ▲ gate drive
           4 × ESC MCU (AT32F421K8U7, AM32 firmware, 48 kHz PWM)
                ▲ bidirectional DShot600 (1 wire per motor)
           STM32H743VIT6 FC (ArduPilot), on the same PCB stack
```

| Parameter | Value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage | 6S: 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal | 40 V-rated FETs |
| Burst current | 80 A per motor, ≤ 5 s | Battery-side, at D ≈ 1 |
| Continuous current target | 45–55 A per motor | Depends on airflow (see §2.6) |
| PWM frequency | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous rectification) | |
| MOSFET (assumed) | 40 V, R_DS(on) = 0.9 mΩ typ at 25 °C, 5×6 mm DFN | 6 per motor, 24 total |
| Board form factor | 30.5 × 30.5 mm mounting, ~36 × 36 mm outline | Two-board stack: FC + ESC |
| ESC PCB | 6-layer, 2 oz outer / 2 oz inner | |

> **Reality check:** 80 A on a 30.5 mm 4-in-1 is at the extreme edge of feasibility. At full burst the four ESCs dissipate about 100 W combined (derived below). This board can only survive 80 A as a short burst with forced airflow. The model in §2.6 quantifies exactly how short.

## 1. Power-Stage Loss Model

### 1.1 Phase Current vs Battery Current

For 6-step trapezoidal commutation with duty cycle D (neglecting ripple):

$$I_{ph} \approx \frac{I_{bat}}{D}, \qquad I_{bat} = D \cdot I_{ph}$$

At burst (D → 0.95–1.0): I_ph ≈ 80–84 A. **I_ph = 80 A** is used as the design point.

### 1.2 Temperature-Dependent On-Resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ /K$$

| T_j | R_DS(on) |
|---|---|
| 25 °C | 0.90 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 1.27 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 1.40 mΩ (design value) |

A further +10 % is added for PCB copper and solder interconnect: **R_eff = 1.54 mΩ** per switch position.

### 1.3 Conduction Losses

In any commutation sector, one high-side (HS) and one low-side (LS) switch carry current, in two different legs. The PWM leg alternates between HS (fraction D) and its synchronous LS (fraction 1 − D):

$$P_{cond,PWM\ leg} = D\,I_{ph}^2 R + (1-D)\,I_{ph}^2 R = I_{ph}^2 R$$
$$P_{cond,static\ leg} = I_{ph}^2 R$$
$$\boxed{P_{cond} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{eff}}$$

At 80 A:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.54\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.7\ W\ per\ motor}$$

Each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical cycle, so the average per-FET dissipation is P_cond / 6 ≈ **3.3 W**. The peak instantaneous dissipation is I²R = 9.9 W while the FET is conducting.

### 1.4 Switching Losses at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches hard. The terms are evaluated at V_bus = 25.2 V and I = 80 A.

**(a) Voltage-current overlap (hard-switched HS)**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

The transition time follows from the gate charge, assuming Q_gs2 + Q_gd ≈ 18 nC and gate current I_g ≈ (V_drv − V_plateau) / R_g = (10 − 3.5) / 4.7 Ω ≈ 1.4 A:

$$t_{r} \approx t_f \approx \frac{18\text{ nC}}{1.4\text{ A}} \approx 13\text{ ns} \;\Rightarrow\; t_r + t_f \approx 26\text{ ns}$$

$$P_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 26\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.26\ W}$$

**(b) Output capacitance**, with C_oss,eff ≈ 1.6 nF:

$$P_{oss} = C_{oss} V^2 f_{sw} \text{ (both FETs in leg, ½CV² each)} = 1.6\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = 0.049\text{ W}$$

**(c) Reverse recovery of the LS body diode**, with Q_rr ≈ 45 nC:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 45\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.054\text{ W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction**, with t_dt = 200 ns per edge, 2 edges per cycle and V_F ≈ 0.8 V at 80 A:

$$P_{dt} = V_F I_{ph} (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

> Dead time is the dominant switching-related loss. Reducing it to 100 ns (the AM32 `DEAD_TIME` setting) halves this term. Verify shoot-through margin on the scope before doing so.

**(e) Gate drive**, with Q_g ≈ 60 nC and V_drv = 10 V, for the 2 switching FETs:

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 0.058\text{ W}$$

This loss is dissipated mostly in R_g and the driver, not in the FET.

### 1.5 Other Losses (per motor)

| Source | Model | Value at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Current shunt (0.25 mΩ, shared bus, divided by 4) | I_bus² R / 4 | ≈ 0.4 W |
| Bus capacitor ESR (§1.6) | I_cap,rms² ESR | ≈ 0.3 W |
| Copper traces and planes (beyond the FET +10 %) | — | ≈ 1.0 W |

### 1.6 Bus Capacitor Ripple Current

$$I_{cap,rms} = I_{ph}\sqrt{D(1-D)}$$

| Condition | Ripple current |
|---|---|
| Worst case, D = 0.5, I_ph = 80 A | 40 A |
| Realistic D = 0.5 load point, I_ph ≈ 45 A | 22.5 A |
| Per-motor share with shared low-ESR bank | ≈ 6
