# STM32H743 Flight Controller + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, Bidirectional DShot600 DMA Plan, and Netlist

## 0. Critical Findings

I have no prior record of "Job #31," so this works from first principles. The stated assumptions can be swapped for your actual BOM values.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot is a flight-control stack. It sends DShot to ESC firmware and does not run ESC firmware itself. The realistic architecture is:

- The H743 FC runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to the ESC.
- The ESC has four small MCUs (AT32F421 running AM32, or EFM8/BLHeli_S), one per motor. Each drives a 3-phase gate driver and six MOSFETs.

The DMA section below covers the FC side. The loss model covers the ESC power stage.

**2. 80 A per channel on a "nano" (20×20 mm) board is not thermally achievable.** Section 3 shows that the board reaches its thermal limit in about 1–2 s at full burst and cannot dissipate the steady-state heat. I recommend one of:

- reinterpret 80 A as the total board current,
- move to a 30.5×30.5 mm or larger ESC with a heatsink, or
- spec 80 A as a ≤1 s burst with firmware current limiting.

## 2. Loss Model (Per Motor Channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

In 6-step operation, two legs are active in each sector. One leg PWMs and the other leg's low-side FET is held on.

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for duty fraction D. The synchronous low-side FET conducts for (1−D).
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET conducts 100% of the sector.

Therefore:

```
P_cond = I²·R·D + I²·R·(1−D) + I²·R = 2·I²·R_hot
```

This result is independent of duty cycle, ignoring ripple.

To include inductor ripple, use the RMS current instead of I: I_rms² = I² + ΔI²/12.

**At 80 A:**

```
P_cond = 2 × 6400 × 1.4 mΩ = 17.9 W per motor
```

Each FET conducts about 1/3 of an electrical revolution, so the average is about 3.0 W per FET. The instantaneous peak is 8.96 W in each conducting FET.

### 2.2 Switching Loss (Hard-Switched Leg Only)

Use t_sw ≈ Q_sw / I_g ≈ 15 ns per edge, with two edges (rise and fall) per PWM period.

| Loss component | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 30 ns × 48 k | 1.45 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2·t_dt·f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k | 0.61 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.05 W |
| Coss | Q_oss·V·f_sw (½·Q_oss·V per transition) | 0.06 W |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw | 0.06 W (dissipated mostly in the driver) |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.2 W** |

Sensitivity: the overlap loss scales linearly with t_sw. With a weak 0.3 A driver, t_sw ≈ 50 ns and the overlap term rises to about 4.8 W.

### 2.3 Other Per-Channel Losses

| Source | Calculation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Shunt | I²·R_sh = 6400 × 0.5 mΩ | 3.2 W |
| PCB copper | 2 oz, ~10 mm path, ~0.3 mΩ effective round-trip | ~1.9 W |

The shunt loss is large. Consider a 0.25 mΩ shunt or sensing across R_DS(on).

### 2.4 Totals at 80 A Burst

| Scope | Power |
|---|---|
| Per motor | 17.9 + 2.2 + 3.2 + 1.9 ≈ **25 W** |
| 4-in-1 board | ≈ **100 W** |

Input power is about 4 × 25.2 V × 80 A × D. At 50% duty this is about 4 kW, so stage efficiency is about 97.5%. That is excellent efficiency, but the absolute heat is far too high for the board area.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Junction to Board (Per FET, Peak)

R_θJC ≈ 1.0 K/W. R_θ(case→board), through the solder and 9+ thermal vias, is about 3 K/W.

```
ΔT_J−board ≈ 8.96 W × 4 K/W ≈ 36 K above the local copper
```

Use the datasheet Z_th(t) curve for pulses under 100 ms. It gives roughly 0.3–0.5 × R_θ.

### 3.2 Board Heat Capacity (Lumped, 20×20 mm)

| Material | Mass | Specific heat | Heat capacity |
|---|---|---|---|
| FR4 | ~1.4 g | 1.1 J/gK | 1.54 J/K |
| Copper (8 L, 2 oz) | ~1.2 g | 0.385 J/gK | 0.46 J/K |
| FETs and parts | ~1 g | ~0.7 J/gK | ~0.7 J/K |
| **Total C** | | | **≈ 2.7 J/K** |

```
dT/dt = 100 W / 2.7 J/K ≈ 37 K/s
```

Allowable rise: from 50 °C to a 110 °C board limit is 60 K, giving t_burst,max ≈ **1.6 s**. This is optimistic, because hot-spot gradients cut it to about 1 s.

### 3.3 Steady-State Capability

Assume convection under prop wash with h ≈ 100 W/m²K over both faces, A = 8×10⁻⁴ m².

```
R_θ,board→air ≈ 1 / (h·A) = 12.5 K/W
```

At ΔT = 60 K, the board can dissipate about **4.8 W continuous**. Solving 4 × (2 × I² × R_hot + …) ≈ 4.8 W gives about **15–18 A continuous per motor**.

| Option | Expected capability |
|---|---|
