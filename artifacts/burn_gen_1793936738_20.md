# STM32H743 Nano AIO: 80 A Burst ESC + ArduPilot Bidirectional DShot600

**Design Dossier Rev A (Job #15)**

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Key Assumptions

**The STM32H743 is the flight controller MCU, not the ESC commutation MCU.** ArduPilot does not run motor commutation. A practical "4-in-1 ESC nano FC" therefore uses this split:

| Block | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| FC | STM32H743VIH6 (TFBGA100) or VIT6 (LQFP100) | ArduPilot, sends bidirectional DShot600 |
| ESC MCUs | 4× STM32G071GBU6 (or AT32F421) running AM32 | 6-step commutation at 48 kHz PWM, eRPM telemetry |
| Gate drivers | 4× 3-phase half-bridge driver (FD6288Q-class) | Gate drive at about 10 V |
| Power stage | 24× N-MOSFET, 40 V, PDFN3.3×3.3 | 4 channels × 6 FETs |

**Parameter assumptions.** These are representative values for a 40 V PDFN3333 class part. Replace them with your selected part's datasheet values and rerun the model; the equations are part-agnostic.

**Feasibility flag (up front):** 80 A per motor on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board is a **burst rating only**, on the order of 2–3 seconds (derived below). Continuous capability is about 25 A per channel with prop-wash cooling. Design, firmware current limits, and marketing must all reflect this.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Six-step synchronous commutation

In each 60° commutation sector:
- One phase leg is PWM-switched with complementary drive (HS for duty D, LS for 1−D).
- A second phase's low-side FET is held statically ON.
- The third phase floats (BEMF sensing).

Motor current I flows through **two series FETs at all times**, ignoring dead time.

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2\left[D\,R_{HS} + (1-D)R_{LS}\right] + I^2 R_{LS,static} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\left[1 + \alpha_R (T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha_R \approx 0.005\,\text{K}^{-1}$$

This is self-consistent with the thermal model in §3, which is solved iteratively.

At T_j = 125 °C, R = 2.1 mΩ:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 0.0021 = \mathbf{26.9\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each phase spends 1/3 of the electrical cycle as the PWM leg, 1/3 as the static low side, and 1/3 floating.

| FET | Average power at 80 A | Instantaneous power while conducting |
|---|---|---|
| Low side | (1/3)·I²R + (1/3)(1−D)·I²R | 13.4 W |
| High side | (1/3)·D·I²R | 13.4 W |

At D = 0.9:
- Low side: 4.48 + 0.45 = **4.93 W**
- High side: **4.03 W**

### 1.4 Copper and interconnect

Path resistance (pad + 2 oz inner pours + motor pad) is about 0.4–0.6 mΩ per conduction path:

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.2\ W/ch}$$

This is not negligible. Use 4 parallel 2 oz layers for phase and power nets.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Hard-switching transition (high side only)

The low side soft-switches via body-diode commutation, so only the high side incurs this loss.

Gate currents during the Miller plateau:

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{pl}}{R_g} = \frac{10-3}{11.5} = 0.61\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_g} = \frac{3}{11.5} = 0.26\ \text{A}$$

Transition times:

$$t_{on} = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = 13\ \text{ns},\qquad t_{off} = \frac{8\,\text{nC}}{0.26} = 31\ \text{ns}$$

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_{on}+t_{off}) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(44\times10^{-9})(48\times10^3) = \mathbf{2.13\ W}$$
