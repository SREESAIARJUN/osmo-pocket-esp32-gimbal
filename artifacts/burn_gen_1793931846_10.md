# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC: Architecture, Loss Model, DShot DMA Plan, and Netlists

## 0. Architecture Decision and Design Assumptions

### 0.1 Reality check on the brief

Two items in the brief need correcting before any math is useful.

**1. The STM32H743 should not commutate the motors.**
- Running ArduPilot's main loop while also commutating 12 half-bridges (4 motors × 3 phases) with BEMF zero-cross detection at 48 kHz is not viable on one core.
- The standard, flight-proven partition is:
  - **H743 = flight controller.** It sends bi-directional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs.
  - **4 × AT32F421 (or EFM8BB51) = ESC MCUs** running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. Each drives one 3-phase bridge at 48 kHz.
- Everything below assumes this partition.

**2. 80 A per motor is burst-only on a "nano" board.**
- At 80 A on all four channels, the board dissipates about 75 W.
- A 30×30 mm board can shed roughly 5–15 W continuously in prop-wash.
- The thermal section therefore calculates a **burst time limit**, not a continuous rating.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 0.85 × 1.45 = **1.23 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification

In each 60° electrical sector, current flows through two phase legs: one high-side position and one low-side position.

- **PWM leg:** the high-side and low-side FETs alternate with duty D (high side on) and 1−D (synchronous freewheel).
- **Static leg:** the low-side FET stays on.

Total conduction loss for the channel is therefore independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.23\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{15.7\ W}$$

### 1.3 Copper and interconnect conduction

Per path, assume 0.25 mΩ total. This covers 2 oz power planes, via arrays, and motor pads.

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.25\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{1.6\ W}$$

Copper also heats with temperature: about +0.39 %/K.

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

This matters for junction temperature, not just board temperature.

| Quantity | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Instantaneous loss of a conducting FET | I²R | 7.9 W |
| Time-average per FET (each conducts about 1/3 of the electrical cycle) | — | ≈ 2.6 W conduction + switching share |

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the PWM leg switches in a given sector. Hard switching occurs on the high-side FET. The low-side FET switches near zero voltage after the dead time, but its body diode conducts during the dead time and must reverse-recover.

**(a) Voltage/current overlap (high-side FET)**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\times25.2\times80\times40\text{n}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery (low-side body diode → high-side turn-on)**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 60\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.073\ W}$$

The Q_rr datasheet figure is usually quoted at di/dt = 100 A/µs. At ESC di/dt values (500–1000 A/µs), Q_rr is often 2–3× larger, so budget about 0.2 W.

**(c) Output capacitance**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5\times80\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period)**

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{n}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

Cutting dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns (where the driver allows) saves about 0.3 W per channel.

**(e) Gate drive** (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET)

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2\ (\text{HS}+\text{LS}) = 100\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k}\times2 = \mathbf{0.096\ W}$$
