## 0. Architecture Corrections

Two points in the brief will cause design errors if taken literally.

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production AIO design, the H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It sends DShot to **four dedicated ESC MCUs**, typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32, and those MCUs drive the gate drivers. Field-oriented control for four motors from the H743 is possible in principle, but ArduPilot does not support it. The rest of this package assumes the standard partitioned architecture.
2. **"Nano" (20×20 mm) plus 80 A burst per motor is thermally marginal.** The mathematics below shows a transient budget of about 2 seconds at full burst. Nothing close to that is sustainable. Treat 80 A as a **≤1–2 s burst rating** and say so in the product specification.

```
VBAT(6S) ─┬─ FC section: STM32H743 @400 MHz, ArduPilot
          │     │ 4× bidirectional DShot600 (single-wire, open-drain-ish w/ pull-up)
          └─ ESC section ×4: AT32F421 (AM32) → FD6288Q 3-ph driver → 6× N-MOSFET → motor
```

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Current paths

The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM:

- In each 60° sector, phase A is PWM-high-side, phase B is low-side-on, and phase C is floating.
- During the on-time D, current flows HS_A → motor → LS_B.
- During the off-time (1−D), current freewheels through LS_A (synchronous) → motor → LS_B.

So at **every instant, exactly two MOSFETs carry I_ph** (ignoring dead time). This gives:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)] \approx R_{25}\cdot k_T$$

### 2.2 Numbers

Using hot R_DS(on) = 1.6 mΩ:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.6\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{20.5\ W}$$

The copper path adds:

$$P_{cu} = 2\, I^2 R_{cu} = 2 \times 6400 \times 0.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{6.4\ W}$$

> This assumes the current traverses two phase legs' worth of copper. If you treat R_cu as the whole loop, use 3.2 W.

### 2.3 Per-MOSFET distribution

Each FET's share depends on duty:

- **HS FET:** conducts for 1/3 of the electrical cycle at duty D.
- **LS FET:** conducts for 1/3 of the cycle (sink role) plus 1/6 × (1−D) (freewheel role).

At full throttle (D ≈ 1), the loss is shared evenly:

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{20.5}{6} \approx \mathbf{3.4\ W\ per\ FET}$$

The worst case is the LS FET at mid-throttle, but phase current is lower there.

### 2.4 Phase current versus battery current

The two are not the same:

$$I_{bat} \approx D \cdot I_{ph}$$

At full throttle, 80 A phase current on all four motors means **about 320 A from the battery**. Size the XT60/XT90 connector, the battery leads, and the bulk capacitor ripple rating accordingly:

$$I_{C,rms} \approx I_{ph}\sqrt{D(1-D)} \;\Rightarrow\; \text{≈40 A rms per motor at } D=0.5$$
