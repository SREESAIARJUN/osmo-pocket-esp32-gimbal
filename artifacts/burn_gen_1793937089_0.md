# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage, Thermal & Bidirectional DShot Design Package (Job #50)

The thermal analysis shows that 80 A is achievable only as a **seconds-long burst** on a nano-format stack. It is not a continuous rating. The design below treats it that way.

---

## 0. Architecture Note

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. A sensorless BLDC ESC needs one dedicated MCU per motor channel, or one multi-channel MCU, running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The partition is:

| Block | Device (representative) | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100), 400 MHz | ArduPilot, sends bidirectional DShot600 |
| ESC MCU ×4 | AT32F421 / STM32G071 class | AM32 firmware, 48 kHz PWM, BEMF zero-cross, eRPM telemetry |
| Gate driver ×4 | FD6288Q-class 3-phase half-bridge driver | Bootstrap high-side drive |
| MOSFETs ×24 | 40 V, ~0.9 mΩ class, 3.3×3.3 or 5×6 mm | Three half-bridges per motor |

The **48 kHz** figure is an ESC firmware setting (AM32 PWM frequency). The **DShot600** link is the FC→ESC interface. The two are independent and are modelled separately below.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current path

The ESC uses trapezoidal 6-step commutation with synchronous complementary PWM. In any 60° sector:

- **Phase A (PWM leg):** the high-side FET conducts for D·T_sw. The low-side FET conducts synchronously for (1−D)·T_sw.
- **Phase B (return leg):** the low-side FET conducts for 100 % of the sector.

So **exactly two channels are always in series with the motor current**, independent of duty cycle.

### 2.2 Equations

Total bridge conduction loss:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds,hot}$$

Peak loss in a single FET while it is conducting:

$$P_{FET,inst} = I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds,hot}$$

Average loss per FET over an electrical cycle (each FET conducts about 1/3 of the time):

$$\bar P_{FET,cond} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds,hot}$$

### 2.3 Numbers at 80 A

| Quantity | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| I² | 80² | 6400 A² |
| P_FET,inst | 6400 × 1.35 mΩ | 8.64 W |
| P_cond,bridge | 2 × 8.64 W | **17.3 W per motor** |
| P̄_FET,cond | 8.64 W / 3 | 2.88 W |

### 2.4 Copper path loss (often ignored, and significant)

The bus path is roughly 30 mm of 2 oz copper, 6 mm wide, paralleled over 2 layers. Resistance per layer:

$$R = \rho L / (w t) = 1.72\times10^{-8} \times 0.03 \,/\, (0.006 \times 70\times10^{-6}) \approx 1.23\ \text{m}\Omega$$

With 2 layers in parallel this is about 0.61 mΩ. The supply and return paths together give about 1.2 mΩ. At 80 A:

$$P_{Cu} \approx 6400 \times 1.2\ \text{m}\Omega \approx 7.8\ \text{W per motor}$$

This assumes all four motors draw from shared busbars. Use **thick copper (3 oz or heavier), via stitching, and soldered bus wires** to cut this term.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches hard. The synchronous low-side FET turns on with zero voltage after dead time (ZVS), so its switching loss is negligible. The diode terms are what remain.

| Mechanism | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap (hard switching) | ½ · V_bus · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = 0.5 · 25.2 · 80 · 40 ns · 48 kHz | 1.94 W |
| Output capacitance (C_oss) | Q_oss · V_bus · f_sw = 30 nC · 25.2 · 48 kHz | 0.036 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw = 40 nC · 25.2 · 48 kHz | 0.048 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_SD · I · 2 · t_dt · f_sw = 0.8 · 80 · 200 ns · 48 kHz | 0.61 W |
| Gate drive (6 FETs, dissipated mostly in driver and R_g) | 6 · Q_g · V_drv · f_sw = 6 · 40 nC · 10 V · 48 kHz | 0.12 W |
| **P_sw, total per motor** | | **≈ 2.76 W** |

**Sensitivity:** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it is about 1.4 W; at 96 kHz about 5.5 W. At 80 A the design is **conduction-dominated**. 48 kHz is a reasonable trade-off: lower current ripple and quieter motors for roughly 2 W of extra loss.

## 4. Total Power Budget at 80 A Burst (all 4 motors)

| Term | Per motor | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| MOSFET conduction | 17.3 W | 69.2 W |
| Switching + diode + gate | 2.76 W | 11.0 W |
| PCB copper (estimate) | ~7.8 W | ~31 W |
| **Total** | **~27.9 W** | **~111 W** |

Electrical output at 80 A × 4 × ~25 V is roughly 8 kW. The ESC efficiency is therefore about 98.6 %, but **111 W on a 30×30 mm board** is the governing constraint.
