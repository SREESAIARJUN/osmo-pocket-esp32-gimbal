# STM32H743 + 4-in-1 ESC Nano Stack: 80 A Burst Electrical, Thermal, and DShot Design

## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility

Several of the request's parameters conflict with each other, so this section fixes the assumptions before any math is done.

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Architecture | The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller (FC). The 4-in-1 ESC has **four independent ESC MCUs** (e.g., AT32F421 running AM32). The H743 does not commutate motors. It sends bidirectional DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry. |
| Supply | 6S Li-ion/LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V maximum. Analysis uses 22.2 V nominal and 25.2 V worst case. |
| "80 A burst" | 80 A **per ESC channel**, battery-side DC current, for ≤ 2–3 s. |
| PWM frequency | 48 kHz, center-aligned, complementary (synchronous rectification), trapezoidal 6-step commutation. |
| MOSFET (reference part) | 40 V, N-channel, 5×6 or 3.3×3.3 DFN. R_DS(on),25 = 0.90 mΩ at V_GS = 10 V. Q_g = 60 nC, Q_rr = 40 nC, C_oss = 1.5 nF, t_r + t_f ≈ 40 ns at the chosen gate drive, R_θJC = 1.0 K/W. **Substitute your actual datasheet values.** |
| Form factor | "Nano" = 20×20 mm (M2 mounting) or 25.5×25.5 mm. |

**Feasibility verdict.** A nano-class 4-in-1 board *can survive* an 80 A per-channel burst of about 2–3 s. It cannot sustain 80 A. The continuous rating works out to roughly **25–30 A per channel** (derived in §2.6). Any marketing of "80 A" must specify the burst duration and the starting temperature.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths

In 6-step commutation with complementary PWM, two phase legs carry the motor current at any instant:

- One leg is PWM-switched: its high-side (HS) conducts for D·T_sw and its low-side (LS) for (1−D)·T_sw, minus dead time.
- The other leg is held on: its LS is static.

There are therefore **always exactly two MOSFET channels in series** with the phase current I_ph.

### 1.2 Phase current vs. battery current

Battery current relates to phase current through the duty cycle:

$$I_{bat} = D \cdot I_{ph} \quad\Rightarrow\quad I_{ph} = I_{bat}/D$$

At full throttle (D → 1), I_ph ≈ I_bat = 80 A. At partial throttle, phase current exceeds battery current. This is why conduction loss at D = 0.5 with I_bat = 40 A matches the full-throttle 80 A case. Design to **I_ph,max = 80 A** at any duty cycle, and enforce it with the ESC current limit.

### 1.3 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha(T_j - 25)],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 120 °C: R_DS = 0.90 × 1.43 = **1.28 mΩ**. Use **1.4 mΩ** to include packaging and solder resistance.

### 1.4 Per-ESC conduction loss

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j) + I_{ph}^2 R_{Cu}$$

- MOSFET term at I_ph = 80 A, R = 1.4 mΩ: 2 × 6400 × 0.0014 = **17.9 W**.
- Copper term: path resistance of 2 oz copper, ~6 mm wide, ~15 mm per path is R_Cu ≈ 0.25 mΩ. This gives 6400 × 0.00025 = **1.6 W**.

**P_cond,ESC ≈ 19.5 W.**

### 1.5 Per-device distribution

Each FET conducts the full current for about 1/3 of the electrical period, split between active and freewheel intervals.

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{2 I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}}{6} = \frac{I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}}{3} = 3.0\ \text{W (average)}$$

Instantaneous dissipation is 8.96 W during conduction intervals. The 120° conduction windows last ~100 µs at 100k eRPM, which is short compared to the junction thermal time constant, so the average value governs.

### 1.6 Self-consistent junction temperature

Because R_DS(on) rises with temperature, conduction loss and junction temperature must be solved together. Iterate:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_{pcb} + R_{\theta JC,eff}\, P_{FET}\big(R_{DS}(T_j^{(n)})\big)$$

Stability requires the loop gain to stay below 1:

$$\frac{\partial T_j}{\partial T_j} = R_{\theta JC}\,\frac{I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha}{3} = 1.0 \times \frac{6400 \times 0.0009 \times 0.0045}{3} = 0.0086 \ll 1$$

The local junction is stable. **Thermal runaway risk is board-level, not junction-level** (see §3).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switched leg's HS/LS pair commutates at f_sw. Worst case is V = 25.2 V, I = 80 A.

### 2.1 Hard-switching (V-I overlap)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12\, V_{bus}\, I_{ph}\,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

Rise and fall times are set by gate drive:

$$t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,R_{G,total}}{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}$$

With the FD6288-class driver (~1.0 A source / 1.5 A sink), R_G = 4.7 Ω, Q_gd = 15 nC, and V_drv − V_plateau ≈ 7 V, this gives ≈ 20 ns per edge.

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I_{ph}\cdot 2 t_{dt}\cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 2(50\text{ns}) \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = V_{bus}\, Q_{rr}\, f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 40\text{nC} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

Q_rr itself scales with di/dt and I. At 80 A, use the datasheet value at the matching di/dt; it can be 2–3× the headline figure.
