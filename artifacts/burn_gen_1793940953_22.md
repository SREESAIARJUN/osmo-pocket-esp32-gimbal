# STM32H743 Nano AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC): Power-Stage Thermal Model, Switching Losses, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Allocation

**Design Review Package, Job #12, Rev A (analytical baseline)**

---

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Finding

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and generates bi-directional DShot600. It does **not** commutate the motors. Each of the four ESC channels has its own small MCU (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32), and that MCU produces the 48 kHz PWM. The loss model in Sections 1–3 applies to those ESC power stages.

**Feasibility finding (read before tape-out).** 80 A per channel on a nano (20×20 mm) board is **only viable as a short burst lasting a few seconds**. It is not a continuous rating. The thermal model in Section 3 shows:

- All four channels at 80 A dissipate about 85 W.
- A 20×20 board has roughly 2.4 J/K of heat capacity, so it heats at about 35 K/s.
- The board reaches its thermal limit in about 2–3 s.

I recommend one of two paths:
- **(a)** Rate the board at 80 A burst for ≤1 s and roughly 25–35 A continuous, or
- **(b)** Move to a 30.5×30.5 mm footprint if 80 A is a customer-facing spec.

Current-handling limits of pads and motor wires (Section 3.6) reinforce this.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right]$$

At T_j = 115 °C: R = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0045 × 90) = **1.405 mΩ** (1.4 mΩ is used below).

This creates **positive thermal feedback** (loss ∝ R(T)). The coupled solution is handled in Section 3.4.

### 2.2 Conduction loss

In 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification, current flows through exactly two switch positions at every instant: one high-side or its complementary low-side in the PWM leg, plus one low-side (or high-side) in the return leg.

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.4\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{17.9\ W}$$

The per-FET average, with each switch position conducting about 1/3 of the electrical cycle and stresses rotating across six FETs, is ≈ **3.0 W**. The **instantaneous** per-FET dissipation while conducting is I²R = **8.96 W**. This instantaneous figure sets the junction ripple at low eRPM, where a single commutation sector can last milliseconds.

### 2.3 Hard-switching (V–I overlap) loss

Only the PWM leg switches. Rise and fall times are set by the Miller plateau:

$$t_r \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g} = 8\text{ ns}$$

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 16\text{ ns}\times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.77\ W}$$

### 2.4 Secondary switching terms

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| C_oss (per switching leg) | ½·C_oss·V²·f_sw × 2 FETs | 0.046 W |
| Reverse recovery | V_bus·Q_rr·f_sw | 0.048 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I_ph·2·t_dt·f_sw | 0.31 W |
| Gate drive (driver IC, not FET) | Q_g·V_GS·f_sw × 2 | 0.043 W |
| **Subtotal** | | **≈ 0.45 W** |

### 2.5 Shunt and copper

- **Shunt:** P_sh = I²·R_sh = 6400 × 0.5 mΩ = **3.2 W** (DC-link current ≈ I_ph at high duty).
- **Copper (PCB, ~0.3 mΩ effective path):** 6400 × 0.3 mΩ ≈ **1.9 W**.
