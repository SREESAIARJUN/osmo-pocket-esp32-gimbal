## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

In six-step commutation, two phases conduct at any instant. With complementary PWM, the current always flows through exactly one FET in each of the two active legs:

- the high-side FET during D,
- the low-side FET during (1−D).

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j) + I^2 R_{Cu}$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

This gives the typical ×1.6 factor at 125 °C.

| T_j | R_ds | 2·I²·R_ds at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W |
| 100 °C | 1.45 mΩ | **18.6 W** |
| 125 °C | 1.60 mΩ | 20.5 W |

### 1.3 PCB copper

The path is: battery pad → bus plane → FET → phase pad → motor wire pad. It runs on 2 oz outer layers plus 2 oz inner layers, stitched with vias. The estimated round-trip resistance is R_Cu ≈ 0.5 mΩ.

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.5\text{ m}\Omega = 3.2\ \text{W per channel}$$

Copper loss is frequently ignored and is often the second-largest loss term.

### 1.4 Hottest single FET

The worst case is the high side of the active phase at D → 1:

$$P_{FET,max} = I^2 R_{ds}(100^\circ C) = 6400 \times 1.45\text{ m}\Omega = 9.3\ \text{W}$$

The junction-to-case rise is ΔT_JC = 9.3 W × 1.0 K/W = 9.3 K. **The FET junction is not the bottleneck. The board is.**
