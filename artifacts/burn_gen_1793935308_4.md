# STM32H743 Flight Controller + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal and DShot Architecture

## 0. Architectural Correction

The STM32H743 should not commutate the motors directly. In every production design of this class, ArduPilot on the H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32), and each ESC MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM.

Doing six-step commutation on the H743 would consume 24 PWM channels, about 12 comparator/ADC zero-cross paths and hard-real-time ISRs alongside the EKF. That is not viable with ArduPilot. This document therefore covers three layers:

1. **Power stage:** losses and thermal behavior, per ESC channel.
2. **ESC MCU:** 48 kHz PWM generation.
3. **H743:** bidirectional DShot600 timers and DMA.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Six-step with synchronous rectification

In any 60° commutation sector:

- The **PWM leg** conducts through its high-side FET for duty D and its low-side FET for (1 − D). Because both have the same R_ds, the leg dissipates I²R regardless of D.
- The **return leg** low-side FET is statically on and dissipates I²R.

The per-motor conduction loss is therefore:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

### 2.2 Ripple correction

Ripple adds to the RMS current:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{DC}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

The worst case is D = 0.5:

ΔI = 25.2 × 0.25 / (8 µH × 48 kHz) = **16.4 A pk-pk**

ΔI²/12 = 22.4 A², which is negligible against 80² = 6400 A² (+0.35 %).

At 80 A burst, I_rms² ≈ 6422 A².

### 2.3 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ /\mathrm{K}$$

| Case | R_ds | P_cond per motor at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Typ, 25 °C | 0.85 mΩ | 10.9 W |
| Typ, 125 °C | 1.28 mΩ | **16.4 W** |
| Max, 125 °C (design worst case) | 1.50 mΩ | **19.3 W** |

Because R_ds rises with temperature, the thermal solution must be iterated (Section 5.4).

## 3. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Its low-side FET soft-switches (ZVS via the body diode), so hard-switching loss occurs in the high-side FET only.

### 3.1 V–I overlap loss

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

= 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 kHz = **1.94 W**

### 3.2 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = V_{bus} Q_{rr} f_{sw}$$

= 25.2 × 40 nC × 48 kHz = **0.048 W**

Note: Q_rr scales with di/dt and current. At 80 A use about 2× the datasheet value, giving **≈ 0.10 W**.

### 3.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,tr} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw}$$

= 0.5 × 1.5 nF × 635 × 48 kHz = **0.023 W**

### 3.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f\,I\,(2 t_{dt})\,f_{sw}$$

= 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 kHz = **0.61 W**

This is a significant term. Keep dead time at or below 100 ns; AM32 allows tuning.

### 3.5 Gate charge

$$P_g = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw}$$

= 60 nC × 10 V × 48 kHz = 0.029 W per switching FET, or **0.058 W** per leg. Most of this dissipates in the driver and R_g, not in the FET.

### 3.6 Per-motor burst budget at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

| Loss | W (typ) | W (worst) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 16.4 | 19.3 |
| Switching overlap | 1.94 | 2.9 (t_r + t_f = 60 ns) |
| Dead time | 0.61 | 0.92 |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.12 | 0.15 |
| Gate (FET share) | 0.03 | 0.03 |
| **Total per motor** | **19.1** | **23.3** |
| **4 motors** | **76.4** | **93.2** |

Efficiency check: 4 × 80 A × ~22 V ≈ 7 kW mechanical draw, so 76 W corresponds to about 1.1 % power-stage loss. That is plausible.

### 3.7 Switching-frequency sensitivity

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial f_{sw}} \approx \frac{2.7\ \mathrm{W}}{48\ \mathrm{kHz}} = 56\ \mathrm{mW/kHz}$$

per motor at 80 A. Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.35 W per motor but doubles ripple (65 A² extra, which is still negligible) and adds audible noise and motor iron loss. 48 kHz is a reasonable choice.
