**Job #48: Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, ArduPilot Bi-Directional DShot600 Allocation, and Netlists**

## 0. Architectural Corrections and Feasibility Findings

Three findings change the scope of this design and should be resolved first.

### Finding 1: The H743 cannot commutate the motors

ArduPilot is flight-control firmware. It does not perform BLDC commutation. A 4-in-1 ESC needs dedicated ESC MCUs running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. The system is therefore partitioned as follows.

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──DShot600 bidir ×4──► 4× ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32)
                                              └─► 4× 3-phase gate driver ─► 24× MOSFET
```

There are two ways to implement this:

- **Option A:** 4 single-channel ESC MCUs. This is the reference design in this document.
- **Option B:** 1 MCU driving 4 motors. This option has no mainstream firmware support.

### Finding 2: "80A burst" is not a nano-class number

At 6S full charge, four simultaneous 80 A channels draw:

**320 A × 25.2 V ≈ 8 kW**

Section 3 shows that a 30.5 × 30.5 mm board heats at **≈13 K/s** under that load. This is only viable as a short (≤2–3 s), per-channel, non-simultaneous rating. The honest continuous rating is **~30 A/channel** (Section 3.4). The datasheet should specify:

- "80 A burst, ≤2 s, single channel, 25 °C start"
- "30 A continuous with propwash cooling"

A true 20 × 20 mm "nano" board cannot carry these currents through its copper and connectors. This design assumes a **30.5 × 30.5 mm** board.

### Finding 3: Voltage headroom on 40 V FETs at 6S is marginal

This is derived in Section 2.6. Either select 60 V FETs or enforce a ≤2 nH commutation loop.
