## Design Model: 80 A Burst Thermal, 48 kHz Switching Losses, Bi-Directional DShot600 DMA

## 0. Architecture Decisions and Feasibility

Two points drive the rest of this document.

**1. The STM32H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC controller.** It runs ArduPilot and emits DShot600. Each motor channel is commutated by a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware on an AT32F421 or STM32G071. Commutating four motors from the H743 while it runs ArduPilot is not a supported ArduPilot configuration. "48 kHz switching" therefore means the ESC-side PWM frequency.

**2. 80 A per channel on a nano board is a short-transient rating only.** Section 3 shows that a 25×25 mm board running four channels at 80 A dissipates about 90 W into roughly 3 J/K of thermal mass. That is about 30 K/s of adiabatic temperature rise. I recommend specifying the board as:

- **Single-channel burst:** 80 A, ≤1 s
- **All-channel burst:** 50 A, ≤2 s
- **Continuous:** 35 A per channel with prop-wash airflow

The model below lets you re-derive these limits with your actual MOSFET datasheet values.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-Step Commutation with Synchronous Rectification

In each 60° commutation state, phase A is PWM'd (high side on for duty D, low side on for 1−D through synchronous rectification), and phase B's low side is held on continuously. Summing the conducting resistances over one PWM period:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{hot}\left[D + (1-D) + 1\right] = 2\,I^2 R_{hot}
$$

Conduction loss is **independent of duty cycle** when synchronous rectification is used. The only exception is dead-time diode conduction, handled below.

Each MOSFET carries current for 120° of every 360° electrical cycle, so the average per-device conduction loss is:

$$
P_{cond,FET} = \frac{2 I^2 R_{hot}}{6}
$$

### 1.2 Hot Resistance Including Copper

The effective resistance is the MOSFET plus the PCB copper path:

$$
R_{hot} = k_T R_{25} + R_{Cu}, \qquad R_{Cu} = \rho_{Cu}(T)\,\frac{\ell}{w\,t}
$$

Example copper path: 6 mm long, 4 mm wide, stitched across three 2 oz layers (t ≈ 3 × 70 µm).

$$
R_{Cu} \approx \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\times 1.39 \times 0.006}{0.004 \times 210\times10^{-6}} \approx 0.17\ \text{m}\Omega \text{ per conduction path (at 125 °C)}
$$

Each conduction state has two paths (in and out), so:

$$
R_{hot} \approx 1.5 + 2(0.17) \approx 1.84\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.84\text{ m}\Omega = 23.6\ \text{W}
$$

Of this, the MOSFETs alone account for 19.2 W.

### 1.4 Dead-Time Body-Diode Loss

$$
P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 2(200\text{n}) \times 48\text{k} = 1.23\ \text{W}
$$

Reducing dead time to 100 ns halves this loss. Verify shoot-through margin on the gate driver's propagation-delay spread first.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd high side hard-switches. The low side soft-switches after its body diode conducts, but it contributes Q_rr loss to the high side.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½ · V_bus · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw | ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 kHz = **1.94 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw | 50 nC × 25.2 × 48 kHz = **0.06 W** |
| C_oss discharge | ½ · C_oss · V_bus² · f_sw | 0.5 × 1 nF × 635 × 48 kHz = **0.015 W** |
| Gate drive (6 FETs) | 6 · Q_g · V_GS · f_sw | 6 × 30 nC × 10 × 48 kHz = **0.086 W** (dissipated in driver and gate resistors) |
| **Total per channel** | | **≈ 2.1 W** |

### Frequency Sensitivity

$$
\frac{\partial P_{sw}}{\partial f_{sw}} \approx 43\ \mu\text{W/Hz}
$$

- Moving from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves about 1 W per channel at 80 A.
- Moving to 96 kHz adds about 2 W per channel.

At 80 A, conduction dominates by about 10:1. **MOSFET selection should prioritize R_DS(on) over Q_g.**

### DC-Link Capacitor Ripple

Worst case occurs at D = 0.5:

$$
I_{C,rms} \approx I\sqrt{D(1-D)} = 40\ \text{A}_{rms} \text{ per channel}
$$

Interleaving across four asynchronous ESCs reduces the aggregate to roughly 40√4 / 2 ≈ 40–60 A_rms. This needs:

- About 8–10 × 10 µF/50 V X7R 1210 ceramics distributed at the half-bridges
- A ≥470 µF low-ESR electrolytic on the battery leads

The ESR loss is ≈ I²·ESR_eff. With ESR_eff = 1 mΩ, that is about 2 W, mostly in the ceramics. Account for X7R DC-bias derating (about −60% at 25 V).

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Power Budget at 80 A

| Source | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| MOSFET conduction | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
