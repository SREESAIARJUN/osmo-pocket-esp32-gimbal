# STM32H743 AIO 4-in-1 ESC Flight Controller (Job #36): Mathematical Model, DMA Allocation, Netlist and Thermal Analysis

## 0. Architecture Correction and Feasibility Finding

Two problems need to be resolved before the analysis.

**1. The STM32H743 does not run the ESC.** In every production 4-in-1 design, the H743 is the flight controller running ArduPilot, and it *generates* bidirectional DShot600. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071. That MCU does the commutation at 48 kHz and drives a 3-phase gate driver plus 6 MOSFETs. The model below follows this split.

**2. "80 A burst" in a nano (20×20 mm) form factor is not thermally viable if it means 80 A per channel.** Section 3 shows roughly 86 W of dissipation into about 2 J/K of thermal mass, which means the board fails within about 1–2 s. The design is therefore analyzed two ways:

| Case | Per-channel current | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A | 80 A/ch burst | Infeasible on 20×20 mm. Requires at least a 30.5×30.5 mm board, 5×6 mm FETs and a heatsink. |
| B | 80 A total (20 A/ch), 4 × 20 A | Feasible on 20×20 mm with prop-wash airflow. |

## 1. Electrical Model: One ESC Channel (6-Step Trapezoidal, Synchronous/Complementary PWM)

### 1.1 Parameters

| Symbol | Meaning | Value (assumed) |
|---|---|---|
| V_in | 6S full charge | 25.2 V |
| f_sw | PWM frequency | 48 kHz (T = 20.83 µs) |
| R_ds(on),25 | MOSFET, 3.3×3.3 class (nano) / 5×6 class | 2.5 mΩ / 1.0 mΩ |
| k_T | R_ds tempco at T_j ≈ 110 °C | 1.45–1.5 |
| t_r + t_f | V–I overlap time (driver ~1 A, Q_gd ≈ 8–10 nC) | 30 ns |
| Q_rr | Body-diode reverse recovery | 40 nC |
| C_oss | Output capacitance at V_in | 2 nF |
| Q_g | Total gate charge | 50 nC |
| V_GS | Gate drive voltage | 10 V |
| t_dt | Dead time per edge | 100 ns |
| V_F | Body-diode forward voltage | 0.8 V |

### 1.2 Conduction Loss

In 6-step operation with complementary PWM, current always flows through exactly two FETs in series:

- **During the on-time (duty D):** the high-side of the PWM phase and the low-side of the sink phase conduct.
- **During the off-time (1−D):** the low-side of the PWM phase (synchronous freewheel) and the sink low-side conduct.

The conduction loss is therefore independent of D to first order:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2\,R_{ds(on)}\,k_T$$

### 1.3 Switching Losses (per channel; one leg switching at a time)

**V–I overlap:**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{in}\, I\,(t_r+t_f)\, f_{sw}$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{in} f_{sw}$$

**C_oss charge and discharge (2 FETs in the leg):**

$$P_{oss} = 2\cdot\tfrac12 C_{oss}V_{in}^2 f_{sw}$$

**Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors):**

$$P_{gate} = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (2 edges per period):**

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\cdot 2 t_{dt}\, f_{sw}$$

**Total per channel:**

$$P_{ch} = P_{cond}+P_{VI}+P_{rr}+P_{oss}+P_{gate}+P_{dt}$$

### 1.4 Numerical Results

| Term | Case A: 80 A, 5×6 FET (1.5 mΩ hot) | Case A′: 80 A, 3.3×3.3 FET (3.6 mΩ hot) | Case B: 20 A, 3.3×3.3 FET |
|---|---|---|---|
| P_cond | 6400 × 3.0 mΩ = **19.2 W** | 6400 × 7.2 mΩ = **46.1 W** | 400 × 7.2 mΩ = **2.88 W** |
| P_VI | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 30n × 48k = **1.45 W** | 1.45 W | **0.36 W** |
| P_rr | 40n × 25.2 × 48k = **0.048 W** | 0.048 W | 0.048 W |
| P_oss | 2n × 635 × 48k = **0.061 W** | 0.061 W | 0.061 W |
| P_gate | 2 × 50n × 10 × 48k = **0.048 W** | 0.048 W | 0.048 W |
| P_dt | 0.8 × 80 × 200n × 48k = **0.61 W** | 0.61 W | **0.15 W** |
| **P_ch** | **≈ 21.4 W** | **≈ 48.3 W** | **≈ 3.55 W** |
| **×4 channels** | **≈ 86 W** | **≈ 193 W** | **≈ 14.2 W** |

Note: In Case B, conduction is computed at the hot R_ds. At a realistic T_j of about 80 °C, P_ch is closer to 3.0 W and the 4-channel total is about 12 W.

**Key insight:** at 48 kHz, conduction loss dominates by roughly 10:1. Switching frequency is not the thermal problem; R_ds(on) and copper are.

**Switching-frequency trade-off.** Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. Moving from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves only about 1 W per channel at 80 A, at the cost of audible whine and higher current ripple. For a phase inductance L of about 15 µH, the ripple is:

$$\Delta I_{pp} \approx \frac{V_{in}D(1-D)}{L f_{sw}}$$

At D = 0.5 and 48 kHz this gives about 8.75 A. 48 kHz is therefore justified.

## 2. Bus Capacitor Ripple (Per Channel)

The input capacitor RMS current is approximately:

$$I_{C,rms} \approx I\sqrt{D(1-D)}$$

At 80 A this is 40 A_rms worst case (D = 0.5). At 20 A it is 10 A_rms.

| Case | Capacitor requirement |
|---|---|
| A | Bulk low-ESR electrolytic of at least 1000 µF/35 V (off-board), plus about 4 × 10 µF/50 V X7R 1206 per channel |
| B | 470 µF off-board, plus 2–3 × 10 µF X7R per channel |

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Steady State

The junction temperature follows the thermal-resistance chain:

$$T_j = T_a + P\,(R_{\theta JC} + R_{\theta C\text{-}PCB} + R_{\theta PCB\text{-}A})$$

For a 20×20 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz board:

| Condition | R_θ,PCB-A (whole board) |
|---|---|
| Still air | ≈ 18 °C/W |
| Typical prop wash, 5–10 m/s | ≈ 6 °C/W |

**Case B steady state:** 12–14 W × 6 °C/W gives a 72–85 °C rise. With T_a = 35 °C, the board reaches about 110–120 °C. This is at the limit and acceptable only with prop wash. It also confirms that 20 A per channel is effectively a high-duty burst rating on this form factor. Plan for roughly 12–15 A continuous.

### 3.2 Burst (Lumped Capacitance)

The board's thermal mass is estimated as:

$$C_{th} \approx m_{FR4}c_{FR4} + m_{Cu}c_{Cu} + C_{FETs}$$
