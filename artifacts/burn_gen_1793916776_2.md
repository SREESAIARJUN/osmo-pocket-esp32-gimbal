## 0. Architectural Correction and Assumptions

**Correction.** The STM32H743 is the **flight controller**. It does not commutate the motors. In every practical 4-in-1 design, commutation is done by dedicated ESC MCUs (AM32 or BLHeli_32 class, e.g. AT32F421 or STM32G071). There is one MCU per motor, or one MCU driving two motors. The ESC MCUs generate the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 sends bi-directional DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry.

This document models both sides on that basis.

**Assumptions.** The datasheet values below are representative of a 40 V N-channel MOSFET in a PDFN 3.3×3.3 package. **Replace them with your actual BOM values.** The equations are exact; the numbers are only as good as the inputs.

| Symbol | Meaning | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | Battery voltage (6S, full charge) | 25.2 V |
| I_pk | Burst current, **per motor** | 80 A |
| f_sw | ESC PWM frequency | 48 kHz |
| R_ds(on),25 | On-resistance at 25 °C | 1.2 mΩ |
| α | R_ds temperature coefficient | 0.0055 /K |
| Q_gd + Q_gs2 | Switching charge (Q_sw) | 8 + 4 = 12 nC |
| Q_g | Total gate charge @ 10 V | 40 nC |

| V_drv | Gate-drive supply | 10 V |

**Topology.** Trapezoidal 6-step commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. This is the AM32 / BLHeli_32 default.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

During any 60° commutation sector, current flows through two phase legs:

- The **PWM leg**. The high-side (HS) switch conducts for a fraction D of each PWM cycle. The complementary low-side (LS) switch conducts for the remaining (1−D).
- The **static leg**. The LS switch conducts 100 % of the sector.

So, ignoring dead time, **exactly two channels are always in series with the motor current**, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j) + I_{rms}^2\,R_{pcb}$$

With flat-top trapezoidal current, I_rms ≈ I_pk during the burst.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{ds} = 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega \times [1 + 0.0055 \times 85] = 1.76\,\text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Per-FET conduction duty

Each phase is active for 2/3 of the electrical period. It spends 1/3 as the PWM leg and 1/3 as the static leg.

| Switch | Conduction fraction of electrical period |
|---|---|
| HS | D/3 |
| LS | 1/3 + (1−D)/3 |

The sum over all six FETs is 2, which is consistent with section 1.1. The **LS FETs are the hottest at low duty**: they conduct close to 2/3 of the period.

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 110 °C

Board copper resistance is taken as R_pcb ≈ 0.5 mΩ per motor path (2 oz, 8-layer stack).

| Quantity | Result |
|---|---|
| FET conduction | 2 × 80² × 1.76 mΩ = **22.5 W** |
| PCB copper | 80² × 0.5 mΩ = **3.2 W** |
| **Total per motor** | **25.7 W** |
| Worst-case LS FET (D → 0) | (2/3) × 80² × 1.76 mΩ = **7.5 W** |

**Electro-thermal feedback.** R_ds rises with T_j, which raises loss, which raises T_j. The fixed point is:

$$T_j = T_a + R_{\theta}\,I^2 R_{ds,25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

This becomes unstable (thermal runaway) when:

$$R_\theta\, I^2 R_{ds,25}\, \alpha \ge 1$$

For the worst LS FET at 80 A:

- The loop gain is 7.5 W × 0.0055 / 1.47 ≈ 0.028 W/K.
- Runaway therefore occurs when the effective R_θ exceeds about **36 K/W**.

A FET sitting on a 2-oz plane with thermal vias is typically 15–25 K/W, so it is stable. A poorly via'd FET is not.
