**Scope:** Loss and thermal models at an 80 A burst with 48 kHz PWM, ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 timer and DMA allocation, and reference netlists.

## 0. Architecture Decisions and Assumptions

### 0.1 Topology

The H743 does **not** commutate the motors directly. ArduPilot does not run sensorless BLDC commutation. The board is therefore split into two domains:

- **Flight controller (FC):** STM32H743 running ArduPilot. It sends DShot600 frames and receives eRPM telemetry back over the same wire.
- **ESC:** four independent ESC MCUs (AT32F421 or STM32G071) running AM32 at 48 kHz PWM. Each drives its own 3-phase gate driver and six MOSFETs.

### 0.2 Feasibility Flags (read before layout)

1. **"80 A burst" means per-motor phase current, sustained for seconds, not minutes.** §3.4 shows the board's thermal mass limits this to about 5 s from a 40 °C start.
2. **Battery-side current is much lower than the phase sum, except at full throttle.**
   - Battery current per motor is I_batt ≈ D·I_phase.
   - At D ≈ 1 with all four motors at 80 A, the pack would supply about 320 A. That exceeds any XT60/XT90 and any nano-format input pad.
   - The design therefore sets a **pack-side budget of 180 A peak**, enforced by ArduPilot `BATT_*` limits and AM32 current limiting.
3. **Format:** 30.5 × 30.5 mm mounting, about 36 × 36 mm outline, 8-layer board with 2 oz copper on the outer and power layers.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation Path

In 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM, phase current at any instant flows through exactly **two** FET channels:

- one high-side FET, either the active switch or the synchronous freewheel FET, and
- one low-side FET.

This holds for both parts of the PWM period: during D·T_sw the active high-side switch conducts, and during (1−D)·T_sw the complementary low-side FET conducts. The two-FET path is therefore duty-independent, apart from the dead-time intervals handled in §2.3.

### 1.2 Temperature-Dependent Resistance

The on-resistance rises with junction temperature:

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big]
$$

The per-channel conduction loss, using the RMS phase current, is:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

With 120° trapezoidal conduction, each phase carries current for 2/3 of the electrical cycle. Each individual FET conducts for about 1/3 of the cycle, sharing that time with its complement according to D. For a channel-level burst estimate, treat I_ph,rms ≈ I_ph = 80 A. The flat-top current makes this conservative.

### 1.3 Electro-Thermal Fixed Point

Because R_ds depends on T_j, and T_j depends on the loss, the equations must be solved iteratively:

$$
T_j^{(k+1)} = T_{ref} + P_{FET}\big(T_j^{(k)}\big)\cdot R_{th,j\text{-}ref}
$$

The iteration converges when the loop gain is below 1:

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\,R_{th} = I^2\cdot 2R_{25}\alpha \cdot R_{th} < 1
$$

**Results at 80 A, iterated to T_j ≈ 110 °C:**

- R_ds(110 °C) = 0.70 × (1 + 0.0045 × 85) = **0.968 mΩ**
- P_cond,ch = 80² × 2 × 0.968 mΩ = **12.39 W per motor channel**

### 1.4 Copper Loss

Phase and bus copper add to the conduction loss. Assume about 0.25 mΩ of effective path per channel, using 2 oz copper on three paralleled layers plus via fields:

- P_Cu,ch = 80² × 0.25 mΩ = **1.60 W**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Hard-Switching V–I Overlap

Only the active switch hard-switches. The synchronous FET turns on with zero voltage, after its body diode has already been conducting.

The gate-drive currents during the Miller plateau are:

$$
t_{r,f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g}},\qquad
I_{g,on}=\frac{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}{R_g},\quad
I_{g,off}=\frac{V_{plateau}}{R_g}
$$

Inputs: V_plateau ≈ 4.5 V, V_drv = 10 V, and total gate resistance R_g = 2.2 Ω external + 1 Ω internal + driver output resistance ≈ 6 Ω.

- I_g,on ≈ 0.92 A, giving t_r ≈ 13 ns
- I_g,off ≈ 0.75 A, giving t_f ≈ 16 ns
- Add about 30% for current-commutation time and layout inductance: t_r + t_f ≈ **38 ns**

$$
P_{sw,VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 38\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.84\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Reverse Recovery and Output Capacitance

Reverse recovery of the synchronous FET's body diode:

$$
P_{Qrr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 40\text{nC}\times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

Output capacitance energy, dissipated at hard turn-on:

$$
P_{Coss}=\tfrac12 C_{oss(er)}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw}\cdot 2 = 1.6\text{n}\times 25.2^2\times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.049\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM period:

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD}\,I_{ph}\,(2t_{dead})\,f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times400\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}
$$

> **Lever:** cutting dead-time to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per channel. Verify shoot-through margin on the bench first: measure V_gs crossover with a 1 GHz probe on both FETs of one leg.
