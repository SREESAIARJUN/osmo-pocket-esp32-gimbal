# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #39): Power-Stage, Thermal, and Bidirectional DShot600 Design Model

## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

### 0.1 Architecture correction

ArduPilot running on the STM32H743 does not commutate motors. The correct architecture is:

```
[STM32H743 – ArduPilot]  --4x bidirectional DShot600-->  [4x ESC MCUs (AM32/BLHeli_32-class,
                                                          e.g. STM32G071 / AT32F421)]
                                                          --> 4x 3-phase bridges (24 FETs)
```

The **48 kHz PWM** belongs to the ESC MCUs. The **DShot timers and DMA** belong to the H743. The two are modeled separately below.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous (complementary) PWM. At any instant, current flows through:

- one high-side or low-side FET of the PWM leg (whichever is on), plus
- one low-side FET of the return leg.

So there are always **two FETs in series** with the current.

### 1.2 Equations

Hot channel resistance:

$$R_{path} = 2\,k_T R_{ds(on),25} + R_{cu} = 2(1.5)(1.0) + 0.3 = 3.3\ \text{m}\Omega$$

Conduction loss per channel:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{path}$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 80^2 \times 3.3\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{21.1\ W} \qquad (\text{FETs: } 19.2\text{ W, copper: } 1.9\text{ W})$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each of the 6 FETs in a channel carries current about 1/3 of the electrical period. On average:

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{19.2}{6} = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

**Worst case (stall or low eRPM):** commutation slows, and one FET pair can carry current for the full thermal time constant. The design must assume **9.6 W in a single FET** for short intervals.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only one leg per channel switches at a time in 6-step mode.

### 2.1 Loss terms

**V/I overlap (hard switching):**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

$$P_{VI} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(40\text{n})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss,er} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot1.5\text{n}\cdot635\cdot48\text{k} = 0.023\ \text{W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (2 edges per period):**

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD} I \cdot 2t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot100\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = 0.307\ \text{W}$$

**Gate drive (dissipated in the driver, not the FET):**

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2\cdot60\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k} = 0.058\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Totals

| Loss term | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Conduction (copper) | 1.9 W | 7.7 W |
| Switching (V·I) | 1.94 W | 7.8 W |
| Dead time | 0.31 W | 1.2 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.08 W | 0.3 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 23.4 W** | **≈ 93.8 W** |

**Efficiency at the 80 A burst point.** Output power is roughly 25.2 V × 80 A × duty. At full throttle that is about 2 kW per channel, so the stage is about 98.8% efficient. Even so, nearly 94 W must be absorbed by a 4 cm² board.

**Sensitivity:**

- Conduction scales with **I²**, and switching scales with **I · f_sw**. Conduction dominates by 10×.
- Dropping f_sw to 24 kHz saves only about 1.1 W per channel and costs motor ripple/noise.
- The lever that matters is **R_ds(on)**. Paralleling FETs halves conduction loss, but you will not fit them on a 20×20 board.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Adiabatic burst (two-node lumped model)

For short bursts, convection is negligible and the board heats as a thermal capacitance.

| Element | Volume | c_v | C_th |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copper (6 layers × 2 oz ≈ 70 µm, 4 cm²) | 0.168 cm³ | 3.45 J/cm³K | 0.58 J/K |
| FR-4 (1.6 mm, 4 cm²) | 0.64 cm³ | 1.85 J/cm³K | 1.18 J/K |
| 24 FETs plus components | — | — | ≈ 0.7 J/K |
| **Total C_board** | | | **≈ 2.5 J/K** |

Heating rate with all four channels at 80 A:

$$\frac{dT}{dt} = \frac{P}{C} = \frac{93.8}{2.5} \approx 37\ \text{K/s}$$

Starting at 40 °C, the board reaches 110 °C in **about 1.9 s**.
