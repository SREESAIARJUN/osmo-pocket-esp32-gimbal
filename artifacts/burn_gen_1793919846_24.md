## 0. Architecture Clarification and Headline Findings

### 0.1 What "STM32H743 4-in-1 ESC" means here

The H743 does not commutate the motors. A realistic all-in-one (AIO) partitions the work as follows:

| Function | Device | Firmware |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (480 MHz max, run at 400 MHz in ArduPilot) | ArduPilot (ChibiOS) |
| ESC MCUs (×4) | AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071GBU6 | AM32 (supports a 48 kHz PWM option) |
| Gate drivers (×4) | FD6288Q (3-phase, 250 mA / 500 mA) | n/a |
| Power MOSFETs (×24) | 40 V N-ch, PDFN 3.3×3.3 | n/a |

**The 48 kHz switching frequency is set in AM32, not in ArduPilot.** The H743 only emits DShot600 frames and decodes eRPM telemetry.

### 0.2 Headline findings

1. **80 A per channel on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board is a transient rating of roughly 1–2 s, not a continuous one.** The board dissipates about 115–125 W at 4×80 A, and its thermal capacitance is about 5 J/K (derived in §3).
2. **Total battery current at 4×80 A is about 300 A.** This exceeds what XT60 connectors and typical nano pad and wire geometries can carry. The realistic system-level burst is "80 A on one channel, briefly," as in an aggressive yaw or punch-out transient.
3. **Bidirectional DShot600 needs 4 motors on 2 timers.** This uses 4 DMA streams after sharing, which fits comfortably in the H7's 16 DMAMUX-routed streams (§4).

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Commutation topology

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM. At any instant within a 60° sector:

- The **PWM phase** alternates its high-side FET (during D) and low-side FET (during 1−D).
- The **return phase** holds its low-side FET continuously ON.

Two FETs therefore carry I_ph at all times.

### 2.2 Conduction loss

Temperature-dependent on-resistance:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{DS} = 1.3 \times (1 + 0.425) = 1.85\ \text{m}\Omega \;\rightarrow\; \text{use } 2.0\ \text{m}\Omega \text{ (spread margin)}$$

The RMS current includes the PWM ripple ΔI. For a motor with L_ph ≈ 15 µH:

$$\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\, D}{2 L_{ph} f_{sw}} \approx \frac{1.3 \times 0.95}{2 \cdot 15\mu \cdot 48k} \approx 0.9\ \text{A}$$

The ripple contribution to RMS current is ΔI²/12. This is negligible at 80 A, so I_rms ≈ I_ph.
