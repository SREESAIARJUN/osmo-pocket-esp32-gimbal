# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #32): Design Package

This package covers the architecture, loss models, thermal analysis, ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 timer/DMA plan, and schematic netlists.

**Read this first.**
- **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every shipping AIO, the H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot to dedicated ESC MCUs (AT32F421 or STM32G071) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The 48 kHz PWM, dead-time and commutation live there. The model below follows that split.
- **Component parameters are representative assumptions.** Replace them with values from your selected MOSFET and driver datasheets. The equations stay valid.
- **The headline result is negative.** The thermal math shows 80 A per motor is only a short transient (about 1 s) on a nano-class board. That conclusion drives the design.

## 1. System Architecture

```
 VBAT (6S, 25.2 V max) ──┬── TVS + 4×470µF-equiv bulk ── 3-phase bridges ×4
                         ├── 5V/2A buck ── 3V3 LDO (FC)  ── STM32H743VIT6 (ArduPilot)
```

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{DS} = 0.9 \times 1.55 = 1.40\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 2.2 Current path (trapezoidal / 6-step)

At any instant one high-side and one low-side leg conduct, with phase current I_ph.

With synchronous rectification, the PWM-ed leg alternates between its HS and LS device:
- The HS conducts for duty D.
- The LS of the same leg conducts for (1 − D).
- The static LS in the opposite leg conducts 100 % of the time.

Total conducted resistance per motor is therefore always two devices in series:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot 2 R_{DS}(T_j) + P_{trace}$$

### 2.3 Phase current vs. DC current

The burst is specified as DC-link current I_dc = 80 A. Phase current relates to it through duty: I_ph ≈ I_dc / D.

- At full throttle (D → 0.95–1.0), I_ph ≈ 80–84 A.
- Ripple at 48 kHz: ΔI = V(1−D)D / (L_ph · f_sw). With L_ph ≈ 15 µH, the worst case at D = 0.5 is about 8.8 A p-p.
- The RMS penalty from ripple is I_rms² = I² + ΔI²/12, which adds less than 0.1 %. It is negligible at full throttle.

Take I_ph,rms = 82 A.

### 2.4 Numbers

| Term | Equation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (hot) | 82² × 2 × 1.40 mΩ | 18.8 W |
| Copper (phase + bus, ~0.35 mΩ effective) | 82² × 0.35 mΩ | 2.4 W |
| Shunt share (0.25 mΩ, total current 4 × 80 A) | (320²×0.25 mΩ)/4 | 6.4 W per motor-equivalent (25.6 W total!) |

**Shunt finding.** A single common shunt at 320 A total dissipates 25.6 W, which is unacceptable. Use one of these instead:
- 0.1 mΩ in parallel (Kelvin) gives 10.2 W total.
- A Hall-effect sensor (e.g., ACS-class or TMCS1100-class) gives about 0.1–0.3 W but needs a larger footprint.

Recommendation: 2× 0.2 mΩ 2512 in parallel (0.1 mΩ), with gain re-scaled in `BATT_AMP_PERVLT`.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 3.1 Hard-switching transition time

Using the Miller plateau, with V_pl ≈ 4.5 V and gate-loop resistance R_g,tot = 4.7 + 1.2 (internal) + driver output ≈ 8 Ω:

$$t_{sw,on} \approx \frac{Q_{gd} R_{g}}{V_{drv}-V_{pl}} = \frac{12\text{nC}\cdot 8}{12-4.5} = 12.8\ \text{ns}$$

$$t_{sw,off} \approx \frac{Q_{gd} R_{g}}{V_{pl}} = \frac{12\text{nC}\cdot 8}{4.5} = 21.3\ \text{ns}$$

Add Q_gs2 (V–I overlap): about +5 ns each, giving t_on ≈ 18 ns and t_off ≈ 26 ns.

### 3.2 Switching loss (one hard-switched device per motor)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_{on}+t_{off}) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 82 \times 44\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 2.18\ \text{W}$$

### 3.3 Reverse recovery and Coss

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

Q_rr increases with di/dt; derate ×3 to get **0.15 W**.

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,eq} V^2 f_{sw}\times 2 = 0.5 \times 2.5\text{nF}\times 25.2^2 \times 48\text{k}\times 2 = 0.15\ \text{W}$$

### 3.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD} I_{ph} \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 82 \times 400\text{ns}\times 48\text{k} = 1.26\ \text{W}$$

Lever: cutting dead-time to 100 ns (if the driver and FETs permit) saves 0.63 W per motor.

### 3.5 Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g)

$$P_g = Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} \times N_{switching} = 55\text{n}\times 12\times 48\text{k}\times 2 = 0.063\ \text{W}$$

### 3.6 At full throttle (D ≈ 1)

Real PWM switching nearly vanishes at 100 % duty. However, AM32 still commutates (6 transitions per electrical cycle) and typically caps duty below 100 %.

**Use the 48 kHz figures as the conservative bound.** They represent the worst case at around 80–90 % throttle, where current is still about 75 A.

## 4. Loss Budget per Motor and Board Total (80 A Burst, T_j = 125 °C)

| Loss | W per motor |
|---|---|
| FET conduction | 18.8 |
| Copper traces | 2.4 |
| Switching (V–I overlap) | 2.18 |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.30 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.26 |
| Gate drive | 0.06 |
| Shunt (0.1 mΩ option, share) | 2.6 |
| **Total per motor** | **≈ 27.6 W** |
| **Board total (×4)** | **≈ 110 W** |
| Plus FC + regulators | ≈ 2 W |

**Efficiency check:** P_out ≈ 25.2 × 320 = 8.06 kW, so η_ESC ≈ 98.6 %. The efficiency is fine; the heat *density* is the problem.

For comparison, at 45 A continuous: conduction scales as (45/82)² → 5.7 W, switching scales roughly with I → 1.2 W. That is about **8.4 W per motor (≈ 34 W board)**.

## 5. Thermal Model

### 5.1 Junction rise during the burst (device level)

Per FET, conduction + share of switching is about 9.4 + 1.7 ≈ 11 W for the hottest device (the static LS, which conducts 100 %).

Transient Z_θJC at 1 s is about 0.8 K/W, giving ΔT_JC ≈ 9 K. That is small; **the PCB is the bottleneck.**
