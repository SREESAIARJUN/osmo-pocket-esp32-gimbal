## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Feasibility Flags

Three issues need to be resolved before any modeling.

### 0.1 The H743 does not commutate the motors

ArduPilot runs on the STM32H743 as the flight controller only. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. A typical choice is the STM32G071 or AT32F421, driving a 3-phase gate driver at 48 kHz PWM.

The H743's job is to:
- generate DShot600 frames, and
- decode bidirectional eRPM telemetry.

The 48 kHz switching analysis below therefore applies to the ESC power stage, not the H743.

### 0.2 80 A burst on a nano form factor is not thermally viable as stated

A 20×20 mm board cannot physically host 24 power MOSFETs (6 per channel × 4 channels) in packages rated for 80 A. Section 2.6 quantifies this:
- **20×20 mm board:** the burst window collapses to about 2 s.
- **30.5×30.5 mm board with 2 paralleled FETs per switch position:** this is the minimum viable configuration.

**Recommendation:** treat "nano" as the FC board (20×20) stacked on a 30.5×30.5 ESC board. Alternatively, derate the AIO to 45 A burst / 35 A continuous.

### 0.3 "80 A" must be defined as phase current

Battery current and phase current differ at partial throttle:

$$I_{phase} = I_{batt}/D$$

All models below use **80 A phase RMS per channel** as the worst case.

## 2. Thermal and Loss Model (Per ESC Channel)

### 2.1 Conduction loss

With complementary PWM in 6-step mode, current always flows through exactly two switch positions:
- the non-PWM leg's fixed device, and
- either the HS or LS device of the PWM leg (synchronous rectification).

Neglecting dead time, the total conduction loss is:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \cdot I^2 \cdot \frac{R_{DS(on)}(T_j)}{n}$$
