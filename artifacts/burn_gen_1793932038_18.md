# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #8): Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and Bidirectional DShot600 Timer/DMA Allocation

This document covers architecture, conduction and switching loss models at 80 A burst and 48 kHz, a transient thermal model, the DShot600 timing and DMA plan for ArduPilot, and reference netlists. Every number traces to a stated assumption, so the model can be recomputed with real datasheet values.

---

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Design Findings

### 0.1 Architecture

The STM32H743 cannot drive the bridges directly. It runs ArduPilot and generates DShot. Commutation and 48 kHz PWM run on dedicated ESC MCUs.

```
 6S LiPo ──► Power stage (24 × N-MOSFET, 4 × 3-phase bridges)
                 ▲ gate drive
          4 × FD6288Q-class 3-phase gate drivers
                 ▲ 6 × PWM each
          4 × AT32F421 / STM32G071 ESC MCU (AM32 firmware, 48 kHz PWM)
                 ▲ bidirectional DShot600 (1 wire each)
          STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ── IMU (SPI), Baro (I²C), OSD, UARTs
```

### 0.2 Findings to resolve before layout

| # | Issue | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | "80 A burst" is undefined. It could be per channel or total. | Losses scale with I². 80 A/ch dissipates about 16× more than 20 A/ch (80 A total). |
| F2 | 80 A per channel on a 20×20 / 25.5×25.5 nano board | About 80 W total dissipation. Adiabatic duration is about 2–3 s. It cannot be a continuous rating. |
| F3 | 4 × 80 A × D gives about 290 A battery current | Exceeds XT60/XT90 practicality, pad ampacity, and connector ratings. |
| F4 | 48 kHz PWM | Switching loss is small compared with conduction at 80 A. It dominates only at low current. |

I model the worst case (80 A per channel), then provide a scaling table.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation and current paths

The model assumes AM32 6-step commutation with complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM on the high side. In each 60° sector, one high-side FET (H_x) and one low-side FET (L_y) conduct:

- **On-time (D):** current flows V_bus → H_x → motor → L_y → GND.
- **Off-time (1−D):** current freewheels through L_x (synchronous) → motor → L_y.

Two R_DS(on) are always in series with the phase current. This gives the per-channel result directly:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Per-FET RMS currents

Each phase is active for 2/3 of the electrical cycle (1/3 as source, 1/3 as sink).

$$I_{H,rms}^2 = \frac{D}{3}I^2 \qquad I_{L,rms}^2 = \frac{1}{3}I^2 + \frac{1-D}{3}I^2 = \frac{2-D}{3}I^2$$

Check: 3·(I_H² + I_L²)·R = 3·(2/3)·I²·R = 2I
