# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #7): Power, Thermal and DShot Architecture

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

Two architectural points need settling first, because they change the math.

**1. The H743 should not commutate the motors.**
- In every production 4-in-1 stack, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller.
- Each motor channel has its own small MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071) running AM32 or BLHeli_32.
- One H743 cannot do sensorless 6-step commutation for four motors at 48 kHz PWM while also running the EKF.
- This document therefore assumes **H743 FC → bidirectional DShot600 → 4× ESC MCU → 4× 3-phase bridges**.

**2. "80A burst" is interpreted as 80 A phase current per channel.**
- Duration is 1–5 s, battery is 6S (25.2 V full, 22.2 V nominal).
- The PCB is a nano 20×20 mm stack using 3.3×3.3 mm DFN MOSFETs.

No MOSFET part number was specified, so the model is **parametric**. The worked numbers use this representative 40 V, 3.3×3.3 DFN device:

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| R_DS(on) @ 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | R₂₅ | 1.5 mΩ |
| R_DS(on) temperature coefficient | α | 0.0045 /K |
| Rise + fall time (with driver) | t_r + t_f | 15 + 15 ns |
| Total gate charge | Q_g | 45 nC |
| Output charge | Q_oss | 40 nC |
| Reverse recovery charge | Q_rr | 30 nC |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in trapezoidal (6-step) drive with synchronous rectification

During any 60° commutation sector, one leg PWMs and one leg's low-side FET is held ON:

- **PWM-ON (fraction D):** current flows through HS_a → motor → LS_b.
- **PWM-OFF (fraction 1−D):** current flows through LS_a (synchronous rectification) → motor → LS_b.

Therefore **exactly two FETs are always in series with the phase current**:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot R_{DS}(T_j)\cdot\left[D + (1-D) + 1\right] = 2\,I_{ph,rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Ripple correction

With phase inductance L_ph, the peak-to-peak ripple current is:

$$\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}-E_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}, \qquad I_{rms}^2 = I_{avg}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

For a typical 5" motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH line-to-line, D = 0.85 at burst, V_bus − E ≈ 4 V):

- ΔI = (4 × 0.85)/(15e-6 × 48e3) = 4.7 A
- The ripple term ΔI²/12 = 1.85 A², which is negligible against 6400 A².

### 1.3 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on), solved self-consistently

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\right]$$

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}\,R_{\theta JC}$$

Iterate at T_j = 110 °C:

- R_DS = 1.5 × (1 + 0.0045 × 85) = **2.07 mΩ**
- **P_cond,ch = 2 × 80² × 2.07e-3 = 26.5 W per channel**

At 25 °C it would be 19.2 W, so the temperature penalty alone is +38%.

### 1.4 Copper and shunt losses

| Element | R | Loss @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Shunt (if per-channel low-side) | 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| PCB traces + vias (2 oz, 6L, ~0.4 mΩ path) | 0.4 mΩ | 2.6 W |
| Motor solder pads/wires (board-side share) | 0.2 mΩ | 1.3 W |

The copper share is about **5.5 W per channel**. This heat lands in the same board, which is critical on a 20×20.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches; each PWM period has one turn-on and one turn-off of the HS FET. Each term is evaluated with V = 25.2 V, I = 80 A, f = 48 kHz.

**Hard-switching V–I overlap:**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}$$

0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 30e-9 × 48e3 = **1.45 W**

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V f_{sw}$$

40e-9 × 25.2 × 48e3 = **0.048 W**

**Reverse recovery (LS body diode during dead time):**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw}$$

30e-9 × 25.2 × 48e3 = **0.036 W**

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges):**

$$P_{dt} = V_F I \cdot 2t_{dt} f_{sw}$$

0.8 × 80 × 100e-9 × 48e3 = **0.31 W**

**Gate drive (dissipated in driver plus R_g, not in the FET):**

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2$$

45e-9 × 10 × 48e3 × 2 = **0.043 W**

**P_sw,ch total ≈ 1.85 W per channel.**

Sensitivity:
- P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it is about 0.9 W; at 96 kHz it is about 3.7 W.
- At 80 A, conduction dominates by 14:1, so **48 kHz is not the thermal problem**. It is a sound choice for current ripple and acoustic behavior.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Network (Cauer, per FET pair → board)

```
P_FET ──[R_θJC 1.5 K/W]── T_case ──[R_θCB 0.8 K/W (solder+via array)]── T_board
                                                                          │
                                         C_board ≈ 4.5 J/K               [R_θBA]
                                                                          │
                                                                        T_ambient
```

### 4.2 Board heat capacity (20×20 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz outer + 1 oz inner)

| Component | Calculation | Heat capacity |
|---|---|---|
| Copper | ≈ 0.75 g | ≈ 0.29 J/K |
| FR4 | 0.4 cm³ × 1.85 g/cm³ × 1.1 J/gK | ≈ 0.81 J/K |
| 24 FETs + solder + capacitors | ≈ 2.5 g | ≈ 1.3 J/K |
| Optional 2 g aluminium heat spreader/frame | | ≈ 1.8 J/K |
| **Total C_th** | | **≈ 2.4 J/K bare, ≈ 4.2 J/K with spreader** |

### 4.3 Convection under prop wash

Assume h ≈ 60 W/m²K and an effective area including the stack and wires of about 1.6e-3 m²:

- R_θBA ≈ 1/(60 × 1.6e-3) ≈ **10 K/W**
- Steady-state sink capability at ΔT = 60 K is about **6 W**.
- Conclusion: **the burst is purely a thermal-capacitance problem.**

### 4.4 Transient solution

$$T_b(t) = T_a + P R_{\theta BA}\left(1-e^{-t/\tau}\right), \quad \tau = R_{\theta BA} C_{th}$$

With spreader: τ = 10 × 4.2 = 42 s. Since t ≪ τ, the adiabatic approximation holds:

$$\frac{dT_b}{dt} \approx \frac{P}{C_{th}} = \frac{136}{4.2} \approx 32\ \text{K/s}$$

FET junction offset above the board:

- P_FET,peak ≈ 26.5/2 + 1.85 ≈ 15 W in the HS FET.
- ΔT_jb = 15 × (1.5 + 0.8) ≈ **35 K**.
