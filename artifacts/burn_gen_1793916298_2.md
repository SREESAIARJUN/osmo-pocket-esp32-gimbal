## 0. Architecture Decisions and Assumptions

Some parts of the brief need clarifying before the math is meaningful.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** Four 6-step bridges need 24 gate signals plus per-channel BEMF and current sensing. The H743 has two advanced timers (TIM1/TIM8) plus HRTIM, which is not enough for four independent bridges at 48 kHz while also running ArduPilot. The architecture is therefore:

- **STM32H743 (ArduPilot)** sends bidirectional DShot600 to **4× AT32F421 ESC MCUs (AM32 firmware)**.
- Each ESC MCU drives one 3-phase gate driver and six MOSFETs, with PWM at 48 kHz.

**2. "Nano" and "80 A burst" conflict.** I assume a 30.5×30.5 mm ESC area and a stacked or adjacent FC. On a true 20×20 mm board, 80 A is not physically sustainable for more than about 1 second (see §3). The model below quantifies this rather than assuming it away.

**3. Electrical operating point:** 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V maximum.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At the design point T_j = 100 °C:

$$R_{DS}(100) = 1.0 \times (1 + 0.006 \times 75) = 1.45\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Bridge conduction

With 6-step commutation and synchronous (complementary) PWM, exactly two MOSFETs are in the current path at every instant: one high-side or its complementary low-side in the active leg, plus the low-side in the return leg.

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\, I_{ph,rms}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

PWM ripple adds a ΔI²/12 term. At 48 kHz with typical phase inductance (~10–30 µH), ΔI_pp ≈ 5–10 A, so this term contributes less than 0.15% and is neglected.
