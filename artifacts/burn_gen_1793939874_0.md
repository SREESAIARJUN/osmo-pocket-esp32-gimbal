# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #1): Architecture, Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and DShot DMA Plan

## 0. Findings That Change the Design

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every viable 4-in-1 design, the H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bi-directional DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32). These MCUs drive three-phase gate drivers. ArduPilot has no motor-commutation firmware, and the H743's timer and ADC resources would be badly over-committed. This document assumes that split.
2. **80 A burst on a nano board (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) is a seconds-scale rating, not a thermal steady state.** The model below shows:
   - about **1.5 s** with all four channels at 80 A,
   - about **6–7 s** with a single channel at 80 A,
   - about **18–20 A per channel** sustained.

   Marketing "80 A burst" numbers for this size class are usually optimistic.
3. **Supply side.** At 6S with all four channels at 80 A phase current and high duty cycle, battery current approaches roughly **250–290 A**. The XT60 connector, the pigtail, and the battery itself all become limits before the FETs do.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Topology state analysis

In any commutation sector, two legs carry I_ph:

- **Leg A (PWM leg):** the high-side conducts for D·T; the complementary low-side conducts for (1−D)·T.
- **Leg B (return leg):** the low-side is held ON for the whole sector.
