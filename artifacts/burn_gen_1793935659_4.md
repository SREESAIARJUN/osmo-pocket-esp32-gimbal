## 0. Scope and Assumptions

I have no access to an internal "Job #62" specification, so this design rests on the explicit assumptions below. Every equation is parameterized, so you can substitute your actual BOM values.

| Parameter | Assumed value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus,max = 25.2 V | 4S is also covered by the equations |
| MOSFET | 40 V N-ch, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN, R_DS(on),max = 1.5 mΩ @ 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | One FET per switch, 6 per motor, 24 total |
| Gate driver | 3-phase half-bridge driver (e.g. FD6288Q class), V_CC ≈ 10–12 V | |
| ESC MCU | AT32F421 / STM32G071 running AM32 (or BLHeli_32-class firmware) | The H743 does **not** commutate the motors |
| PWM | 48 kHz, synchronous (complementary) rectification | |
| Burst | 80 A per motor, all four motors simultaneously (worst case) | |
| FC timer clock | 200 MHz (H743 at 400 MHz) or 240 MHz (480 MHz) | |

> ⚠️ **Feasibility flag:** An 80 A per-motor burst on a 20×20 mm "nano" 4-in-1 is not physically credible with 3.3×3.3 FETs. Real 20×20 boards are typically rated 35–45 A burst. The math below shows the board can tolerate only about 2 seconds at 4×80 A even on 30×30. Treat 80 A as a per-motor, sub-second transient, or move to a 30×30 board with 5×6 FETs and heavy copper.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C, the multiplier is 1.45, so R_DS ≈ 2.2 mΩ (≈ 2.25 mΩ is used below for margin).

### 1.2 Per-motor topology (trapezoidal, 6-step, synchronous PWM)

In each 60° commutation sector, two phases are active:

- **PWM phase:** the high-side conducts for duty D, and the low-side (synchronous rectifier) conducts for 1 − D.
- **Return phase:** the low-side is on continuously.

The instantaneous current path always passes through exactly two FETs. Including inductor ripple:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j), \qquad I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}$$

Ripple estimate at 48 kHz, assuming L_phase-phase ≈ 15 µH (typical 2207–2306 motor) at D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \cdot 0.25}{15\mu \cdot 48k} \approx 8.75\ \text{A}$$

This gives ΔI²/12 ≈ 6.4 A², which is negligible against 6400 A².

**At 80 A and T_j = 125 °C:**

$$P_{cond} = 2 \cdot 6406 \cdot 2.25\,\text{m}\Omega \approx \mathbf{28.8\ W\ per\ motor}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Averaged over an electrical cycle, each FET carries 1/6 of the total, about 4.8 W. However, the low-side conducts longer at low duty (1 − D), so the worst-case low-side FET reaches about 6–7 W averaged over its 120° active window.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model (f_sw = 48 kHz)

| Term | Equation | Values | Loss / motor |
|---|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap (hard-switched high-side) | ½ V I (t_r + t_f) f | ½·25.2·80·(20+20 ns)·48k | **1.94 W** |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | 2 V_F I t_dt f | 2·0.8·80·200 ns·48k | **1.23 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr V f | 50 nC·25.2·48k | 0.06 W |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss V f | 30 nC·25.2·48k | 0.04 W |
| Gate drive (dissipated mostly in driver) | 2 Q_g V_GS f | 2·40 nC·10·48k | 0.04 W |
| **Total switching** | | | **≈ 3.3 W** |

Design levers:

- Dead-time loss is the second-largest term. Reducing t_dt from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per motor, but only if the driver's propagation-delay mismatch allows it.
- Dropping f_sw to 24 kHz halves the switching terms (saving about 1.6 W per motor). The cost is roughly 2× ripple and more audible motor whine.

## 3. Total Board Loss at 4 × 80 A

| Contributor | Per motor | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 28.8 W | 115.2 W |
| Switching + dead-time | 3.3 W | 13.2 W |
| PCB copper (≈0.3 mΩ bus/phase path, 2 oz internal) | ~1.9 W | 7.7 W |
| Current shunt (0.25 mΩ, I² R) | 1.6 W | 6.4 W (if one shunt per ESC) |
| **Total** | | **≈ 140 W** |

Efficiency at the burst point: P_out ≈ 4·80·25.2·D. At full throttle that is about 8 kW, so efficiency is ~98%. The problem is not efficiency; it is **heat density**: 140 W concentrated in about 9 cm².
