# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #25): Power-Stage, Thermal, and Bi-Directional DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope and Ground Rules

**Architectural correction.** On every production 4-in-1 stack, the STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. It runs ArduPilot and emits DShot600. A dedicated ESC MCU commutates the FETs. That MCU is typically an AT32F421 running AM32, or an EFM8BB51 running Bluejay. The model below splits the design accordingly:

- **Domain A:** FC (H743). Covers DShot generation, bi-directional telemetry capture, and DMA allocation.
- **Domain B:** ESC (×4 channels). Covers 3-phase bridge losses, gate drive, and thermals.

**Feasibility flag.** "80 A burst per motor" on a **20×20 mm nano** board is beyond what the copper and package area can dissipate. The numbers in §2 show this. Shipping 20×20 4-in-1s are realistically 40–60 A burst. I model 80 A as requested, then derive the allowable burst duration and the derating curve.

**Device parameters are parametric.** No specific MOSFET is locked in. The baseline below is a representative 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm (PDFN/DFN) class device. Substitute datasheet values before release.

| Parameter | Symbol | Baseline |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S, full charge) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| R_DS(on) @ 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | R₂₅ | 1.0 mΩ |
| R_DS(on) temperature coefficient | α | 0.0060 /K |
| Gate–drain charge | Q_gd | 8 nC |
| Total gate charge | Q_g | 45 nC |
| Reverse-recovery charge | Q_rr | 40 nC |
| Output charge | Q_oss | 50 nC |
| Body-diode forward voltage | V_SD | 0.8 V |
| Driver plateau current | I_g | ≈ 1.0 A |
| Dead time | t_d | 200 ns |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| Junction-to-case resistance | R_θJC | 1.5 K/W |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (Per ESC Channel)

### 1.1 Topology assumption
The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM, which is AM32's default.

At any instant the current path passes through **exactly two conducting channels**:
- one high-side FET, or the synchronous low-side FET of the PWM leg, and
- one low-side FET of the return leg.

This holds independent of duty cycle, because the synchronous FET replaces the body diode during off-time.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

R_DS(T_j) = R₂₅ · [1 + α(T_j − 25)]

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.0 mΩ × 1.60 = **1.60 mΩ**

### 1.3 Channel conduction loss

P_cond = I² · (2·R_DS(T_j) + R_cu)

R_cu is the PCB copper path: pad → phase pour → motor pad. Two-ounce inner pours on a 20×20 board give a realistic R_cu ≈ 0.4 mΩ.

At I = 80 A (I² = 6400 A²):

| T_j | 2·R_DS | P_FET | P_cu | P_cond total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 2.0 mΩ | 12.8 W | 2.6 W | 15.4 W |
| 100 °C | 2.9 mΩ | 18.6 W | 2.6 W | 21.1 W |
| 125 °C | 3.2 mΩ | 20.5 W | 2.6 W | **23.0 W** |

Per individual FET at 125 °C, the load splits across the two conducting devices: **≈ 10.2 W each.**

### 1.4 Per-FET RMS (for device rating)

Each FET in 6-step carries current for 1/3 of the electrical cycle. It shares that interval with its complement in proportion to the duty D.

- High-side FET: I_rms,HS = I·√(D/3)
- Low-side FET: I_rms,LS ≈ I·√((2−D)/3)

The low-side carries the return-leg 1/3 plus the freewheel portion. The worst case is the low side at low duty.

At D = 0.9: I_rms,LS = 80·√(1.1/3) = **48 A**. This value sizes the per-device thermal design.

### 1.5 Electro-thermal stability (runaway criterion)

The steady state is stable only if the loop gain of loss versus temperature is below unity:

(∂P/∂T_j) · R_θJA < 1

Here ∂P/∂T_j = I²·2·R₂₅·α = 6400 × 2e-3 × 0.006 = **0.077 W/K**.

This requires **R_θJA < 13 K/W** per channel at 80 A continuous. That is satisfied. However, the steady-state temperature is far too high, as §3 shows.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In 6-step operation only **one leg switches at a time**: one hard-switched FET plus one synchronous FET.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard-switched transition)

The transition times follow from the gate-drain charge and the driver plateau current:

t_r ≈ t_f ≈ Q_gd / I_g = 8 nC / 1.0 A = 8 ns

The overlap loss is:

P_VI = ½ · V_bus · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw

P_VI = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 16e-9 × 48e3 = **0.77 W**

### 2.2 Reverse recovery of the synchronous FET body diode

P_rr = Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw = 40e-9 × 25.2 × 48e3 = **0.05 W**

This is load-dependent: Q_rr rises with di/dt and current. Budget 2× in practice, giving **0.10 W**.

### 2.3 Output capacitance loss

P_oss = ½ · Q_oss · V_bus · f_sw = 0.5 × 50e-9 × 25.2 × 48e3 = **0.03 W**

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

The body diode conducts twice per PWM cycle, once at each edge:

P_dt = V_SD · I · 2·t_d · f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 400e-9 × 48e3 = **1.23 W**

**This is the dominant switching term.** Reducing dead time from 200 ns to 100 ns saves ~0.6 W per channel. In AM32 this is the dead-time setting. Validate shoot-through margin on the scope first.

### 2.5 Gate drive loss (dissipated in driver/gate resistors, not the FET)

P_g = Q_g · V_drv · f_sw · 2 = 45e-9 × 10 × 48e3 × 2 = **0.04 W**

### 2.6 Switching loss summary (per channel, 80 A)

| Term | Loss |
|---|---|
| V–I overlap | 0.77 W |
| Reverse recovery (×2 margin) | 0.10 W |
| Coss | 0.03 W |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 W |
| Gate drive | 0.04 W |
| **P_sw total** | **2.17 W** |

### 2.7 Frequency trade-off

The switching loss scales linearly with frequency: P_sw ∝ f_sw. Current ripple scales inversely: ΔI_ripple ≈ V_bus·D(1−D)/(L_ph·f_sw).

For a low-inductance racing motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH) at D = 0.5:

ΔI = 25.2 × 0.25 / (15e-6 × 48e3) = **8.75 A pk-pk**

The RMS conduction penalty is (ΔI²/12)/I² ≈ 0.1 %, which is negligible. Therefore **48 kHz is justified**: the extra ~1 W over 24 kHz buys better low-throttle linearity and lower acoustic noise.
