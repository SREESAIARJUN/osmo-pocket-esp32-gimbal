# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Engineering Model (Job #19)

## 0. Feasibility Review

**80A burst on a "nano" (20×20 mm) AIO is not physically credible.** The math below shows why. I've completed the full model, but the design must change before layout.

| Constraint | 4 × 80A burst requirement | 20×20 nano reality |
|---|---|---|
| Battery current at full duty | up to ~320 A | XT30 ≈ 15 A cont.; 2 oz pours on 20 mm ≈ 30–40 A |
| Total bridge loss (Section 2) | ~78 W | Board heat capacity ≈ 5 J/K, so ≈ 15 K/s adiabatic rise |
| MCU package | H743 LQFP100 is 14×14 mm | Only TFBGA100 (8×8) fits, which needs 6–8 layers and µvias |
| 24 MOSFETs + 4 drivers + ESC MCUs | ~24 × 3.3×3.3 mm footprints | Physically won't fit with the FC on 20×20 |

**Recommended architecture:**
- **Option A:** A 30.5×30.5 stack. H743 FC on one board, and a separate 4-in-1 ESC rated "80A burst (10 s) / 55A continuous per channel."
- **Option B:** Keep the nano form factor and derate to **≈ 12–15A per motor** (typical whoop/toothpick AIO).

The model below is parameterized, so both options use the same equations.

## 2. Loss Model

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

In 6-step drive, two of the six devices carry current at any instant:
- one high-side device on phase A, during PWM-on, or the complementary low-side device on the same leg during PWM-off;
- one low-side device on phase B, held on for the whole 60° sector.

With synchronous rectification, current always flows through exactly two channels (dead time is handled separately). So per motor:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds(on),25}\,\alpha$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 0.9\,\text{m}\Omega \cdot 1.5 = \mathbf{17.3\ W}$$

Each device conducts about 1/3 of an electrical cycle (120° window, split by duty between the HS/LS pair), so the average per device is:

$$P_{cond,dev} \approx \frac{P_{cond,motor}}{6} \approx 2.9\ \text{W}$$

**Electrothermal feedback.** R_ds(on) rises with T_j, so solve iteratively:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + R_{\theta}\, P(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + 0.006\,(T_j - 25)]$$

This converges when $R_\theta \cdot \partial P/\partial T < 1$. At 80A with R_θ ≈ 15 K/W effective (Section 3), the loop gain is ≈ 0.27. It converges, but contributes about +35% over the cold estimate.

### 2.2 Switching Losses (Hard-Switched Leg Only)

Only the PWM'd leg switches. Transition times are set by the Miller plateau charge:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{src}} = \frac{8\,\text{nC}}{1.0\,\text{A}} = 8\,\text{ns} \;\;\rightarrow\;\; \text{use } 15\,\text{ns (layout/package inductance)}$$

$$t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{sink}} \approx 5\,\text{ns} \;\;\rightarrow\;\; \text{use } 15\,\text{ns}$$

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I_{ph}\,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 30\,\text{ns} \cdot 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

**Secondary terms** (per motor):

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Coss energy | $\tfrac12 C_{oss,er} V^2 f_{sw}$ (1 nF) | 0.015 W |
| Reverse recovery | $Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw}$ | 0.060 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | $V_f\, I_{ph} \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw}$ | 0.614 W |
| Gate drive (lost in driver/R_g, not the FET) | $2\, Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw}$ (2 switching FETs) | 0.046 W |

### 2.3 Totals at 80A Burst

| | Per motor | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 17.3 W | 69.2 W |
| Switching | 1.45 W | 5.8 W |
| Dead time + Q_rr + Coss | 0.69 W | 2.8 W |
| **Bridge total** | **≈ 19.5 W** | **≈ 78 W** |
| Copper (0.3 mΩ path at 320 A, if bus copper is shared) | — | 30 W |

The ~30 W copper term is why Option B (smaller current) or wide 2–3 oz pours with a short battery path are mandatory.

**Efficiency at burst:** P_out ≈ 25.2 V × 320 A ≈ 8 kW, so bridge loss is about 1%. That ratio is fine. The **absolute watts per mm²** is what kills the nano form factor.

**f_sw sensitivity:** only about 2.1 W/motor scales with f_sw. Going from 48 to 24 kHz saves only ~4 W total, but raises current ripple and audible noise. **Conduction dominates; buy lower R_ds(on), not lower f_sw.**
