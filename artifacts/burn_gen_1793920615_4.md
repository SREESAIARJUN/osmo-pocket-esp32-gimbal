## 0. Architecture Corrections and Scope

1. **The STM32H743 does not switch the MOSFETs.** In a 4-in-1 stack, the H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600. A separate ESC MCU per motor (or one shared MCU) runs AM32 or BLHeli_32 and generates the 48 kHz PWM. Section 1–3 losses belong to the ESC board. Section 4 belongs to the H743.
2. **80 A burst on a "nano" (20×20 mm) board is not thermally credible.** Section 3 shows that even a 30.5×30.5 mm board survives 80 A on all four motors for only a few seconds. I recommend either:
   - defining the 80 A burst as **per-motor, ≤5 s, non-simultaneous**, or
   - moving to a 30×30 mounting pattern with a heat spreader.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Paths in 6-Step Complementary PWM

During each 60° sector, two phases carry current. Call them leg A (the PWM leg) and leg B (the return leg).

- **Q_AH** conducts for a fraction D of each period.
- **Q_AL** (synchronous rectifier) conducts for (1 − D − 2t_dt·f_sw).
- **Q_BL** conducts 100% of the sector.

Phase current relates to battery current by I_ph ≈ I_bat / D. At full throttle D → 1.

### 1.2 RMS Current Including Ripple

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - E_{bemf})\,D}{2 L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

Assume L_ph = 8 µH, D = 0.95, and V_bus − E = 3 V:

- ΔI = 3.7 A
- ΔI²/12 = 1.1 A², which is negligible against 6400 A².

### 1.3 Bridge Conduction Loss

$$P_{cond,bridge} = I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,k_T\,R_{ds,25}$$

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2 \times 6401 \times 1.6\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{20.5\ W\ per\ motor}$$

Add copper and trace resistance. Assume 0.3 mΩ per current path (2 oz inner pours plus pads):

$$P_{Cu} = 2 \times 6400 \times 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega = 3.8\ \text{W per motor}$$

This heat is distributed into the PCB, not the junctions.

### 1.4 Per-FET Average

Over one electrical revolution, each FET serves equally as PWM, synchronous, and return switch. The electrical frequency is in the kHz range, far faster than any thermal time constant, so averaging is valid:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} = 20.5 / 6 = 3.42\ \text{W}$$
