# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #14): Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Timer/DMA Allocation

## 0. Architecture Corrections

Two points in the brief need correcting before the math can be trusted.

1. **The STM32H743 is the flight controller MCU, not the ESC MCU.** A "4-in-1 ESC" running ArduPilot bi-directional DShot needs one ESC MCU per motor, running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. Typical choices are the AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 only *generates* DShot frames and *captures* eRPM telemetry. The architecture below is an AIO board: an H743 FC plus four independent ESC channels on one PCB.
2. **80 A per channel on a nano (20×20 mm) board is a burst rating only.** The model below shows it is thermally sustainable for roughly 1–2 s, not continuously. It also implies up to 320 A of battery current, which nano-class XT30/XT60 leads and pads cannot carry. I treat 80 A as a per-motor transient and recommend a realistic continuous rating.

All device parameters below are **stated assumptions**. Substitute your selected MOSFET's datasheet values; every equation is parameterized.

## 2. Conduction-Loss Model

### 2.1 Current path in synchronous 6-step drive

Consider the active sector. Phase A is PWM'd high, phase B is held low, and phase C floats.

- **During D·T_s:** current flows through Q_AH and then Q_BL.
- **During (1–D)·T_s:** current flows through Q_AL (synchronous freewheel) and then Q_BL.

So exactly **two FETs carry I at every instant**, regardless of duty cycle.

### 2.2 Per-channel conduction loss

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds(on),25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right] \approx k_T\,R_{ds(on),25}
$$

At 80 A and T_j = 100 °C:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Per-FET distribution (worst case)

Q_BL conducts for the full 1/3 commutation period, while Q_AH conducts for D/3.

$$
P_{Q,max,inst} = I^2 R_{ds(on)} = 9.6\ \text{W (instantaneous, during its sector)}
$$

Averaged over the electrical cycle, each FET conducts about 1/3 of the time:

$$
\bar P_{Q} \approx \tfrac{19.2}{6}\cdot\tfrac{6}{3}\cdot\tfrac12 = 3.2\ \text{W average per FET}
$$

This ignores the small ripple term $I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \Delta I^2/12$. That term is negligible here: with ΔI ≈ 10 A, it adds about 0.2 %.

### 2.4 Copper and shunt losses

The phase path is roughly 4 mm of 2 oz copper plus vias, giving R_Cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ per path.

| Element | Formula | Loss @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| PCB copper | $I^2 R_{Cu}$ | ≈ 1.9 W/ch |
| Current shunt (if per-channel) | $I^2 \times 0.25\ \text{m}\Omega$ | ≈ 1.6 W |

## 3. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches hard. The low-side sync FET achieves ZVS turn-on through its body diode during dead time.

### 3.1 V-I overlap (hard-switched high side)

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}
$$

### 3.2 Body-diode reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 50\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}
$$

In practice this is about 3× higher because Q_rr scales with di/dt. Use **0.2 W**.

### 3.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$
P_{dt} = 2\,V_f\,I\,t_{dt}\,f_{sw} = 2 \times 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

### 3.4 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,er}\,V_{bus}^2\,f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = 0.023\ \text{W}
$$

### 3.5 Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g)

$$
P_g = 2\,Q_g\,V_{gs}\,f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}
$$

### 3.6 Switching total

$$
P_{sw,ch} \approx 0.97 + 0.2 + 0.61 + 0.02 + 0.05 \approx \mathbf{1.85\ W}
$$

### 3.7 Per-channel and board totals at 80 A burst

| Term | W/channel | W/board (×4) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (T_j = 100 °C) | 19.2 | 76.8 |
| Switching | 1.85 | 7.4 |
| Copper + shunt | 3.5 | 14.0 |
| **Total** | **24.6** | **≈ 98 W** |

Efficiency at burst: P_out ≈ 25.2 × 80 × D. At D = 0.9, that is 1814 W per channel, giving η ≈ 98.6 %. The efficiency is acceptable. **Heat density is the problem.**

### 3.8 Sensitivity to switching frequency

$$
\frac{\partial P_{sw}}{\partial f_{sw}} \approx 38.5\ \mu\text{W/Hz}
$$

Moving from 24 kHz to 48 kHz therefore costs about 0.9 W per channel. That is small next to conduction loss, so **48 kHz is justified** for lower current ripple and quieter motors.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Junction rise above board (steady per-FET)

With R_θJC ≈ 1.0 K/W and solder/via-array R_θCB ≈ 2.0 K/W:

$$
\Delta T_{J-B} = P_Q\,(R_{\theta JC}+R_{\theta CB}) = 3.2 \times 3.0 \approx 10\ \text{K}
$$

The FET-to-board path is fine. The bottleneck is the board-to-air path.

### 4.2 Board heat capacity (lumped)

For a 20×20 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz board with 1.6 mm FR4 plus 24 MOSFETs:

| Component | Mass × specific heat | C |
|---|---|---|
| Copper | ≈ 1.1 g × 0.385 J/g·K | 0.42 J/K |
| FR4 | ≈ 1.2 g × 1.1 J/g·K | 1.32 J/K |
| FETs | 24 × 0.1 g × 0.7 J/g·K | 0.17 J/K |
| **Total** | | **C_th ≈ 2.0 J/K** |

### 4.3 Board-to-air resistance

$$
R_{\theta,BA} = \frac{1}{h A}
$$

With A ≈ 8 cm² (both faces) and h ≈ 100 W/m²·K (prop-wash forced air), R_θBA ≈ 12.5 K/W.

Time constant: τ = R_θBA × C_th ≈ 25
