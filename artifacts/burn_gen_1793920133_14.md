## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Flag

Two points need agreement before the math, because they change it.

**1. The STM32H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC MCU.**
- The H743 runs ArduPilot and generates bidirectional DShot600.
- Each of the four ESC channels has its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical choices are the AT32F421 and STM32G071.
- Those ESC MCUs generate the 48 kHz commutation PWM.
- The 48 kHz figure is therefore an ESC firmware parameter. It is not an H743 timer.

**2. "80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board is thermally marginal to infeasible** if all four channels run at 80 A simultaneously. Section 3 shows roughly 90 W of dissipation into about 5 J/K of board heat capacity. The board can survive single-digit seconds of that. I recommend specifying 80 A as a **per-channel, ≤2 s, non-simultaneous** burst. Section 3 sizes the board to that spec.

## 2. Loss Model per ESC Channel

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

In 6-step operation with synchronous rectification, the phase current always flows through exactly two device positions:
- one high-side position (the HS FET during D, or its LS synchronous partner during 1−D), and
- one low-side FET on the return phase.

Ignoring ripple, the RMS current in each conducting position equals I_ph, independent of duty cycle.

Hot on-resistance:

$$R_{DS,hot} = k_T \cdot R_{DS,25} = 1.6 \times 0.90 = 1.44\ \text{m}\Omega$$

Ripple correction for a low-inductance motor (L_ph ≈ 10 µH, worst case D = 0.5):

$$\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{10\mu \times 48k} = 13.1\ \text{A}_{pp}$$

$$I_{rms} = \sqrt{I^2 + \tfrac{\Delta I^2}{12}} = \sqrt{6400 + 14.3} = 80.09\ \text{A}$$

The ripple contributes less than 0.2%, so it is negligible.

Conduction loss per channel:

$$P_{cond,FET} = 2 \cdot I_{rms}^2 \cdot R_{DS,hot} = 2 \times 6414 \times 1.44\text{m} = \mathbf{18.5\ W}$$

$$P_{cond,Cu} = 2 \cdot I^2 \cdot R_{Cu} = 2 \times 6400 \times 0.4\text{m} = \mathbf{5.1\ W}$$

**Self-consistency check.** At T_j = 80 °C instead of 125 °C, k_T ≈ 1.3 and P_cond,FET = 15.0 W. Iterate the R_DS(T) and T_j relationship from §3 to converge on a final value. 125 °C is used here as the conservative bound.

### 2.2 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only one half-bridge per channel is PWM-switching at any instant.

**(a) Hard-switching V·I overlap (HS turn-on and turn-off):**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery of the LS body diode at HS turn-on:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

**(c) Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,eff} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} \times 2 = 1.8\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.055\ W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period):**

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD} \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dead} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**(e) Gate charge.** This is dissipated mostly in the gate driver and gate resistors, not the FET. Two FETs switch per period.

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.058\ W}$$

**Switching subtotal (FET-dissipated, a through d):** ≈ **2.18 W per channel**

### 2.3 Channel and Board Totals

| Loss term | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (125 °C) | 18.5 W | 74.0 W |
| Copper conduction | 5.1 W | 20.5 W |
| Switching (a–d) | 2.2 W | 8.7 W |
| Gate drive | 0.06 W | 0.23 W |
| **Total** | **25.9 W** | **103.4 W** |

Efficiency at 80 A, D = 0.9:

$$\eta = \frac{P_{out}}{P_{in}} \approx \frac{25.2 \cdot 0.9 \cdot 80 - 25.9}{25.2 \cdot 0.9 \cdot 80} = 98.6\%$$

The efficiency looks good. The problem is power density, not efficiency.
