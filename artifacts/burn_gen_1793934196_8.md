# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage, Thermal, and Bidirectional DShot Design Package (Job #32)

## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

**Recommendation on the "nano" form factor.** An 80 A per-channel burst rating cannot be honestly achieved on a 20×20 mm board. With 24 MOSFETs, a gate driver, and current sensing, the copper area and thermal mass are insufficient. This package assumes:

- A **30.5×30.5 mm stack**: FC board (H743) plus a separate ESC board.
- 80 A is a **≤5 s burst** rating, not continuous.

**Partitioning.**
- The **STM32H743 runs ArduPilot** and generates bidirectional DShot600.
- Each ESC channel group is driven by a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, for example an STM32G071 or AT32F421. That MCU owns the 48 kHz commutation PWM.
- The H743 does not drive gates directly. ArduPilot has no field-oriented or six-step motor control path for this purpose.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 1.1 Topology reasoning

In six-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous (complementary) PWM, two phases conduct at every instant:

- **PWM leg:** current flows through the high-side FET for fraction D of the period and through the synchronous low-side FET for fraction (1−D).
- **Return leg:** current flows through its low-side FET for 100% of the period.

So the current always passes through exactly **two R_ds(on) in series**:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)\cdot\frac{1}{N_p}$$

where N_p is the number of parallel FETs per switch position.

### 1.2 Ripple correction

The phase current carries PWM ripple. With peak-to-peak ripple ΔI and ripple ratio r = ΔI/I:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2\left(1+\frac{r^2}{12}\right)$$

The phase-current ripple is:

$$\Delta I \approx \frac{(V_{bus}-E)\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

where E is the motor back-EMF and L_ph is the phase inductance. For a typical 5–7" motor (L ≈ 15 µH) at 48 kHz, r ≈ 0.2. This makes the correction factor ≈ 1.003, which is negligible.

### 1.3 Numbers at T_j = 125 °C

Hot on-resistance:

$$R_{ds,hot} = 0.70 \times 1.6 = 1.12\ \text{m}\Omega$$

| Configuration | P_cond per channel |
|---|---|
| N_p = 1 | 2 × 6400 × 1.12 mΩ = **14.3 W** |
| N_p = 2 (recommended for 80 A) | **7.2 W** |

### 1.4 PCB copper loss

Assume 2 oz copper, 4 layers paralleled, with an estimated path resistance of R_cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ per channel (bus → FETs → motor pad). Copper resistance rises about 0.39%/K.

$$P_{cu} = I^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.3\ \text{m}\Omega \approx 1.9\ \text{W at 25 °C}, \quad \approx 2.3\ \text{W hot}$$

### 1.5 Per-FET distribution

The fixed low-side FET in the return leg carries the full I²R loss during its 120° electrical conduction interval. Above about 100 Hz electrical frequency (any spinning motor), the die thermal time constant averages this out.

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{P_{cond,ch}+P_{sw,ch}}{6 N_p}$$
