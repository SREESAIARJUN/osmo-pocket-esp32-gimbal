## 0. Scope and Engineering Caveats

These points determine whether the model is valid.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each motor (or motor pair) has a dedicated ESC MCU, typically an AT32F421, STM32G071 or GD32E230 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 generates only the DShot600 command stream and decodes the bidirectional eRPM telemetry. The model below follows this split.
2. **80 A burst on a "nano" (20×20 mm) board is not thermally credible.** The MOSFET footprint (3.3×3.3 mm DFN) and copper area cannot sink about 100 W. This model assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz** board. The math scales: substitute your own values and the conclusions follow.
3. **All device parameters below are placeholders.** They are representative of 40 V, about 1 mΩ class FETs. Replace them with datasheet values for the selected part before sign-off.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At a design point of T_j ≈ 135 °C:

$$R_{hot} = 1.2\,\text{mΩ} \times (1 + 0.0045 \times 110) = 1.79 \approx 1.8\,\text{mΩ}$$

### 2.2 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

In any 60° commutation sector, one phase leg is PWM-switched and one phase leg's low-side is held ON. Current therefore always flows through **exactly two FETs in series**:
- PWM on: HS(A) + LS(B)
- PWM off: sync-LS(A) + LS(B)

$$P_{cond,channel} = 2\,I^2 R_{hot} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.8\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{23.0\ W}$$

This is independent of duty D because synchronous rectification puts the same R_DS in the freewheel path.

### 2.3 Per-device distribution

Each FET is active for 1/3 of the electrical period.

$$P_{HS} = \frac{I^2 R_{hot}\,D}{3},\qquad P_{LS} = \frac{I^2 R_{hot}(1-D)}{3} + \frac{I^2 R_{hot}}{3}$$

At burst (D → 1): P_HS = P_LS = 80² × 1.8 m / 3 = **3.84 W per FET**.
Check: 6 FETs × 3.84 W = 23.0 W ✓

### 2.4 PCB copper loss

Assume a phase/bus path of about 0.3 mΩ (2 oz, 8-layer, stitched):

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.3\,\text{mΩ} = 1.9\,\text{W per channel}$$

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

The following terms apply per channel. Only one leg switches at a time.

**(a) V–I overlap (hard switching):**
$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 27\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.31\ W}$$

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period):**
$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**(c) Reverse recovery:**
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 60\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.073\ W}$$

**(d) Output capacitance:**
$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.024\ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive (dissipated in the driver, not the FET):**
$$P_{g} = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

**Switching subtotal (FETs):** ≈ **2.0 W per channel**

**Design levers for switching loss:**
- Dead time is the largest controllable term. Cutting t_dt from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel.
- Dropping to 24 kHz halves P_sw but raises current ripple and acoustic noise.
