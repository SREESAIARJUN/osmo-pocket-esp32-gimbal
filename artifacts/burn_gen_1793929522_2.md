# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #23): Thermal, Switching, and Bi-Directional DShot Design Package

## 0. Scope and Feasibility Findings

These points are deliberate. They change how the rest of the package should be read.

1. **The H743 does not switch the bridge.** 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC firmware (AM32 / BLHeli_32) on dedicated ESC MCUs. The H743 sends DShot600 frames and receives eRPM telemetry. This document models both sides, but they are separate silicon.
2. **80 A on a 20×20 mm board is thermally a burst rating only.** Section 2.6 shows the board heats at roughly 30 K/s at 80 A on all four channels. A sustained 80 A is impossible on this form factor. A realistic burst window is 1–2 s, and continuous current is about 20–25 A per channel with prop-wash airflow.
3. **Device parameters are representative values, not a vendor's datasheet.** I used a 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm N-MOSFET class device. Replace the numbers in the parameter table with your chosen part's datasheet values; every equation is written symbolically first.
4. **The netlists are reference netlists for the critical nets, not a fab-ready EDA export.** Treat them as the schematic capture spec.

## 2. Loss Model (Per ESC Channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

With synchronous rectification in 6-step commutation, current always flows through exactly one high-side path and one low-side path. These are either the active FET or its complementary FET. The duty-cycle terms cancel:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\left[D + (1-D)\right]_{HS} + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)_{LS} = 2\, I^2 R_{ds,25}\,\alpha_T
$$

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.70\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.5 = \mathbf{13.44\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Copper (PCB) Loss

$$
P_{cu} = I^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.20\ W}
$$

This term is board heat, not die heat, but it loads the same thermal mass.

### 2.3 Switching (V–I Overlap) Loss

Only the active high-side FET hard-switches. The low-side FET switches at zero voltage via body-diode conduction.

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}
= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 27\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.31\ W}
$$

### 2.4 Dead-Time Diode, Reverse Recovery, C_oss, and Gate Losses

$$
P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_{dead}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}
$$

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}
$$

$$
P_{oss} = C_{oss,er} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1.5\,\text{nF} \times 635 \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}
$$

$$
P_{gate} = 2\, Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\,\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}
$$

Gate loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors.

### 2.5 Loss Totals

| Term | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 13.44 W | 53.8 W |
| Copper | 3.20 W | 12.8 W |
| Switching | 1.31 W | 5.2 W |
| Dead-time | 1.23 W | 4.9 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.17 W | 0.7 W |
| **Total** | **19.35 W** | **77.4 W** |

Two observations follow from the totals:

- **Conduction dominates (about 70% of loss).** The biggest lever is R_ds(on) and copper weight, not f_sw.
- **48 kHz costs only about 2.7 W per channel** in switching plus dead-time loss. Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.3 W per channel at 80 A. That is worth it only if motor inductance permits acceptable ripple.

### 2.6 Thermal Model

**Junction rise over PCB.** In 6-step operation each FET conducts for 120° electrical. The worst-case FET carries about 1/3 of the channel's semiconductor loss (16.15 W):

$$
P_{FET} \approx \tfrac{16.15}{3} \approx 5.4\ \text{W (peak-weighted)}, \qquad
\Delta T_{jc} = 5.4 \times 1.5 \approx \mathbf{8\ K}
$$

The junction tracks the board closely. **The board is the bottleneck.**
