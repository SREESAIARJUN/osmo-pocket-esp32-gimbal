## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step with complementary PWM

At any commutation step, current flows through one phase leg (the PWM leg) and returns through the low-side of another phase (held fully on).

- PWM leg high-side conducts for fraction D.
- PWM leg low-side (synchronous rectifier) conducts for fraction 1−D.
- Return low-side conducts 100%.

This gives:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

The result is **independent of duty cycle**, which is a key property of synchronous rectification.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence (self-consistent)

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

At T_j = 100°C with the typical part: R = 1.0 × (1 + 0.006 × 75) = **1.45 mΩ**.
With the max-spec part: 1.3 × 1.45 = **1.89 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Results at 80A

| Term | Typical | Worst-case (max R_DS) |
|---|---|---|
| MOSFET conduction, 2·I²R | 2 × 6400 × 1.45 mΩ = **18.6 W** | 2 × 6400 × 1.89 mΩ = **24.2 W** |
| PCB copper, I²R_cu | 6400 × 0.3 mΩ = **1.9 W** | 2.5 W (copper also +0.39 %/K) |
| **Conduction per motor** | **20.5 W** | **26.7 W** |

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

Each FET conducts during 120° of the 360° electrical period, so the average per FET is about P_cond/6. That is **≈3.1 W typ** and **4.0 W worst**.

The instantaneous peak is higher: 80² × 1.45 mΩ = **9.3 W** for a return low-side in its conduction window. The electrical period at typical eRPM is about 0.2–2 ms. Die thermal time constants are about 1–10 ms, so the junction partially tracks this ripple. Allow about 3–5 K of ripple margin on T_j.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. The high-side switches hard. The low-side switches nearly at zero voltage, because its body diode conducts during dead time.

### 2.1 Gate drive currents

$$
I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{pl}}{R_{g,int} + R_{g,ext}} = \frac{10 - 3.5}{3.2} = 2.03\ \text{A}
\qquad
I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{3.2} = 1.09\ \text{A}
$$

### 2.2 Transition times

$$
t_{on} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2} + Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = \frac{14\ \text{nC}}{2.03} = 6.9\ \text{ns}
\qquad
t_{off} \approx \frac{14\ \text{nC}}{1.09} = 12.8\ \text{ns}
$$
