**Three architectural findings that change the design:**

1. **The H743 cannot directly run the ESCs if you want bidirectional DShot.** Bidir DShot is a protocol between the flight controller and *ESC firmware* (AM32 / BLHeli_32 / Bluejay). The 4-in-1 section therefore needs its own ESC MCUs. Typical choices are 4× AT32F421 or STM32G071, each driving a 3-phase gate driver. The H743 only generates and captures DShot. This is the topology used below.
2. **80 A × 4 simultaneously is roughly 120 W of dissipation on a nano board.** The board survives this only for about 2 seconds (Section 2.6). The rating must be defined as a **time-limited, single-motor or short-burst figure**, or the board must grow.
3. **Input ripple current is the hidden failure mode.** It is about 40 A RMS per channel (Section 2.5). Capacitor selection matters as much as the FETs.

## 1. Power Stage Topology (per channel)

- Three half-bridges, giving 6 FETs per channel and 24 FETs total.
- In each 60° commutation sector:
  - Phase X runs PWM: the high side switches and the low side synchronously rectifies.
  - Phase Y has its low side held ON.
  - Phase Z floats and is used for BEMF zero-cross sensing.
- **The motor current always flows through exactly 2 FET channels in series**, whether in the HS+LS(Y) or LS(X)+LS(Y) path.

## 2. Mathematical Loss Model

### 2.1 Conduction loss (dominant)

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}\ (\Rightarrow \times1.6 \text{ at } 125^\circ\text{C})
$$

Because two FETs always conduct with sync rectification:

$$
P_{cond} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

For flat-top 6-step current, I_rms ≈ I_ph, with a small ripple correction: I_rms² = I² + ΔI²/12, negligible for typical motor inductance at 48 kHz.

$$
P_{cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.92\,\text{m}\Omega = 6400 \times 3.84\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{24.6\ W/channel}
$$

**Self-consistency (thermal–electrical coupling).** Solve the fixed point

$$
T_j = T_{ref} + R_{\theta}\,P(T_j)
$$

with α > 0. This converges only if

$$
R_\theta \cdot \frac{dP}{dT_j} = R_\theta \cdot I^2 \cdot 2R_{25}\,\alpha < 1
$$

With R_θ ≈ 10 K/W per FET (Section 2.6), the loop gain is about 10 × 6400 × 2.4 mΩ × 0.006 / 3 (per-FET share), which is roughly 0.3. The loop converges, so thermal runaway is not triggered by R_DS(on) alone at 80 A. It would be if R_θ exceeded ~30 K/W.

### 2.2 Switching loss at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-phase high side hard-switches. The low side commutes softly into its body diode during dead time.

| Mechanism | Formula | Result |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f = ½ · 25.2 · 80 · 30 ns · 48 kHz | **1.45 W** |
| C_oss | Q_oss · V · f = 40 nC · 25.2 · 48 k | 0.05 W |
| Body-diode reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f = 50 nC · 25.2 · 48 k | 0.06 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_SD · I · 2t_dt · f = 0.8 · 80 · 200 ns · 48 k | **0.61 W** |
