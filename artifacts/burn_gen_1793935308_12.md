# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #18): Power, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

## 0. Feasibility Findings

These findings constrain everything that follows:

1. **"80 A burst" on a nano board (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) is a seconds-scale transient rating, not a thermal operating point.** The model in §3 shows that four channels at 80 A simultaneously dissipate about 110 W. A bare 30×30 mm board heats at about 20 K/s under that load. The realistic continuous rating under prop wash is about 15–20 A per channel.
2. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot sends DShot to the ESC MCUs and receives eRPM back. Each ESC needs its own MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, e.g. AT32F421 or STM32G071 (one per motor, or a dual-motor MCU). The 48 kHz PWM is generated there, not on the H743.
3. **Battery-side current at 4×80 A is 320 A.** That exceeds what an XT60 or any nano-scale copper can carry. The system is only viable because real flight loads are asymmetric and short.

The derivation still models the full 80 A case, because that is the design-limit envelope.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current paths in 6-step with synchronous rectification

In each 60° sector:
- **PWM phase:** the high side (HS) conducts for duty D and the low side (LS) for (1−D), so one R_ds(on) is always in the path.
- **Return phase:** the LS is fully on for the whole sector, adding a second R_ds(on).
- **Floating phase:** carries zero current.

Conduction loss per motor, independent of D:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 2.2 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(125^\circ C) = 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega \cdot [1 + 0.006 \cdot 100] = 1.92\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 2.3 At 80 A

$$P_{cond} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.92\times10^{-3} = 24.6\ \text{W per motor}$$

**Per-FET distribution.** Over one electrical cycle, each FET carries its share of the sector rotation. The average loss is about 24.6 / 6 ≈ **4.1 W per FET**. The peak instantaneous loss, during an LS-full-on sector, is 80² × 1.92 mΩ = **12.3 W**.

The electrical period is 0.1–2 ms. The FET junction-to-case time constant is about 1–10 ms, so the junction sees mostly the average loss plus about 3–8 K of ripple.

### 2.4 Electrothermal convergence

R_ds depends on T_j, which in turn depends on P. Solve iteratively:

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + R_{\theta,j\text{-}pcb}\,P_{FET}, \quad P_{FET} = \tfrac{1}{6}\,2I^2R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

With R_θ,j-pcb = 4 K/W (R_θjc ≈ 1 K/W plus about 3 K/W through vias and copper) and T_pcb = 90 °C, the loop converges to T_j ≈ 108 °C at P_FET ≈ 4.4 W including switching.

Thermal runaway occurs when the loop gain reaches 1. The criterion is:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}R_\theta = \tfrac{1}{3}I^2R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_\theta$$

At 80 A this evaluates to 0.012. Each FET is stable in isolation; the board, not the junction, is the limit.
