## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

In any 60° commutation sector, two phases carry current:

- **Switching leg:** the high-side FET conducts for duty fraction D. The low-side (synchronous) FET conducts for (1 − D).
- **Static leg:** the low-side FET conducts 100% of the sector.

So at any instant, current flows through exactly **two** R_DS(on) in series, independent of D:

$$
P_{cond,ch}(T_j) = 2\, I^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + I^2 R_{Cu}
$$

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right]
$$

### 1.2 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS(on) | 2·I²·R | Copper (R_Cu ≈ 0.4 mΩ path) | **P_cond per channel** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W | 2.6 W | **15.4 W** |
| 100 °C | 1.375 mΩ | 17.6 W | 3.0 W* | **20.6 W** |
| 125 °C | 1.50 mΩ | 19.2 W | 3.1 W* | **22.3 W** |

*Copper tempco is 0.39 %/K.

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each FET conducts for 120° of each electrical revolution. At 80 A the electrical period is well under 1 ms, which is far shorter than the die thermal time constant (roughly 10–100 ms), so averaging over a revolution is valid:

$$
P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{2 I^2 R_{DS(on)}}{6} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \frac{19.2}{6} = 3.2\ \text{W per FET at } 125\,°\text{C}
$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switching leg incurs these losses, one leg per sector. Averaged over all sectors, the loss spreads across all six FETs.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = \tfrac12 (25.2)(80)(30\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = \mathbf{1.45\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two transitions per period)

$$
P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\,\text{ns} \cdot 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\,\text{nC}\cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}
$$

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 1.5\,\text{nF}\cdot 635 \cdot 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.02\ W}
$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (two FETs switching per leg)

$$
P_{g} = 2\, Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 50\,\text{nC} \cdot 10 \cdot 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}
$$

This loss lands mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FETs.

### 2.6 Switching total

$$
P_{sw,ch} \approx 1.45 + 0.61 + 0.06 + 0.02 = \mathbf{2.14\ W \text{ per channel (FETs)}}
$$

**Sensitivity:** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.07 W per channel, at the cost of current ripple and audible noise. At 80 A, conduction dominates (about 90%), so **R_DS(on) and copper are the levers, not f_sw.**
