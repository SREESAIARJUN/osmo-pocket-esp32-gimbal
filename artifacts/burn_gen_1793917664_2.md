# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #20): Power, Thermal, and Bi-Directional DShot Model

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Feasibility Warnings

Before the math, here are the decisions and problems that shape everything below.

1. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In every production design (and in ArduPilot's model), the STM32H743 is the *flight controller*. It emits bi-directional DShot600 to four (or one quad-core) ESC MCUs running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. Typical ESC MCUs are the AT32F421 or STM32G071. The **48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs**, not the H743. This document models it that way.
2. **"Nano" plus 80 A is a burst-only rating.** A 20×20 mm mounting pattern (about 30×30 mm board) cannot dissipate the roughly 120 W generated when all four channels run 80 A. The analysis below shows a burst window of about 1.5–2 s. Treat "80 A" as a short burst rating, never a continuous one.
3. **System current is 320 A peak.** The battery lead, XT60 (60 A continuous class), pads, and pack C-rating all become limiting before the FETs do. Specify XT90 or direct-solder AWG10 if the 4×80 A case is real.
4. **Component values are representative, not vendor-locked.** Parameters are for a 40 V, ~1 mΩ, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN MOSFET class. Re-run the equations with your chosen part's datasheet values.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_DS = 1.3 × (1 + 0.005 × 85) = **1.85 mΩ**

Because loss raises temperature and temperature raises R_DS, this is a positive-feedback loop. Thermal runaway occurs when ∂P/∂T × R_th ≥ 1. Check:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T} = 2 I^2 R_{25}\alpha = 2(6400)(1.3\text{e-}3)(0.005) = 0.083 \text{ W/K}$$

The stability condition is therefore R_th,eff < 12 K/W per channel. The design below meets it, but only with good heat spreading.

### 1.2 Six-step commutation with synchronous PWM

In any 60° sector:
- The high-side FET of phase A switches at duty D.
- The low-side FET of phase A conducts for (1 − D) as the synchronous rectifier.
- The low-side FET of phase B is on 100%.

Exactly two FETs are always in the current path:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2\,(R_{DS}(T_j) + R_{cu})$$

### 1.3 Ripple correction

$$\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L f_{sw}} \;\Rightarrow\; \Delta I_{max}(D{=}0.5) = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\text{e-}6 \times 48\text{e}3} = 6.56 \text{ A}_{pp}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.6 \approx 6404 \text{ A}^2$$

The ripple contributes +0.06%, which is negligible. 48 kHz is more than sufficient for current smoothness.

### 1.4 Result per channel (80 A, T_j = 110 °C)

| Component | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| MOSFETs | 6404 × 2 × 1.85 mΩ | **23.7 W** |
| PCB copper | 6404 × 2 × 0.5 mΩ | **6.4 W** |
| **Total conduction** | | **30.1 W** |

**Key insight:** copper is about 21% of conduction loss. Use 2 oz or heavier inner power planes, 8+ vias per FET drain/source pad, and stitched bus bars. Halving R_cu saves about 3 W per channel at 80 A.
