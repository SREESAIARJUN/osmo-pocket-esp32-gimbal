# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC: Thermal, Switching, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Architecture

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Design Flags

I don't have the Job #38 BOM, stackup, or layout files, so everything below is **parametric**. Worked numbers use the representative values in §0.2. Replace them with your datasheet values before sign-off.

### 0.1 Architecture Assumed

```
Battery 6S (25.2 V max) ──► 4-in-1 ESC board (20×20, M3 nano stack)
                              ├─ ESC MCU ×1 or ×4 (AM32/BLHeli_32-class, e.g., AT32F421 / STM32G071)
                              ├─ 3-phase gate driver ×4 (FD6288-class)
                              └─ 24 × N-MOSFET (6 per motor)
FC board (20×20) ── STM32H743 @ 400 MHz, ArduPilot ── 4× bi-dir DShot600 ──► ESC MCU(s)
```

The H743 does **not** commutate the motors. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCU. The H743's job is DShot600 output plus eRPM telemetry capture.

### 0.3 Design Flags to Resolve Before Layout

1. **"80 A burst" must be defined per channel, not simultaneous.** 4 × 80 A = 320 A of battery current. That exceeds any XT60/XT90 connector and any 20×20 copper pour. Specify the rating as "80 A single-channel, ≤ X s, from T_board = Y °C."
2. **Bi-directional DShot cannot use complementary (CHxN) timer outputs.** Input capture only exists on CHx pins. Motor pins must sit on TIMx_CH1–CH4.
3. **At 48 kHz with 25 °C-class FETs on a nano footprint, the design is thermal-capacitance-limited, not R_θ-limited.** The burst duration in §3 is the real specification.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-Step Trapezoidal Commutation with Synchronous Rectification

In each 60° sector, one leg is PWM'd high-side (duty D), one leg is held low-side ON, and one leg floats.

Per electrical cycle, each leg spends 1/3 of the time as the "PWM leg" and 1/3 as the "low leg."

| FET | Conduction fraction | I_rms² |
|---|---|---|
| High side (PWM leg) | D · (1/3) | I_m² · D/3 |
| Low side, sync-rectifying (PWM leg) | (1−D) · (1/3) | I_m² · (1−D)/3 |
| Low side, low leg | 1/3 | I_m²/3 |

Summing over 3 legs:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 3\cdot\frac{I_m^2 R_{ds}(T_j)}{3}\big[D + (1-D) + 1\big] = \boxed{2\,I_m^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)}
$$

This result is **independent of D**: two FETs are always in the current path.

$$
