# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC (Job #40): Loss Model, Thermal Budget, DShot600 DMA Plan, and Reference Netlist

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Architectural Corrections

No prior specification for Job #40 was provided, so every number below rests on the stated assumptions. Swap in your real MOSFET datasheet values. The equations are written so you can re-run them directly.

**Two architectural points to settle first:**

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot cannot run four sensorless BLDC motors itself. A "4-in-1 ESC" is four separate ESC MCUs (e.g., 4× AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32), each driving its own 3-phase bridge. The H743 sends bi-directional DShot600 to them. The 48 kHz figure is the **ESC-side PWM frequency**, not an H743 parameter.
2. **"80A" on a nano form factor is a short burst, not continuous.** The thermal section shows that a 30×30 mm board reaches its limit in about 4–9 s at 4×80A. Treat 80A as a burst rating with a defined duration.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Conduction topology

In 6-step commutation, two phases carry current at any instant. With complementary PWM:

- During **ON time (D)**: HS(A) + LS(B) conduct.
- During **OFF time (1−D)**: LS(A) (synchronous freewheel) + LS(B) conduct.

So the phase current always flows through **exactly two channels**, regardless of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph,RMS}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds(on)}(T_j) \cdot k_{ripple}$$

The ripple factor accounts for inductor current ripple ΔI:

$$k_{ripple} = 1 + \frac{1}{12}\left(\frac{\Delta I}{I_{ph}}\right)^2$$

For a typical 5 µH motor at 48 kHz, ΔI/I is about 0.1–0.2, giving k ≈ 1.003. This is negligible and k = 1 is used below.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\,/\text{K}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 0.70 × 1.6 = **1.12 mΩ**.

This creates a positive-feedback (thermal runaway) loop. The stability condition is:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\, R_{\theta} < 1 \;\Rightarrow\; I^2\, 2R_{25}\,\alpha\, R_\theta < 1$$

With I = 80 A and R_θ(J→A, effective) ≈ 4 K/W: 6400 × 1.4e-3 × 0.006 × 4 = **0.215 < 1**. The loop is stable, with a margin factor of ≈1.27 on final temperature.

### 1.3 Numbers per ESC channel (80 A, T_j = 125 °C)

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 80² × 2 × 1.12 mΩ | **14.34 W** |
| PCB copper | 80² × 0.40 mΩ | **2.56 W** |
| **Conduction total** | | **16.90 W** |

Per-FET split depends on duty cycle D. The two worst-case FETs are:

- **Low-side B:** always on, so 80² × 1.12 mΩ = **7.17 W**.
- **HS A / LS A:** share the other 7.17 W in proportion D : (1−D).

The 7.17 W FET is the junction-temperature design point.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one half-bridge is PWM-switching at a time, so these losses apply per channel.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard-switched HS edge)

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g}} = \frac{10\,\text{nC}}{1.0\,\text{A}} = 10\,\text{ns (each edge)}$$

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.968\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (LS body diode recovered by HS turn-on)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \times 2 = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction (often the dominant "hidden" loss)

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I_{ph}\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.229\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

Dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET:

$$P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 45\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.043\ W}$$

### 2.6 Switching subtotal and frequency scaling

**Switching subtotal: ≈ 2.34 W per channel.**

Every switching term scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz this drops to about 1.17 W, but at the cost of audible noise and higher current ripple.

**The cheapest single improvement is dead time.** Cutting t_dt from 200 ns to 100 ns saves ≈0.6 W per channel.
