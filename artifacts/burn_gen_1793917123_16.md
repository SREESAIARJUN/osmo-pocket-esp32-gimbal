# STM32H743 Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #9): Engineering Design Package

**Scope:** 80A burst conduction and switching losses at 48 kHz, thermal transient model, ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 timer/DMA allocation, and reference netlists.

---

## 0. Architectural Ground Truth and Design Risks

Four constraints shape the rest of this document.

1. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In a 4-in-1 stack, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical parts are the STM32G071 and AT32F421. That MCU performs commutation and the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 only generates DShot600 frames and decodes the bidirectional eRPM telemetry. The loss model (§1–3) therefore applies to the ESC power stage. The DMA model (§4) applies to the H743.
2. **"80A burst" means per channel, short duration (≤5–10 s).** Four channels at 80A simultaneously would draw 320A from the pack. No nano-class battery lead, XT60/XT90 connector or bulk-capacitor bank supports that continuously. I model 80A on all four channels as the worst case, but the realistic flight envelope is lower.
3. **Form factor.** The board is assumed to be 30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz outer/inner copper, with an aluminium top spreader. A true 20×20 "nano" board cannot sink the losses computed below, and the 80A rating should not be claimed at that size.
4. **Placeholder component values.** All MOSFET and driver parameters are representative of the 40V DFN5×6 class needed for 6S. Before layout freeze, replace them with the selected vendor's datasheet values, especially R_DS(on)(T), Q_rr and Q_oss.

**Commutation model.** The model assumes six-step trapezoidal drive with complementary synchronous PWM.

- At any instant, one phase is PWM'd on the high side, with its low side synchronously rectified.
- A second phase's low side conducts continuously as the return path.
- The third phase floats.

This matches the AM32/BLHeli_32 default.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.0067\ \text{K}^{-1}\Rightarrow k_T(100^\circ\text{C})\approx1.5$$

$$R_{hot} = 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 2.2 Per-channel conduction loss

**PWM leg.** The high side conducts for duty D and the synchronous low side for (1−D), minus dead time:

$$P_{c,PWM} = I^2R_{hot}\,D + I^2R_{hot}\,(1-D) = I^2R_{hot}$$

**Return leg.** The low side conducts continuously:

$$P_{c,RET} = I^2R_{hot}$$

$$\boxed{P_{cond,ch} = 2I^2R_{hot} = 2(80)^2(1.5\times10^{-3}) = 19.2\ \text{W}}$$

**Ripple correction.** With phase inductance L ≈ 10 µH and peak-to-peak ripple ΔI = V·D(1−D)/(L·f_sw) ≈ 13A:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} \Rightarrow 6400 + 14 \approx 6414\ \text{A}^2\quad(+0.2\%,\ \text{negligible})$$

**PCB copper loss.** This term is not negligible. The phase and bus path is about 25 mm of 2 oz copper, 6 mm wide, on 4 paralleled layers:

$$R_{Cu} = \rho\,\frac{l}{w\,t\,n} = 1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot\frac{0.025}{0.006\cdot70\times10^{-6}\cdot4}\approx 0.26\ \text{m}\Omega$$

The current crosses this path twice (bus plus phase), giving roughly 0.5 mΩ:

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.5\times10^{-3} \approx 3.2\ \text{W per channel}$$
