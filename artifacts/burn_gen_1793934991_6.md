# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Nano Stack): Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Timer/DMA Architecture

## 0. Architecture Note and Assumptions

**Partitioning.** On a 4-in-1 ESC the H743 does not drive MOSFETs. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32 (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071), which generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bi-directional DShot600 to those four MCUs. The analysis below is split along that boundary.

**Datasheet values.** No specific MOSFET or driver was given, so I use a representative 40 V PDFN5×6 N-FET class. Replace the values in this table with the parts you select. Every equation stays valid; only the numbers change.

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |

| V_bus | 25.2 V | 6S fully charged (worst case for switching loss) |

| R_ds(on),25 | 1.0 mΩ @ V_gs = 10 V | Per FET, one FET per switch position |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

With 6-step trapezoidal commutation and synchronous rectification (complementary PWM), the winding current always flows through two FET positions in series:

- one high-side FET (or its sync partner during the off-time), and
- one low-side FET.

The current is essentially DC within a 60° commutation sector, so I_rms ≈ I_phase.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

### 1.3 Per-channel conduction loss

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

At an initial guess of T_j = 100 °C:

- R_ds = 1.0 × 1.375 = 1.375 mΩ
- P_cond,ch = 2 × 6400 × 1.375×10⁻³ = **17.6 W**

### 1.4 Electro-thermal coupling

R_ds rises with T_j, which raises loss, which raises T_j. The fixed-point iteration is:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_{ref} + Z_\theta \cdot P(T_j^{(k)})$$

Runaway occurs if

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\,Z_\theta = 2I^2R_{ds,25}\,\alpha\, Z_\theta \ge 1$$

At 80 A: 2 × 6400 × 1 m × 0.005 × Z_θ = 0.064 · Z_θ. This stays below 1 while the effective Z_θ < 15.6 K/W. The loop is therefore stable, but it adds about 10–15 % loss compared with a fixed-R_ds estimate (see §3.4).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per channel, one FET hard-switches per PWM cycle. Its sync partner turns on at zero voltage (ZVS) after dead time.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 1.45\text{ W}$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance loss

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.036\text{ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.048\text{ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{n}\times48\text{k} = 0.61\text{ W}$$

This term is significant. Cutting dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel.

### 2.5 Gate drive

$$P_{g} = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2\times50\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k} = 0.048\text{ W}$$

This loss lands in the gate driver, not the FETs.

### 2.6 Per-channel total

| Loss term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (T_j = 100 °C) | 17.60 |
| V–I overlap | 1.45 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.08 |
| Gate (driver) | 0.05 |
| **Total per channel** | **≈ 19.8 W** |
| **4 channels** | **≈ 79 W** |

The total is about 80 % conduction loss. Lowering R_ds(on), for example by paralleling FETs, is far more effective than reducing f_sw. Dropping f_sw from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves only about 1 W per channel.

### 2.7 PCB copper loss (often overlooked)

$$R_{trace} = \frac{\rho\, L}{w\, t}, \quad \rho_{Cu}=1.72\times10^{-8}\,\Omega\text{m}\,[1+0.0039(T-20)]$$

Example: 10 mm path, 6 mm wide, 2 oz (70 µm) copper, on 2 parallel layers:

- R ≈ 0.2 mΩ
- P = 80² × 0.2 m ≈ 1.3 W per phase path

The battery input carries up to 4 × 80 = 320 A. The pads and heavy pours there need to stay at or below 0.1 mΩ, or the input copper alone dissipates more than 10 W.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Loss spreading across FETs

Each FET carries current for 1/3 of the electrical cycle. At typical eRPM, the electrical period is short compared with the junction time constant, so the averaged per-FET dissipation applies:

- P_FET ≈ 19.8 W / 6 ≈ **3.3 W average**
- Peak sector dissipation ≈ 9.9 W for about 50–150 µs. This produces a ripple of only about 1–2 K at the junction.
