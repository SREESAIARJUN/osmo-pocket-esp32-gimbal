# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 80A ESC (Job #59): Design Model

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

**Architecture correction.** The STM32H743 should be the flight controller, not the ESC controller. Each ESC channel needs its own low-latency commutation MCU. This design uses four AT32F421 (or STM32G071) MCUs running AM32, one per motor. The H743 runs ArduPilot and drives them over bidirectional DShot600. This is the only architecture ArduPilot's bidirectional DShot and AM32 telemetry actually support.

**Feasibility warning.** "Nano" (20×20 mm mounting) combined with "80A" is only valid as a short burst rating. The math below shows the board saturates thermally within seconds. I treat 80A as a burst of 3 s or less per motor and derive the continuous rating separately.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-step synchronous PWM, per motor channel

During any commutation sector, two half-bridges carry current:
- The **PWM leg** has its high side on for duty D and its synchronous low side on for (1−D).
- The **held leg** has its low side on 100% of the sector.
- The third leg floats and is used for BEMF sensing.

Conduction loss per channel:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This is independent of D when using synchronous rectification. That is the key simplification for this model.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence (electrothermal coupling)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}\cdot R_{\theta,jc}$$

Solve iteratively (2–3 iterations converge). The thermal runaway condition is:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\,R_{\theta,ja} = 2I^2 R_{ds,25}\,\alpha\,R_{\theta,ja} < 1$$

The stability margin must be checked at continuous current.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per channel |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 2·6400·0.001 = **12.8 W** |
| 100 °C | 1.45 mΩ | **18.6 W** |
| 125 °C | 1.60 mΩ | **20.5 W** |

### 1.4 Per-FET average over an electrical cycle

Each FET is active for 120° as either the PWM device or the held device. The six FETs share the load roughly evenly, giving ≈3.1 W conduction per FET at 100 °C. Hot spots are mitigated by the rotation.

### 1.5 Copper and trace loss

Assume a 6-layer, 2 oz board with phase and bus pours of about 0.3 mΩ effective per current path:

$$P_{Cu} \approx 2\,I^2 R_{trace} = 2 \cdot 6400 \cdot 0.0003 = 3.8\text{ W per channel}$$

This is not negligible. Use solid pours, via fields of 0.3 mm vias at roughly 1 A each at burst, and short battery-pad-to-bridge paths.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Per commutation, one high-side and one low-side FET form the switching pair.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap (high side)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot30\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k} = 1.45\text{ W}$$

Edge time estimate:

$$t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_g},\quad I_g = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}{R_g + R_{drv}}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (low-side body diode)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.048\text{ W}$$

This term scales with di/dt. Q_rr at 80 A is often 2–3× the datasheet test value, so budget 0.15 W.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = E_{oss}\,f_{sw} \approx 0.4\mu\text{J}\cdot48\text{k} = 0.019\text{ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot100\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k} = 0.31\text{ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in driver and R_g, not the FET)

$$P_{g} = Q_g\,V_{gs}\,f_{sw}\cdot 2 = 40\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k}\cdot2 = 0.038\text{ W}$$
