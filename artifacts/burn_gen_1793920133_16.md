## 0. Architecture and Baseline Assumptions

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step trapezoidal drive

In any 60° commutation sector, two phases conduct:

- **Leg A (PWM leg):** the high-side conducts for D·T_sw and the low-side (synchronous) conducts for (1−D)·T_sw.
- **Leg B (return leg):** the low-side is held ON for the whole sector.

The instantaneous conduction power per ESC is:

$$P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2\left[R_{HS}D + R_{LS}(1-D)\right] + I_{rms}^2 R_{LS} + I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{pcb}$$

With matched FETs (R_HS = R_LS = R_ds), this simplifies to:

$$\boxed{P_{cond} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,\big(R_{ds}(T_j) + R_{pcb}\big)}$$

### 1.2 Ripple correction

The current ripple is:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L\,f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\,\mu H \times 48\,kHz} = 6.56\ \text{A}$$

The RMS current including ripple is:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.6 \approx 6404\ \text{A}^2$$

The ripple contribution is negligible at 80 A. It only matters at light load.

### 1.3 Temperature dependence and thermal runaway criterion

On-resistance rises with junction temperature:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha_R (T_j - 25)]$$

The loop is stable only if the loss gain times the thermal resistance stays below one:

$$\frac{\partial P_{cond}}{\partial T_j}\cdot R_{th,j\text{-}a} = 2I^2 R_{ds,25}\,\alpha_R\, R_{th,j\text{-}a} < 1$$

At 80 A: 2 × 6400 × 1e-3 × 0.0045 = **0.0576 W/K**. Stability therefore requires R_th,j-a < **17.4 K/W** per conduction pair.

- A soldered PDFN on an 8-layer, 2 oz board meets this with a large margin, at roughly 3–6 K/W effective.
- A poor solder void fraction (>30%) or a missing via array can break the margin locally.

### 1.4 Worked numbers per ESC at 80 A

| T_j | R_ds | P_FET (2·I²·R_ds) | P_pcb (2·I²·0.3 mΩ) | **P_cond** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W | 3.84 W | **16.6 W** |
| 100 °C | 1.34 mΩ | 17.1 W | 3.84 W | **21.0 W** |
| 125 °C | 1.45 mΩ | 18.6 W | 3.84 W | **22.4 W** |

*Correction to the simplified form:* 2·I²·R_pcb counts two pcb segments of 0.3 mΩ, giving 3.84 W. If your R_pcb is defined as the whole loop, use 1.92 W instead.

**Per-FET distribution:** each FET is the "full-on" low-side for 1/3 of the electrical cycle and the PWM-leg device for another 1/3. Averaged over the cycle, each FET carries about 1/6 of P_FET, roughly 3.1 W at 125 °C. Instantaneous peaks reach about 9.3 W during the full-on sector, so use Z_th(t) at the commutation period rather than R_th.
