## 0. Architecture Correction and Assumptions

**Architecture correction.** The STM32H743 should not commutate the bridges directly. ArduPilot has no on-board ESC commutation firmware, and doing so would waste the H7's timers and DMA. The design below is the standard AIO split:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot FC) ──4× bidir DShot600──► 4× ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32)
                                                  └─► 4× gate driver ─► 4× 3-phase bridge (24 FETs)
```

Each ESC MCU runs AM32 at 48 kHz PWM with complementary (synchronous-rectified) six-step commutation.

**Baseline parameters.** These are explicit assumptions. Replace them with your BOM datasheet values; every equation is kept symbolic.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

This produces a positive feedback loop: loss raises T_j, which raises R_ds, which raises loss. The loop is solved iteratively in §3.4.

### 1.2 Six-step with synchronous rectification

At any instant, two phases conduct:

- **PWM leg:** the HS FET carries current for fraction D of the period, and the LS FET (sync-rect) carries it for 1−D. Conduction loss is therefore independent of D.
- **Static leg:** the LS FET is on for the full 60° sector.

$$P_{cond,ESC} = I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{rms}^2 R_{ds} = 2\, I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Ripple correction, with motor phase inductance L_ph:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I \approx \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5" motor (L_ph ≈ 20 µH line-line, D ≈ 0.6), ΔI ≈ 6 A. The ripple term adds only ~0.05%, so it is ignored at burst.

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each of the six FETs conducts ~120° of 360°:

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{P_{cond,ESC}}{6}\cdot 2 \;\;(\text{normalised over shared conduction}) = \frac{I^2 R_{ds}}{3}$$

The commutation period at burst is ~100–300 µs, far below the die thermal time constant (~ms). The FET therefore responds to the average loss, not the 9.6 W instantaneous peak.

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.6\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{20.5\ W}$$
$$P_{FET,avg} = 80^2 \times 1.6\text{ m}\Omega / 3 = \mathbf{3.4\ W}$$

### 1.5 Copper and shunt conduction

Trace resistance:

$$R_{trace} = \frac{\rho_{Cu}(T)\, \ell}{w\, t}, \quad \rho_{Cu}(T) = 1.72\times10^{-8}[1+0.00393(T-20)]$$

Example: a 2 oz (70 µm) outer pour plus two 1 oz inner pours stitched with vias gives an effective 140 µm. Over an 8 mm wide × 10 mm path at 100 °C, R ≈ 0.16 mΩ. At 80 A in both supply and return paths, that is **~2 W per ESC**. This loss is not negligible on a nano board.

Shunt (if per-phase or DC-link): at 0.2 mΩ, P = 80² × 0.2 m = **1.3 W**. Use one DC-link shunt per ESC, or rely on R_ds sensing if current telemetry tolerance permits.
