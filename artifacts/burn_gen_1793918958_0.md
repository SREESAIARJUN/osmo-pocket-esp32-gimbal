## 0. Scope, Assumptions & Architectural Correction

**Architectural note.** An STM32H743 does not commutate the motors in a 4-in-1 ESC. The standard and recommended split is:

| Block | Device | Firmware | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100) | ArduPilot | Sends DShot600 and receives bidirectional eRPM telemetry |
| ESC MCUs (×4) | AT32F421 / STM32G071 (one per motor) | AM32 or BLHeli_32 | Commutation, 48 kHz PWM, dead-time |
| Gate drivers | 3× half-bridge per motor (e.g., FD6288Q equivalent) | — | Gate drive |
| Power stage | 6 N-ch MOSFETs per motor (24 total) | — | Inverter |

If Job #3 truly requires the H743 to commutate all four motors directly, that is a different design. It would need 24 complementary PWM outputs with dead-time. The H743 has only TIM1/TIM8 as advanced timers, so that design is not feasible for four motors. I have therefore designed to the split architecture above.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation, two phases conduct at any instant. During the PWM ON-state, current flows through the high-side FET of phase A, the motor, and the low-side FET of phase B.

During the OFF-state, with synchronous rectification, current freewheels through the low-side FET of phase A and the low-side FET of phase B.

So at every instant, **exactly two MOSFET channels carry I_ph**, independent of duty cycle D (excluding dead-time).

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\qquad \alpha = \frac{k_T - 1}{100} = 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

### 1.3 Conduction loss per motor bridge

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)\cdot\left(1 - \frac{2 t_{dt}}{T_{sw}}\right)$$

With 2·t_dt/T_sw = 2·200 ns·48 kHz = 0.0192:

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.55 W |
| 100 °C | 1.41 mΩ | 17.7 W |
| 125 °C | 1.55 mΩ | **19.46 W** |

### 1.4 Distribution across FETs

The duty cycle sets how the loss splits among the three FETs involved:

- Active high-side FET: D·I²R
- Its synchronous low-side FET: (1−D)·I²R
- Return low-side FET: I²R (worst case)

**The return low-side FET carries 100% conduction for one-third of the electrical cycle.** It is the hottest device: 80²·1.55 mΩ = **9.92 W peak instantaneous**.

### 1.5 Copper and trace loss

Assume 2 oz copper, 6 layers, with phase and bus planes in parallel on 3 layers. The effective path is about 15 mm long and 4 mm wide.

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{wt\,n} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot 0.015}{0.004\cdot 70\mu m\cdot 3} \approx 0.31\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \cdot 0.31\,\text{m}\Omega\cdot 2 \approx 4.0\ \text{W per motor}$$

Copper loss is non-trivial; route phase current on parallel internal planes.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Switching transition times (Miller plateau method)

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{plat}}{R_{g,int} + R_{g,ext} + R_{drv,src}} = \frac{12 - 4}{1.0 + 3.3 + 6.7} \approx 0.73\ \text{A}$$

$$t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = \frac{12\,\text{nC}}{0.73} \approx 16.4\ \text{ns}$$

$$I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{plat}}{R_{g,int}+R_{g,ext}+R_{drv,snk}} = \frac{4}{1.0+3.3+2.7} \approx 0.57\ \text{A}\ \Rightarrow\ t_f \approx 21\ \text{ns}$$

### 2.2 Hard-switching V·I overlap loss

Only one leg (the PWM'd high side) hard-switches per PWM period:

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2}V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot37.4\,\text{ns}\cdot48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.81\ W}$$

### 2.3 Body-diode reverse recovery

The low-side body diode conducts during dead-time and then recovers when the high side turns on:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

Qrr scales with di/dt and current. At 80 A, derate by about 2× to get **~0.10 W**.

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = 2\,V_f\, I_{ph}\, t_{dt}\, f_{sw} = 2\cdot0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

**Lever:** 100 ns of dead-time would halve this loss. Validate shoot-through margin against t_f + driver propagation skew first.

### 2.5 Output capacitance (Coss)

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot 2\ \text{(HS on + LS sync)} \approx 45\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.054\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Gate drive loss

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET channel. Two FETs toggle per period in the switching leg.

$$P_{g} = 2\,Q_gV_{drv}f_{sw} = 2\cdot60\text{n}\cdot12\cdot48\text{k} = 0.069\ \text{W}$$

### 2.7 Per-motor and system loss summary at 80 A burst (T_j = 125 °C)

| Loss component | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 19.46 W | 77.8 W |
| Copper | 4.0 W | 16.0 W |
| V·I switching | 1.81 W | 7.2 W |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 W | 4.9 W |
| Reverse recovery | 0.10 W | 0.4 W |
| Coss | 0.05 W | 0.2 W |
| Gate drive | 0.07 W | 0.3 W |
| **Total** | **26.7 W** | **106.8 W** |

**Efficiency check.** Assume D ≈ 0.85 at burst and a 25.2 V bus.

- Input power per motor ≈ V·I·D = 25.2 × 80 × 0.85 ≈ 1714 W
- η ≈ 1 − 26.7/1714 ≈ **98.4%**

**Key finding:** conduction dominates at about 73% of losses. Switching losses are only about 12% at 48 kHz. Reducing R_ds(on) (e.g., by paralleling FETs) yields far more benefit than lowering f_sw.

```

         │                           │                                 │
       C_die                     C_case                         C_board (shared)
```

### 3.2 Board thermal mass (30.5 × 30.5 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz)

| Material | Calculation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| FR-4 | 30.5 × 30.5 × 1.6 mm = 1.49 cm³; × 1.85 g/cm³ × 1.1 J/gK | 3.0 J/K |

| 24 MOSFETs + solder | — | ≈ 0.6 J/K |
| **C_board** | — | **≈ 5.0 J/K** |

### 3.3 Burst transient (adiabatic board approximation)

Board→air resistance with prop wash at about 5 m/s is roughly Rθ_ba ≈ 6 K/W. That gives a time constant τ = R·C ≈ 30 s. For bursts of a few seconds, the board is effectively adiabatic:

$$\Delta T_{board}(t) \approx \frac{P_{tot}}{C_{board}}\,t = \frac{106.8}{5.0}t = 21.4\ \text{K/s}$$
