# STM32H743 Nano AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #27): Systems Architecture

---

## 0. Architecture Corrections

Two architectural facts shape everything below.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot runs on the H743 and emits DShot. Each motor needs its own ESC MCU, such as AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The ESC MCU handles commutation, BEMF zero-cross detection and 48 kHz PWM. The H743 cannot run ArduPilot plus four commutation loops at the required latency. This design therefore uses **1× H743 + 4× AT32F421 + 4× three-phase gate drivers + 24 MOSFETs**.

2. **"80 A" on a nano board (≈30×30 mm, 20×20 mounting) is a burst rating, never a continuous one.** Section 3 shows the board can sustain roughly **20–25 A continuous per motor**. 80 A is survivable for about **1.5 s**. This must be reflected in the spec sheet and in the AM32 current limit configuration.

All device parameters below are **placeholders typical of 40 V-class 3.3×3.3 mm power MOSFETs**. Replace them with your selected datasheet values. The model is fully parametric.

## 2. Loss Model (per motor channel, 6-step trapezoidal, synchronous rectification)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

In 6-step commutation, current always flows through exactly two phases. With complementary (synchronous) PWM, the conduction path in every PWM sub-interval contains exactly **two MOSFETs**:

- **On-time:** high-side A and low-side B.
- **Off-time:** low-side A (synchronous rectifier) and low-side B.

The duty cycle therefore cancels out:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{ph,rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Include current ripple:

$$I_{ph,rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I \approx \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

**Numerical evaluation:**

- R_ds(110 °C) = 1.2 mΩ × (1 + 0.005·85) = **1.71 mΩ**
- Ripple, worst case at D = 0.5 with L_ph = 20 µH: ΔI = 6.3/0.96 = 6.6 A. Ripple adds ΔI²/12 = 3.6 A², which is negligible against 6400 A².
- P_cond = 2 × 6400 × 1.71×10⁻³ = **21.9 W**

Add parasitic copper and shunt resistance. For 1 mΩ of PCB/shunt per path: +6.4 W.

**Layout matters here.** At 80 A, every 0.1 mΩ of copper costs 0.64 W.

### 2.2 Switching losses (one hard-switched leg active at any instant)

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | ½·25.2·80·30 ns·48k = **1.45 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 30 nC·25.2·48k = **0.036 W** |
| C_oss | ½·C_oss·V²·f_sw | ½·1.5 nF·635·48k = **0.023 W** |
| Dead-time diode | V_F·I·2·t_dt·f_sw | 0.8·80·100 ns·48k = **0.307 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_drv·f_sw | 2·40 nC·10·48k = **0.038 W** (mostly in driver) |

Total switching ≈ **1.85 W**.

The overlap time t_r,f depends on gate driver current through the Miller plateau:

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g}} = \frac{Q_{gd}\,(R_{drv}+R_g+R_{g,int})}{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}$$

**Why 48 kHz matters:** switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz this term halves to about 0.9 W. At 96 kHz it rises to about 3.7 W. Conduction loss dominates at 80 A, so 48 kHz is an acceptable trade for lower motor current ripple and acoustic noise.
