**Feasibility verdict (detailed in §3):**
- 80A per channel is achievable only as a burst of a few seconds.
- Continuous rating with prop-wash cooling is about 25–35A per channel on this footprint.
- 4 × 80A at full duty implies roughly 320A of battery current. An XT60 cannot carry that, and neither can a single pair of 12 AWG leads. The burst spec must be per-motor-transient, not a simultaneous full-throttle condition, unless an XT90 or AS150 connector and 10 AWG leads are used.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in synchronous 6-step commutation

In any commutation sector, one phase is the high side and one is the low side. The PWM-switched leg alternates between its control FET and its complementary (synchronous) FET. The static leg holds its FET on. Therefore exactly two FETs are in the current path at every instant (ignoring dead-time).

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot \left( R_{ds,HS}(T_j) + R_{ds,LS}(T_j) \right) = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature coefficient of R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C, the factor is 1.6.

| Option | R_ds,25 | R_ds,125 |
|---|---|---|
| A (5×6) | 1.0 mΩ | 1.6 mΩ |
| B (3.3×3.3) | 1.8 mΩ | 2.88 mΩ |

### 1.3 Ripple contribution

The phase-to-phase motor inductance is L ≈ 20 µH (typical 2207–2810 class). Worst case is D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L\,f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\times10^{-6}\times 48\times10^{3}} = 6.56\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{dc}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.59 = 6403.6\ \text{A}^2$$

The ripple adds less than 0.06%, so it can be neglected at 48 kHz. That is one of the benefits of the higher PWM frequency.
