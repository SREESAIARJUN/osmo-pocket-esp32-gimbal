## 0. Baseline Assumptions and Sanity Flags

| Parameter | Assumed value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus,max = 25.2 V | Design for 30 V transients |
| Form factor | "Nano" = 20×20 mm mounting (≈25×25 mm board) | |
| MOSFET | 40 V, PQFN 3.3×3.3 class, R_DS(on),25 = 1.0 mΩ @ V_GS = 10 V | e.g., BSZ010N04LS6-class; verify against the datasheet |
| Gate driver | FD6288Q-class 3-phase bootstrap driver, ~1.2 A source / 1.5 A sink | |
| ESC MCU | Dedicated per-channel MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32 (AT32F421 / STM32G071) | See Flag A |
| PWM | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous rectification), trapezoidal 6-step | |
| Burst | 80 A phase current per channel | |

**Flag A: architecture.** The H743 does not commutate the motors. In every production AIO design, the H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs (or one quad-channel MCU), and those MCUs generate the 48 kHz PWM. Two consequences follow:
- The 48 kHz switching frequency is an ESC firmware parameter (e.g., AM32 `PWM_FREQUENCY`), not an ArduPilot one.
- The math below covers the power stage. Section 4 covers the H743 timer and DMA plan.

**Flag B: 80 A on a nano board is not physically sustainable.** The math below shows that 80 A per channel produces roughly 88 W of loss across the board. At full throttle it also draws up to about 320 A of battery current, which is roughly 5× an XT60 rating and beyond what 20×20 copper can carry. Treat 80 A as a sub-second burst rating with a thermal-foldback requirement, or re-rate the product to about 35–45 A continuous / 55–60 A burst, which matches what the nano class actually achieves.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous PWM

During each 60° commutation sector, one phase is PWM'd high (phase A), one is held low (phase B), and one floats (phase C). With complementary PWM at duty D:

- **HS_A** conducts during D·T_sw.
- **LS_A** conducts during (1−D)·T_sw, as synchronous freewheeling.
- **LS_B** conducts 100% of the sector as the return path.

Phase current RMS includes ripple:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5" motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH line-to-line), the worst case at D = 0.5 and V_bemf ≈ V_bus·D gives:

ΔI ≈ (25.2 − 12.6)·0.5 / (15 µH · 48 kHz) ≈ 8.75 A

The ripple term is therefore ΔI²/12 ≈ 6.4 A², which is negligible against 6400 A².

### 1.2 Sector conduction loss (per channel)

$$P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{rms}^2 R_{DS}(T_j) = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

This total is independent of D because synchronous rectification removes the diode term outside dead time.

### 1.3 Temperature dependence (self-consistent)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}\cdot R_{\theta,jc}$$

These two equations are coupled, so solve them by fixed-point iteration. Thermal runaway occurs when ∂P/∂T_j · R_θ ≥ 1:

$$\text{Stable iff}\quad I^2 R_{DS,25}\,\alpha\,R_{\theta,j\text{-}amb} < 1$$

### 1.4 Numerical result at 80 A (T_pcb = 95 °C)

Iteration for the worst-case FET, LS_B, which carries 100% of the sector:

| Iteration | T_j (°C) | R_DS (mΩ) | P_LS_B (W) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 95 | 1.385 | 8.86 |
| 1 | 95 + 8.86·1.4 = 107.4 | 1.453 | 9.30 |
| 2 | 108.0 | 1.457 | 9.32 |

The worst-case FET converges at **T_j ≈ 108 °C (R_θjc = 1.4 K/W)**.

Per-channel conduction loss: P_cond ≈ 2 · 6400 · 1.45 mΩ ≈ **18.6 W**
