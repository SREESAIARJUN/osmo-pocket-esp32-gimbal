## Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and Bi-directional DShot600 Timer/DMA Architecture

## 0. Architectural Clarification and Assumptions

**Partitioning.** The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. A 4-in-1 ESC uses its own controllers (AM32 or BLHeli_32) for six-step commutation, PWM, and back-EMF sensing. The architecture therefore splits into two parts:

| Domain | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| FC | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100), 480 MHz | ArduPilot, sensor fusion, bidirectional DShot600 master |
| ESC (×4) | AT32F421K8U7 (QFN32) running AM32 | 6-step commutation, 48 kHz PWM, eRPM telemetry |
| Gate drive (×4) | FD6288Q, 3-phase half-bridge driver | 6 gate outputs per motor |
| Power (×24) | 40 V N-MOSFET in PDFN3.3×3.3 | 6 per motor |

**Design assumptions.** All numbers below follow from these. Replace them with your selected MOSFET datasheet values before sign-off.

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 25.2 V | 6S fully charged (worst case for switching loss) |
| I_ph | 80 A | Burst *phase* current per motor (not battery current) |
| f_sw | 48 kHz | ESC PWM frequency |
| R_ds(on),25 | 1.0 mΩ | MOSFET at V_gs = 10 V |
| k_T | 1.5 | R_ds(on) multiplier at T_j ≈ 110 °C |
| t_r, t_f | 10 ns each | Effective V-I overlap times with FD6288 (~1.5 A source / 1.8 A sink) |
| Q_rr | 40 nC | Body-diode reverse recovery charge |
| Q_oss | 30 nC | Output charge at V_bus |
| Q_g | 50 nC | Total gate charge at 10 V |
| V_SD | 0.8 V | Body-diode forward voltage at 80 A |
| t_dt | 100 ns | Dead time per edge |
| Board | 20×20 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz outer / 1 oz inner | Nano form factor |

> ⚠️ **Feasibility flag (read first):** 80 A per motor on a 20×20 mm board is only achievable as a short burst. Section 3 shows that the steady-state rating is roughly 15–25 A per motor. The 80 A figure must be enforced as a *time-limited* rating, both in firmware and in the product spec.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous commutation

In each commutation sector, one phase is the PWM high-side (HS), one is held low (LS sink), and the third floats for back-EMF sensing. AM32 uses complementary (synchronous) PWM, so the conduction pattern over one PWM period is:

| Device | Conducts for | Current |
|---|---|---|
| HS of PWM phase | D·T | I_ph |
| LS of PWM phase (synchronous rectifier) | (1−D)·T − 2t_dt | I_ph |
| LS of sink phase | T | I_ph |

### 1.2 Conduction loss derivation

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0059/\text{K} \Rightarrow k_T(110^\circ C)\approx 1.5
$$

$$
P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,\big[D + (1-D) + 1\big] = 2\,I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

This result is **independent of duty cycle** under synchronous rectification. It is a useful property: the loss is the same at any throttle for a given phase current.

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ motor}
$$

### 1.3 Copper (PCB) conduction loss

Phase and bus copper path: about 15 mm total of 2 oz copper, 4 mm wide, with outer plus inner layers in parallel. The effective resistance is R_Cu ≈ 0.25 mΩ, assuming a 70 µm-equivalent stack with vias stitched and ρ_Cu at 100 °C.

$$
P_{Cu} \approx I_{ph}^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.25\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{1.6\ W\ per\ motor}
$$

### 1.4 Worst-case single-device dissipation

The sink-phase LS device conducts 100 % of the time:

$$
P_{LS,sink} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 6400 \times 1.5\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{9.6\ W}
$$

This device sets the T_j limit.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one half-bridge switches per sector. Each loss term is derived per PWM period, then multiplied by f_sw.

### 2.1 V-I overlap (hard switching, inductive load)

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(20\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz}) = \mathbf{0.97\ W}
$$

The overlap times come from the driver current and the Miller plateau:

$$
t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gd} R_{G,tot}}{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}
$$

With Q_gd ≈ 10 nC, R_G,tot ≈ 4.7 Ω (ext) + 1 Ω (int) + driver, and (12 − 4.5) V, this gives ≈ 8–10 ns. That is consistent with the assumed values.

### 2.2 Body-diode reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{ nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{ nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}
$$

The full Q·V form is used rather than ½Q·V: the switching device dissipates its own E_oss and also charges the complementary device's C_oss.

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD} I_{ph}\,(2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{ ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

This is the dominant secondary loss. Reducing dead time to 50 ns halves it, but the shoot-through margin must then be verified on a scope.

### 2.5 Gate drive

$$
P_{gate} = 2\,Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2\times50\text{ nC}\times12\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.058\ W}
$$

Most of this dissipates in the FD6288 and the gate resistors, not in the MOSFETs.

### 2.6 Loss budget per motor at 80 A burst

| Term | W | % |
|---|---|---|
| MOSFET conduction | 19.20 | 85.8 |
| PCB copper | 1.60 | 7.1 |
| V-I switching | 0.97 | 4.3 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 | 2.7 |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.14 | 0.6 |
| **Total per motor** | **22.5 W** | |
| **4 motors simultaneous** | **≈ 90 W** | |

**Conclusion:** at 80 A the system is conduction-dominated. Raising f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz adds only about 0.9 W per motor. The 48 kHz choice is justified because it gives lower current ripple and quieter motors. The engineering lever is R_ds(on), either through paralleled devices or lower-R parts, not switching speed.

### 2.7 Efficiency at burst

Motor-side power is about 80 A × 25.2 V × D. At full throttle that is roughly 2 kW per motor, so the ESC efficiency is about 1 − 22.5/2000 ≈ **98.9 %**. The loss is small relative to power
