### Architecture Assumption

The H743 is the flight controller only. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU, for example an AT32F421 running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM.

The H743 sends DShot600 to the ESC MCUs and receives bidirectional eRPM telemetry back.

### Feasibility Flag

A true 20×20 mm "nano" board cannot physically host 24 PDFN3.3 FETs plus drivers. It also cannot sink the burst losses calculated below.

The math is valid as written. However, 80 A burst realistically requires a 30.5×30.5 mm ESC stacked under a 20×20 FC, or a 30×30 single board. I recommend resolving this before layout.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Path (Six-Step Trapezoidal, Synchronous Rectification)

In any commutation sector, one phase is PWM-switched and one phase is held low. The current path therefore always contains exactly two channels in series:

- **PWM-on interval (D):** HS(A) → motor → LS(B)
- **PWM-off interval (1−D):** LS(A) (synchronous) → motor → LS(B)

This gives the instantaneous conduction loss per motor:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j) \;+\; I_{ph}^2 R_{Cu}$$

The temperature-dependent on-resistance is:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\big[1+\alpha (T_j-25)\big],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\,/\mathrm{K} \Rightarrow k(125^\circ C)\approx1.5$$

### 1.2 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

**FET conduction:**
- P_cond,FET = 2 · 80² · 1.5 mΩ = **19.2 W per motor**

**Copper and solder path:**
- Assumed R_Cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ for pad + via + 2 oz plane + phase pad.
- P_Cu = 6400 · 0.3 mΩ = **1.9 W per motor**

### 1.3 Per-FET Distribution Over an Electrical Cycle

Each switch position conducts for 1/3 of the electrical period. A low-side FET carries current both as the "held-low" device and as the synchronous rectifier, so the low side is the hottest device.

**Low-side FET (worst case), at D → 1 during a punch-out burst:**

$$P_{LS} \approx \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R + \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D) I^2 R$$

**High-side FET:**

$$P_{HS} \approx \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I^2 R$$

**Instantaneous peak in one FET while conducting:**
- 80² × 1.5 mΩ = **9.6 W**

This instantaneous value is what drives the junction-to-case rise in Section 3.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches.

### 2.1 Loss Terms

| Term | Formula | Value @ 25.2 V, 80 A, 48 kHz |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw | ½ · 25.2 · 80 · 40 ns · 48k = **1.94 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_F · I · 2t_dt · f_sw | 0.8 · 80 · 200 ns · 48k = **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw | 30 nC · 25.2 · 48k = **0.04 W** |
| C_oss hard-switch | Q_oss · V · f_sw | 40 nC · 25.2 · 48k = **0.05 W** |
| Gate charge (mostly in driver) | 2 · Q_g · V_GS · f_sw | 2 · 50 nC · 10 · 48k = **0.05 W** |
| **Total P_sw per motor** | | **≈ 2.7 W** |

### 2.2 Per-Motor and Board Totals at Burst

**Per motor:**
- P_motor ≈ 19.2 (FET) + 1.9 (Cu) + 2.7 (switching) ≈ **23.8 W**

**All four motors simultaneously at 80 A:**
- P_board ≈ **95 W**, plus ≈1–2 W from drivers, LDOs, and MCUs.

### 2.3 Switching-Frequency Sensitivity

The switching terms scale linearly with frequency:
- **24 kHz:** saves ≈1.3 W per motor.
- **96 kHz:** costs ≈2.7 W more per motor, and also degrades current ripple trade-offs differently.

At 48 kHz, conduction dominates by roughly 7:1. R_DS(on) and copper are therefore the levers that matter, not f_sw.

## 3. Thermal Model (Burst)

### 3.1 Two-Node Transient Model

$$T_j(t) = T_a + \Delta T_{board}(t) + P_{FET,pk}\, Z_{\theta JC}(t)$$

The board heats approximately adiabatically during a burst, because convective loss is small over a few seconds:

$$\Delta T_{board}(t) \approx \frac{P_{board}\, t}{C_{th}}, \qquad \text{or with cooling: } \Delta T = P R_{\theta BA}\left(1-e^{-t/\tau}\right),\ \tau = R_{\theta BA} C_{th}$$
