## 0. Critical Findings First

1. **Architecture.** The H743 does not commutate the motors directly. In every production 4-in-1 design, each ESC channel has its own MCU (AT32F421, STM32G071, or similar running AM32 or BLHeli_32). The H743 sends DShot600 to those MCUs and receives bidirectional eRPM back. This document models it that way.

2. **"80A burst" on a nano form factor is thermally marginal to infeasible** if it means 80A per channel on all four channels at once. The worked numbers in §1 and §3 show that 4×80A on a 20×20 mm board with 3×3 mm FETs is about 240 W of dissipation. The FET junctions reach their limit in roughly 100–300 ms. Defensible interpretations are:
   - 80A burst on a single channel, 1–2 s, with 5×6 mm FETs; or
   - an 80A total battery-current burst; or
   - a 30.5×30.5 mm board rather than 20×20.

   **Lock down this spec before layout.**

3. **The DC-link capacitor ripple current (§2.4) is the hidden killer.** At 80A it is about 40A RMS per channel.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Assumptions

| Parameter | Symbol | Value A (3×3 DFN, nano-realistic) | Value B (5×6 DFN, 30.5 mm board) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S, full) | V_bus | 25.2 V | 25.2 V |
| FET V_DS rating | – | 40 V | 40 V |
| R_DS(on) @ 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | R₂₅ | 3.0 mΩ | 1.0 mΩ |
| Temperature coefficient | α | 0.0045 /K | 0.0045 /K |
| Burst phase current | I_ph | 80 A | 80 A |
| PCB trace + solder per current path | R_pcb | 0.5 mΩ | 0.3 mΩ |

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C the multiplier is 1.45:
- Case A: 4.35 mΩ
- Case B: 1.45 mΩ

### 1.3 Six-step trapezoidal with synchronous rectification

In any commutation sector, exactly two phases conduct:

- **PWM leg:** current flows through the high-side FET for duty D and through the low-side FET (sync rectification) for 1−D. The total conduction time on that leg is always 100%.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET is fully on.

So conduction loss is independent of duty. This is the key simplification:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2\,\big(2R_{DS}(T_j) + R_{pcb}\big)$$

The per-FET peak, relevant for the junction calculation, is the return-leg low-side FET:

$$P_{FET,max} = I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 1.4 Worked numbers at 80A, T_j = 125 °C

| | Case A | Case B |
|---|---|---|
| P_cond per channel | 6400 × (8.7 + 0.5) mΩ = **58.9 W** | 6400 × (2.9 + 0.3) mΩ = **20.5 W** |
| P per hottest FET | 6400 × 4.35 mΩ = **27.8 W** | **9.3 W** |
| 4 channels simultaneous | **235 W** | **82 W** |

### 1.5 Averaging over commutation

Each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical cycle (120° block). The time-averaged loss per FET is therefore:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6}\quad\text{(6 FETs share it)}$$

However, the electrical frequency at high eRPM is much faster than the junction thermal time constant (≈ms). Example: 2-pole-pair-equivalent at 30k eRPM gives 500 Hz electrical. Use the averaged value for junction temperature at high speed. Use P_FET,max for stall or low-speed bursts, such as desync or prop strike.
