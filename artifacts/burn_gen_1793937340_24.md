## 0. Architectural Clarifications and Top-Level Findings

### 0.1 Architecture assumed

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors directly. ArduPilot does not run ESC firmware. The board is therefore partitioned as follows:

```
            ┌──────────────────────── STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ───────────────────────┐
 DShot600 ──┤ TIMx_CHy (bidir, DMA)  ×4                                            │
            └──────┬──────────────┬──────────────┬──────────────┬──────────────────┘
                   │              │              │              │   (single-wire, bidirectional)
             ┌─────▼────┐   ┌─────▼────┐   ┌─────▼────┐   ┌─────▼────┐
             │ ESC MCU1 │   │ ESC MCU2 │   │ ESC MCU3 │   │ ESC MCU4 │  AT32F421 / STM32G071 (AM32)
             │ 48k PWM  │   │          │   │          │   │          │  or EFM8BB51 (Bluejay)
             └─────┬────┘   └─────┬────┘   └─────┬────┘   └─────┬────┘
              3-ph driver    3-ph driver    3-ph driver    3-ph driver   (e.g. FD6288Q-class)
              6× N-MOSFET    6× N-MOSFET    6× N-MOSFET    6× N-MOSFET
```

### 0.2 Key findings (read first)

| # | Finding | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | At 80 A phase current, each ESC channel dissipates about **16 W**. All four channels together dissipate about **64 W**. | — |
| F2 | On a 20×20 mm "nano" PCB, the board-to-air thermal resistance is about 10–15 K/W, even in propwash. **Continuous 80 A is physically impossible.** The burst duration is limited by thermal mass to about **3–5 s**, starting from a warm board. | **Critical** |
| F3 | The H743 sits on the same copper as about 64 W of heat. Specify the **temperature-range-6 / industrial (Tj 125 °C)** variant, and isolate the MCU thermally (Section 3.6). | High |
| F4 | Input-capacitor ripple reaches about 40 A RMS per channel at 50 % duty. This drives bulk-cap count, and it is frequently the real failure mode rather than the FETs. | High |
| F5 | On the H7, DMAMUX makes bi-directional DShot allocation flexible. Even so, **stream count (16 + BDMA) is the binding constraint**, not timer channel mapping. | Medium |

**Recommendation:** Either rate the board "80 A burst ≤ 3 s / 45 A continuous on 30.5×30.5 mm", or move to a 30.5 mm form factor with an 8-layer, 2 oz-or-heavier copper stack. Details follow.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

In 6-step commutation, exactly two phase legs conduct during each 60° electrical interval:

- **PWM leg:** the high side conducts for D·T, and the low-side synchronous rectifier conducts for (1−D)·T. The motor current is continuous because L_motor·di/dt ≫ the PWM period.
- **Return leg:** the low side is on for 100 % of the interval.

So at any instant, exactly two channels of R_DS(on) carry I_ph:

$$
P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 R_{DS,hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{ph}^2 R_{DS,hot} = 2\,I_{ph}^2 R_{DS,hot}
$$

This holds independent of D, provided dead time is negligible relative to T.

**Temperature dependence** (linearised around the datasheet normalised curve):

$$
R_{DS,hot} = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j - 25)],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \mathrm{K^{-1}}
$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS,hot = 0.75 × 1.5 = **1.125 mΩ**.

**Copper path resistance.** This is not negligible at 80 A. With 2 oz copper on an inner and outer pair, the sheet resistance is about 0.25 mΩ/□ per layer, and 4 paralleled layers give about 0.0625 mΩ/□. The battery-pad → FET → phase-pad path is about 4 squares out and 4 back, so:

$$
R_{Cu} \approx 8 \times 0.0625 \approx 0.5\ \mathrm{m\Omega}\ \text{(at 25 °C)};\quad \alpha_{Cu}=0.0039 \Rightarrow 0.62\ \mathrm{m\Omega}\ \text{at 55 °C}
$$

**Worked numbers at I_ph = 80 A:**

| Term | Expression | Result |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 2 × 80² × 1.125 mΩ | **14.4 W** |
| Copper conduction | 80² × 0.62 mΩ | **4.0 W** |
| Per-FET instantaneous (one conducting device) | 80² × 1.125 mΩ | 7.2 W |
| Per-FET average | Each device conducts about 1/3 of the electrical cycle (HS 120° and LS 120° per leg, shared) | ≈ 4.8 W |

Copper loss is spread across the board, so it is not junction heat. It still counts toward the board thermal budget.
