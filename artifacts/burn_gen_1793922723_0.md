# STM32H743 + 4-in-1 ESC Nano FC: Power-Stage Thermal Model and Bidirectional DShot600 Allocation

## 0. Assumptions and Architecture Notes

No BOM or stackup was supplied, so every equation below is parametric. Numbers are worked for a **reference design**. Replace the parameters with your datasheet values before trusting the results.

**Architectural point.** ArduPilot does not commutate BLDC motors. In any ArduPilot "FC + 4-in-1" board:

- The **STM32H743** runs ArduPilot and emits 4× bidirectional DShot600.
- **Four ESC MCUs** run AM32 or BLHeli_32 and drive the 24 MOSFETs at 48 kHz. Typical choices are the AT32F421 or STM32G071, or one EFM8/F051 per motor.

Section 3 therefore covers the H743 timers and DMA. Sections 1–2 cover the ESC power stage.

**Reference parameters**

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 25.2 V | 6S full |
| I_pk | 80 A | per-motor burst phase current |
| f_sw | 48 kHz | ESC PWM |
| R_ds,on(25°C) | 0.70 mΩ | 30–40 V, 5×6 or 3.3×3.3 FET class |
| α | 0.005 /K | R_ds temperature coefficient |
| t_r, t_f | 10 ns each | with ~2 A gate driver |
| Q_g | 50 nC @ 10 V | total gate charge |
| Q_rr | 40 nC | body-diode reverse-recovery charge |
| Q_oss | 30 nC | output charge |
| V_f | 0.8 V | body-diode forward voltage |
| t_dt | 100 ns | dead time per edge |
| R_θJC | 1.2 K/W | junction-to-case |
| Topology | 6-step trapezoidal | complementary (synchronous) PWM |

> **Reality check.** 80 A per motor is 320 A aggregate and roughly 60 W of dissipation. A true "nano" 20×20 mm board cannot carry this, even as a burst. The numbers below assume a **30.5×30.5 mm (≈36×36 mm) board, 8-layer, 2 oz**. Even then, 80 A is a **seconds-scale burst rating**, not continuous. See §2.3.

## 1. Loss Model (per motor, per 6-step sector)

In 6-step operation, two legs conduct at any time:

- **Leg A** is the PWM leg. Current alternates between the high-side FET (duty D) and the low-side FET (synchronous rectification, 1 − D).
- **Leg B** is the return leg. Its low-side FET is on for the whole sector.

### 1.1 Conduction loss

R_ds,on(T_j) = R_ds,on,25 · [1 + α(T_j − 25)]

Per-leg conduction losses:

- P_cond,A = I²R_ds·D + I²R_ds·(1 − D) = I²·R_ds(T_j)
- P_cond,B = I²·R_ds(T_j)

Total:

**P_cond = 2 · I_rms² · R_ds,on(T_j)**

Including PWM current ripple:

I_rms² = I_avg² + ΔI²/12, with ΔI = V_bus·D(1 − D) / (L_phase · f_sw)

For L = 20 µH and D = 0.5:

- ΔI = 6.6 A
- ΔI²/12 = 3.6 A², which is negligible against 6400 A²

At T_j = 125 °C, R_ds = 0.70 × 1.5 = **1.05 mΩ**:

P_cond = 2 × 80² × 1.05e-3 = **13.44 W per motor**

### 1.2 Switching loss (hard-switched PWM leg only)

| Loss term | Equation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | P_sw = ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 20e-9 × 48e3 | **0.968 W** |
| Reverse recovery | P_rr = Q_rr·V·f_sw = 40e-9 × 25.2 × 48e3 | **0.048 W** |
| Output capacitance | P_oss ≈ ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw | **0.018 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | P_gate = 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw | **0.048 W** (lands in driver/MCU LDO) |
| Dead-time diode | P_dt = 2·V_f·I·t_dt·f_sw = 2 × 0.8 × 80 × 100e-9 × 48e3 | **0.614 W** |

Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. Doubling to 96 kHz adds about 1.7 W per motor.

### 1.3 Copper / shunt loss

- Copper: P_Cu = I²·R_trace. At 0.15 mΩ effective per motor path, this gives **0.96 W**.
- Shunt: if a 0.25 mΩ low-side shunt is used, it adds 80² × 0.25e-3 = **1.6 W** while it conducts.

### 1.4 Totals at 80 A burst

| | Per motor | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 13.44 W | 53.8 W |
| Switching + RR + Coss | 1.03 W | 4.1 W |
| Dead-time | 0.61 W | 2.5 W |
| Copper (no shunt) | 0.96 W | 3.8 W |
| **Total** | **≈16.0 W** | **≈64 W** |

**Conclusion: conduction loss dominates (~84%).** Lowering R_ds,on or paralleling FETs is worth far more than chasing switching speed.

### 1.5 Thermal-runaway criterion (conduction-dominated)

The positive temperature coefficient makes temperature and loss feed each other:

T_j = T_a + R_θ · 2I²·R_25·[1 + α(T_j − 25)]

Solving for T_j:

T_j = [T_a + R_θ·2I²R_25·(1 − 25α)] / (1 − R_θ·2I²R_25·α)

**The design is stable only if R_θ · 2I²·R_25 · α < 1.**

At 80 A: 2I²·R_25·α = 2 × 6400 × 0.7e-3 × 0.005 = 0.0448 W/K.

So **steady-state runaway occurs at R_θ ≥ 22 K/W** per motor-pair path. You are safe from runaway, but not from overtemperature (§2).

## 2. Thermal Model

### 2.1 Network (Cauer-style, lumped)

```
P_FET ──[R_θJC 1.2]──[R_θ,via+Cu ≈4.5 K/W per FET]──┐
                                                     ├── T_board (C_board) ──[R_θ,BA]── T_ambient
P_FET(x24), P_Cu ───────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 2.2 Board heat capacity (36×36 mm, 1.6 mm, 8L × 2 oz)

| Component | Calculation | Heat capacity |
|---|---|---|
| FR-4 | 2.07 cm³ × 1.85 g/cm³ × 1.1 J/gK | 4.2 J/K |
| Copper (80% fill) | 0.58 cm³ × 8.96 × 0.385 | 2.0 J/K |
| FETs, caps, solder | — | ≈1.0 J/K |
| **C_board** | | **≈7.2 J/K** |

R_θ,BA (board-to-ambient):

- Prop-wash airflow (3–6 m/s): about **2.5 K/W**
- Still air: about **10 K/W**

### 2.3 Burst response

**Board temperature.** With time constant τ = R_θ,BA · C_board = 2.5 × 7.2 = **18 s**:

T_board(t) = T_0 + P_tot·R_θ,BA·(1 − e^(−t/τ))

**Junction rise above board.** The hot FET carries about 1/3 of the per-motor loss averaged over sectors. This is ~5.3 W peak-sector FET loss, ~2.7 W average per active FET.
