## 0. Architecture Corrections and Feasibility Flags

Three issues must be resolved before layout.

### 0.1 The H743 should not commutate the motors

The H743 is the flight controller. It outputs bidirectional DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry.

Each motor bridge should run on a dedicated ESC MCU, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. That MCU owns the 48 kHz PWM, dead-time insertion, and BEMF zero-cross detection.

Running 12 half-bridges plus flight control on one H743 is possible in principle but unsupported by ArduPilot. It also places a 48 kHz commutation loop alongside the EKF scheduler. The analysis below therefore assumes 1× H743 plus 4× ESC MCUs.

### 0.2 "80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is not thermally credible as stated

| Configuration | Total current | Dissipation (§2) | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 motors × 80 A simultaneously | 320 A | ~80 W | Exceeds the XT60 and pad rating by about 5×. Not credible. |
| 80 A per motor, short and asymmetric (yaw/punch transient) | Lower | Lower | Physically defensible for ≤ 1–2 s |

- **Recommendation:** Specify per-channel 80 A for ≤ 1 s with a 10% duty cycle, and a total battery current limit of ≤ 120 A burst.
- **Alternative:** Move to a 30.5×30.5 mm form factor.

### 0.3 The bus voltage must be fixed

The analysis assumes 6S: Vbus,max = 25.2 V, nominal 22.2 V. The MOSFET rating must be ≥ 40 V because ringing on 6S routinely reaches 32–35 V.

## 1. Power Stage Topology and Assumed Device Parameters

Each motor uses three half-bridges (6 FETs), for 24 N-channel FETs total in PQFN 3.3×3.3 mm packages.

| Total gate charge | Qg | 45 nC |
| Gate–drain (Miller) charge | Qgd | 8 nC |

| Gate driver | — | 10 V, 1.5 A source / 2.5 A sink, Rg,ext = 2.2 Ω |
| Dead time | t_dt | 100 ns per edge |
| Switching frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |

### Commutation model

The model uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification:

- In each 60° sector, one high-side (HS) FET is PWM-switched at duty D.
- Its complementary low-side (LS) FET conducts for (1−D).
- The opposite leg's LS is held fully on.
- Each FET therefore conducts for 120° of 360°, or 1/3 of the electrical period.

## 2. Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At Tj = 125 °C: R = 0.90 × 1.45 = **1.31 mΩ**. Using the hot value is mandatory because the model is solved iteratively (§3.4).

### 2.2 Conduction loss

With PWM duty D in a sector, the current path is (HS or HS-complement LS) plus the return LS. At any instant, two FETs are in series, so the instantaneous bridge conduction loss is:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This holds for any D, because synchronous rectification gives D·R + (1−D)·R = R.

The phase-current ripple correction factor at 48 kHz with typical 10–20 µH drone motor inductance is:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2\left(1 + \frac{1}{12}\left(\frac{\Delta I}{I}\right)^2\right),\qquad \Delta I = \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L f_{sw}}$$

Worst case at D = 0.5 and L = 12 µH: ΔI = 25.2·0.25 / (12e-6·48e3) = 10.9 A. At 80 A the correction is 1.0015, which is negligible.

At I = 80 A and Tj = 125 °C:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.31\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{16.8\ W}$$

The worst single FET is the return LS, which is fully on for 120°:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{I^2 R_{ds}}{3} = \frac{6400 \times 1.31\text{m}}{3} = \mathbf{2.79\ W}$$

### 2.3 Switching loss (hard-switched HS edge)

The edge times come from the Miller plateau. With Vplateau ≈ 4.5 V, Rg,total = 2.2 + 1.0 (internal) + driver output ≈ 5 Ω:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd} R_g}{V_{drv}-V_{pl}} = \frac{8\text{n}\cdot 5}{5.5} = 7.3\ \text{ns},\qquad t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}R_g}{V_{pl}} = 8.9\ \text{ns}$$

Adding current-rise time (from Qgs2) and layout inductance, the practical total is t_r + t_f ≈ 35 ns.

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 35\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.69\ W}$$

### 2.4 Capacitive, reverse-recovery, dead-time and gate losses (per bridge)

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Coss | Q_oss · V_bus · f_sw | 50n·25.2·48k = 0.060 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw | 40n·25.2·48k = 0.048 W |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_sd · I · 2t_dt · f_sw | 0.85·80·200n·48k = 0.653 W |
| Gate drive (2 switching FETs, dissipated mostly in the driver) | 2 · Q_g · V_drv · f_sw | 2·45n·10·48k = 0.043 W |

### 2.5 Copper (PCB) losses, which are often ignored and must not be

Assume each phase/bus path is 2 oz (70 µm) copper, 6 mm wide, 15 mm long, on 2 layers in parallel:

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{w t}\cdot\frac12 = \frac{1.72\text{e-}8 \cdot 0.015}{0.006 \cdot 70\text{e-}6}\cdot\frac12 = 0.31\ \text{m}\Omega$$

With heating to 80 °C, R_Cu ≈ 0.38 mΩ. The current flows through the bus-in path and the two phase paths:

$$P_{Cu} \approx 80^2 \times (0.38\text{m} \times 2) = \mathbf{4.9\ W}$$

This is comparable to switching plus dead-time losses combined. Use 4–6 layers of 2 oz copper on a nano board.
