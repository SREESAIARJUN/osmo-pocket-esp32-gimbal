# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #44): Design Package Rev A

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Flags

Two points need to be settled before the math.

**1. The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production AIO/4-in-1 design, and in ArduPilot's model, the H743 is the flight controller. It sends DShot to four dedicated ESC MCUs (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32). Those MCUs generate the 48 kHz complementary PWM and do the commutation. The H743 cannot do sensorless 6-step commutation for 4 motors at 48 kHz while also running EKF3. This package assumes:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──DShot600 bidir (4 lines)──► 4× AT32F421 (AM32) ──► 4× FD6288Q gate driver ──► 24× N-MOSFET
```

**2. 80 A burst per channel on a "nano" board is thermally marginal to infeasible.** The calculations in §3 show about 16 W per channel at 80 A, so about 64 W if all four channels burst together. On a 20×20 mm board this is not survivable beyond about 1–2 s. On a 30.5×30.5 mm board it is survivable for about 5 s only with airflow and 5×6 mm MOSFETs.

**Recommendation:** rate the product **80 A / 5 s per channel, not simultaneous; 45 A continuous**, on a 30.5×30.5 mm board. Alternatively, split into a stacked FC + ESC. I flag this now because marketing an "80A nano AIO" is a field-failure and returns risk.

**Component values below are design assumptions.** They are representative of 40 V, 5×6 PDFN trench MOSFETs. Substitute the actual datasheet numbers for your selected part; all formulas are parametric.

## 2. Loss Model: Single ESC Channel, 6-Step Trapezoidal, Complementary PWM

At any instant one phase is high, one is low, and one floats. The current path is HS(phase A) → motor → LS(phase B). One half-bridge is PWM-switched with complementary synchronous rectification.

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

The on-resistance at junction temperature is:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{ds} = 0.75 \times (1 + 0.0045 \times 85) = 1.04\ \text{m}\Omega$$

Two FETs are always in series in the current path. Over the PWM period, the switched leg shares conduction between its HS (D) and LS sync-rectifier (1−D), so the total stays at two R_ds-equivalents:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j) = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.04\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{13.3\ W}$$

The 6-step rotation spreads this over 6 FETs. Each FET conducts 1/3 of the electrical cycle, so the average per FET is about 4.4 W. The per-FET **instantaneous** load, however, is about 6.7 W, and that number governs junction ripple at low eRPM.

Add copper and shunt losses:
- **Shunt** (0.25 mΩ, if a per-channel shunt is used): 80² × 0.25 m = **1.6 W**. This is why many 4-in-1 designs use a single bus shunt instead.
- **Copper** (2 oz, about 0.3 mΩ effective path): about **1.9 W**.

### 2.2 Hard-Switching (V-I Overlap) Loss

Only the PWM'd leg switches:

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 33\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.60\ W}$$

### 2.3 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f \, I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \, f_{sw} = 0.85 \times 80 \times 100\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.33\ W}$$

### 2.4 Reverse Recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 45\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.054\ W}$$

The Q_rr loss also appears as extra turn-on loss in the HS FET. Add about 30% margin to P_sw if the diode is slow.

### 2.5 Output Capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 45\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.054\ W}$$

### 2.6 Gate Drive

Two FETs are switched per period (HS + LS complementary):

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.058\ W}$$

This loss is dissipated mostly in the FD6288Q and the gate resistors, not in the FETs.

### 2.7 Per-Channel Totals @ 80 A

| Loss | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 13.3 |
| Switching | 1.60 |
| Dead-time | 0.33 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.11 |
| **FET subtotal** | **15.3** |
| Shunt + copper | 3.5 |
| Gate driver | 0.06 |
| **Channel total** | **≈ 18.9 W** |

**Board total:** 4 × 18.9 = **75.6 W**, plus about 2 W for the FC, BEC and logic. Electrical efficiency at this point is P_out ≈ 25.2 × 80 × 0.95 ≈ 1915 W per channel, so about 99.0%. The efficiency is good, but the dissipation density is the problem.

### 2.8 Sensitivity: Choosing f_sw

Frequency-dependent losses at 80 A are (P_sw + P_dt + P_rr + P_oss + P_g)(f) ≈ 2.1 W at 48 kHz, which scales linearly with f.
- 24 kHz saves about 1.0 W per channel.
- 96 kHz costs about 2.1 W per channel.

At 80 A, conduction loss dominates (about 85%), so 48 kHz is a reasonable choice: it reduces current ripple and audible noise for a small loss penalty.

**Ripple check** (motor L ≈ 15 µH phase-phase, 50% duty worst case):

$$\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{15\mu \times 48\text{k}} \approx 8.75\text{ A p-p}$$

This is acceptable.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Steady-State Network (Per FET, Hottest Device)

```
Tj ──[Rθjc 1.0]── pad ──[Rθ,spread 6 K/W*]── board ──[Rθ,ba]── ambient
        * per-FET share of board spreading, 2oz/6-layer, thermal vias 0.3mm × 12
```

Board-to-ambient resistance:
- Still air, 30.5² board, both sides: h ≈ 10 W/m²K, A ≈ 1.9×10⁻³ m², so R_θ,ba ≈ 53 K/W (total board).
- Prop wash, ~8 m/s: h ≈ 60–80 W/m²K, so R_θ,ba ≈ 7–9 K/W.

**Continuous rating check** (prop wash, R_ba = 8 K/W, T_amb = 35 °C, max board temperature 105 °C):

$$P_{board,max} = (105-35)/8 \approx 8.75\ \text{W}$$

So the sustainable per-channel current with all four motors loaded is:

$$I_{cont} \approx \sqrt{\frac{8.75/4}{2R_{ds}+R_{cu}}} \approx \sqrt{\frac{2.19}{2.1\text{m}+0.55\text{m}}} \approx 29\text{ A}$$

That is the honest **all-motor continuous rating, about 30 A**. A 45 A rating is valid only for 1–2 channels or for short hover-to-punch transients.
