# STM32H743 AIO Nano Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Systems Architecture Package (Job #1)

**Status:** Preliminary engineering model. Release Rev A requires datasheet confirmation of the chosen MOSFET and driver, plus thermal validation on hardware.

---

## 0. Architecture Correction and Assumptions

**The H743 should not commutate the motors.** In every production AIO board, the H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot. Each ESC channel (or the whole 4-in-1) has its own commutation MCU, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Running four sensorless FOC/6-step loops at 48 kHz plus ArduPilot's EKF3 on one H743 is not viable. Its ADC and timer resources are also not structured for 4× three-phase sensing.

The design therefore splits into two sections:

- **FC section:** H743 running ArduPilot, sending bidirectional DShot600 on 4 lines.
- **ESC section:** 4× AT32F421 running AM32 at 48 kHz PWM, each with a 3-phase gate driver and 6 MOSFETs (24 total).

| Parameter | Assumed value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal | Typical |
| Burst phase current | 80 A per channel, ≤ 5 s | Requirement |
| PWM frequency | 48 kHz, complementary (active freewheel) | AM32 setting |
| MOSFET | 40 V, R_DS(on) = 1.0 mΩ @ 25 °C, Q_g = 50 nC, Q_gd = 10 nC, Q_rr = 40 nC, Q_oss = 30 nC, R_θJC = 1.0 K/W | Representative 3.3×3.3 mm or 5×6 mm DFN class |
| R_DS(on) hot multiplier | 1.5× at T_J ≈ 110 °C | Typical silicon α |
| Gate driver | FD6288Q-class, ~1 A source / 1.5 A sink, 12 V V_DRV | Typical |
| Dead time | 100 ns per edge | AM32 default region |
| Form factor | 20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner power | Nano AIO |

**Design flag:** 80 A per channel on a nano-format board is a **transient-only** rating, as Section 3 shows. It cannot be a continuous rating. The datasheet and marketing must state burst duration.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Topology
Assume 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification. At any instant, two phase legs carry current:

- **PWM on-time (D):** high-side FET of phase A and low-side FET of phase B conduct.
- **PWM off-time (1−D):** low-side FET of phase A (synchronous freewheel) and low-side FET of phase B conduct.

Either way, exactly **two channels' R_DS(on)** are in series with the phase current I_ph. Ripple is small at 48 kHz with motor inductance of 10–30 µH, so I_rms ≈ I_ph.

### 1.2 Equations

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_J) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_J - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0059/\text{K} \Rightarrow 1.5\times \text{ at } 110^\circ\text{C}$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_J)$$

Per FET, averaged over a full electrical cycle (each FET conducts about 1/3 of the time in 6-step):

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}\,I_{ph}^2 R_{DS(on)} \quad(\text{high/low split depends on } D)$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| Case | R_DS(on) | P_cond per channel | P_cond for 4 channels |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cold (25 °C) | 1.0 mΩ | 2·6400·0.001 = **12.8 W** | 51.2 W |
| Hot (110 °C) | 1.5 mΩ | 2·6400·0.0015 = **19.2 W** | 76.8 W |

**Copper and trace loss (often forgotten).** Assume about 0.3 mΩ effective per phase path, covering pad, via, and plane spreading from the battery pad to the motor pad.

$$P_{Cu,ch} = 2 \cdot 6400 \cdot 0.0003 \approx 3.8\text{ W}$$

**Instantaneous peak FET loss.** While a single FET is conducting:

$$I^2R = 6400 \times 0.0015 = 9.6\text{ W}$$

This instantaneous figure is what drives the junction-temperature ripple in Section 3.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the PWM-switched FET per channel (the active high side, plus its synchronous partner) sees hard switching.

### 2.1 Transition times (gate-charge method)

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{G,src}},\qquad t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{G,sink}}$$

With the plateau-limited gate current at about 1.0 A source and 1.0 A sink after gate resistance:

$$t_r \approx 10\text{ ns},\quad t_f \approx 10\text{ ns}$$

### 2.2 Loss components

| Term | Formula | Value @ 25.2 V, 80 A, 48 kHz |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | ½·25.2·80·20 ns·48k = **0.97 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 40 nC·25.2·48k = **0.048 W** |
| Output charge | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw·2 | 30 nC·25.2·48k = **0.036 W** |
| Dead-time body diode | V_F·I·2·t_dt·f_sw | 0.8·80·200 ns·48k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (driver loss) | Q_g·V_DRV·f_sw per switched FET (×2) | 2·50 nC·12·48k = **0.058 W** |
| **Total per channel** | | **≈ 1.72 W** |

### 2.3 Total per channel at 80 A burst (hot)

$$P_{ch} = 19.2 + 1.72 + 3.8 \approx \mathbf{24.7\ W}$$

$$P_{4ch} \approx \mathbf{99\ W}\quad(\text{all four channels at 80 A simultaneously})$$

**Frequency sensitivity:**

$$\frac{\partial P_{sw}}{\partial f} \approx 1.72/48\text{k} \approx 36\ \mu\text{W/Hz}$$

Going from 24 kHz to 48 kHz therefore costs about 0.86 W per channel. The trade-off is lower current ripple and quieter motors. At 80 A the design is **conduction-dominated, at about 11:1**, so 48 kHz is acceptable.

**Efficiency check.** At D ≈ 0.9 and I_batt ≈ D·I_ph = 72 A per channel, P_in = 22.2·72 ≈ 1.6 kW per channel. Loss of 24.7 W gives η ≈ 98.5%.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Thermal network (Foster-to-Cauer simplified)

```
P_FET ──[R_θJC 1.0]──●──[R_θ,CB 2.0/FET]──●──[R_θ,BA 6–12 K/W (board→air, prop-wash)]── T_amb
                     C_J ≈ 0.02 J/K       C_board ≈ 5 J/K
```

**Board heat capacity estimate (25.5×25.5 mm, 8 L, populated):**

- Copper: 8 layers × 6.5 cm² × ~50 µm avg × 8.96 g/cm³ ≈ 2.3 g → ×0.385 J/gK ≈ 0.9 J/K
- FR4: ~2.5 g × 1.1 J/gK ≈ 2.8 J/K
- Components (24 FETs, capacitors): ≈ 1.3 J/K
- **C_board ≈ 5 J/K.** Thermal time constant τ = R_θBA·C ≈ 8·5 = 40 s.

### 3.2 Steady state (why 80 A cannot be continuous)

$$\Delta T_{board,ss} = P \cdot R_{\theta BA} = 99 \times 8 \approx 790\ \text{K}$$

This is physically impossible. With a 110 °C board limit at 35 °C ambient, the sustainable total loss is:

$$P_{max} = \frac{75}{8} \approx 9.4\text{ W total} \Rightarrow \approx 2.3\text{ W/ch} \Rightarrow I_{cont} \approx \sqrt{\frac{2.3 - 0.4}{2(0.0015)+0.0006}} \approx 23\text{ A/ch}$$

Realistic continuous rating is **20–25 A per channel**, consistent with commercial nano AIOs. Those boards rate about 40–55 A burst.

### 3.3 Transient (burst) model

Because t_burst ≪ τ, the board behaves adiabatically:

$$\Delta T_{board}(t) = P R_{\theta BA}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right) \approx \frac{P\,t}{C_{board}}$$

| Burst duration | ΔT_board (99 W, all 4 ch) | ΔT_board (24.7 W, 1 ch) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 s | 19.8 K | 4.9 K |
| 2 s | 39.6 K | 9.9 K |
| 5 s | 99 K ❌ | 24.7 K |
