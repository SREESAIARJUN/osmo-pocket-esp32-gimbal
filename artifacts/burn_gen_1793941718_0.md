# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #31): Power-Stage Thermal Model, Switching Losses, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Allocation

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

Two points about the system partitioning affect every model below:

1. **The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs, not the H743.** In a practical 4-in-1 design, each phase bridge is commutated by a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical parts are the AT32F421 and STM32G071. The H743 only produces the DShot600 command stream and decodes the bidirectional eRPM telemetry. The loss model below therefore applies to the ESC power stage.
2. **No MOSFET part number was specified.** I have parameterised everything and used representative values for a 40 V, 5×6 or 3.3×3.3 mm trench FET. **Substitute your actual datasheet values before sign-off.**

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 25.2 V | 6S, full charge |
| I_ph | 80 A | Burst phase current per channel |
| f_sw | 48 kHz | PWM frequency |
| R_ds(on),25 | 1.0 mΩ | Maximum at V_gs = 10 V, 25 °C |
| α | 0.006 /K | R_ds tempco, so the normalised value is ≈1.6 at 125 °C |
| t_r / t_f | 20 / 15 ns | Effective V-I overlap times with the chosen gate resistors |
| Q_rr | 50 nC | Body diode reverse recovery |
| Q_oss | 40 nC at 25 V | Output charge |
| Q_g | 50 nC | Total gate charge |
| V_f | 0.8 V | Body diode forward voltage |
| t_dt | 100 ns | Dead time per transition |
| R_θJC | 1.5 K/W | Junction to case/pad |

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Path

In each commutation sector, two phases are active:
- **Phase A (PWM leg):** the high-side FET conducts for duty D. The low-side FET conducts by synchronous rectification for (1 − D).
- **Phase B (return leg):** the low-side FET is on continuously.

The total conduction loss per ESC channel is therefore:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

This result is **independent of duty cycle** with synchronous rectification. Without synchronous rectification (diode freewheel), the (1 − D) term becomes $V_f I (1-D)$ instead, which is much worse.

### 1.2 Temperature Dependence

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

$$
T_j = T_{board} + P_{FET}\,R_{\theta JC}
$$

This forms a positive-feedback loop. The fixed-point solution is:

$$
P_{FET} = \frac{I^2 R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_{board}-25)]}{1 - I^2 R_{ds,25}\,\alpha\,R_{\theta JC}}
$$

**Thermal-runaway criterion:** the system is stable only if $I^2 R_{ds,25}\,\alpha\,R_{\theta JC} < 1$.

At 80 A this term is 6400 × 0.001 × 0.006 × 1.5 = **0.0576**. That is stable with margin at the FET level. Runaway risk on this design comes from board-level heating, not from junction feedback.

### 1.3 Worked Values per Channel at T_board = 90 °C

- $R_{ds}(90) = 1.0 × (1 + 0.006 × 65) = 1.39$ mΩ
- Per conducting FET: $P = 6400 × 0.00139 / (1 − 0.0576) ≈ 9.44$ W
- Junction temperature: $T_j = 90 + 9.44 × 1.5 ≈ 104$ °C. A one-step re-iteration converges at about **9.5 W per FET**.
- **P_cond per channel ≈ 19 W**

Conduction loss dominates switching loss by about 8:1 (see §2). Choosing a lower-R_ds FET, or paralleling two FETs per switch, is the biggest single lever: paralleling cuts conduction loss by about 50%.

### 1.4 Copper and Shunt Losses

These add I²R terms:
- **Current shunt:** 0.25 mΩ gives 1.6 W.
- **Phase pours, each way:** about 0.3 mΩ for 2 oz copper on 20 mm traces. This adds roughly 2 W per active path pair.

**Per-channel conduction plus parasitics ≈ 22–23 W.**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Each PWM period contains one turn-on and one turn-off of the hard-switched FET. The complementary FET switches with near-zero voltage after the dead time.

| Loss term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | $\tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}$ | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 35 ns × 48k = **1.69 W** |
| Reverse recovery | $Q_{rr} V f_{sw}$ | 50 nC × 25.2 × 48k = **0.06 W** |
| Coss charge (×2 FETs) | $Q_{oss} V f_{sw}$ | 2 × ½ × 40 nC × 25.2 × 48k = **0.05 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | $V_f I \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw}$ | 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs, dissipated in driver/R_g) | $Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw}$ × 2 | 2 × 50 nC × 10 × 48k = **0.05 W** |
| **Total P_sw per channel** | | **≈ 2.46 W** |

**Scaling sensitivities:**
- **Frequency:** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. Going from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves about 1.2 W per channel but increases current ripple and motor audible noise.
- **Dead time:** dead-time loss is significant. Reducing t_dt from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W, but only if the driver propagation-delay mismatch allows it.
- **Ringing:** high di/dt from tight t_f increases ringing. Budget a snubber or a soft low-side gate resistor (R_g,off) if you see V_ds overshoot above 32 V on 6S.

## 3. Total Board Losses and Burst Thermal Budget

### 3.1 Loss Summary

| Scenario | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| 80 A burst, all motors | 22.5 + 2.5 ≈ **25 W** | **≈ 100 W** |
| 40 A sustained (conduction ¼, switching ½) | ≈ 6.8 W | ≈ 27 W |

### 3.2 Transient Thermal Model

A first-order lumped RC model of the board:

$$
C_{th}\frac{dT}{dt} = P_{loss} - \frac{T - T_{amb}}{R_{\theta,BA}}
$$

$$
T(t) = T_{amb} + P R_{\theta,BA}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right) + (T_0 - T_{amb})e^{-t/\tau}, \quad \tau = R_{\theta,BA} C_{th}
$$

**Estimated parameters for a 30×30 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz board with 24 FETs (about 9 g total):**
- C_th ≈ 9 g × ~0.75 J/(g·K) blended Cu/FR4/Si ≈ **6.8 J/K**
- R_θ,BA with prop-wash airflow at ~5 m/s ≈ **3 K/W** (about 12 K/W in still air)
- τ ≈ 20 s

**Burst endurance:** starting at T₀ = 50 °C and limiting the board to 105 °C, so that T_j stays at or below about 125 °C with the 9.5 W × 1.5 K/W ≈ 14 K rise from board to junction:

$$
t_{max} = -\tau \ln\!\left(1 - \frac{T_{lim}-T_0}{P R_{\theta,BA} - (T_0 - T_{amb})}\right)
$$

With P = 100 W, R = 3 K/W, T_amb = 30 °C:

t_max = −20 · ln(1 − 55/280) ≈ **4.4 s** for an all-motor 80 A burst.
