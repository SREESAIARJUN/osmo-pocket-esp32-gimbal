# STM32H743 + 4-in-1 ESC Nano Stack (Job #21): Architecture, Loss Model, BDShot DMA Plan

## 0. Architectural Corrections

These change how the problem has to be modeled.

1. **The STM32H743 does not generate the 48 kHz bridge PWM.** In any ArduPilot 4-in-1 system, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical parts are AT32F421, STM32G071 or GD32E230. The ESC MCU does the 48 kHz complementary PWM, the commutation and the bidirectional DShot (BDShot) telemetry reply. The H743 only:
   - generates DShot600 frames,
   - captures the GCR-encoded eRPM replies,
   - consumes current and voltage telemetry over ADC and UART.
2. **"80 A burst" on a "nano" (20×20 mm) board is not thermally feasible** for any useful burst length. The thermal math in §3 shows this. I size the ESC as a 30.5×30.5 mm board stacked under a 20×20 or 30.5 FC. If 20×20 is a hard constraint, the burst rating must drop to about 35–40 A.
3. I interpret "80 A burst" as **80 A per motor channel**, for 320 A pack-side peak. Every number below scales with I².

## 2. Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 2.1 Conduction loss

In 6-step complementary PWM, the current path at any instant runs through one PWM-leg FET and one static-leg FET in series. During ON, the PWM leg conducts through its high side; during OFF, through its synchronous low side. That gives:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot \left(2\,k_T\,R_{ds(on),25} + R_{cu}\right)$$

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \cdot (2 \cdot 1.5 \cdot 0.70 + 0.25)\,\text{m}\Omega = 6400 \cdot 2.35\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{15.04\ W}$$

- FET silicon alone: 13.44 W. Copper path: 1.60 W.
- Conduction loss is independent of duty cycle, because synchronous rectification keeps two FETs in the path at all times.

### 2.2 Hard-switching loss (PWM leg only)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus}\, I\,(t_r+t_f)\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM period:

$$P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 400\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

> Lever: cutting dead time to 100 ns saves 0.6 W per channel. AM32 exposes dead time per target, so verify shoot-through margin on the scope.

### 2.4 Reverse recovery, C_oss and gate drive

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse recovery | P_rr = V_bus · Q_rr · f_sw | 25.2 · 50 nC · 48 k = 0.060 W |
| Output capacitance | P_oss = ½ · C_oss · V_bus² · f_sw · 2 | 0.061 W |
| Gate drive (2 switching FETs) | P_g = 2 · Q_g · V_GS · f_sw | 0.058 W, dissipated mostly in the driver |

### 2.5 Totals

| Loss | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET + copper) | 15.04 W | 60.2 W |
| Switching | 1.94 W | 7.8 W |
| Dead time | 1.23 W | 4.9 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.18 W | 0.7 W |
| **Total** | **18.4 W** | **73.6 W** |

At 80 A burst, efficiency is 1 − 18.4 / (25.2 · 80 · D). At D = 0.9 that is about 99.0 %, which looks good. The problem is not efficiency; it is that 74 W must leave a very small board.

**Per-FET distribution.** Each phase conducts 2/3 of the electrical cycle, split between its high-side and low-side FETs. The average per FET is about 18.4 / 6 ≈ 3.1 W, with an instantaneous peak around 7–8 W on the PWM high side.
