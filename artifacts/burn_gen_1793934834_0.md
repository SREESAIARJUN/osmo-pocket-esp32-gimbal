### Three architectural flags to resolve before layout

1. **The H743 cannot be the ESC controller.** Bi-directional DShot is a protocol between a flight controller and *separate* ESC MCUs. The H743 has only two advanced timers (TIM1 and TIM8) with complementary outputs, which is enough for 2 motors, not 4. ArduPilot also does not run BLDC commutation.
   - The correct topology is an H743 running ArduPilot, sending DShot600 to 4 ESC MCUs (AT32F421 or STM32G071) running AM32 or BLHeli_32.
   - The netlists below follow that topology.
2. **80 A on a nano board is a burst rating, not a continuous one.** With all four motors at 80 A, the board dissipates about 140 W. Section 3 shows the realistic burst window is about 2 s for all four motors, and 8–10 s for a single motor. Any marketing "80 A" figure must specify duration.
3. **Phase current is not battery current.** At 80 A phase current and duty D, the battery current is roughly D × 80 A. Size the battery-side shunt and bulk capacitors from the actual duty profile.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (6-Step, Synchronous Rectification)

In any 60° commutation sector, current flows through exactly one high-side and one low-side path. With complementary PWM, the PWM leg alternates between its HS FET (on-time D) and its LS FET (freewheel 1−D). The non-PWM leg stays on. The conducting resistance is therefore always 2·R_DS regardless of D. The only exception is dead time, which is handled in §2.

**Bridge conduction loss per motor:**

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha (T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\,/^\circ C$$

**Numbers at 80 A and T_j = 125 °C (R = 2.4 mΩ):**

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2(80)^2(0.0024) = 30.7\ \text{W per motor}$$
