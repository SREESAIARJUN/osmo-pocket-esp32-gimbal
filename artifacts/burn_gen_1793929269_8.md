# STM32H743 AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC), Job #63: Systems Architecture Model

## 0. Architectural Ground Truth (Read First)

Two points shape every model below.

**1. The STM32H743 does not drive the power stage.** In an ArduPilot AIO, the H743 runs the flight stack and emits DShot600. Each of the four ESC channels has its own MCU running AM32, BLHeli_32, or Bluejay, for example an AT32F421 or STM32G071. That MCU generates the 48 kHz six-step PWM, handles dead-time, and returns bidirectional eRPM telemetry. ArduPilot does not do motor commutation. The partition is therefore:

```
[STM32H743 / ArduPilot] --DShot600 BDShot x4--> [4x ESC MCU] --> [4x 3-phase gate driver] --> [24x MOSFET]
```

**2. "80A burst" and "nano" are thermally incompatible.** Section 3 shows this quantitatively. At 80 A per channel on a 20×20 mm board, the adiabatic temperature rise exceeds 15 K/s. I model the full requirement honestly and then give the realistic envelope.

All device parameters below are **representative assumptions** (a typical 30 V, ~1 mΩ, 3.3×3.3 mm N-channel FET). They must be replaced with datasheet values from the selected parts before release.

## 2. Loss Model (Per ESC Channel, Six-Step Trapezoidal, Synchronous Rectification)

### 2.1 Conduction topology

In any 60° commutation sector, two phases are active:
- Leg A: the high-side is PWM'd at duty D, and its complementary low-side conducts during (1−D).
- Leg B: the low-side is held on.

The motor current therefore always flows through exactly **two** conducting MOSFET channels, regardless of D.

### 2.2 Conduction loss

$$
I_{rms} \approx I_{ph}\sqrt{1 + \frac{1}{12}\left(\frac{\Delta I}{I_{ph}}\right)^2}
$$

With typical ripple (ΔI/I < 0.3 at 48 kHz), the correction is under 0.4%, so I_rms ≈ I_ph.

$$
R_{ds,hot} = k_T \cdot R_{ds,25} = 1.6 \text{ mΩ}
$$

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \cdot I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{ds,hot} = 2 \cdot 6400 \cdot 0.0016 = \mathbf{20.48\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Hard-switching (V-I overlap) loss

Only the PWM'd high-side hard-switches. The synchronous low-side switches at near-zero V_ds after body-diode commutation.

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\text{ ns} \cdot 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

### 2.4 Secondary switching terms

| Term | Equation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw | 0.036 W |
| C_oss discharge | E_oss · f_sw | 0.014 W |
| Gate drive (HS + sync LS) | 2 · Q_g · V_drv · f_sw | 0.048 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f · I_ph · 2t_dt · f_sw | 0.614 W |

The gate-drive term is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET junction.

### 2.5 Copper loss

$$
P_{cu} = I_{ph}^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \cdot 0.0005 = 3.2\text{ W}
$$

### 2.6 Total

| Component | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 20.48 W | 81.9 W |
| Switching (all terms) | 2.65 W | 10.6 W |
| Copper | 3.20 W | 12.8 W |
| **Total power stage** | **26.3 W** | **105.3 W** |
| FC + ESC MCUs + regulators | — | ~2.5 W |
| **Board total** | | **≈108 W** |

**Key insight:** at 80 A, conduction dominates (78%). Raising f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz costs only about 1.3 W per channel. **R_ds(on) and copper resistance are the levers; switching frequency is not.**

Efficiency check: P_out ≈ 4 × 25.2 × 80 × D_eff. At full throttle the stage is about 1 − 108/(8064 + 108) ≈ 98.7% efficient. The problem is not efficiency. It is power density.
