**Architecture decision.** The H743 is the flight controller only. Each motor gets its own ESC MCU (for example AT32F421 or GD32 running AM32, or the BLHeli_32 class). The H743 does not commutate motors: it emits bidirectional DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry. Driving 4 × 3 half-bridges directly from the H743 would require 24 complementary PWM outputs with dead time, 12+ ADC channels for BEMF/current, and ArduPilot does not support it.

**Feasibility warning.** 80 A per channel on a 20×20 board is physically marginal, as §3 shows. Treat "80 A burst" as a few-second rating.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in complementary 6-step
During any 60° commutation sector:
- **Leg A (PWM leg):** high-side conducts for duty D, low-side for (1−D) (synchronous rectification).
- **Leg B (return leg):** low-side is on 100 %.
- **Leg C:** floating (BEMF sensing).

So at any instant two switches carry the full phase current I:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

This is independent of D. That is why synchronous PWM ESCs heat up at low throttle with high current.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence (must be iterated)
$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\left(1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right),\quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\text{–}0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 110 °C, R_DS ≈ 1.0 × (1 + 0.0055·85) ≈ **1.47 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A
$$P_{cond} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.47\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{18.8\ W\ per\ motor}$$

Per switch, worst case (low-side of return leg, 100 % on): 80² · 1.47 mΩ = **9.4 W** during that sector. Averaged over 6 sectors, each FET conducts about 1/3 of the time, giving roughly 3.1 W average per FET. The junction still sees 9.4 W pulses lasting ~1/6 of an electrical period.

### 1.4 Copper/PCB conduction loss (often forgotten)
Phase trace at 2 oz (70 µm), 5 mm wide, 8 mm long, outer layer:

$$R = \frac{\rho L}{wt} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot 0.008}{0.005 \cdot 70\times10^{-6}} \approx 0.39\ \text{m}\Omega$$

That adds 80² × 0.39 mΩ × 2 paths ≈ **5 W per motor**, plus the shared battery bus. Parallel layers and stitching vias are mandatory.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. One hard-switched transition pair occurs per period.

| Term | Formula | Value @ 25.2 V, 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ V I (t_r + t_f) f_sw | 0.5·25.2·80·20 ns·48 k = **0.97 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr V f_sw | 40 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.05 W** |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss V f_sw | 35 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.04 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_F I (2 t_dt) f_sw | 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs, mostly in driver) | 2 Q_g V_GS f_sw | 2·45 nC·10·48 k = **0.04 W** |
| **Total P_sw per motor** | | **≈ 1.7 W** |

**Interpretation.** At 48 kHz, switching is about 8 % of loss at 80 A. Conduction dominates, so prioritise low R_DS(on) over low Q_g. Dead-time loss is the largest switching term. Trim dead time to the minimum the driver/FET pair allows without shoot-through: verify with a scope at the switch node, cold and hot.

### 2.1 Total per motor and per board
$$P_{motor} = P_{cond} + P_{sw} + P_{Cu} \approx 18.8 + 1.7 + 5 \approx \mathbf{25.5\ W}$$

$$P_{board,4\times80A} \approx \mathbf{102\ W}$$

Efficiency at 80 A × ~20 V effective ≈ 1600 W/motor gives about 98.4 %. The efficiency is good, but 100 W in a ~4 cm² area is the problem.

### 2.2 Bus capacitor ripple current
$$I_{C,rms} \approx I\sqrt{D(1-D)} \le 0.5\,I = 40\ \text{A}_{rms}\ \text{per motor (at } D = 0.5)$$

Distribute low-ESR MLCCs at every half-bridge (≥ 4 × 10 µF/50 V X7R 1210, derated about 70 % at 25 V DC bias). Add one bulk 35 V/1000 µF low-ESR electrolytic or polymer on the battery pads. With bus ESR ≈ 3 mΩ total, ripple dissipation ≈ (√4 · 40)² × 3 mΩ ≈ 19 W worst case if not distributed. This is a further argument for local MLCCs.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Foster/lumped network
```
P_FET ──[R_jc 0.8K/W]── case ──[R_cb 1.5K/W (solder+vias)]── PCB ──[R_ba]── ambient
           C_j≈0.05J/K            C_case≈0.2J/K              C_board
```

### 3.2 Short-term (adiabatic) burst capacity
With all 4 motors at 80 A, heat cannot leave the board in seconds, so heat capacity governs.

| Mass | m (g) | c_p (J/g·K) | C (J/K) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copper (20×20, 4-layer heavy Cu) | 2.5 | 0.385 | 0.96 |
| FR4 | 2.0 | 1.1 | 2.2 |
| 24 FETs + passives | 2.0 | ~0.7 | 1.4 |
| **Total (20×20)** | | | **≈ 4.6 J/K** |
| **Total (30.5×30.5, scale ~2.3×)** | | | **≈ 10.5 J/K** |

$$\frac{dT_{board}}{dt} = \frac{P}{C} = \frac{102}{4.6} \approx \mathbf{22\ K/s}\ (20\times20),\qquad \approx 9.7\ \text{K/s}\ (30.5\times30.5)$$

Junction is above board by P_FET,peak × (R_jc + R_cb) = 9.4 × 2.3 ≈ **22 K**.

Time to T_j = 125 °C from a 45 °C pre-flight board:
$$t = \frac{125 - 22 - 45}{22} \approx \mathbf{2.6\ s}\ (20\times20),\qquad \approx 6\ s\ (30.5\times30.5)$$
