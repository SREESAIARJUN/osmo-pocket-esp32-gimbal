# STM32H743 AIO 4-in-1 ESC Nano FC (Job #19): Thermal, Switching, and DShot Design

## 0. Architecture Corrections Before Any Math

Two of the brief's premises would break the design if taken literally.

**1. The H743 cannot be the ESC controller for four motors.** It has only two advanced-control timers with complementary outputs and dead-time (TIM1 and TIM8). HRTIM is an option but awkward alongside ArduPilot's scheduler. Driving 4 × 3-phase bridges at 48 kHz with commutation and BEMF/ZC sensing on the same core as ArduPilot is not viable. The correct topology is:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot)  ──DShot600 bidir ×4──►  4 × ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32 firmware)
                                                  └─► 4 × gate driver ─► 24 × MOSFET
```

The 48 kHz figure is an ESC-side PWM setting (AM32 supports 24/48 kHz). It is not an H743 timer configuration.

**2. 80 A per channel on a nano (20×20 / 25.5 mm) board is burst-only.** The model below shows there is no thermal equilibrium at that current. Firmware current limiting is mandatory, not optional.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

Assume 6-step trapezoidal control with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM). In each commutation sector, two phases carry current I:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side conducts for D·T, the low-side conducts for (1−D)·T. Current flows through exactly one FET at all times.
- **Static leg:** the low-side conducts continuously.

Per channel, therefore:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS} = 2 I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

This is independent of duty cycle, which is why synchronous rectification is used. Including temperature dependence:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At 80 A:

| Condition | P_cond per channel | P_cond for 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 2·6400·0.0012 = **15.4 W** | 61.4 W |
| 110 °C | 2·6400·0.0018 = **23.0 W** | 92.2 W |

**Copper loss, often ignored.** A phase trace on 2 oz copper (R ≈ 0.25 mΩ/sq), around 5 squares long, adds about 1.25 mΩ. Across 2 conducting phases that is 2·6400·0.00125 ≈ **16 W per channel**. The fix is heavy copper (3–4 oz inner planes), stitched pours, and soldering motor wires directly at the FET bridge.

## 3. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the PWM leg switches. Per channel:

$$P_{sw} = \underbrace{\tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f}_{\text{V–I overlap}} + \underbrace{Q_{rr} V f}_{\text{reverse recovery}} + \underbrace{\tfrac12 C_{oss} V^2 f}_{\text{output cap}} + \underbrace{2 V_f I t_{dt} f}_{\text{dead-time diode}} + \underbrace{2 Q_g V_{gs} f}_{\text{gate (driver)}}$$

| Term | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| Overlap | 0.5·25.2·80·40e-9·48e3 | 1.94 W |
| Q_rr | 25.2·30e-9·48e3 | 0.04 W |
| C_oss | 0.5·1.5e-9·25.2²·48e3 | 0.02 W |
| Dead-time | 2·0.8·80·50e-9·48e3 | 0.31 W |
| Gate | 2·40e-9·10·48e3 | 0.04 W (dissipated in driver) |
| **Total** | | **≈2.35 W per channel** |

Switching loss scales linearly with frequency. Going from 24 kHz to 48 kHz costs about +1.2 W per channel. That is about 5% of total loss, which buys smoother low-throttle operation and less audible noise.

## 4. Total Dissipation at Burst

| Item | Per channel | For 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (hot) | 23.0 W | 92 W |
| FET switching | 2.35 W | 9.4 W |
| Copper (optimised, ~0.5 mΩ path) | ~6.4 W | 25.6 W |
| **Total** | **~31.8 W** | **~127 W** |

**Per-FET hot spot:** the static low-side carries I²R = 11.5 W continuously during its sector.

With R_θJC ≈ 1.5 K/W, ΔT_jc ≈ 17 K. The junction-to-case drop is not the bottleneck. The bottleneck is the board-to-air path.
