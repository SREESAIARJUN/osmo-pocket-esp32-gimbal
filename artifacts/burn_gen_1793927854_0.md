# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with Integrated 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model

## Scope, Assumptions and a Feasibility Warning

I have no prior context for "Job #9," so this document states every assumption explicitly. All part-specific numbers are representative and must be replaced with values from your chosen parts' datasheets before layout freeze.

**Architecture clarification.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller (FC) MCU running ArduPilot. The ESCs need their own MCU, either:

- one MCU per motor (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 firmware), or
- a single 4-channel ESC MCU.

The H743 does not commutate the motors. It sends bi-directional DShot600 to the ESC MCUs and receives eRPM telemetry back. The design below follows that split.

The 80 A figure must be specified as a per-channel transient with a defined duty profile. A sustained rating is not credible at this size.

## 1. Thermal and Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.5 mΩ × 1.6 = **2.4 mΩ**. Use the hot value in all burst calculations. Conduction loss and temperature are coupled, which leads to the fixed-point solution in section 1.5.

### 1.2 Conduction loss per motor channel

In 6-step drive with synchronous rectification, one phase leg PWMs and one leg's low side is held on.

- **PWM leg:** current flows through the high-side FET for duty fraction D and through the low-side FET for 1 − D.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET conducts continuously.

Total conduction loss is therefore independent of D:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

At 80 A and 125 °C:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.4\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{30.7\ W}$$

Assumptions and corrections:

- Phase current is approximated as flat-top, so I_rms ≈ I_dc.
- Add ripple: $I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \Delta I_{pp}^2/12$. With a typical 5–10 µH motor at 48 kHz this adds < 1 %.

### 1.3 Switching loss at 48 kHz (PWM leg only)

**(a) V·I overlap (hard switching on the high side):**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

**(b) Output capacitance.** Both FETs in the leg cycle C_oss:

$$P_{oss} = 2 \times \tfrac12 C_{oss} V^2 f_{sw} = 1\text{ nF} \times 25.2^2 \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.03\ W}$$

**(c) Body-diode reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = 30\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.04\ W}$$

**(d) Dead-time diode conduction.** There are two dead-time intervals per period:

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD} I \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 2 \times 100\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**(e) Gate charge.** This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET:

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{ nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{ k} = 0.048\ W$$

**Sensitivity.** Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. Moving from 24 kHz to 48 kHz roughly doubles terms (a)–(d), adding about +0.8 W per channel. That cost is small compared with conduction loss, so 48 kHz is acceptable for the smoother low-throttle behavior it gives.

### 1.5 Junction-temperature fixed point

The steady-state junction temperature depends on R_DS, which itself depends on T_j:

$$T_j = T_a + P(T_j)\, R_{\theta,ja}$$

Expanding the conduction term gives:

$$T_j = T_a + R_{\theta}\Big[2I^2R_{25}\big(1+\alpha(T_j-25)\big) + P_{sw}\Big]$$

Solving for T_j:

$$T_j = \frac{T_a + R_\theta\big(2I^2R_{25}(1-25\alpha) + P_{sw}\big)}{1 - R_\theta\, 2I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha}$$

**Thermal runaway** occurs when the denominator reaches zero, i.e. when $R_\theta \ge 1/(2I^2R_{25}\alpha)$. At 80 A this limit is 1/(2·6400·0.0015·0.006) = **8.7 K/W per channel**.

### 1.6 Board-level thermal capacity: the binding constraint

Estimate the lumped heat capacity of a 30×30 mm, 6-layer board with 2 oz copper:

| Material | Mass | Specific heat | Heat capacity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copper | ~2.5 g | 0.385 J/g·K | 0.96 J/K |
| FR4 | ~4 g | 1.1 J/g·K | 4.4 J/K |
| FETs, components, solder | | | ~1 J/K |
| **Total C_board** | | | **≈ 6.4 J/K** |

Convective resistance to air with prop-wash: R_θ,ba ≈ 2.5–4 K/W for the whole board in good airflow.

**Steady-state continuous capability** with T_a = 40 °C and a board limit of 110 °C (ΔT = 70 K) at R_θ = 3 K/W:

- Allowed power: P_max ≈ 23 W total, about 6 W per channel.
- Per-channel continuous current from $I \approx \sqrt{6/(2\times2.2\text{ m}\Omega)}$, less extras, gives roughly **30–35 A continuous per channel**, all four running together.

**Burst model** (single-pole RC):

$$T(t) = T_0 + P R_\theta\,(1 - e^{-t/\tau}), \quad \tau = R_\theta C \approx 19\text{ s}$$

For short bursts this is nearly adiabatic: ΔT ≈ P·t/C.

| Scenario | P | Rate of rise | Time from 60 °C to 110 °C |
|---|---|---|---|
| All four at 80 A | 144 W | 22 K/s | **≈ 2.2 s** |
| One channel at 80 A, others at 20 A | ~36 + 3×3 = 45 W | 7 K/s | ~7 s |

**Junction overshoot.** The FET die sits on top of the board temperature. Z_th,jc(1 s) for a 3×3 DFN is about 1.5 K/W, so at 36 W per FET-pair spread over 2 dies the junction runs ~27 K above the pad. Keep the board-level limit at ≤ 110 °C so T_j stays ≤ 150 °C.

**Specification wording:** "80 A burst per channel, 1 s, ≥ 10 s recovery; 35 A continuous per channel with airflow."
