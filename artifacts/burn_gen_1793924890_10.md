## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Engineering Caveats

### 0.1 System partitioning

A "4-in-1 ESC" does not run its commutation on the STM32H743. The stack is split into two boards:

| Board | MCU | Role |
|---|---|---|
| FC (20×20 mm, M3/M2 mounting) | STM32H743VIH6 @ 400 MHz (ArduPilot default clock) | Attitude control, DShot600 + bi-dir telemetry master |
| 4-in-1 ESC (20×20 mm) | 4× AT32F421K8U7 (AM32) or EFM8/STM32G071 (BLHeli_32-class) | 6-step or sinusoidal commutation, 48 kHz PWM |

The H743 timer/DMA analysis below covers the FC. The loss and thermal analysis covers the ESC.

### 0.2 Design point

| Parameter | Symbol | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|---|

> ⚠️ **Feasibility flag (stated up front):** The model below shows that **80 A per channel on a 20×20 mm board is a ~1–2 second burst rating, not a sustained one.** With 4 channels at burst, the board dissipates ≈90 W into roughly 3 J/K of thermal mass. Treat 80 A as a marketing-style burst limit, and enforce it in firmware (§5).

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths with synchronous rectification (6-step, complementary PWM)

During each of the 6 commutation sectors, one phase is high-side PWM, one phase is low-side ON, and one phase floats.

- **PWM ON (duty D):** current flows V_bus → HS(A) → motor → LS(B) → GND. Two FETs conduct.
- **PWM OFF (1−D):** current freewheels through LS(A) (synchronous) → motor → LS(B). Two FETs conduct.

Therefore, independent of duty:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds(on)}(T_j)
$$

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$
R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds(on),25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$
R_{ds(on)} = 1.0\,\text{mΩ} \times 1.6 = 1.6\,\text{mΩ}
$$

### 1.3 Per-channel conduction loss at 80 A

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.6\times10^{-3} = 20.48\ \text{W}
$$

### 1.4 Distribution across FETs

Over one electrical cycle, each of the 6 FETs takes an equal share in 6-step operation:

$$
P_{cond,FET} = \frac{20.48}{6} \approx 3.41\ \text{W (average)}
$$

The instantaneous FET loss is 80² × 1.6 mΩ = 10.24 W while that FET conducts. This matters at low eRPM, where the sector dwell time approaches the FET thermal time constant.

### 1.5 Copper and parasitic conduction (often larger than expected)

$$
P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{path}
$$

Assume R_path is about 0.4 mΩ per channel (2 oz outer + 2 oz inner pours, vias, and solder pads). Then:

$$
P_{Cu,ch} = 80^2 \times 0.4\times10^{-3} = 2.56\ \text{W}
$$

The battery-side bus carries I_batt = D · I_ph per channel. At D = 0.8 with 4 channels, the main pads see about 256 A. That is why **the XT60 pad, the bulk-cap placement, and the battery lead gauge (≥12 AWG) are part of the thermal design.**

### 1.6 Conduction-loss sensitivity to the thermal model

The dependence is self-reinforcing. Because R_ds(on) rises with T_j, the loss is:

$$
P_{cond}(T_j) = 2I^2R_{25}\left[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\right]
$$

Combined with T_j = T_a + R_th · P, the closed-form steady-state solution is:

$$
T_j = \frac{T_a + R_{th}\cdot 2I^2R_{25}(1-25\alpha)}{1 - R_{th}\cdot 2I^2R_{25}\alpha}
$$

**Thermal runaway** occurs when the denominator reaches 0:

$$
R_{th} \geq \frac{1}{2I^2R_{25}\alpha} = \frac{1}{2(6400)(10^{-3})(0.006)} = 13\ \text{K/W}
$$

At 80 A, any effective per-channel R_th above **13 K/W** has no steady state. The estimate in §3 gives a nano board R_th to air of about 12.5 K/W for the whole board, which is far worse per channel. **Steady-state 80 A is mathematically unreachable.** Only the transient regime applies.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWMed high-side switch hard-switches. The complementary low-side switch turns on after its body diode is already conducting (near-ZVS).

### 2.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}
= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\times10^{-9} \times 48\,000 = 0.968\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM period:

$$
P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I_{ph} \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw}
= 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\times10^{-9} \times 48\,000 = 0.614\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\times10^{-9}\times 25.2 \times 48\,000 = 0.060\ \text{W}
$$

The induced HS turn-on current spike adds roughly 30–50% more to P_sw in practice. Fold that into a 1.3× margin below.

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2}Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 0.5\times 40\times10^{-9}\times 25.2\times 48\,000 = 0.024\ \text{W}
$$
