## 80 A Burst Thermal, 48 kHz Switching, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Architecture

## 0. Architectural Ground Rules and Assumptions

Four decisions shape everything that follows.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot outputs DShot. It does not run motor control loops. Each of the four ESC channels therefore gets its own commutation MCU. This design uses the AT32F421 running AM32, which supports bi-directional DShot, a 48 kHz PWM option, and is open source. The H743 and the ESC MCUs share one PCB stack but sit on separate power domains.

**2. "80 A burst" means per motor, for a bounded duration.** Four motors at 80 A each from 6S would draw 320 A from the pack. No nano-class connector or pack survives that. The model below covers:
- **Case A:** one channel at 80 A burst while the others run at cruise.
- **Case B:** all four at 80 A, used to derive the maximum burst time.

**3. Baseline parameters.** These are representative, not tied to a specific datasheet. Substitute your selected part's values; every equation is kept symbolic first.

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 25.2 V | 6S fully charged (worst case for switching loss) |
| R_ds,25 | 0.8 mΩ | 40 V class MOSFET at V_gs = 10 V, 25 °C |
| α | 0.007 /K | R_ds(on) temperature coefficient |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-step (trapezoidal) commutation

In any 60° electrical sector, one high-side and one low-side FET carry the phase current I. With synchronous (complementary) PWM at duty D:

- High-side PWM FET: P_HS = I²_rms · R_ds(T_j) · D
- Synchronous low-side, during the off-time: P_LS,sync = I²_rms · R_ds(T_j) · (1 − D)
- Static low-side FET on the return leg: P_LS,static = I²_rms · R_ds(T_j)

Summing per channel:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 \, R_{ds}(T_j)\,\big[D + (1-D) + 1\big] = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This is independent of D when switching is synchronous.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence and ripple

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big]$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp} \approx \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{2 L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}$$

**Ripple check.** Take a typical 5-inch motor with L_ph ≈ 15 µH, D = 0.5, and V_bus − V_bemf ≈ 8 V under load:

ΔI_pp ≈ 8 · 0.5 / (2 · 15e-6 · 48e3) ≈ 2.8 A, so ΔI²/12 ≈ 0.65 A². This is negligible against 6400 A². The 48 kHz switching frequency buys low ripple.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per channel |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 0.80 mΩ | 2 · 6400 · 0.80e-3 = **10.2 W** |
| 100 °C | 1.22 mΩ | **15.6 W** |
| 125 °C | 1.36 mΩ | **17.4 W** |

### 1.4 Electro-thermal fixed point

Conduction loss and temperature are coupled, so solve them iteratively:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_a + P\big(T_j^{(k)}\big)\cdot Z_{th}(t)$$

The solution converges if

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\,Z_{th} = 2 I^2 R_{ds,25}\,\alpha\, Z_{th} < 1$$

At 80 A that gives 0.072 · Z_th < 1, so **Z_th < 13.9 K/W** is required to avoid thermal runaway.
- This is easily met for the junction-to-board path.
- It **fails** for a still-air board-to-ambient path (≈ 15–25 K/W). Never sustain 80 A without prop-wash airflow.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In six-step, only **one FET per channel hard-switches** at any time: the PWM'd high side. Its complementary low side switches softly (ZVS after dead time) but incurs body-diode and reverse-recovery losses.
