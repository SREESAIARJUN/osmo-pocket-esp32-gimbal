# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage, Thermal, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Design Model

## 0. Architecture Corrections Before the Math

**1. The H743 does not commutate motors.** In this product class, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 talks to those MCUs over bi-directional DShot600. The model below assumes 1× H743 + 4× ESC MCUs.

**2. 80 A burst on a 20×20 mm "nano" board is not thermally realizable for more than about 1–2 seconds.** Section 3 shows why. I recommend one of two paths:
- **Option A:** Keep the nano form factor (20×20 mm, M2 mounting) and derate to **40 A continuous / 50 A burst**.
- **Option B:** Keep 80 A burst and move to a 30.5×30.5 mm board with 5×6 mm MOSFETs.

The equations are parametric, so either option drops straight in.

**3. All device parameters below are assumptions.** They are representative values for a 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN trench MOSFET. Replace them with datasheet values for the parts you select before any design review.

| Symbol | Meaning | Assumed value |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | Bus voltage (6S, fully charged) | 25.2 V |
| I_pk | Burst phase current per ESC | 80 A |
| R_ds(on),25 | On-resistance at 25 °C (V_gs = 10 V) | 0.70 mΩ |
| α | R_ds(on) temperature multiplier at T_j = 110 °C | 1.55 |
| t_r, t_f | Rise / fall time with gate driver (~2 A, R_g = 2.2 Ω) | 18 ns / 15 ns |
| Q_oss | Output charge at V_bus | 45 nC |
| Q_rr | Reverse-recovery charge | 30 nC |
| Q_g | Total gate charge | 55 nC |
| V_SD | Body-diode forward voltage | 0.8 V |
| t_dead | Dead time | 200 ns |
| f_sw | PWM switching frequency | 48 kHz |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous (complementary) PWM. In any commutation sector, current flows through exactly two half-bridges: one phase sourcing, one phase sinking.

- **Active leg:** during the on-time D, the high-side FET conducts. During the off-time (1 − D), the low-side FET freewheels the same current.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET conducts continuously for the whole sector.

Current therefore always passes through exactly two R_ds(on) elements in series, independent of duty cycle (ignoring the dead-time intervals handled in Section 2).

### 1.2 Equations

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + k(T_j - 25)] \approx \alpha\,R_{ds,25}$$

$$P_{cond,ESC} = I_{pk}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{ds}(T_j) + I_{pk}^2 \cdot R_{Cu,path}$$

### 1.3 Numbers

- R_ds(110 °C) = 0.70 × 1.55 = **1.085 mΩ**
- MOSFET conduction: 80² × 2 × 1.085 mΩ = **13.9 W per ESC**

**Copper path loss.** The path is pad → phase pour → motor pad, roughly 12 mm × 2 layers, using 2 oz copper (70 µm) and 6 mm effective width.

- Per layer: R = ρL/(wt) = 1.72e-8 × 0.012 / (0.006 × 70e-6) ≈ 0.49 mΩ
- Two layers in parallel: ≈ 0.25 mΩ
- Two phases plus the bus share: ≈ 0.6 mΩ total
- Copper loss: 6400 × 0.6 mΩ ≈ **3.8 W per ESC**

This term is large on a nano board. **Use 3 oz copper on the outer layers and 2 oz on the inner layers, with a 6–8 layer stack.**

**Total conduction loss: ≈ 17.7 W per ESC.**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing high-side FET hard-switches. The low-side FET soft-switches after its body diode conducts during dead time.

### 2.1 Equations

| Loss term | Equation |
|---|---|
| V–I overlap | P_sw = ½ · V_bus · I_pk · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw |
| Output capacitance | P_oss = Q_oss · V_bus · f_sw |
| Reverse recovery | P_rr = Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw |
| Dead-time diode conduction | P_dt = V_SD · I_pk · 2 t_dead · f_sw |
| Gate drive (from the gate-driver supply, not the FET) | P_g = 6 · Q_g · V_gs · f_sw |

### 2.2 Numbers

| Term | Calculation | Watts |
|---|---|---|
| P_sw | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 33e-9 × 48e3 | 1.60 |
| P_oss | 45e-9 × 25.2 × 48e3 | 0.054 |
| P_rr | 30e-9 × 25.2 × 48e3 | 0.036 |
| P_dt | 0.8 × 80 × 400e-9 × 48e3 | 1.23 |
| P_g (gate driver) | 6 × 55e-9 × 10 × 48e3 | 0.16 |
| **Total** | | **≈ 3.1 W** |

**Two design notes:**
- Dead-time loss is about as large as overlap loss. Trim t_dead to 100 ns if your gate driver and layout allow it; this saves about 0.6 W per ESC.
- 48 kHz roughly doubles switching loss versus 24 kHz (about 1.5 W per ESC). The benefit is lower current ripple and smoother low-RPM control. AM32 supports 48 kHz.

### 2.3 Ripple check

For a typical 5" motor (L_phase-phase ≈ 25 µH), the worst case is at D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{25\text{e-}6 \times 48\text{e}3} \approx 5.3\ \text{A}_{pp}$$

This is acceptable. Ripple adds less than 1% to the RMS conduction loss.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Total dissipation at burst

- Per ESC: 17.7 + 3.1 ≈ **20.8 W**
- All four motors at burst: **≈ 83 W**
- Adding H743, regulators, and gate drivers (≈ 1.5 W): **≈ 85 W**

### 3.2 Steady-state network

The thermal path is junction → case → PCB → air.

| Element | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| R_θJC (3.3×3.3 DFN) | ≈ 1.2 °C/W | per FET |
| Effective junction-to-board (parallel FETs) | ≈ 0.6 °C/W | 2 FETs share the load at a time |
| R_θBA, 20×20 board | ≈ 6 °C/W | ~1 m/s prop-wash convection, ~7 cm² both sides |
| R_θBA, 30×30 board | ≈ 3 °C/W | same conditions |

Steady-state board temperature rise at 83 W on a 20×20 board would be **≈ 500 °C**. Continuous 80 A is impossible on this board.

### 3.3 Transient (burst) model

Treat the board as a lumped heat capacity:

$$C_{th} = \sum m_i c_i \approx (2.2\,\text{g FR4} \times 1.1) + (2.5\,\text{g Cu} \times 0.385) + (\text{FETs/solder} \approx 0.5) \approx 3.9\ \text{J/K}$$

$$T_{board}(t) = T_0 + P R_{θBA}\left(1 - e^{-t/τ}\right), \qquad τ = R_{θBA} C_{th} \approx 23\ \text{s}$$

$$T_j = T_{board} + P_{FET} \cdot Z_{θJC}(t)$$

Assume T_0 = 50 °C at hover, a T_j limit of 150 °C, and a 25 °C margin. That leaves a usable board rise of about **75 °C**, minus the junction offset (≈ 15 °C at 10 W/FET × 1.2 × duty), so about **60 °C** of budget.

Near-adiabatic burst duration:

$$t_{burst} \approx \frac{C_{th}\,ΔT}{P} = \frac{3.9 \times 60}{83} \approx 2.8\ \text{s}$$

This is all four motors at 80 A simultaneously. A single motor at 80 A lasts about 10 s.
