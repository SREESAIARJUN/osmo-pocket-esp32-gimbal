## 1. Electrical Operating Model

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, exactly two phase legs carry the motor current at any instant.

- **Active leg (PWM-switched):** the high-side conducts for fraction D of each PWM period. The low-side conducts for (1−D) as the synchronous rectifier.
- **Return leg:** the low-side conducts continuously during the 60° commutation sector.

Every MOSFET is in conduction for 1/3 of each electrical cycle (120°).

The **instantaneous** conduction path therefore always contains two R_DS(on) in series. This holds regardless of D, because the synchronous rectifier replaces the high-side during the off-time.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At the design junction temperature T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{DS(on)}(125) = 0.90 \times 1.50 = 1.35\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 2.2 Per-channel conduction loss

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = 80^2 \times 2.70\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{17.3\ W}$$

### 2.3 Per-device loss

Average per FET, since each conducts for 1/3 of the time and the path holds 2 of the 6 FETs:

$$\bar P_{FET} = \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6}\times 2 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; \bar P_{FET} = \frac{17.3}{6} = \mathbf{2.88\ W}$$

Instantaneous loss while conducting:

$$P_{FET,inst} = I^2 R_{DS(on)} = \mathbf{8.64\ W}$$

### 2.4 Copper and shunt losses (often neglected, but they matter)

| Element | R | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase current shunt (0.25 mΩ, if per-channel) | 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| PCB phase trace (2 oz ×4 layers, ~0.3 mΩ) | 0.3 mΩ | 1.9 W |
| Battery pad → bus (shared, ~0.2 mΩ at 320 A total) | 0.2 mΩ | 20 W (all four channels at 80 A) |

> The shared bus copper term grows with the square of total current. Four channels at 80 A gives 320 A through the battery entry. This is the strongest argument that "80 A ×4 simultaneous" must not appear on the datasheet. Specify a **total** burst limit as well, for example 200 A.

### 2.5 Self-consistent temperature iteration

Losses depend on T_j and T_j depends on losses. Iterate to convergence:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_a + P\big(T_j^{(k)}\big)\cdot Z_{th}(t)$$

This typically converges in 3–4 iterations. If it fails to converge, the system is in **thermal runaway** at that operating point.

## 3. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

All losses are computed per active (switching) leg. Only one leg per channel switches at a time.

### 3.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap loss

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = \tfrac12(25.2)(80)(40\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

- t_r and t_f are set by the gate resistor. Assumed values: R_G ≈ 4.7 Ω, t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 20 ns.
- You can estimate them from the driver current and Q_gd: $t \approx Q_{gd}/I_{G}$.

### 3.2 Reverse recovery

Recovery of the synchronous FET body diode during dead time:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 60\,\text{nC}\times25.2\times48\,\text{kHz} = 0.073\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{oss} = 2\cdot\tfrac12 C_{oss(er)} V^2 f_{sw} = 2\cdot\tfrac12(2\,\text{nF})(635)(48\,\text{kHz}) = 0.061\ \text{W}$$
