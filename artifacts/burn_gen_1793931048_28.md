# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC Stack: Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Budget, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Timer/DMA Plan

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Design Flags

I have no stored context for "Job #46," so I have rebuilt it from first principles. Every component value below is a stated assumption. Swap in your actual BOM parameters and the equations still apply.

**Architecture assumed.** The STM32H743 is the **flight controller** MCU. A 4-in-1 ESC does **not** run on the H743. Each motor channel has its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, plus a gate driver and six MOSFETs. ArduPilot on the H743 generates bidirectional DShot600 and decodes eRPM telemetry. Section 3 covers this.

| Parameter | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max (22.2 V nominal) |
| Burst current | 80 A **per motor**, 5 s burst |
| PWM frequency | f_sw = 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous) PWM, 6-step trapezoidal |
| MOSFET (×24) | 40 V, PDFN 3.3×3.3 class: R_ds(on),25 = 1.0 mΩ, Q_g = 50 nC, Q_rr = 30 nC, Q_oss = 40 nC, V_SD = 0.8 V, R_θJC = 1.2 K/W |
| Gate drive | V_GS = 10 V, t_r + t_f (effective V-I overlap) = 30 ns, dead time t_dt = 100 ns |

> **Design flag 1. Total current.** 4 × 80 A = **320 A** of combined burst current. On a "nano" footprint (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) this is not physically credible: battery pads, current shunt, and copper planes would dominate losses (§1.5). The math below uses the 30.5 mm format. Even there, the "80 A" figure is only valid as a short burst. Treat it as a single-motor rating, not four motors at once.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \mathrm{K^{-1}}$$

At the design point T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{ds}(110) = 1.0\,(1 + 0.005\cdot85) = 1.425\ \mathrm{m\Omega}$$

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step complementary PWM

At any instant, two phases are energized and one floats. The current always flows through exactly **two** MOSFET channels:

- the static low-side FET (or high-side, depending on the scheme), and
- either the PWM high-side FET (for duty D) **or** its complementary low-side FET (for 1−D).

Synchronous rectification means the 1−D interval also uses an FET channel, not the body diode, except during dead time. The total conduction loss per motor is therefore independent of duty:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.425\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{18.24\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Over one electrical revolution, each of the 6 FETs carries its share. The average loss per FET is:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} = \frac{18.24}{6} = 3.04\ \mathrm{W}$$

During any 60° sector, the instantaneous worst case is the static FET, which carries the full I² R:

$$P_{inst} = 80^2 \cdot 1.425\,\mathrm{m\Omega} = 9.12\ \mathrm{W}\ \text{for } 120^\circ$$

This matters for junction ripple at low eRPM (§2.4).

### 1.4 RMS form, for parameterized use

For a single FET in a 120° conduction scheme:

$$I_{rms,static} = I\sqrt{1/3}, \quad I_{rms,PWM-HS} = I\sqrt{D/3}, \quad I_{rms,sync-LS} = I\sqrt{(1-D)/3}$$

Summing over the three roles reproduces 2I²R.

### 1.5 Board-level parasitic conduction losses

These are frequently ignored, and they matter at 320 A.

| Path | Resistance | Current | Loss |
|---|---|---|---|
| Battery pads → bus plane (×2 polarity) | 0.15 mΩ total | 320 A | 15.4 W |
| Current shunt (single, total-current) | 0.1 mΩ | 320 A | 10.2 W |
| Phase copper, FET → motor pad (per motor, 2 legs active) | 0.2 mΩ | 80 A | 1.28 W ×4 = 5.1 W |
| **Subtotal** | | | **≈ 30.7 W** |

> **Design flag 2. Current sensing.** A typical 0.25 mΩ shunt would dissipate **25.6 W** at 320 A. Either use a 0.1 mΩ shunt with a 50 V/V gain amplifier (INA186-class), or use per-motor phase-current estimation from the ESC MCU.
