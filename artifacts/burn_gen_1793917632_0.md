# STM32H743 Nano AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC): 80 A Burst Electro-Thermal Model, 48 kHz Switching Model, and ArduPilot Bidirectional DShot600 Allocation

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Constraints

**The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In any ArduPilot-compatible 4-in-1 design, the system is partitioned as follows:

- **H743 (FC):** Runs ArduPilot and generates DShot600 frames. It captures the bidirectional eRPM telemetry.
- **4× ESC MCUs:** Each runs AM32 or BLHeli_32 and generates the 48 kHz PWM. Suitable parts are the AT32F421 or STM32G071.
- **4× three-phase gate drivers:** FD6288Q class.
- **24× N-MOSFETs:** One three-phase bridge per motor.

**Feasibility warning.** The model below shows that 80 A per channel on a 20×20 / 25.5×25.5 mm board is a **sub-2-second adiabatic burst rating**, not a sustainable operating point. The continuous rating under prop wash is roughly 15–25 A per channel. The derivation follows so the burst rating can be specified defensibly.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification (AM32/BLHeli_32 default), two MOSFETs carry phase current I at every instant:

- the PWM leg's high-side switch during D, or its low-side switch (synchronous rectification) during 1−D;
- the static low-side switch of the return leg.

So regardless of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + I_{ph}^2 R_{pcb}$$

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right]$$

At T_j = 100 °C:

$$R = 1.5\,(1 + 0.0045 \cdot 75) = 2.006\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| Term | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| MOSFET conduction | 2 · 80² · 2.006 mΩ | **25.7 W** |
| PCB copper | 80² · 0.5 mΩ | **3.2 W** |
| **Conduction per channel** | | **28.9 W** |

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

At normal electrical frequencies, each of the six FETs conducts about 1/3 of each electrical cycle. The average loss per FET is about 25.7 / 6 ≈ 4.3 W.

The junction-to-case rise is therefore

$$\Delta T_{JC} \approx 4.3 \times 1.5 \approx 6.5\ \text{K}$$

This is small. The binding constraint is the **board bulk temperature**, not the die.

**Exception: stall or low eRPM.** If the rotor is stalled, one FET pair can carry 12.85 W for an extended time, giving ΔT_JC ≈ 19 K plus spreading resistance. The ESC firmware must enforce a stall current limit.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the PWM leg switches. One hard-switching transition pair occurs per PWM period.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f I_{ph} \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 100\text{ns}\cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

### 2.4 Output capacitance (both FETs of the leg)

$$P_{oss} = 2 \cdot \tfrac12 C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FETs.

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\cdot 40\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.04\ W}$$

### 2.6 Total switching-related loss

**About 2.4 W per channel.** Switching is roughly 8% of conduction loss at 80 A.

Raising f_sw from 24 kHz to 48 kHz costs about 1.2 W per channel. That is negligible at burst, but it matters more at hover, where conduction loss is small.
