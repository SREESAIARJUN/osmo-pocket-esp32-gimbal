# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Engineering Design Package (Job #9)

## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Assumptions

These points affect every calculation that follows.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In a production 4-in-1 stack, each ESC channel has its own MCU (for example AT32F421 or GD32E230 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM and drives a 3-phase gate driver. The H743 runs ArduPilot, sends bidirectional DShot600, and decodes the eRPM telemetry. Switching losses are therefore a property of the ESC power stage. The DMA problem belongs to the H743.
2. **"80A burst" is interpreted as 80 A per channel**, which is 320 A aggregate. This is a short-duration rating. On a 20×20 mm "nano" footprint it is thermally limited to seconds, not minutes (see §3.4). If marketing intends "80A continuous," the design cannot meet it.
3. **Reference component values** are representative and must be replaced with datasheet values for the selected parts:

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 100 °C: R_ds = 1.2 × 1.45 = **1.74 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-step / trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification

At any instant, two phases conduct. During the PWM on-time (duty D), current flows through high-side (HS) FET A and low-side (LS) FET B. During the off-time, the complementary LS FET A freewheels instead of the body diode. As a result, two channel resistances are always in series with phase current I:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2(80)^2(1.74\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{22.3\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-device distribution

Each leg conducts for 2/3 of the electrical period. It is high-side driven for 1/3 and low-side for 1/3.

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,I^2 R_{ds}\,D \qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,I^2 R_{ds}\,(2 - D)$$
