```
IMU/fusion     8 kHz : dual IMU read → notch/LPF → fusion → attitude (Mahony, 1–2 kHz ok)
Angle loop     1 kHz : attitude error → ω*
```

Target bandwidths:

| Loop | Bandwidth |
|---|---|

| Velocity | ~150–300 Hz |

## 1. Field-Oriented Control Equations

### 1.1 Clarke Transform (amplitude-invariant)

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \frac{1}{\sqrt3}(i_a + 2 i_b)\quad(\text{using } i_a+i_b+i_c=0)
$$

### 1.2 Park Transform

The electrical angle is θₑ = p·θₘ − θ_offset.

$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix}=
\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}
$$

### 1.3 PMSM Model in dq Frame

$$
v_d = R i_d + L\frac{di_d}{dt} - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad
v_q = R i_q + L\frac{di_q}{dt} + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e \lambda_m
$$

The torque equation is:

$$
\tau = \tfrac32 p \lambda_m i_q = K_t\, i_q
$$

### 1.4 Current PI Controller with Decoupling and Back-EMF Feedforward

The PI gains place a pole-zero cancellation at R/L:

$$
K_{p,i}= L\,\omega_{ci},\qquad K_{i,i}= R\,\omega_{ci}
$$

This gives a closed-loop transfer function of $\frac{\omega_{ci}}{s+\omega_{ci}}$.

The voltage commands, with decoupling and back-EMF terms added, are:

$$
v_d^* = PI_d(i_d^*-i_d) - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad
v_q^* = PI_q(i_q^*-i_q) + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e\lambda_m
$$

**Voltage limit.** The voltage vector is clamped to the linear SVPWM circle, with d-axis priority:

$$
|v| \le V_{bus}/\sqrt3
$$

**Discrete PI with back-calculation anti-windup** (forward Euler, sample period T):

$$
u_k = K_p e_k + I_k,\quad u^{sat}_k=\text{sat}(u_k),\quad
I_{k+1} = I_k + K_i T e_k + K_{aw}(u^{sat}_k-u_k),\quad K_{aw}\approx \frac{K_i}{K_p}T
$$

> **Gimbal note:** With R ≈ 10 Ω and low currents, *voltage-mode* FOC (v_q = R·i_q* + ω_e·λ_m, no current loop) is a common, valid simplification. The code supports both modes via `use_current_loop`.

### 1.5 Inverse Park and SVPWM (Min-Max Zero-Sequence Injection)

The inverse Park transform is:

$$
v_\alpha = v_d\cos\theta_e - v_q\sin\theta_e,\quad v_\beta = v_d\sin\theta_e + v_q\cos\theta_e
$$

The phase voltages are:

$$
v_a=v_\alpha,\; v_b=-\tfrac12 v_\alpha+\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}v_\beta,\; v_c=-\tfrac12 v_\alpha-\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}v_\beta
$$

The zero-sequence offset and duty cycles are:

$$
v_0 = -\tfrac12(\max(v_{abc})+\min(v_{abc})),\qquad D_x = \tfrac12 + \frac{v_x+v_0}{V_{bus}}
$$

This is mathematically equivalent to 7-segment SVPWM and gives a 15.5% higher linear range than sine PWM.

### 1.6 Encoder Velocity Estimation (Type-II PLL)

Differentiating encoder counts directly produces quantization noise of 2π/16384 rad per tick. A type-II PLL avoids this:

$$
e_k=\text{wrap}_\pi(\theta_{meas}-\hat\theta_k),\quad
\hat\omega_{k+1}=\hat\omega_k + K_i T e_k,\quad
\hat\theta_{k+1}=\hat\theta_k + T(\hat\omega_{k} + K_p e_k)
$$

The gains are K_p = 2ζω_n and K_i = ω_n², with ζ = 1 and ω_n ≈ 2π·400 rad/s or higher (it must exceed the velocity loop bandwidth).
