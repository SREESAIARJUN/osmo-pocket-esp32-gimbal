# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #6): Architecture & Design Model

## 0. Corrections to the Brief

Four parts of the brief need correcting before the design work starts.

1. **The STM32H743 cannot be the ESC controller.** It is too large and costly, and AM32 and BLHeli_32 firmware do not target it.
   - The correct architecture is an H743 flight controller running ArduPilot.
   - It drives four ESC MCUs. I use the AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 here.
   - The bi-directional DShot DMA analysis applies to the H743. The 48 kHz switching analysis applies to the ESC side.
2. **"Nano" form factor and 80 A burst conflict.**
   - On a 20×20 mm board, 80 A per channel is not thermally survivable beyond a fraction of a second. Section 3.5 shows the math.
   - This model assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm stack, 8-layer, 2 oz copper** with a separate ESC board. The 20×20 numbers are shown for contrast.
3. **The 80 A "burst" duration is unspecified.** I derive the allowable burst time instead of assuming it.
4. **Component values are representative.** Final values must come from the selected MOSFET datasheets and must be validated by thermal imaging. The netlist is a reference topology, not a fab-ready output.

## 1. System Architecture

```
 6S LiPo (25.2 V max)
   │
   ├──► ESC board: 4× [AT32F421 (AM32) → FD6288Q 3-phase driver → 6× N-MOSFET]
   │        ▲  bidir DShot600 (1 wire per motor, open-drain-like, pulled up)
   │        │  shunt current sense → FC ADC
   │
   └──► FC board: BEC 5 V/3 A, 3.3 V LDO → STM32H743VIT6 (400 MHz rev Y / 480 MHz rev V)
            ArduPilot, IMU (SPI1), Baro (I2C2), OSD (SPI2), SD (SDMMC1)
            Motor outputs on TIM3/TIM5 with DMAMUX → DMA1/DMA2 streams
```

The two boards connect through an 8-pin JST-SH:

- VBAT
- GND
- CURR
- TLM
- M1, M2, M3, M4

### 2.2 Conduction Losses

Commutation is 6-step trapezoidal with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM). At any instant, exactly one high-side and one low-side path carries the phase current.

- The PWM'd leg alternates between HS and LS.
- The return leg's LS is on continuously.
- So **two R_DS(on) are always in series** with I:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right]$$

Because R depends on T_j, the model must be solved self-consistently:

$$T_j = T_{PCB} + P_{FET}\,(R_{\theta JC}+R_{\theta CB})$$

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond (channel, both FETs) | Per FET (worst) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W | 6.4 W |
| 100 °C | 1.41 mΩ | 18.1 W | 9.0 W |
| 125 °C | 1.55 mΩ | 19.8 W | 9.9 W |

The worst-case FET is the continuously-on return LS, which sees the full I²R at 100 % duty.

### 2.3 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg hard-switches. The HS turns on and off once per period.

**Transition times** are set by the gate current through the Miller plateau:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}(R_{drv,on}+R_g)}{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}, \quad t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}(R_{drv,off}+R_g)}{V_{pl}}$$

With R_drv,on ≈ 10 Ω, R_drv,off ≈ 5 Ω, R_g = 4.7 Ω (internal plus external), and V_drv = 12 V:

- t_r ≈ 8 nC × 14.7 Ω / 9 V ≈ **13 ns**
- t_f ≈ 8 nC × 9.7 Ω / 3 V ≈ **26 ns**

The turn-off time dominates, so the sink path should be strengthened (anti-parallel diode on R_g).

**V–I overlap loss:**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 39\text{ ns} × 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.89\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery** (LS body diode during dead time, then HS turn-on):

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{ nC} × 25.2 × 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two edges per period):

$$P_{dt} = V_f\,I\,(2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 × 80 × 200\text{ ns} × 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**Output capacitance:** with Q_oss ≈ 30 nC,

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} ≈ 0.02\text{ W}$$

**Gate drive** (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g):

$$P_g = Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} × 2 = 45\text{ nC} × 12 × 48\text{ k} × 2 = 0.05\text{ W}$$

### 2.4 Total per Channel at 80 A, T_j ≈ 110 °C

| Loss term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (2 × I²R at 1.47 mΩ) | 18.8 |
| Switching overlap | 1.89 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss + gate | 0.12 |
| Phase copper (≈ 0.15 mΩ trace + pad) | 0.96 |
| **Channel total** | **≈ 22.4 W** |
| **4-in-1 total (all motors at 80 A)** | **≈ 90 W** |

Efficiency at this point: P_out ≈ 25.2 V × 80 A × 4 ≈ 8 kW, so η ≈ 98.9 %. The efficiency is high, but the dissipation density is the actual problem.

### 2.5 Frequency Trade-off

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial f_{sw}} ≈ \frac{1.89+0.61+0.12}{48\text{k}} ≈ 55\ \mu W/\text{Hz}$$

- Going from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves ≈ 1.3 W per channel.
- Conduction dominates (≈ 85 %), so **R_DS(on) and copper area are the primary levers**, not f_sw.
- 48 kHz is justified for low-inductance motors (ripple current $\Delta I = V_{bus}D(1-D)/(L f_{sw})$). For L = 15 µH at D = 0.5, ΔI ≈ 8.75 A pk-pk.
