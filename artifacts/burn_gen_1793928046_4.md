# 3-Axis Gimbal: FOC Control, Anti-Cogging Feedforward, and Dual-IMU Vibration Rejection

**Assumptions.** I have no specification for "Job #25", so this design uses typical direct-drive gimbal parameters:

- Motors: GBM-class 12N14P motors (7 pole pairs).
- Position sensing: 14-bit magnetic encoder per axis (AS5048A or MA730).
- Drive: 3-phase driver with 2 low-side shunts.
- Controller: STM32G4/H7-class MCU with an FPU.
- IMUs: two ICM-42688-P on the camera plate, sharing a clock (CLKIN), on separate SPI buses.

Every symbolic result below holds for any motor. Replace the numbers in `Config` with values from your system identification.

## 1. Loop Architecture and Timing

| Loop | Rate | Bandwidth target | Feedback |
|---|---|---|---|
| Current (FOC, d/q PI) | 20 kHz (PWM-synchronous) | 1.5–2 kHz | Phase shunts + encoder θₑ |
| Velocity (PI) | 4 kHz | 150–250 Hz | **Gyro rate** (inertial), projected onto the joint axis |
| Angle (P + rate FF) | 1 kHz | 20–40 Hz | Attitude estimate (dual-IMU AHRS) |
| IMU sampling | 8 kHz ODR, both IMUs | Hardware AAF ≈ 1 kHz | — |

Keep a bandwidth separation of at least 5–10× between adjacent loops. With that separation, each inner loop can be treated as unity gain when tuning the outer loop.

The velocity loop uses the **gyro**, not the encoder, as feedback. The encoder measures the angle of the joint relative to the base. The goal is camera stabilization in inertial space, so base disturbances must appear inside the rate loop. The encoder is used only for commutation, the cogging phase, and the Jacobian.

## 2. FOC Current Loop

### 2.1 Electrical angle

$$\theta_e = p\,(\theta_m - \theta_{\text{off}}) \bmod 2\pi$$

Here *p* is the number of pole pairs and θ_off is the encoder offset, found by forcing d-axis alignment.

### 2.2 Clarke and Park transforms (amplitude-invariant)

$$i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}(i_a + 2i_b),\qquad (i_c=-i_a-i_b)$$

$$\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}$$

### 2.3 Plant model and PI design

The d/q voltage equations are:

$$v_d = R i_d + L_d \dot i_d - \omega_e L_q i_q,\qquad v_q = R i_q + L_q \dot i_q + \omega_e L_d i_d + \omega_e \lambda_m$$

With decoupling feedforward applied, each axis becomes the first-order plant $G(s) = 1/(Ls+R)$. Choose the PI zero to cancel the electrical pole, which gives a closed loop of $\omega_c/(s+\omega_c)$:

$$K_p = L\,\omega_c,\qquad K_i = R\,\omega_c\qquad(\omega_c = 2\pi f_c)$$

Decoupling and back-EMF feedforward terms:

$$v_d^{ff} = -\omega_e L_q i_q,\qquad v_q^{ff} = \omega_e (L_d i_d + \lambda_m)$$

### 2.4 Voltage limiting and anti-windup

1. Limit the voltage vector to the inscribed SVPWM circle, $|v| \le V_{bus}/\sqrt3$. Give d priority:
   $$v_d^{sat} = \text{clip}(v_d, \pm V_{max}),\qquad v_q^{max}=\sqrt{V_{max}^2 - v_d^{sat\,2}}$$
2. Use back-calculation anti-windup, with $K_b \approx K_i/K_p$:
   $$\dot I = K_i e + K_b\,(u_{sat}-u)$$

### 2.5 Inverse Park and SVPWM (min-max zero-sequence injection)

$$v_\alpha = v_d\cos\theta_e - v_q\sin\theta_e,\qquad v_\beta = v_d\sin\theta_e + v_q\cos\theta_e$$

$$v_a=v_\alpha,\quad v_b=-\tfrac12 v_\alpha+\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}v_\beta,\quad v_c=-\tfrac12 v_\alpha-\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}v_\beta$$

$$v_0 = -\tfrac12\left(\max(v_{abc})+\min(v_{abc})\right),\qquad D_x = \tfrac12 + \frac{v_x+v_0}{V_{bus}}$$

### 2.6 Delay compensation

PWM update and sampling add about 1.5 Tₛ of delay. Advance the angle used for the inverse Park:

$$\theta_e' = \theta_e + 1.5\,\omega_e T_s$$

## 3. Cascaded Velocity and Angle Control

### 3.1 Mechanical plant (per axis)

$$J\dot\omega = K_t i_q - B\omega - \tau_{cog}(\theta_m) - \tau_{dist}$$

$$K_t = \tfrac32 p \lambda_m$$

### 3.2 Gimbal kinematics

The camera's inertial angular velocity, expressed in the camera frame, is:

$$\boldsymbol\omega_{cam} = R_{cb}(\mathbf q_j)\,\boldsymbol\omega_{base} + \mathbf J(\mathbf q_j)\,\dot{\mathbf q}_j$$

For a yaw–roll–pitch stack, joint *k* only drives the camera-rate component along its own axis $\hat{\mathbf z}_k$, expressed in the camera frame. The rate feedback for joint *k* is therefore:

$$\omega_k^{fb} = \hat{\mathbf z}_k^{cam}(\mathbf q_j)^\top\,\boldsymbol\omega_{cam}^{gyro}$$

When the joints are near their nominal angles, $\mathbf J \approx I$. Near gimbal lock (roll ≈ ±90°), use the damped pseudo-inverse $\mathbf J^\top(\mathbf J\mathbf J^\top+\lambda^2 I)^{-1}$ to map camera-rate commands to joint commands.
