## 0. Architectural Corrections and Assumptions

Several points need to be fixed up front, because they change the math.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** A 48 kHz, 6-step or FOC drive needs a dedicated ESC MCU per motor. Driving 24 FETs with dead-time insertion and BEMF sensing from the FC would conflict with ArduPilot's scheduler.
   - **Architecture:** STM32H743VIT6 (FC, ArduPilot) → bidirectional DShot600 → 4× AT32F421K8U7 (AM32 firmware) → 4× 3-phase gate driver → 24 N-MOSFETs.
2. **"80 A burst" is per-motor phase current.** Four motors at 80 A and duty ≈ 0.9 means about **290 A of battery current.** A nano (20×20 mm mounting, ~26×26 mm outline) board cannot sustain this. It is a **transient rating of roughly 1–1.5 s**, shown below. Treat it as a burst spec, never a continuous one.
3. **Battery:** 6S, V_bus = 25.2 V (full), 22.2 V nominal. The worst-case switching analysis uses 25.2 V.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_J)=R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_J-25)]$$

At T_J = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.2 mΩ × 1.45 = **1.74 mΩ** (use 1.8 mΩ with board-level spread).

### 1.2 Current paths (6-step, complementary PWM, duty D)

In any commutation sector, one high-side (HS) FET and one low-side (LS) FET carry the phase current I. During the PWM off-time, the LS of the switched leg conducts synchronously. So **exactly two channels are always in series with I:**

$$P_{cond,motor}=2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_J)$$

Per-device split over an electrical cycle (each leg is active for 2/3 of the cycle, 1/3 as HS-active and 1/3 as LS-active):

$$P_{HS}=\tfrac{1}{3}D\,I^2R_{DS},\qquad P_{LS}=\tfrac{1}{3}(2-D)\,I^2R_{DS}$$

The LS dissipates more at partial throttle. That is a layout-relevant asymmetry: give the LS FETs the better copper.
