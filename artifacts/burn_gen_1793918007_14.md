## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Feasibility Flags

Please read this section before the math. Two findings change the scope of the job.

### Flag 1: The H743 does not commutate the motors

In every production 4-in-1 design, the STM32H743 is the flight controller only. It talks DShot to four ESC MCUs, and those MCUs run the commutation firmware (AM32 or BLHeli_32), typically on AT32F421 or STM32G071 parts. Driving 24 MOSFETs at 48 kHz with sensorless BEMF observers directly from the FC MCU would rule out ArduPilot entirely. The architecture modeled here is:

```
H743 (ArduPilot) ──DShot600 bidir ×4──► 4× AT32F421 (AM32) ──► 4× FD6288Q ──► 24× N-MOSFET
```

### Flag 2: "80A burst" on a nano form factor is thermally marginal

Section 3 shows that 80 A per channel on a 20×20 mm board produces roughly 110 W of total loss. Bounded by junction temperature, the burst lasts about 4 s from a 40 °C start. Recommendations:

- Rate the board at 80 A for ≤2 s and 45–50 A continuous.
- Alternatively, move to a 30.5×30.5 mm footprint.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation-state model

In six-step trapezoidal drive, one high-side (HS) switch and one low-side (LS) switch conduct the phase current I at any instant. With complementary PWM at duty D, the conduction is shared as follows:

- **Active leg:** the HS conducts for a fraction D of each period. The LS of the same leg synchronously rectifies for 1 − D.
- **Return leg:** its LS conducts continuously.

Ignoring dead time (accounted for in §2), the energy per PWM period gives:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\left[D + (1-D) + 1\right] = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

**Key result:** conduction loss is independent of duty under synchronous rectification. Two device-resistances are always in the current path.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$
R_{ds} = 1.2 \times 1.5 = 1.8\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

### 1.3 Results at 80 A

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per channel | Per active FET (2 conducting) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.2 mΩ | 2 · 6400 · 1.2e-3 = **15.4 W** | 7.7 W |
| 125 °C | 1.8 mΩ | 2 · 6400 · 1.8e-3 = **23.0 W** | 11.5 W |

### 1.4 Electrothermal feedback

R_ds rises with temperature, so the operating point is the fixed point of:

$$
T_j = T_{board} + R_{\theta JC}\cdot P_{FET}(T_j)
$$

Expanding:

$$
T_j = T_b + R_{\theta JC} I^2 R_{25}\left[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\right]
$$

$$
\Rightarrow\; T_j = \frac{T_b + k(1-25\alpha)}{1 - k\alpha}, \qquad k = R_{\theta JC} I^2 R_{25}
$$

Plugging in values: k = 1.5 · 6400 · 1.2e-3 = 11.52 K, so kα = 0.058, which is well below 1. **There is no thermal runaway at the device level.** The gradient across the device is ΔT_jc ≈ 12–17 K above the board.

### 1.5 Copper path loss

The current path per channel is:

```
battery pad → bus plane → HS → phase pad → motor → phase pad → LS → shunt → GND plane
```

| Contributor | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Planes and vias (2 oz, nano geometry) | ≈ 0.35 mΩ |
| Current-sense shunt | 0.25 mΩ (if per-channel; often only one shared shunt) |
| **Total R_Cu (per channel)** | ≈ 0.5 mΩ |

$$
P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.5\text{e-}3 = 3.2\ \text{W per channel}
$$
