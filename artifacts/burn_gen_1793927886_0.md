# Job #55: STM32H743 AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC), 80 A Burst Design Package

## 0. Architectural Correction

**The STM32H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC.** ArduPilot runs on the H743 and emits bi-directional DShot600. Each motor channel needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical choices are the AT32F421 or STM32G071. No production ESC firmware drives 12 FET gates directly from an H743 alongside ArduPilot. The architecture below is therefore:

```
H743 (ArduPilot) ──DShot600 bidir ×4──► 4× AT32F421 (AM32) ──► 4× 3-phase driver ──► 24 MOSFETs
```

**Second flag: "80 A burst" on a nano (≤30×30 mm) board is a marketing-class number.** The math below shows it is survivable for about 2–3 s on one or two channels, not for 4×80 A sustained. That limit should be stated on the datasheet.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

**Current paths.** Under 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM, two phases conduct at any instant:

- **PWM-on interval:** the high-side FET of phase A and the low-side FET of phase B conduct.
- **PWM-off interval:** the low-side FET of phase A and the low-side FET of phase B conduct (synchronous freewheel).

Either way, there are always **two R_DS(on) in series** with the phase current.

**Ripple correction.** Ripple adds to the RMS current:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2\left(1+\frac{r^2}{12}\right),\quad r=\Delta I/I$$

At a typical r ≈ 0.2, the correction factor is 1.003, so it is negligible here.

**Conduction loss per ESC channel:**

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{hot} = 2(80)^2(1.8\text{ m}\Omega) = \mathbf{23.0\ W}$$

**Temperature dependence.** Use a self-consistent R(T):

$$R(T_j)=R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\quad \alpha\approx0.004/°C$$

At T_j = 125 °C this gives R = 1.2 × 1.4 = 1.68 mΩ. Using 1.8 mΩ is a conservative datasheet worst case.

**Per-FET distribution.**

- Within one 60° sector, the non-PWM low-side FET carries the full 80 A continuously. That is 80² × 1.8 mΩ = **11.5 W instantaneous**.
- Commutation rotates this duty across all 6 FETs every electrical cycle. At burst RPM, the electrical frequency is about 1–3 kHz. That is fast compared with the die thermal time constant (roughly 5–20 ms).
- Each die therefore sees the averaged loss: **P_FET,avg ≈ 23.0 / 6 ≈ 3.8 W**.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd phase leg hard-switches.

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = 0.5·25.2·80·35 ns·48 kHz | **1.69 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·100 ns·48 kHz | **0.31 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 40 nC·25.2·48 kHz | **0.05 W** |
| C_oss energy | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw | **0.03 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw | 0.06 W (dissipated mostly in driver/R_g) |
| **Total P_sw per channel** | | **≈ 2.1 W** |

**Per-channel total:** P_ch ≈ 23.0 + 2.1 = **25.1 W**. This is about 1.2 % of the 2016 W delivered, i.e. ~98.8 % stage efficiency.

**Four channels at 80 A simultaneously:** about **100 W** total.

**Frequency trade.** Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it falls to about 1.05 W, but motor current ripple rises. 48 kHz is fine because conduction loss dominates by 11:1. **Optimise R_DS(on) first, not t_sw.**

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Junction-over-board rise (quasi-steady at die scale)

**Commutation-averaged case:**

$$\Delta T_{jb} = P_{FET,avg}\cdot R_{jb} = (3.8 + 0.35)\times 3.5 \approx \mathbf{15\ °C}$$

The 0.35 W is the switching loss share assigned to the switching FETs.

**Worst case: stalled or low-RPM motor.** A long dwell in one sector means one FET carries 11.5 W:

$$\Delta T_{jb} = 11.5\times3.5 = \mathbf{40\ °C}$$

This is why AM32 stall protection and low-RPM current limiting must be enabled.
