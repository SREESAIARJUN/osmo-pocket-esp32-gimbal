### Feasibility findings (read first)

1. **Architecture.** The H743 runs ArduPilot and emits DShot. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU, or one MCU per pair. "H743 4-in-1" means an FC/ESC stack or an AIO board, not H743-driven commutation.
2. **"Nano" form factor.** On a 20 × 20 mm footprint, 80 A per channel is not physically supportable. There is not enough copper cross-section, FET area or thermal mass, as shown in §3. The analysis below assumes 30.5 × 30.5 mm.
3. **Burst duration.** 80 A × 4 channels is only survivable for **a few seconds**, even with prop-wash airflow. A credible rating is roughly 40–50 A continuous with airflow and 80 A for ≤ 2–5 s.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in six-step with complementary PWM

In each 60° commutation sector, two phases conduct and one floats:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side (HS) FET conducts for fraction D; the low-side (LS) FET synchronously rectifies for (1 − D).
- **Static leg:** the low-side FET conducts continuously for the whole sector.

The instantaneous conduction loss in a channel is therefore:

$$
P_{cond}(t) = I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}(T_j) = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

This holds independent of D, because synchronous rectification keeps exactly one PWM-leg FET on at all times. The relation to battery current is $I_{bat} \approx D \cdot I_{ph}$. At burst (D → 0.95–1.0), $I_{ph} \approx I_{bat}$.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent resistance, with feedback

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

$$
T_j = T_{board} + P_{FET}\,R_{\theta,j\text{-}b}
$$

Here $R_{\theta,j\text{-}b}$ is junction-to-board thermal resistance (die → package → solder → copper). These two equations form a positive-feedback loop. Thermal runaway occurs if $\partial P/\partial T_j \cdot R_\theta \ge 1$, that is, if

$$
I^2 R_{DS,25}\,\alpha\, R_\theta \ge 1
$$

### 1.3 Include copper and shunt resistance

Per-channel series path: FETs + PCB traces/planes + shunt + motor-pad solder joints.

| Element | Value | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| PCB copper, phase path (2 oz, ~15 mm, multi-layer, effective) | ≈ 0.4 mΩ | 2.6 W |
| Shunt (low-side DC, conducts ~always) | 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| Pads / solder / wire joints | ≈ 0.15 mΩ | 1.0 W |

Copper resistance also rises about 0.39 %/K. **Board losses at 80 A are comparable to switching losses and are often ignored.**

### 1.4 Worked numbers (80 A, using max R_DS(on))

**Single FET per position**, solving at T_j = 100 °C:

- $R_{DS}(100) = 1.5 \times (1 + 0.005 \times 75) = 2.06\ \text{m}\Omega$
- $P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.06 \times 10^{-3} = 26.4\ \text{W}$ per channel
- The static-leg LS FET alone carries 13.2 W.

**Two FETs in parallel per position** (assume 10 % current mismatch):

- $R_{eff} \approx 1.03\ \text{m}\Omega$
- $P_{cond} \approx 2 \times 6400 \times 1.03 \times 10^{-3} \times 1.1 = 14.5\ \text{W}$ per channel
- Hottest FET ≈ 3.6 W

**Runaway check** (single FET, R_θ,j-b ≈ 2 K/W):

$$
6400 \times 1.5 \times 10^{-3} \times 0.005 \times 2 = 0.096 \ll 1
$$

The loop is stable at the device level. Runaway risk lives at the **board** level (§3), not the junction.
