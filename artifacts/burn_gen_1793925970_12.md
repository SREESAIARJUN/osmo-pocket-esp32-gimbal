# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Budget, and Bidirectional DShot600 Timer/DMA Allocation

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Key Finding

**What runs where.** The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. On every production AIO design:

- **H743 (ArduPilot):** sends bidirectional DShot600 commands to four ESC MCUs and receives eRPM telemetry back.
- **ESC MCUs (×4):** typically AT32F421, STM32G071 or GD32, running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Each one runs 6-step commutation at 48 kHz PWM, driving a 3-phase gate driver and six MOSFETs.

This document models both domains.

**Key finding.** An 80 A *per-motor* burst on a 20×20 mm ("nano") board is not thermally sustainable. The model below shows it dissipates about 120 W total. The board's own thermal mass then limits the burst to about **1–1.5 s** before the copper reaches 110 °C. Section 3 quantifies this so you can set realistic ratings and ESC current limits.

**Assumed parameters.** These are representative of 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN MOSFETs. Replace them with your selected part's datasheet values.

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 25.2 V | 6S, fully charged (worst case for switching loss) |
| I_ph | 80 A | Burst phase current per motor |
| R_ds(on),25 | 1.5 mΩ | At V_gs = 10 V |
| R_ds(on) factor at T_j = 110 °C | 1.45 | So R_ds(on),Tj = 2.18 mΩ |

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model (6-Step, Synchronous Rectification)

### 1.1 Which MOSFETs conduct

In each 60° commutation sector, current flows through three MOSFETs:

- **HS_a:** the high-side MOSFET on the PWM phase, conducting for duty D.
- **LS_a:** its complementary low-side MOSFET, conducting the freewheel current for (1−D) under synchronous rectification.
- **LS_b:** the low-side MOSFET on the return phase, conducting continuously.

### 1.2 Conduction loss per ESC channel

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

The duty cycle cancels out, so conduction loss is independent of throttle at a given phase current.

### 1.3 Temperature dependence of R_ds(on)

Linear approximation, with α ≈ 0.0053 /K for this class of trench MOSFET:

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.18\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{27.9\ W\ per\ channel}
$$

### 1.4 Electrothermal feedback

Conduction loss raises T_j, which raises R_ds, which raises loss. Solve iteratively:

$$
T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}\,R_{\theta JC}, \qquad P_{FET} = f\big(R_{ds}(T_j)\big)
$$

Thermal runaway occurs if the loop gain exceeds 1:

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\cdot R_\theta = 2I^2 R_{ds,25}\,\alpha\, R_{\theta,eff} \ge 1
$$

With R_θ,eff ≈ 8 K/W (FET pad to air on a nano board):

$$
2 \times 6400 \times 1.5\text{m} \times 0.0053 \times 8 \approx 0.81
$$

**Result:** the loop is stable but marginal at 80 A. Above about 88 A the steady-state solution diverges, and only the ESC current limit protects the board.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg (HS_a/LS_a) switches per sector. LS_a turns on after the dead time, so it switches at roughly zero voltage (ZVS). All hard-switching loss lands on HS_a.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 30 ns × 48 k | 1.452 W |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss·V·f_sw = 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.048 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 30 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.036 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2·t_dt·f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 100 ns × 48 k | 0.307 W |
| Gate drive (2 FETs, mostly dissipated in the driver) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw | 0.048 W |
| **P_sw per channel** | | **≈ 1.89 W** |

**Sensitivity to switching frequency.** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. Moving from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves about 0.95 W per channel, which is under 4 % of the total. At 80 A the design is conduction-dominated, so 48 kHz is justified by its lower current ripple and quieter motor operation.

### 2.1 Other per-channel losses

**Shunt resistor** (0.25 mΩ, low-side or bus):

$$
P = 80^2 \times 0.25\text{m} = 1.6\ \text{W}
$$

**Bus capacitor ripple current.** RMS ripple peaks at D = 0.5:

$$
I_{cap,rms} = I\sqrt{D(1-D)} \to 40\ \text{A}_{rms}
$$
