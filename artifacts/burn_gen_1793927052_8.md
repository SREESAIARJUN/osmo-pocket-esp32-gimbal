# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80 A ESC: Systems Architecture Model (Job #31)

## 0. Architectural Assumptions and Red Flags

A few assumptions need to be settled before the math.

| Item | Assumption / Finding |
|---|---|
| MCU partitioning | The **STM32H743 is the flight controller**. It runs ArduPilot. Each ESC channel runs its own small MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32). The H743 does not commutate motors. |
| Bus voltage | 6S LiPo: 25.2 V full, 22.2 V nominal. Design for a 30 V transient, so 40 V MOSFETs are required. |
| "80 A burst" | Interpreted as **80 A per channel, all four channels simultaneously**, which is the worst case. |
| "Nano" form factor | Interpreted as a 30.5 × 30.5 mm stack-compatible board, 6-layer, 2 oz copper. |
| Commutation | Trapezoidal with synchronous rectification and complementary PWM at 48 kHz. |

**Red flag:** 4 × 80 A at 25 V is **about 8 kW of motor power** on a 30 × 30 mm board. The math below shows this is a **seconds-scale burst rating only**. It cannot be a continuous rating. Marketing specs for this class usually mean 80 A for about 1–3 s per channel. The model makes that limit explicit.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Current path

In 6-step trapezoidal commutation, two phases conduct at every instant:
- one high-side FET (phase A, PWM'd), and
- one low-side FET (phase B, held on).

During the PWM off-time, synchronous rectification turns on the low-side of phase A. The motor current therefore always flows through **exactly two R_ds(on) in series**, independent of duty cycle D.

Per-channel conduction loss:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2 R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right]$$

At 48 kHz with typical motor inductance (≥10 µH), ripple is small. So I_rms ≈ I_DC = 80 A.

### 2.2 Numerical evaluation (T_j = 125 °C, R_ds = 1.8 mΩ)

$$P_{cond,ch} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.8\,\text{m}\Omega = 6400 \times 3.6\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{23.0\ W}$$

### 2.3 Per-FET distribution

Each phase's high-side and low-side FET each conduct 120° of 360° electrical, which is one third of the time:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{I^2 R_{ds}}{3} = \frac{6400 \times 1.8\times10^{-3}}{3} = \mathbf{3.84\ W}$$

### 2.4 PCB copper loss (not negligible at 80 A)

The phase and bus traces add resistance. Assume about 0.3 mΩ effective per channel (2 oz, parallel layers, about 8 mm path):

$$P_{Cu,ch} = 80^2 \times 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega \approx \mathbf{1.9\ W}$$
