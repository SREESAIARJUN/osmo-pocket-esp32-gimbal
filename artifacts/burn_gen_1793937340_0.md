# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: 80 A Burst Thermal, 48 kHz Switching, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Design

## 0. Architecture Corrections

These issues change the design, so they come first.

| Issue | Reality | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| **The H743 does not run the ESC.** | The H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. Each 4-in-1 ESC needs its own commutation MCU(s), typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32. | Use a 2-board stack: H743 FC plus a 4-in-1 ESC with 4 commutation MCUs. The interface is 4× bi-directional DShot600, current, voltage and telemetry. |
| **80 A on a nano board cannot be sustained.** | At 80 A the board dissipates about 65 W total in roughly 4 cm² (section 3). | 80 A is a **burst rating of about 2 s maximum**. Plan on 30×30 mm or larger for real 80 A work. |
| **All device parameters below are assumptions.** | Representative values for a 40 V, about 1 mΩ-class DFN5×6 MOSFET. | Replace them with your selected MOSFET's datasheet values, especially R<sub>DS(on)</sub>(T<sub>J</sub>), Q<sub>g</sub>, Q<sub>rr</sub> and Z<sub>th</sub>. |

## 1. Assumed Operating Point

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S, fully charged) | V<sub>bus</sub> | 25.2 V |
| Phase burst current | I<sub>ph</sub> | 80 A |
| PWM frequency | f<sub>sw</sub> | 48 kHz |
| R<sub>DS(on)</sub> at 25 °C | R<sub>25</sub> | 0.70 mΩ |
| Temperature coefficient (125 °C / 25 °C) | k<sub>T</sub> | 1.5 → R<sub>hot</sub> = 1.05 mΩ |
| Effective rise + fall time at 80 A | t<sub>r</sub> + t<sub>f</sub> | 40 ns |
| Dead time | t<sub>dt</sub> | 100 ns |
| Body-diode forward voltage | V<sub>f</sub> | 0.8 V |
| Reverse-recovery charge | Q<sub>rr</sub> | 50 nC |
| Total gate charge | Q<sub>g</sub> | 60 nC at V<sub>GS</sub> = 10 V |
| Output capacitance (energy-equivalent) | C<sub>oss(er)</sub> | 1.5 nF |
| Copper and solder resistance per current path | R<sub>pcb</sub> | 0.25 mΩ |

**Commutation model:** six-step trapezoidal with complementary (synchronous) PWM on the high-side phase. At any instant two phases conduct:
- One phase alternates between its high-side and low-side FET. Together they conduct 100% of the period.
- The return phase's low-side FET is on continuously.

So two FET channel resistances are always in the current path.

## 2. Loss Model per Motor Channel (One 3-Phase Bridge)

### 2.1 Conduction loss

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_J), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_J) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha(T_J-25)\right]$$

$$P_{cond} = 2(80)^2(1.05\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{13.44\ W}$$

The split depends on duty D:
- PWM-leg high-side FET: D·I²R
- PWM-leg low-side FET: (1−D)·I²R
- Return low-side FET: I²R = **6.72 W** (the hottest device)

PCB copper adds 2·I²·R<sub>pcb</sub> = **3.2 W**. This is often ignored, but it is significant.

### 2.2 Switching loss (hard-switched PWM leg)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2}V_{bus}I_{ph}(t_r+t_f)f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(40\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.3 Dead-time (body-diode) loss

$$P_{dt} = 2V_f I_{ph} t_{dt} f_{sw} = 2(0.8)(80)(100\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.4 Reverse recovery, C<sub>oss</sub> and gate loss

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 0.060\text{ W}$$

$$P_{oss} = C_{oss(er)}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.046\text{ W}$$

$$P_{g} = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 0.058\text{ W}$$

Gate loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET.

### 2.5 Totals

| Loss term | Per motor (W) | 4-in-1 (W) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 13.44 | 53.8 |
| Copper | 3.20 | 12.8 |
| Switching | 1.94 | 7.8 |
| Dead time | 0.61 | 2.4 |
| Q<sub>rr</sub> + C<sub>oss</sub> + gate | 0.16 | 0.7 |
| **Total** | **19.4** | **77.5** |

**Key observations:**
- Conduction dominates at about 70%. Choose MOSFETs on R<sub>DS(on)</sub> first.
- Raising f<sub>sw</sub> from 24 kHz to 48 kHz costs only about 1.3 W per motor. The 48 kHz choice is justified by lower current ripple and acoustic benefits.
- Electrical efficiency at full burst: P<sub>out</sub> ≈ 4·D·V·I. At D = 1 this is about 8 kW, so efficiency is about 99%. The problem is power density, not efficiency.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Steady state: not feasible

For a 20×20 mm board with both faces cooled, A ≈ 8×10⁻⁴ m². With prop-wash convection, h ≈ 100 W/m²·K:

$$R_{\theta,BA} = \frac{1}{hA} \approx 12.5\ \text{K/W} \;\Rightarrow\; \Delta T = 77.5 \times 12.5 \approx 970\ \text{K}$$

At 80 A, steady state is physically impossible.

The maximum continuous rating for ΔT = 70 K is P ≈ 5.6 W total. Scaling by I², this corresponds to roughly **20–22 A per motor continuous** on a 20×20 board. This matches real-world nano ESC ratings.

### 3.2 Burst: lumped thermal capacitance

Board heat capacity (FR4 plus 2 oz copper on 6 layers, plus FETs and solder):

$$C_{th} \approx m_{FR4}c_{FR4} + m_{Cu}c_{Cu} \approx (1.4)(1.1) + (2.0)(0.385) \approx 2.3\ \text{J/K}$$

$$T(t) = T_a + P R_\theta\left(1-e^{-t/R_\theta C_{th}}\right), \qquad \tau = R_\theta C_{th} \approx 29\ \text{s}$$

For t ≪ τ the heating is quasi-adiabatic:

$$\frac{dT}{dt} \approx \frac{P}{C_{th}} = \frac{77.5}{2.3} \approx 34\ \text{K/s}$$

Starting from a 40 °C board, reaching a 110 °C board limit gives:

$$t_{burst} \approx \frac{70}{34} \approx \mathbf{2.1\ s}$$

### 3.3 Junction rise above the board (hottest FET)

$$T_J = T_{board} + P_{FET}\cdot Z_{th,JB}(t_p)$$

- Return low-side FET: P ≈ 6.7 W conduction plus about 1 W share of switching/dead-time loss ≈ 7.7 W.
- With Z<sub>th,JB</sub>(1 s) ≈ 1.2 K/W (DFN5×6 on thermal vias): ΔT<sub>JB</sub> ≈ 9 K.
- T<sub>J</sub> ≈ 119 °C at the end of the burst, inside the 150–175 °C limit.

**The limit is board temperature, which degrades solder, the capacitors and the FC above it, not the junction.**

### 3.4 Recommendations

- Firmware burst limiter: ESC current limit of 80 A for ≤ 2 s, folding back to 25 A, gated by a board NTC above 100 °C.
