# STM32H743 AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC): Power-Stage, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Stated Assumptions

**The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every viable 4-in-1 design, the H743 runs ArduPilot and emits DShot600. Four dedicated ESC MCUs then run AM32 or BLHeli_32 and generate the 48 kHz 6-step PWM. These are typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 parts, either one per motor or a dual-core arrangement. The H743 has no hardware path to drive 12 complementary half-bridges with dead-time while also running EKF3, so the model below keeps the two domains separate.

No MOSFET part number, layer stack-up, or form factor was specified for Job #2. All numbers below therefore use the following **parametric baseline**. Substitute your actual datasheet values and the equations still hold.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous commutation

At any instant two phases conduct: one high-side FET (HS) and one low-side FET (LS). With complementary (synchronous) PWM at duty D, the following holds per 60° sector:

- **PWM'd leg:** the HS conducts for D·T and its complementary LS conducts for (1−D)·T.
- **Fixed leg:** the LS (or HS) is on for 100% of the sector.

So **exactly two channels carry I at every instant**, regardless of D. Total per-motor conduction loss is:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + I^2 R_{Cu}
$$

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

Per-device RMS current over one electrical revolution (each FET is active for 1/3 of the cycle):

$$
I_{HS,rms}=I\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{3}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{D}{2}\right)},\quad
I_{LS,rms}=I\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{3}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{1-D}{2}\right)}
$$

This assumes PWM on the HS of the leading phase, the typical AM32 scheme. At D → 1 (full-throttle burst), the HS devices carry I·√(1/3) and the dissipation concentrates there.

### 1.2 Electro-thermal fixed point

Because R_DS(on) depends on T_j, solve iteratively:

$$
T_j^{(n+1)} = T_{pcb} + R_{\theta,j\text{-}pcb}\cdot I_{rms}^2 R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j^{(n)}-25)]
$$

This converges when the loop gain $R_{\theta}\,I_{rms}^2 R_{25}\,\alpha < 1$. If it does not converge, you are in thermal runaway. This is the real burst limit, not the datasheet I_D.

### 1.3 Numbers (80 A, T_j ≈ 125 °C → R_DS ≈ 1.5 mΩ, R_Cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ per path)

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| P_cond per motor (FETs) | 2 × 80² × 1.5 mΩ = **19.2 W** |
| P_copper per motor | 80² × 0.3 mΩ = **1.9 W** |
| Worst FET (HS, D ≈ 1), I_rms = 46.2 A | 46.2² × 1.5 mΩ = **3.2 W** |
| **4 motors, simultaneous burst** | **≈ 84 W** |

Loop-gain check: R_θ,j-pcb ≈ 3 K/W gives 3 × 2134 × 1.0 mΩ × 0.005 = 0.032, which is far below 1. Junction runaway is therefore not the limit. **Board heat capacity is the limit** (§3).
