# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC: Loss Model, DShot Allocation, Netlist

This is an engineering baseline. Every device parameter below is an **assumed placeholder** chosen to match typical 40 V, 3.3×3.3 / 5×6 mm power MOSFETs. Before release, substitute the values from your chosen device's datasheet and re-run the equations. The equations themselves are general.

---

## 0. Architecture Clarification (Read First)

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design, the H743 runs ArduPilot (attitude control, EKF, DShot master). Each ESC channel has its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, which generates the 48 kHz PWM. Here that MCU is assumed to be an AT32F421K8 with AM32. A single H743 cannot practically do sensorless 6-step on 4 motors at 48 kHz while also running ArduPilot.
2. **"Nano" plus "80 A burst" is thermally marginal.** Section 3 shows that a 20×20 mm board survives 80 A on all four motors for only about 1 s. That is acceptable as a punch-out burst rating, but not as a continuous rating. I recommend a 25.5×25.5 mm or 30.5×30.5 mm form factor, or a burst rating that states its time limit.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Path

In 6-step commutation with complementary PWM, current always flows through exactly two MOSFETs:
- **During the on-time:** the high side (HS) of the PWM leg plus the low side (LS) of the return leg.
- **During the off-time:** the freewheel path is the LS of the PWM leg (synchronous rectification) plus the same return-leg LS.

The total conduction loss per motor is therefore independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

### 1.2 Ripple Correction

With phase inductance L_ph (assume 15 µH line-to-line), the peak-to-peak ripple is worst at D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}D(1-D)}{L f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2(0.25)}{15\mu\cdot48k} = 8.75\text{ A}$$

$$I_{rms} = \sqrt{I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}} = \sqrt{6400 + 6.4} = 80.04\text{ A}$$

The correction is negligible, so I_rms ≈ 80 A.

### 1.3 Numerical Results

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond per motor | Average per FET (÷6) | Instantaneous in a conducting FET |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W | 2.13 W | 6.4 W |
| 100 °C | 1.45 mΩ | 18.6 W | 3.10 W | 9.3 W |
| **125 °C (design)** | **1.60 mΩ** | **20.5 W** | **3.41 W** | **10.2 W** |

### 1.4 Copper Loss

Assume an effective path resistance of R_cu ≈ 0.5 mΩ (battery pad → bus → FET → phase pad, 2 oz copper on 4 layers, rising about 0.39 %/°C):

$$P_{cu} = I^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\text{ m}\Omega = 3.2\text{ W per motor}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-leg HS is hard-switched. Its LS partner commutates softly (ZVS through the body diode).

### 2.1 Gate Currents

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{src}+R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}} = \frac{6.8}{8} = 0.85\text{ A}$$

$$I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{snk}+R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}} = \frac{3.2}{5} = 0.64\text{ A}$$
