## 1. Field-Oriented Current Loop (20 kHz)

### 1.1 Transforms

Electrical angle from the mechanical encoder angle $\theta_m$ and the calibrated offset $\theta_0$:

$$\theta_e = p\,\theta_m - \theta_0 \pmod{2\pi}$$

Clarke transform (amplitude-invariant, with $i_a+i_b+i_c=0$):

$$i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \frac{i_a + 2 i_b}{\sqrt3}$$

Park transform:

$$\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}$$

### 1.2 Plant and decoupling

The SPM motor model in the $dq$ frame is:

$$v_d = R i_d + L\dot i_d - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad v_q = R i_q + L\dot i_q + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e\lambda_m$$

with $\omega_e = p\,\omega_m$, $\lambda_m = \tfrac{2K_t}{3p}$, and torque $\tau = \tfrac32 p\lambda_m i_q = K_t i_q$.

The controller adds decoupling feedforward to the PI outputs:

$$v_d^* = u_d^{PI} - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad v_q^* = u_q^{PI} + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e\lambda_m$$

### 1.3 PI tuning by pole-zero cancellation

With decoupling, each axis reduces to $G(s)=\frac{1}{Ls+R}$. Choose current bandwidth $\omega_c$:

$$K_{p,i} = L\,\omega_c,\qquad K_{i,i} = R\,\omega_c$$

This gives a closed loop of $\frac{\omega_c}{s+\omega_c}$. Keep $\omega_c \le 2\pi f_{pwm}/20$ because of the 1.5-sample delay. With $f_{pwm} = 20$ kHz, $\omega_c = 2\pi\cdot 1000$ rad/s, which gives $K_p = 11.3\,V/A$ and $K_i = 34{,}500\,V/(A\,s)$.

### 1.4 Discrete PI with back-calculation anti-windup

$$u_k = K_p e_k + I_k,\quad u_k^{sat}=\mathrm{sat}(u_k),\quad I_{k+1} = I_k + T_s\left[K_i e_k + K_{aw}(u_k^{sat}-u_k)\right],\quad K_{aw}\approx \frac{K_i}{K_p}$$

The voltage vector is limited as a circle: $|v_{dq}|\le V_{dc}/\sqrt3$. $v_d$ gets priority:

$$v_{q,max}=\sqrt{V_{max}^2 - v_d^2}$$
