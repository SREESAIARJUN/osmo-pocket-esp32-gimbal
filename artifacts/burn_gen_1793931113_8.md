# STM32H743 Nano AIO with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #51): Reference Design Package

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Design Review Flags

### Architecture

The H743 is the flight controller. It runs ArduPilot and transmits bi-directional DShot600. It does **not** drive the bridges directly.

Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 (BLHeli_32 is discontinued). The ESC MCU is an AT32F421 or STM32G071, paired with a 3-phase gate driver and 6 N-channel MOSFETs.

The DShot and eRPM telemetry math in Section 4 depends on this split. If you intended the H743 to commutate the motors itself, that is a different (FOC) design and the timer plan changes completely.

### Operating point

- **Bus voltage:** V_bus = 25.2 V (6S fully charged, the worst case for switching loss).
- **Burst current:** I_ph = 80 A per motor.
- **PWM frequency:** f_sw = 48 kHz.
- **Dead time:** t_dt = 100 ns.
- **Gate drive voltage:** V_drv = 10 V.
- **Driver resistance:** R_src = 2 Ω (source), R_snk = 1 Ω (sink).
- **External gate resistor:** R_g = 3.3 Ω.

### Design review flags (headline conclusions; derivations follow)

1. **"80 A burst" on a nano board lasts about 1–2 s on all four channels, not 10 s.** The limit is thermal mass, not the silicon.
2. **Sustained capability is roughly 20–25 A per channel** with prop-wash cooling.
3. **The bus-capacitor ripple current is the hidden killer.** See Section 3.4.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j)=R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 0.80 × 1.60 = **1.28 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-step commutation with synchronous rectification

AM32 uses complementary PWM. In any 60° sector:

- The high-side FET of phase A switches at duty D and carries I² R D.
- Its complementary low-side FET (synchronous rectifier) carries I² R (1−D).
- The low-side FET of phase B is held fully ON and carries I² R.

$$P_{cond,ch}=I^2R\,D+I^2R(1-D)+I^2R=2I^2R_{DS}(T_j)$$

The conduction loss is **independent of duty cycle**. This is the key insight: a low-throttle, high-current burst (prop strike, stall) is just as hot as full throttle.

### 1.3 Self-consistent junction temperature

The loss and the junction temperature depend on each other:

$$T_j = T_b + P_{FET}(T_j)\cdot R_{\theta JB}$$

Solve by fixed-point iteration. It converges in about 3 steps.

Evaluated at 80 A with T_j = 125 °C:

$$P_{cond,ch}=2(80)^2(1.28\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{16.4\ W}$$
