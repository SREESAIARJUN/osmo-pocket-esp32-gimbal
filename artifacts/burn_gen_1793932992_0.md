# Job #51: STM32H743 Nano FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC Stack
## Systems Architecture, Loss Model, DShot Timer/DMA Plan, and Netlists

## 0. Architecture Corrections

Two points in the brief would cause a design failure if taken literally.

**1. The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.**
An "H743 4-in-1 ESC" is not a viable single-MCU architecture. Each of the four motors needs its own commutation loop running at 48 kHz PWM, with BEMF zero-cross detection on a fast comparator. The correct partition is:

| Board | MCU | Firmware | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (480 MHz) | ArduPilot | Attitude control, DShot600 master, bidirectional eRPM consumer |
| 4-in-1 ESC | 4× AT32F421 / STM32G071 (one per motor) | AM32 (or BLHeli_32-class) | 48 kHz PWM, commutation, eRPM telemetry |

The 48 kHz switching analysis applies to the ESC. The DShot/DMA analysis applies to the H743.

**2. 80A per channel on a "nano" footprint is a burst rating only.**
The thermal model in §2 shows a 20×20 mm board cannot sustain this. Even a 30.5×30.5 mm board sustains 80A for only about 1–3 seconds. This document therefore designs on the **30.5 mm mounting pattern**, rates **80A as a ≤2 s burst**, and treats roughly **45A as the continuous rating**. Any marketing claim beyond that must be backed by the validation in §6.

**Assumptions.** All component parameters below are representative of current 40V 5×6 mm MOSFETs. Replace them with the datasheet values for your selected parts before freezing the design.

## 2. Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

**Commutation model.** The ESC uses six-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. At every instant, the motor current flows through exactly **two** MOSFETs in series:

- During the PWM on-time: high side of phase X plus low side of phase Y.
- During the PWM off-time: low side of phase X (synchronous freewheel) plus low side of phase Y.

The conduction loss for the channel is therefore independent of duty cycle, to first order:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

**Temperature correction.** On-resistance rises with junction temperature:

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0055 × 100) = **1.55 mΩ**

**Burst result at 80A:**

| Condition | Calculation | P_cond |
|---|---|---|
| Cold (T_j = 25 °C) | 6400 × 2 × 1.0 mΩ | 12.8 W |
| Hot (T_j = 125 °C) | 6400 × 2 × 1.55 mΩ | **19.8 W** |

**Self-consistent junction temperature.** Because loss raises T_j, and T_j raises R_DS(on), the operating point must be solved iteratively:

$$T_j = T_{board} + P_{FET}\,R_{\theta J\text{-}board}$$
$$P_{FET} = f\big(R(T_j)\big)$$

This converges in 3–4 iterations. §2.5 gives the result.

**Distribution across the six FETs.** Each phase is active for 2/3 of an electrical cycle. Averaged over a cycle, each FET carries roughly 1/3 of the channel conduction loss on its "leg pair" basis, which works out to about **3.3 W average per FET** at 80A hot.

The low-side FETs run hotter than the high-side FETs at low duty cycle, because they carry the freewheel current. Lay out the low-side devices with the better copper.

### 2.2 Switching losses (48 kHz)

**Hard-switched transitions.** Only the switching leg commutates at f_sw. The loss uses the linear V·I overlap approximation:

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

$$P_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = V_{bus} Q_{rr} f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 50\,\text{nC} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = 0.06\ W$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.04\ W$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction:**

$$P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = 0.61\ W$$

**Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver IC and gate resistors):**

$$P_g = 2 \cdot Q_g V_{dr} f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\,\text{nC} \times 10\ \text{V} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = 0.06\ W$$

**Frequency sensitivity.** All switching terms scale linearly with f_sw:

| f_sw | Switching subtotal (P_sw + P_rr + P_oss + P_dt) |
|---|---|
| 24 kHz | ~1.3 W |
| 48 kHz | ~2.65 W |
| 96 kHz | ~5.3 W |

At 48 kHz, switching is about 12% of total loss at burst current. At hover current (~15A), it is a much larger fraction, which is why 48 kHz is the efficiency-versus-acoustic-noise compromise point.

### 2.3 Copper and shunt losses

| Element | Resistance | Loss at 80A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase/bus copper, 4 oz equivalent, ~0.3 mΩ path | 0.3 mΩ | 1.9 W |

### 2.4 Totals at 80A burst

| Loss term | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (hot) | 19.8 W | 79.2 W |
| Switching + Q_rr + Q_oss + dead time | 2.65 W | 10.6 W |
| Gate drive | 0.06 W | 0.24 W |
| Copper + shunt | ~2.0 W | ~8 W |
| **Total** | **~24.5 W** | **~98 W** |

**Efficiency check.** The output power is roughly 4 × 80A × 25.2V × (effective duty ≈ 0.9) ≈ 7.3 kW. That gives about **98.7% efficiency**. The losses are small in relative terms, but huge in absolute terms for a board weighing about 12 g.

### 2.5 Thermal model

**Static junction rise (per FET).** The junction-to-board path is:

$$R_{\theta J\text{-}board} = R_{\theta JC}\,(1.2) + R_{pad+vias}\,(\approx 2.5) = 3.7\ \text{K/W}$$

$$\Delta T_{J\text{-}board} = 3.3\ \text{W} \times 3.7\ \text{K/W} \approx 12\ \text{K}$$

The junctions are therefore **not** the bottleneck. The board's thermal mass is.

**Board heat capacity (30.5 mm board, approximately 36×36 mm outline, 1.6 mm thick, 8 layers × 2 oz, about 70% copper fill):**
