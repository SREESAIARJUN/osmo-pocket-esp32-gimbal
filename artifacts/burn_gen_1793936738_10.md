**Architecture note:** Bidirectional DShot requires a microcontroller on each ESC channel to decode commands and return eRPM. The topology is therefore:

- STM32H743 (flight control),
- 4× ESC MCUs (e.g., AT32F421 running AM32),
- 4× three-phase gate drivers,
- 24 MOSFETs.

**Voltage-margin warning:** 40 V FETs on 6S leave about 59% margin, and inductive ringing can consume most of it. Validate V_DS overshoot on the bench, or move to 60 V parts and accept roughly 1.5–2× higher R_DS(on).

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At a design point of T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{DS}(110) = 0.75 \times (1 + 0.0045 \times 85) = 0.75 \times 1.383 = 1.04\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Current path in six-step commutation with synchronous rectification

At any instant, two phase legs carry I_ph:

- **Switching leg:** current alternates between the high-side FET (duty D) and the complementary low-side FET (1 − D). Exactly one FET of this leg conducts at a time, excluding dead time.
- **Return leg:** one low-side FET conducts continuously during that commutation sector.

Therefore exactly two FET channels are in series with the current at all times, regardless of D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.04\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{13.3\ W}$$

### 1.3 Copper path loss

$$P_{Cu} = I_{ph}^2 R_{pcb} = 6400 \times 0.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

This loss heats the board directly rather than the FET junctions, but it still loads the same thermal mass.

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

Each phase conducts for 2/3 of the electrical cycle (positive or negative sector), with load rotating across all six FETs. The long-term average per FET is:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{13.3}{6} = 2.2\ \text{W}$$

Within a single 60° sector, the return-leg low-side FET carries the full I²R continuously, about 6.7 W. At high eRPM this averages out. At stall or very low eRPM (e.g., a stuck motor), it does not, and one FET sees about 6.7 W. That case drives the junction-transient check in §3.3.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the switching leg's FET pair commutates each PWM period.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I_{ph}\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

$$P_{VI} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\times10^{-9} \times 48\,000 = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

The low-side body diode conducts during dead time and then recovers when the high side turns on:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 60\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.07\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per period:

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I_{ph}\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

Two FETs switch per period. This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET.

$$P_{g} = 2\,Q_g\,V_{GS}\,f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

### 2.6 Per-motor loss budget at 80 A burst, 25.2 V, T_j = 110 °C

| Loss | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 13.30 |
| Copper | 3.20 |
| V·I switching | 1.94 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Q_oss + Q_rr | 0.13 |
| Gate drive | 0.06 |
| **Total per motor** | **≈ 19.2 W** |
| **Four motors simultaneously** | **≈ 77 W** |

**Efficiency check:** The electrical input is roughly 25.2 V × 80 A × D. At D ≈ 0.8, that is about 1.6 kW per motor, giving roughly 98.8% stage efficiency.

**Sensitivity:** 48 kHz costs about 2.6 W per motor in switching-related losses versus about 1.3 W at 24 kHz. Conduction dominates (~70%), so R_DS(on) and copper are the levers, not f_sw. The 48 kHz choice is justified by lower current ripple on low-inductance racing motors, not by efficiency.

### 3.2 Board-level burst limit

This is the governing constraint.

**Steady-state check:** A 30×30 mm board in prop wash typically achieves Rθ_ba of about 2–3 K/W. At 77 W:

$$\Delta T_{ss} = 77 \times 2.5 \approx 190\ \text{K}$$

**80 A on all four motors cannot be continuous.** It is strictly a thermal-capacitance-limited burst.
