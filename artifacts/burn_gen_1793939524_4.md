# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal, and DShot Architecture Model

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

No MOSFET part number, board outline, stackup, or ESC MCU was specified for Job #46. This model is therefore **parametric**. Each equation is given symbolically, then evaluated with the stated baseline values. Replace the baseline values with datasheet values once the BOM is frozen.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0055 × 100) = **1.55 mΩ**

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

During any commutation sector:
- The **PWM leg** conducts I through the high-side FET for duty D, and through the low-side FET for (1−D).
- The **return leg** low-side FET conducts I continuously.

So the instantaneous conduction loss is independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{ds}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 1.3 Self-consistent junction temperature

Because R_DS(on) rises with T_j, conduction loss and junction temperature must be solved together:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + P_{FET}(T_j)\cdot R_{\theta,j\text{-}ref}$$

Iterate this numerically. Thermal runaway occurs if the following holds:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\,R_{\theta} \ge 1$$

which is equivalent to 2I²R₂₅ · α · R_θ ≥ 1 per channel path.

### 1.4 Baseline numbers (T_j = 125 °C)

| Term | Expression | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Per-channel FET conduction loss | 2 × 80² × 1.55 mΩ | **19.8 W** |
| Per-FET average (6 FETs share 2 conduction slots) | 19.8 / 3 per FET-pair slot ≈ I²R/3 | **≈ 3.3 W per FET** |
| PCB copper and solder (R_cu ≈ 0.4 mΩ path) | I² × R_cu | 2.6 W |

**Copper resistance.** Use the following:

$$R_{cu} = \frac{\rho_{cu}(T)\,L}{w\,t}, \qquad \rho_{cu}(T) = 1.72\times10^{-8}\,[1+0.00393(T-20)]\ \Omega\!\cdot\!\text{m}$$

For 2 oz (70 µm) copper, a 6 mm wide × 15 mm long pour gives 0.61 mΩ per layer. Paralleling layers with via stitching is mandatory on a nano board.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the PWM leg switches. Per channel:

| Loss mechanism | Equation | Baseline value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap (hard switching) | ½ · V_bus · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw | ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 k = **1.94 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw | 50 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = 0.06 W |
| C_oss (charge and discharge) | C_oss · V_bus² · f_sw | 1.5 nF × 635 × 48 k = 0.05 W |
| Dead-time body diode conduction | V_f · I · 2t_dt · f_sw | 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver and R_g) | 2 · Q_g · V_gs · f_sw | 2 × 60 nC × 10 × 48 k = 0.06 W |
| **Switching subtotal** | | **≈ 2.7 W per channel** |

**Sensitivity to frequency.** The switching loss scales linearly with f_sw:

$$\frac{\partial P_{sw}}{\partial f_{sw}} \approx 56\ \mu\text{W/Hz}$$

Moving from 24 kHz to 48 kHz adds about 1.35 W per channel at 80 A. Dead-time loss is the most controllable term: reducing it from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel.

## 3. Total Loss Budget

| Condition | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (125 °C) | 19.8 W | 79.2 W |
| Switching | 2.7 W | 10.8 W |
| Copper path | 2.6 W | 10.4 W |
| Current-sense shunt (0.25 mΩ, if inline) | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| **Total at 80 A burst** | **≈ 26.7 W** | **≈ 107 W** |

Efficiency check: at 80 A × 25.2 V ≈ 2.0 kW per channel, the loss fraction is about 1.3%. That is consistent with good 4-in-1 designs. The problem is not efficiency. **The problem is power density.**

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Steady state: shows that 80 A cannot be continuous

The thermal path for a nano board is junction → case → PCB → convection from prop wash:

$$T_j = T_a + P_{FET}R_{\theta jc} + P_{board}\,R_{\theta,board\text{-}a}$$

For a 20×20 mm board (~8 cm² two-sided) with prop-wash h ≈ 60–100 W/m²K, R_θ,board-a ≈ 15–20 K/W.

At 107 W this predicts a temperature rise of over 1500 K. **80 A per channel is only valid as a transient burst.**
