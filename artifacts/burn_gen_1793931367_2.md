# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: System Architecture & Mathematical Model

I don't have a prior spec for "Job #9," so every parameter below is stated as an explicit assumption. Swap in your real values and the equations still hold.

---

## 0. Architectural Clarification

The STM32H743 does **not** switch the power MOSFETs. The stack is split into two domains:

| Domain | MCU | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743 (480 MHz) | ArduPilot, sends **bidirectional DShot600**, receives eRPM telemetry |
| 4-in-1 ESC | 4× AT32F421 / STM32G071 (AM32 or BLHeli_32) | 6-step / FOC commutation at **48 kHz PWM**, drives 24 MOSFETs |

The thermal and switching losses (§1–§3) belong to the ESC. The DMA and timer allocation (§4) belongs to the H743.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80A Burst)

### 1.1 Current Path in 6-step with Synchronous Rectification
At any instant two phases conduct, so the current flows through exactly **two FETs in series** (one high-side, one low-side, or the low-side freewheeling path during PWM off-time with sync-rect active). Each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical period, with PWM duty D on the high side and (1−D) on the complementary low side.

The conduction loss per motor channel is:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_J)$$

The loss per FET, averaged over the electrical cycle, is:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6}$$

### 1.2 Temperature-Coupled R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_J) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_J-25)]$$

$$T_J = T_{PCB} + P_{FET}\,R_{\theta JC} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{solve by fixed-point iteration}$$

Convergence requires $\alpha\,P_{cond,FET}\,R_{\theta,total} < 1$. Otherwise you get thermal runaway.

### 1.3 Numbers at T_J = 125 °C (R_DS = 1.5 mΩ)

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2(80)^2(1.5\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

$$P_{cond,FET} = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

### 1.4 Parasitic Copper and Shunt
- **Phase shunt** (0.25 mΩ): $80^2 \times 0.25\text{m} = 1.6$ W per channel.
- **Copper path** (estimate 0.3 mΩ effective, 2 oz plus vias): $\approx 1.9$ W per channel.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the PWM-ing leg switches hard. The commutation events happen at f_elec, which is far below 48 kHz, so they are negligible.

| Term | Equation | Value per channel |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | $\tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}$ = ½·25.2·80·40 ns·48 kHz | **1.94 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | $V_{SD}\, I\, 2t_{dead}\, f_{sw}$ = 0.8·80·100 ns·48 kHz | **0.31 W** |
| Reverse recovery | $V\,Q_{rr}\,f_{sw}$ = 25.2·50 nC·48 kHz | **0.06 W** |
| Coss | $\tfrac12 C_{oss} V^2 f_{sw}$ (C_oss ≈ 1.5 nF) | **0.02 W** |
| Gate drive (2 active FETs) | $2 Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw}$ | **0.05 W** (dissipated in driver) |
| **Σ switching** | | **≈ 2.38 W** |

**Design lever:** Dropping f_sw to 24 kHz halves this (to ~1.2 W) at the cost of current ripple and audible noise. Because conduction loss dominates by roughly 8:1 at 80 A, **R_DS(on) is the primary optimization target, not f_sw.**

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Total Burst Dissipation

| | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching | 2.38 W | 9.5 W |
| Shunt + copper | 3.5 W | 14.0 W |
| **Total** | **25.1 W** | **≈ 100 W** |

Efficiency check: the output is ≈ 4 × 25.2 V × 80 A × D. At D ≈ 0.9 that is ~7.2 kW, so the loss is ~1.4%. That is efficient, but **100 W into a 36 mm board is the real problem.**

### 3.2 Steady-State Feasibility (it isn't)
Convective sink, using prop-wash h ≈ 100 W/m²K and both faces A ≈ 2 × 0.036² = 2.6×10⁻³ m²:

$$R_{\theta,SA} = \frac{1}{hA} \approx 3.9\ \text{K/W} \;\Rightarrow\; \Delta T_{ss} = 100 \times 3.9 \approx 390\ \text{K}$$

**80 A × 4 is strictly a transient rating.** Continuous capability is roughly $I_{cont} \approx 80\sqrt{\tfrac{(100-40)/3.9}{100}} \approx 31$ A per motor.

### 3.3 Transient (Lumped-Capacitance) Model

| Mass | m | c_p | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| FR4 | 6 g | 1.1 J/gK | 6.6 J/K |
| Copper planes | 5 g | 0.385 J/gK | 1.9 J/K |
| 24 FETs + caps | 3 g | ~0.7 | 2.1 J/K |
| **C_th** | | | **≈ 10.6 J/K** |

$$T_{PCB}(t) = T_a + P R_{\theta}\left(1-e^{-t/\tau}\right), \quad \tau = R_\theta C_{th} \approx 41\ \text{s}$$

Allowable burst duration to a PCB limit of T_max = 105 °C from T_a = 40 °C:

$$t_{burst} = -\tau \ln\!\left(1 - \frac{T_{max}-T_a}{P R_\theta}\right) = -41\ln\!\left(1-\frac{65}{390}\right) \approx \mathbf{7.5\ s}$$
