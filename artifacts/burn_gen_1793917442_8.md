## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right]$$

At T_j = 115 °C: R = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0055 × 90) ≈ **1.50 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-step trapezoidal, synchronous PWM

During each 60° sector, two phase legs are active. The high side of leg A PWMs at duty D, and its low side conducts during (1 − D). Leg B's low side is on continuously.

- **Switching leg:** $P_{A} = D\,I^2R + (1-D)\,I^2R = I^2R$. This is independent of D because of synchronous rectification.
- **Static leg:** $P_{B} = I^2R$

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\cdot\frac{1}{N_p}$$

N_p is the number of paralleled FETs per switch position.

| Case | R_DS | P_cond / motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 FET/position, T_j = 25 °C | 1.0 mΩ | 12.8 W | 51.2 W |
| 1 FET/position, T_j = 115 °C | 1.5 mΩ | **19.2 W** | **76.8 W** |
| 2 FET/position, T_j = 115 °C | 0.75 mΩ eff. | 9.6 W | 38.4 W |

**Copper/trace loss (often forgotten).** A 2 oz layer has ≈0.25 mΩ/sq. The phase path runs about 3 squares across 2 parallel layers, so R ≈ 0.375 mΩ per leg. That adds 2 × 80² × 0.375 mΩ ≈ **4.8 W/motor**, which is comparable to the FETs. Use 3 oz internal planes, or as many parallel layers as possible.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Per motor, only one leg hard-switches at a time.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard-switched FET)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (complementary body diode)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = E_{oss} f_{sw} \times 2 = 0.3\mu \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = 0.029\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f I_{ph} (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_G, not the FET)

$$P_{g} = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2 = 50\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k}\times2 = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Summary per motor (80 A, 6S, T_j = 115 °C, 1 FET/position)

| Loss term | W |
|---|---|
| FET conduction | 19.2 |
| PCB copper | 4.8 |
| V–I switching | 1.45 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.31 |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.13 |
| **Total / motor** | **≈ 25.9 W** |
| **4-in-1 total** | **≈ 104 W** |

**Efficiency check.** Motor power ≈ 25.2 V × 80 A × D. At full throttle (D ≈ 1) that is 2016 W/motor, so ESC efficiency is ≈ 98.7%. Efficiency is high, but the absolute heat is enormous for a 20×20 board.

**Sensitivity.** P_sw scales with f_sw. Dropping to 24 kHz saves ≈0.9 W/motor. Conduction dominates (~75%), so paralleling FETs and adding copper are the effective levers, not PWM frequency.
