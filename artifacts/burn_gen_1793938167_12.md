# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #8): Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Timer/DMA Plan

## 0. Architectural Corrections and Assumptions

Three points need fixing before the math, because they change the design.

1. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In a "4-in-1 ESC nano FC" the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and only generates DShot. Each ESC channel has its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, which drives a 3-phase gate driver. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by those ESC MCUs, not the H743. Typical parts are AT32F421K8U7 and STM32G071. The architecture below reflects this.

2. **80 A per channel on a nano/AIO footprint is a short-burst rating only, and the model must state the burst duration.** Section 3 shows that four simultaneous 80 A channels cannot be sustained on a 30.5 × 30.5 mm board for more than about 5 s.

3. **Assumed operating point.** The numbers below use these values. Substitute your own datasheet values into the equations.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S, full charge) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Burst phase current per ESC | I_ph | 80 A |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| MOSFET | — | 40 V N-ch, PDFN 3.3×3 (e.g. class of ~1.2 mΩ parts) |
| R_DS(on) @ 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | R_25 | 1.2 mΩ |
| R_DS(on) temperature coefficient | α | ≈ 0.4 %/K, giving ≈ 1.5× at 125 °C |
| Effective rise + fall time at 80 A | t_r + t_f | 20 ns |
| Body-diode reverse recovery charge | Q_rr | 40 nC |
| Output charge | Q_oss | 30 nC |
| Total gate charge | Q_g | 50 nC @ 10 V |

| Commutation / PWM scheme | — | 6-step trapezoidal, complementary (synchronous) PWM |
| Board size / mounting | — | 30.5 × 30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz outer copper |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation with complementary PWM, two phases conduct at any instant:

- During the PWM **on-time (D)**, current flows through the high-side FET of phase A, the motor, and the low-side FET of phase B.
- During the **off-time (1−D)**, the high-side of A turns off, the low-side of A turns on synchronously, and current freewheels through LS-A, the motor, and LS-B.

In both intervals, exactly **two R_DS(on) are in series**, independent of duty cycle (dead time excluded).

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big]
$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$
R_{DS} = 1.2\,\text{mΩ} \times 1.5 = 1.8\,\text{mΩ}
$$

### 1.3 Conduction loss per ESC channel

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.8\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{23.0\ W}
$$

At T_j = 25 °C the same expression gives 15.4 W. Positive feedback between temperature and R_DS(on) is therefore significant, so the junction temperature must be solved iteratively (Section 3.4).

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

Each of the 6 FETs conducts for 1/3 of the electrical period, and 2 FETs conduct at any time. The average loss per FET is therefore:

$$
P_{FET,avg} = \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6}\times 2\times\frac{1}{2}... \;=\; I^2 R_{DS}\cdot\tfrac{1}{3} = 6400 \times 1.8\text{m}\times\tfrac13 = \mathbf{3.84\ W}
$$

- **Instantaneous loss while conducting:** I²R = 11.5 W.
- **Check:** 6 FETs × 3.84 W = 23.0 W.

### 1.5 Copper (trace/plane) conduction loss

For a phase path of length l, width w, copper thickness t (2 oz ≈ 70 µm), and resistivity ρ_Cu = 1.72×10⁻⁸ Ω·m (×1.39 at 125 °C):

$$
R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho\,l}{w\,t}
$$

Example: l = 6 mm, w = 4 mm, stacked across 4 layers in parallel (effective t = 280 µm):

$$
R_{Cu} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\times0.006}{0.004\times280\times10^{-6}} = 92\ \mu\Omega
$$

$$
P_{Cu} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 92\,\mu\Omega \approx \mathbf{1.2\ W/ch}
$$

The factor of 2 accounts for the two active phase paths. Battery bus copper and pads add more on top of this.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

At any instant only one half-bridge is PWM-switching per channel (the other conducting phase is held statically on its low side). All terms below are therefore per channel.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap loss

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I_{ph}\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw}
= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}
$$

For accuracy, derive t_r and t_f from the gate driver:

$$
t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,R_{G,tot}}{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}, \qquad
t_{f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,R_{G,tot}}{V_{plateau}}
$$

where R_G,tot = R_driver + R_G,ext + R_G,int. Example: Q_gd = 12 nC, R_G,tot = 6 Ω, V_plateau = 4 V, V_drv = 10 V gives t_r ≈ 12 ns and t_f ≈ 18 ns. In that case use 30 ns, which gives P_sw ≈ 1.45 W.
