# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #61): Power, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

## 0. Design Review Findings

These issues change the design and should be resolved first.

| # | Finding | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | **The H743 should not commutate the motors.** Production 4-in-1 stacks use one small MCU per ESC (AT32F421, STM32G071, or GD32E230) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot to them. | The architecture below assumes 1× H743 (FC) and 4× ESC MCUs. |
| F2 | **80 A per motor on a "nano" (20×20 mm) board is not thermally plausible**, even as a burst (see §3). | Use a 30.5×30.5 mm ESC footprint at minimum, or redefine 80 A as a ≤2 s, single-motor or total-board figure. |
| F3 | **48 kHz PWM roughly doubles switching and dead-time loss** relative to 24 kHz. | It is acceptable for smoothness, but must be budgeted (§2.2). |
| F4 | **Specify "80 A" as per-motor phase current, per-motor battery current, or total.** | This doc assumes **80 A per motor** (worst case). Scale with I² if it is total. |

**Assumed component parameters.** These are typical for the device class and must be replaced with real datasheet values.

- Bus voltage: 6S, V_bus = 25.2 V max.
- MOSFET: 40 V N-ch DFN5×6.
  - R_ds(on) = 1.0 mΩ at 25 °C, 10 V V_gs.
  - Q_g = 50 nC, Q_rr = 50 nC, C_oss(eff) = 1 nF, V_sd = 0.8 V.
  - R_θJC = 1.5 K/W.
- Gate driver: 3-phase half-bridge driver (FD6288Q-class).
  - V_gs = 10 V.
  - t_r + t_f ≈ 40 ns at 80 A.
- Dead time: t_dt = 200 ns per edge.
- PWM: f_sw = 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous rectification), trapezoidal 6-step.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.004\text{–}0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C with α = 0.005: R_ds = 1.0 × 1.5 = **1.5 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

At any instant, current flows through exactly one high-side or its complementary low-side FET in the PWM leg, plus one low-side FET in the return leg. That gives **two R_ds in series** for the full period:

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Phase current relates to battery current by I_ph ≈ I_batt / D. At full throttle (D → 1), I_ph ≈ I_batt = 80 A.

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ ESC}$$

Per FET (2 conducting): **9.6 W**.

**Partial-throttle warning.** At D = 0.5 with 40 A battery current, phase current is still 80 A. Conduction loss is unchanged, while the dead-time and switching share rises. Current limiting must act on **phase** current, not battery current.

### 1.3 Thermal runaway criterion

Losses feed back into T_j:

$$T_j = T_a + R_{\theta JA}\, I^2 R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Solving for T_j:

$$T_j = \frac{T_a + R_{\theta JA} I^2 R_{25}(1-25\alpha)}{1 - \alpha R_{\theta JA} I^2 R_{25}}$$

The solution is stable only if:

$$\alpha R_{\theta JA} I^2 R_{25} < 1$$

At 80 A (I²R_25 = 6.4 W per FET), the limit is **R_θJA < 31 K/W**. This is easily met in steady state, but the effective R_θJA of a 30×30 board in prop wash is about 3–8 K/W *for the whole board*. The real limit is absolute temperature, not runaway (§3).

### 1.4 Copper loss (often ignored, significant here)

Trace or plane resistance:

$$R = \rho L / (w t)$$

with ρ_Cu = 1.72×10⁻⁸ Ω·m and 2 oz copper (t = 70 µm). For a 10 mm × 5 mm path:

R ≈ 1.72e-8 × 0.01 / (0.005 × 70e-6) ≈ **0.49 mΩ** per layer.

- With 4 paralleled layers: ~0.12 mΩ, giving 80² × 0.12 m × 2 (go and return) ≈ **1.6 W per ESC**.
- **Recommendation:** use 4-layer 2 oz or 6-layer, put the battery pads at the centre, and keep phase pads outboard.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

In 6-step, only one leg is PWM-switching at a time. That leg has one hard-switched FET and one synchronous FET.

### 2.1 Loss components (per ESC)

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = 0.5·25.2·80·40n·48k | **1.94 W** |
| Dead-time body diode | V_sd·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·400n·48k | **1.23 W** |
| Reverse recovery | V·Q_rr·f_sw = 25.2·50n·48k | **0.06 W** |
| C_oss | ½·C_oss·V²·f_sw ×2 = 1n·635·48k | **0.03 W** |
| Gate drive (driver dissipation) | Q_g·V_gs·f_sw ×2 = 50n·10·48k·2 | **0.05 W** |
| **Subtotal (f-dependent)** | | **≈ 3.3 W** |

### 2.2 Frequency sensitivity

$$P_{sw}(f) \approx 68.8\ \mu\text{J} \times f_{sw}$$

| f_sw | P_sw per ESC |
|---|---|
| 24 kHz | 1.65 W |
| 48 kHz | 3.3 W |
| 96 kHz | 6.6 W |

**Mitigation.** Reduce dead time to about 100 ns, which needs a tight gate loop and verified shoot-through margin. That saves 0.6 W per ESC. AM32's "variable PWM" option, which uses a lower frequency at high duty, is also worth enabling.

### 2.3 Total loss budget (80 A per motor, T_j = 125 °C)

| Source | Per ESC | ×4 board |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching + dead time | 3.3 W | 13.2 W |
| PCB copper | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, 1 per ESC) | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| Driver, LDO, MCU | 0.2 W | 0.8 W |
| **Total** | **~25.9 W** | **~104 W** |

At 4 × 80 A × 25.2 V ≈ 8 kW, efficiency is about 98.7%. The efficiency is fine; the **power density is the problem**.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Two-node RC network

Junction → board (case) → ambient/airflow:

$$C_{b}\frac{dT_b}{dt} = P_{tot} - \frac{T_b - T_a}{R_{\theta BA}}, \qquad T_j = T_b + P_{FET}\,Z_{\theta JC}(t)$$

Board solution:

$$T_b(t) = T_a + P R_{\theta BA}\,(1 - e^{-t/\tau}),\quad \tau = R_{\theta BA}C_b$$

### 3.2 Parameters

| Parameter | 20×20 nano | 30.5×30.5 |
|---|---|---|
| Board + FET mass | ~5 g | ~11 g |
| c_p (FR4/Cu mix) | ~0.7 J/g·K | ~0.7 J/g·K |
| C_b | **3.5 J/K** | **7.7 J/K** |
| R_θBA (prop wash, ~10 m/s) | ~4 K/W | ~2 K/W |
| τ | 14 s | 15 s |

### 3.3 Burst capability (T_a = 40 °C, board limit T_b = 105 °C, ΔT = 65 K)

During a short burst the board is near-adiabatic, so the heating rate is P/C_b:

**20×20 board, P = 104 W:**
- dT/dt ≈ 30 K/s, so the board reaches limit in **~2.2 s**.
- Steady state would be 40 + 104 × 4 = 456 °C, which is impossible.
- 20×20 sustained capability: ΔT = 65 K at R = 4 K/W allows P ≈ 16 W. That corresponds to about **28 A per motor continuous**.

**30.5×30.5 board:**
- dT/dt ≈ 13.5 K/s, so the board reaches limit in **~5 s** (exact: t = −τ·ln(1 − ΔT/(P·R)) ≈ 5.4 s).
- Continuous capability: P ≈ 32 W, about **40 A per motor**.

### 3.4 Junction overshoot

Per FET, P = 9.6 W (conduction) + ~1.6 W (switching share) ≈ 11 W. Steady state gives ΔT_j
