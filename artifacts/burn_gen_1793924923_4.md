# FOC Motor Control & Dual-IMU Sensor Fusion for a 3-Axis Gimbal

I don't have the Job #46 specifications, so this design uses the representative parameters listed in Section 0. All gains are derived from those parameters by formula. Substitute your measured motor and inertia values and the gains re-derive automatically through `designGains()`.

## 0. System Assumptions and Loop Timing

| Item | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Motor | Gimbal BLDC (e.g. GBM2804-class), 7 pole pairs (14P), 12 slots |
| Winding parameters | R = 5.6 Ω, L = 2.1 mH (phase), λ = 0.0085 V·s/rad, K_t = 1.5·p·λ ≈ 0.089 N·m/A |
| Load inertia | J ≈ 1.2×10⁻⁴ kg·m² (per axis, motor plus payload reflected) |
| Bus voltage | V_dc = 12 V |
| Encoder | 14-bit absolute magnetic encoder (AS5048A / MT6701) on each motor |
| IMUs | 2× ICM-42688-P on the camera plate, SPI at 24 MHz, ODR 8 kHz |
| MCU | Cortex-M7 or M4F with FPU (single-precision float throughout) |

**Loop rate hierarchy (each loop at least 4–5× faster than the bandwidth of the loop outside it):**

| Loop | Rate | Target bandwidth |
|---|---|---|
| Current (FOC) | 20 kHz, PWM-synchronous ISR | ~1.5 kHz |
| IMU acquisition and vibration filters | 8 kHz, IMU data-ready interrupt | n/a |
| Velocity (inertial rate) | 4 kHz | ~120 Hz |
| Attitude estimator and angle loop | 1 kHz | ~25 Hz |

A key architectural point: the velocity loop is closed on the **IMU gyro**, which measures inertial rate in the camera frame, not on encoder speed. That choice is what makes the gimbal reject base motion. The encoder supplies only commutation angle, the cogging phase, and the joint angles needed for the kinematic Jacobian.

## 1. Field-Oriented Current Loop

### 1.1 Transforms (amplitude-invariant)

**Clarke transform**, assuming i_a + i_b + i_c = 0:

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \frac{i_a + 2 i_b}{\sqrt3}
$$

**Park transform**, with electrical angle θ_e = p(θ_m − θ_off):

$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix}=
\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}
$$

**Inverse Park transform:**

$$
v_\alpha = v_d\cos\theta_e - v_q\sin\theta_e,\qquad v_\beta = v_d\sin\theta_e + v_q\cos\theta_e
$$

### 1.2 Plant model and decoupled PI design

The d-q voltage equations for a surface PMSM (L_d = L_q = L) are:

$$
v_d = R i_d + L\dot i_d - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad
v_q = R i_q + L\dot i_q + \omega_e (L i_d + \lambda)
$$

The cross-coupling and back-EMF terms are cancelled by feedforward:

$$
v_d = \text{PI}_d(i_d^* - i_d) - \omega_e L\, i_q,\qquad
v_q = \text{PI}_q(i_q^* - i_q) + \omega_e (L i_d + \lambda)
$$
