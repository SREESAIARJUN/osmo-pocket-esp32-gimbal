## 0. Feasibility Findings

These findings constrain everything that follows.

1. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** A 4-in-1 ESC needs its own commutation MCU(s), for example 4× AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to those MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs, not the H743.
2. **80 A per channel on a "nano" board (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) is burst-only.** The model below shows roughly 24 W dissipated per channel at 80 A, or about 97 W for the board with all four channels at full load. The steady-state convective capacity is about 0.2 W/K, so continuous operation is impossible. Section 3.5 shows the burst window is about 3–4 s. Specify the product as "80 A burst (≤2 s, 10% duty), ~25–30 A continuous with prop wash."
3. **The battery side carries up to 320 A.** This is a connector, trace, and capacitor-ripple problem as much as a FET problem (Section 4).

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, two phases carry current at any instant:
- During the PWM on-time, the current flows through the high-side FET of phase A and the low-side FET of phase B.
- During the off-time, the current flows through the low-side FET of phase A (synchronous freewheel) and the low-side FET of phase B.

In both states, exactly two FET channels carry the full motor current I. Therefore:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

This result is independent of duty cycle, apart from dead-time intervals, which are handled in Section 2.3.

### 2.2 Electrothermal iteration

R_DS(on) depends on junction temperature, which in turn depends on loss, so the model must be iterated:

$$R(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$
$$T_j = T_{board} + P_{FET}\,(R_{jc} + R_{spread})$$

Here R_spread ≈ 8 K/W is the local spreading resistance from the FET pad into an 8-layer, 2 oz board through a via array.

| Iteration | T_j (°C) | R_DS(on) (mΩ) | P_cond,bridge (W) | P per FET (W, ÷6) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 25 | 1.20 | 15.4 | 2.56 |
| 1 | 100 | 1.74 | 22.3 | 3.71 |
| 2 | 110 | 1.81 | 23.2 | 3.86 |
| Converged | ~112 | ~1.83 | **≈ 23.4 W** | ≈ 3.9 W |

The ÷6 averaging is valid because over one electrical revolution each FET conducts for 120° in its high- or low-side role. The instantaneous per-FET loss is higher (about 2× during its conduction interval). For electrical periods above about 10 ms this matters, because the die thermal time constant is only a few ms. At normal eRPM, averaging is acceptable.

### 2.3 Dead-time and body-diode conduction

During each dead-time interval, the freewheeling current flows through a body diode:

$$P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing half-bridge switches hard. The low-side FET of the conducting phase is static.

### 3.1 V–I overlap loss

The transition times are set by the Miller plateau:

$$t_{r} \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{gate}} \approx \frac{9\,\text{nC}}{\sim0.75\,\text{A (effective at plateau)}} \approx 12\,\text{ns}$$

$$P_{ov} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 24\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.16\ W}$$
