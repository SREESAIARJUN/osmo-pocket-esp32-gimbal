# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #12): 80A Burst Thermal, 48 kHz Switching Loss, and Bidirectional DShot600 DMA Allocation

## 0. Architecture Clarification

**ArduPilot cannot commutate motors from the STM32H743.** No supported firmware path exists for driving gate drivers directly from the flight controller. The correct partitioning is:

| Block | Device | Firmware |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (480 MHz, LQFP100) | ArduPilot (custom `hwdef.dat`) |
| ESC MCUs (×4) | AT32F421K8U7 (QFN32) | AM32, bidirectional DShot, EDT |
| Gate drivers (×4) | FD6288Q (3-phase, 250 V, bootstrap) | n/a |
| Power stage | 24× N-MOSFET, 40 V, PDFN 3.3×3.3 or 5×6 | n/a |

The link from the FC to each ESC is bidirectional DShot600 on one wire per motor. The rest of this document uses that architecture.

**Baseline assumptions** (all flagged; replace with your actual BOM values):

- 6S pack: V_bus = 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal.
- f_sw = 48 kHz, 6-step trapezoidal with complementary (synchronous) PWM.
- Burst requirement: 80 A per channel.
- Board ambient: T_a = 40 °C.
- Reference MOSFET (40 V, PDFN 3.3×3.3 class):
  - R_ds(on) = 1.2 mΩ at 25 °C, V_gs = 10 V.
  - Q_g = 50 nC, Q_rr = 30 nC, Q_oss = 40 nC.
  - Z_th(j-c) steady-state = 1.5 K/W.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Conduction topology

In 6-step operation with synchronous rectification, phase current always flows through exactly two MOSFETs in series:

- the PWM leg (high side for duty D, low side for 1–D), and
- the static low-side FET of the return phase.

The instantaneous conduction resistance is therefore:

$$R_{path}(T_j) = 2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j) + R_{Cu}$$

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{ds(on)} = 1.2 \times 1.6 = 1.92\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Per-channel conduction loss at 80 A

Trapezoidal current is approximately flat-topped, so I_rms ≈ I_dc.

$$P_{cond} = I^2\left(2R_{ds}(T_j) + R_{Cu}\right)$$

$$P_{cond} = 80^2\left(3.84\ \text{m}\Omega + 0.5\ \text{m}\Omega\right) = 6400 \times 4.34\ \text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{27.8\ W}$$

This splits into:

- MOSFETs: 24.6 W, or ≈4.1 W average per FET across 6 FETs.
- Copper: 3.2 W, assuming R_Cu = 0.5 mΩ for the phase pad, bus, and shunt path. This is optimistic for a nano board.
- Shunt: if a shunt is used, add I²R separately (0.5 mΩ → 3.2 W). Most nano 4-in-1 boards share one shunt or omit it.

### 1.4 Electrothermal convergence

R_ds depends on T_j, and T_j depends on P. Iterate:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_{ref} + P_{FET}\!\left(T_j^{(k)}\right) \cdot Z_{th}(t)$$

Converge until |ΔT_j| < 0.5 K. **Thermal runaway** occurs if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\, Z_{th} \ge 1 \;\Rightarrow\; I^2 \cdot 2R_{25}\,\alpha \cdot Z_{th} \ge 1$$

At 80 A:

$$6400 \times 2 \times 1.2\times10^{-3} \times 0.006 \times Z_{th} = 0.092\,Z_{th}$$

- **Runaway threshold:** Z_th(total) ≥ 10.9 K/W. A nano board in still air without airflow can approach this value, so **prop-wash airflow is mandatory**.

## 2. Switching Loss at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. The loss components are:

$$P_{sw} = \underbrace{\tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f}_{\text{V-I overlap}} + \underbrace{Q_{oss} V f}_{\text{C}_{oss}} + \underbrace{Q_{rr} V f}_{\text{reverse recovery}} + \underbrace{2 V_f I\, t_{dt} f}_{\text{dead-time diode}} + \underbrace{2 Q_g V_{drv} f}_{\text{gate}}$$

Inputs: V = 25.2 V, I = 80 A, f = 48 kHz, t_r + t_f = 40 ns (FD6288 at ~1.5 A source/sink with R_g = 4.7 Ω), t_dt = 100 ns, V_f = 0.8 V, V_drv = 10 V.

| Term | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | 0.5 · 25.2 · 80 · 40 ns · 48 k | **1.94 W** |
| C_oss | 40 nC · 25.2 · 48 k | 0.05 W |
| Q_rr | 30 nC · 25.2 · 48 k | 0.04 W |
| Dead-time diode | 2 · 0.8 · 80 · 100 ns · 48 k | **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (driver dissipation) | 2 · 50 nC · 10 · 48 k | 0.05 W |
| **Total per channel** | | **≈2.7 W** |

### Sensitivity

- P_sw scales linearly with f. At 24 kHz it is ≈1.35 W; at 96 kHz it is ≈5.4 W.
- Reducing dead time to 50 ns (AM32 `DEAD_TIME` tuning) saves ≈0.3 W per channel.
- A 48 kHz rate is a reasonable compromise between audible noise and loss. Conduction loss dominates by a factor of about 10.

## 3. Total Burst Thermal Budget

| Case | Per-channel loss | Board total |
|---|---|---|
| 1 motor at 80 A, 3 motors at 20 A cruise | 30.5 W + 3 × ~2.1 W | ≈ 37 W |
| **All 4 at 80 A** | 30.5 W | **≈ 122 W** |

### 3.1 Lumped transient model (2-node Cauer)

$$C_{b}\frac{dT_b}{dt} = P_{tot} - \frac{T_b - T_a}{R_{b-a}}$$

$$T_j = T_b + P_{FET,peak}\cdot Z_{th(j-b)}(t)$$

Nano 30×30 board estimates (8-layer, 2 oz):

- Thermal mass: C_b ≈ 5 J/K (FR4 plus copper plus FETs, ≈ 6 g).
- R_b-a: ≈ 4 K/W with prop-wash airflow, ≈ 12 K/W in still air.

### 3.2 All four channels at 80 A

Initial slope:

$$\frac{dT_b}{dt} = \frac{122}{5} \approx 24\ \text{K/s}$$

The time constant is τ = R·C = 20 s with airflow. Solving for T_b(t) = T_a + P·R(1 − e^{−t/τ}) with a limit of T_b = 105 °C (leaving ≈20 K of junction rise headroom):

$$65 = 488\,(1 - e^{-t/20}) \;\Rightarrow\; t \approx \mathbf{2.9\ s}$$

At that point:

- Peak FET loss: ≈5.5 W (static low-side, worst case).
- Junction rise: ΔT_j-b ≈ 5.5 × 1.5
