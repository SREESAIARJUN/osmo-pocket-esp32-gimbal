# STM32H743 FC + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC: Engineering Model Package (Job #59)

## 0. Architecture and Scope

**The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each ESC channel has its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32 firmware, typically an STM32G071 or AT32F421. That MCU drives a 3-phase gate driver and six MOSFETs. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to each ESC over one bidirectional DShot600 wire. The math below follows that partition:

| Domain | Owner | Section |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction and switching losses at 48 kHz | ESC MCU + power stage | 1–3 |
| Board thermal / burst duration | Shared PCB | 4 |
| Bidirectional DShot600, timers, DMA | H743 / ArduPilot | 5 |
| Netlists | Both | 6 |

**Physical reality check.**
- 4 × 80 A at 6S is about 8 kW peak. That is battery-limited (a 1300 mAh 120C pack gives about 156 A) and far beyond any nano board's steady-state capacity.
- The "80 A" figure is therefore treated as a **time-limited burst rating**, and Section 4 calculates how long it can last.
- The board is assumed to be 36 × 36 mm with a 30.5 mm mount. A true 20 × 20 board cannot credibly carry 80 A per channel.
