# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80 A ESC: Electrothermal and DShot Architecture (Job #49)

## 0. Two Architectural Corrections

Two points need settling before any numbers, because they change the design.

1. **The H743 should not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 stack, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running BLHeli_32, AM32 or Bluejay firmware. Typical parts are the AT32F421, STM32G071 or EFM8. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to those ESC MCUs. The 48 kHz PWM, dead-time and back-EMF zero-crossing loops live on the ESC side. This document models it that way.
2. **80 A on a nano (20×20 mm) form factor is a short-burst rating, not a continuous one.** The thermal model in §3 shows the board survives about 1–3 s at 80 A per motor. Treat "80 A" as a ≤2 s burst spec, or move to 30×30 mm with heavier copper.

All device parameters below are stated assumptions, chosen to be representative of a 40 V, ~0.8 mΩ, 5×6 mm DFN MOSFET. Substitute your selected part's datasheet values into the equations.

## 2. Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

**Hot on-resistance:**

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)] = 0.80\,(1+0.0045\cdot85) = 1.106\ \text{m}\Omega$$

**Commutation-state analysis.** The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM. In any 60° sector, two phases carry current:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for D·T. The low-side FET conducts synchronously for (1−D)·T, minus dead-time.
- **Static leg:** one low-side FET is on 100%.

**Channel conduction loss.** At every instant, exactly two R_ds are in series with the phase current, independent of duty:

$$P_{cond,FET} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[\underbrace{1}_{\text{static}} + \underbrace{D + (1-D)}_{\text{PWM leg}}] = 2I^2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,FET} = 2\cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.106\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{14.16\ W}$$

**Copper loss:**

$$P_{cu} = I^2 R_{cu}(T) = 6400\cdot0.5\text{ m}\Omega\cdot(1+0.0039\cdot60) \approx \mathbf{3.95\ W}$$

The copper resistance is scaled to an ~85 °C trace temperature.

**Per-FET average.** Each of the 6 FETs carries its role for 1/3 of the electrical period, so the average is about 14.16 / 6 ≈ 2.36 W per FET. The instantaneous peak is higher, at up to I²R ≈ 7.1 W while conducting.

### 2.2 Switching losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Each transition is hard on one edge and soft (diode-clamped) on the other.

**V–I overlap loss:**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}V_{bus}I(t_r+t_f)f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot30\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead-times per period):

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\,I\,(2t_{dt})f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 50\text{nC}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,eff}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot1.5\text{nF}\cdot635\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.02\ W}$$

**Gate drive** (two FETs switching, dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors):

$$P_g = 2\,Q_gV_{drv}f_{sw} = 2\cdot60\text{nC}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

**Total switching loss:**

$$P_{sw} \approx 1.45+0.61+0.06+0.02 = \mathbf{2.14\ W}$$

The gate-drive term is excluded here and treated as driver loss.

### 2.3 Totals at 80 A burst

| Loss | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 14.16 W | 56.6 W |
| Switching | 2.14 W | 8.6 W |
| Copper | 3.95 W | 15.8 W |
| Gate driver + shunt + misc. | ~0.5 W | 2 W |
| **Total** | **~20.8 W** | **~83 W** |

**Efficiency.** Output power is about 2.0 kW per motor at full duty, so efficiency is roughly 99%. The absolute dissipation is still large for the board area.

**Design sensitivity.** Conduction dominates, roughly 87% of the total.

- **Lowering f_sw does little.** Going from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves only about 1 W per channel.
- **Lowering R_ds is the effective lever.** Paralleling two FETs per switch halves P_cond, saving about 7 W per channel, but costs area you do not have on 20×20 mm.
