# Job #49: STM32H743 AIO Nano Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC
## Electro-Thermal Model, Bi-Directional DShot600 DMA Plan, and Reference Netlist

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Architectural Clarification

**The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every practical AIO design, each motor channel has its own small ESC MCU running AM32, BLHeli_32, or Bluejay, with a 3-phase gate driver. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends **bi-directional DShot600** to each ESC MCU. ArduPilot never sees the 48 kHz PWM. The 48 kHz is the ESC firmware's `PWM_FREQ` setting.

No MOSFET part was specified, so I use a parameterized model and evaluate it with a representative 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm PQFN device. **Replace these numbers with your selected part's datasheet values before sign-off.**

| Symbol | Meaning | Value used |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 6S nominal-full | 25.2 V |
| I_ph | Phase current at burst | 80 A per motor |
| f_sw | ESC PWM frequency | 48 kHz |
| R_ds25 | R_DS(on) @ 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | 1.0 mΩ |
| α | R_DS(on) tempco (linearized) | 0.006 /K |
| N_p | FETs in parallel per switch | 2 (12 FETs per motor, 48 total) |
| t_r, t_f | Effective V-I overlap times | 15 ns each |
| Q_rr | Body diode reverse recovery charge | 40 nC |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.006 × 85) = **1.51 mΩ**.

With N_p FETs in parallel per switch, the effective switch resistance is:

$$
R_{sw} = \frac{R_{ds}(T_j)}{N_p}
$$

### 1.2 Six-step (trapezoidal) commutation with synchronous rectification

At any instant, two phases carry current. Current flows through one high-side switch and one low-side switch in series:

- The PWM'd leg alternates between its HS (fraction D) and its synchronous LS (fraction 1−D).
- The return leg's LS is fully on for the 60° sector.

Regardless of D, the current path always passes through exactly two switch positions:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{sw}
$$

Per-device RMS current, with each switch position conducting for 120° of 360° (1/3 duty):

$$
I_{rms,switch} = I_{ph}\sqrt{1/3}, \qquad I_{rms,FET} = \frac{I_{ph}}{N_p}\sqrt{1/3}
$$

### 1.3 Evaluated at 80 A burst, T_j = 110 °C

| Configuration | R_sw | P_cond per motor | P_cond, 4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|
| N_p = 1 (6 FETs) | 1.51 mΩ | 80² × 3.02 mΩ = **19.3 W** | 77.3 W |
| N_p = 2 (12 FETs) | 0.755 mΩ | **9.66 W** | 38.7 W |

**Conclusion:** single-FET-per-switch is not viable at 80 A on a nano footprint. N_p = 2 is assumed below.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches hard. Each component below is per motor.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V-I overlap

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}
= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = 1.45\ \text{W}
$$

The overlap times can be estimated from the driver strength and the Miller charge:

$$
t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}}, \quad I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}{R_{g,ext} + R_{g,int} + R_{drv,on}}
$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

The LS body diode recovers when the HS turns on:

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance (hard-switched Q_oss)

$$
P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.036\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time edges per PWM period:

$$
P_{dt} = 2 V_F I_{ph} t_{dt} f_{sw} = 2 \times 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}
$$

This term is significant. Tune dead time to the minimum safe value (AM32 `DEAD_TIME`). Going from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per motor.

### 2.5 Gate drive

Two parallel FETs switch per edge on the active leg:

$$
P_{gate} = N_p Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2 = 2 \times 40\,\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\,\text{k} \times 2 \approx 0.077\ \text{W}
$$

This power is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FETs.

### 2.6 Duty-cycle caveat

At true 100% throttle (D → 1), AM32 holds the HS on and switching loss approaches zero. The worst-case electro-thermal point is high current at D ≈ 0.85–0.95. That is a burst under load with sag, where switching continues. The full switching budget is applied at 80 A for conservatism.

### 2.7 Per-motor loss summary at 80 A, 6S, 48 kHz, N_p = 2

| Loss term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction | 9.66 |
| V-I overlap | 1.45 |
| Dead time | 0.61 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.08 |
| Gate (driver side) | 0.08 |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ shared bus shunt, per-motor share) | 1.6 |
| **Total per motor** | **≈ 13.5 W** |
| **4 motors at 80 A simultaneously** | **≈ 54 W** |

### 2.8 Frequency trade-off

The frequency-dependent portion of the loss is:

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial f_{sw}} \approx \frac{2.14\ \text{W}}{48\ \text{kHz}} = 44.6\ \text{mW/kHz per motor}
$$

Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.07 W per motor, at the cost of higher current ripple and audible noise. The 48 kHz choice is justified for low-inductance racing motors. Note that phase ripple is ΔI ≈ V(1−D)D / (L f_sw).
