## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Flags

### Topology

The STM32H743 cannot directly commutate four motors at 48 kHz in a production design. The correct topology is:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot FC) ──4× bidir DShot600──► 4× ESC MCU (AT32F421K8U7, AM32 firmware)
                                                    └─► 4× FD6288Q 3-phase gate driver
                                                          └─► 24× N-MOSFET (4 × 3 half-bridges)
```

### Feasibility flags to raise before layout

| # | Issue | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | "80A" on a nano (20×20 mm mount, ~27×27 mm PCB) | Achievable only as a **burst rating of ≤2–3 s**. It is not a continuous rating (see §2.6). Recommend marketing "80A burst / 35A continuous per channel." |
| F2 | System current: 4 × 80 A = 320 A at 6S, about 8 kW | XT60 (60 A cont.), 12 AWG leads, and the battery pads cannot carry this. Realistic simultaneous peak is about 4 × 50 A. Per-channel 80 A applies only to asymmetric transients. |
| F3 | A single shared current-sense shunt at 320 A | 0.25 mΩ × 320² = **25.6 W**. Reject. Use per-channel low-side sensing in the ESC MCU, plus a Hall-type or 0.1 mΩ telemetry shunt sized for 200 A. |
| F4 | Bulk capacitance | A nano footprint cannot hold enough ripple-rated capacitance. An external 1000 µF/35 V low-ESR cap on the battery leads is a mandatory accessory. |

## 2. Thermal and Loss Model

### 2.1 Current-path model (6-step, synchronous)

During any 60° commutation sector, one phase is PWM'd and one is held low. The current path is always **two FETs in series**:

- **On-state (duty D):** HS_A → motor → LS_B
- **Freewheel (1−D):** LS_A (sync-rectified) → motor → LS_B

So the RMS-equivalent conduction resistance per channel is always 2·R_ds(on), independent of D, ignoring dead time.

### 2.2 Conduction loss

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot \left(2 R_{ds(on)}(T_j) + R_{Cu}\right)
$$

$$
R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds(on),25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

At T_j = 125 °C, R_ds = 1.6 mΩ:

$$
P_{cond} = 80^2 \times (3.2 + 0.4)\ \text{m}\Omega = 6400 \times 3.6\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{23.0\ W\ per\ channel}
$$

- FET share: 20.5 W. Copper share: 2.56 W.
- Per-FET average: each FET conducts about 1/3 of the electrical cycle, so 20.5 / 6 ≈ **3.4 W average** per FET.
- Instantaneous peak per FET: 80² × 1.6 mΩ = **10.2 W**.

**Electro-thermal feedback.** Because R_ds depends on T_j, the loss must be solved iteratively:

$$
T_j = T_a + P(T_j)\cdot R_{th,ja} \;\Rightarrow\; T_j = \frac{T_a + I^2 R_0 (1-25\alpha) R_{th}}{1 - I^2 R_0 \alpha R_{th}}
$$

Thermal runaway occurs if I²·R₀·α·R_th ≥ 1. This is the continuous-rating limit used in §2.6.

### 2.3 Switching losses at 48 kHz (per channel, one hard-switched leg)

**(a) V-I overlap:**

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.968\ W}
$$

**(b) Reverse recovery** (low-side body diode conducts during dead time):

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

**(c) Output capacitance:**

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \cdot 2 = 30\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}
$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead times per PWM period):

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD} \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.614\ W}
$$

**(e) Gate drive** (dissipated mostly in R_g and the driver, not the FET):

$$
P_g = 2 \cdot Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

**Switching subtotal: about 1.71 W per channel.**

> **Design note.** Dead-time loss (0.61 W) is almost two-thirds of the switching loss. Cutting t_dt from 100 ns to 50 ns saves 0.3 W per channel, but you must verify there is no shoot-through across process, voltage, and temperature. At 48 kHz, 100 ns is also 0.96 % of the PWM period, which costs about 1 % of effective duty and adds low-throttle nonlinearity.
