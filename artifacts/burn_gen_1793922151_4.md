## 0. Architecture Clarification and Constraints

The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. A correct 4-in-1 architecture is:

| Block | Device (reference) | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller MCU | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100, 480 MHz max, run at 400 MHz) | ArduPilot, DShot600 TX, eRPM RX |
| ESC MCU (×4, or ×1 per pair) | AT32F421K8U7 / STM32G071KBU6 | AM32 firmware, 48 kHz complementary PWM |
| Gate driver (×4) | FD6288Q (3-phase, bootstrap) | High-side and low-side drive |
| Power MOSFETs (×24) | 40 V, ~1.0 mΩ, PQFN 3.3×3.3 class | 6 per motor |

**Three findings up front.** These drive the rest of the document.

1. **"80 A burst" is a time-limited rating, not a thermal steady state.** On a nano-class board it is limited to roughly 2–5 s, depending on starting temperature (derived in §3).
2. **At true 100 % throttle, switching losses collapse to zero.** The worst combined-loss operating point is high current at mid duty, not "full throttle" (see §2).
3. **The bus capacitor ripple current (~40 A RMS per channel at D = 0.5) is the hidden failure mode** of nano 4-in-1 boards, not the FETs (see §4).

### 1.2 Per-FET conduction duty in 6-step trapezoidal drive

Over one electrical cycle, each half-bridge leg spends three intervals:
- **120° as the "PWM" leg.** The high side (HS) conducts for fraction D, and the low side (LS) synchronously rectifies for 1−D.
- **120° as the "return" leg.** The LS conducts 100 %.
- **60° floating**, used for back-EMF zero-cross sensing.

This gives the following average conduction fractions:

$$\delta_{HS} = \frac{D}{3},\qquad \delta_{LS} = \frac{(1-D) + 1}{3} = \frac{2-D}{3}$$

Summing over 3 HS and 3 LS devices gives 3·(D/3) + 3·((2−D)/3) = 2. **Exactly two FETs carry current at every instant, independent of D.**
