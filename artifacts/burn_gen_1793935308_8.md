# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC (Job #16): Architecture & Design Package

## 0. Architectural Ground Truth

Three corrections shape the rest of the design.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** The 4-in-1 ESC needs its own commutation MCU (AT32F421 running AM32, or EFM8/STM32G071 running Bluejay/BLHeli_32). The H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600. The 48 kHz PWM, dead-time and back-EMF sensing all live on the ESC MCU.
2. **"80A" must be a burst rating with a defined duration.** A 20×20 mm (nano) stack cannot dissipate 80A per motor continuously. Section 3 derives a sustained rating of about 15–20 A per motor and a burst window of roughly 1 s. Any marketing spec must say "80A burst (≤1 s), 20A continuous."
3. **Input capacitance is the hidden killer, not the FETs.** Section 4 shows that a nano form factor cannot hold enough bulk capacitance. An external low-ESR capacitor on the battery pads is mandatory.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-step synchronous commutation

In any commutation sector, one phase is PWM'd high-side with a complementary synchronous low-side, one phase is held low-side ON, and one phase floats for BEMF sensing. With duty D:

- **PWM high-side:** I²·R·D
- **Synchronous low-side (same leg):** I²·R·(1−D)
- **Return-leg low-side:** I²·R·1

Summing the three terms:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{hot}\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I^2 R_{hot}$$

This result is independent of duty, which is a useful property of synchronous rectification.

### 1.2 Including the copper path

PCB plus solder resistance per current path is R_Cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ for 2 oz inner layers stacked across 3 layers over a ~8 mm path:

$$P_{cond,total} = 2 I^2 (R_{hot} + R_{Cu}) $$

### 1.3 Numbers at the 80A burst

| Item | Calculation | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 2 × 80² × 1.26 mΩ | **16.1 W** | 64.5 W |
| Copper | 2 × 80² × 0.30 mΩ | 3.8 W | 15.4 W |
| **Subtotal** | | **19.9 W** | **79.9 W** |

### 1.4 Worst single FET

The return-leg low-side conducts 100% of its sector, which is one-third of the electrical cycle. The worst-case instantaneous dissipation in that FET is:

$$P_{FET,max} = I^2 R_{hot} = 6400 \times 1.26\text{ mΩ} = 8.1\text{ W (instantaneous, 1/3 duty)}$$

Averaged over rotation, each FET carries about (2/6) of the motor's conduction loss, i.e. about 2.7 W each.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Hard-switching transition loss (high-side PWM FET only)

The transition times follow from the Miller charge and gate current:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,src}} = \frac{8\text{ nC}}{1.0\text{ A}} = 8\text{ ns}, \quad t_f \approx \frac{8\text{ nC}}{1.5\text{ A}} \approx 5.3\text{ ns}$$

Add about 30% for the gate-loop inductance and plateau sag, giving t_r + t_f ≈ 17 ns:

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 17\text{ns} \times 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.82\text{ W}}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction (synchronous FET)

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.61\text{ W}}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

Use Q_rr ≈ 30 nC at 80 A with a fast di/dt:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{nC}\times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.036\text{ W}}$$

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} \approx 40\text{nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k}\times0.5 = \mathbf{0.024\text{ W}}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (2 switching FETs per motor; dissipated in driver and R_g, not in the FETs)

$$P_{gate} = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{nC} \times 10\text{V} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\text{ W}}$$

### 2.6 Switching summary

| Loss | Per motor |
|---|---|
| Transition | 0.82 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.06 W |
| Gate drive | 0.05 W |
| **Total switching** | **1.54 W** |

**Takeaway:** at 48 kHz
