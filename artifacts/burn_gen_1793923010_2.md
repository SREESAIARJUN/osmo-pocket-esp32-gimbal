# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Thermal Model and Bidirectional DShot600 Architecture

## 0. Architectural Corrections and Assumptions

These points determine whether the rest of the numbers hold, so they come first.

1. **The STM32H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC controller.** Every commercial 4-in-1 design uses one small MCU per motor (AT32F421, STM32G071 or EFM8BB51) running AM32 or BLHeli_32/Bluejay firmware. The H743 cannot do sensorless 6-step commutation, back-EMF zero-cross detection and 48 kHz complementary PWM for four motors while also running ArduPilot's EKF3 loop. This document assumes **H743 (ArduPilot) → 4× ESC MCUs (AM32) over bidirectional DShot600**.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is not physically sustainable beyond about 1–3 s**, as shown in §2. The model below is valid. The conclusion is that a 20×20 board needs 30×30 layout, a heatsink, or a lower rating.
3. **All device parameters are representative placeholders.** Replace them with your selected MOSFET and driver datasheet values before signing off. Equations are given so you can re-plug numbers.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value used |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S, full) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Burst phase current | I | 80 A |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| MOSFET R_DS(on) @ 25 °C (40 V, DFN5×6 class) | R_25 | 1.0 mΩ |
| R_DS(on) tempco factor @ T_j = 125 °C | k_T | 1.5 → R_hot = 1.5 mΩ |

## 1. Electrical Loss Model (per motor, 6-step trapezoidal, complementary PWM)

### 1.1 Conduction loss

In any 60° commutation sector, two phase legs carry current I:
- **PWM leg:** the high-side conducts for D, and the synchronous low-side conducts for (1−D).
- **Return leg:** the low-side is fully on.

Each active leg therefore always has exactly one FET in series with the current path. Ignoring dead time:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 \left[ D R_{hot} + (1-D) R_{hot} \right] + I^2 R_{hot} = 2 I^2 R_{hot}
$$

$$
P_{cond} = 2 (80)^2 (1.5\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{19.2\ W}
$$

Copper loss: P_pcb = I²·R_pcb = 6400 × 0.5 mΩ = **3.2 W**

Ripple correction: if peak-to-peak ripple is ΔI, multiply by (1 + ΔI²/(12 I²)). This is negligible at 80 A with typical motor inductance.

### 1.2 Switching loss (hard-switched PWM leg only)

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 (25.2)(80)(40\times10^{-9})(48\times10^3) = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

### 1.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 (80)(200\times10^{-9})(48\times10^3) = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

### 1.4 Reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} (25.2)(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.060\ W}
$$

### 1.5 C_oss loss

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,er} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 (1.5\text{n})(635)(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.023\ W}
$$

### 1.6 Gate drive (two switching FETs in PWM leg)

$$
P_{g} = 2 Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2(50\text{n})(10)(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

This is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FETs.

### 1.7 Totals at 80 A burst

| Loss | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 19.20 W | 76.8 W |
| PCB copper | 3.20 W | 12.8 W |
| Switching | 1.94 W | 7.8 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W | 2.4 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.08 W | 0.3 W |
| Gate drive | 0.05 W | 0.2 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 25.1 W** | **≈ 100 W** |

Electrical efficiency at burst: P_out ≈ 4 × 25.2 V × 80 A × D. At D ≈ 0.9 that is about 7.26 kW, giving η ≈ 98.6%. Efficiency looks fine; **heat density is the actual problem**.

**Key insight:** conduction loss dominates by roughly 10:1, so 48 kHz vs 24 kHz barely matters for thermals (−1.3 W/motor at 24 kHz). The value of 48 kHz is lower current ripple and acoustic noise. The lever that matters is R_DS(on) and copper.

### 1.8 Per-FET dissipation

With synchronous rectification, each leg is active for 2/3 of the electrical cycle. Averaged:

$$
P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{P_{cond}+P_{sw}+P_{dt}}{6} \approx \frac{21.8}{6} \approx \mathbf{3.6\ W}
$$

The instantaneous peak per FET while conducting full current is I²R_hot = **9.6 W**.

## 2. Thermal Model

### 2.1 Network (per FET → board → air)

```
Tj ──[Rth_jc 1.2 K/W]── Tcase ──[Rth_cb 2 K/W, solder + via array]── PCB node ──[Rth_ba]── Tamb
        │ Cth_j ~0.05 J/K          │                                   │ Cth_board
```

### 2.2 Steady state (shows why "burst" is the only legal rating)

The board-to-air resistance for the whole board is roughly R_th,ba ≈ 4–6 K/W for 30×30 in prop-wash (≈8 m/s), and ≈ 8–12 K/W for 20×20.

For a 30×30 board at 100 W:

$$
\Delta T_{board} = 100 \times 5 = 500\ \text{K}
$$

**Continuous 80 A is impossible.** The sustainable continuous loss for ΔT_board = 60 K (T_amb 35 °C, board ≈ 95 °C) is P ≈ 12 W total. Solve for current with conduction dominating, P ≈ 4 × 2.5 I² R_eff, where R_eff ≈ 2R_hot + R_pcb = 3.5 mΩ:

$$
I_{cont} \approx \sqrt{\frac{12}{4 \times 3.5\times10^{-3}}} \approx \mathbf{29\ A\ per\ motor}
$$

That is a realistic continuous rating for a 30×30, 1 mΩ-class design. It is about 15–20 A for a 20×20 board with 3×3 FETs.

### 2.3 Transient (burst) model

Board lumped heat capacity, 30×30, 8-layer 2 oz, with FETs and connectors:
- Copper ≈ 6 g × 0.385 J/(g·K) = 2.3 J/K
- FR4 ≈ 8 g × 0.9 J/(g·K) = 7.2 J/K
- Components ≈ 1 J/K
- **C_board ≈ 10.5 J/K**

The time constant is τ = R_th,ba × C_board ≈ 5 × 10.5 ≈ 52 s. Since τ is much longer than a burst, the board is near-adiabatic during it:

$$
\Delta T_{board}(t) \approx \frac{P}{C} t = \frac{100}{10.5} t \approx 9.5\ \text{K/s}
$$
