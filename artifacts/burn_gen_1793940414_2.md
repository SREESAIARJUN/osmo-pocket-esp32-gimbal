## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. In any production 4-in-1 design, it is the **flight controller (FC)**. Each of the four ESC channels has its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to those MCUs and receives eRPM telemetry back on the same wire.

```
            ┌──────────────────────────┐   DShot600 bidir (×4, single wire each)
 IMU/Baro ──┤  STM32H743 FC (480 MHz)  ├──────────────┬────────┬────────┬────────┐
 RX/GPS   ──┤  ArduPilot ChibiOS       │              │        │        │        │
            └──────────────────────────┘          ESC MCU1  ESC MCU2  ESC MCU3  ESC MCU4
                                                  (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32, 48 kHz PWM)
                                                      │        │        │        │
                                                  3-ph gate driver + 6× N-MOSFET per channel
```

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology Behaviour

The ESC drives the motor trapezoidally (six-step) with complementary, synchronous-rectified PWM. In each 60° sector, two phase legs are active:

- **PWM'd leg:** current flows through the high-side FET for duty D, then the low-side FET for (1 − D).
- **Return leg:** its low-side FET is held on continuously.

So at every instant, exactly **two** FET channels carry the full phase current I, independent of duty cycle.

### 1.2 Temperature-Dependent Resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

### 1.3 Per-Channel Conduction Loss

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

**Self-heating feedback.** Junction temperature depends on the loss, and the loss depends on junction temperature:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + P_{FET}\,R_{\theta}$$

Here P_FET ≈ I²R_ds(T_j) is the loss of one conducting FET. Solving gives:

$$T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + I^2 R_{25} R_\theta (1-25\alpha)}{1 - I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\, R_\theta}$$

**Stability criterion (thermal runaway):** the denominator must stay positive.

$$I^2 R_{25}\alpha R_\theta < 1$$

At 80 A with R_θ = 1.5 K/W:

$$6400 \times 0.001 \times 0.006 \times 1.5 = 0.058$$

This is well below 1, so the stage is stable at the junction-to-case level.

**Hot-case values.** Taking T_j ≈ 108 °C (from Section 3):

- R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.006 × 83) ≈ **1.50 mΩ**
- P_cond,ch = 2 × 6400 × 0.0015 = **19.2 W per channel**
- Per conducting FET: **9.6 W**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg hard-switches. The model counts one hard turn-on and one hard turn-off per PWM period.

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw | ½ · 25.2 · 80 · 20 ns · 48 k = **0.968 W** |
| C_oss discharge | ½ · C_oss · V² · f_sw | ½ · 1.5 n · 635 · 48 k = **0.023 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw | 50 n · 25.2 · 48 k = **0.060 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_SD · I · 2t_dt · f_sw | 0.8 · 80 · 200 ns · 48 k = **0.614 W** |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver/R_g) | 2 · Q_g · V_GS · f_sw | 2 · 60 n · 10 · 48 k = **0.058 W** |
| **Total switching-related** | | **≈ 1.67 W per channel** |

**Key observations:**

- **Dead-time loss is nearly as large as the overlap loss.** Trimming dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns (if the driver and FET allow it) saves about 0.3 W per channel.
- **Frequency trade-off.** At 48 kHz, switching loss is about 8% of conduction loss. Moving to 24 kHz saves about 0.8 W per channel but raises ripple current and acoustic noise. 48 kHz is a reasonable choice for low-inductance quad motors.

## 3. Total Loss and Thermal Model

### 3.1 Loss Budget

| | Per channel | Board (4 ch, all at 80 A) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching + dead time + Q_rr | 1.67 W | 6.7 W |
| Shunt (0.2 mΩ, if per-channel) | 1.28 W | 5.1 W |
| Input capacitor ESR (see 3.4) | ~0.5 W | ~2 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 22.6 W** | **≈ 90 W** |

### 3.2 Steady-State Verdict: Not Sustainable

Assume a well-cooled nano board in prop wash with a board-to-ambient thermal resistance of R_θBA ≈ 3–5 K/W.

90 W × 3 K/W gives a **270 K rise**, which is physically impossible. **"80 A" must be specified and enforced as a time-limited burst rating.**

The continuous rating follows from the allowed board rise:

$$4 \cdot 2 I_{cont}^2 R_{ds,hot} \approx \frac{\Delta T_{allow}}{R_{\theta BA}}$$

With ΔT_allow = 60 K, R_θBA = 3 K/W, and R_ds,hot = 1.4 mΩ:

$$I_{cont} \approx \sqrt{\frac{20}{8 \times 0.0014}} \approx 42\ \text{A per channel (all four loaded)}$$

A realistic label for this design is **40 A continuous / 80 A burst**.

### 3.3 Burst (Lumped-Capacitance) Model

**Board heat capacity:**

| Component | Calculation | Heat capacity |
|---|---|---|
| Copper | 9.3 cm² × 70 µm × 8 layers × 8.96 g/cm³ ≈ 4.7 g × 0.385 J/g·K | 1.8 J/K |
| FR-4 | 9.3 cm² × 1.6 mm × 1.85 g/cm³ ≈ 2.75 g × 1.1 J/g·K | 3.0 J/K |
| FETs, solder, caps | — | ~0.7 J/K |
| **Total C_th** | | **≈ 5.5 J/K** |

**Governing equation and solution:**

$$C_{th}\frac{dT_b}{dt} = P_{loss}(T_b) - \frac{T_b - T_a}{R_{\theta BA}}$$

$$T_b(t) = T_a + P R_{\theta BA}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right), \qquad \tau = R_{\theta BA}\,C_{th} \approx 3 \times 5.5 = 16.5\ \text{s}$$

**Allowed burst duration** (board limit 100 °C, T_a = 35 °C, so ΔT = 65 K):

$$t_{burst} = -\tau \ln\left(1 - \frac{\Delta T}{P R_{\theta BA}}\right)$$
