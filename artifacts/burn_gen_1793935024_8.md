# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #20): Design Package Rev A

## 0. Architecture Decisions and Assumptions

### 0.1 The H743 should not commutate the motors

ArduPilot cannot run four sensorless 48 kHz commutation loops alongside the flight stack. The design that works, and that the DShot requirement implies, is:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──bidir DShot600 ×4──► 4× AT32F421K8U7 (AM32 firmware)
                                              └─► FD6288Q gate driver ─► 6× N-FET per channel
```

All switching-loss math below applies to the ESC power stage driven by the AT32 MCUs.

### 0.2 Form factor

80 A per motor is not survivable on a true 20×20 mm nano. The 24 FETs need 5×6 mm or 3.3×3.3 mm dual-cooled packages. The model assumes a 30.5×30.5 mm stack (board 36×36 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner power planes). Section 2.6 shows how hard the thermal limit is.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Six-step with synchronous (complementary) PWM

At any instant, exactly two phase legs are active. The motor current I_M flows through one FET in each active leg:

- In the PWM leg, either the high-side FET carries it (during D) or the low-side FET carries it (during 1−D, synchronous rectification).
- In the other leg, a FET is held on for the full step.

The series path therefore always contains two FETs, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_M^2\,R_{ds(on),hot}$$

$$R_{ds(on),hot} = k_T \cdot R_{ds(on),25} = 1.5 \times 1.0 = 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

### 1.2 Per-FET distribution

Each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical period, on average:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{I_M^2 R_{ds,hot}}{3} = \frac{6400 \times 0.0015}{3} = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

Check: 6 FETs × 3.2 W = 19.2 W ✓

### 1.3 Copper and shunt losses

**Copper.** Battery bus, phase pours, and vias. Target an effective series path of R_cu ≤ 0.5 mΩ per channel:

$$P_{cu,ch} = I_M^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\times10^{-3} = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

**Shunt (if used).** A low-side 0.25 mΩ shunt carries battery current, not phase current. Worst case at D → 1:

$$P_{shunt} = I^2 R = 1.6\ \text{W}$$
