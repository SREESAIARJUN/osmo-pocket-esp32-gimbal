# STM32H743 AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC): Loss Model, Thermal Budget, and Bidirectional DShot Allocation

## 0. Architectural Corrections and Assumptions

Two premises in the brief need correcting before the math, because they change the results.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** The FC sends DShot to four (or one quad-capable) ESC MCUs running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The 48 kHz PWM and dead-time live in those ESC MCUs. The H743's job is DShot600 output and eRPM telemetry capture.
   - **Assumed topology:** H743 + 4× AT32F421 (AM32) + 4× FD6288-class 3-phase gate drivers + 24 N-FETs on a shared PCB.
2. **80 A per channel on a nano (20×20 mm) board is not thermally achievable for more than about 1 s.** Section 3 quantifies this. I've kept 80 A as the design case and derived the derating.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Path in 6-Step with Synchronous Rectification

Take commutation state A+ / B−:

- **PWM on (D):** HS_A → motor → LS_B
- **PWM off (1−D):** LS_A (sync rect) → motor → LS_B

Exactly two FETs are always in the current path, so:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Here α ≈ 0.005 /K, which gives k_T ≈ 1.5 at 125 °C.

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W/channel}$$

### 1.2 PCB Copper

$$P_{Cu} = I_{ph}^2 R_{pcb} = 6400 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.2\ W/channel}$$

Copper resistance rises by about 0.39 %/K. At +60 K that adds roughly 23 %, bringing P_Cu to about 3.9 W.

### 1.3 Per-FET Distribution

- The high-side FET conducts for 120° of the electrical cycle, at duty D.
- The low-side FET conducts for its own 120° window, plus (1−D) of the opposite phase's window.

$$P_{HS,inst} = I^2 R\,D = 9.1\ \text{W (during its 120° window)}$$
