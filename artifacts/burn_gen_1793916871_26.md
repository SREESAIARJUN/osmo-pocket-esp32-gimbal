## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current path

In 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation with synchronous rectification, two FETs are always in the current path: one high-side or its sync low-side, plus the opposite-phase low-side.

At full throttle (D → 1), the phase current approximately equals the battery current per channel:

$$
I_{ph} \approx \frac{I_{bat,ch}}{D}
$$

The conduction loss per channel is:

$$
P_{cond} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

with the temperature-dependent on-resistance:

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds(25)}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

The 6-step phase current is quasi-trapezoidal. With commutation ripple below 10%, take I_rms ≈ I_ph.

### 2.3 PCB copper loss (often ignored, but significant)

Phase trace example: 3 mm wide, 15 mm long, 2 oz (70 µm) on two paralleled layers. Using ρ_Cu = 1.72×10⁻⁸ Ω·m at 25 °C:

$$
R = \frac{\rho L}{A} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot 0.015}{2\cdot(3\times10^{-3}\cdot 70\times10^{-6})} \approx 0.61\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

The current traverses about two such segments plus bus planes. Take roughly 1.5 mΩ total at 80 °C:

$$
P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} \approx 9.6\ \text{W per channel}
$$

This is comparable to the FET loss. **Mitigations:**
- Solder-wicked or copper-bar reinforced motor pads.
- 4-layer paralleling of phase nets.
- Via farms of at least 30× 0.3 mm vias per phase transition.
