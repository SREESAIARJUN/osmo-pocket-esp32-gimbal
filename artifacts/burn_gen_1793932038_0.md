# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal and DShot Design Model

## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Feasibility Findings

These points shape every number in this document.

1. **The H743 does not commutate motors.** It runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 commands. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU, for example an AT32F421 running AM32 or an EFM8 running BLHeli_S/Bluejay. The 48 kHz figure is the **ESC PWM frequency** (AM32 supports 24–48 kHz). It is not an H743 timer rate.

2. **"80A burst ×4 on a nano (20×20 mm) board" is not physically achievable.** Two constraints rule it out:
   - Twenty-four power MOSFETs, four gate drivers, four MCUs and bulk capacitance cannot fit in 20×20 mm.
   - The model below shows roughly 88 W of dissipation at full burst on all four channels.

   I have modelled a **30.5×30.5 mm stacked ESC** with a 20×20 FC on top. I recommend specifying **80A as a single-channel burst of 5 s or less**, not a simultaneous 4×80A rating.

3. **All component parameters are assumptions.** Replace them with datasheet values for your selected parts.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology Logic

In 6-step drive, two phases conduct at any instant and the third floats. With complementary PWM, the current always flows through exactly one FET per active leg: the high-side during the duty cycle D, and the low-side during (1−D). The path therefore always contains two R_ds(on) in series, **independent of duty cycle**:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha (T_j-25)\right] \approx k_T R_{25}$$

### 1.2 Per-Channel Value at 80 A

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\,\text{mΩ} = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET Distribution

Each phase spends 1/3 of the electrical cycle high, 1/3 low and 1/3 floating. Within its "high" third, the high-side FET carries current for fraction D and the low-side FET for (1−D).

$$P_{HS,avg} = \frac{D}{3}I^2R_{ds}, \qquad P_{LS,avg} = \frac{1}{3}I^2R_{ds}\left[1 + (1-D)\right]$$

- The LS term includes its "low-phase" third plus synchronous freewheel during the "high" third.
- At D → 1, the worst-case per-FET average is I²R/3 = **3.2 W**.
- The instantaneous peak is **9.6 W**. This matters because the die thermal time constant (~1–5 ms) is comparable to the electrical period at low eRPM.

### 1.4 Parasitic Copper

Add the board copper loss:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{trace}$$

- 2 oz copper is about 0.25 mΩ/□. A 3-square phase path contributes about 0.75 mΩ, adding **~4.8 W per channel**.
- Use 3–4 oz inner layers, stitched pours, and solder-reinforced phase pads.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches; the other conducting leg is static. Per channel:

$$P_{sw,ch} = \underbrace{\tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}}_{V\text{-}I\ \text{overlap}} + \underbrace{Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}}_{\text{recovery}} + \underbrace{Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}}_{\text{C}_{oss}} + \underbrace{2V_fI\,t_{dt}f_{sw}}_{\text{dead-time}} + \underbrace{2Q_gV_{gs}f_{sw}}_{\text{gate (driver)}}$$

| Term | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| Overlap | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 k | 1.94 W |
| Q_rr | 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.05 W |
| Q_oss | 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.05 W |
| Dead-time diode | 2 × 0.8 × 80 × 100 ns × 48 k | 0.61 W |
| Gate (dissipated in driver/R_g) | 2 × 50 nC × 10 × 48 k | 0.05 W |
| **Total** | | **≈ 2.70 W** |

**Design sensitivity:**
- Overlap loss scales linearly with f_sw. Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1 W per channel at 80 A, at the cost of audible noise and current ripple.
- Dead-time loss is the second-largest term. Use 50–80 ns if the driver and FET Q_g allow it.
