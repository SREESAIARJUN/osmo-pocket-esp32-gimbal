# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Design Package for Job #49

## 0. Architecture Decisions and Assumptions

**Architecture.** ArduPilot cannot commutate motors, so the H743 does not drive the gates directly. The architecture is:

```
STM32H743VIT6 (ArduPilot, 480 MHz)
   │  4× Bidirectional DShot600 (single-wire, half-duplex, inverted)
   ▼
4× AT32F421K8U7 (AM32 firmware, 48 kHz complementary PWM, one MCU per motor)
   │
4× FD6288Q 3-phase gate drivers → 24× N-MOSFET (6 per motor)
```

**Baseline parameters.** Swap in your vendor datasheet values and re-run the equations in §1–§3.

> **⚠ Form-factor warning.** 80 A per motor does not fit a true 20×20 "nano" board. PDFN 5×6 parts do not fit on 20×20 with 24 FETs, and 3×3 DFN parts (R_ds ≈ 2.5–3 mΩ) roughly triple conduction loss. The analysis below assumes a **30×30 ESC stack** under a compact FC. §4.4 quantifies the penalty for a 20×20 board.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (6-Step Trapezoidal, Complementary PWM)

During each 60° sector, current I flows high-side (phase A) → motor → low-side (phase B). With synchronous complementary PWM on the high side at duty D:

| Device | Conduction fraction | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| HS of phase A (PWM) | D | I²·R·D |
| LS of phase A (sync rectifier) | (1−D) − 2t_d·f_sw | I²·R·(1−D) |
| LS of phase B (static ON) | 1 | I²·R |

The total per motor is independent of D:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

The R_ds temperature model is:

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\left(\frac{T_j+273}{298}\right)^{2.3} \;\approx\; 1.0\ \text{m}\Omega \times 1.5 = 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega \;@\;125^\circ\text{C}
$$

**Numbers at 80 A:**

- P_cond,motor = 2 × 80² × 1.5 mΩ = **19.2 W**
- Per-FET average (commutation rotates load over 6 FETs, each carrying it ⅓ of the electrical cycle): 19.2 / 6 = **3.2 W**
- Per-FET sector peak (static low-side): 80² × 1.5 mΩ = **9.6 W**. This peak drives junction ripple at low eRPM, when sector duration exceeds the FET thermal time constant (~1–5 ms).

**Thermal runaway check.** Conduction loss rises with temperature, so iterate T_j = T_board + P(T_j)·R_θ. The loop is stable when ∂P/∂T · R_θ < 1:

∂P/∂T ≈ I²·R₂₅·0.0077/K = 6400 × 1e-3 × 0.0077 ≈ 0.049 W/K per FET.

With R_θ(J→board) ≈ 3.2 K/W, the loop gain is 0.16. This is stable but non-negligible: include the iteration in your thermal sim.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap (the PWM-ing half-bridge only)

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two dead-time intervals per period)

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2t_d)\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}
$$

Cutting dead time to 100 ns halves this loss. Validate shoot-through margin against the FD6288Q propagation mismatch (≤50 ns) before doing so.

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}
$$

Q_rr is also dissipated in the opposite FET as additional turn-on current. A conservative term adds I_rr·t_rr·V/2, giving approximately **0.10 W**.

### 2.4 C_oss charge loss

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss(er)} V^2 f_{sw} \times 2 = 1.5\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = 0.046\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g, not the FET)

$$
P_g = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} \times 2 = 60\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = 0.058\ \text{W}
$$

At the 12 V gate rail, total gate drive for four motors is ≈ 0.23 W, which sizes the LDO/boost for the FD6288 VCC.

### 2.6 Loss budget at the 80 A burst

| Term | Per motor (W) | ×4 motors (W) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.20 | 76.8 |
| V·I overlap | 1.94 | 7.8 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 | 4.9 |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.15 | 0.6 |
| Gate | 0.06 | 0.2 |
| Current-sense shunt (0.25 mΩ, single shared low-side per motor) | 1.60 | 6.4 |
| Copper traces/planes (est. 0.3 mΩ path) | 1.92 | 7.7 |
| **Total** | **≈ 26.1** | **≈ 104 W** |

Efficiency at 80 A × 4 × 25.2 V × D≈0.9: about 104 W lost from about 7.3 kW, roughly **98.6 %**. The absolute heat is still the problem, not the percentage.

**Frequency sensitivity.** About 3.3 W per motor scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz you save about 1.6 W per motor (≈ 6 %), at the cost of more current ripple. The motor inductance check is in §3.2.
