## 0. Architecture Review: Two Issues to Resolve Before Schematic Capture

### 0.1 The H743 cannot also be the ESC controller

Driving four BLDC motors with synchronous rectification requires 4 × 3 half-bridges, which is 24 PWM signals (12 complementary pairs with dead-time).

The STM32H743 has only two advanced-control timers with hardware dead-time insertion:

| Timer | Complementary pairs |
|---|---|
| TIM1 | 3 (CH1/1N, CH2/2N, CH3/3N) |
| TIM8 | 3 |
| HRTIM | Possible, but not supported by ArduPilot or AM32 |

That is enough for 2 motors at most, and ArduPilot has no FOC or 6-step commutation core in any case.

**Correct architecture:** STM32H743 (ArduPilot) → 4 × bidirectional DShot600 → 4 × dedicated ESC MCUs (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or BLHeli_32-class firmware) → 3-phase gate drivers → 24 MOSFETs. "48 kHz" is the ESC MCU's PWM frequency. It is independent of the H743.

### 0.2 "80 A burst" on a nano board is thermally time-limited

Section 3 shows the board heats at roughly 13–17 K/s at 4 × 80 A. The burst rating must therefore be specified with a duration. Based on the model, I recommend stating **"80 A / 1 s, 60 A / 5 s, 40 A continuous"**, with the continuous figure confirmed on a thermal test bench.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (Per ESC Channel)

### 2.1 Conduction loss

In 6-step operation with synchronous rectification, exactly two MOSFETs carry the phase current at every instant:

- **PWM on:** HS(A) + LS(B)
- **PWM off:** LS(A) + LS(B)

The conduction path is therefore always 2 × R_DS(on), independent of duty cycle. Duty only changes how the loss is shared between the HS and LS devices.

**Temperature-dependent resistance:**

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)]$$

**Conduction loss per channel:**

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) + I^2 R_{Cu} + I_{rms,shunt}^2 R_{sh}$$

FET-only figures at 80 A:

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond (FETs) |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 2 · 6400 · 0.001 = **12.8 W** |
| 100 °C | 1.49 mΩ | **19.0 W** |
| 125 °C | 1.65 mΩ | **21.1 W** |

**PCB copper:** 2 oz copper is about 0.25 mΩ/□. With roughly 3 squares in the phase and bus path, R_Cu ≈ 0.75 mΩ, giving 6400 × 0.00075 ≈ **4.8 W per channel**. This is not negligible. Mitigate with 3 oz outer layers and parallel inner-layer pours stitched with via arrays (more than 20 vias per pad).

**Electrothermal feedback:** because R_DS rises with temperature, solve iteratively:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_a + R_{th,ja}\,P(T_j^{(k)})$$

This converges only if $R_{th,ja} \cdot \partial P/\partial T < 1$, where

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T} = 2 I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha = 0.083\ \text{W/K at 80 A}$$

With an effective R_th of about 10 K/W per channel, the loop gain is 0.83. That is close to thermal runaway, which is a further argument that 80 A must be a time-limited rating.

### 2.2 Switching loss at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing phase leg switches. That is one hard-switched HS edge pair per period, with the LS acting as synchronous rectifier.

**(a) V–I overlap (hard switching, HS device):**

Transition time is set by the Miller plateau:

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g} = \frac{10\ \text{nC}}{1.5\ \text{A}} \approx 7\ \text{ns}$$

Allowing for the plateau and loop inductance, use t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 15 ns.

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I\, (t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 30\,\text{ns} \cdot 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery of the LS body diode:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 50\,\text{nC} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

Q_rr is strongly di/dt-dependent and can triple at fast edges. Budget 0.2 W.

**(c) Output capacitance (both FETs in the leg):**

$$P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 40\,\text{nC} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period):**

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I \cdot 2 t_{dt}\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\,\text{ns} \cdot 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

This is the second-largest switching term. Reducing dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns halves it, but verify shoot-through margin first.
