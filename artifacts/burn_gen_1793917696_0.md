# STM32H743 AIO 4-in-1 ESC Flight Controller: Design Model (Job #50)

**Scope:** 80A burst conduction losses, 48 kHz switching losses, thermal model, ArduPilot bi-directional DShot600 timer/DMA allocation, and a reference netlist.

---

## 0. Assumptions and Corrections

These assumptions drive every number below. Change any of them and the results change.

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Architecture | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100) runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** Each ESC channel uses a dedicated ESC MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071) running AM32. That is the only realistic way to get bi-directional DShot and 48 kHz PWM. |
| Form factor | "Nano" here means a 30.5×30.5 mm mount, 8-layer PCB with 2 oz copper. A 20×20 board cannot physically carry 24 power FETs rated for 80A bursts. |
| Battery | 6S, V_bus = 25.2 V maximum |
| MOSFETs | 40 V N-ch DFN3.3×3.3 class: R_DS(on) = 1.0 mΩ at 25 °C, 1.5 mΩ hot (×1.5 at T_j ≈ 110 °C), Q_g = 50 nC, Q_gd = 10 nC, Q_rr = 40 nC, Q_oss = 60 nC, R_θJC = 1.2 K/W |
| Gate driver | FD6288Q-class 3-phase driver, about 1 A effective gate current, dead time t_d = 100 ns |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal with synchronous (complementary) rectification |
| "80A burst" | 80 A RMS *phase* current per channel, all 4 channels at once (worst case) |

**Engineering note:** "80A burst" on a 30 mm AIO is a marketing figure. Section 3 shows it is sustainable for about **2–4 seconds**, not continuously. Treat the datasheet rating accordingly.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80A Burst)

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, exactly **two FETs carry the phase current at every instant**:
- During the PWM on-time: high-side of phase A plus low-side of phase B.
- During the PWM off-time: low-side of A plus low-side of B.

**Per channel:**

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \cdot I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{DS(on),hot}$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

**Per FET** (each FET conducts about 1/3 of the electrical cycle):

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{I^2 R}{3}\cdot 2 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} = \frac{6400 \times 0.0015}{3} \approx \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

**Board total (4 channels):** 4 × 19.2 = **76.8 W**

**PCB copper loss (add to the above):**

$$R_{trace} \approx \frac{\rho L}{A}$$

For 2 oz copper (70 µm), about 15 mm path length, 8 mm effective width, and 2 parallel layers, R_trace is about 0.23 mΩ per path. A phase current passes through two such paths, about 0.46 mΩ total:

$$P_{Cu,ch} \approx 80^2 \times 0.46\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 2.9\text{ W} \quad\Rightarrow\quad 11.8\text{ W board total}$$

Shunt and connector losses are excluded here and must be added for your specific layout.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (f_sw = 48 kHz)

Only the actively PWM'd half-bridge switches hard. The complementary low-side switches at zero voltage after the dead time.

### 2.1 Hard-switching overlap (V–I crossover)

The transition times come from gate charge and drive current:

$$t_r \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g} = \frac{10\,\text{nC}}{1\,\text{A}} \approx 10\text{ ns} \;(\text{use } 15\text{ ns with parasitic inductance})$$

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\times25.2\times80\times30\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Body-diode conduction during dead time

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM period:

$$P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I_{ph}\cdot 2t_d \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5\times60\text{nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET)

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\times50\text{nC}\times10\text{V}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 2.6 Switching summary

| Term | Per channel |
|---|---|
| V–I overlap | 1.45 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W |
| Q_rr | 0.05 W |
| Q_oss | 0.04 W |
| **Switching total (FET)** | **≈ 2.15 W** |
| Gate drive (driver IC) | 0.05 W |

**Frequency sensitivity:** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw.
- Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.1 W per channel.
- In return, current ripple doubles: ΔI = V·D(1−D)/(L·f). On low-inductance motors, that ripple raises the RMS current and therefore the conduction loss.
- 48 kHz is the right choice for low-inductance 2–3" motors. For larger motors, consider 24–32 kHz.
