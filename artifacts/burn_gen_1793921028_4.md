# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Systems Architecture Package (Job #14)

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

I don't have the Job #14 specification sheet, so every parameter below is an explicit assumption. Each one is tagged **[A#]** so you can substitute real values and recompute. The equations are the deliverable. The numbers are a worked example.

The STM32H743 does **not** generate the 48 kHz motor PWM. In every production 4-in-1 design, ArduPilot on the H743 sends bi-directional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs (AT32F421/EFM8/GD32 running AM32 or BLHeli). Those MCUs run commutation and the 48 kHz PWM. The architecture below follows that split.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path in six-step synchronous commutation

At any instant, exactly two phase legs are active:
- **PWM leg:** The high-side (HS) FET conducts for duty D. The low-side (LS) FET conducts for (1 − D) as a synchronous rectifier.
- **Return leg:** The LS FET conducts continuously.

Phase current is approximately flat (I_ph ≈ I_motor) in trapezoidal drive at high load. That means two R_ds(on) are always in series with the current, independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + k\,(T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C with k = 0.0059 /K, R = 0.75 × 1.50 = **1.125 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Copper and interconnect resistance

Per channel, the current path includes:
- the bus plane,
- the FET pads and vias,
- the phase pad,
- the solder joint to the motor wire.

The estimated total is R_cu ≈ 0.4 mΩ (2 oz, about 10 mm effective at 5 mm width, plus parallel layers).

$$P_{cu,ch} = I^2 R_{cu}$$

### 1.4 Worked numbers at I = 80 A

| Term | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| P_cond (FETs) | 2 × 6400 × 1.125 mΩ | **14.4 W** |
| P_cu | 6400 × 0.4 mΩ | **2.56 W** |
| **Conduction subtotal per channel** | | **≈ 17.0 W** |

### 1.5 Distribution among the six FETs

Each FET acts as HS-PWM, LS-sync, or LS-return across the 6 commutation sectors. Time-averaged over one electrical revolution, each FET carries **≈ P_cond/6 ≈ 2.4 W**.

The electrical period at the eRPM of interest is ≤ 1 ms. The die thermal time constant is about 1–5 ms. The per-FET average is therefore valid for junction estimation.

At low eRPM or standstill (startup or desync), one FET pair carries the full current. That case is covered in §3.4.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. The loss components per channel are:

**(a) V-I overlap (hard switching, inductive load)**
$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction**
$$P_{dt} = V_F\,I\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f_{sw}$$

**(c) Reverse recovery (LS body diode → HS turn-on)**
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw}$$

**(d) Output capacitance**
$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw}$$
This uses the charge-based form, which is more accurate than ½CV² for nonlinear C_oss.

**(e) Gate drive** (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, two FETs per leg)
$$P_g = 2\,Q_g\,V_{GS}\,f_{sw}$$

### 2.1 Worked numbers (V_bus = 25.2 V, I = 80 A, V_F = 0.8 V)

| Term | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| P_VI | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 20 ns × 48 k | **0.968 W** |
| P_dt | 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k | **0.614 W** |
| P_rr | 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | **0.048 W** |
| P_oss | 35 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | **0.042 W** |
| P_g | 2 × 45 nC × 10 × 48 k | **0.043 W** (driver) |
| **Switching subtotal (FETs)** | | **≈ 1.67 W per channel** |

### 2.2 Design notes

- Dead-time loss is about 40 % of switching loss. AM32 lets you trim dead time. Each 25 ns removed saves about 0.15 W per channel at 80 A, but you must verify shoot-through margin on the scope.
- Doubling f_sw to 96 kHz adds about 1.7 W per channel. At 80 A, 48 kHz is the right trade.
