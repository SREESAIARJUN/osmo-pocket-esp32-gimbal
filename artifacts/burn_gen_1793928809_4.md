## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)] = 1.2\,\text{mΩ}\times1.375 = 1.65\ \text{mΩ}$$

### 1.2 Active current path per commutation step

In any 6-step sector, one phase is PWM'd and one is held on. Take the case where phase A is PWM'd high and phase B's low-side is held on:

- **A-high** conducts for duty D.
- **A-low** carries the synchronous-rectification freewheel current for (1 − D).
- **B-low** conducts for 100 % of the period.

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

With synchronous rectification, conduction loss is **independent of duty cycle**. This is an important result: an 80 A burst at partial throttle costs the same conduction loss as at full throttle.

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.65\text{m} = \mathbf{21.1\ W\ per\ channel}$$

### 1.3 Copper path loss

Assume a phase/bus path resistance R_Cu of about 0.5 mΩ, covering pours, vias and pads:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\text{m} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

R_Cu also rises by about 0.39 %/K. Include this if the board heats by more than 30 K.

### 1.4 Per-device average loss

Over one electrical revolution, each of the 6 FETs takes an equal share:

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx P_{cond}/6 \approx 3.5\ \text{W}$$

**Caveat at low eRPM or stall:** sector dwell time can exceed the FET's thermal time constant (a few ms for PDFN). In that case a single FET sees the full I²R:

$$80^2 \times 1.65\text{m} = 10.6\ \text{W}$$

This **stall/startup case is the true worst case** for an individual junction.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches; there is one hard-switched leg per channel at any time.

| Loss term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | ½·25.2·80·30 ns·48 k = **1.45 W** |
| Dead-time body diode | 2·V_f·I·t_dt·f_sw | 2·0.8·80·100 ns·48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 50 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.06 W** |
| C_oss charge | ½·C_oss·V²·f_sw × 2 FETs | **0.05 W** |
| Gate drive (driver-side) | Q_g·V_gs·f_sw × 2 FETs | 2·60 nC·10·48 k = **0.06 W** |
| **Total P_sw** | | **≈ 2.2 W per channel** |

### 2.1 Edge-time estimate

$$t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,R_{g,tot}}{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}} = \frac{10\text{n}\times5\,Ω}{10-3.5} \approx 7.7\ \text{ns}$$

The 15 ns budget in the table allows margin for common-source inductance and ringing. Measure the real edge with a probe and update the model.

### 2.2 Dead-time trade-off

The dead-time term is now the second-largest switching loss. Reducing t_dt from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel. Verify shoot-through margin against driver propagation-delay mismatch (FD6288-class drivers have roughly ±20 ns).

### 2.3 Why 48 kHz?

Overlap loss scales linearly with f_sw. Going from 24 to 48 kHz roughly doubles it, about +1 W per channel. The benefit is lower current ripple on low-inductance motors:

$$ΔI_{pp} ≈ \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{2 L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

For a 10 µH phase-to-phase motor at D = 0.5, this gives about 6.6 A ripple. That ripple adds ΔI²/12 to the RMS current: negligible at 80 A, but it matters for low-throttle acoustic noise.
