## 0. Feasibility Findings

These issues affect the requirements themselves, so they come first.

| # | Finding | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | **80 A burst per motor × 4 = 320 A at the battery.** At 6S (25.2 V full, about 22 V under sag) that is about 7 kW. XT60 connectors are rated about 60 A continuous. 12 AWG leads, about 3 mΩ round trip, dissipate about 300 W. | You need XT90 or direct-solder 10 AWG, plus a 2 oz+ heavy-copper bus. A true "nano" (20×20) form factor cannot do this. |
| F2 | **20×20 mm cannot carry 6 power FETs per motor at 80 A.** Total dissipation is about 80 W (§2). | Board must be at least 30.5×30.5 mm, preferably a stacked 2-board design: FC on top, 4-in-1 ESC below. |
| F3 | **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In the ArduPilot architecture, the H743 sends DShot600. Each ESC channel has its own MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32) that drives the gate driver at 48 kHz. | The 48 kHz switching model applies to the ESC MCUs and FETs, not to H743 timers. |
| F4 | **80 A is a burst rating, not continuous.** §4 shows it is thermally capacity-limited to about 3–5 s from a warm start. | Firmware must enforce a current/temperature derate (§4.4). |

The design proceeds as a **30.5×30.5 mm stack: H743 FC plus a 4-in-1 ESC at 55 A continuous / 80 A burst on 6S.**

### 2.2 Temperature-dependent conduction loss

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

In 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation with complementary/synchronous PWM, phase current flows through one high-side or low-side switch in each of the two active phases at every instant. So two R_ds sit in series with the motor current:

$$P_{cond}=I_{ph,rms}^{2}\cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

For FOC/sinusoidal drive, use $P_{cond}=\tfrac{3}{2}\hat I^{2}R_{ds}(T_j)$ with $\hat I$ the phase peak. That result is within about 10% of the 6-step figure at equal DC current.

At **T_j = 110 °C**: R_ds = 1.1 × (1 + 0.0045 × 85) = **1.52 mΩ**.

$$P_{cond}=80^{2}\times2\times1.52\,\text{m}\Omega=\mathbf{19.5\ W/motor}$$

**Electro-thermal fixed point.** R_ds depends on T_j, and T_j depends on P:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + R_{\theta,eff}\,P(T_j)\;\Rightarrow\; T_j=\frac{T_{ref}+R_{\theta}\,I^2 2R_{25}(1-25\alpha)}{1-R_{\theta}\,I^2\,2R_{25}\,\alpha}$$

A stable solution requires the loop gain to stay below 1: $R_\theta I^2 2R_{25}\alpha < 1$.

With R_θ,eff = 1.2 K/W per FET and 80 A, loop gain = 1.2 × 6400 × 2.2e-3 × 0.0045 / 2 ≈ 0.038. This is stable with no local runaway. Global runaway is set by the board, not the junction (§4).

### 2.3 Switching losses at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the high-side FET of the PWM'd phase hard-switches. The low side commutates softly through its body diode during dead-time. V_bus = 25.2 V, I = 80 A.

**Driver current.** The FD6288Q gives about 1.0 A source and 1.2 A sink; 4.7 Ω gate resistors reduce this to roughly 0.8 / 1.0 A at the Miller plateau.

$$t_r\approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}}=\frac{9\text{nC}}{0.8\text{A}}\approx11\ \text{ns},\quad t_f\approx\frac{9}{1.0}=9\ \text{ns}$$

Add 4 ns each for current rise/fall: **t_r ≈ 15 ns, t_f ≈ 13 ns**.

| Mechanism | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw | 0.5·25.2·80·28e-9·48e3 = **1.35 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 40e-9·25.2·48e3 = **0.05 W** |
| C_oss (HS+LS) | 2·½·C_oss·V²·f_sw | 1.4e-9·635·48e3 = **0.04 W** |
| Dead-time diode | V_f·I·2·t_dt·f_sw (t_dt = 100 ns) | 0.85·80·200e-9·48e3 = **0.65 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs switching) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw | 2·45e-9·10·48e3 = **0.04 W** (dissipated in driver/R_g) |
| **Total switching-related** | | **≈ 2.1 W** |
