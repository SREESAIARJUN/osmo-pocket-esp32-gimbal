## 0. Architecture Baseline and a Feasibility Flag

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Form factor | 20×20 mm (M3 mounting holes). Two-board stack: FC on top, ESC on bottom |
| FC MCU | STM32H743VIH6 (TFBGA100, 7×7 mm), 400 MHz |
| ESC MCUs | 4× STM32G071 running AM32 (bidirectional DShot capable) |
| Battery | 6S LiPo: 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal |
| MOSFETs | 40 V N-ch, PDFN 3.3×3.3. R_DS(on) = 1.0 mΩ @25 °C, Qg = 45 nC, Qrr = 50 nC, Qoss = 40 nC, V_SD = 0.8 V |
| Gate drive | 3× half-bridge drivers per channel (FD6288-class), V_GS = 10 V, t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 10 ns at the switch node |
| PWM | 48 kHz, center-aligned, complementary (synchronous rectification), dead time 100 ns |
| PCB (ESC) | 6-layer, 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner, FR4 Tg170 |

**Feasibility flag.** "80A burst" on a 20×20 mm ESC is a marketing-class rating. The math below shows each channel dissipates about 22 W at 80 A. A nano board has roughly 3 J/K of total heat capacity, so this rating is only defensible as a **≤1–2 s single-motor burst** with airflow. Rate it explicitly as such (e.g. "80 A burst 1 s / 35 A continuous").

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ /\mathrm{K}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.0 mΩ × 1.6 = **1.6 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Bridge current path (6-step, complementary PWM)

At any instant two phases carry the motor current I_M:
- During **PWM-on**, the current flows through the high-side FET (HS) of phase A and the low-side FET (LS) of phase B.
- During **PWM-off**, it freewheels through LS_A (synchronously rectified) and LS_B.

Two FETs therefore always sit in series with the current, regardless of duty cycle D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_M^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j) \qquad \text{(independent of } D \text{ with sync rectification)}$$

How the loss splits between devices:

$$P_{HS} = I_M^2 R\,D\cdot\tfrac{1}{3},\qquad P_{LS} = I_M^2 R\,(1 + (1-D))\cdot\tfrac{1}{3}\quad\text{(per FET, averaged over an electrical revolution)}$$

At low throttle the low-side FETs run hotter. At D → 1 the high-side and low-side FETs equalize.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| Term | Expression | Value |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (hot) | 2 × 80² × 1.6 mΩ | **20.5 W** |
| FET conduction (cold, first ~50 ms) | 2 × 80² × 1.0 mΩ | 12.8 W |
| PCB copper, phase + bus (≈0.25 mΩ path) | 80² × 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| Battery pad/lead to bulk caps (shared) | I_bat² × 0.15 mΩ | see §3.4 |

**Hottest single FET.** This is the low-side FET at D ≈ 0.5, where its share = (1.5/3) × 80² × 1.6 mΩ = **5.1 W**, plus its share of switching loss.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-modulated leg switches. Per PWM period, one HS/LS pair commutates once on and once off.

### 2.1 Component equations

$$P_{sw,VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I_M\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw}$$
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw}$$
$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw}\quad(\approx 2\cdot\tfrac12 C_{oss,eq}V^2 f \text{, charge-based form})$$
$$P_{dt} = 2\,V_{SD}\,I_M\,t_{dt}\,f_{sw}$$
$$P_{gate} = 2\,Q_g\,V_{GS}\,f_{sw}\quad(\text{dissipated mostly in driver and } R_g)$$

### 2.2 Numbers at V_bus = 25.2 V, I_M = 80 A, f = 48 kHz

| Loss | Calculation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V·I overlap | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 20 ns × 48 k | **0.97 W** |
| Reverse recovery | 50 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.06 W |
| Coss | 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.05 W |
| Dead-time body diode | 2 × 0.8 × 80 × 100 ns × 48 k | **0.61 W** |
| Gate charge | 2 × 45 nC × 10 V × 48 k | 0.04 W (driver) |
| **Total switching** | | **≈1.73 W** |

Two design notes follow from this:
- **Dead time is the second-largest switching term.** Cutting it from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel. Validate shoot-through margin against the actual t_d(off) first.
- **Raising PWM frequency costs little.** Switching loss is under 8 % of total at 80 A. Going 24 → 48 kHz costs about 0.85 W per channel and improves current ripple and acoustics, so 48 kHz is justified.

### 2.3 Per-channel total at 80 A burst (T_j = 125 °C)

$$P_{ch} = 20.5 + 1.6 + 1.73 \approx \mathbf{23.8\ W}$$

Efficiency at 80 A, 22.2 V, D ≈ 0.9: P_out ≈ 1600 W, so η ≈ 98.5 %. The efficiency is excellent; the absolute heat is the problem.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network (Foster/Cauer hybrid)

```
P_FET ──[Zth_jc 0.8 K/W, τ≈2 ms]──●case──[R_solder+via 2.5 K/W]──●local Cu──[R_spread 8 K/W]──●board bulk (C_board)──[R_conv]──● T_amb
                                                         │
                                                  C_local ≈ 0.08 J/K
```

| Parameter | Derivation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| R_th,j-c (bottom) | Datasheet-class PDFN3.3 | 0.8 K/W |
| R_th via array | 12 × 0.3 mm vias plated 25 µm, ≈30 K/W each in parallel, plus solder | ≈2.5 K/W |
| R_spread (local pour to bulk) | 2 oz pours, 20×20 board | ≈8 K/W |
| C_board | Cu ≈ 4.5 g × 0.385 + FR4 ≈ 1.2 g × 1.1 + components ≈ 0.3 | **≈3.3 J/K** |
| R_conv (prop wash, ~5 m/s, both faces) | h ≈ 60 W/m²K, A ≈ 8 cm² | ≈21 K/W |

### 3.2 Board bulk temperature during a burst

$$T_{board}(t) = T_{amb} + P_{tot}R_{conv}\left(1-e^{-t/\tau}\right),\quad \tau = R_{conv}C_{board}\approx 69\ \mathrm{s}$$

The burst is much shorter than τ, so the board behaves as a near-adiabatic slope:

$$\frac{dT}{dt}\approx\frac{P_{tot}}{C_{board}}$$
