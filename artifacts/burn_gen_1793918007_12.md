## 0. Architecture Decisions and Assumptions

Some parts of the brief need correcting before the math is meaningful.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot is a flight controller stack and does not implement BLDC commutation. Each of the four ESC channels needs its own commutation MCU, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. The architecture is therefore:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) --DShot600 bidir x4--> 4x ESC MCU --> 4x 3-ph gate driver --> 24x MOSFET
```

**2. "80A burst" is interpreted as 80 A per motor (phase current) for a burst of ≤2 s.** Four motors at 80 A means 320 A of battery current. A nano-format board cannot sustain that. The numbers below show it survives only as a short transient, and only if the copper, connectors and battery path are sized for it. The real-world limit is usually the battery lead and connector, not the FETs.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.5 mΩ × 1.6 = **2.4 mΩ**. This is used as the hot design value, then verified self-consistently in §3.

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step with complementary (synchronous) PWM

The current always flows through exactly two FETs:

- **On-state:** the high-side of the PWM'd phase plus the low-side of the return phase.
- **Freewheel:** the low-side of the PWM'd phase (synchronous rectification) plus the low-side of the return phase.

Per ESC channel, total conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle (neglecting dead time):

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

At 80 A and 2.4 mΩ:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2 \times 6400 \times 0.0024 = \mathbf{30.7\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET average

Each phase leg conducts for 2/3 of the electrical cycle, split between its high-side and low-side according to D. Averaged over the 6 FETs:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} = \frac{2 I^2 R}{6} = \frac{I^2R}{3} = \mathbf{5.12\ W}$$
