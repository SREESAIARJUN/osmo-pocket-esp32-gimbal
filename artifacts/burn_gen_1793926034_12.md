# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Reference Architecture (Job #59)

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Two Design-Level Findings

**This is a reference-level engineering model.** Every MOSFET parameter below is a representative value, not a specific part. You must substitute datasheet values for your chosen part and validate the result with thermal imaging and a thermocouple-instrumented burst test before release.

### Finding 1: Architecture

The STM32H743 cannot be the ESC commutation controller in an ArduPilot system. ArduPilot has no in-tree 6-step or FOC motor-drive firmware. The architecture is therefore:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──bidir DShot600 ×4──► 4× ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32 firmware)
                                               └─► 4× 3-phase gate driver ─► 24 MOSFETs
```

The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs. The H743 only handles DShot.

### Finding 2: Thermal

An 80A-per-motor rating on a nano (≤30×30 mm) board is a **burst rating of roughly 2–3 seconds** with one FET per switch. It is not a continuous rating. The math in §3 shows this.

Co-locating the H743 (Tj max 125 °C for industrial grade) on the same copper as roughly 90 W of FET dissipation is the highest-risk decision in this design. I recommend a stacked FC/ESC configuration, or at minimum a thermal moat (see §3.6).
