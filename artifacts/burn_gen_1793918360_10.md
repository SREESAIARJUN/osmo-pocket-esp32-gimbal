# STM32H743 FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC (Nano): Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Budget & Bi-Directional DShot600 Allocation

## 0. Scope, Assumptions & Architectural Caveats

**I have no prior context for "Job #72".** I have not seen your BOM, stack-up, or board outline. Everything below is a parametric model populated with stated, representative values. Swap in your datasheet numbers. The equations are the deliverable, and the numbers show where the design lands.

**Two architectural points need to be settled first:**

1. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In a 4-in-1 stack, the H743 is the flight controller. Each ESC channel has its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32 (for example STM32G071, AT32F421, or GD32). The FC→ESC link is bi-directional DShot600. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs, not the H743.
2. **"Nano" + 80 A burst is thermally marginal.** The model below shows roughly 4 s of all-motor 80 A burst before junction limits, and about 36 A per motor continuous with prop-wash cooling. If marketing wants "80A", define it as a burst rating with a time limit.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation topology
The model assumes six-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM on the active high-side leg. Within each 60° sector:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side (HS) conducts for fraction D. The low-side (LS) of the same leg conducts for 1−D (sync rect.).
- **Return leg:** the LS conducts for 100% of the sector.
- At every instant, **exactly two FETs carry I_ph** (ignoring dead time).

### 1.2 Bridge-level conduction loss

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)] \approx k_T R_{ds,25}
$$

$$
P_{cond,bridge} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

With the assumed values:

$$
P_{cond,bridge} = 2(80)^2(1.35\,\text{m}\Omega) = \mathbf{17.28\ W\ per\ motor}
$$

### 1.3 Per-device distribution (worst-case FET)
Each FET is active in 2 of 6 sectors (one as PWM device, one as return device).

$$
P_{HS} = I^2R_{ds}\cdot\tfrac{1}{3}D,\qquad
P_{LS} = I^2R_{ds}\cdot\tfrac{1}{3}\left[(1-D) + 1\right]
$$

The LS is worst at low duty, which is the typical case for a high-current low-throttle punch-out with a high-Kv motor. With I²R_ds = 8.64 W:

| D | P_HS | P_LS |
|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | 1.44 W | 4.32 W |
| 0.2 | 0.58 W | 5.18 W |

### 1.4 Copper loss (do not ignore)
For each current path:

$$
P_{Cu} = I^2 \frac{\rho_{Cu}(T)\,L}{W\,t}
$$

Take a 2 oz (70 µm) pour, 8 mm wide, with an effective 10 mm path for each of the phase and return paths. This gives about 0.31 mΩ per path at 25 °C, or 0.4 mΩ hot.

$$
P_{Cu} \approx 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.4\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 5.1\ \text{W per motor}
$$

This is comparable to the FET loss. Recommendations:
- Use 3 oz outer layers plus paralleled inner planes.
- Stitch with via arrays.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. The HS hard-switches against the LS body diode or channel.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (HS)

$$
I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_g} = \frac{10-4.5}{4} = 1.375\ \text{A},\quad
I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_g} = 1.125\ \text{A}
$$

$$
Q_{sw} = Q_{gd} + \tfrac{1}{2}Q_{gs} = 19.5\ \text{nC}
$$

$$
t_{on} = 14.2\ \text{ns},\quad t_{off} = 17.3\ \text{ns}
$$

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_{on}+t_{off})f_{sw} = \tfrac12(25.2)(80)(31.5\text{n})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.52\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Secondary terms

| Term | Expression | Value | Lands in |
|---|---|---|---|
| Output capacitance | $\tfrac12 C_{oss} V^2 f \times 2$ | 0.05 W | HS |
| Reverse recovery | $Q_{rr} V_{bus} f$ | 0.10 W | HS (dissipated), LS |
| Dead-time diode | $2 V_f I t_{dt} f$ | 0.61 W | LS |
| Gate charge | $2 Q_g V_{drv} f$ |
