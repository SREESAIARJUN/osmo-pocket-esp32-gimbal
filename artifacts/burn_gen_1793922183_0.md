## 0. Feasibility Summary

The first-order math shows that **80 A burst on all four channels of a nano-class (20×20 mm) board is not thermally sustainable** beyond roughly 1–3 seconds without a heatsink. The calculations in §2–§4 support this. The design below is therefore specified as:

- **80 A single-channel burst, ≤ 2 s**, with an MCU-enforced thermal/current limiter
- **~35–45 A continuous per channel**, with airflow and copper as specified

Second, an architectural point. ArduPilot does not drive MOSFET gates directly from the H743. A working AIO uses:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──DShot600 bidir──► 4× ESC MCU (AM32/BLHeli_32-class) ──► gate drivers ──► 24 MOSFETs
```

All numeric values below use stated example parameters. **Replace them with your chosen MOSFET datasheet values before release.** Every equation is parameterized for that purpose.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 100 °C: R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0045·75) = **1.34 mΩ**

### 2.2 Current path in 6-step synchronous drive

In any commutation sector, current flows through one high-side and one low-side device. With complementary PWM, the "off" interval is carried by the synchronous FET rather than the body diode. The series resistance of the active path is therefore always ≈ 2·R_ds:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Per-device RMS current (high-side, leg active 1/3 of the electrical period at duty D):

$$I_{HS,rms} = I_{ph}\sqrt{D/3}, \qquad I_{LS,rms} = I_{ph}\sqrt{(1-D)/3 + 1/3}$$

Low-side devices run hotter at partial throttle. Place them on the best copper.

### 2.3 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 100 °C

$$P_{cond,ch} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.34\,\text{m}\Omega = 6400 \times 2.68\,\text{m} = \mathbf{17.2\ W\ per\ channel}$$

At full throttle (D → 1), the worst single low-side device gets:

$$I_{LS,rms} = 80\sqrt{1/3} = 46.2\ \text{A}, \qquad P = 46.2^2 \times 1.34\,\text{m} = \mathbf{2.86\ W}$$

### 2.4 Copper and shunt losses (often forgotten)

- **Phase copper:** 2 oz outer, ~5 mm wide × 8 mm long ≈ 0.4 mΩ round trip → 80² × 0.4 m = **2.6 W/channel**
- **Shared battery shunt:** a single 0.25 mΩ shunt carrying 4 × 80 A = 320 A dissipates 320² × 0.25 m = **25.6 W**. This is unacceptable.
  - **Decision:** use per-channel ESC current sensing (phase-leg shunt read by each ESC MCU), or a 0.1 mΩ shunt with a Hall-effect alternative for the battery monitor.
