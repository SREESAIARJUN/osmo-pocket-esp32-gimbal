## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Flags

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. The correct partitioning is:

- **FC (H743, ArduPilot):** generates 4× bidirectional DShot600 and decodes eRPM telemetry.
- **ESC (4× local MCUs, e.g. AT32F421 running AM32, or one per channel):** performs 6-step commutation at f_PWM = 48 kHz through 3-phase gate drivers into 24 MOSFETs.

**Red flags to resolve before layout:**

| Issue | Finding |
|---|---|
| "80A" definition | Per channel (4 × 80 = 320A battery) or total? This doc assumes **80A per channel, burst**. |
| Battery interface | 320A peak exceeds XT60 (60A continuous) and XT90 ratings. 80A-class 4-in-1 boards are realistically 30×30 mm. A 20×20 "nano" cannot shed this heat (see §3). |
| Burst duration | Must be specified. The thermal model below yields **≈5 s** at full 4×80A. |

## 2. Loss Model (Per Motor Channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, two phases are active at any instant:

- The PWM'd high-side FET conducts for time D; its complementary low-side conducts for 1−D.
- The opposite phase's low-side conducts 100% of the time.

The current path therefore always sees **two R_DS(on) in series**, independent of D:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

Across one electrical revolution, each of the 6 FETs carries roughly 1/3 of this load, so average per FET ≈ 3.2 W.

**Electrothermal coupling.** R_DS(on) depends on T_j, which depends on loss. This is a fixed-point problem: solve T_j = T_board + P(T_j)·R_th,jb iteratively. Using the 125 °C value directly, as above, gives a conservative closed-form bound.

**PCB copper loss** (add this; it is often ignored):

- Phase and bus path R_Cu ≈ 0.4 mΩ (2 oz, 4 layers, stitched) **[A]**
- P_Cu = 80² × 0.4 mΩ ≈ **2.6 W**

### 2.2 Switching Loss at 48 kHz

| Term | Formula | Result |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48k | 1.94 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f = 50n × 25.2 × 48k | 0.06 W |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss·V·f = 60n × 25.2 × 48k | 0.07 W |
| Dead-time diode | V_SD·I·(2·t_dead)·f = 0.8 × 80 × 400 ns × 48k | 1.23 W |
| Gate drive | 2 FETs × Q_g·V_GS·f = 2 × 60n × 10 × 48k | 0.06 W* |
| **P_sw total** | | **≈ 3.36 W** |

*Gate-drive loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g, not the FET.

**Design levers:**

- Dead time is the second-largest term. Reducing it to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per channel. Verify shoot-through margin with a scope at minimum V_GS.
- Dropping to 24 kHz halves P_sw, at the cost of increased current ripple and audible noise.

### 2.3 Totals

| | Per motor | ×4 burst |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Copper | 2.6 W | 10.4 W |
| Switching | 3.4 W | 13.4 W |
| **Total** | **25.2 W** | **≈ 100.6 W** |

Electrical efficiency at full burst: 25.2 W / (25.2 V × 80 A ≈ 2016 W in) ≈ **98.7%**. That looks excellent, but it does not mean the board survives. Heat density is the problem.
