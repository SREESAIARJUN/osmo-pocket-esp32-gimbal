## 0. Feasibility Findings

These findings shape everything below.

1. **"80A burst" on a nano form factor is a thermal-capacitance rating, not a steady-state one.** Section 3 shows four channels at 80A can be sustained for only about **1.5–3 s** before the board reaches its thermal limit. A single channel at 80A with the others at cruise lasts much longer. The datasheet must say which one is meant. I recommend specifying **"80A burst, 1 channel, ≤5 s; 4×45A burst ≤3 s"** or similar, validated on the bench.
2. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** Bidirectional DShot terminates at four ESC MCUs. The candidates are AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, both of which support EDT/bidirectional DShot. The H743's role is the timer/DMA layer only.
3. **The H743 is physically large for a nano board.** Its smallest practical packages are LQFP100 (14×14 mm) and TFBGA100 (8×8 mm). A 20×20 mm "nano" AIO needs a **two-board stack**: an FC board with the H743 in TFBGA100 and an ESC board. A single-board design is only realistic at 25.5×25.5 mm or 30.5×30.5 mm. I assume a 20×20 stack with the ESC board on the bottom.
4. **All device parameters below are placeholders.** They are representative of current 40V PQFN 3.3×3.3 MOSFETs. Replace every value marked ⟨DS⟩ with the selected part's datasheet value before sign-off. The equations are the deliverable; the numbers illustrate them.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in six-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification

At any instant one phase is high-side PWM, one is low-side ON, and one is floating for BEMF sensing. With complementary (synchronous) PWM:

- **During on-time (D):** current flows HS(A) → motor → LS(B).
- **During off-time (1−D):** current flows LS(A) freewheeling → motor → LS(B).

The current always passes through **exactly two conducting channels**, so:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \left[ 2R_{ds}(T_j) + R_{Cu} + R_{shunt,share} \right]
$$

### 1.2 Per-FET distribution

Each switch conducts for 120° of the 360° electrical period, which is a 1/3 conduction fraction.

- **HS FET:** conducts for D of its 1/3 share.
- **LS FET of the PWM leg:** conducts for 1−D of its share.
- **LS FET of the "solid" leg:** conducts for the full 1/3.

So the worst-case FET is a low-side device:

$$
P_{cond,FET,max} = \frac{1}{3} I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

At D≈1 the high-side carries the PWM-leg share, and the per-FET average is identical.

### 1.3 Temperature coupling and thermal-runaway check

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

$$
T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}(T_j)\, R_{\theta JC}
$$

Solving for the fixed point:

$$
T_j = \frac{T_{pcb} + R_{\theta JC} \cdot \tfrac{1}{3}I^2R_{25}(1-25\alpha) + R_{\theta JC}P_{sw,FET}}{1 - R_{\theta JC}\cdot\tfrac{1}{3}I^2R_{25}\alpha}
$$

The **runaway criterion** is that the denominator must stay greater than 0:

$$
\tfrac{1}{3}I^2R_{25}\alpha R_{\theta JC} = \tfrac{1}{3}(6400)(0.001)(0.0055)(1.2) = 0.014 \ll 1
$$
