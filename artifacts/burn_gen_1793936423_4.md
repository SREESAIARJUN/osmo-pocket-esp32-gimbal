## 1. FOC Current Loop

### 1.1 Coordinate transforms

**Electrical angle:**
θ_e = p·(θ_m − θ_off) mod 2π

**Clarke transform** (amplitude-invariant, with i_a + i_b + i_c = 0):
- i_α = i_a
- i_β = (i_a + 2i_b)/√3

**Park transform:**
- i_d = i_α cos θ_e + i_β sin θ_e
- i_q = −i_α sin θ_e + i_β cos θ_e

**Inverse Park:**
- v_α = v_d cos θ_e − v_q sin θ_e
- v_β = v_d sin θ_e + v_q cos θ_e

### 1.2 Plant and PI design by pole-zero cancellation

The dq model is:
- L di_d/dt = v_d − R i_d + ω_e L i_q
- L di_q/dt = v_q − R i_q − ω_e L i_d − ω_e λ_m

**Decoupling feedforward:**
- v_d,ff = −ω_e L i_q
- v_q,ff = ω_e L i_d + ω_e λ_m, where λ_m = 2Kt/(3p)

After decoupling, each axis is the first-order plant G(s) = 1/(Ls + R). A PI controller C(s) = K_p + K_i/s with zero at R/L cancels the plant pole. The closed loop becomes a first-order lag with bandwidth ω_c:

- K_p = L·ω_c
- K_i = R·ω_c
- ω_c ≤ 2π·f_pwm/20

For 20 kHz, ω_c = 2π·1000 rad/s, giving K_p = 13.2 V/A and K_i = 35,200 V/(A·s).

### 1.3 Voltage limiting and SVPWM

**Circular voltage limit:**
|v_dq| ≤ V_max = V_dc/√3 (the linear SVPWM region)

**SVPWM** uses min-max zero-sequence injection:
- v_a = v_α
- v_b = −½v_α + (√3/2)v_β
- v_c = −½v_α − (√3/2)v_β
- v_0 = −(max(v_x) + min(v_x))/2
- Duty: d_x = ½ + (v_x + v_0)/V_dc

### 1.4 Discrete PI with back-calculation anti-windup

- u_n = K_p e_n + I_n
- ū_n = sat(u_n)
- I_{n+1} = I_n + T_s [K_i e_n + K_aw(ū_n − u_n)]

Choose K_aw ≈ K_i/K_p, so the tracking time constant T_t = √(T_i·T_d) collapses to T_i for a PI controller.

## 2. Velocity Estimation: Encoder PLL Plus Gyro Blend

### 2.1 Encoder PLL (type-II tracking observer)

- e = wrap(θ_meas − θ̂)
- θ̂_{n+1} = θ̂_n + T_s(ω̂_n + k_1 e)
- ω̂_{n+1} = ω̂_n + T_s k_2 e

Gains: k_1 = 2ζω_n, k_2 = ω_n², with ζ = 0.9 and ω_n = 2π·250 rad/s.

This avoids differentiating a quantized angle. Velocity noise is roughly σ_q·ω_n^1.5 instead of σ_q/T_s.

### 2.2 Inertial rate for stabilization

The stabilization velocity loop must regulate inertial camera rate, not motor-relative rate. The rate projected onto joint axis j is:

ω_j,inertial = â_jᵀ · ω_cam

Here â_j is the joint axis expressed in the camera frame, from the gimbal kinematics. The roll and pitch axes are coupled through the current joint angles.

Blend the two sources with a complementary filter at f_b ≈ 30 Hz:

ω_fb = ω_gyro,j + LPF_fb[(ω̂_enc − ω_base,j) − ω_gyro,j]

The gyro provides high-bandwidth inertial rate. The encoder relative rate provides low-frequency consistency. If the base-rate estimate is unavailable, use ω_fb = ω_gyro,j directly. That is the usual choice on handheld or drone gimbals.
