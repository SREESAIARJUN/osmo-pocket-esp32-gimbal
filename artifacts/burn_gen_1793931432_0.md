## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right]$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{ds} = 1.5 \times (1 + 0.45) = 2.18\ \text{m}\Omega$$

Use **2.2 mΩ** as the hot design value.

### 1.2 Current paths under trapezoidal (6-step) commutation with synchronous rectification

At any instant two phases conduct: one high-side leg (HS) and one low-side leg (LS).

With complementary PWM at duty cycle D:
- The HS FET conducts for a fraction D of each period.
- The synchronous LS FET of the same leg conducts for the remaining (1 − D).
- The opposite leg's LS FET conducts 100% of the time.

Because the phase current is continuous (L_motor ≫ 1/f_sw), the losses sum as:

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{ph}^2 R_{ds} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This result is **independent of D**. That is useful: worst-case conduction loss does not depend on throttle position at a fixed phase current.

> **Note:** Phase current is not battery current: $I_{ph} = I_{bat}/D$. An "80 A burst" ESC rating normally refers to phase current.

### 1.3 Conduction-loss results

| Condition | I_ph | R_ds | P_cond per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Burst, hot | 80 A | 2.2 mΩ | 2·6400·0.0022 = **28.2 W** | **112.6 W** |
| Burst, cold start | 80 A | 1.5 mΩ | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Continuous, hot | 35 A | 2.2 mΩ | 5.4 W | 21.6 W |

Add PCB copper resistance. Phase and bus traces at 2 oz, roughly 0.3 mΩ per path, give:

$$P_{Cu} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 0.0003 = 3.8\ \text{W per motor at burst}$$

Do not neglect this term.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switched leg contributes switching loss.

### 2.1 Voltage–current overlap (hard-switched HS turn-on and turn-off)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 20\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.968\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Body-diode reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

Q_rr scales with di/dt and I_ph. At 80 A, derate the datasheet value upward by about 1.5×, giving roughly 0.09 W.

### 2.3 Output capacitance (C_oss)

$$P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 60\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.073\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two dead-time intervals per period)

$$P_{dt} = V_f I_{ph} \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 100\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.307\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (HS + LS of the switching leg)

Most of this power dissipates in the driver and R_g, not in the FET:

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GD} f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 45\text{n} \cdot 10 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.043\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Switching-loss summary per motor at 80 A

| Term | Power |
|---|---|
| V·I overlap | 0.97 W |
| Q_rr (derated) | 0.09 W |
| C_oss | 0.07 W |
| Dead time | 0.31 W |
| Gate | 0.04 W |
| **Total P_sw** | **≈ 1.48 W** |

**Sensitivity:** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it is about 0.74 W; at 96 kHz about 2.96 W.

At 80 A, conduction loss is about 19× switching loss. Therefore:
- **Prioritise R_ds(on).** It matters far more than fast edges.
- **Do not reduce R_g aggressively** to chase switching loss. It worsens ringing and EMI on the IMU.

## 3. Total Loss Budget and Efficiency

$$P_{tot,motor} = P_{cond} + P_{Cu} + P_{sw} = 28.2 + 3.8 + 1.5 = 33.5\ \text{W}$$

**All four motors at 80 A burst: ≈ 134 W.**

Efficiency check at 80 A phase current, D = 0.8:
- Battery current = 64 A per motor
- Input power = 22.2 V × 64 A ≈ 1421 W per motor
- **η ≈ 97.6 %**

The efficiency is excellent. The problem is the heat *density*, not the percentage loss.

Worst case: the LS FET in the non-switched leg sees the full current continuously.

$$P_{FET,max} = 80^2 \cdot 0.0022 = 14.1\ \text{W}$$
