# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal & DShot Architecture

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility

**The STM32H743 cannot commutate the motors directly.** It runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071. So this design is:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──bidir DShot600 ×4──► 4× ESC MCU ──► 4× 3-phase gate driver ──► 24 MOSFETs
```

**80 A burst on a nano (20×20 or 25×25 mm) form factor is a short transient rating only.** The math below shows it is sustainable for roughly 1 to 2 seconds. Continuous capability is about 25 to 30 A per channel. All numbers below are a parametric model with stated assumptions. Substitute values from your actual MOSFET datasheet before committing.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

In 6-step commutation, at any instant two legs carry current:
- **PWM leg:** high-side (HS) conducts for a fraction D of the period; its complementary low-side (LS) conducts for (1 − D).
- **Sink leg:** LS is held fully on.

Instantaneous conduction loss therefore always involves exactly two R_DS(on) in series:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I^2 R_{hot} = 2(80)^2(1.5\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ channel}$$

**Per-FET distribution.** Each leg spends 1/3 of the electrical cycle as the PWM leg, 1/3 as the sink, and 1/3 floating:

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I^2 R,\qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)\, I^2R + \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R$$

At D → 1 (full-throttle burst), the LS sink role dominates. Each FET averages about 3.2 W, with instantaneous peaks of 9.6 W.

**Copper path.** Add the PCB trace and pad resistance. As a rule of thumb, 2 oz copper is about 0.25 mΩ/□. For a realistic 0.3 mΩ round-trip path, the extra loss is 80² × 0.3 mΩ ≈ 1.9 W per channel.

**Paralleling.** With n FETs per switch, R becomes R/n, so P_cond falls as 1/n. This is the primary lever, but board area forbids n > 1 on a 20×20 board.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg's HS sees hard switching. The LS turns on and off with near-zero V_DS because the body diode is already conducting during dead time.

### 3.1 Transition times (charge-based)

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_g+R_{src}} = \frac{7}{6.7} = 1.04\ A \;\Rightarrow\; t_{on} = \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{g,on}} = 13.4\ ns$$

$$I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_g+R_{snk}} = \frac{3}{5.7} = 0.53\ A \;\Rightarrow\; t_{off} = 26.6\ ns$$

### 3.2 Loss terms

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V·I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_on + t_off)·f_sw = ½(25.2)(80)(40 ns)(48k) | **1.94 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 60n·25.2·48k | 0.07 W |
| C_oss (HS + LS node) | 2·½·C_oss·V²·f_sw | 0.05 W |
| Dead-time body diode | 2·V_f·I·t_dt·f_sw = 2(0.8)(80)(200n)(48k) | **1.23 W** |
| Gate drive (mostly in driver/R_g) | 2·Q_g·V_drv·f_sw | 0.05 W |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 3.3 W per channel** |

**Design levers:**
- Cutting dead time to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per channel. Validate shoot-through margin against the driver's propagation-delay mismatch.
- Dropping to 24 kHz halves every switching term, at the cost of more audible noise and current ripple.

$$P_{ch} \approx 19.2 + 1.9 + 3.3 \approx \mathbf{24.4\ W}, \qquad P_{4ch} \approx \mathbf{98\ W}$$
