# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model (Job #15)

## 0. Constraints That Drive the Design

The brief has a physics conflict that has to be resolved before anything else.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In a production 4-in-1 stack, ArduPilot runs on the H743. It sends bidirectional DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs, which run AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware on parts such as the STM32G071, STM32F051 or AT32F421. Each ESC MCU handles commutation, back-EMF zero-cross detection and the 48 kHz PWM. The model below assumes this partition.

**2. 80 A per motor cannot be sustained on a nano (20×20 mm) board.** Section 3 shows that sustained 80 A per channel requires a 30.5×30.5 mm or larger board with real heatsinking. The 80 A rating is therefore treated as a timed burst. I derive the allowable burst duration rather than assume it.

**3. All component parameters are representative placeholders.** Values for R_DS(on), Q_g, Q_rr, R_θJC and so on are typical of 40 V MOSFETs in 3.3×3.3 mm DFN packages. Substitute datasheet values for the parts you actually select. The equations stay the same.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

**Current path.** The ESC uses 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation with synchronous rectification and complementary PWM. At every instant, exactly one high-side FET and one low-side FET carry the phase current. This holds during both the PWM on-time and the freewheel interval. Conduction loss is therefore approximately independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

**Temperature dependence of R_DS(on):**

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) \approx R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0067\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

This gives k_T = 1.5 at T_j = 100 °C, so R_hot = 1.5 mΩ.

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ channel}$$

That is 9.6 W in each of the two conducting FETs.

**Thermal runaway check.** R_DS(on) rises with temperature, which raises loss, which raises temperature. The loop converges only if the loop gain is below 1:

$$G = I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\, R_{\theta JA} < 1$$

For a single FET at 80 A with R_θJA ≈ 40 K/W (a 3×3 mm DFN on a small board, with no heatsink):

$$G = 6400 \times 0.001 \times 0.0067 \times 40 \approx 1.7$$

**G > 1, so the FET is thermally unstable at steady state.** This independently confirms that 80 A can only be a time-limited burst on this board.

**Wiring and shunt losses** (add these to the total):

- Phase-current shunt, 0.25 mΩ (if per-phase sensing is used): 80² × 0.25 m = 1.6 W
- Copper traces, roughly 0.3 mΩ per path: ≈1.9 W

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per channel, one hard-switched leg operates at any time.

**(a) V-I overlap loss**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction**

$$P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

This term is larger than the overlap loss. Reducing dead time to 100 ns cuts it in half, but only if gate-driver propagation matching allows it without shoot-through.

**(c) Reverse recovery**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

**(d) C_oss discharge**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V^2 f_{sw} = 0.015\ \text{W}$$

**(e) Gate drive**

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FETs. With 6 FETs per channel and roughly 2 of them actively switching:

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2 \approx 0.06\ \text{W}$$

**Switching subtotal: ≈2.3 W per channel.**

### Per-channel and stack totals at the 80 A burst

| Loss term | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs, hot) | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching + dead time + Q_rr + C_oss | 2.3 W | 9.2 W |
| Shunt + copper | 3.5 W | 14.0 W |
| **Total** | **≈25 W** | **≈100 W** |

**Efficiency check.** Assume output power ≈ V_bus × D × I at an effective duty of about 0.9. That gives 4 × 25.2 × 0.9 × 80 ≈ 7.26 kW, so efficiency ≈ 98.6 %. The efficiency is excellent; the problem is entirely the heat density.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Junction-to-board temperature rise

Each conducting FET dissipates about 9.6 W from conduction plus about 1.2 W from switching, for P_FET ≈ 10.8 W.

$$\Delta T_{JC} = 10.8 \times 1.5 = \mathbf{16\ K}$$

The junction therefore sits about 16 K above the copper beneath it.

### 3.2 Board heat capacity (20×20 mm, 8 layers, 2 oz copper, 1.6 mm thick)

| Copper heat capacity | 1.41 g × 0.385 J/g·K | 0.54 J/K |
| FR-4 | ≈0.5 cm³ × 1.85 g/cm³ × 1.1 J/g·K | 1.02 J/K |
| 24 FETs, caps, pads | estimate | ≈0.3 J/K |
| **Total C_th** | | **≈1.9 J/K** |

### 3.3 Steady-state dissipation capability

With prop-wash convection at h ≈ 100 W/m²·K over both board faces (A = 8×10⁻⁴ m²):

$$R_{\theta,conv} = \frac{1}{hA} = 12.5\ \text{K/W}$$

**Usable thermal budget:**

- Ambient: T_amb = 40 °C
- Board limit: T_board,max = 105 °C (FR-4 and ceramic capacitor derating)
- Junction ceiling: T_j,max = 150 °C (175 °C rated, with margin), which is consistent with 105 + 16 K

**Sustainable power:**

$$P_{ss} = \frac{105-40}{12.5} = \mathbf{5.2\ W\ total}$$

Each loss term scales as I². Inverting the loss model gives a sustainable current of only **about 18 A per channel continuous** on a bare 20×20 mm board. This is consistent with commercial 20×20 mm 4-in-1 ESCs, which are rated around 25–45 A with burst ratings that are optimistic.
