# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #18): Power-Stage, Thermal, and Bidirectional DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and a Feasibility Warning

**Architecture assumed.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller. It runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs (AT32F421-class, running AM32 or BLHeli_32). Each ESC MCU drives a 3-phase gate driver and 6 N-channel MOSFETs.

If you intended the H743 to commutate the motors directly, DShot does not apply, and Section 4 should be replaced by a 6-step/FOC timer design. Tell me and I'll redo it.

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus,max = 25.2 V (design point), nominal 22.2 V |
| MOSFET (per switch) | 40 V N-ch, R_ds(on) = 1.0 mΩ @ 25 °C, ×1.6 at T_j = 125 °C → 1.6 mΩ |
| Gate charges | Q_g = 40 nC, Q_gd = 10 nC, Q_rr ≈ 50 nC, Q_oss ≈ 40 nC, V_SD = 0.8 V |
| Gate drive | 10 V drive, ~10 Ω effective driver + 2.2 Ω R_g |
| PWM | 48 kHz, synchronous (complementary), dead time t_d = 100 ns |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal (two phases active, one PWM leg) |
| Burst | 80 A per motor, all four motors simultaneously (worst case) |
| Board | "Nano" = 20×20 mm mounting (~25×25 mm outline), 6-layer, 2 oz copper |

> ⚠️ **Feasibility verdict, up front.** As the math below shows, 80 A × 4 on a 20×20 board is a **~1.5–2 second burst rating, not a continuous one**.
> - Realistic continuous capability is **~15–20 A per motor**.
> - Total battery current at full burst is **320 A**, which no 20×20 power-entry pad or connector can carry.
>
> I've modeled it rigorously so the limit is quantified rather than guessed. Do not put "80 A" on a datasheet without the time qualifier.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Per-channel conduction

In 6-step synchronous PWM, current I flows through exactly two legs at any instant:

- **Non-PWM active leg:** one FET conducts 100% of the sector.
- **PWM leg:** the high side conducts for D, the low side (synchronous rectifier) for 1−D. The sum is always 1.

Conduction loss is therefore independent of duty:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[1] + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

Temperature dependence (linearized):

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1} \Rightarrow 1.6\ \text{m}\Omega \text{ at } 125\,°\text{C}
$$

**At 80 A, T_j = 125 °C:**

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.6\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{20.5\ W\ per\ channel}
$$

### 1.2 PCB copper loss (frequently ignored, not negligible)

Assume a phase path of about 0.3 mΩ of copper (2 oz, short pour, vias) in each of two current paths. Bus-side copper is shared between channels.

$$
P_{Cu} \approx 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.3\times10^{-3} = 3.8\ \text{W per channel}
$$

### 1.3 Per-FET hotspot

The non-PWM-leg FET dissipates I²R = 10.2 W while it conducts, i.e. for 1/3 of the electrical cycle.

- Electrical frequency at 80 A is ~1–3 kHz.
- That period is far shorter than the die/package thermal time constant (~10–50 ms).
- So the **time-averaged** value is valid: averaged over all 6 switches, ≈ 23.2 W / 6 ≈ **3.9 W per FET** (total loss from Section 3).
