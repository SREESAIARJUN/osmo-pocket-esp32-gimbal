**Architecture correction.** In a 4-in-1 ESC, the H743 does not commutate the motors. Each ESC channel has its own MCU (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32). The H743 sends bi-directional DShot600 to those ESC MCUs. The DMA and timer analysis in §3 therefore covers the H743-to-ESC-MCU link.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Path

With complementary PWM in 6-step commutation, two phases are active at any instant. Current always flows through exactly two MOSFET channels:

- the switching leg (high side during the on-time, low side during the off-time), and
- the static low side of the return phase.

Dead-time intervals are the exception: current then flows through the body diode, which is handled in §2.4.

### 1.2 Temperature-Corrected R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0067\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 100 °C this gives R_ds ≈ 1.5 mΩ.

### 1.3 Conduction Loss per ESC Channel

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_m^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)\,(1 - 2 t_{dt} f_{sw})$$

The dead-time correction factor is 1 − 2(200 ns)(48 kHz) = 0.981, so:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.5\times10^{-3} \cdot 0.981 = \mathbf{18.8\ W}$$

### 1.4 Phase Current vs. Battery Current

The two currents are related by

$$I_{bat} = D \cdot I_m$$

where D is the PWM duty cycle. If "80 A burst" refers to battery current, the phase current at partial throttle is higher than 80 A, and conduction loss scales with the square. The ESC firmware current limit must specify which current it limits. AM32 limits on the shunt (battery) current, so I recommend adding a phase-current estimate as a separate limit.

### 1.5 Shunt and Copper Losses

| Loss | Expression | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, per channel if used) | I_bat² · R_sh | ≤ 1.6 W at 80 A |
| Phase traces and pads (est. 0.3 mΩ total path) | I_m² · R_cu | 1.9 W |

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ed leg switches. The static low side is commutated at electrical frequency (≪ f_sw), so its switching loss is negligible.

### 2.1 Hard-Switching Overlap (High-Side Turn-On and Turn-Off)

The transition times follow from the Miller charge and gate current:

$$t_r \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g} = \frac{8\,\text{nC}}{0.53\,\text{A}_{\text{eff, plateau}}} \approx 15\ \text{ns}$$

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_m (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 30\,\text{ns} \cdot 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse Recovery (Low-Side Body Diode, Charged at HS Turn-On)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\,\text{nC} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\,\text{kHz} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Output Capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \approx 0.060\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD} I_m (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 400\,\text{ns} \cdot 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

Dead time is the largest lever after R_ds(on). Reducing it to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per channel, but you must verify shoot-through margin with the gate driver's propagation-delay mismatch.

### 2.5 Gate Drive (Dissipated in Driver and Gate Resistors)

$$P_g = 2 \cdot Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 50\,\text{nC} \cdot 10 \cdot 48\,\text{kHz} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Loss Budget at 80 A Burst

| Loss term | Per ESC | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 18.8 W | 75.2 W |
| Switching overlap | 1.45 W | 5.8 W |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 W | 4.9 W |
| Q_rr + Q_oss + gate | 0.16 W | 0.6 W |
| Copper + shunt | 3.5 W | 14.0 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 25.1 W** | **≈ 100 W** |
