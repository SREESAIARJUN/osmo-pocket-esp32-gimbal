# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Basis (Job #20)

## 0. Architecture Correction (Read First)

The brief combines two functions that must stay on separate silicon. Getting this wrong invalidates the timer and DMA plan.

| Function | Silicon | Firmware | Timing domain |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flight control, DShot master, bidirectional telemetry decode | STM32H743VIT6 | ArduPilot | DShot600 frames at loop rate (1–8 kHz) |
| Motor commutation, 48 kHz PWM, BEMF/zero-cross, current sense | 4× AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071GBU6 (one per motor) | AM32 (or BLHeli_32) | 48 kHz PWM, 6-step |

**The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz PWM.** It sends DShot600 to four ESC MCUs. Each ESC MCU runs its own 48 kHz complementary PWM through a 3-phase gate driver into six MOSFETs.

The power and switching analysis below therefore applies to the ESC stage. The DMA and timer analysis applies to the H743.

**Form-factor warning.** "Nano" (20×20 mm, M2) and an "80 A burst per motor" are physically incompatible beyond roughly 1 second. Section 3 quantifies this. The baseline here is a **30.5×30.5 mm** board, with the 20×20 numbers shown for contrast.

All component parameters below are **stated design assumptions**, representative of 40 V shielded-gate MOSFETs in 3.3×3.3 mm DFN packages. Substitute datasheet maxima for your selected parts and re-run.

### 1.2 Temperature-Dependent Resistance (Fixed-Point Problem)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + R_{\theta JC}\,P_{FET}(T_j)$$

Iterate until T_j converges. The loop runs away (thermal runaway) if:

$$R_{\theta JC}\cdot\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j} = R_{\theta JC}\, I^2 R_{DS,25}\,\alpha \ge 1$$

At 80 A this term is 1.5 · 6400 · 1.2 m · 0.0055 = 0.063. That is far below 1, so the MOSFET junction itself is stable. The PCB is the limit (§3).

Design point: T_j ≈ 100 °C gives R_DS ≈ 1.2 × 1.41 = **1.70 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Current Path in 6-Step Commutation with Synchronous Rectification

In any 60° sector, current flows through exactly **two** MOSFETs:
- one high-side switch (or its complementary low-side partner during PWM off-time), and
- one low-side switch held on for the full sector.

The PWM duty cycle D cancels out of the total:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2R_{DS} = 2I^2R_{DS}$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2(80)^2(1.70\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{21.8\ W\ per\ motor\ channel}$$

**Per-device average.** Each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical cycle. At 30k RPM with 14 poles the electrical frequency is about 3.5 kHz, which is much faster than the die thermal time constant (milliseconds). So the junction sees the average:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} = \frac{I^2R_{DS}}{3} = \mathbf{3.63\ W}$$
