## 0. Architecture Decision and Feasibility Findings

### 0.1 System partitioning

ArduPilot cannot commutate four BLDC motors from the H743. The board therefore uses a split architecture:

```
            ┌────────────────────────── FC domain (3V3) ──────────────────────────┐
 RX/GPS ──► │ STM32H743VIT6 @ 480 MHz   IMU(SPI1)  BARO(I2C2)  OSD(SPI2)  SD     │
            │   TIMx_CH1..4  ── bidirectional DShot600 (single wire, open-drain)   │
            └──────┬──────────┬──────────┬──────────┬─────────────────────────────┘
                   │M1        │M2        │M3        │M4
            ┌──────▼───┐┌─────▼────┐┌────▼─────┐┌───▼──────┐
            │AT32F421  ││AT32F421  ││AT32F421  ││AT32F421  │  AM32 / BLHeli_32-class
            │ 48 kHz   ││ 48 kHz   ││ 48 kHz   ││ 48 kHz   │  ESC MCU, 1 per motor
            │ 3-ph drv ││ 3-ph drv ││ 3-ph drv ││ 3-ph drv │
            │ 6× NMOS  ││ 6× NMOS  ││ 6× NMOS  ││ 6× NMOS  │
            └──────────┘└──────────┘└──────────┘└──────────┘
```

- The 48 kHz PWM is generated by each ESC MCU.
- The H743 only generates DShot600 frames and decodes eRPM telemetry.

### 0.2 Feasibility flag on the 80 A burst rating

The math in Sections 1 and 2 shows the following. Read these numbers before committing to the "80 A" label.

| Condition | Total dissipation | Board survival (nano, 30×30 mm) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 motor @ 80 A | ~24 W | a few seconds, localized hotspot |
| 4 motors @ 80 A | ~96 W | **≈ 4–6 s to T_j = 125 °C** |
| Continuous capability with propwash | ~10–15 W total | ≈ 25–30 A per motor continuous |

**Recommendation:** Market the board as "80 A, ≤ 2 s burst; 30 A continuous per channel." Enforce this with firmware thermal derating (Section 2.5). An 80 A burst on a nano-class footprint is only defensible as a short transient.

### 1.2 Conduction loss

**Operating assumption:** 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous (complementary) PWM on the active leg.

- At any instant, current flows through exactly two FET positions in series: one in the PWM leg and one static.
- Synchronous rectification means the PWM leg is always conducting through either its high or its low FET.
- Conduction loss is therefore approximately independent of duty cycle:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2\left[2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j) + R_{pcb}\right], \qquad R_{ds(on)}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\left[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\right]
$$

where α ≈ 0.006 /K, which gives k_T ≈ 1.6 at 125 °C.

**At 80 A:**

| Junction temperature | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| T_j = 25 °C | 6400 × (2.0 m + 0.3 m) | **14.7 W** |
| T_j = 125 °C | 6400 × (3.2 m + 0.3 m) | **22.4 W** |

**Electrothermal feedback.** Because R_ds rises with T_j, the loss grows as the board heats. Solve iteratively, or use the closed form for a lumped thermal resistance R_θ:

$$
P_{cond}(T)=P_0\,[1+\alpha'(T-25)] ,\quad T = T_a + R_\theta P \;\Rightarrow\; P=\frac{P_0[1+\alpha'(T_a-25)]}{1-\alpha' P_0 R_\theta}
$$

Thermal runaway occurs when α′·P₀·R_θ ≥ 1. At 80 A continuous with R_θ ≈ 8 K/W, α′P₀R_θ ≈ 0.005 × 14.7 × 8 ≈ 0.6. That is stable but strongly amplified, which is another reason 80 A must remain a burst-only rating.

### 1.3 Switching loss at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Each PWM period contains one hard-switched turn-on and one turn-off.

**(a) V–I overlap loss**

$$
P_{VI}=\tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(40\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction**

$$
P_{dt}=V_F\, I\, (2t_{dead})\, f_{sw}=0.8(80)(400\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz})=\mathbf{1.23\ W}
$$

This is the largest tunable loss. Cutting dead time from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per channel. Verify that shoot-through margin remains, with gate-driver propagation skew under 30 ns.

**(c) Reverse recovery**

$$
P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}=30\,\text{nC}(25.2)(48\,\text{k})=\mathbf{0.036\ W}
$$

This is small, but Q_rr also drives switch-node ringing and EMI.

**(d) Output capacitance**

$$
P_{oss}=C_{oss,eff}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw}=1.5\,\text{nF}(635)(48\,\text{k})=\mathbf{0.046\ W}
$$

This term combines the ½CV² of the two FETs in the leg.

**(e) Gate drive**

This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET.

$$
P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2(60\,\text{n})(10)(48\,\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.058\ W}
$$

**Switching subtotal: ≈ 3.3 W per channel.**

### 1.4 Per-channel and board totals at 80 A

| Loss | T_j = 25 °C | T_j = 125 °C |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 14.7 W | 22.4 W |
| Switching (VI) | 1.94 W | 1.94 W |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 W | 1.23 W |
| Qrr + Coss + gate | 0.14 W | 0.14 W |
| **Per channel** | **18.0 W** | **25.7 W** |
| **4 channels** | **72 W** | **103 W** |

**Efficiency check:**

- Output power ≈ 25.2 V × 80 A × duty. At full-throttle burst (D ≈ 0.95), that is about 1.9 kW per motor.
- ESC efficiency is therefore about 98.7 %, which is plausible.
- The problem is not efficiency. It is **power density**: roughly 100 W in about 9 cm² of PCB.

### 1.5 Frequency sensitivity

At 80 A and 125 °C:

$$
P_{sw}(f) \approx 6.9\times10^{-5}\, f \ \text{[W]}
$$

| PWM frequency | Switching loss | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 24 kHz | 1.6 W | more current ripple and acoustic noise on low-inductance motors |
| 48 kHz | 3.3 W | baseline |
| 96 kHz | 6.6 W | |

48 kHz is a reasonable compromise for 5–7" class motors with phase inductance of about 20–40 µH.
