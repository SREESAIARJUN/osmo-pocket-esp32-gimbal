## 1. Rate Hierarchy and Bandwidth Separation

Cascaded loops are only stable and decoupled if each inner loop is substantially faster than the loop outside it.

```
          1 kHz                5 kHz                 20 kHz
θ_ref ─► [Angle P+FF] ─ω_ref─► [Vel PI + cog FF] ─iq_ref─► [Current PI dq + decoupling] ─► SVPWM
   ▲                        ▲                                ▲
   │ IMU attitude (fused)   │ PLL ω̂ (encoder) ⊕ gyro rate    │ ia, ib (shunts), θ_e (encoder)
```

The bandwidth design rule is

$$
\omega_{c,i} \ge 5\,\omega_{c,v},\qquad \omega_{c,v} \ge 4\,\omega_{c,\theta},\qquad \omega_{c,i} \le \frac{2\pi f_{loop}}{10}
$$

The chosen bandwidths are:

- **Current loop:** ω_c,i = 2π·1500 rad/s.
- **Velocity loop:** ω_c,v = 2π·150 rad/s.
- **Angle loop:** ω_c,θ = 2π·25 rad/s.

## 2. FOC Current Loop

### 2.1 Transforms

The electrical angle comes from the mechanical encoder angle and the calibrated offset:

$$
\theta_e = p\,\theta_m - \theta_{off}
$$

**Clarke transform** (amplitude-invariant, with i_a + i_b + i_c = 0):

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \frac{i_a + 2 i_b}{\sqrt3}
$$

**Park transform:**

$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix}=
\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}
$$

**Inverse Park transform:**

$$
v_\alpha = v_d\cos\theta_e - v_q\sin\theta_e,\qquad v_\beta = v_d\sin\theta_e + v_q\cos\theta_e
$$

### 2.2 Plant and Decoupling

The dq-frame voltage equations of the motor are:

$$
v_d = R i_d + L\dot i_d - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad
v_q = R i_q + L\dot i_q + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e\lambda
$$

Apply the following feedforward terms:

$$
v_d^{ff} = -\omega_e L i_q,\qquad v_q^{ff} = \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e \lambda
$$

With these terms, each axis reduces to the first-order plant

$$
G_i(s) = \frac{1}{Ls + R}
$$

### 2.3 PI Design (Pole–Zero Cancellation)

Use the PI controller

$$
C_i(s) = K_p + \frac{K_i}{s},
\qquad K_p = L\,\omega_{c,i},\qquad K_i = R\,\omega_{c,i}
$$

The PI zero at K_i/K_p = R/L cancels the electrical pole. The open loop becomes ω_c,i/s, so the closed loop is a first-order low-pass at ω_c,i.
