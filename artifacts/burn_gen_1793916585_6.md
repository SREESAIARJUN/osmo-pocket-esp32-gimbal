## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Feasibility Flags

The standard partitioning for this class of board places responsibilities on different devices:

- **STM32H743 (FC):** runs ArduPilot and generates bi-directional DShot600.
- **Four dedicated ESC MCUs** (AT32F421 or STM32G071, running AM32 or BLHeli_32): handle 6-step commutation at **48 kHz PWM**.
- **The H743 never drives gates directly.** The 48 kHz switching analysis therefore applies to the ESC MCU's timer and gate driver.

Two feasibility flags need sign-off before layout starts.

| # | Flag | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | 80 A burst on all four channels at once means about 320 A from the pack. | That exceeds XT60 (~60 A continuous) and XT90 ratings. Section 4 models it, but realistically it is only a sub-2-second event. |
| F2 | A 20×20 or 30×30 mm "nano" PCB cannot reject ~100 W in steady state. | 80 A must be specified as a **time-limited burst**. Section 4.4 derives the allowed duration. |

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Current Path

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. In every PWM sub-interval, current flows through exactly two FETs in series:

- **ON state:** high-side FET of phase A, then the motor, then the static low-side FET of phase B.
