## 0. Scope, Architecture Decision, and Assumptions

### 0.1 Architecture clarification

An STM32H743 should **not** commutate the motors directly. ArduPilot has no BLDC commutation firmware, and running 4× six-step commutation at 48 kHz alongside the flight stack is not viable.

The baseline is therefore a two-tier system:

| Tier | Device | Firmware | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100, 480 MHz) | ArduPilot | Attitude control, sends DShot600, receives eRPM |
| ESC (×4) | AT32F421K8U7 (QFN32) or equivalent | AM32 (or BLHeli_32-class) | Commutation, 48 kHz complementary PWM, bidir DShot responder |
| Gate drive (×4) | FD6288Q-class 3-phase half-bridge driver | — | 6 gate outputs per motor, bootstrap high side |
| Power stage | 24× N-MOSFET, 40 V, DFN5×6 | — | 6 FETs per motor |

The design can be built as a single board or as a stack. Section 3.6 explains why a **stacked FC/ESC (separate PCBs)** is strongly recommended.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path in six-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification

At any commutation step, two phases conduct and one floats (BEMF sensing).

- **PWM on-time (D):** HS of phase A and LS of phase B conduct.
- **PWM off-time (1−D):** LS of phase A (synchronous freewheel, after dead time) and LS of phase B conduct.

Phase current magnitude is ≈ I in both intervals because motor inductance holds it. Two FETs therefore always carry current, independent of duty cycle:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot R_{ds,hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{rms}^2 \cdot R_{ds,hot} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds,hot}
$$

### 1.2 RMS including PWM ripple

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{BEMF})\,D}{L_{ph-ph}\,f_{sw}}
$$

Example: 5 µH phase-to-phase (typical 2207-class motor), V_bus = 25.2 V, D = 0.5, V_BEMF ≈ 12.6 V gives:

ΔI_pp = (12.6 × 0.5) / (5×10⁻⁶ × 48 000) = **26 A**

ΔI²/12 = 56 A
