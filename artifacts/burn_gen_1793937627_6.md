## 0. Architecture Decision and Baseline Assumptions

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. It is the flight-controller MCU and talks bi-directional DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs (for example AT32F421 running AM32). Those MCUs generate the 48 kHz complementary PWM. This matches every production "AIO/stack" design. Commutating four motors from the H743 while also running ArduPilot is not a supported ArduPilot configuration.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (6-Step Trapezoidal, Synchronous)

During any 60° commutation sector, current I flows through two phases:

- **PWM phase.** The high-side FET conducts for duty D. The low-side FET of the same phase conducts for (1 − D) as the synchronous rectifier.
- **Return phase.** The low-side FET is on for the full sector.

The resistive path therefore always contains exactly two R_ds(on) elements, independent of D:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{hot}\big[D + (1-D)\big] + I^2 R_{hot} = 2\,I^2 R_{hot}$$

Dead time is neglected here; it appears in the diode term in Section 2.

**Result at 80 A burst:**

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ channel}$$

**Result at 35 A continuous:** 2 × 1225 × 1.5 mΩ = **3.7 W**

**Distribution per FET.** Each phase conducts for 4 of the 6 sectors. Averaged over one electrical cycle, the 19.2 W spreads across 6 FETs, giving about 3.2 W average per FET.

- The low-side FET in the "return" role sees about 9.6 W instantaneously.
- The electrical period is short compared with the FET thermal time constant. For example, at 30,000 eRPM the period is 2 ms, while τ_jc is about 5–20 ms. So the cycle average is valid above roughly 5,000 eRPM.
- Below that, at stall or startup, use 9.6 W for the hot-spot calculation.

**Copper loss is not negligible.** A 6 mm × 15 mm path on 2 oz copper is about 0.6 mΩ per layer, and with 4 paralleled layers about 0.15 mΩ. Across the bus and phase paths this adds roughly I² × 0.3 mΩ ≈ **1.9 W per channel at 80 A**. A phase-current shunt (if fitted, for example 0.25 mΩ) adds **1.6 W**.

**V–I overlap (hard switching):**
