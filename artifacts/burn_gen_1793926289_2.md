**Architecture clarification:** In a stacked or integrated "4-in-1 + H743" design, the H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600. Four independent ESC MCUs commutate the bridges at 48 kHz. Keeping the H743 out of the commutation loop is deliberate: it keeps ArduPilot's scheduler deterministic and isolates FC faults from bridge faults.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous commutation

In each 60° electrical sector, two phases conduct:
- **Leg A (PWM'd):** HS on for duty D, LS on for (1−D) through synchronous rectification.
- **Leg B (return):** LS fully on.
- **Leg C:** floating, used for BEMF zero-cross sensing.

With synchronous rectification, motor current always flows through **exactly two channels**, regardless of D. Neglecting the dead-time interval (handled in §2):

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_0\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

### 1.2 Evaluation at 80 A

At T_j = 115 °C: R_DS = 1.3 mΩ × 1.405 = **1.83 mΩ**

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.83\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{23.4\ W\ per\ motor}$$

### 1.3 Copper and interconnect losses

These are often larger than designers expect.

- **Phase-pad and bus copper:** With 4 layers paralleled at 2 oz, roughly 8 mm wide and 10 mm long, sheet resistance is about 0.25 mΩ/□ per layer at 25 °C. That gives R ≈ 0.25/4 × (10/8) ≈ 0.08 mΩ per path; use about 0.2 mΩ for the total loop including vias.
- **Shunt:** 0.5 mΩ (if per-ESC current sensing is used).
- **Battery lead pad and solder joint:** about 0.1 mΩ.

$$P_{Cu,ch} \approx 80^2 \times 0.2\text{m} = 1.3\ W$$

The shared battery-input path carries the sum of all four motor DC currents, not the phase current. At full throttle, D → ~0.9 and I_bus ≈ D·I_ph ≈ 72 A per motor, so the total is about 288 A:
- P_shunt (shared) = 288² × 0.5 mΩ = **41 W**. This is unacceptable, so shared bus shunts must be ≤ 0.1 mΩ, or you should sense current per ESC and sum in firmware.
- P_entry (0.1 mΩ) = **8.3 W**
