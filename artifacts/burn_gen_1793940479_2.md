# STM32H743 AIO 4-in-1 ESC Nano FC: Electro-Thermal and Firmware Interface Model

## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility Warnings

I don't have the Job #61 requirements document, so every assumption below is stated explicitly. Swap in your actual BOM values. All equations are parametric, so the numbers update directly.

### 0.1 Architectural correction

The STM32H743 should not commutate the motors. ArduPilot has no field-oriented control or 6-step commutation driver for direct FET control. The standard and supportable architecture is:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot FC) ──4× bidir DShot600──► 4× AT32F421 / STM32G071 (AM32 or BLHeli_32)
                                                   └─► 4× 3-phase gate driver ─► 24 MOSFETs
```

The model below assumes this split.

### 0.2 Feasibility warning on "80 A burst" in a nano (20×20 mm) format

The calculations in §1–3 show roughly **94 W of loss dissipated in about 3 g of board**. That is about 31 K/s of adiabatic temperature rise. In this format, 80 A is a **≤ 2–3 s per-motor burst rating** at best. Continuous capability is roughly 35–45 A per motor with prop-wash airflow.

Recommendations:
- Specify the rating as "80 A / 2 s burst, 40 A continuous."
- Or move to a 25.5×25.5 / 30.5×30.5 mm footprint with 2 FETs in parallel per switch.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation topology

In 6-step operation, two phases conduct in each 60° sector. One leg is PWM-modulated at duty D. With complementary PWM, current always flows through exactly **two FET channels** in series: either (HS_a + LS_b) during on-time, or (LS_a + LS_b) during freewheel.

The total channel conduction loss per motor is therefore approximately independent of D:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds(on),25}\, k_T
$$

$$
= 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.0\times10^{-3} \cdot 1.5 = \mathbf{19.2\ W}
$$

### 1.2 Per-FET distribution

Each switch conducts during a 120° window (one third of the electrical period). Worst case is D → 1, where the high-side carries the full current for its whole window:

$$
P_{cond,FET,max} = \frac{I_{ph}^2 R_{ds,hot}}{3} = \frac{6400 \cdot 1.5\times10^{-3}}{3} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}
$$

At D ≈ 0.5, this splits between the HS and LS of the PWM leg (about 1.6 W each). The non-PWM leg's LS still sees 3.2 W.

### 1.3 Copper loss

$$
P_{Cu,motor} = I_{ph}^2 R_{Cu,path} = 6400 \cdot 0.3\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{1.92\ W}
$$

### 1.4 Thermal–electrical coupling

R_ds(on) rises with temperature, so the operating point must be solved self-consistently:

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\,/\text{K}
$$

$$
T_j = T_a + P(T_j)\, Z_{th}(t)
$$

Thermal runaway occurs if $\partial P / \partial T_j \cdot Z_{th} \geq 1$, which corresponds to:

$$
I^2 R_{ds,25}\, \alpha\, Z_{th} / 3 \geq 1
$$

With Z_th = 20 K/W (steady state, nano board, no airflow), the limit is:

$$
I_{crit} = \sqrt{\frac{3}{R_{ds,25}\,\alpha\,Z_{th}}} \approx 158\ \text{A}
$$

Runaway is therefore not the binding limit. Total thermal mass is.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-modulated leg switches. That is one HS/LS pair per motor at any instant.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,eff} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 1.5\text{n} \cdot 635 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.023\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD} I_{ph} \cdot 2 t_{dead} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 100\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.307\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss sits mostly in the driver and R_g, not the FET:

$$
P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 50\text{n} \cdot 10 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.6 Switching total

$$
P_{sw,motor} \approx 1.94 + 0.05 + 0.02 + 0.31 + 0.05 = \mathbf{2.37\ W}
$$

**Sensitivity:**
- Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it falls to about 1.2 W.
- That saving is small compared with the 19 W conduction loss.
- 48 kHz is justified
