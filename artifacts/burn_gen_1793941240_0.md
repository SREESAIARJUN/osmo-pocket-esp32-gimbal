# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Thermal Model and Bi-Directional DShot Timer/DMA Allocation

## 0. Architecture Clarification

The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs. In any ArduPilot-compatible 4-in-1 design, the power stage is split into two domains.

| Domain | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100) running ArduPilot | Generates bi-directional DShot600 and decodes eRPM telemetry |
| ESC | 4× ESC MCUs (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32) or one MCU per motor | Six-step commutation at 48 kHz PWM, gate drive, and the eRPM response |

The 48 kHz switching loss therefore belongs to the ESC firmware's PWM setting. The DShot/DMA problem belongs to the H743.

**Assumptions** (substitute your actual BOM values):

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | V_bus | 6S, 25.2 V max |
| MOSFET | — | 40 V, R_ds(on) = 1.0 mΩ at 25 °C, Qg = 40 nC, Qrr = 30 nC, Coss = 1 nF |
| Gate drive | — | 10 V |
| Switching time | t_r + t_f | 20 ns total |
| Dead time | t_dt | 100 ns per edge |
| Body diode drop | V_f | 0.8 V |
| Burst current | I | 80 A phase current **per motor** |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| Board | — | 30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz copper |

**Design-review flag:** 80 A per motor on a "nano" (20×20 mm) footprint is not credible thermally. Even 30.5×30.5 mm with 3.3×3.3 mm FETs is marginal, and the numbers below show why. Treat 80 A as a time-limited burst and enforce the limit in firmware.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

**Commutation pattern.** In six-step commutation with synchronous rectification (AM32 complementary PWM), motor current always flows through exactly two MOSFETs:
- During the PWM on-time: the high side of the active phase plus the low side of the return phase.
- During the off-time: the complementary low side plus the return low side.

This gives:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 \cdot 2 \cdot R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

**Temperature dependence.**

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.004\text{–}0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j ≈ 110 °C, R ≈ 1.5 mΩ.

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W/motor}$$

**Thermal runaway.** Because R rises with T, the system has positive feedback. The loop is stable only if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\cdot R_{th} < 1 \;\Rightarrow\; I^2 \cdot 2 \cdot R_{25}\,\alpha \cdot R_{th,j\text{-}a} < 1$$

With R_th,j-a,eff ≈ 15 K/W, this evaluates to 6400 × 2 × 1e-3 × 0.005 × 15 ≈ 0.96. That is right at the runaway margin in steady state. This is the mathematical reason 80 A must be a burst rating only.

**Board copper loss.** For phase and bus pours (2 oz, about 0.25 mΩ effective path):

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.25\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 1.6\ \text{W/motor}$$

## 2. Switching Loss at 48 kHz

In six-step commutation, one leg is PWM-switched at a time. The loss terms are:

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw | ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 20 ns × 48 k = **0.97 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f · I · 2t_dt · f_sw | 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw | 30 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.036 W** |
| Coss | ½ · Coss · V² · f_sw | **0.015 W** |
| Gate drive (6 FETs, mostly dissipated in the driver) | Q_g · V_gs · f_sw · n | 40 nC × 10 × 48 k × 2 active = **0.04 W** |
| **Total** | | **≈ 1.67 W/motor** |

**Frequency sensitivity.** Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw:
- 24 kHz: ≈ 0.84 W
- 96 kHz: ≈ 3.3 W

At 80 A, conduction loss dominates (about 92%). The 48 kHz choice is justified
