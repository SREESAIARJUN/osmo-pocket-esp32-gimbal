## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Design Risks

### 0.1 System partitioning

The STM32H743 is the flight controller MCU. It does not commutate the motors. The 4-in-1 ESC carries four independent ESC MCUs that run BLHeli_32, AM32 or Bluejay-class firmware. This document assumes AM32 on AT32F421.

```
[STM32H743 FC] --(4x bidir DShot600, single-wire)--> [4x ESC MCU] --> [3-ph gate driver] --> [6x MOSFET] --> motor
             <--(ADC: VBAT, IBAT, telemetry UART)--
```

- The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs.
- The DShot DMA allocation lives on the H743.
- The two subsystems are modelled separately below.

### 0.2 Critical risk: 80A in a "nano" form factor

Nano boards are 20×20 mm, or 25.5×25.5 mm for "mini". The model in §3 shows about 95 W of total dissipation at 80 A on all four channels.

- **No 20×20 board can sustain this.**
- The 80A rating is defensible only as a **short burst (≤2–3 s)** on a board of at least 30.5×30.5 mm with heavy copper.
- I recommend renaming the rating "80A burst / 45A continuous per channel" and adopting the 30.5×30.5 mm footprint. The calculations below assume that footprint.

**Modulation model:**
- Six-step trapezoidal with complementary (synchronous) PWM.
- At any instant, exactly two phases conduct: one high-side (HS) and one low-side (LS) in different legs.
- At 100% throttle the duty cycle approaches 1, so phase current approaches DC current (80 A).
- At partial throttle, phase RMS current exceeds the DC battery current. The relation is I_phase ≈ I_batt / D (continuous conduction).

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Governing equations

Temperature-dependent on-resistance:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right]$$

At any instant the current path passes through one HS and one LS device. With synchronous rectification, the freewheel path is also a MOSFET channel rather than a diode, except during dead time. Instantaneous conduction loss per channel is therefore:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot \left(2R_{DS}(T_j) + R_{pcb}\right)$$

Each of the six devices carries current for about 1/3 of the electrical cycle, so per-device average loss is:

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{1}{3} I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 2.2 Numerical evaluation (80 A, T_j = 125 °C)

$$R_{DS}(125) = 1.0 \times (1 + 0.005 \times 100) = 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 80^2 \times (2 \times 1.5 + 0.3)\times10^{-3} = 6400 \times 3.3\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{21.1\ W}$$

$$P_{cond,FET} = \tfrac{1}{3}\times 6400 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{3.2\ W\ per\ device}$$

### 2.3 Electro-thermal coupling

R_DS depends on T_j, and T_j depends on loss, so the two must be solved together. Iterate the following until it converges:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_a + P_{tot}\!\left(T_j^{(n)}\right) \cdot R_{\theta,ja}$$

**Stability condition (no thermal runaway):**

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\, R_{\theta,ja} < 1 \;\Rightarrow\; I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha \cdot k \cdot R_{\theta,ja} < 1$$

## 3. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Under complementary PWM, the **active (hard-switching) device** sees V·I overlap on both edges. The synchronous partner turns on after the dead time at near-zero voltage (soft switching). Its loss shows up instead as body-diode conduction during dead time and as Q_rr.

### 3.1 Component terms (per channel)

**(a) Hard-switching V·I overlap:**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

**(c) Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \cdot 2 = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

$$P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$
