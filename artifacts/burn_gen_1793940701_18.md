### Architectural point

ArduPilot runs on the H743, but it does **not** commutate the motors.

- The H743 sends DShot600 frames to four ESC MCUs and receives eRPM telemetry back from them.
- Each ESC MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32) generates the 6-channel complementary PWM and handles BEMF sensing.
- The netlist below reflects this split.

### Headline result

80 A per motor on a 20×20 mm board is **thermally feasible only as a ~1 s burst**.

- At 80 A on all four motors, the stage dissipates about 115 W.
- The board's heat capacity is only about 2.6 J/K.
- Section 3 quantifies this. Either define "burst" as ≤1 s, or move to a 30×30 mm board with a heat spreader.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths

In 6-step drive, at any instant two phases carry current I and the third phase floats. With complementary PWM at duty D:

- **On-time (D):** current flows through the high-side FET (HS) of phase A, the motor, then the low-side FET (LS) of phase B.
- **Off-time (1 − D):** HS_A turns off and LS_A conducts synchronously, so current flows through LS_A, the motor, then LS_B.

There are therefore **always two channels conducting in series** in the current path:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.006\ /\text{K} \;\Rightarrow\; R_{125} = 1.2 \times 1.6 = 1.92\ \text{m}\Omega$$

Because R_DS(on) rises with temperature, conduction loss and junction temperature feed back on each other. Section 3.4 checks that this loop converges.

### 1.3 Per-device split

Each phase leg is active for 120° of every 360° (2/3 of the electrical period). It splits as follows:

| Device | Conducts for | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| HS (PWM switch) | D × 1/3 of the period | I²R · D/3 |
| LS of the same leg | (1 − D) × 1/3 | I²R · (1 − D)/3 |
| LS (static, return leg) | 1/3 | I²R · 1/3 |

The per-FET worst case is at D → 1:

$$P_{HS,max} = \tfrac{1}{3}I^2R = \tfrac{1}{3}(80^2)(1.92\times10^{-3}) = 4.10\ \text{W}$$

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| P_cond per motor channel | 2 × 6400 × 1.92 mΩ = **24.6 W** |
| P_cond, 4 channels | **98.3 W** |
| (Same at T_j = 25 °C, for reference) | 61.4 W |

### 1.5 PCB copper (parasitic) conduction

The return path through the bus planes is estimated at R_Cu ≈ 0.4 mΩ per channel:

- Path length ≈ 15 mm.
- Equivalent width ≈ 8 mm.
- 2 oz copper on two parallel layers.
- Copper temperature coefficient of +0.39 %/K included at 80 °C.

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.4\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 2.6\ \text{W per channel}$$

### 1.6 Input capacitor ripple current (often overlooked)

$$I_{C,rms} = I\sqrt{D(1-D)} \;\xrightarrow{D=0.5}\; 40\ \text{A}_{rms}\ \text{per channel}$$

- Each channel needs ≥4 × 10 µF X7R 50 V 1210 ceramics (about 3 A rms each, sharing the high-frequency part).
- A shared low-ESR bulk electrolytic (≥470 µF, 35 V) is still required off-board or at the pads.
- Without this, the ceramics self-heat and V_bus ringing exceeds the 40 V FET rating.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing high-side FET hard-switches. The synchronous LS FET switches at near-zero V_DS (the body diode clamps it), but its body diode both conducts during dead time and recovers.

### 2.1 Hard-switching overlap loss (V–I crossover)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}
= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = 1.94\ \text{W}$$

A more exact version separates the current-rise and voltage-fall intervals, which depend on the Miller plateau and gate current:

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g} = \frac{Q_{gd}\,R_g}{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance loss

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,Q_{oss}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 60\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{ k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Body-diode reverse recovery

The recovery energy is dumped into the HS FET at turn-on:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 40\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{ k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time windows per PWM period:

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ k} = 1.23\ \text{W}$$

**Lever:** reducing dead time to 100 ns saves 0.6 W per channel. Verify this against the driver's propagation skew.
