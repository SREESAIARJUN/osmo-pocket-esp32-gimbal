## 0. Architecture Corrections and Scope

These points change the model and need to be settled first.

1. **The H743 does not switch the MOSFETs.** In a 4-in-1 stack, each ESC channel has its own MCU (e.g. AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32) that generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bi-directional DShot600 to those four MCUs. The loss model applies to the ESC board. The DMA model applies to the FC.
2. **"Nano" (20×20 mm) and 80 A burst conflict.** Section 3 shows that a 4×80 A burst dissipates about 100 W, which a 20×20 board cannot absorb for more than about 1–2 s. You should either:
   - move to a 30.5×30.5 mm footprint (and probably split the FC and ESC into a stack), or
   - re-rate the board to roughly 45–55 A burst.
3. **Assumed operating point:**
   - 6S pack, 25.2 V maximum
   - 80 A phase current per channel, 10 s burst
   - 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification and complementary PWM
   - Ambient 40 °C

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### Per-FET duty in 6-step

During each electrical cycle, each switch position carries the phase current for 120°, i.e. 1/3 of the period. With synchronous rectification, the conducting interval is shared between the PWM-ed FET and its complementary FET in proportion to D and (1 − D). For each switch position the time-average reduces to:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{1}{3}\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS}(T_j)=R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

The ripple correction is (1 + r²/12), where r is the ripple fraction. It is below 1 % at 48 kHz with typical motor inductance, so it is neglected here.
