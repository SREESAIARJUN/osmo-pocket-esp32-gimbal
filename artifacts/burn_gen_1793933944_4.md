# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC (Job #66): Reference Design Model

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Corrections

Before the math, three points about the request need clarifying, because they change the design.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In any ArduPilot stack, the STM32H743 is the flight controller. It sends bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs, typically running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware on an STM32G071 or AT32F421. ArduPilot has no firmware path for an H743 driving 12 MOSFETs directly. The architecture below therefore uses an **H743 FC section plus 4 independent ESC MCUs** on one stack or board.

2. **"80A burst" on a nano board is thermally a transient problem, not a steady-state one.** Section 3 shows that four channels at 80A dissipate about 100W. A 20–30mm board cannot reject that continuously. The model therefore derives a **burst-duration limit** rather than a continuous rating.

3. **The netlists are reference-level, not fab-ready.** Exact part numbers, footprints and pin maps must be checked against your chosen MOSFET, driver and package datasheets, and against ArduPilot's `dma_resolver.py` output. A complete production netlist can only be generated from your EDA tool. What I provide is the canonical connectivity in netlist form.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-Dependent On-Resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 100°C:

$$R_{ds} = 1.2 \times (1 + 0.0065 \times 75) = 1.785\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Six-Step Commutation with Synchronous Rectification

At any instant, the motor current flows through exactly **two conducting channels** in one leg pair:

- During the PWM on-time: high-side of phase A, then low-side of phase B.
- During the PWM off-time: low-side A in synchronous rectification, then low-side B.

Synchronous rectification keeps a channel conducting through both intervals. The instantaneous conduction loss per motor is therefore independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j) = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.785\times10^{-3} = 22.85\ \text{W}$$

Per FET, averaged over an electrical cycle across 6 switches:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} = \frac{P_{cond,motor}}{6} \approx 3.81\ \text{W}$$

The loss is not shared evenly within a commutation window. A high-side FET carrying the conduction interval at D → 1 takes (1/3)·I²R ≈ **7.6 W** averaged over its window. Size the thermal path for that worst-case FET, not the average.

### 1.3 Shunt Loss

The DC-link current is approximately D·I_ph. At D = 1:

$$P_{sh} = I^2 R_{sh} = 80^2 \times 0.25\times10^{-3} = 1.6\ \text{W per channel}$$

### 1.4 Electrothermal Feedback

Because R_ds depends on T_j, the junction temperature satisfies a self-consistent equation:

$$T_j = T_c + R_{\theta JC}\cdot I^2 R_{ds,25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]\cdot k$$

Here k is the conduction fraction for the FET. Solving for T_j:

$$T_j = \frac{T_c + R_{\theta JC} k I^2 R_{ds,25}(1 - 25\alpha)}{1 - R_{\theta JC} k I^2 R_{ds,25}\,\alpha}$$

**Thermal-runaway condition:** the denominator must stay positive.

$$R_{\theta JC}\, k\, I^2 R_{ds,25}\,\alpha < 1$$

For the worst FET (k = 1/3): 1.5 × 0.333 × 6400 × 1.2e-3 × 0.0065 = 0.025. This is far below 1, so the junction-to-case path is stable. In practice, runaway is governed by the **board-to-ambient** path (Section 4).
