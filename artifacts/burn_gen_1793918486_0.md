## 0. Scope, Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and does not drive the MOSFETs directly. The power stage is controlled by four independent ESC MCUs that run motor commutation. Bidirectional DShot600 travels between the H743 and those ESC MCUs. This split governs every partition decision below.

```
LiPo 6S ──► 4-in-1 power stage (4× ESC MCU + 3-phase gate driver + 6 MOSFETs each)
                 ▲  DShot600 bidir (1 wire/motor, open-drain-ish, pulled up)
                 │
STM32H743VIT6 (ArduPilot) ── IMU(SPI) ── Baro(I²C) ── OSD/UARTs ── SD (SDMMC)
```

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step / trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification

In each 60° commutation sector, one phase is driven high, one is held low and one floats. With complementary (synchronous) PWM on the driven leg:

- **During the on-time (D·T):** current flows through the high-side switch of leg A, the motor, then the low-side switch of leg B.
- **During the off-time ((1−D)·T):** current freewheels through the low-side switch of leg A, the motor, then the low-side switch of leg B.

Exactly **two MOSFET channels** carry the phase current at every instant. This holds for any duty cycle, ignoring dead-time intervals, which are handled in §2.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]
$$

### 1.3 Per-ESC conduction loss

The phase current ripple ΔI is small compared with 80 A at 48 kHz for typical motor inductance (tens of µH). It is included for completeness:

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I_{pk}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}
$$

$$
P_{cond,ESC} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

### 1.4 Distribution across individual switches

Each leg is "driven" for 120° out of 360° and "held low" for 120°. Averaged over an electrical revolution:

$$
P_{HS,leg} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,D\,I^2 R, \qquad
P_{LS,leg} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,(1-D)\,I^2R + \tfrac{1}{3}\,I^2R
$$

The low-side devices run hotter at low duty. This is a known 4-in-1 hotspot, so place low-side FETs on the best copper.

### 1.5 Electrothermal fixed point

R_DS depends on T_j, and T_j depends on loss. Solve by iteration:

$$
T_j^{(k+1)} = T_{ref} + P_{FET}\big(R_{DS}(T_j^{(k)})\big)\cdot R_{\theta,J\text{-}ref}
$$

The iteration converges if

$$
\alpha \cdot P_{FET} \cdot R_{\theta} < 1
$$

With P_FET ≈ 5 W and R_θ ≈ 6.5 K/W: 0.0055 × 5 × 6.5 = 0.18, so it converges. If this product approaches 1, thermal runaway is possible, which is the burst-rating hazard.

### 1.6 Worked result at 80 A, T_j ≈ 100 °C

- R_DS = 1.8 mΩ × 1.41 = **2.54 mΩ**
- P_cond,ESC = 2 × 6400 × 0.00254 = **32.5 W per ESC**
- Average per FET ≈ 32.5 / 6 ≈ **5.4 W**
- Worst-case low-side FET at D = 0.3 during the 240° it conducts: about **7.5 W**

**Copper and interconnect losses must be added.** A 2 oz, 4 mm wide, 15 mm long trace is about 1.8 mΩ at 25 °C, so about 11.5 W at 80 A.
- Busbars or thick-copper inner layers are therefore mandatory.
- The shunt contributes I_bat² × R_sh: with 0.25 mΩ and I_bat = D × 80 A, this is up to 1.6 W.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

The hard-switched leg is the PWM'd leg. Only one switching transition pair occurs per PWM period per ESC: the high-side turns on, the low-side turns off, and vice versa.

### 2.1 Voltage/current overlap (hard switching, inductive clamp)

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_{ri}+t_{fv})_{on} f_{sw} + \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_{rv}+t_{fi})_{off} f_{sw}
$$

Using an effective combined crossover of 35 ns:

$$
P_{VI} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 35\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.69\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery (low-side diode recovered by high-side turn-on)

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 50\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}
$$
