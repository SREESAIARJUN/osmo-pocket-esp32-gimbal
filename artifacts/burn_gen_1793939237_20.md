## 0. Scope and Architectural Corrections

These points shape the rest of the analysis.

1. **The H743 does not switch the MOSFETs.** In every production 4-in-1 stack, each ESC channel (or a shared multi-channel MCU) runs its own firmware, such as AM32 on an AT32F421 or STM32G071, or BLHeli_32. That firmware generates the 48 kHz commutation PWM. The H743 only produces bidirectional DShot600 and decodes the eRPM telemetry. This document models the ESC power stage and the FC timer/DMA side separately.
2. **80 A burst per channel is not achievable on a 20×20 "nano" footprint.** The copper cross-section, FET package area, and thermal mass are insufficient. The numbers below assume a **30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz board**. If 20×20 is a hard constraint, de-rate to about 35–45 A burst.
3. **All component values are representative of the device class.** Final values must come from the selected parts' datasheets (R_DS(on) vs. T_j curves, Q_gd, Q_rr, Z_th(j-c) curves) and be validated on the bench with thermocouples or IR.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Path

In 6-step commutation with complementary PWM, current always flows through exactly **two conducting channels**:

- The PWM leg, which alternates between the high side (duty D) and the synchronous low side (1−D).
- The low side of the return leg, which is on continuously for that 60° sector.

The losses therefore sum independent of duty:

$$P_{cond,FET} = I_{rms}^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{rms}^2 R_{DS}(T_j) = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 RMS Current with Ripple

The phase-current ripple at 48 kHz, for motor phase-phase inductance L_pp:

$$\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{pp}\,f_{sw}}$$

The ripple is worst at D = 0.5. For a typical 5" motor with L_pp ≈ 15 µH:

$$\Delta I = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{15\times10^{-6}\times 48\times10^{3}} = 8.75\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{rms} = \sqrt{I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}} = \sqrt{6400 + 6.4} \approx 80.04\ \text{A}$$

Ripple is negligible at 80 A, so I_rms ≈ 80 A.
