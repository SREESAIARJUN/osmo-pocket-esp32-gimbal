**Upfront findings. Read these before layout.**

1. **80 A is not a continuous rating on this form factor.** The model below gives about **24 A per channel continuous** with prop-wash cooling. It gives about **2–3 s at 80 A on all four channels** before the board hits its thermal limit.
2. **Electrothermal runaway exists at 80 A in steady state.** The loop gain is greater than 1 (§3.4). The ESC firmware *must* enforce a time-limited current or temperature derating.
3. **A 40 V MOSFET on 6S has thin avalanche margin.** Turn-off overshoot with about 2 nH of loop inductance reaches about 34 V (§2.6). This demands a disciplined commutation-loop layout. Otherwise, use 60 V parts at a modest R_DS(on) penalty.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous PWM

At any instant, two phase legs carry the phase current I. One leg is the PWM leg. The other is the "static" leg, held low-side-on (or high-side-on, depending on the sector).

- In the PWM leg, current alternates between the high-side (HS) FET for time D·T and the low-side (LS) FET for time (1−D)·T, through synchronous rectification.
- The static leg conducts continuously.

So exactly **two channels are always in series with the motor current**, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Each leg conducts for 2/3 of the electrical period. Per FET, averaged over the electrical cycle, with even HS/LS distribution:

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6}$$

The actual split is skewed by D. At high throttle, the HS FETs take more.
