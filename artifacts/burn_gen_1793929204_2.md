## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step complementary PWM

In any 60° commutation sector, phase X is PWM'd high, phase Y is held low, and phase Z floats.

- **On-time (D):** current flows through HS_X and LS_Y.
- **Off-time (1−D):** current freewheels through LS_X (synchronous rectification) and LS_Y.

Exactly two FETs are always in the current path. The conduction loss per motor channel is therefore independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

with the temperature dependence

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_0\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right]$$

### 1.2 Distribution across the six FETs

Over one electrical revolution, each phase leg spends 120° active and 60° floating. In high-throttle operation (D → 1), the high-side FETs carry most of the current.

| Device | Conduction fraction | Average power share |
|---|---|---|
| Each HS FET | 1/3 of period × D | $\frac{D}{3} I^2 R$ |
| Each LS FET | 1/3 (held low) + 1/3·(1−D) (freewheel) | $\frac{2-D}{3} I^2 R$ |

Check: summing over three HS and three LS FETs gives $3\cdot\frac{D}{3} + 3\cdot\frac{2-D}{3} = 2$. This matches the two-device total ✓.

At burst (D ≈ 0.9), each LS FET dissipates about 0.37·I²R and each HS FET about 0.30·I²R. The **low-side FETs are the hotspot**, which is relevant for copper pour and via placement.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A burst

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond per channel | P_cond, worst LS FET |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W | 2.37 W |
| 100 °C | 1.34 mΩ | 17.1 W | 3.17 W |
| 125 °C | 1.45 mΩ | 18.6 W | 3.43 W |

At 45 A continuous and 100 °C: P_cond,ch = 45² × 2 × 1.34 mΩ = **5.4 W per channel**.

### 1.4 Electro-thermal fixed point and runaway criterion

Conduction loss depends on T_j, and T_j depends on loss. Define:

- $P_{0} = P_{cond}(25\,°C)$
- $P_x$ = loss that is temperature-independent (switching, roughly)
- $R_\theta$ = effective junction-to-ambient thermal resistance

Then

$$T_j = T_a + R_\theta\left[P_0\left(1+\alpha(T_j-25)\right) + P_x\right]$$

Solving for T_j:

$$\boxed{T_j = \frac{T_a + R_\theta\left[P_0(1-25\alpha) + P_x\right]}{1 - \alpha P_0 R_\theta}}$$

**Thermal runaway condition:** $\alpha P_0 R_\theta \ge 1$.

For the worst LS FET at 80 A, P₀ = 2.37 W, so runaway would occur if $R_\theta \ge 1/(0.0045 \times 2.37) = 94$ K/W. Steady-state runaway is not the risk. The real limit is **total board heat**, covered in §3.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd high-side FET hard-switches in each sector. The complementary LS device switches at near-zero voltage after the dead time, but it is responsible for reverse recovery and dead-time diode losses.

### 2.1 Voltage-current overlap loss

Transition time:

$$t_{r} \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_g} = \frac{12\text{ nC}}{0.6\text{ A}} = 20\text{ ns}$$

Overlap loss:

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Body-diode reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

Qrr also increases the turn-on current spike in the HS FET. Add about 10–20 % to P_VI if Qrr exceeds about 80 nC.
