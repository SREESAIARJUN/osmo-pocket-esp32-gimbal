## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Initial Flags

The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs. It runs ArduPilot and sends DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs, one per motor, running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by those ESC MCUs, not by the H743.

| Parameter | Value (assumed; verify against your BOM) |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal |
| MOSFETs | 40 V N-ch, PDFN 3.3×3.3, R_DS(on) = 1.2 mΩ @ 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V |
| R_DS(on) temperature factor | ×1.5 at T_j ≈ 125 °C, giving 1.8 mΩ |
| Switching transition times | t_r + t_f ≈ 40 ns (driver-limited, R_g ≈ 2–3 Ω) |
| Gate charge, Q_g | 40 nC @ 10 V |
| Reverse-recovery charge, Q_rr | 40 nC |
| C_oss | ≈ 1 nF |
| Body-diode forward voltage, V_f | 0.8 V |
| Dead time | 100 ns per edge |
| Board | 30.5×30.5 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz copper |
| ESC MCUs | 4× STM32G071 / AT32F421 (AM32) |
| Gate driver | FD6288Q (3-phase, 1 per motor) |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal with complementary (synchronous) PWM |

> **Design flag:** 80 A per motor on a 30.5 mm board is achievable only as a **burst of about 2–3 s**. Section 3 quantifies this. Size the firmware current limit and the thermal-throttle policy around that result, not around marketing ratings.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, phase current always flows through exactly two leg positions in series: one switch on the "+" phase and one on the "−" phase. Each position is the high-side (HS) FET for a fraction D of the time and the low-side (LS) FET for the remaining (1 − D).

For each motor channel:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{DS(on)}(T_j)\cdot\big[D + (1-D)\big]\cdot 2 = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

The total does not depend on D. Only the split between HS and LS does:

$$
P_{HS} = 2D\,I^2R, \qquad P_{LS} = 2(1-D)\,I^2R
$$

Ripple adds a small correction:

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}
$$

Example: with L_ph = 15 µH, D = 0.5 and f_sw = 48 kHz, ΔI ≈ 8.75 A. That adds only about 0.1 % to I_rms² at 80 A, so it is negligible.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence (positive feedback)

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\ /\mathrm{K}
$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A burst

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond per channel |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.2 mΩ | 2 × 6400 × 0.0012 = **15.4 W** |
| 125 °C | 1.8 mΩ | 2 × 6400 × 0.0018 = **23.0 W** |

Copper trace and via resistance must be added. Assume 0.3 mΩ per phase path, with the current crossing two paths:

$$
P_{Cu} = 2\cdot I^2 \cdot R_{trace} = 2 \cdot 6400 \cdot 0.0003 = 3.8\ \mathrm{W\ per\ channel}
$$

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switching leg commutates hard. The other conducting leg is static for the duration of the 60° sector.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I_{ph}\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw}
= 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\,\mathrm{ns}\cdot 48\,\mathrm{kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Body-diode reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = V_{bus}\,Q_{rr}\,f_{sw} = 25.2\cdot 40\,\mathrm{nC}\cdot 48\,\mathrm{kHz} = 0.048\ \mathrm{W}
$$

At 80 A, Q_rr scales up, so roughly double this: **≈ 0.1 W**.

### 2.3 Dead-time diode conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_f\,I_{ph}\cdot 2t_{dt}\cdot f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot 80\cdot 200\,\mathrm{ns}\cdot 48\,\mathrm{kHz} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

### 2.4 Output-capacitance (C_oss) loss

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot 1\,\mathrm{nF}\cdot 635\cdot 48\,\mathrm{kHz} = 0.015\ \mathrm{W}
$$

### 2.5 Gate-drive loss

Most of this energy is dissipated in the driver and the gate resistors, not in the FET.

$$
P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw}\times 2 = 40\,\mathrm{nC}\cdot 10\cdot 48\,\mathrm{kHz}\cdot 2 = 0.038\ \mathrm{W}
$$

### 2.6 Per-channel and total budget (80 A, T_j = 125 °C)

| Loss term | W / channel | W total (×4) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 23.04 | 92.2 |
| PCB copper | 3.84 | 15.4 |
| Switching overlap | 1.94 | 7.8 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 | 2.4 |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.15 | 0.6 |
| **Total** | **29.6 W** | **≈ 118 W** |

Equivalent efficiency at 80 A, D ≈ 0.9: P_out ≈ 4 × 22.2 × 0.9 × 80 ≈ 6.4 kW, so η ≈ 98.2 %. The efficiency looks good. The problem is **power density**: 118 W concentrated on a 9.3 cm² board.

### 2.7 f_sw trade-off

Total loss as a function of switching frequency:

$$
P_{total}(f) = 2I^2R + I^2R_{Cu} + f\cdot\left[\tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) + 2V_f I t_{dt} + V Q_{rr} + \tfrac12 C_{oss}V^2 + 2Q_gV_{GS}\right]
$$

The frequency-dependent coefficient is about **57 µJ per cycle at 80 A**.
