# STM32H743 FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model and Reference Design

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Architecture

### Architecture

The STM32H743 is the flight controller. It does not commutate motors. Each of the four ESC channels has its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 talks to them with bidirectional DShot600, and the ESC MCUs generate the 48 kHz PWM. The math therefore splits cleanly:

| Domain | Owner | Key model |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction and switching losses, thermal | ESC power stage | §1–§3 |
| DShot600 bidirectional timing, DMA | H743 under ArduPilot | §4 |
| Interconnect, power, sensing | Both | §5 netlists |

### Physical feasibility

80 A burst per motor on a 20×20 "nano" footprint is not thermally or electrically credible:
- The copper cross-section cannot carry 4×80 A.
- The FETs that fit cannot carry it either.

This design therefore uses:
- **30.5×30.5 mm** for the ESC.
- **20×20 or 30×30** for the H743 FC, as a stack.

The thermal model in §3 shows that even 30×30 is limited to a **few-second burst** at 80 A.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation topology

In six-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous (complementary) PWM, motor current always flows through exactly two FET channels in series, plus copper:

- **PWM-on interval (D):** high-side of phase A, then motor, then low-side of phase B (static for 60° electrical).
- **PWM-off interval (1−D):** low-side of phase A (synchronous freewheel), then motor, then low-side of phase B.

Either way, two R_DS(on) values are in series with the phase current I_m. The exception is dead time, covered in §2.4. So:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_m^2\,\big[\,2\,R_{DS}(T_j) + R_{cu}\big]$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_DS = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0045·85) = **1.38 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Burst conduction loss

$$P_{cond,ch} = 80^2\,(2\cdot1.38 + 0.30)\times10^{-3} = 6400 \times 3.06\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.6\ W}$$
