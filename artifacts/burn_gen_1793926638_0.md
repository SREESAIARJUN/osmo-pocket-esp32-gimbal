# STM32H743 AIO + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Model, DShot Timing, and Reference Netlist

## 0. Architecture Correction and Baseline Assumptions

**Architectural point first.** The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. In any real 4-in-1 design, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU (AT32F421, STM32G071 or GD32 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM and handles BEMF sensing. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to those MCUs over bi-directional DShot600.

The model is therefore split into three parts:

| Domain | Owner | Topics covered |
|---|---|---|
| Power stage | ESC MCU ×4 + gate drivers + 24 MOSFETs | Conduction loss, switching loss, thermal |
| Command/telemetry link | H743 timers + DMA ↔ ESC MCU | DShot600 timing, bdshot capture, DMA map |
| System | H743 | Notch filter / RPM telemetry integration |

**Baseline assumptions.** No part numbers were specified, so the values below are representative. Every equation is parametric, so you can substitute your datasheet values.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S full) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Burst phase current per channel | I | 80 A |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step, complementary (synchronous) PWM

During any commutation sector, one phase is PWM-switched and one phase has its low side held on. Let D be the duty cycle.

- **PWM phase, high side:** conducts I for D·T.
- **PWM phase, low side (synchronous rectification):** conducts I for (1−D)·T, minus dead time.
- **Return phase, low side:** conducts I for the full period T.

### 1.2 Per-channel conduction loss

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{hot}\left[D + (1-D) + 1\right] \approx 2\,I^2 R_{hot}
$$

R_DS(on) depends on junction temperature:

$$
R_{hot} = R_0\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right]
$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$
R_{hot} = 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega \times 1.4 \approx 2.1\ \text{m}\Omega\ \text{(use 2.25 m}\Omega\text{ for margin)}
$$

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.25\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{28.8\ W}
$$

**Hottest single device.** The return-phase low side conducts 100% of the period within its sector:

$$
P_{FET,max} = 80^2 \times 2.25\ \text{m}\Omega = 14.4\ \text{W (instantaneous)}
$$

Averaged over a full electrical revolution, each FET conducts for about 1/3 to 2/3 of the time. That averaging is why the transient thermal behavior in Section 3 matters more than peak power alone.

### 1.3 PCB copper loss (frequently ignored, not negligible)

Copper resistance per channel is:

$$
R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho\,L}{w\,t}, \qquad \rho = 1.72\times10^{-8}\ \Omega\cdot\text{m}
$$

Example path: 2 oz copper (70 µm), paralleled on 2 layers, 6 mm wide, 15 mm effective length.

$$
R_{Cu} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\times0.015}{0.006\times140\times10^{-6}} \approx 0.31\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

At 100 °C, multiply by 1.3, giving about 0.4 mΩ. The bus and phase paths are both in series with the current:

$$
P_{Cu} \approx 80^2 \times 0.4\ \text{m}\Omega \times 2 \approx \mathbf{5.1\ W\ per\ channel}
$$

### 1.4 Battery current versus phase current

$$
I_{batt} \approx D\cdot I_{phase} + \frac{P_{loss}}{V_{bus}}
$$

An "80 A burst" rating is only physically meaningful as phase current near D → 1. At part throttle, phase current exceeds battery current. Your current-limit firmware must therefore clamp phase current, not battery current.
