# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal, and DShot Architecture (Job #39)

This is an engineering baseline, not a release package. Component values are representative assumptions, called out explicitly so you can swap in datasheet numbers. Netlists are reference-level and need DRC/ERC, SI review, and bench validation before fab.

---

## 0. Architecture Feasibility Notes

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** The flight controller (H743, ArduPilot) sends bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs. In a 4-in-1 these are usually AT32F421, STM32G071 or EFM8, running AM32, BLHeli_32 or Bluejay. The 48 kHz PWM and FET gate timing live on those MCUs, not on the H743.
2. **80 A burst on a nano form factor (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) is thermally marginal to infeasible beyond a few seconds.** Section 3 shows roughly 80–90 W of board dissipation at a full 4×80 A burst. Treat "80 A" as a sub-second-to-few-second transient rating with a firmware-enforced current/temperature limit, not a continuous rating.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per motor, 6-step trapezoidal, synchronous rectification)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

In 6-step commutation, two legs carry current at any instant. One leg is PWM-modulated: its high-side and low-side alternate, but one of them is always conducting because of synchronous rectification. The other leg's low-side is held on. The current therefore always flows through exactly two R_DS(on):

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I^2\, R_{DS}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

Evaluated at T_j = 110 °C: R = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0045 × 85) = **1.38 mΩ**

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.38\text{e-}3 = \mathbf{17.7\ W}$$

Averaged over the 6 FETs of the bridge, that is about **2.95 W per FET**. The instantaneous distribution is uneven: a static low-side carries the full current for a 120° interval.

**Copper/PCB path.** Add phase trace, bus plane and solder joint resistance R_cu, typically 0.3–0.6 mΩ on a nano board:

$$P_{cu} = 2 I^2 R_{cu} \approx 2 \times 6400 \times 0.4\text{e-}3 = 5.1\ W$$

This term is often ignored and is not negligible.

### 2.2 Switching losses at 48 kHz

| Mechanism | Formula | Result |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 20 ns × 48 kHz | **0.968 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw = 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 kHz | 0.048 W |
| Output capacitance | ½ · Q_oss · V · f_sw | 0.018 W |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_f · I · 2t_d · f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 kHz | **0.614 W** |
| Gate drive (mostly in the driver) | Q_g · V_gs · f_sw × 2 FETs switching | 0.048 W |

$$P_{sw,motor} \approx \mathbf{1.70\ W}$$

Design levers:
- Overlap loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it is about 0.85 W, but you get more audible noise and ripple.
- Dead-time loss is the second-largest term. Trimming t_d from 100 to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per motor, with shoot-through risk. Validate on a double-pulse test.

### 2.3 Totals at burst

| Item | Per motor | 4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 17.7 W | 70.8 W |
| Copper | 5.1 W | 20.5 W |
| Switching | 1.7 W | 6.8 W |
| **Total** | **24.5 W** | **≈ 98 W** |

Efficiency at the burst point: output is roughly 25.2 V × 80 A × duty. At high throttle (~2 kW per motor), losses are around 1.2%. Electrically efficient, but the absolute heat in roughly 6 cm² of board is the problem.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Steady-state (why continuous 80 A is impossible)

Board-to-ambient resistance for a 25×25 mm, 8-layer board in prop wash:
- θ_BA ≈ 8–15 K/W in airflow
- θ_BA ≈ 25+ K/W in still air

Even at an optimistic 8 K/W:

$$\Delta T = 98 \times 8 = 784\ \text{K} \quad \Rightarrow \text{not physical}$$

**Sustainable continuous loss** for a ΔT of 70 K is 70/8 ≈ 8.75 W. Solving 4 × [2I²(R + R_cu)] + switching ≈ 8.75 W gives **I_cont ≈ 22–25 A per motor**. That is consistent with real 20×20 / 25.5 boards rated 25–45 A with airflow.

### 3.2 Transient (burst) capability: lumped RC model

Heat capacity:
- FR4 + copper + FETs + caps ≈ 7 g
- c_eff ≈ 0.7 J/(g·K)
- C_th ≈ 5 J/K

$$T(t) = T_a + P\,\theta\,(1 - e^{-t/\tau}), \qquad \tau = \theta C_{th} \approx 40\ \text{s}$$

For t ≪ τ, the board heats near-adiabatically: dT/dt ≈ P/C ≈ 98/5 ≈ 19.6 K/s.

| Start temperature | Limit | Board-level burst time |
|---|---|---|
| 45 °C | 125 °C (cap/solder/FET derating) | **≈ 4 s** |

### 3.3 Junction rise over board (per FET)

Use the datasheet transient impedance curve Z_th,jc(t). For a 5×6 package, roughly 0.5 K/W steady and about 0.25 K/W at 10 ms.

Worst-case static low-side FET during its 120° interval: I²R = 6400 × 1.38 mΩ ≈ 8.8 W.

$$\Delta T_{j-c} \approx 8.8 \times 0.25 \approx 2.2\ \text{K}$$

The junction-to-case rise is small. **The board heat sink is the binding constraint, not the die.**

### 3.4 Thermal-runaway stability check

With dP/dT = 2I²R₂₅α and θ_BA = 8 K/W, the loop gain must stay below 1:

$$G = \frac{dP}{dT}\,\theta_{BA} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1\text{e-}3 \times 0.0045 \times 8 \times 4\ \text{motors} = 1.84 \;>\; 1 \;\Rightarrow\; \textbf{unstable}$$

At full 4×80 A the system is unconditionally runaway-prone. **A firmware current limit plus NTC-based derating is mandatory, not optional.**

### 3.5 Recommended protection envelope

- Per-ESC current limit: 80 A for ≤ 1 s, then a ramp to 35 A (AM32 current limit plus burst logic).
- NTC thermistor under the FET cluster, read by the ESC MCU. Derate linearly from 100 °C, cut at 120 °C.
- In ArduPilot, use the ESC telemetry temperature, `BATT_*` current limits, and `MOT_BAT_CURR_MAX`/`MOT_BAT_CURR_TC` to enforce a pack-level limit.
