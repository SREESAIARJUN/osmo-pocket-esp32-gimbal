| Parameter | Assumed value |

| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal, complementary (synchronous) PWM, 48 kHz, dead time 100 ns |

**The H743 cannot drive the bridges itself.** Twelve half-bridges with BEMF/zero-crossing control and four motor control loops are not a sensible job for the flight-control MCU. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs. This split is the architecture used by every production AIO board.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path
In 6-step synchronous commutation, two phases conduct at any instant. During PWM-on, current flows through high-side Q_H(x) and low-side Q_L(y). During PWM-off it freewheels through Q_L(x), which is synchronously rectified, and Q_L(y). In both cases two MOSFET channels are in series with the motor current, so:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\text{–}0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

R_DS(on)(110 °C) = 1.2 × (1 + 0.0047 × 85) ≈ **1.68 mΩ**

### 1.3 Gate-drive derating
The FD6288Q is fed from roughly 10–12 V via an LDO. If V_GS sags to about 7 V during bootstrap droop at high duty, R_DS(on) rises 10–20 %. Use the curve for your actual V_GS, not the 10 V headline value.

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.68\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{21.5\ W/channel}$$

### 1.5 Copper loss (frequently ignored, and it matters here)
The phase trace resistance is

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{w\,t}$$

where ρ = 1.72×10⁻⁸ Ω·m at 25 °C (+0.39 %/K).

Example: one 2 oz layer (t = 70 µm), w = 3 mm, L = 10 mm gives R = 0.82 mΩ. That is roughly equal to one MOSFET.

- **Mitigation:** parallel all 6 layers on the phase and bus pours with via stitching, giving R ≈ 0.15–0.2 mΩ.
- Each conduction path has bus+, bus−, and two phase segments. The result is P_Cu ≈ 80² × ~0.5 mΩ ≈ **3 W/channel**.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only one half-bridge per motor is PWM-switching at a time, so one hard-switched transition pair occurs per PWM period.

### 2.1 V–I overlap

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

The transition time is set by the Miller charge and the gate current:

$$t_{r,f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g},\quad I_g \approx \frac{V_{DRV}-V_{plateau}}{R_g + R_{drv}}$$

With the FD6288Q (about 1.0 A source / 1.5 A sink) and R_g = 4.7 Ω, t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 15 ns.

$$P_{VI} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (body diode of the synchronous FET after dead time)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.05\ \text{W}$$

Q_rr scales with di/dt and I_F, so a value of 2–3× the datasheet figure is realistic at 80 A. Take **about 0.15 W**.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V^2 f \times 2 = 0.5 \times 1.5\,\text{nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = 0.05\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F I_{ph}\, 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\,\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$
