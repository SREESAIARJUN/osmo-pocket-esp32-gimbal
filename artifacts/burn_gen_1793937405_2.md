# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC Nano Stack: Thermal, Switching and BDShot Design

## Scope Notes

Before the math, four points shape the rest of the design.

1. **Missing project context.** I don't have prior Job #66 documents, a BOM or a MOSFET selection. Every device parameter below is a stated assumption. Swap in your datasheet values; the equations stay the same.
2. **The 48 kHz PWM lives on the ESC MCUs, not the H743.** In a standard ArduPilot stack, the H743 only emits DShot600 frames and receives eRPM telemetry. Commutation and 48 kHz PWM run on the ESC firmware (AM32 or BLHeli_32), typically on 4× STM32G071 or AT32F421. The models below follow that split.
3. **"80 A burst" on a nano form factor is the main design risk.** Section 2.6 shows that a 20×20 / 25.5×25.5 board without a heatsink can only sustain 4×80 A for about 1–3 s. You should rate the product with an explicit duration, or the claim will be invalidated in review.
4. **The netlist is a reference topology, not a verified layout.** It is not a sign-off netlist. Validate all pin and alternate-function (AF) mappings against the STM32H743 datasheet (DS12110, Table 9) and ArduPilot's hwdef documentation before committing.

## 2. Thermal Mathematical Model

### 2.1 Conduction Losses (Synchronous 6-Step)

With complementary (synchronous) PWM in 6-step commutation, the full phase current always flows through exactly two MOSFETs:

- **ON interval:** HS(phase X) + LS(phase Y)
- **OFF / freewheel interval:** LS(phase X) + LS(phase Y)

The channel conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{rms}^2 \, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

The current is close to trapezoidal and ripple is small at 48 kHz with typical L_phase of 10–50 µH. So I_rms ≈ I_pk, with ripple correction:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}-2E)\,D}{2L\,f_{sw}}$$

At 80 A burst with T_j = 125 °C (R = 1.5 mΩ):

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

The copper path adds:

$$P_{cu} = I^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.3\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{1.92\ W}$$

Per-FET average loss: each phase leg sees a current window of 2/3 of the electrical cycle (120° positive, 120° negative). Splitting by duty D:

$$\bar P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\, D\, I^2 R, \qquad \bar P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(2-D)\, I^2 R$$

The low-side FETs run hotter at low duty because freewheeling makes them the dominant conduction path. At D = 0.8 and 80 A:

- HS: 2.56 W
- LS: 3.84 W

The sum over 3 HS + 3 LS = 19.2 W ✓.

### 2.2 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

The commutated switch hard-switches with clamped inductive load.

**Gate-transition times:**

$$t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{(V_{drv}-V_{pl})/R_{g,on}}, \qquad t_{f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{V_{pl}/R_{g,off}}$$

With R_g,on = 4.7 Ω and R_g,off = 2.2 Ω:

- t_r ≈ 8 nC / 1.38 A ≈ 5.8 ns
- t_f ≈ 8 nC / 1.59 A ≈ 5.0 ns

Add the current-transition time (Q_gs2 ≈ 4 nC) for an effective switching time of **t_r + t_f ≈ 20 ns** total.

**V–I overlap loss:**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus}\, I\, (t_r+t_f)\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$
