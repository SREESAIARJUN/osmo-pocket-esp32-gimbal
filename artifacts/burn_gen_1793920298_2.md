## 0. Architecture Corrections

Three points in the request change the design and must be settled first.

1. **The STM32H743 does not drive the power stage.** In every ArduPilot-compatible 4-in-1 design, the H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. Each motor bridge is commutated by its own ESC MCU (for example, 4× AT32F421 running AM32, or EFM8/BB21 running BlueJay). The 48 kHz figure is the ESC MCU's PWM frequency. The H743 only generates bidirectional DShot600 frames. ArduPilot has no firmware path for the H743 to commutate four BLDC motors directly.
2. **80 A burst is not sustainable on a "nano" (20×20 mm) AIO.** The model in §2 shows ~120 W of conduction loss at 80 A × 4 motors. A nano board has a few joules per kelvin of thermal mass and is limited by copper current capacity. Practical figures are:
   - 20×20 AIO: ~25–35 A burst per motor.
   - 30.5×30.5 mm board with 8-layer, 2 oz inner copper: 80 A burst (≤ 2–5 s) is achievable on a single motor, not on all four at once.

   The models below are parametric. Plug in your real limits.
3. **Every device parameter below is an assumption.** Replace each one with values from the datasheets of the parts you actually select before sign-off.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step complementary PWM

Exactly two phases carry the motor current I in every 60° sector. One leg is the PWM leg and one is the "sink" leg, which is held on.

**PWM leg (complementary drive):**
- The high-side FET conducts for duty D.
- The low-side FET conducts for 1 − D as a synchronous rectifier.

**Sink leg:** the low-side FET conducts continuously for the sector.

Because the PWM leg has synchronous rectification, the current always flows through exactly two channel resistances, regardless of D. The motor-level conduction loss is therefore:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

The temperature dependence of R_ds is:

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \mathrm{K^{-1}}
$$

Each FET is active in 2 of 6 sectors (120°), sharing time between its PWM and sink roles. The per-FET average is:

$$
P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{1}{6}\,P_{cond,motor}
$$

Two corrections are omitted from this first-order model:
- Dead-time diode conduction, which is covered in §2.
- The I_RMS/I_avg ripple correction, which is about 1 + (ΔI/I)²/12 and is under 1% at 48 kHz with typical motor inductance.

### 1.2 Numbers at 80 A

| Condition | R_ds | P per motor | P per FET (avg) | P for 4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T_j = 25 °C (start of burst) | 1.50 mΩ | 2 × 6400 × 0.0015 = **19.2 W** | 3.2 W | 76.8 W |
| T_j = 125 °C | 2.25 mΩ | **28.8 W** | 4.8 W | **115.2 W** |

**Thermal runaway check.** R_ds rises with temperature, so the loss feeds back into the junction temperature:

$$
T_j = T_a + P_{FET}(T_j)\cdot R_{\theta,JA,eff}
$$

The solution stays stable only if the loop gain is below one:

$$
\frac{dP}{dT}\cdot R_{\theta} < 1
$$

With dP/dT = 3.2 W × 0.005 = 0.016 W/K per FET, stability requires R_θ,eff < 62 K/W. That condition is met easily, so the problem is absolute temperature, not runaway.

### 1.3 Copper and shunt losses (often larger than designers expect)

**Power-plane copper.** For a 2 oz (70 µm) plane, 10 mm wide, 15 mm path:

$$
R = \frac{\rho L}{w t} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot 0.015}{0.01\cdot 70\times10^{-6}} = 0.37\ \mathrm{m\Omega}
$$

At 80 A that is 2.4 W per layer path. You need at least 3 paralleled 2 oz layers, plus via fields of 30 or more 0.3 mm vias per FET pad.

**Current-sense shunt.** A 0.25 mΩ shunt at the full 320 A total gives P = 25.6 W, which is not survivable. Use a single 0.25 mΩ shunt rated for burst only, or sense current through FET R_ds (less accurate).

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the PWM leg switches. That is one high/low FET pair per motor at any instant.

| Mechanism | Equation | Value @ 80 A, 25.2 V |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap (hard switching) | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | 0.5·25.2·80·20 ns·48 kHz = **0.968 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 50 nC·25.2·48k = **0.060 W** |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss·V·f_sw (≈ ½ C_oss,eq V²·f·2) | **0.048 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2·t_dt·f_sw | 0.8·80·200 ns·48k = **0.614 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs, dissipated in the driver/R_g) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw | 2·40 nC·10·48k = **0.038 W** |
| **Total per motor** | | **≈ 1.73 W** |
| **Total, 4 motors** | | **≈ 6.9 W** |

Interpretation:
- At 80 A, switching loss is only about 6% of the total, so conduction dominates.
- Raising f_sw to 96 kHz would add only about 1.7 W per motor.
- Lowering R_ds(on) is the effective lever: use 5×6 parts or parallel FETs. Lowering f_sw is not.
- Shortening dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns saves 0.3 W per motor. Verify shoot-through margin first.

### Total Loss Budget (all 4 motors at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C)

$$
P_{tot} = 115.2\ (\text{FET cond.}) + 6.9\ (\text{switching}) + \sim 10\ (\text{copper}) \approx \mathbf{132\ W}
$$
