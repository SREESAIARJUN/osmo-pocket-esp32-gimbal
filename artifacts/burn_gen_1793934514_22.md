# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC (Job #23): Architecture, Loss Model, Thermal Budget, DShot DMA Plan

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

### 0.1 Architecture correction

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. ArduPilot has no BLDC commutation firmware. The partitioning is:

| Domain | Device | Firmware | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100) or VIH6 (TFBGA100) | ArduPilot (ChibiOS) | Attitude control, bidirectional DShot600 master |
| ESC, ×4 | STM32G071 class (or AT32F421) | AM32 | 6-step commutation, 48 kHz PWM, eRPM telemetry |
| Power stage, ×4 | 6 N-FETs + 3-phase gate driver | n/a | Inverter |

### 0.3 Feasibility flag

80 A × 4 motors × 25 V ≈ **8 kW electrical**, about 320 A from the battery. On a 20×20 or 25×25 mm "nano" board, this is only survivable for roughly 1 s with all four motors at burst. Section 3 quantifies this. Treat "80A burst" as a **per-motor, short-duration** rating.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-step trapezoidal with synchronous rectification (AM32 complementary PWM)

During each 60° commutation sector:
- One phase is PWM-high: the high-side (HS) FET conducts for D, the low-side (LS) FET conducts for 1−D.
- One phase is held low: its LS FET is fully on.
- One phase floats, used for BEMF sensing.

Over one electrical cycle, each leg spends 1/3 of the time active-high, 1/3 active-low, and 1/3 floating. The per-FET average losses are:

$$P_{HS} = \frac{I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,D}{3}$$

$$P_{LS} = \frac{I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,(1-D)}{3} + \frac{I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)}{3}$$

Summing over all six FETs:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 3\left(P_{HS}+P_{LS}\right) = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This result is independent of D. Two R_ds are always in series with the phase current.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds(25)}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right]$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{ds} = 1.2 \times 1.5 = 1.8\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| P per FET (HS or LS at D = 1) | 6400 × 1.8 m / 3 = **3.84 W** |
| P_cond per motor | 2 × 6400 × 1.8 m = **23.0 W** |
| PCB copper and pads (≈ 0.3 mΩ effective path) | 6400 × 0.3 m = **1.9 W** |
| **Conduction total per motor** | **≈ 24.9 W** |

### 1.4 RMS correction

Trapezoidal current is not flat. Use I_rms instead of I_pk if your 80 A rating is a peak. For 120° conduction with flat tops, I_rms,phase = I·√(2/3).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

At any instant exactly one leg is PWM-switching. Its active period rotates through the three legs, so the switching loss per motor is continuous rather than divided by 3.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 25\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.21\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (LS body diode → HS turn-on)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.024\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period)

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_{dead}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$
