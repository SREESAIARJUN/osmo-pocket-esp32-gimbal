# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 80A-Burst 4-in-1 ESC (Job #31): Architecture, Loss Model, Thermal Model, Bi-Directional DShot Timer/DMA Plan, and Reference Netlist

---

## 0. Architecture Correction

The brief bundles three things that live on **different processors**. This must be fixed first, because it changes the timer budget.

| Function | Executes on | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ArduPilot, bi-directional DShot600 master | **STM32H743** | Flight control |
| 48 kHz complementary PWM, commutation, FOC/6-step | **4× ESC MCUs** (e.g. AT32F421 / STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32) | 12 half-bridges with dead-time cannot share an H743 that also runs EKF3, and DShot is by definition a link to a separate ESC MCU |
| Gate drive | 4× 3-phase drivers (e.g. FD6288Q class) | Bootstrap high-side drive |

The H743 therefore needs **4 DShot channels plus their input-capture DMA**. It does not need 24 PWM outputs.

### Feasibility check on "nano + 80 A burst"

The numbers below show 80 A per motor generates about **80 W of heat** across the ESC. A 20×20 mm nano board can reject roughly 5–8 W continuously under prop-wash. Two consequences follow:

- 80 A is a **≤ 2–3 s** burst rating at best. The thermal model below quantifies this.
- I recommend a **30.5×30.5 mm** (or 25.5 mm whoop) AIO with 2 oz copper or more and 5×6 mm FETs. A 20×20 mm board with 3×3 mm FETs will not survive this spec. Section 2 makes this case numerically.

## 1. Loss Model (per motor, then per board)

### 1.1 Symbols and baseline parameters (design assumptions, to be replaced with the chosen FET's datasheet values)

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 25.2 V | 6S full charge |
| I_ph | 80 A | Burst phase current (6-step, trapezoidal) |
| f_sw | 48 kHz | PWM frequency |
| R_ds,25 | 0.90 mΩ | 40 V 5×6 FET @ V_GS = 10 V |
| α_R | 0.0065 /K | R_ds(on) temperature coefficient (≈ normalized curve) |
| T_j | 100 °C | Evaluation junction temperature |
| t_r + t_f | 40 ns | Effective V-I overlap with ~2 A gate drive |
| Q_oss | 60 nC | Output charge @ V_bus |
| Q_rr | 40 nC | Body-diode reverse recovery |
| V_F | 0.8 V | Body-diode forward voltage |
| t_dt | 100 ns | Dead time per edge |
| Q_g | 60 nC | Total gate charge @ 10 V |
| V_drv | 10 V | Gate drive voltage |
| D | Duty cycle | Cancels out of conduction loss with synchronous rectification |

### 1.2 Conduction loss

Hot on-resistance:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha_R (T_j-25)] = 0.90 \times 1.4875 = 1.34\ \text{m}\Omega$$

In 6-step with complementary (synchronous) PWM, two legs are active at any moment:

- **PWM leg:** current flows through the high-side FET for D·T and the low-side FET (sync rect) for (1−D)·T. That is one R_ds in the path at all times.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET is on continuously. That is one more R_ds.

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2 I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 (80)^2 (1.34\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{17.2\ W\ per\ motor}$$

Because R_ds rises with T_j, conduction loss and temperature feed each other. This is a positive feedback loop. Solve self-consistently:

$$T_j = T_a + P(T_j)\,R_{th,ja} \;\Rightarrow\; \text{runaway if } \frac{dP}{dT_j}R_{th,ja} \ge 1$$

with $\frac{dP}{dT_j} = 2I^2R_{ds,25}\alpha_R = 0.075\ \text{W/K}$ per motor.

### 1.3 Switching losses (only the PWM leg switches)

**V-I overlap:**
$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 (25.2)(80)(40\text{n})(48\text{k}) = 1.94\ \text{W}$$

**Output capacitance:**
$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 60\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.073\ \text{W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period):**
$$P_{dt} = V_F I \,(2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

**Gate drive** (two FETs switching; dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors):
$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 0.058\ \text{W}$$

### 1.4 Additional losses

**Shunt resistor** (if a single low-side bus shunt is used, 0.25 mΩ):
$$P_{sh} = I_{bus}^2 R_{sh}$$
Under burst the bus current per motor is about D·I. Budget about 1 W per motor at D ≈ 0.9.

**PCB copper** (phase pads to FETs, 2 oz, ~0.3 mΩ path):
$$P_{cu} \approx 2 I^2 R_{cu} = 2(6400)(0.3\text{m}) = 3.8\ \text{W per motor}$$
This term is the one designers routinely forget, and it dominates on nano boards.

### 1.5 Totals at 80 A burst

| Loss | Per motor | ×4 board |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 17.2 W | 68.8 W |
| Switching (V-I) | 1.94 W | 7.8 W |
| Coss + Qrr | 0.12 W | 0.5 W |
| Dead time | 0.61 W | 2.4 W |
| Gate | 0.06 W | 0.2 W |
| **FET subtotal** | **19.9 W** | **79.7 W** |
| Copper + shunt | ~4.8 W | ~19 W |
| **Board total** | **~24.7 W** | **~99 W** |

**Efficiency check:** about 7.6 kW of motor power flows at this point (4 × 80 A × 25.2 V × D). Losing ~99 W means ESC efficiency is about 98.7%. That figure is electrically fine; the problem is purely thermal density.

### 1.6 Optimizing f_sw

48 kHz is a reasonable choice for low-inductance racing motors, because ripple current falls as f_sw rises. The f_sw-dependent losses total about 2.7 W per motor (1.94 + 0.12 + 0.61 + 0.06):

- Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.35 W per motor.
- The cost is a rise in RMS ripple of about $\Delta I \approx \frac{V(1-D)D}{2 L f}$. For L = 15 µH at D = 0.5, ripple goes from 4.4 A to 8.8 A peak-to-peak. This adds a small amount of I²R loss and audible whine.

**Recommendation:** run 48 kHz, but derate to 24 kHz automatically above 85 °C (AM32 supports variable PWM).

## 2. Thermal Model

### 2.1 Per-FET distribution

Each motor has 6 FETs. Conduction rotates every 60° electrical, so each FET averages about 1/3 duty in conduction.

- **Peak instantaneous loss** in a conducting FET: I²R = 8.6 W.
- **Electrically averaged:** about 3.3 W per FET (19.9 / 6).
