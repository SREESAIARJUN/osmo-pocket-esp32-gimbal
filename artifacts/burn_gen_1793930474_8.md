**Architectural correction.** The STM32H743 should be the flight controller, not the motor commutation MCU. Each ESC channel needs its own small MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware). The H743 sends bi-directional DShot600 to those four MCUs.

**Feasibility flag (important).** "Nano" (20×20 mm) with 80 A burst on all four channels is not thermally achievable; Section 3 shows why. I have sized this design on 30×30 mm. The 80 A figure should be treated as a single-channel, short-duration transient rating, not a simultaneous 4×80 A rating.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification

During any 60° commutation sector, one phase is PWM-switched and one is held low:

- **Switching leg:** the high-side (HS) FET conducts for duty D. The low-side (LS) FET of the same leg conducts for (1−D), as a synchronous rectifier.
- **Return leg:** the LS FET conducts 100% of the sector.

So motor current always flows through exactly two channels in series, independent of D:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I^2\, R_{ds,on}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{ds,on}(T_j) = R_{ds,on}(25^\circ C)\,\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\,/\text{K}$$

At T_j = 100 °C: R ≈ 0.9 × 1.34 ≈ 1.35 mΩ.

### 1.3 Copper path

The copper path per channel adds about 0.4 mΩ: bus plane, vias, and motor pad. For a 2 oz trace, R = ρL/(wt) with ρ_Cu = 1.72×10⁻⁸ Ω·m.

### 1.4 Burst numbers, per channel at 80 A

$$P_{cond,FET} = 2 (80)^2 (1.35\times10^{-3}) = 17.3\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{cond,Cu} = (80)^2 (0.4\times10^{-3}) = 2.6\ \text{W}$$

**Thermal runaway check.** Because R rises with T_j, the loss feeds back on itself. The stable solution exists only if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\,R_{\theta,JA} < 1 \;\Rightarrow\; 2 I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\, R_{\theta JA} < 1$$

At 80 A: 2·6400·0.9e-3·0.0045 = 0.052 W/K.

- Stable only if R_θJA,eff < 19 K/W per channel pair.
- A bare-board PDFN sits at about 30–40 K/W.
- **Conclusion: 80 A continuous is thermally unstable without a heatsink or airflow. A time limit on the burst is mandatory.**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches (one HS/LS pair per channel).

### 2.1 V·I overlap

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_g} = \frac{15\,nC}{1\,A} = 15\ \text{ns (each edge)}$$

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = \tfrac12(25.2)(80)(30\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{k}) = 1.45\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (LS body diode → HS turn-on)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = 30n \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48k = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V f_{sw} = 40n \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48k \approx 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction (two dead times per period)

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200n \cdot 48k = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

This term is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET.

$$P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2\cdot 50n\cdot 10\cdot 48k = 0.048\ \text{W}$$
