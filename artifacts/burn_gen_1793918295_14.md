# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC: Architecture Specification (Job #9)

## 0. Feasibility Findings

Three facts shape the rest of this design.

1. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** ArduPilot is a flight controller stack. It outputs DShot and does not run motor commutation. A 4-in-1 ESC needs its own MCU per motor, or a dual-motor MCU, running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical choices are the AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 talks to those MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. This document models both halves.
2. **80A on a nano (20×20 mm) footprint is a burst rating only.** The math in §2–3 shows about 80–90W of total dissipation at 80A×4. That is thermally sustainable only for about 1–3 s on a 20×20 board, even with prop wash. **I recommend a 30.5×30.5 mm ESC board** for any 80A claim. The rest of the document assumes a 30.5 mm ESC and a stacked 20×20 FC with a 30.5 adapter, and also gives 20×20 numbers.
3. **Component values below are representative.** Examples include a 40V, ~1.0 mΩ, 5×6 / 3.3×3.3 class MOSFET and an FD6288Q-class gate driver. Replace every parameter marked ⚠ with datasheet values for the parts you actually procure before signing off.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous mode
In each 60° sector, two phases conduct and the third floats.

- **Driven leg (PWM):** the high-side (HS) FET conducts for fraction *D*. The low-side (LS) FET conducts for *(1–D)* as the synchronous rectifier.
- **Return leg:** the LS FET is on for the full sector.

### 2.2 Instantaneous conduction power per motor

P_cond = I² · R_ds(T_j) · [D + (1–D) + 1] = **2 · I² · R_ds(T_j)**

The sum is independent of D, so conduction loss does not vary with throttle at a fixed current.

At 80 A and T_j = 100 °C:

P_cond = 2 × 80² × (1.0 mΩ × 1.5) = 2 × 6400 × 1.5×10⁻³ = **19.2 W per motor**

### 2.3 Per-FET distribution
Each of the 6 FETs carries current about 1/3 of the electrical period. The low-side FETs carry extra load because synchronous rectification adds (1–D). At D ≈ 0.5, the hottest FET is a low-side device:

P_FET,LS ≈ I²R · (1/3 + (1–D)/6) = 9.6 × (0.333 + 0.083) ≈ **4.0 W**
P_FET,HS ≈ I²R · D/6 ≈ **0.8 W**

### 2.4 Copper and shunt losses (not optional at 80A)
- **Phase/bus copper:** 2 oz, about 4 mm wide × 15 mm long ≈ 0.9 mΩ per path. Two paths give 1.8 mΩ × 6400 ≈ **11.5 W.** This is comparable to the FET loss, so you need 4–6 layers of 2–3 oz copper in parallel to bring it below about 2 W.
- **Current shunt:** 0.25 mΩ total shunt: P = 80² × 0.25 m = **1.6 W.** Alternatively use inline Hall sensing, or a 0.1 mΩ shunt with a high-gain INA.

## 3. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the PWM leg switches. Its HS and LS FETs share one hard-switched commutation per edge.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw | 0.5·25.2·80·20 ns·48 k = **0.968 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 40 n·25.2·48 k = **0.048 W** |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss·V·f_sw | 30 n·25.2·48 k = **0.036 W** |
| Dead-time body diode | V_SD·I·2t_dead·f_sw | 0.8·80·200 n·48 k = **0.614 W** |
| Gate drive (driver loss) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw | 2·50 n·10·48 k = **0.048 W** |
| **P_sw total** | | **≈ 1.71 W per motor** |

**Sensitivity:** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz you save about 0.85 W per motor, but you get more audible noise and current ripple.

## 4. Total Burst Dissipation

| Term | Per motor | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (100 °C) | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching | 1.71 W | 6.8 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ) | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| Copper (well-designed, ~0.3 mΩ) | 1.9 W | 7.7 W |
| **Total** | **~24.4 W** | **~97.7 W** |

### 4.1 Steady-state check (why this is burst-only)
Estimated board-to-air resistance with prop-wash airflow:
- 30.5 mm board: R_θBA ≈ 8 K/W
- 20×20 board: R_θBA ≈ 15 K/W

ΔT_ss = 97.7 W × 8 K/W ≈ **780 K.** A continuous 80A×4 rating is physically impossible at this size.

### 4.2 Transient model (lumped two-node)

C_th,board · dT_b/dt = P_total − (T_b − T_amb)/R_θBA
T_j = T_b + P_FET · Z_θJC(t)

Estimated heat capacity of a 30.5 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz board:
- Copper: 9.3 cm² × 6 × 70 µm = 0.39 cm³ → 3.5 g × 0.385 J/gK = 1.35 J/K
- FR-4: 9.3 cm² × 1.6 mm → 2.75 g × 1.1 J/gK = 3.0 J/K
- FETs, caps, solder: about 1.2 J/K
- **C_th ≈ 5.5 J/K**, giving τ = R·C ≈ 44 s

Initial slope: dT/dt ≈ 97.7 / 5.5 ≈ **17.8 K/s**

Junction rise of the hottest low-side FET, with Z_θJC(1 s) ≈ 0.9 K/W and 4.0 W + 0.4 W switching share:

ΔT_jc ≈ 4.4 × 0.9 ≈ 4 K. This is small. **The board heat capacity is the bottleneck, not the junction.**
