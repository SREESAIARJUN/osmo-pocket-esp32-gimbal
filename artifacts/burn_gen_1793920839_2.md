# 3-Axis Gimbal Control: FOC, Cascaded Control, Anti-Cogging, and Dual-IMU Vibration Rejection

I have no specification for "Job #32", so this design is built on the **assumptions in §0**. Every equation is parameterized, so you can substitute your real motor and IMU values. Items marked **[TUNE]** need values measured on your hardware.

## 0. Assumptions and Loop Architecture

| Item | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Motor | Gimbal BLDC, 14 poles (p = 7 pole pairs), 12 slots, R ≈ 5 Ω, L ≈ 2 mH, λ ≈ 0.01 V·s/rad (PM flux) |
| Position sensor | 14-bit absolute magnetic encoder (AS5047P/MT6701), one per axis |
| IMU | 2× ICM-42688-P on the camera plate, SPI, ODR 8 kHz, shared CLKIN |
| MCU | Cortex-M4F/M7 (STM32G4/H7), single-precision FPU |
| Bus | V_dc = 12–16 V |

The loop timing is as follows:

```
 IMU ISR 8 kHz ─► Dual-IMU align/fuse ─► notch bank ─► LPF ─► Mahony (1 kHz) ──┐
                                   │ (gyro rate, 8 kHz → 4 kHz decimated)        │ attitude q
                                   ▼                                            ▼
 Angle loop 1 kHz:   θ* ──► [P + ω_ff] ──► ω* ──┐
 Velocity loop 4 kHz:                    [PI + J·α_ff/Kt] ──► i_q*  ──┐
 Anti-cogging:     θ_m ──► Fourier FF (LMS)  ───────────────► + i_cog ┤
 Current loop 20 kHz:         [PI_d, PI_q + decoupling] ──► SVPWM ──► inverter
```

Design rules for bandwidth separation:

- ω_c(current) ≈ 2π·2 kHz
- ω_v ≈ 2π·150–250 Hz
- ω_θ ≈ 2π·20–40 Hz
- Keep a ratio of at least 5–10 between adjacent loops.

## 1. Field-Oriented Current Loop

### 1.1 Transforms (amplitude-invariant)

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \frac{i_a + 2 i_b}{\sqrt3}
$$

$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix} =
\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix},
\qquad \theta_e = p\,\theta_m - \theta_{\text{off}}
$$

Here θ_off is the encoder-to-rotor electrical offset, found by d-axis alignment at commissioning.

### 1.2 Machine model in the dq frame (surface PMSM, L_d = L_q = L)

$$
v_d = R i_d + L\dot i_d - \omega_e L i_q
$$

$$
v_q = R i_q + L\dot i_q + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e \lambda
$$

$$
\tau_e = \tfrac32 p\lambda\, i_q \equiv K_t\, i_q
$$

### 1.3 PI with decoupling and back-EMF feedforward

$$
v_d^* = \text{PI}_d(i_d^*-i_d) - \hat\omega_e \hat L\, i_q
$$

$$
v_q^* = \text{PI}_q(i_q^*-i_q) + \hat\omega_e(\hat L\, i_d + \hat\lambda)
$$

With ideal decoupling, each axis reduces to $1/(Ls+R)$. Pole–zero cancellation gives a first-order closed loop with bandwidth ω_c:

$$
K_p = L\,\omega_c,\qquad K_i = R\,\omega_c,\qquad \text{constraint: } \omega_c \le \frac{2\pi f_{pwm}}{10}
$$

### 1.4 Discrete PI with back-calculation anti-windup (forward Euler, sample time T_s)

$$
u_k = K_p e_k + I_k
$$

$$
u_k^{sat} = \operatorname{sat}(u_k)
$$

$$
I_{k+1} = I_k + K_i T_s e_k + K_{aw} T_s\,(u_k^{sat}-u_k),\qquad K_{aw}\approx K_i/K_p
$$
