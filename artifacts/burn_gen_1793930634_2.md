**Job #9: Design Basis Document, Rev A (Preliminary)**

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Flags

Before the math, three points constrain the design.

1. **The H743 does not drive the gates.** In an AIO stack, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600. Each of the four ESC channels has its own small MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32-class firmware). That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM, dead-time, and commutation. The switching-loss model in §3 therefore belongs to the ESC MCU domain. The DMA model in §5 belongs to the H743 domain.

2. **"Nano" vs. 80 A burst is a hard conflict.** A 20×20 mm board cannot carry 4×80 A. It lacks the copper cross-section, the phase-pad area, and the thermal mass. This document assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm mounting pattern on a ~36×36 mm board**, which is the minimum credible size. On a 20×20 board, de-rate to roughly 35–40 A burst.

3. **"Burst" must be time-bounded.** The analysis defines burst as **80 A per motor, all four simultaneously, for ≤2 s, at ≥10 s intervals**. Continuous rating is derived separately in §4.4.

## 1. Electrical Model of One ESC Channel

Six-step (trapezoidal) commutation with synchronous rectification works as follows:

- At any instant, two phase legs carry the motor current I_ph: one sourcing and one sinking.
- The third leg is floating and used for BEMF sensing.
- In the PWM-ed leg, the high-side and low-side FETs alternate. Exactly one conducts at any instant, apart from dead-time.
- In the static leg, one FET is held on.

The result is that **two FET channels carry I_ph at all times** (outside dead-time), regardless of duty cycle D.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-Corrected R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}\ (\text{40 V trench})$$

At the design limit T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{DS}(125) = 0.9 \times (1 + 0.45) = 1.31\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 2.2 Per-Channel Conduction Loss

The motor current is assumed flat-topped (inductive smoothing; at 48 kHz the ripple is under ~10%). Ripple adds a correction factor:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)\left(1 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12\,I_{ph}^2}\right)$$

At I_ph = 80 A with ΔI/I = 10%:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.31\times10^{-3} \times 1.0008 \approx \mathbf{16.8\ W}$$

### 2.3 Distribution Between High-Side and Low-Side FETs

Within the PWM leg:

- The high-side FET carries the current for fraction D.
- The low-side FET carries it for fraction (1−D).
- The static-leg FET carries 100%.

**The hottest device is always the static-leg FET**, at I²R = 8.4 W during its 120° electrical conduction interval. Averaged over one electrical cycle, each FET conducts for 1/3 of the cycle in its static role. This gives a per-FET average of roughly 2.8–4.2 W.

The thermal analysis in §4 uses the per-channel total. It also checks the worst single device separately.

### 2.4 Copper and Interconnect Conduction Loss (Often Ignored, and Not Negligible)

| Path | Est. R | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Battery pads → bus planes (shared, 320 A total) | 0.15 mΩ | 15.4 W (board total) |
| Per-phase trace / pad, 3 oz, 2 layers | 0.25 mΩ ×2 active | 3.2 W per channel |
| Current shunt (if single-shunt sense, 0.25 mΩ) | 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W per channel |

The bus-plane figure is why burst current must be time-limited. The board copper itself becomes a heater. Specify **≥3 oz outer / 2 oz inner, 6–8 layers, with V_bus and GND each paralleled across ≥2 layers**.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ed leg switches, so there is one hard-switched commutation pair per PWM period.

### 3.1 Hard-Switching (V-I Overlap) Loss

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

$$= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 27\times10^{-9} \times 48000 = \mathbf{1.31\ W}$$

### 3.2 Reverse Recovery (Body-Diode Q_rr)

The body diode of the complementary FET conducts during dead-time. It must recover when the opposite FET turns on.

$$P_{rr} = V_{bus}\,Q_{rr}\,f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 40\times10^{-9} \times 48000 = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

Q_rr rises with di/dt and T_j, so budget 2×: **0.10 W**.

### 3.3 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I_{ph}\,(2\,t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\times10^{-9} \times 48000 = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

> **Design lever:** Halving dead-time to 100 ns saves 0.6 W per channel. Do this only after scope-verifying zero shoot-through at the hot-corner condition: T_j = 125 °C, V_GS(th) at its minimum.

### 3.4 Output Capacitance Loss

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.2\times10^{-9} \times 635 \times 48000 = 0.018\ \text{W}$$

### 3.5 Gate Drive Loss

This loss is dissipated in the driver and R_g, not the FET. Only the two FETs of the PWM leg switch at f_sw:

$$P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 45\times10^{-9} \times 10 \times 48000 = 0.043\ \text{W}$$

### 3.6 Switching Loss Summary per Channel (80 A, 25.2 V, 48 kHz)

| Component | W |
|---|---|
| V-I overlap | 1.31 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 |
| Q_rr (2× margin) | 0.10 |
| C_oss | 0.02 |
| Gate (driver side) | 0.04 |
| **Total switching** | **2.70** |

### 3.7 Frequency Sensitivity

The switching-dominated terms scale linearly with f_sw:

$$P_{sw,total}(f) \approx 2.70 \times \frac{f}{48\text{ kHz}}$$

| f_sw | P_sw / channel | Board total (×4) |
|---|---|---|
| 24 kHz | 1.35 W | 5.4 W |
| 48 kHz | 2.70 W | 10.8 W |
| 96 kHz | 5.40 W | 21.6 W |

At 48 kHz, conduction dominates by about 6:1, so 48 kHz is justified. It improves current ripple, motor-side loss, and acoustics at a modest FET cost.
