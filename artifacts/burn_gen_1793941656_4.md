# 3-Axis Gimbal: FOC Control Loop and Dual-IMU Sensor Fusion Design

I don't have the original Job #43 specification, so this design starts from a stated baseline. Every constant lives in one config block, so you can replace my assumptions with your actual hardware values.

## 0. Baseline Assumptions

| Item | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| MCU | STM32G474 class (Cortex-M4F, 170 MHz, single-precision FPU) |
| Motors | 3× gimbal BLDC, 12N14P (pole pairs `p = 7`), R ≈ 10 Ω, L ≈ 2.5 mH, Kt ≈ 0.08 N·m/A |
| Bus voltage | 12–16 V (measured every cycle) |
| Rotor sensor | 14-bit absolute magnetic encoder per axis (AS5048A/MT6701 class) |
| Current sensing | 2-shunt low-side, sampled at PWM center |
| PWM | 20 kHz center-aligned |
| Loop rates | current 20 kHz, velocity 4 kHz, angle 1 kHz, IMU 8 kHz ODR |
| IMUs | 2× ICM-42688-P on the camera stage, on separate SPI buses, sample-synced through CLKIN (32 kHz) |
| Gimbal order | yaw (base) → roll → pitch (camera) |

## 1. FOC Current Loop (20 kHz)

### 1.1 Electrical angle with latency compensation

Raw mechanical angle from the encoder:

$$
\theta_m = \frac{2\pi \cdot \text{raw}}{2^{14}} - \theta_{\text{offset}}
$$

The electrical angle is predicted forward by the total loop delay τ_d. This is about 1.5 Ts_i: one sample of computation plus half a PWM period.

$$
\theta_e = p\,(\theta_m + \hat\omega_m \tau_d) \pmod{2\pi}
$$

### 1.2 Clarke transform (two-shunt)

The third phase current is reconstructed from the other two:

$$
i_c = -(i_a + i_b)
$$

$$
i_\alpha = i_a, \qquad i_\beta = \frac{i_a + 2 i_b}{\sqrt3}
$$

### 1.3 Park transform

$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d \\ i_q \end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e \\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha \\ i_\beta \end{bmatrix}
$$

### 1.4 PMSM model in the dq frame

$$
v_d = R i_d + L\frac{di_d}{dt} - \omega_e L i_q
$$

$$
v_q = R i_q + L\frac{di_q}{dt} + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e \lambda_m
$$

The electromagnetic torque is:

$$
\tau_e = \tfrac32 p \lambda_m i_q = K_t i_q
$$

### 1.5 Current PI with decoupling and back-EMF feedforward

The PI zero is placed on the electrical pole R/L, which cancels it. Choosing a closed-loop bandwidth ω_c gives the gains:

$$
K_{p,i} = L\,\omega_c, \qquad K_{i,i} = R\,\omega_c
$$

Rule of thumb: ω_c ≤ 2π·f_PWM/10. For 20 kHz that is ≈ 2π·1.5 kHz.

The controller outputs, including decoupling and back-EMF terms:

$$
v_d^* = \mathrm{PI}_d(i_d^* - i_d) - \hat\omega_e \hat L\, i_q
$$
