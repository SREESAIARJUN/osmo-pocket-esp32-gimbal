# STM32H743 4-in-1 AIO: 80A Burst Thermal, 48 kHz Switching, and Bidirectional DShot600 DMA Design

## 0. Architecture Review: Issues to Resolve Before Layout

Three points in the spec need correction or confirmation. The model below quantifies each.

| # | Issue | Impact | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The STM32H743 is the flight controller MCU, not the ESC MCU.** No mainstream ESC firmware (AM32, BLHeli_32, Bluejay) runs 4 motors on an H743 alongside ArduPilot. | Architecture | Use an H743 for the FC plus 4× dedicated ESC MCUs (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32), linked by bidirectional DShot600. |
| 2 | **80A burst per motor on a 20×20 "nano" board is not thermally viable** beyond about 1–2 s (see §2.6). Commercial 20×20 AIOs are rated around 35–45A per channel. | Thermal and safety | Either define 80A as a ≤1 s peak, or move to a 30.5×30.5 (or larger) ESC board. |
| 3 | **80A through 20 mm of PCB copper costs watts by itself**, not just milliwatts. | Efficiency and hot spots | Use direct-soldered battery leads onto multi-layer stitched pours (§2.5). |

All component parameters below are **representative values for modeling**. Substitute your selected MOSFET and driver datasheet values before sign-off.

## 2. Mathematical Model

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

At any commutation step, current flows through:
- the PWM'd high side (duty D),
- its complementary low side (duty 1−D),
- the statically-on low side of the return phase (duty 1).

So for each motor channel:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ /\mathrm{K}$$

At 80A:
- At T_j = 25 °C: P_cond = 2 · 6400 · 1.0 mΩ = **12.8 W**
- At T_j = 125 °C (1.5 mΩ): P_cond = **19.2 W**

Because R_ds rises with temperature, conduction loss feeds back into heating. Solve this iteratively in the transient model (§2.6).

### 2.2 Hard-Switching Loss (one switching leg per channel)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I\, (t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}$$

0.5 · 25.2 · 80 · 20 ns · 48 kHz = **0.97 W**

### 2.3 Reverse-Recovery, C_oss, Dead-Time, and Gate Losses

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw | 40n · 25.2 · 48k = **0.048 W** |
| C_oss | ½ · Q_oss · V_bus · f_sw · 2 | **0.042 W** |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_f · I · 2t_dt · f_sw | 0.8 · 80 · 200 ns · 48k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs switching) | 2 · Q_g · V_gs · f_sw | **0.043 W** (dissipated mostly in driver and R_g) |

### 2.4 Per-Channel and Board Totals at 80A

| Loss | Cold die (25 °C) | Hot die (125 °C) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 12.8 W | 19.2 W |
| Switching + Q_rr + C_oss | 1.06 W | 1.06 W |
| Dead time | 0.61 W | 0.61 W |
| Gate | 0.04 W | 0.04 W |
| **Per channel** | **≈ 14.5 W** | **≈ 20.9 W** |
| **4 channels** | **≈ 58 W** | **≈ 84 W** |

**Interpretation:** conduction loss dominates at about 88%. Raising f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz costs only about 1.6 W per channel. The lever that matters is **R_ds(on)**: paralleling 2 FETs per switch cuts conduction loss by half.

### 2.5 Copper (PCB) Loss

For a battery pour 20 mm long, 4 mm wide, 2 oz (70 µm), one layer:

$$R = \frac{\rho L}{w t} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot 0.02}{0.004\cdot 70\times10^{-6}} = 1.23\ \mathrm{m\Omega}$$

At the total battery current of 4 × 80A × D (worst case around 250A at high throttle), this one pour would dissipate tens of watts. Required mitigations:
- 4 paralleled 2 oz layers for the V+ and GND pours,
- via stitching at ≥ 0.3 mm vias on a 1 mm pitch,
- battery leads soldered directly over the FET bank,
- AWG10–12 leads.

Target total copper resistance is **< 0.25 mΩ**.

### 2.6 Transient Thermal Model (Burst Capability)

**Board heat capacity (20×20 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz):**

| Element | Calculation | Heat capacity |
|---|---|---|
| Cu | 4 cm² · 8 · 70 µm · 8.96 g/cm³ = 0.20 g × 0.385 J/gK | 0.08 J/K |
| FR4 | 0.64 cm³ · 1.85 g/cm³ = 1.18 g × 1.1 J/gK | 1.30 J/K |
| 24 FETs, caps, connectors | | ≈ 1.0 J/K |
| **C_th,board** | | **≈ 2.4 J/K** |

**Lumped first-order model (board node):**

$$C_{th}\frac{dT_b}{dt} = P_{tot}(T) - \frac{T_b - T_a}{R_{th,ba}}$$

R_th,ba ≈ 8 K/W in prop-wash airflow (estimate this from flight logging).

**Junction temperature:**

$$T_j = T_b + P_{FET} \cdot Z_{th,jc}(t_p) + P_{FET}\cdot R_{th,cb}$$

**Adiabatic burst limit** (from T_a = 40 °C to T_b,max = 100 °C, leaving 25 K of die margin to 125 °C):

$$t_{burst} \approx \frac{C_{th}\,\Delta T}{P_{tot}} = \frac{2.4 \cdot 60}{\sim70\ \mathrm{W}} \approx \mathbf{2.0\ s}$$

The cooling term (≈ 7.5 W at ΔT = 60 K) is negligible against 70 W. **The 80A rating is therefore a ≤ 2 s burst, and roughly 1 s with margin.**
