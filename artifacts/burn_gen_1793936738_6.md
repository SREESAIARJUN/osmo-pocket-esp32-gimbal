# STM32H743 AIO Nano: 4-in-1 ESC and Flight Controller Design Package (Job #20)

**Scope:** Electro-thermal model at 80 A burst, switching losses at 48 kHz, ArduPilot bi-directional DShot600 timer/DMA allocation, reference netlist, and thermal budget.

**Feasibility verdict:** The electrical and timing parts are straightforward. **80 A burst per channel on a 20×20 mm "nano" board is thermally marginal.** It holds only for bursts of about 2 s or less, with heavy copper and airflow. See §4.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification, phase current always flows through **one high-side-position switch and one low-side switch**. During PWM-off time, the complementary FET carries the current instead.

Therefore the instantaneous conducting resistance is always about 2·R_DS(on), **independent of duty cycle**:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0067\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

At T_j = 100 °C this gives 1.8 mΩ.

**Ripple correction.** At 48 kHz with typical motor inductance (L ≈ 20–50 µH), ripple ΔI is about 2–5 A. Using I_rms² = I_dc² + ΔI²/12, the correction is below 0.1 % and can be ignored.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.8\,\text{m}\Omega = 6400 \times 0.0036 = \mathbf{23.0\ W}
$$

**Per FET (averaged over a commutation cycle).** Each of the 6 FETs conducts about 1/3 of the electrical period:

$$
P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{23.0}{6} \approx 3.8\ \text{W}
$$

The peak instantaneous dissipation in a conducting FET is 80² × 1.8 mΩ = **11.5 W**.

### 1.4 Copper and shunt losses

| Element | R | P at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase pad and trace, 2 oz, about 1 mΩ | 1.0 mΩ | 6.4 W (shared across 2 phases → about 6.4 W per channel) |
| Current shunt, 0.25 mΩ (on shared DC return) | 0.25 mΩ | depends on battery current, see §1.5 |
| Bus planes | 0.3 mΩ | depends on battery current |

Copper loss is **not negligible**. It is often the second-largest term on nano boards.

### 1.5 Battery current

$$
I_{bat} = \sum_{k=1}^{4} D_k \cdot I_{ph,k}
$$

At full throttle (D ≈ 0.95) with all four channels at 80 A, I_bat ≈ 304 A. That is beyond any XT60/XT30 and beyond nano-board input pads.

Realistic aggregate bursts are limited by the battery's internal resistance. A 6S 1300 mAh pack sags heavily above about 150 A. **Design the bus for 160 A aggregate burst**; the per-channel 80 A rating is not simultaneous.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switching half-bridge, one per channel at any instant, incurs hard-switching loss.

### 2.1 V–I overlap

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}
= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery (hard commutation of the body diode)

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}
$$

Q_rr rises with di/dt and current. A conservative design value is ×2, giving **0.12 W**.

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

With dead time t_dt = 100 ns per edge and 2 edges per period:

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD} I_{ph} \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

This is a strong argument for minimizing dead time. In AM32, use the lowest dead-time value that is stable for the chosen driver.

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in driver and R_g, not in the FET)

$$
P_{gate} = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2\ \text{FETs} = 45\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = 0.043\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.6 Per-channel total at 80 A

| Loss | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 23.0 |
| Copper (phase) | ~6.4 |
| V–I switching | 0.97 |
| Dead time | 0.61 |
| Q_rr (derated) | 0.12 |
| Q_oss | 0.05 |
| Gate | 0.04 |
| **Total per channel** | **≈ 31.2 W** |
| **4 channels simultaneous** | **≈ 125 W** |

**Efficiency check.** Output power is about 25.2 × 0.95 × 80 ≈ 1915 W per channel, so η ≈ 98.4 %. This is excellent electrically, but the loss is concentrated in about 4 cm² of board.

### 2.7 Sensitivity to switching frequency

$$
\frac{\partial P_{sw}}{\partial f_{sw}} \approx \frac{0.97+0.61+0.17}{48\text{k}} = 36\ \mu\text{W/Hz}
$$

Moving from 24 kHz to 48 kHz adds about 0.9 W per channel. This is minor compared with conduction loss. **Rule:** frequency is not the thermal lever; R_DS(on) and copper are.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network (Cauer, simplified)

```
P_FET ─►[Tj]─R_θJC 1.5K/W─►[Tcase]─R_θCB 0.5K/W (solder+vias)─►[Board, C_b]─R_θBA─►[Ambient]
                                                                  │
                                                     C_board ≈ 4 J/K
```

**Board heat capacity (20×20 mm, 8-layer, about 1.6 mm thick):**

- FR4 at about 1.5 g × 1.1 J/(g·K) = 1.65 J/K
- Copper at about 3.5 g × 0.385 = 1.35 J/K
- FETs, drivers, and solder: about 0.5 J/K
- Optional heat-spreader or pad: +1 J/K
- **C_b ≈ 4–4.5 J/K**

**Board-to-ambient resistance R_θBA:**

| Condition | R_θBA |
|---|---|
| Still air | ~25 K/W |
| Prop wash, 5–10 m/s | ~6–8 K/W |
