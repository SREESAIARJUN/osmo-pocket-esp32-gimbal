# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: 80 A Burst Thermal, 48 kHz Switching and Bidirectional DShot600

## 0. Architecture Clarifications

Three issues change the math, so they come first.

1. **The H743 does not switch the FETs.** In every ArduPilot-compatible 4-in-1 design, the STM32H743 is the flight controller. Each ESC channel (or one shared ESC MCU) runs AM32/BLHeli_32/Bluejay and generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743's job is to emit DShot600 and capture the bidirectional eRPM telemetry. ArduPilot cannot commutate motors directly. The model below follows that split.
2. **80 A is treated as a per-motor phase-current burst** at 6S (25.2 V worst case), on a 30.5×30.5 mm AIO board. I also show the 20×20 "nano" case. On a nano board, 4 × 80 A is not thermally credible beyond about 1 second. The numbers below show why.
3. **Component values are representative assumptions** for a 40 V PDFN3.3×3.3 MOSFET and a 3-phase bootstrap driver. Substitute your actual datasheet values. The equations are general.

## 2. Conduction-Loss Model

Assume 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification (AM32/BLHeli default, complementary PWM). In each 60° sector:

- **Switching leg:** high side (HS) conducts for D, low side (LS) conducts for (1 − D).
- **Static leg:** LS conducts for 100% of the sector.

Total MOSFET conduction loss per motor:

$$P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 R_{ds,hot}\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I_{rms}^2 R_{ds,hot}$$

With synchronous rectification, this is **independent of duty cycle**.

Ripple correction:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I \approx \frac{(V_{bus}-V_{emf})\,D}{2 L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

For a typical low-inductance motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH), ΔI ≈ 5–8 A. That adds less than 1%, so it is neglected below.

**Per-motor conduction loss:**

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.25\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{28.8\ W}$$

**Per-FET average** (6 FETs per motor, each conducting about 1/3 of the electrical cycle):

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx 4.8\ W$$

**Copper loss per motor:**

$$P_{Cu} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.4\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{5.1\ W}$$

This loss is spread in the board rather than concentrated in the FETs.

**Rds(on)–temperature feedback:**

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

Iterate this together with Section 4. Thermal runaway is possible if Rθ_ja × ∂P/∂T_j ≥ 1.

## 3. Switching-Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the switching-leg HS is hard-switched. The LS commutates through its body diode (soft switching).

### 3.1 Gate current and transition times

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{g}} = 1.3\ \text{A}, \qquad I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_g} = 0.7\ \text{A}$$

$$t_{on} = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = 6.2\ \text{ns}, \qquad t_{off} = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,off}} = 11.4\ \text{ns}$$

### 3.2 V–I overlap loss

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_{on}+t_{off}) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 17.6\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.85\ W}$$

### 3.3 Reverse-recovery loss

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.036\ W$$

Practical note: the recovery current also adds to HS turn-on overlap. Multiply by roughly 3 for a realistic estimate, giving about 0.11 W.

### 3.4 Output-capacitance loss

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,tr} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.6\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = 0.024\ W$$

### 3.5 Dead-time diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f I_{ph} (2 t_{dead}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

This is the second-largest switching term. Reducing dead time to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per motor.

### 3.6 Gate-drive loss

This is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET.

$$P_{gate} = Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} \times 2 = 30\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = 0.029\ W$$

### 3.7 Per-motor and board totals

| Term | W/motor |
|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 28.80 |
| Copper | 5.10 |
| V–I overlap | 0.85 |
| Dead time | 0.61 |
| Q_rr (with overlap factor) | 0.11 |
| Coss + gate | 0.05 |
| **Total** | **≈ 35.5 W** |

**Board total at a 4 × 80 A burst: ≈ 142 W.**
