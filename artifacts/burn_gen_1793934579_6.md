## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

**The STM32H743 cannot be the ESC commutation controller.** In a 4-in-1 stack, the H743 runs ArduPilot on the flight controller. Each of the four ESC channels has its own MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 talks to those four MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. This document models both halves.

**The netlists below are representative reference designs, not a released schematic.** Every part number and parameter is an assumption. Verify each one against current datasheets before layout.

| Parameter | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal |
| Burst current | 80 A per motor, ≤ 10 s |
| PWM frequency | f_sw = 48 kHz, synchronous rectification ("damped light") |
| MOSFET | 40 V, R_DS(on) = 0.70 mΩ at 25 °C, PQFN 5×6 class |
| MOSFET switching | Q_g = 60 nC, Q_rr = 50 nC, Q_oss = 60 nC, V_f(body) = 0.8 V |
| Switching edges | t_r ≈ 15 ns, t_f ≈ 12 ns (≈ 1.5 A gate drive) |
| Dead time | t_dt = 100 ns per edge |
| Board | 30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz copper (70 µm) all layers |
| Motor | 14 poles (7 pole pairs) |

**Feasibility flag.** A true "nano" 20×20 mm board cannot dissipate the numbers derived below, even for short bursts. The 80A burst target realistically requires a ≥30×30 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz board. See Section 3.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Conduction path in 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification

In any commutation sector, three devices are involved:
- the high-side FET of phase A, which PWMs at duty D,
- the complementary low-side FET of phase A, which conducts for (1−D),
- the low-side FET of phase B, which is on continuously.

Current therefore always flows through exactly two R_DS(on) in series:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

This holds independent of duty cycle, ignoring dead-time intervals (handled in §2).

### 1.2 Temperature correction

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R = 0.70 \times 1.6 = 1.12\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Per-motor conduction loss at 80 A

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.12\times10^{-3} = 14.3\ \text{W}$$

### 1.4 PCB copper loss

Assume 0.15 mΩ of copper per conduction path segment, with two segments in the path (≈ 0.3 mΩ total):

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.30\times10^{-3} = 1.9\ \text{W}$$

This term is not negligible. Use stitched, paralleled phase and bus pours on at least four layers.

### 1.5 Shunt loss (if per-ESC current sensing is used)

For a 0.25 mΩ shunt:

$$P_{shunt} = 80^2 \times 0.25\text{m} = 1.6\ \text{W}$$

An alternative is to sense across the low-side R_DS(on), which removes this loss at the cost of accuracy.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Each PWM period has one hard-switched turn-on and one turn-off. Both occur on the PWMing high-side device; the low side switches at near-zero voltage after the dead time.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 27\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 1.31\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery of the low-side body diode

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance loss

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \times 2 = 0.07\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per period:

$$P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

This term is significant. Trimming dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per motor, at the cost of shoot-through margin.

### 2.5 Gate drive loss

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FETs:

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2 = 60\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = 0.058\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Per-motor loss budget at 80 A, 48 kHz, T_j = 125 °C

| Loss term | Power |
|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 14.3 W |
| PCB copper | 1.9 W |
| V–I overlap | 1.31 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.13 W |
| Gate drive | 0.06 W |
| **Total per motor** | **≈ 18.3 W** |
| **Board total (4 × 80 A)** | **≈ 73 W** (+6.4 W with shunts) |
