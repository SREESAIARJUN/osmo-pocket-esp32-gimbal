# Job #24: STM32H743 Nano AIO with 4-in-1 ESC, 80A Burst

## 0. Architecture Decision

The STM32H743 cannot practically commutate four BLDC motors directly while running ArduPilot. That would require 24 complementary PWM outputs, 4 independent BEMF comparator sets, and hard-real-time commutation inside a non-RT-safe flight stack. The design is therefore partitioned as follows.

| Block | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100) | ArduPilot, bidirectional DShot600 master |
| ESC MCUs (×4) | AT32F421K8U7 (QFN32), running AM32 | 6-step commutation, 48 kHz complementary PWM, eRPM telemetry |
| Gate drivers (×4) | FD6288Q (3-phase, bootstrap, 250 V) | HS/LS gate drive |
| Power stage (×24) | 40 V N-MOSFET, PDFN3.3×3.3 (class: ~1.0 mΩ typ @ 25 °C) | 6 per motor |
| Bus | 6S (25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal) | |

All device parameters below are design assumptions representative of the part class. Substitute datasheet values for the final BOM. Every result is given as a formula so it can be re-evaluated.

## 2. Conduction Losses (6-Step Trapezoidal, Synchronous Rectification)

### 2.1 Conduction topology

In 6-step commutation there are 6 sectors of 60° each. In every sector:
- One leg is PWM-active: the high side conducts for D, and the low side conducts for (1−D) as a synchronous rectifier.
- One leg is held low: its low side is fully on.
- One leg floats and is used for BEMF sensing.

Across the active PWM leg, current always flows through exactly one FET of that leg, plus the static low-side FET. Therefore **two FETs carry I_ph at all times**.

### 2.2 Per-motor conduction loss

P_cond,motor = 2 · I_rms² · R_ds(T_j)

The PWM ripple adds to the RMS current:

ΔI_pp = V_bus · D(1−D) / (L_ph · f_sw)

The worst case is at D = 0.5:

ΔI_pp = 25.2 × 0.25 / (20e-6 × 48e3) = **6.56 A**

I_rms² = I_ph² + ΔI_pp²/12 = 6400 + 3.59 = 6403.6 A²

The ripple contribution is negligible (0.06%), so 48 kHz is well chosen.

P_cond,motor = 2 × 6403.6 × 1.5e-3 = **19.2 W**

### 2.3 Per-FET distribution (worst-case FET)

Each leg conducts for 240° of 360° (2/3 of the period).

High-side FET: P_HS = I² · R_ds · D / 3
Low-side FET: P_LS = I² · R_ds · (1/3 + (1−D)/3) = I² · R_ds · (2−D)/3

| Duty | P_HS | P_LS | Hottest FET |
|---|---|---|---|
| D = 1.0 | 3.20 W | 3.20 W | 3.20 W (both sides) |
| D = 0.5 | 1.60 W | 4.80 W | **LS: 4.80 W** |
| D = 0.2 | 0.64 W | 5.76 W | **LS: 5.76 W** |

This is a key finding: **the low-side FETs are the thermal limiter at partial throttle under high current**. That condition corresponds to a punch-out recovery or prop-wash burst at mid-stick with a heavy prop. Place the LS FETs over the largest ground-plane copper.

## 3. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-active high-side FET hard-switches. The low-side synchronous FET switches at near-zero V_ds, because its body diode conducts first during dead time.

### 3.1 V–I overlap loss (hard-switched HS)

P_sw = ½ · V_bus · I_ph · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw
= 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40e-9 × 48e3 = **1.935 W**

### 3.2 Reverse recovery (LS body diode, recovered at HS turn-on)

P_rr = Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw = 50e-9 × 25.2 × 48e3 = **0.060 W**

Note that Q_rr scales with di/dt and current, so at 80 A it may be 1.5 to 2× the datasheet figure. Budget **0.12 W**.

### 3.3 Output capacitance

P_oss = Q_oss · V_bus · f_sw = 40e-9 × 25.2 × 48e3 = **0.048 W**

### 3.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction (LS)

P_dt = V_f · I_ph · 2 · t_dt · f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 2 × 100e-9 × 48e3 = **0.614 W**

AM32's dead-time setting directly trades shoot-through margin against this loss. Do not exceed roughly 100 ns at 48 kHz.

### 3.5 Gate-drive loss (dissipated in the FD6288 and R_g, not the FET)

P_g = 2 · Q_g · V_drv · f_sw = 2 × 60e-9 × 10 × 48e3 = **0.058 W** per active leg

### 3.6 Switching summary per motor at 80 A

| Term | Power |
|---|---|
| Overlap | 1.94 W |
| Reverse recovery (derated) | 0.12 W |
| Coss | 0.05 W |
| Dead time | 0.61 W |
| **Switching subtotal** | **2.72 W** |
| Conduction | 19.2 W |
| Shunt and copper (see 3.7) | ~3.5 W |
| **Total per motor** | **≈ 25.4 W** |
| **Total, 4 motors** | **≈ 102 W** |

### 3.7 Copper and shunt losses

- **Shunt:** 0.2 mΩ shared, with the bus current I_bus ≈ D·I_ph summed. Use 4 × 80 A × D. At D = 0.6: (192 A)² × 0.2 mΩ = **7.4 W** if a single shared shunt is used. This is far too much. Use per-motor 0.25 mΩ shunts, or sense through FC-side bus current only via an external sensor. With per-motor shunts: (48 A)² × 0.25e-3 = **0.58 W** each.
- **PCB copper:** a 30×30 mm board with 2 oz × 6 layers has power paths of roughly 0.3 to 0.5 mΩ effective, giving about 3 W per motor at 80 A. This is a significant loss and argues for **6-layer, 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner power layers**.
