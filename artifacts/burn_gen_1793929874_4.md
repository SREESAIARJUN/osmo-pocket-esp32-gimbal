# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #77): Reference Design Package

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and One Critical Finding

Two points shape the rest of the design.

**A. Partitioning.** The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. In ArduPilot-class stacks the H743 runs the flight controller and sends bi-directional DShot600. Each ESC channel uses a dedicated low-cost MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. This design assumes AT32F421 parts, either four single-channel MCUs or the usual 4-in-1 arrangement. That is how commercially viable 4-in-1 ESCs are built. If you intended the H743 to run sensorless FOC/6-step for all four motors as well as flight control, treat that as a separate trade study. Doing so would consume the advanced timers and DMA needed for DShot.

**B. "80A burst" is only feasible for about 1 s per channel on a 20×20 board.** The derivation is in §2. At 80A on all four channels (320A total), a nano board heats at roughly 45 K/s. The defensible ratings are:
- **80A total burst** (20A/channel): thermally sound.
- **80A single-channel burst**: about 1 s.

The model is parameterized so you can substitute your actual MOSFET datasheet values.

## 1. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel)

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal, synchronous (complementary) PWM. At any instant two half-bridges conduct the phase current I:
- One leg is PWM-ing. Its high side conducts for D and its low side for (1−D) under synchronous rectification.
- The other leg's low side is held on.

### 1.1 Conduction Loss

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds,hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds,hot} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds,hot}$$

The loss is independent of duty cycle because synchronous rectification removes the duty-cycle split. With the temperature dependence written out:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\,/\mathrm{K}$$

### 1.2 Switching Loss (hard-switched leg, 2 edges per period)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

### 1.3 Dead-Time (Body-Diode) Loss

$$P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw}$$

### 1.4 Reverse Recovery, C_oss, and Gate Drive

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw}, \quad P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} \times 2, \quad P_{gate} = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} \times 2$$

The gate-drive term is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors. Only the PWM leg's two FETs switch at f_sw.

### 1.5 Path Losses

$$P_{path} = I^2 (R_{shunt} + 2R_{cu})$$

### 1.6 Results at V_bus = 25.2 V, f_sw = 48 kHz

| Term | 20A/ch (80A total) | 40A/ch | **80A/ch** |
|---|---|---|---|
| P_cond = 2I²R_ds,hot | 1.92 W | 7.68 W | **30.72 W** |
| P_sw | 0.24 W | 0.48 W | 0.97 W |
| P_dt | 0.31 W | 0.61 W | 1.23 W |
| P_rr (30 nC) | 0.04 W | 0.04 W | 0.04 W |
| P_oss | 0.03 W | 0.03 W | 0.03 W |
| P_gate | 0.04 W | 0.04 W | 0.04 W |
| P_path (1.1 mΩ) | 0.44 W | 1.76 W | 7.04 W |
| **P_total / channel** | **≈ 3.0 W** | **≈ 10.6 W** | **≈ 40.1 W** |
| P_total, 4 channels | 12 W | 42 W | 160 W |

**Interpretation.** At high current, conduction loss dominates (I² terms make up about 94% at 80A). Raising f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz adds only about 1.1 W per channel at 80A. Frequency is therefore not the lever. The levers are R_ds(on), a lower-resistance shunt (or shunt-less sensing), and copper weight.

**Optimal f_sw sanity check.** Ripple current scales as $\Delta I \propto V/(L f)$. Low-inductance racing motors (L ≈ 10–20 µH) benefit from 48 kHz through lower ripple-RMS copper loss in the motor. That justifies the switching penalty.

## 2. Thermal Model

### 2.1 Board Heat Capacity (20×20 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz copper)

| Material | Volume | Mass | c_p | C |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cu, 8 × 70 µm × 4 cm² | 2.24×10⁻⁷ m³ | 2.0 g | 0.385 J/g·K | 0.77 J/K |
| FR-4, 1.6 mm | 6.4×10⁻⁷ m³ | 1.2 g | 1.1 J/g·K | 1.3 J/K |
| FETs, caps, solder | — | ~1.5 g | ~0.6 J/g·K | ~0.9 J/K |
| **C_th,board** | | | | **≈ 3.0 J/K** |

### 2.2 Steady State (lumped)

$$\theta_{BA} = \frac{1}{h A_{eff}}, \quad A_{eff} \approx 2 \times 4\,\mathrm{cm^2} = 8\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}$$

| Airflow | h (W/m²K) | θ_BA | ΔT at 12 W (80A total) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Still air | 15 | 83 K/W | ~1000 K (impossible, so derate) |
| Hover prop-wash | 100 | 12.5 K/W | 150 K |
| Forward flight | 200 | 6.25 K/W | 75 K |

**Conclusion.** Even 80A total cannot be sustained on a bare 20×20 board. 80A is a burst rating. A realistic sustained rating is about 30–40A total (4–6 W) unless you add a heat spreader. For example, a 0.5 mm aluminium plate on the FET side with a thermal pad of about 4 K/W roughly halves θ_BA.

### 2.3 Transient (burst) Model: First-Order Board Node

$$T_B(t) = T_A + P\,\theta_{BA}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right), \quad \tau = \theta_{BA} C_{th} \approx 12.5 \times 3.0 = 37.5\,\mathrm{s}$$

For t ≪ τ the board behaves adiabatically: $\dot{T} \approx P / C_{th}$.

| Case | P | dT/dt | Time to ΔT = 60 K (board at 100 °C from 40 °C) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 80A total (20A/ch) | 12 W | 4 K/s | ~19 s (with τ: ≈ 24 s) |
| 40A/ch all motors | 42 W | 14 K/s | ~4.3 s |
| **80A/ch all motors** | 160 W | 53 K/s | **~1.1 s** |
| 80A one channel, others at 20A | 40 + 9 = 49 W | 16 K/s | ~3.7 s board-level, but see §2.4 |

$$T_j(t) = T_B(t) + P_{FET}\sum_{i=1}^{n} R_i\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau_i}\right)$$
