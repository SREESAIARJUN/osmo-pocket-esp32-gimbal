**Engineering Design Note, Job #6, Rev A (analysis draft)**

## 0. Architecture Corrections Before the Math

Three points in the brief would cause problems downstream if left unaddressed.

1. **The H743 should not commutate the motors directly.** Hardware complementary PWM with dead-time is available only on the advanced timers TIM1 and TIM8. Each provides 3 complementary pairs, so together they drive at most **2 motors**, not 4. HRTIM could add channels, but then the H743 would be running FOC/6-step for 4 motors alongside the flight stack.
   - **Recommended architecture:** the H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller and sends bidirectional DShot600 to **4 dedicated ESC MCUs** (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32).
   - The 48 kHz PWM and dead-time are generated on those ESC MCUs.
   - Everything below assumes this split.
2. **"80 A" is interpreted as 80 A peak phase current per motor, burst duration ≤ 2 s.** On a nano footprint (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm), 80 A on all four motors simultaneously is only survivable for roughly 1–2 s. Section 3 shows this. Marketing ratings in this class are typically measured with one motor loaded, or with heavy airflow.
3. **All device parameters below are representative placeholders.** They describe a 40 V, PDFN 3.3×3.3 MOSFET class. Replace them with the datasheet values of the selected FET before release.

## 2. Loss Model per Motor

The drive is 6-step trapezoidal with synchronous (complementary) PWM. At any instant, one phase sources current and one phase sinks it.

- **PWM on-time:** current flows through the high-side FET of phase A and the low-side FET of phase B.
- **PWM off-time:** current flows through the low-side FET of phase A (synchronous rectification) and the low-side FET of phase B.

Either way, the current passes through **two conducting FETs in series for 100 % of the period**.

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

$$
R_{ds,hot} = k_T \cdot R_{ds(on),25} = 1.5 \times 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega = 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \cdot I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{ds,hot} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W}
$$

**Per-FET distribution:** the losses are spread unevenly across the 6 FETs.

- Each phase is active for 2/3 of the electrical cycle.
- The low-side FETs carry current during both the PWM on-time (as sink) and the off-time.

Worst case at 100 % throttle: each FET conducts roughly 1/3 of the time, carrying the full 80 A.

$$
P_{FET,avg} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}\, I^2 R_{ds,hot} = 3.2\ \text{W}
$$

The instantaneous dissipation is 9.6 W during the device's conducting interval. Use that figure for the junction transient check, because the commutation period at high eRPM is much shorter than the die time constant.

**Temperature coupling:** the conduction loss and the junction temperature depend on each other. A self-consistent solution uses:

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ /\text{K}
$$

Iterate this together with the thermal model in Section 3. At T_j = 110 °C, α gives a factor of 1.51, consistent with k_T = 1.5.

### 2.2 Switching Loss (Hard-Switched High-Side Edges)

**V-I overlap**

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.968\ W}
$$

**Reverse recovery** (the low-side body diode recovers at high-side turn-on)

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}
$$

**Output capacitance**

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.024\ W}
$$

**Dead-time diode conduction** (two edges per period)

$$
P_{dt} = V_f I_{ph} \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.614\ W}
$$

**Gate drive** (two switching FETs per period; this loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistor)

$$
P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

**Switching subtotal:**

$$
P_{sw,total} \approx \mathbf{1.69\ W}
$$

**Design note:** dead-time loss is about 36 % of the switching budget. Reducing t_dt from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per motor. AM32 exposes dead-time as a configurable value. Validate the reduction against shoot-through margin on the bench.

### 2.3 Total per Motor and Board

| Component | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching + dead-time + Q_rr + Q_oss | 1.64 W | 6.6 W |
| Gate drive | 0.05 W | 0.2 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, single bus shunt) | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| Copper traces (est. 0.5 mΩ effective path) | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 25.7 W** | **≈ 103 W** |

**Delivered power for comparison:** about 4 × 25.2 V × 80 A × m, where m is the modulation index, giving roughly 6–8 kW at full throttle. The efficiency is therefore about 98.5 %. The problem is not efficiency but **power density**: roughly 100 W in about 6 cm² of board.

**Lower-current reference point:** losses scale with I² for the dominant terms, so they fall quickly at lower current. At 40 A per motor, the board total is about 28 W. This is the realistic continuous ceiling with propwash cooling.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network (Foster form, per hot FET)

```
P_FET ─► [Zth j-c: R=1.2 K/W, τ≈5 ms] ─► [solder+Cu spreading: R=8 K/W, τ≈0.5 s]
       ─► [board bulk: C_board, R_conv→amb] ─► T_amb
```

### 3.2 Board Thermal Capacitance (20×20 mm, 1.6 mm, 6-layer 2 oz outer / 1 oz inner)

| Material | Mass | Specific heat | Heat capacity |
|---|---|---|---|
| FR4: 0.64 cm³ × 1.85 g/cm³ | 1.18 g | 1.10 J/g·K | 1.30 J/K |
| Copper: ~0.30 mm equivalent × 400 mm² × 0.75 fill = 90 mm³ | 0.80 g | 0.385 J/g·K | 0.31 J/K |
| 24 FETs + solder + passives | ~0.6 g | ~0.6 J/g·K | 0.36 J/K |
| **Total C_board** | | | **≈ 2.0 J/K** |

Adding a 0.5 g aluminium heat spreader contributes +0.45 J/K. Moving to a 25.5×25.5 mm board increases the area, and therefore the capacitance, by about 1.6×.
