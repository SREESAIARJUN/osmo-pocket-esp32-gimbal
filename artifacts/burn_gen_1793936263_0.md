## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current Path Topology

In 6-step operation with synchronous rectification, phase X is PWM-switched and phase Y is held low:

- **On-interval (D):** current flows V_bus → HS_X → motor → LS_Y → GND
- **Off-interval (1−D):** current freewheels LS_X (synchronous) → motor → LS_Y

So the phase current **always flows through exactly two MOSFET channels**, independent of duty cycle:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot \left[\, 2R_{ds}(T_j) + R_{pcb} \,\right] \cdot k_{ripple}
$$

The ripple factor accounts for the RMS of the triangular ripple:

$$
k_{ripple} = 1 + \frac{1}{12}\left(\frac{\Delta I_{pp}}{I}\right)^2,\qquad
\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}
$$

For a typical 5" motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH line-to-line) at 48 kHz with D ≈ 0.5 and a 6 V headroom margin, ΔI_pp ≈ 4 A. That gives k_ripple ≈ 1.0002, so it is negligible at 80 A. It matters at low current.

### 1.2 Temperature-Coupled R_ds(on): Self-Consistent Solution

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big]
$$

$$
T_j = T_{board} + P_{FET}\cdot R_{th,jc}
$$

The temperature rise increases R_ds(on), which increases loss. Iterate to convergence, or solve the closed form:

$$
P_{FET} = \frac{I_{rms,FET}^2\, R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_{b}-25)]}{1 - I_{rms,FET}^2\, R_{ds,25}\,\alpha\, R_{th,jc}}
$$

If the denominator approaches 0, the device is in **thermal runaway**. This is a useful design-rule check.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A Phase Current, Board at 100 °C

Each FET conducts for roughly one-third of the electrical period (HS_X for D/3, LS_X for (1−D)/3, LS_Y for 1/3, averaged over rotation).

$$
I_{rms,FET} = 80/\sqrt{3} = 46.2\ \text{A}
$$

Taking R_th,jc = 1.5 K/W:

- R_ds(100 °C) = 1.5 × (1 + 0.0055 × 75) = **2.12 mΩ**
- Denominator: 1 − 2134 × 1.5e-3 × 0.0055 × 1.5 = 0.974
- P_FET = 2134 × 2.12e-3 / 0.974 = **4.65 W per FET (time-averaged)**
- T_j ≈ 100 + 4.65 × 1.5 ≈ 107 °C, which gives R_ds ≈ 2.17 mΩ (consistent)

| PCB copper | 80² × 0.5 mΩ | **3.2 W** |
