# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #14): Power-Stage, Thermal, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Architecture

## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility Warning

I don't have the Job #14 BOM, stack-up, or pinout. All numbers below use **stated example parameters**, and every equation is written so you can substitute your real datasheet values.

**Architecture assumption.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller running ArduPilot. The ESC section uses four dedicated ESC MCUs (AM32 or BLHeli_32 class, e.g. AT32F421 or STM32G071). The H743 does not commutate the motors. It sends bi-directional DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry.

**Feasibility warning.** An "80 A burst per motor" rating on a nano (20×20 mm) board is not thermally credible for more than about 1 s with all four motors loaded. Section 4 shows the math. The total would be 320 A from the battery, roughly 85 W dissipated in about 2 J/K of thermal mass. Before layout, I recommend redefining the spec as one of:

- **80 A single-motor burst for ≤1 s**, or
- **a 30.5×30.5 mm form factor**.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.0 mΩ × 1.5 = **1.5 mΩ**.

### 2.2 Per-device duty factors

This assumes six-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM on the high side. Each phase leg spends 120° PWM-driven high, 120° held low, and 120° floating.

| Device | Conduction fraction of electrical period |
|---|---|
| High-side FET | D / 3 |
| Low-side FET | (1 − D)/3 (sync rectification) + 1/3 (low phase) = (2 − D)/3 |
| Leg total | 2/3 |

### 2.3 Per-FET, per-motor, and board losses

Ripple is neglected here (I_rms ≈ I_dc); add the ripple term from 2.4 if your motor inductance is low.

$$P_{HS} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\frac{D}{3}, \qquad P_{LS} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\frac{2-D}{3}$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = 3 \times I^2 R_{ds}\cdot\tfrac{2}{3} = \boxed{2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)}$$

At 80 A, R_ds = 1.5 mΩ:

| Quantity | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Per motor | 2 × 6400 × 1.5 mΩ | **19.2 W** |
| Worst single FET (low side, D → 0) | 6400 × 1.5 mΩ × 2/3 | **6.4 W** |
| Worst single FET (low side, D = 1) | 6400 × 1.5 mΩ × 1/3 | 3.2 W |
| Four motors | 4 × 19.2 W | **76.8 W** |

A 6.4 W hotspot in a 3×3 or 5×6 mm package on a nano board is the binding constraint.

### 2.4 Ripple correction (optional)

With ripple $\Delta I = \dfrac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{2L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

### 2.5 Electro-thermal fixed point

R_ds depends on T_j, which depends on loss. Iterate until it converges:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_a + R_{th,ja}\,P\!\left(T_j^{(n)}\right)$$

The loop is stable only if:

$$\alpha\, R_{th,ja}\, P_{cond}(25\,^\circ\text{C}) < 1$$

Otherwise you get **thermal runaway**. Check this explicitly for steady-state current, not just burst.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-active leg hard-switches, so there is **one switching leg per motor** at any instant.

### 3.1 Transition times (gate-charge method)

$$t_r \approx \frac{(Q_{gd}+Q_{gs2})\,R_{g,tot}}{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}, \qquad t_f \approx \frac{(Q_{gd}+Q_{gs2})\,R_{g,tot}}{V_{pl}}$$

With Q_gs2 ≈ 4 nC:

- t_r ≈ 12 nC × 6 Ω / 7 V ≈ 10 ns
- t_f ≈ 12 nC × 6 Ω / 3 V ≈ 24 ns
- Sum: **≈ 34 ns**

### 3.2 Loss components (per motor, at 80 A and 25.2 V)

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 34 ns × 48 kHz | **1.65 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 0.036 W |
| Output capacitance (hard turn-on) | ≈ Q_oss·V·f_sw | 0.048 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | 2·V_f·I·t_dt·f_sw | **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (driver, not FET) | 2·Q_g·V_drv·f_sw | 0.038 W |
| **Total** | | **≈ 2.4 W/motor** |

**Combined per motor at burst:** 19.2 W conduction + 2.4 W switching ≈ **21.6 W**.
