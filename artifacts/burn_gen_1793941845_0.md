# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC: Design Derivation (Job #9)

## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Assumptions

Three points shape the rest of the analysis:

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to the ESC. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCU(s), for example 4× AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The switching-loss model below therefore applies to the ESC power stage. The DShot model applies to the H743.
2. **"80A burst" on a nano form factor is thermally marginal.** The math in §3 shows it is sustainable for roughly 1–2 s, not continuously. The deliverable includes a derating curve rather than a pass/fail claim.
3. **All component parameters are representative placeholders.** Substitute real datasheet values before release.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths

In 6-step commutation, two phases conduct at any instant:
- The **PWM leg** alternates between its high-side FET (for duty D) and its synchronous low-side FET (for 1−D).
- The **return leg** low-side FET is on 100% of the commutation step.

The current therefore always flows through exactly two R_DS(on) in series:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + k\,(T_j - 25)\right] \approx R_{25}\cdot 1.5 \;\;(T_j{=}125^\circ C) = 1.8\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Per-FET distribution

Over the electrical cycle, each phase spends 1/3 of the time as the PWM leg, 1/3 as the return leg, and 1/3 floating. At the instant of worst stress, however, the return-leg low-side FET carries the full current:

$$P_{FET,peak} = I^2 R_{DS} = 80^2 \times 1.8\text{m} = \mathbf{11.52\ W}$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 11.52 = \mathbf{23.04\ W}$$

For thermal time constants longer than the electrical period (more than about 1 ms), average across the 6 FETs. The FET-averaged dissipation is P_cond,motor / 6 × 2 (two FETs active at any time), which gives **≈ 7.7 W average per FET** under rotation.

**Locked-rotor or startup case:** the peak value of 11.52 W persists on one FET. This is the design-limiting case.

### 1.3 Copper and shunt losses

- **Phase trace:** 2 oz copper (R_s ≈ 0.25 mΩ/□), effective 3 squares per phase path, 4 layers in parallel. This gives R ≈ 0.19 mΩ and P = 80² × 0.19m ≈ **1.2 W per phase**, so about 2.4 W per motor.
- **Bus shunt:** 0.25 mΩ, carrying the bus current I_bus = D·I_ph. At D = 0.8, I_bus = 64 A per motor and 256 A total. P = 256² × 0.25m ≈ **16 W** at full burst.
  - This is a strong argument for **per-channel 0.5 mΩ shunts or a Hall sensor** instead of a single shunt.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. The return leg is static.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V-I overlap (high-side FET)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.452\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (low-side body diode)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.024\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I_{ph}\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.614\ W}$$

Reducing dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns saves 0.3 W per motor. Tune this on the bench with AM32's dead-time parameter while watching for shoot-through.

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in the driver, not the FET)

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 60\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.029\ \text{W per switching FET}$$

### 2.6 Total per motor at 80 A

| Loss | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (2 FETs) | 23.04 |
| Switching overlap | 1.45 |
| Dead time | 0.61 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.08 |
| Phase copper | 2.4 |
| **Total per motor** | **≈ 27.6 W** |
| **×4 motors** | **≈ 110 W** |
| + shunt (single) | 16 |
| **Board total** | **≈ 126 W** |

### 2.7 Frequency sensitivity

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial f_{sw}} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) + Q_{rr}V + \tfrac12 Q_{oss}V + 2V_f I t_{dt} \approx 45\ \mu\text{J}/\text{cycle}$$

So **48 kHz costs about 1.1 W more per motor than 24 kHz**. That is small compared to conduction losses. The penalty for 48 kHz is driver heating and EMI, not FET loss.

### 2.8 Worst single FET

The worst case is the PWM-leg high-side FET at high duty, or the return-leg low-side during a stall:

$$P_{FET,max} \approx 11.52 + 1.45 + 0.08 \approx \mathbf{13.1\ W}$$
