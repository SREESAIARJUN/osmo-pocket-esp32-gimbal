# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model

**Architecture review · Job #7 · Rev A (first-pass model)**

## 0. Assumptions and Architecture Corrections

Several points in the brief need correcting or pinning down before the math is meaningful.

### 0.1 The STM32H743 should not commutate the motors

In a bidirectional-DShot system, the H743 runs ArduPilot and *sends* DShot600. Each motor needs its own ESC MCU that receives DShot, commutates the bridge, and returns eRPM telemetry. ArduPilot has no in-tree 4-motor BLDC commutation for the H743.

Proposed architecture:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──DShot600 bidir ×4──► 4× AT32F421 / STM32G071 (AM32)
                                               └─► 3-ph gate driver ─► 6× N-MOSFET each
```

### 0.2 "Nano" and 80 A are in tension

On a 20×20 mm board, 80 A per channel cannot be held at steady state. It is a **transient (burst) rating** limited by board thermal mass. Section 3 quantifies this. Expect to defend a 1–3 s burst figure, not a continuous one.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

This assumes trapezoidal 6-step with complementary (synchronous) PWM, which is the AM32 default. At any instant two phases conduct, so the current flows through one high-side-path FET and one low-side FET in series.

Phase current relates to battery current by:

$$I_{ph} \approx \frac{I_{bat}}{D}$$

The 80 A phase-current case therefore corresponds to D → 0.9–1.0.

During PWM, the high side conducts for D and the complementary low side for (1−D). The series path always contains exactly two R_ds(on).

### 1.2 Per-channel conduction loss

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha_R (T_j-25)\right]$$

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond,ch @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W |
| 100 °C | 1.41 mΩ | 18.1 W |
| 125 °C | 1.55 mΩ | 19.8 W |

### 1.3 Copper and interconnect loss

Assume about 0.4 mΩ effective series resistance per channel (pads, vias, inner planes, solder joints to motor wires):

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.0004 = 2.6\ \text{W}$$

Treat this as a design target. Achieving it requires many filled vias and very short phase pads.

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

Each FET carries current for 1/3 of an electrical cycle (120°):

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6}\cdot 2 = \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{3}$$

The average is about 6 W per FET during conduction windows. At typical eRPM the electrical period is ≤1 ms, which is shorter than the die-to-board time constant, so averaging is valid at board level.

### 1.5 Electrothermal fixed point

Because R_ds rises with T_j, the loss and temperature must be solved together:

$$T_j = T_{board} + \bar P_{FET}\,R_{th,jc}, \qquad P = f(T_j)$$

Iterating with R_th,jc ≈ 1.2 K/W converges in 2–3 steps. ΔT_jc is only about 7–8 K. **The board, not the die, is the limiting element.**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

These figures are per channel. Only the PWM'd leg switches hard; the other leg switches only at commutation (≪ f_sw).

### 2.1 V–I overlap

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = 1.94\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I_{ph}\,(2 t_{dead})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$
