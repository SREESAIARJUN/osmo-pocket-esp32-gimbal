### Architectural note

The H743 should run ArduPilot as the **flight controller only**. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU, such as AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or a single-chip 4-motor ESC MCU. The H743 talks to these on-board over bidirectional DShot600. Running commutation for four motors on the H743 alongside ArduPilot is not a supported ArduPilot configuration.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C this gives R_ds = 1.5 mΩ × 1.5 = **2.25 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step synchronous PWM

In every commutation sector, current flows through exactly two conducting channels:

- **The PWM leg:** the high-side FET carries current for fraction D of the period, and the complementary low-side FET carries it for (1−D), excluding dead time.
- **The static leg:** its low-side FET is on for the whole sector.

So, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{rms}^2 \frac{R_{ds}(T_j)}{n_p}$$

Phase-current ripple at 48 kHz with typical motor inductance (≥10 µH) is small, so I_rms ≈ I.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

| Case | P_cond per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| n_p = 1 | 2 × 6400 × 2.25 mΩ = **28.8 W** | 115 W |
| n_p = 2 | **14.4 W** | 57.6 W |

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

Each phase leg spends 1/3 of the electrical cycle as the static low side, 1/3 as the PWM leg, and 1/3 floating.

$$P_{LS} \approx I^2 R_{ds}\left[\tfrac13 + \tfrac13(1-D)\right], \qquad P_{HS} \approx I^2 R_{ds}\,\tfrac{D}{3}$$

At D → 1 (full-throttle burst), with I²R = 14.4 W:

- Each high-side FET ≈ 4.8 W.
- Each low-side FET ≈ 4.8 W.
- Average across the 6 FETs = 28.8 / 6 = 4.8 W.

**Conclusion:** the low-side FETs are the hotspots at mid-throttle. Use 4.8 W per FET at burst as the design value.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Hard switching occurs only in the PWM leg: one high-side turn-on and one turn-off per PWM period. The low-side transition is "soft" (ZVS-like) because the body diode clamps during dead time.

### 2.1 V-I overlap

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = \tfrac12 (25.2)(80)(30\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

The transition times depend on the gate driver. Estimate them as t ≈ Q_gd / I_gate. With a weak driver (FD6288-class, ~0.3 A source / 0.6 A sink) and a 40 V part with Q_gd ≈ 8 nC, real t_r is closer to 25–30 ns. **Re-evaluate P_VI with measured edges.** It scales linearly with t_r + t_f.

### 2.2 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.024\ \text{W}$$

Q_rr grows with di/dt and temperature. Use the datasheet value at 125 °C.

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET)

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2 = 40\,\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\,\text{k} \times 2 = 0.038\ \text{W per channel}$$

### 2.6 Channel loss budget at 80 A burst, T_j = 125 °C, n_p = 1

| Loss | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction | 28.80 |
| V-I switching | 1.45 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Q_oss + Q_rr | 0.06 |
| Gate (driver-side) | 0.04 |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, if low-side sensing) | 1.60 |
| **Total per channel** | **≈ 32.6 W** |
| **Total, 4 channels** | **≈ 130 W** |

At 48 kHz, switching losses are only about 7% of the total. **Conduction dominates.** Reducing f_sw buys little, while lowering R_ds or paralleling FETs buys a lot.
