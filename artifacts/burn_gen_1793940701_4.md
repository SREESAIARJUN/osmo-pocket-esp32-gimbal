# STM32H743 Nano AIO (4-in-1 ESC) — Architecture Model, Job #35

## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility

**Ground rules this document assumes:**

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each motor has its own ESC MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32) driving a 3-phase gate driver. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to those four MCUs. The 48 kHz figure is the ESC's PWM frequency, not the H743's.
2. **"Nano" (20×20 mm, M2 mounting) cannot hold an H743, 4 ESC MCUs, 24 MOSFETs and bulk capacitance on one PCB.** The architecture below is a **two-board stack**: an FC board and an ESC board, joined by an 8-pin connector.
3. **80 A per motor is a burst rating only (≤1 s).** It is not thermally sustainable on a 4 cm² board. The model below shows this quantitatively. Continuous ratings for honest 20×20 designs are about 25–35 A.
4. **Component values are representative placeholders.** All parameters are listed so you can substitute datasheet values.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

With 6-step trapezoidal commutation and complementary (synchronous) PWM, two phases conduct at any instant. In each switching period one leg PWMs (HS/LS complementary) and the other holds its LS on. The bus current path therefore always passes through **two FET channels in series**, independent of duty:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 \cdot \left(2R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + 2R_{cu}\right)$$

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right]$$

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_FET path (2·I²R) | P_copper | **P_cond / motor** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W | 3.84 W | 16.6 W |
| 100 °C | 1.34 mΩ | 17.1 W | 3.84 W | 21.0 W |
| 125 °C | 1.45 mΩ | 18.6 W | 3.84 W | 22.4 W |

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each FET is active for 120° of every 360° electrical cycle. Within its window:

- The PWMing leg's HS conducts for duty D and its LS for 1−D.
- The held leg's LS conducts 100%.

Averaged over commutation, each phase's FET pair carries 1/3 of the total loss. The worst single FET (the LS on a held leg) dissipates instantaneously:

$$P_{FET,peak} = I^2 R_{DS(on)} = 6400 \times 1.45\,\text{m}\Omega = 9.3\ \text{W}$$

### 1.4 Electro-thermal fixed point

Because R_DS(on) rises with T_j, solve iteratively:

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}(T_j)\cdot R_{th,JC} \quad\Rightarrow\quad T_j = \frac{T_{pcb} + I^2R_{25}(1-25\alpha)R_{th}}{1 - I^2R_{25}\alpha R_{th}}$$

With R_th,JC = 1.5 K/W: I²R₂₅αR_th = 6400 × 1e-3 × 0.0045 × 1.5 = 0.043. This is less than 1, so the loop is stable (no thermal runaway at the junction level). The board level is what limits you (§3).
