# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #36): Power Stage, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope and Feasibility

Three points shape everything below.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** On a 4-in-1 board, each motor channel needs its own ESC MCU running the commutation firmware. This design assumes AM32 on an AT32F421 or STM32G071, one per channel. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to those MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. The 48 kHz switching frequency is an ESC firmware parameter, not an H743 function.
2. **80 A burst on a nano board is a transient rating, not a thermal steady state.** Section 3 shows the board's steady-state limit is about 25–30 A per channel. The 80 A figure is only valid for bursts of roughly 1–2 s, gated by an energy budget.
3. **All four channels at 80 A means 320 A at the battery.** No nano-format input connector or pigtail survives that. In practice the battery draw is limited by the pack and the XT60/XT90 connector. The 80 A rating is per channel and asymmetric, for example during a yaw-punch transient.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step trapezoidal drive, two phase legs are active in each sector. With synchronous rectification, exactly one FET in each active leg carries the full phase current at every instant:

- **PWM-on:** HS(A) and LS(B) conduct.
- **PWM-off:** LS(A) (synchronous freewheel) and LS(B) conduct.

So at any instant, two FETs carry I_ph.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.0060\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_DS = 1.2 mΩ × 1.51 = **1.81 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Per-channel conduction loss

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) + I_{ph}^2\,R_{Cu,path}$$

The copper path includes the phase pad, the bus pour between the bus capacitor and the leg, and the solder joints. For 8-layer 2 oz construction with about 8 mm of path through ~5 mm-wide pours on 4 parallel layers:

$$R_{Cu} \approx \frac{\rho L}{w\,t\,n} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot 8\times10^{-3}}{5\times10^{-3}\cdot 70\times10^{-6}\cdot 4} \approx 0.10\ \text{m}\Omega$$

Adding about 0.15 mΩ for vias and pads gives R_Cu ≈ 0.25 mΩ.

**At 80 A, T_j = 110 °C:**

$$P_{cond} = 2(80^2)(1.81\text{m}) + 80^2(0.25\text{m}) = 23.2 + 1.6 = \mathbf{24.8\ W/channel}$$

Each conducting FET dissipates 11.6 W.

### 1.4 Electro-thermal feedback

R_DS rises with T_j, so P and T_j must be iterated:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_{ref} + P(T_j^{(k)})\cdot Z_{th}$$

This converges only if

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\cdot Z_{th} < 1,$$

where ∂P/∂T_j = I²·R_DS,25·α = 6400 × 1.2 mΩ × 0.006 = 0.046 W/K per FET.

- **Junction-to-board path:** 0.046 × 1.5 K/W ≈ 0.07, so it is stable.
- **Steady state through board-to-air** (R ≈ 8 K/W, Section 3.3): with 2 FETs per channel, 4 channels, and 3 phases sharing the board, the loop gain approaches 1. **Thermal runaway is therefore possible at sustained 80 A.** This is the formal reason 80 A must be burst-limited.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (f_sw = 48 kHz)

Only the PWM'd leg switches hard: one HS/LS pair per channel. The other active leg is static for the whole commutation sector.

### 2.1 Voltage-current overlap (hard-switched HS FET)

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}+Q_{gs2}}{I_{G}}$$

- Turn-on: (6 + 3 nC) / 1.0 A ≈ 9 ns. Adding gate-loop inductance and PCB parasitics gives **t_r ≈ 15 ns** (design value).
- Turn-off at 1.5 A sink gives **t_f ≈ 12 ns**.

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = \tfrac12(25.2)(80)(27\text{n})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.31\ W}$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance (HS turn-on discharges its own C_oss and charges the LS C_oss)

$$P_{oss} = 2\cdot\tfrac12 C_{oss,er} V^2 f_{sw} = (1.0\text{n})(635)(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.03\ W}$$

### 2.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction (LS FET, two edges per period)

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I_{ph}\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.4 Reverse recovery (LS body diode, dumped into the HS FET)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$
