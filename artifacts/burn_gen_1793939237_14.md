# STM32H743 + 4-in-1 ESC Stack: Engineering Model

**Job #41, Rev A (preliminary design basis)**

---

## 0. Key Findings

Three findings affect the architecture before any numbers do:

1. **The H743 should not drive the power stage directly.** ArduPilot bi-directional DShot assumes a separate ESC MCU that decodes DShot and returns eRPM. This design therefore uses:
   - **STM32H743** as the flight controller running ArduPilot.
   - **4× AT32F421 ESC MCUs** running AM32, which supports bi-directional DShot and a configurable 48 kHz PWM.

   Running FOC for four motors on the H743 is possible, but it is not an ArduPilot-supported topology.

2. **"Nano" and 80 A burst per motor conflict.** Section 3 shows roughly 60 W of dissipation at full burst. A 20×20 mm board cannot absorb that.
   - The design basis is a **30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz** stack.
   - A 20×20 variant should be rated for about 40 A burst.

3. **All semiconductor parameters below are placeholders.** They represent a 40 V, ~0.7 mΩ class FET. Replace them with datasheet values from the selected part, and re-run the model before layout freeze.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, current always flows through exactly two FET positions:
- the PWM leg (high-side for duty D, then its low-side complement for 1−D), and
- the static low-side.

The model also includes temperature dependence of R_ds(on):

$$
P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right], \;\; \alpha \approx 0.005\ \mathrm{K^{-1}}
$$

At 125 °C, R_ds(on) = 1.05 mΩ, so:

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.05\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{13.4\ W}
$$

This is the dominant term. The positive feedback through R_ds(on)(T_j) is handled by iteration in §3.4.

### 2.2 Switching losses (48 kHz)

Only the PWM leg hard-switches.

| Mechanism | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·20 ns·48 k | 0.968 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_SD·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·100 ns·48 k | 0.307 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 50 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.060 W |
| Output capacitance | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw | 0.036 W |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_drv·f_sw | 0.048 W* |
| **P_sw total** | | **≈ 1.42 W** |

\*Gate-drive loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FETs.

Frequency scaling:
- Switching loss is linear in f_sw. At 24 kHz it would be about 0.7 W.
- Conduction loss is independent of frequency.

So 48 kHz costs roughly 0.7 W per channel at burst. That is acceptable in exchange for lower ripple current and quieter motors.

### 2.3 Shunt and copper losses

| Element | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Shunt | I²·R_shunt = 6400 × 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| Copper (phase + bus) | ≈0.3 mΩ effective | ≈1.9 W |

### 2.4 Totals at 80 A burst

| Scope | Value |
|---|---|
| Per channel | 13.4 + 1.42 + 1.6 + 1.9 ≈ **18.3 W** |
| Board (×4) | **≈ 73 W** |
| Board (realistic: ~2 motors at peak, 2 at ~60 A) | **≈ 60 W** (used as P_burst below) |

The hottest single FET is the PWM leg: 6.7 W conduction + 1.3 W switching ≈ **8 W**.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Thermal network (two-node RC)

```
P_FET ──[R_θJC 1.0]──[R_θ,local 3.0 (thermal vias + spreading)]──● T_board
                                                                  │
P_total ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────● C_th,board ──[R_θ,BA]── T_amb
```

### 3.2 Board thermal capacitance (30.5×30.5 mm)

| Material | Calculation | C_th |
|---|---|---|
| FR4 | 1488 mm³ × 1.85 g/cm³ = 2.75 g × 1.1 J/gK | 3.0 J/K |
| Copper | 8 layers × 70 µm × 70 % fill × 930 mm² ≈ 0.36 cm³ = 3.2 g × 0.385 J/gK | 1.24 J/K |
| FETs, caps, solder | Estimate | ≈0.4 J/K |
| **Total C_th** | | **≈ 4.6 J/K** |

- R_θ,BA (board to ambient in prop wash, ~8 m/s): ≈ 6 K/W (to be validated in a wind tunnel)
- τ = R·C ≈ **28 s**

### 3.3 Burst-duration limit

Board temperature during a burst:

$$
T_{board}(t) = T_a + P\,R_{\theta BA}\left(1-e^{-t/\tau}\right)
$$

Steady state is impossible: 60 W × 6 K/W = 360 K rise. The design is therefore burst-limited. Setting the limits:
- T_a = 40 °C
- T_board,max = 105 °C (ΔT = 65 K)

$$
t_{max} = -\tau\ln\left(1-\frac{\Delta T}{P R}\right) = -28\ln(1-0.181) \approx \mathbf{5.6\ s}
$$

### 3.4 Junction temperature

Local junction rise above the board, which settles in roughly 0.5 s:

$$
\Delta T_{JB} = 8\ \mathrm{W} \times (1.0 + 3.0) = 32\ \mathrm{K} \;\Rightarrow\; T_j \approx 137\ ^\circ\mathrm{C}\ \text{at end of burst}
$$

This is below the 150 °C limit but leaves only 13 K of margin. Iterating R_ds(on) at 137 °C:
- R_ds(on) rises to 1.09 mΩ.
- Conduction loss on the hot FET rises by about 0.3 W.
- T_j settles near **138 °C**, which converges.

**Requirements derived from this:**
- **Burst rating: 80 A for ≤5 s at a 10 % duty cycle.** Continuous rating is about 35–40 A per motor; compute it from the steady-state solve at R_θ,BA = 6 K/W.
- **AM32 thermal protection:** enable the NTC with derate starting at 95 °C board and cutback at 110 °C.
