# STM32H743 AIO + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Budget, and Bidirectional DShot600 DMA Plan

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

I don't have the Job #48 requirements package, BOM, or layout. Every numeric result below comes from the stated assumptions in §0.2. Replace them with your datasheet values and the model carries through. Each assumption is labeled **[A#]** so it can be traced.

### 0.1 Architecture Corrections

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.**
- ArduPilot is flight-controller firmware. It cannot run sensorless six-step or FOC commutation for four motors.
- The required architecture is H743 (ArduPilot) → 4× DShot600 bidirectional → 4× ESC MCUs. Typical ESC MCUs are AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or BLHeli_32-class firmware.
- Each ESC MCU drives a 3-phase gate driver and six MOSFETs.
- The "48 kHz switching" is the ESC firmware's PWM frequency. It is not an H743 timer.

**2. "Nano" and "80A burst" conflict on thermal grounds.**
- §3 shows roughly 60 W of dissipation at a full 4×80 A burst.
- On a 20×20 or 25×25 mm board, that is only survivable for seconds, not continuously.
- Treat 80 A as a time-limited rating. Specify it as a duration at a given ambient and airflow.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-Step Conduction Topology

In each 60° commutation sector, two phases conduct:
- **PWM leg:** the high-side switch conducts for duty D. The low side of the same leg synchronously rectifies for (1−D).
- **Return leg:** the low-side switch is on for the full sector.

The current path therefore always contains exactly two switch R_DS(on) in series:

$$P_{cond,FET}(T) = I_{rms}^2\,R(T)\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R(T)$$

Notably, this is independent of D under synchronous rectification.

### 1.2 Ripple Correction

With motor phase inductance L_ph (line-to-line inductance L_LL = 2·L_ph):

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{f_{sw}\,L_{LL}},\qquad I_{rms}^2 = I_{avg}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}$$

Example: L_LL = 20 µH (a typical 2207–2810 class motor) and D = 0.5:
- ΔI_pp = 25.2·0.25 / (48 000·20 µH) = **6.6 A**
- The correction term is ΔI²/12 = 3.6 A², which is +0.06 % on 80². It is negligible at this current.
- At light load this term matters, which is one reason to keep 48 kHz rather than 24 kHz.

### 1.3 Temperature-Coupled Resistance

Use a hot junction temperature of T_j = 110 °C:

R(110) = 0.70·[1 + 0.0055·85] = 0.70·1.4675 = **1.027 mΩ**

The conduction losses per ESC channel at 80 A are then:

$$P_{cond,FET} = 2·80^2·1.027\text{ mΩ} = 13.15\text{ W}$$

$$P_{cond,Cu} = 2·80^2·0.25\text{ mΩ} = 3.20\text{ W}\ \text{(dissipated in board copper)}$$

**Takeaway:** copper resistance contributes about 20 % of conduction loss. Layout is a first-order loss term, not a detail.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Hard-Switching Transitions

Only the PWM-leg high-side switch hard-switches. The synchronous low side switches with near-zero voltage after the dead-time diode conduction.

The transition time is dominated by the Miller plateau. With plateau voltage V_pl ≈ 4.5 V, driver supply 10 V, and R_g,total ≈ 4.7 + 1.2 (internal) + ~3 Ω (driver) ≈ 9 Ω:

- I_g,on ≈ (10 − 4.5)/9 ≈ 0.61 A, so t_r ≈ Q_gd/I_g,on = 9 nC/0.61 A ≈ **15 ns**
- I_g,off ≈ 4.5/9 ≈ 0.50 A, so t_f ≈ **18 ns**
- Add about 50 % for the Q_gs2 portion and loop inductance: **t_r + t_f ≈ 50 ns**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5·25.2·80·50\text{ ns}·48\text{ k} = 2.42\text{ W}$$

### 2.2 Secondary Switching Terms

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Output capacitance | Q_oss·V_bus·f_sw | 35 nC·25.2·48k = 0.042 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V_bus·f_sw | 40 nC·25.2·48k = 0.048 W |
| Dead-time diode | V_SD·I·2t_dt·f_sw | 0.8·80·200 ns·48k = **0.614 W** |
| Gate drive (2 switching FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_drv·f_sw | 2·55 nC·10·48k = 0.053 W (dissipated in driver and R_g) |

The dead-time diode loss is the largest secondary term. Trimming t_dt from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel, but only if shoot-through margin allows it.

### 2.3 Per-Channel and Board Totals at 80 A Burst

| Loss | W per channel | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (hot) | 13.15 | 52.6 |
| Copper conduction | 3.20 | 12.8 |
| V·I switching | 2.42 | 9.7 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 | 2.5 |
| Q_oss + Q_rr + gate | 0.14 | 0.6 |
| **Total** | **19.5** | **78.2 W** |

- The share deposited in the FETs is about 16.2 W per channel, or 65 W total.
- For comparison, mechanical output is about 4 × 80 A × ~18 V effective ≈ 5.8 kW, so efficiency is about 98.7 %.
