# STM32H743 AIO "Nano" FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Architecture

This is a reference design analysis, not a released design package. The math is complete and reproducible. Device parameters are representative values that you must replace with your chosen datasheet numbers. Two architectural points come first, because they change the model.

### Architectural corrections

1. **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz motor PWM.** In an ArduPilot AIO, the H743 runs the flight stack and emits DShot600. Each ESC channel has its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32 (AT32F421 or STM32G071 class), and that MCU generates the 48 kHz 6-step PWM. ArduPilot has no firmware that drives MOSFET bridges directly from the FC MCU. The model below uses that split.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano board is a short transient rating, not a thermal operating point.** As shown in §3, all four channels at 80 A dissipate about 84 W into roughly 2.5 J/K of board, which gives about 2 seconds before limits are reached. The datasheet and marketing language should state the burst duration explicitly.

## 2. Electrical loss model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction loss

**Current path.** With 6-step commutation and complementary (synchronous) PWM, current always flows through exactly two MOSFETs in series:
- one high-side FET (or its complementary low-side during the PWM off-time), and
- one low-side FET held on for the whole commutation step.

**General form.** The two legs share the conduction depending on duty D:

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I_{ph,rms}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

**Ripple correction.**

$$I_{ph,rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L\,f_{sw}}$$

The worst case is D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\,\mu\text{H} \times 48\,\text{kHz}} = 6.56\ \text{A}$$

This gives ΔI²/12 = 3.6 A², which is negligible against 6400 A².
