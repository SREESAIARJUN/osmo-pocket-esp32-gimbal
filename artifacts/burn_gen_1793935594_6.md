# Job #31: STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 80A 4-in-1 ESC Architecture Model

## 0. Architecture Clarifications

Three assumptions shape everything that follows:

1. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In a 4-in-1 ESC, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32) and a 3-phase gate driver. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bi-directional DShot600 to those MCUs. The 48 kHz PWM is an ESC-firmware parameter (e.g. AM32 `PWM_FREQUENCY`), not an ArduPilot parameter.
2. **"80A burst" is per motor channel.** A 4×80A simultaneous burst means 320A battery current. That exceeds XT60/XT90 practice, typical pack C-ratings, and the copper capacity of a 20×20 or 30×30 board. I model 80A as a per-channel burst and treat 4×80A as a short-transient worst case.
3. **Assumed baseline values.** Change these and re-run the equations.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S full) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| MOSFET (40V, 3.3×3.3 DFN class) | R_ds(on),25 | 1.0 mΩ |
| R_ds temperature coefficient | α | ≈0.0055 /K → ×1.5 at 100 °C |
| Rise / fall time (driver-limited) | t_r, t_f | 15 ns / 15 ns |
| Dead time | t_dt | 100 ns per edge |
| Body diode V_SD | V_SD | 0.8 V |
| Reverse recovery charge | Q_rr | 50 nC |
| Total gate charge | Q_g | 50 nC @ V_GS = 10 V |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology
Assume trapezoidal (6-step) commutation with synchronous rectification. At any instant, two phase legs carry the motor current I:
- One leg is the PWM leg. Its high-side conducts for duty D and its low-side for (1–D), complementary.
- The other leg is the static leg. Its low-side (or high-side) is on 100%.

So at every instant, exactly **two FETs** carry I. That makes the instantaneous per-motor conduction loss independent of D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

### 1.2 At 80A, T_j = 100 °C
$$R_{ds}=1.0\,\text{m}\Omega\times1.41\approx1.5\,\text{m}\Omega \text{ (datasheet-curve rounded)}$$
$$P_{cond,motor}=2\times80^2\times1.5\times10^{-3}=\mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET average
Each leg is active for 2/3 of an electrical cycle and shares its conduction between high-side and low-side. Over the six FETs of one channel:
$$\bar P_{cond,FET}\approx \frac{19.2}{6}=3.2\ \text{W}$$

The instantaneous peak in one FET is higher: I²R = 9.6 W while that FET is conducting. This matters for Z_th at timescales below a commutation period. At low eRPM (startup or high-load bogging), a single FET can carry current for milliseconds.

### 1.4 Electrothermal feedback (solve iteratively)
$$T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}(T_j)\cdot R_{th,j\text{-}pcb}$$

The loop is stable when the following condition holds:
$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}R_{th}=I^2R_{ds,25}\,\alpha\,R_{th}<1$$

For 80A, 1 mΩ, α=0.0055 and R_th=15 K/W: 6400×0.001×0.0055×15 ≈ 0.53. This is stable, but the margin shrinks quickly with poor layout. At R_th > 28 K/W you approach thermal runaway.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. That gives one hard-switched transition pair per PWM period per motor.

**Hard-switching (V–I overlap):**
$$P_{sw}=\tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}=\tfrac12(25.2)(80)(30\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz})=\mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (two dead-times per period):**
$$P_{dt}=V_{SD}\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw}=0.8\times80\times200\text{ ns}\times48\text{ kHz}=\mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**
$$P_{rr}=V_{bus}Q_{rr}f_{sw}=25.2\times50\text{ nC}\times48\text{ kHz}=\mathbf{0.06\ W}$$
In practice, Q_rr also adds turn-on current overshoot to P_sw, worth roughly +10–20%.

**Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET):**
$$P_g = Q_gV_{GS}f_{sw}\times 2\ \text{(switching FETs)} = 50\text{nC}\times10\times48\text{k}\times2=0.048\ \text{W}$$

**C_oss loss:** ½·C_oss·V²·f. Assuming C_oss ≈ 1.5 nF gives about 0.05 W, which is negligible.

### 2.1 Loss budget per channel @ 80A, 100 °C

| Component | Watts |
|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.2 |
| Switching (incl. ~15% Q_rr overshoot) | 1.67 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Gate + C_oss | 0.10 |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, if present) | 1.6 |
| **Total per channel** | **≈ 23.2 W** |
| **4 channels simultaneous** | **≈ 93 W** |

**Insight:** Conduction dominates at 88%. Raising f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz roughly doubles switching losses but adds only ~1.1 W per channel. The 48 kHz choice is thermally cheap; R_ds(on) and copper are the levers that matter.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network (per FET, Foster RC to board, lumped board to ambient)

```
P_FET ──[R_jc 1.2 K/W]──┬──[R_c-pcb (solder+vias) ~3 K/W]──┬──[R_spread ~10 K/W]── T_pcb
                       C_j (≈0.02 J/K)                    C_pad (≈0.1 J/K)
T_pcb ──[R_pcb-amb]── T_amb ;   C_board
```

### 3.2 Board-level lumped model
- Board mass, 30×30 mm 4-in-1 with 2 oz copper plus components: ≈10 g. Effective c ≈ 0.6 J/g·K, so **C_board ≈ 6 J/K**.
- Convection under prop wash: h ≈ 50 W/m²K. Both sides give A = 1.8×10⁻³ m², so **R_pcb-amb ≈ 11 K/W**.
- Time constant τ = R·C ≈ **66 s**.

$$\Delta T_{pcb}(t)=P\,R\,(1-e^{-t/\tau})$$

### 3.3 Burst-duration limits (T_amb = 35 °C, target T_pcb ≤ 105 °C, i.e. ΔT ≤ 70 K)

| Scenario | P_total | ΔT at 2 s | ΔT at 5 s | Max burst to ΔT = 70 K |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 channel @ 80A, others hover (~8 W) | ≈ 31 W | 9.2 K | 22 K | ≈ 100 % ss (ΔT_ss = 341 K → t ≈ 15 s) |
| 4 channels @ 80A | ≈ 93 W | 27.5 K | 66 K | **≈ 5.3 s** |
| Adiabatic (no airflow), 4×80A | 93 W | 31 K | 78 K | ≈ 4.5 s |

Derivation of the 4×80A limit:

$$t_{max}=-\tau\ln\!\left(1-\frac{\Delta T_{max}}{PR}\right)=-66\ln(1-\tfrac{70}{1023})\approx 4.7\text{–}5.3\ \text{s}$$

### 3.4 Junction rise above board (per FET, 80A)
- Average: 3.2 W × 14.2 K/W ≈ **45 K**, so T_j ≈ T_pcb + 45 K. With T_pcb = 105 °C, T_j ≈ 150 °C, which is exactly at T_j,max (typically 150–175 °C).
- **Conclusion:** At this FET size, 80A must be a ≤ ~2–3 s burst with T_pcb starting below 70 °C. For sustained 80A, use R_ds ≤ 0.7 mΩ (5×6 PowerPAK/TOLL class), which does not fit a nano footprint. Alternatively, use paralleled FETs.

### 3.5 Copper conduction (often the real limiter)
- Phase trace / plane: 2 oz (70 µm), 8 mm wide, 15 mm long gives R ≈ ρL/(wt) = 1.72e-8×0.015/(0.008×70e-6) ≈ **0.46 mΩ**. That is 2.9 W at 80A, the same order as a FET. Use 4-layer heavy copper with stitched pours on all layers. Better still, use 3 oz outer plus 2 oz inner, which cuts this ~3×.
