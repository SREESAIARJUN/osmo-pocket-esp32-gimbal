## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths per commutation step

In any 60° electrical sector, phase A is PWM'd and phase B is the return path:

- **High-side A** conducts for duty D.
- **Low-side A** conducts for 1−D (synchronous rectification).
- **Low-side B** conducts for 100% of the sector.

Adding the paths gives the instantaneous conduction loss per channel:

$$P_{cond}=I^2R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D+(1-D)] + I^2R_{DS}(T_j)= 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

This is independent of duty cycle. That is the key result for synchronous 6-step drive.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j)=R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 100 °C: R = 1.2 × 1.41 = **1.70 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Ripple contribution

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{DC}^2+\frac{\Delta I^2}{12},\qquad \Delta I=\frac{V_{bus}D(1-D)}{L_{ph\text{-}ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

Worst case is D = 0.5 with L = 20 µH:

- ΔI = 25.2 × 0.25 / (20 µH × 48 kHz) = **6.56 A**
- ΔI²/12 = 3.6 A², which is negligible against 6400 A².

$$P_{cond}=2(80^2)(1.70\,\text{m}\Omega)=\mathbf{21.8\ W\ per\ channel}
