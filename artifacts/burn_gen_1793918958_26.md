## 1. Conduction-Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step complementary PWM

In any 60° commutation sector, phase A is PWM'd and phase B is held low. Phase C floats for BEMF sensing.

- **ON interval (D·T_s):** HS_A → motor → LS_B
- **OFF interval ((1−D)·T_s):** LS_A (synchronous rectifier) → motor → LS_B

So two FETs always carry the full phase current I (ignoring dead time):

$$P_{cond,ch}(t) = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

with R_DS(T_j) = R_DS,25 · [1 + α(T_j − 25)] and α = 0.0045 K⁻¹.

### 1.2 Per-FET distribution over one electrical revolution

Each high-side FET PWM-conducts for 120° (at duty D). Each low-side FET is fully on for 120° and rectifies during the remaining off-time of its own PWM sectors.

| FET | Mean loss over electrical cycle |
|---|---|
| HS_x | (1/3)·D·I²·R_DS |
| LS_x | (1/3)·I²·R_DS + (1/3)(1−D)·I²·R_DS |

Sum over 6 FETs = 2·I²·R_DS ✔.

The worst-case device is the low side at low duty, which approaches (2/3)·I²·R_DS. At an 80 A burst the duty is typically high (D ≈ 0.9), so per-FET losses are roughly balanced at about P_total/6.

The electrical frequency is ≥ 500 Hz above about 15k eRPM. The die thermal time constant (~1–5 ms) partially filters the per-sector pulses, so a ripple term is added in §4.
