**Scope:** loss model for 80 A burst, 48 kHz switching analysis, ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 timer/DMA plan, reference netlists, and thermal budget.

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Flag

On an AIO board, the STM32H743 does **not** drive the MOSFETs. The signal chain is:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──DShot600 bidir──► 4× ESC MCU (AT32F421 / GD32E230, AM32 or BLHeli_32)
                                              │ 48 kHz complementary PWM
                                              ▼
                                        3-phase gate driver ──► 6× N-MOSFET per motor
```

The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs. The H743's job is DShot generation, eRPM capture, and the harmonic notch.

**Feasibility warning:** A 20×20 mm "nano" 4-in-1 is realistically rated around 35–45 A continuous per channel. 80 A is achievable only as a **short burst**. Section 3 quantifies this: about 1.5 s with all four motors at 80 A, and about 6 s with one motor. Market the rating as "80 A burst (≤1 s)", not continuous.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

In 6-step operation, one phase leg PWMs complementarily (high-side duty D, low-side 1−D). A second leg holds its low-side FET on statically. The current path always passes through exactly two FET channels.

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2 I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

With R_ds(T_j) = R_ds,25 × [1 + α(T_j − 25)], where α ≈ 0.005 /K gives the ×1.5 factor at 125 °C.

Worked values at 80 A:

- Cold (25 °C): 2 × 6400 × 1.0 mΩ = **12.8 W**
- Hot (125 °C): 2 × 6400 × 1.5 mΩ = **19.2 W**

**Worst single FET:** The statically-on low-side FET carries I²R continuously, which is 9.6 W at T_j = 125 °C. The PWM'd high-side FET carries 9.6·D W plus all switching loss.

### 2.2 Switching Loss at 48 kHz

**Hard-switching V·I overlap** (high-side FET only):

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}
$$
$$
= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = 0.968\ \text{W}
$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = 50\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}
$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$
P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V f_{sw} = 0.036\ \text{W}
$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two edges per period, borne by the low-side FET):

$$
P_{dt} = V_f I (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.614\ \text{W}
$$

**Gate drive** (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g, not in the FET):

$$
P_g = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 60\,\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.029\ \text{W per switching FET}
$$

### 2.3 Channel Total at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

| Loss term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.20 |
| V·I switching | 0.97 |
| Reverse recovery | 0.06 |
| C_oss | 0.04 |
| Dead time | 0.61 |
| Gate (driver) | 0.06 |
| **Total per motor** | **≈ 20.9 W** |
| **Four motors** | **≈ 84 W** |

Two design conclusions follow from this table:

- **Conduction dominates (92%).** Spend effort on R_ds(on) by paralleling FETs or using a larger package. Do not spend it on faster switching.
- **48 kHz is reasonable.** Raising to 96 kHz adds only about 1.6 W per channel. At 24 kHz, the motor current ripple penalty outweighs the switching saving.

**Efficiency at burst:** P_out ≈ 25.2 × 80 = 2016 W, so η ≈ 1 − 20.9/2016 ≈ **98.96 %**. The problem is power density, not efficiency.

### 2.4 Bus Capacitor Ripple

$$
I_{C,rms} = I\sqrt{D(1-D)} \rightarrow 40\ \text{A}_{rms} \text{ at } D = 0.5 \text{ (single channel)}
$$

The ESC MCUs' PWM is unsynchronized, so the four channels add roughly in RMS: about 40·√4 ≈ 80 A_rms worst case.

Recommendations:
- Use ≥ 4× 22 µF 50 V X7R 1210 MLCCs near the bridges. MLCC capacitance derates heavily at DC bias.
- Use one off-board low-ESR electrolytic (≥ 1000 µF / 35 V) at the battery pads. This is mandatory.
