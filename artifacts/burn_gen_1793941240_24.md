# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 80A ESC: Systems Architecture (Job #27)

## 0. Architecture Baseline and Assumptions

Several premises shape the model, so they come first.

**Assumptions**

| Parameter | Value | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal | Typical for 80A-class |
| Board | 30.5×30.5 mm mount, 36×36 mm outline, 8-layer, 3 oz outer / 2 oz inner | See note below |
| MOSFET | 40 V N-ch, PDFN 3.3×3.3, R<sub>DS(on)</sub> = 1.0 mΩ max @ 25 °C, V<sub>GS</sub> = 10 V | Representative class |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal, complementary (synchronous) PWM | AM32 / BLHeli_32 behaviour |
| f<sub>PWM</sub> | 48 kHz | Per brief |
| Rating | 80 A per channel, phase current, burst | Per brief |

**Architecture notes**

1. **The H743 does not drive the power stage.** ArduPilot has no in-tree BLDC commutation firmware for the H743. The correct topology is an H743 running ArduPilot as the flight controller, sending bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs (e.g. AT32F421 running AM32). Each ESC MCU drives a 3-phase gate driver. The model below follows that split.
2. **"Nano" cannot be 20×20 at 80 A.** A 20×20 board cannot fit 24 power FETs with adequate copper, so I've assumed the 30.5 mm pattern. The thermal math in §3 shows that even this board is strictly burst-limited, measured in seconds.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \mathrm{K^{-1}}$$

At T<sub>j</sub> = 125 °C: R<sub>DS</sub> = 1.0 × 1.45 = **1.45 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

At any instant, two phases are active and the third floats (BEMF sensing).

- During the PWM on-time D, current flows through HS(A) → motor → LS(B).
- During the off-time (1−D), it freewheels through LS(A) (synchronous) → motor → LS(B).

In either state, exactly **two FETs carry I<sub>m</sub>**, so the bridge conduction loss is independent of duty:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_m^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each phase is active for 2/3 of the electrical period, half of that sourcing and half sinking.

- High-side FET: $P_{HS} = I_m^2 R_{DS}\,\frac{D}{3}$
- Low-side FET: $P_{LS} = I_m^2 R_{DS}\,\frac{1}{3}\left[(1-D) + 1\right]$

The worst case is the low-side FET at low duty with high current, which happens during a hard punch-out from low throttle. Upper bound:

$$P_{LS,max} = \frac{2}{3}I_m^2 R_{DS}$$

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A, T<sub>j</sub> = 125 °C

| Quantity | Expression | Value |
|---|---|---|
| I<sub>m</sub>² | 80² | 6400 A² |
| P<sub>cond</sub> per channel | 2·6400·1.45 mΩ | **18.6 W** |
| P<sub>LS,max</sub> (worst FET) | ⅔·6400·1.45 mΩ | 6.2 W |
| P<sub>HS</sub> at D = 0.9 | 6400·1.45 mΩ·0.3 | 2.8 W |
| 4-channel conduction | 4 × 18.6 | **74.4 W** |

### 1.5 Copper and shunt losses (often ignored)

**Phase trace.** Assume 3 oz copper (t ≈ 105 µm), 4 mm wide, 8 mm long, paralleled on 3 layers:

$$R_{tr} = \frac{\rho L}{wt\,n} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot 0.008}{0.004\cdot105\times10^{-6}\cdot 3}\approx 0.11\ \mathrm{m\Omega}$$

At 100 °C (+30 %) this is 0.14 mΩ, giving 6400 × 0.14 mΩ × 2 traces ≈ **1.8 W/channel**.

**Shunt.** Assume a 0.25 mΩ shunt in the low-side return. With I<sub>bat</sub> ≈ D·I<sub>m</sub> ≤ 80 A, P = 1.6 W worst case. Many 4-in-1 boards use one shunt for the whole board instead.

**DC bus copper.** Pads to bridge, ~0.2 mΩ at 4×80 A peak (assuming correlated full throttle): roughly 20 W board-wide. This is a significant term. Thick bus bars or soldered copper strap on the battery pads are strongly recommended.
