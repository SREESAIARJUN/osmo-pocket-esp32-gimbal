## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

The STM32H743 is the **flight controller**. It does not commutate the motors. A production-grade 4-in-1 design uses **four dedicated ESC MCUs** (e.g. AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071 running AM32) driving gate drivers and MOSFET half-bridges. The H743 talks to them over bidirectional DShot600. All of the models below follow this partition.

**Feasibility flag:** "80A burst" on a nano form factor (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) is only valid as a **short transient**. The analysis below derives the maximum burst duration rather than assuming 80A is sustainable. Expect continuous ratings of roughly 25–35A per channel on this footprint.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation topology
In 6-step trapezoidal drive (AM32 default) with synchronous rectification:
- At any instant, two phases conduct: one high-side FET and one low-side FET carry I_ph.
- During PWM off-time, the complementary low-side FET of the PWM leg carries the freewheel current.

The current path always contains exactly **two FETs in series**, whatever the duty cycle D. Conduction loss per channel is therefore duty-independent to first order:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_J) + I_{ph}^2 R_{cu}$$

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_J) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_J - 25)\right]$$

At T_J = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.1 mΩ × (1 + 0.0055 × 100) = **1.705 mΩ**

### 1.3 Burst numbers (80A)

| Term | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (2 FETs, hot) | 2 × 80² × 1.705e-3 | **21.8 W** |
| Copper conduction | 80² × 0.5e-3 | **3.2 W** |
| **P_cond,ch** | | **25.0 W** |

### 1.4 Thermal runaway check
Conduction loss rises with temperature. The loop is stable only if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_J}\,R_{\theta} < 1 \quad\Rightarrow\quad 2 I^2 R_{25}\alpha\,R_{\theta} < 1$$

- At 80A: 2 × 6400 × 1.1e-3 × 0.0055 = 0.0774 W/K.
- Runaway therefore occurs if R_θ,total > **12.9 K/W**.
- A nano board quadrant with only prop-wash cooling sits at around 8–10 K/W.

This leaves very little margin. **80A cannot be a steady state on this board.**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. The high-side FET switches hard and the low-side FET switches soft with body-diode commutation.

### 2.1 Hard-switching (V-I overlap)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I_{ph}\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

= 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40e-9 × 48,000 = **1.94 W**

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction
There are two dead-time intervals per PWM period:

$$P_{dt} = V_F\,I_{ph}\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f_{sw}$$

= 0.8 × 80 × 400e-9 × 48,000 = **1.23 W**

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = V_{bus}\,Q_{rr}\,f_{sw}$$

= 25.2 × 50e-9 × 48,000 = **0.06 W**

### 2.4 Output capacitance (C_oss ≈ 2 nF effective)

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2}C_{oss}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw}$$

= 0.5 × 2e-9 × 635 × 48,000 = **0.03 W**

### 2.5 Gate drive
This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FETs. With 2 FETs switching per channel:

$$P_{g} = Q_g\,V_{GS}\,f_{sw}\times 2$$

= 50e-9 × 10 × 48,000 × 2 = **0.048 W**

### 2.6 Switching summary per channel
P_sw,total ≈ **3.26 W**

**Design note:** Switching loss is about 12% of the total at burst, so conduction dominates. Raising f_sw to 96 kHz for smoother low-throttle behavior adds about 3.2 W per channel at burst. Keep 48 kHz, or use AM32 variable PWM.
