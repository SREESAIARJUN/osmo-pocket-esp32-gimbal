## 0. Architectural Clarifications and Assumptions

These points change the math, so they come first.

**1. Two-processor architecture is mandatory.** ArduPilot does not run ESC commutation. The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and generates bi-directional DShot600. Each motor channel (or one quad-capable MCU) runs ESC firmware such as AM32 or BLHeli_32, which handles the 48 kHz PWM. In this document:
- **FC MCU:** STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100) or VIH6 (BGA, for nano size).
- **ESC MCUs:** 4× AT32F421K8U7 (QFN32) running AM32. This is the most common open-firmware choice.

**2. "Nano" vs. 80 A burst is a physical conflict.** On a 20×20 mm stack, 80 A per channel is not achievable. Even 30.5×30.5 mm only manages it for a few seconds. I model the 30.5×30.5 case and quantify the burst time limit in §2.5. If this is truly 20×20, derate to roughly 35–45 A burst.

**3. MOSFET parameters are assumed.** No part was specified, so I use a representative 40 V, 5×6 mm (or 3.3×3.3 mm) trench FET. Substitute datasheet values into the equations; every result below is parametric.

| Parameter | Symbol | Assumed value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S full) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Burst phase current | I_pk | 80 A |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| R_DS(on) @ 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | R_ds25 | 0.90 mΩ |
| R_DS(on) temperature multiplier @ T_j = 125 °C | k_T | 1.5 |
| Total gate charge | Q_g | 60 nC |
| Gate–drain (Miller) charge | Q_gd | 10 nC |

## 1. Commutation Model

The model assumes trapezoidal six-step drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM, which is the AM32 default.

In any commutation sector:
- **Leg A (PWM leg):** the high side conducts for duty D, and the low side synchronously rectifies for (1−D).
- **Leg B (return leg):** the low side stays on continuously.
- **Leg C:** floating, used for back-EMF sensing.

Two consequences follow:
- Current always flows through exactly **two FET channels in series**, regardless of D.
- Only **one half-bridge switches** at any instant.

## 2. Thermal Conduction Losses (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Junction-temperature-corrected resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

$$R_{ds}(125^\circ C) = 0.90 \times 1.5 = 1.35\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 2.2 Per-FET conduction loss

During a 120° conduction sector, each FET carries the phase current. Assuming a flat-top trapezoidal current (I_rms ≈ I_pk within the sector), the losses are:

**High side, PWM leg:**
$$P_{HS} = D \cdot I^2 R_{ds}$$

**Synchronous low side, PWM leg:**
$$P_{LS,sync} = (1-D) \cdot I^2 R_{ds}$$

**Return-leg low side:**
$$P_{LS,ret} = I^2 R_{ds}$$

**Channel total.** This is independent of D because complementary PWM always keeps 2 channels in the path:
$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.35\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{17.28\ W}$$

The worst single FET is the return-leg low side at 8.64 W during its active sector. Averaged over a full electrical revolution, each FET conducts 1/3 of the time, giving about 5.76 W per FET.

### 2.3 PCB copper conduction loss

For 2 oz outer layers plus 2 oz inner layers (4-layer stack, 70 µm each):

$$R_\square = \frac{\rho_{Cu}}{t} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}}{70\times10^{-6}} = 0.246\ \text{m}\Omega/\square$$

Assume each phase path is about 4 squares split across 3 paralleled layers, and multiply by 2 phases in the path:

$$R_{Cu} \approx 2 \times \frac{4 \times 0.246}{3} = 0.66\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{Cu,ch} = 80^2 \times 0.66\times10^{-3} \times 1.2\ (\text{temp}) \approx \mathbf{5.0\ W}$$

Copper loss is about 30% of FET conduction loss at this current density. Battery pads, the DC bus, and the phase pads must be heavily copper-poured with via stitching (≥ 0.3 mm vias, about 1 A/via budget for bursts).

## 3. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Losses are computed per channel.

### 3.1 Transition (V–I overlap) loss

The Miller plateau sets the transition time:
$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,src}} = \frac{10\,\text{nC}}{1.0\,\text{A}} = 10\ \text{ns}, \qquad t_f \approx \frac{10\,\text{nC}}{1.5\,\text{A}} \approx 6.7\ \text{ns}$$

$$P_{tr} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 16.7\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.81\ W}$$

### 3.2 Output capacitance loss

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 60\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.073\ W}$$

### 3.3 Reverse recovery loss

The low-side body diode conducts during dead time, then is recovered when the high side turns on:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$

### 3.4 Dead-time body diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

> **Design lever:** reducing dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel. Verify shoot-through margin against the driver's propagation mismatch, which is about 20–30 ns on an FD6288Q.

### 3.5 Gate drive loss

This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs:

$$P_g = 2 \cdot Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\times60\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.058\ W}$$

### 3.6 Loss summary

| Loss term | Per channel (W) | 4 channels (W) |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 17.28 | 69.1 |
| Copper conduction | 5.0 | 20.0 |
| Transition (V·I) | 0.81 | 3.24 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 | 2.44 |
| Q_oss | 0.07 | 0.29 |
| Q_rr | 0.06 | 0.24 |
| Gate drive | 0.06 | 0.23 |
| **Total** | **≈ 23.9** | **≈ 95.5** |

Conduction dominates at 72% of the total. At 48 kHz, switching loss is only about 7%. **Optimize R_DS(on) and copper before chasing faster switching.** Moving from 24 kHz to 48 kHz costs only about 0.8 W per channel and buys quieter motors and smoother low-throttle behavior.
