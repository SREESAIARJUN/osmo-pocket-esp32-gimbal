## 0. Architecture Issues to Resolve First

**1. The STM32H743 cannot be the ESC commutation controller under ArduPilot.**
- ArduPilot runs flight control on the H743. It does not do sensorless FOC or 6-step commutation for the motors.
- Bi-directional DShot assumes a separate ESC MCU per motor that returns eRPM telemetry.
- The architecture is therefore: **H743 (ArduPilot) → DShot600 → 4× AM32 ESC MCUs (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071) → 4× 3-phase bridges.**
- The H743's job is DShot generation and telemetry capture, not gate driving.

**2. An 80 A burst on a "nano" (20×20 mm) board is thermally marginal for more than about 1 s.**
- Section 3 quantifies this.
- 80 A/channel is realistic on 30.5×30.5 mm boards and above, and even then only as a short burst.
- I'll model it, but the numbers below show where the design breaks.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

In 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification, two switches carry phase current at any instant (one high-side, one low-side). Each phase leg conducts for 2/3 of the electrical cycle.

Per-leg RMS current, high-side switch at duty D:

$$I_{HS,rms} = I_{ph}\sqrt{\tfrac{D}{3}}, \qquad I_{LS,rms} = I_{ph}\sqrt{\tfrac{1-D}{3}} \;(\text{sync rect.}) + \text{active LS term}$$

At burst (D → ~1), the conducting path is effectively 2 × R_DS(on) carrying the full I_ph:

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on),hot}$$

Hot resistance:

$$R_{DS(on),hot} = 1.6\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.5 = 2.4\,\text{m}\Omega$$

Conduction loss:

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 0.0024 = \mathbf{30.7\ W\ per\ channel}$$

Each conducting FET dissipates 15.4 W during its conduction interval. Averaged across the 6 FETs of a bridge, that is about 5.1 W per FET.

**Trace and bus-bar copper loss:**
- Assume 0.5 mΩ effective phase-path copper (2 oz, short pours).
- P_Cu = 80² × 0.5 mΩ ≈ **3.2 W per channel**.

### 2.2 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

One leg switches hard per PWM period (complementary PWM).

**(a) V–I overlap:**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

Check on t_r from Q_gd: with the Miller plateau at ~3.5 V and a 10 V drive, I_g ≈ (10 − 3.5)/(R_g ≈ 4 Ω) ≈ 1.6 A. That gives t ≈ Q_gd / I_g ≈ 9 nC / 1.6 A ≈ 5.6 ns, plus the gate-threshold delay, so about 10 ns is a reasonable estimate.

**(b) Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

**(c) Body-diode conduction during dead time (2 edges per period):**

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD} I_{ph} \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**(d) Reverse recovery:**
- Assume Q_rr ≈ 30 nC at 80 A and a fast di/dt.
- P_rr = Q_rr × V_bus × f_sw = 30 nC × 25.2 × 48 k ≈ **0.04 W**

**(e) Gate drive** (2 FETs switching per period, dissipated in the driver/R_g, not the die):

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 40\,\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.04\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Loss Budget at 80 A Burst

| Loss term | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 30.7 W | 122.9 W |
| Copper | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| Switching (overlap) | 0.97 W | 3.9 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W | 2.4 W |
| C_oss + Q_rr + gate | 0.13 W | 0.5 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 35.6 W** | **≈ 142 W** |

**Efficiency at burst:** P_out ≈ 25.2 × 80 × 4 ≈ 8.06 kW, so η ≈ 98.3%. The electrical efficiency is excellent. The problem is purely thermal density: about 142 W into a 4 cm² board.

**Design lever:** conduction losses dominate (about 86% of the total), while switching is under 5%. Reducing f_sw from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves only about 0.8 W per channel. Halving R_DS(on), by paralleling FETs or using a larger package, saves about 15 W per channel. **Spend board area on R_DS(on), not on switching speed.**

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Junction Rise (Steady-State Within the FET Package)

The worst-case FET carries 15.4 W during its conduction interval. At 48 kHz and typical commutation rates, the die thermal time constant (around 1–10 ms) averages this to about 5.1 W, with a ripple component.

$$\Delta T_{JC,avg} = 5.1 \times 1.5 = 7.7\ \text{K}, \qquad \Delta T_{JC,peak} \lesssim 15.4 \times 1.5 = 23\ \text{K}$$

The die-to-board drop is not the problem.

### 3.2 Board Heat Capacity (Lumped, Adiabatic Burst)

**Copper:**
- Volume: 8 layers × 70 µm × 400 mm² × ~0.5 average fill ≈ 112 mm³
- Mass: 112 mm³ × 8.96 mg/mm³ ≈ 1.0 g
- Heat capacity: 1.0 g × 0.385 J/(g·K) = 0.39 J/K

**FR-4:**
- Volume: about 1.6 mm × 400 mm² = 640 mm³
- Mass: 640 mm³ × 1.85 mg/mm³ ≈ 1.18 g
- Heat capacity: 1.18 g × 1.1 J/(g·K) = 1.3 J/K

**Components:**
- 24 FETs plus capacitors: ≈ 0.4 J/K

**Total:** C_th ≈ 2.1 J/K

**Adiabatic rise rate** at 142 W:

$$\frac{dT}{dt} = \frac{142}{2.1} \approx \mathbf{68\ K/s}$$
