# STM32H743 AIO 4-in-1 ESC Flight Controller: Power, Thermal & DShot Architecture

**Document:** Job #43, Systems Architecture Baseline Rev A

> **Assumptions to validate before this goes to layout.** Every number below follows from the stated parameters in §0. Change a MOSFET, the battery voltage, or the board stack-up and the results change, so recompute with your actual datasheet values. Two things matter most up front:
>
> 1. **The H743 is the flight-controller MCU, not the ESC MCU.** Each ESC channel needs its own commutation controller (AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware on an AT32F421 or STM32G071). The H743 talks to those controllers over bidirectional DShot.
> 2. **80 A per channel on a 20×20 "nano" board is thermally unsustainable.** §3.4 shows it can survive only as a sub-second burst. You need to agree on what "80 A burst" means (duration, duty cycle, airflow) before this design can be signed off.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step complementary PWM

In each 60° commutation sector, one phase is PWM-switched, one phase is held low, and one phase floats.

- **PWM on (fraction D):** current flows through HS(A) → motor → LS(B).
- **PWM off (fraction 1−D):** synchronous freewheel flows through LS(A) → motor → LS(B).

So **exactly two FETs carry the phase current at every instant**, regardless of D (ignoring dead time):

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{rms}^2 \, R_{ds,on}(T_j)$$

$$R_{ds,on}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

Including current ripple (triangular, peak-to-peak ΔI):

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{avg}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

The motor-inductance ripple ΔI is

$$\Delta I \approx \frac{V_{bus}\, D(1-D)}{2 L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5" motor, L_ph ≈ 15 µH (line-to-line, so 2L in series is already folded in as L_LL). At D = 0.5:

ΔI ≈ 25.2 × 0.25 / (15e-6 × 48e3) ≈ **8.75 A**, which adds 8.75²/12 = 6.4 A² to I² = 6400 A². That is a **0.1 % correction, so it is negligible at 80 A.**

### 1.2 Electro-thermal fixed point

Since R depends on T_j and T_j depends on P, solve iteratively:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + P_{FET}\, Z_{th}(t), \qquad P_{FET} = I^2 R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Closed form for the steady state, with R_th the effective junction-to-ambient resistance per FET:

$$T_j = \frac{T_a + I^2 R_{25} R_{th}(1 - 25\alpha)}{1 - \alpha I^2 R_{25} R_{th}}$$

**Thermal runaway condition:** the denominator goes to zero when α I² R₂₅ R_th → 1. At 80 A with R₂₅ = 1 mΩ:

α I² R₂₅ = 0.006 × 6400 × 1e-3 = 0.0384 /K

So runaway occurs at **R_th ≥ 26 K/W per FET**. A 20×20 board with no airflow sits around 30–50 K/W effective per FET, so **80 A continuous diverges**. This is the formal proof that 80 A can only ever be a transient rating.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_ds,on | P per conducting FET | P_cond per channel (2 FETs) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 6.40 W | 12.8 W |

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

With synchronous PWM, only the **PWM-ing high-side** hard-switches. The low-side commutates in "soft" mode (body diode during dead time). The loss terms are as follows.

**(a) V-I overlap, hard-switched HS:**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 20\text{e-9} × 48\text{e3} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction, LS, two edges per period:**

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 × 80 × 200\text{e-9} × 48\text{e3} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**(c) Reverse recovery, dumped into the HS at turn-on:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{e-9} × 25.2 × 48\text{e3} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

Q_rr rises steeply with di/dt and T_j. Use datasheet values at 80 A and 125 °C, which are often 2–3× higher.

**(d) Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,eq} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 × 1\text{e-9} × 635 × 48\text{e3} = \mathbf{0.015\ W}$$

**(e) Gate charge, dissipated in the driver and R_g rather than the FET:**

$$P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 × 50\text{e-9} × 10 × 48\text{e3} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

| Term | W per channel |
|---|---|
| V-I overlap | 0.97 |
| Dead time | 0.61 |
| Q_rr | 0.06 |
| C_oss | 0.02 |
| **Total switching (FETs)** | **≈ 1.66 W** |
| Gate drive (driver IC) | 0.05 |

**Trade-off note on 48 kHz vs 24 kHz.** Dropping to 24 kHz halves switching loss, saving about 0.8 W per channel. That is only about 4 % of total loss at 80 A, so conduction dominates. 48 kHz is justified for quieter, smoother low-throttle behavior. Do not drop the frequency for thermal reasons at high current.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Total dissipation at 80 A burst (T_j = 125 °C)

| Source | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 20.5 W | 82.0 W |
| FET switching | 1.7 W | 6.6 W |
| Copper/solder | 1.9 W | 7.7 W |
| **Total** | **24.1 W** | **≈ 96 W** |

Hottest single FET: the LS on the held-low phase conducts 100 % of the sector, giving 10.24 W plus about 0.3 W of dead-time share, so **about 10.5 W**.

Electrical efficiency check: P_out ≈ 25.2 V × 80 A × 4 ≈ 8 kW at the bus (upper bound with all motors at full duty). Board loss of 96 W is about 1.2 %, which is fine electrically. The problem is purely the heat flux density.
