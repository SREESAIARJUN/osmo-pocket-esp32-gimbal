## 0. Architecture Clarifications

### 0.1 System partitioning

The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs. ArduPilot runs on the H743 as the flight controller. It sends bidirectional DShot600 to **four dedicated ESC MCUs**, which run AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware on AT32F421 or STM32G071-class parts. Each ESC MCU performs commutation, 48 kHz PWM generation, and eRPM telemetry. The H743 only needs:

- 4 timer channels for DShot
- DMA for the DShot output and the telemetry capture
- ADC inputs for battery voltage and current

### 0.2 Feasibility flag

A "nano" form factor (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm mounting) cannot sustain 4 × 80 A. The math in §2–§3 shows:

- Simultaneous 80 A on all four channels is a burst rating of roughly **2–3 s**.
- Realistic continuous current is about **25–30 A per channel**.

The ratings must be stated that way on the datasheet. This is the single most important outcome of this analysis.

### 0.3 Voltage class

The design is evaluated at **6S** (25.2 V full charge). A 40 V MOSFET leaves about 15 V margin for ringing and regen spikes. That is common industry practice but tight. Recommendations:

- Use 40 V FETs only with rigorous layout and bulk capacitance.
- Choose 60 V FETs if 6S abuse margin is required. Re-run §2 with the higher R_DS(on).
