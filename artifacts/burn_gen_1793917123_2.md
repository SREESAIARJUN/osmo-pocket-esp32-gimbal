## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Scope

Some points need to be fixed first, because they change the math:

| Item | Assumption / Correction |
|---|---|
| **Who switches at 48 kHz** | The **ESC-side MCUs** (one per motor, e.g. AT32F421 running AM32) generate the 48 kHz PWM. The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and sends **DShot600** commands. The H743 never drives gates directly. |
| **Supply** | 6S LiPo: V_bus,max = 25.2 V. Design for 30 V transients; use 40 V MOSFETs. |
| **"Nano" form factor** | A 20×20 mm board cannot carry 80A per motor, even in bursts. This design assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm ESC board stacked under a 20×20 mm H743 FC**. 80A is treated as a **burst rating (≤5 s)**, not continuous. |
| **Commutation** | 6-step trapezoidal with complementary (synchronous) PWM and a 200 ns dead time. |
| **MOSFET** | Representative 40 V, 5×6 mm or 3.3×3.3 mm PowerPAK-class part with R_DS(on),25°C = 0.70 mΩ, Q_g = 100 nC, Q_gd = 12 nC, Q_rr = 50 nC, C_oss(er) = 2.5 nF, V_SD = 0.8 V. **Replace these with your actual datasheet values.** |
| **Gate driver** | 3-phase half-bridge driver (FD6288Q-class), ~1.2 A source / 1.5 A sink, with an external bootstrap. |

All numbers below are **first-order engineering estimates**. You must validate them with thermal imaging and IR/thermocouple burst testing before you trust them.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path
In 6-step synchronous operation, phase current always flows through **exactly two MOSFET channels**:
- One high-side and one low-side channel during the PWM ON phase.
- During the OFF phase, the current freewheels through the complementary low-side channel, which is synchronously enhanced. This is still two channels.

So the conduction loss is nearly independent of duty cycle.

$$P_{cond} = 2 \cdot I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{Cu}$$

### 1.2 Temperature derating
$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ /^\circ\text{C}$$

At T_j = 125 °C:
$$R_{DS(on)} = 0.70 \times 1.60 = 1.12\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Copper path resistance
The path runs from the pad, through the phase pour, to the motor pad, plus the bus. With 2 oz copper over 4 paralleled layers, an effective R_Cu ≈ 0.25 mΩ per channel path is achievable.

### 1.4 Result (per motor, I = 80 A)
$$P_{FET,cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.12\times10^{-3} = 14.34\ \text{W}$$
$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.25\times10^{-3} = 1.60\ \text{W}$$

**Per-FET average.** Each of the 6 FETs carries current for about 1/3 of the electrical cycle.

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{14.34}{6} \cdot 1 \approx 2.4\ \text{W} \quad(\text{instantaneous while conducting: } 80^2 \times 1.12\text{m} = 7.2\ \text{W})$$

The electrical period at typical eRPM is 0.3–2 ms, which is much shorter than the FET thermal time constants (~10–50 ms). Using the average loss is therefore valid for junction estimation.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (f_sw = 48 kHz)

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap (high-side FET)
Transition time is set by the Miller plateau:
$$t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}+Q_{gs2}}{I_{G,src}},\quad t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}+Q_{gs2}}{I_{G,sink}}$$

With Q_sw ≈ 15 nC, I_src ≈ 1.2 A (after R_G), and I_sink ≈ 1.5 A:
- t_r ≈ 12.5 ns
- t_f ≈ 10 ns
- Σ ≈ 22.5 ns

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 22.5\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 1.09\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance loss
$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss(er)} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} \times 2 = 0.5 \times 2.5\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = 0.076\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery (low-side body diode during dead time)
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction
$$P_{dt} = V_{SD} \cdot I_{ph} \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 1.23\ \text{W}$$

This term is significant. Cutting dead time from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per motor, but only if your driver propagation mismatch allows it.

### 2.5 Gate drive loss (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_G)
$$P_{gate} = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2 = 100\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = 0.096\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Switching summary per motor
| Term | W |
|---|---|
| V–I overlap | 1.09 |
| C_oss | 0.08 |
| Q_rr | 0.06 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 |
| Gate drive | 0.10 |
| **Σ switching** | **2.56** |
