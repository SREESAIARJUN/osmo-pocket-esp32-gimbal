# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: 80 A Burst Electrothermal Model and Bidirectional DShot600 Architecture

> **Note on numbers:** Every component value below is a **parameterized assumption**. The equations are general, so substitute datasheet values for your selected parts before committing to layout.

---

## 0. Architectural Clarification and Feasibility Flags

Two points need to be settled before the math, because they change the design.

1. **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz motor PWM.** In a 4-in-1 stack, each ESC channel has its own MCU (typically STM32G071, AT32F421, or GD32E230 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). That MCU commutates the bridge at 48 kHz. The H743 runs ArduPilot and only produces **DShot600 commands** and receives **eRPM telemetry**. Section 2 models the ESC power stage. Section 3 models the H743 timer and DMA side.

2. **80 A per motor on a "nano" (20×20 mm) footprint is not a continuous rating.** The model in §1.6 shows the total stage dissipation is about 85–90 W at 4×80 A. On a board with roughly 2 J/K of thermal mass, that is a heating rate of about 40 K/s. The design is therefore valid only as a **burst rating of ≲1–1.5 s**, subject to the current-path constraints in §1.7. The realistic continuous rating is much lower, roughly 25–35 A per motor, depending on airflow.

## 1. Power-Stage Loss Model (per ESC channel, 3-phase bridge)

### 1.1 Assumed Operating Point and Device Parameters

| Symbol | Meaning | Assumed value |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | Bus voltage (6S, full charge) | 25.2 V |
| I_ph | Phase current, burst | 80 A |
| f_sw | PWM frequency | 48 kHz |
| D | Duty cycle (worst case for switching loss ≈ high) | 0.9 |
| R_ds(on),25 | MOSFET on-resistance at V_gs = 10 V, 25 °C | 1.0 mΩ |
| k_T | R_ds temperature factor at T_j ≈ 100 °C | 1.5 |
| t_r, t_f | Current rise/fall time (driver-limited) | 20 ns each |
| Q_g | Total gate charge | 50 nC |
| V_drv | Gate drive voltage | 10 V |
| Q_oss | Output charge at V_bus | 40 nC |
| Q_rr | Body-diode reverse-recovery charge | 30 nC |
| V_SD | Body-diode forward voltage | 0.8 V |
| t_dt | Dead time (per edge) | 100 ns |
| R_θJC | Junction-to-case (bottom pad) thermal resistance | 1.5 K/W |

The MOSFETs are 40 V, 5×6 or 3.3×3.3 PDFN, with 6 per channel and 24 total.

### 1.2 Commutation Topology (6-step, synchronous rectification)

During any 60° sector:
- **Leg A** is PWM-switched. The high side conducts for D·T. The low side synchronously conducts for (1−D)·T.
- **Leg B** has its low side fully on.
- **Leg C** is floating, used for BEMF sensing.

The current path therefore always contains exactly **two** R_ds(on) in series:

$$R_{path} = 2\,k_T\,R_{ds(on),25}$$

### 1.3 Conduction Loss

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2\,k_T R_{ds(on),25} = 80^2 \cdot 2 \cdot 1.5 \cdot 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

Per-FET instantaneous dissipation applies while a given FET is in its 120° conduction window:

- Low side, fully on: $P = I^2 k_T R = 6400 \cdot 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 9.6\ \text{W}$.
- High side: $D \cdot 9.6 = 8.6\ \text{W}$, plus switching loss.

Averaged over an electrical cycle, each FET conducts about one third of the time. The average is $\bar P_{FET} \approx P_{cond}/6 \approx 3.2\ \text{W}$, before adding switching loss.

### 1.4 Switching Loss Components at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches hard: the high side turns on and off into an inductive load. The synchronous low side switches at near-zero voltage after dead time.

**(a) V–I overlap loss (hard-switched high side):**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot40\text{ns}\cdot48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction (two dead-time intervals per period):**

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\,I_{ph}\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{ns}\cdot48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**(c) Reverse recovery of the low-side diode:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{nC}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}$$

Note that Q_rr scales roughly with I and dI/dt. At 80 A, a factor of 2–3× over the datasheet test condition is prudent, giving about 0.1 W.

**(d) Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{nC}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive (two switching FETs).** This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FETs:

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2\cdot50\text{nC}\cdot10\cdot48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

**Switching subtotal (FETs):**

$$P_{sw} \approx 1.94 + 0.61 + 0.10 + 0.05 \approx \mathbf{2.7\ W}$$

### 1.5 Ancillary Losses per Channel

- **Shunt (if low-side current sensing is used):** $I^2 R_{sh}$. With 0.25 mΩ, this is 1.6 W. Many 4-in-1 designs use a single bus shunt instead of one per channel.
- **Copper (traces and pads):** see §1.7. It can easily equal or exceed the FET conduction loss.
- **Bus capacitor ESR (ripple):** $I_{C,rms} \approx I_{ph}\sqrt{D(1-D)}$. At D = 0.5 this is 40 A rms. With an effective ESR of 5 mΩ, $P = 8\ \text{W}$. This is the strongest argument for a large low-ESR bulk cap on the pads plus several ceramics.

### 1.6 Total Stage Dissipation (4 channels simultaneous burst)

| Term | Per channel | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching | 2.7 W | 10.8 W |
| **FET subtotal** | **21.9 W** | **87.6 W** |
| Shunt (optional) | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| Copper (2 oz, est.) | 3–10 W | 12–40 W |

**Optimization note:** Conduction dominates switching by about 7:1. Lowering f_sw from 48 to 24 kHz saves only about 1.3 W per channel. Halving R_ds(on), by paralleling FETs or using better silicon, saves about 9.6 W per channel. Prioritize R_ds(on) and copper.

### 1.7 Current-Path Constraint (often the real limit)

By IPC-2152 rough guidance, 80 A at ΔT = 30 °C on 2 oz external copper needs a trace width on the order of **25–30 mm**.
