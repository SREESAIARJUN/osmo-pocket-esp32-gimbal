# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 80 A ESC (Job #64): Reference Architecture

## 0. Architecture Corrections

Three issues in the brief would sink the design if built literally.

| Brief says | Engineering reality | Resolution used here |
|---|---|---|
| "STM32H743 4-in-1 ESC" | The H743 runs ArduPilot, not the motor commutation. Each motor needs its own low-latency MCU (AM32 or BLHeli_32-class). | H743 is the flight controller. Four AT32F421 (or STM32G071) MCUs run AM32, one per motor, all on one PCB (AIO). |
| "Nano" form factor | 24 power MOSFETs plus gate drivers do not fit on 16×16 or 20×20 mm. | 30.5×30.5 mm board (Ø3 mm, M3 mounting), 8-layer, 2 oz outer and 3 oz inner copper. |
| "80 A burst" | Physically achievable only for a few seconds per channel (see §3). It cannot be a continuous rating on this footprint. | Ratings below are derived, not marketing: **80 A for ≤2 s**, **~35 A continuous** with prop wash. |

All numbers below are a first-pass model. They must be validated with prototype IR thermography and a dyno before release.

## 1. Power Stage Topology and Parameters

**Per channel:**
- 3 half-bridges (6× N-MOSFET), 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM.
- **Gate driver:** FD6288Q (3-phase, bootstrap).

**MOSFET assumption:** 40 V, 5×6 mm DFN class. With a 6S input (25.2 V maximum), 40 V parts give about 1.6× headroom against ringing. 30 V parts are *not* acceptable at 6S.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S, full charge) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Burst phase current | I | 80 A |
| R_DS(on) @ 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | R₂₅ | 1.0 mΩ |
| Temperature coefficient (125 °C / 25 °C) | k_T | 1.6 |
| Rise / fall time (with 4.7 Ω gate resistor) | t_r, t_f | 20 ns / 20 ns |
| Total gate charge | Q_g | 50 nC |
| Reverse-recovery charge (body diode) | Q_rr | 50 nC |
| Body-diode forward voltage | V_F | 0.8 V |
| Dead time | t_dt | 100 ns |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| Junction–case thermal resistance | R_θJC | 1.2 K/W |

## 2. Loss Model (per channel, at 80 A)

In 6-step commutation exactly two phase legs conduct: one high-side FET and one low-side FET in the current path. One of these legs is PWM-switched.

### 2.1 Conduction loss

With synchronous rectification, current flows through one FET in each conducting leg at all times. Duty cycle cancels to first order:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2 \cdot R_{DS(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right]$$

At T_j = 125 °C, R_DS(on) = 1.6 mΩ:

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.6\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{20.5\ W}$$

At a cold start (25 °C): 12.8 W. Because conduction loss rises with junction temperature, it is a positive feedback loop. See the thermal runaway check in §3.4.

### 2.2 Hard-switching loss at 48 kHz

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus}\, I\,(t_r+t_f)\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery, dead-time diode conduction, gate drive

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{gate} = 2 \cdot Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.05\ \text{W}$$

P_gate is drawn from the 10 V gate supply, so it heats the driver and regulator, not the FETs.

### 2.4 Copper and shunt loss

Assume the phase and bus copper path is about 0.4 mΩ round trip, and a 0.25 mΩ shunt is shared on the battery leg:

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.4\text{m} = 2.6\ \text{W per channel}$$

### 2.5 Total

| Term | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (hot) | 20.5 W | 82.0 W |
| Switching | 1.94 W | 7.8 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W | 2.4 W |
| Reverse recovery | 0.06 W | 0.2 W |
| Copper traces | 2.6 W | 10.4 W |
| **Total** | **≈25.7 W** | **≈103 W** |

Conduction loss accounts for about 80% of the total. **Lowering f_sw from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves only about 1.3 W per channel.** The 48 kHz choice is justified by smoother low-throttle response and reduced motor ripple current, at an acceptable cost.

**Ripple check at 48 kHz.** Assume a motor with L_phase–phase ≈ 15 µH and worst-case D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{15\mu \times 48\text{k}} \approx 8.8\ \text{A}$$

This is about 11% of 80 A, which is acceptable. The RMS correction adds less than 0.5% to conduction loss.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network

Per FET, the heat path is:

T_j → R_θJC 1.2 K/W → T_case → solder + thermal vias, ~0.8 K/W → PCB copper planes → R_θ(board–air) → ambient

Each conducting FET dissipates about 10.3 W (half of 20.5 W) plus its share of switching loss:

$$\Delta T_{j-board} \approx 11\ \text{W} \times (1.2 + 0.8) = 22\ \text{K}$$

The junction-to-board path is therefore fine. **The bottleneck is board-to-air.**

### 3.2 Steady-state limit (why 80 A cannot be continuous)

For a 30.5×30.5 mm board with both sides exposed under prop wash (h ≈ 50 W/m²K, A ≈ 2 × 0.93×10⁻³ m², plus fin effect from wires and caps ≈ 1.5×):

$$R_{\theta,BA} \approx \frac{1}{hA} \approx \frac{1}{50 \times 2.8\times10^{-3}} \approx 7\ \text{K/W}$$

At 103 W this gives ΔT ≈ 720 K, which is impossible. For a maximum board temperature of 100 °C at 35 °C ambient (ΔT = 65 K):

$$P_{cont,max} \approx 65/7 \approx 9.3\ \text{W total} \Rightarrow \text{per channel} \approx 2.3\ \text{W} \Rightarrow I_{cont} \approx \sqrt{\frac{2.3}{2\times1.4\text{m} + 0.4\text{m}}} \approx 27\ \text{A}$$

Real quads see duty-cycled throttle averaging 30–50%. That supports a **~35 A "continuous" flight rating**, which is the honest datasheet number.

### 3.3 Burst (adiabatic) capacity

Effective heat capacity of the board assembly:

| Element | Mass × specific heat |
|---|---|
| Copper planes (~6 g) | 6 × 0.385 = 2.3 J/K |
| FR4 (~4 g) | 4 × 1.1 = 4.4 J/K |
| 24 FETs (~0.15 g each) | 24 × 0.15 × 0.7 ≈ 2.5 J/K |
| Caps, connectors, solder | ~1.8 J/K |
| **Total C_th** | **≈11 J/K** |
