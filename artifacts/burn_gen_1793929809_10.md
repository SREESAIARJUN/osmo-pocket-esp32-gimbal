# Job #27: STM32H743 Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC, Nano Form Factor

## Design Package: Loss Models, Thermal Model, DShot DMA Allocation, and Reference Netlist

## 0. Architecture Decisions and Critical Findings

Several points must be stated before the math, because they change the design.

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In any production 4-in-1 stack running ArduPilot, the H743 is the flight controller. Each ESC channel (or all four) runs a dedicated motor MCU, typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or EFM8/BB51 running Bluejay. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to those MCUs. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCU, not the H743. This document models it that way.

2. **80 A burst per channel on a "nano" board is thermally marginal.** On 20×20 mm, the result below shows the burst is only survivable for about 2–4 s without airflow. It needs 5×6 mm (or 3.3×3.3 mm dual-cool) sub-1 mΩ FETs, at least 2 oz copper, and current limiting in firmware. If "nano" means 20×20 mm, I recommend derating the spec to 55 A burst / 40 A continuous, or moving to 30.5×30.5 mm. The math below quantifies this.

3. **Bidirectional DShot cannot use complementary (CHxN) timer outputs.** ArduPilot needs input capture on the same pin, and the N outputs have no capture path. All motor pins must be on non-N channels.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-Dependent On-Resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)]$$

with α ≈ 0.0045 K⁻¹ for modern trench FETs, which gives about 1.5× at 135 °C.

The effective series path per conducting FET also includes copper:

$$R_{path} = R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + R_{Cu}, \quad R_{Cu} \approx 0.25\ \text{m}\Omega$$

R_Cu is the share of 2 oz pour plus via fields per FET position. It is temperature-dependent too, at α_Cu = 0.0039 K⁻¹.

### 1.2 Six-Step Trapezoidal Commutation with Complementary PWM

In any 60° sector, two phases carry current:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side conducts for duty D and the low-side (synchronous rectifier) for 1−D. One FET in this leg is always conducting, apart from dead time.
- **Return leg:** the low-side is on for 100 % of the sector.

So, at any instant, exactly two FETs carry the full phase current I:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{path}(T_j)$$

Over one electrical revolution, each of the 6 FETs takes an equal share on average:

$$\overline{P}_{cond,FET} = \frac{2\,I^2 R_{path}}{6} = \frac{I^2 R_{path}}{3}$$

The peak instantaneous loss in one FET is I²·R_path. This matters only at very low eRPM. At 80 A the motor is spinning hard, with an electrical period of about 0.1–0.5 ms, so the die averages over it.

### 1.3 PWM Ripple Contribution at 48 kHz

The peak-to-peak ripple is:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{LL}\,f_{sw}}$$

At the worst case D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\times10^{-6}\times 48\times10^{3}} = 6.56\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{DC}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.59 = 6403.6\ \text{A}^2$$

The ripple adds 0.06 % and is negligible at 80 A. This is one benefit of 48 kHz. At 24 kHz the ripple would be 13 A pk-pk, still negligible in conduction, but worse for acoustic noise and capacitor ripple.

### 1.4 Numeric Result at 80 A

The thermal solution in §3 converges to T_j ≈ 128 °C, so:

$$R_{DS}(128) = 0.95\,[1 + 0.0045 \times 103] = 1.39\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$R_{Cu}(90^\circ\text{C board}) = 0.25\,[1 + 0.0039 \times 65] = 0.31\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$R_{path} = 1.70\ \text{m}\Omega$$

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| P_cond,bridge per channel | 2 × 6403.6 × 1.70e-3 = **21.8 W** |
| — of which in FET dies | 2 × 6403.6 × 1.39e-3 = 17.8 W |
| — of which in copper | 4.0 W |
| Average per FET | **2.97 W** (die only) |

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Per switching period there is one hard-switched turn-on and one turn-off. Both occur on the high-side when current flows out of the phase. The low-side is soft-switched (ZVS) because its body diode conducts during dead time.

### 2.1 V–I Overlap (Hard-Switched FET)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

$$P_{VI} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\times10^{-9} \times 48\times10^{3} = 1.94\ \text{W}$$

Plug in measured t_r and t_f. Common-source inductance in the package plus layout often pushes the effective overlap to 30–40 ns each, which would give 3–4 W. The layout rules in §6 exist to prevent this.

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 50\times10^{-9} \times 25.2 \times 48\times10^{3} = 0.060\ \text{W}$$
