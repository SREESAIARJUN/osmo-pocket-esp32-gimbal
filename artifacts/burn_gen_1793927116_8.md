# STM32H743 4-in-1 ESC Nano Flight Controller: Power-Stage, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

**Scope note:** I don't have access to any prior Job #72 documents, BOM, or stack-up, so this derivation uses **explicit, representative assumptions**. Every number is parameterized so you can substitute your actual MOSFET datasheet values. Section 0 flags two architectural issues that should be resolved before layout.

---

## 0. Architectural Issues to Resolve First

### 0.1 The H743 should not drive the motors directly

ArduPilot runs on the STM32H743 as the **flight controller**. It does not run 6-step or FOC motor commutation. A 4-in-1 ESC needs **four dedicated ESC MCUs** running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical choices are the AT32F421K8U7 or the STM32G071KBU6.

The topology is therefore:

```
H743 (ArduPilot) ──DShot600 bidir ×4──► 4× ESC MCU (AM32) ──► 4× FD6288Q ──► 24× N-MOSFET ──► 4 motors
```

### 0.2 "80A burst" on a nano footprint is thermally bounded by seconds, not minutes

Section 3 shows the bulk temperature rise at 4 × 80 A is roughly **55–60 K/s**. You must specify a **burst duration** in the requirements. I recommend a maximum of 2 s at 80 A with a 30 s / 45 A continuous rating. Otherwise the "80A" figure is not verifiable.

## 2. Loss Model (Per Motor Channel)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

In 6-step commutation, at any instant **two phase legs** conduct the motor current I. One leg's high-side switch is PWM-ing; the opposite leg's low-side switch is held on.

With synchronous rectification in the PWM leg:

- The high-side FET (HS) conducts for duty D.
- The low-side FET (LS) of the **same leg** conducts for (1 − D).

Per active leg, the conduction loss is:

$$P_{\text{leg}} = I_{\text{rms}}^2 R_{\text{ds}}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] = I_{\text{rms}}^2 R_{\text{ds}}(T_j)$$

Two legs are always active, so:

$$\boxed{P_{\text{cond}} = 2\,I_{\text{rms}}^2\,R_{\text{ds},25}\,\alpha_T}$$

**Current ripple correction.** For triangular ripple Δi on the DC current I:

$$I_{\text{rms}}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta i^2}{12}$$

With a typical 5" motor (L_phase ≈ 15 µH, so L_ll ≈ 30 µH), 48 kHz, and D = 0.5:

$$\Delta i = \frac{V_{\text{bus}}\,D(1-D)}{L\,f_{\text{sw}}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{30\,\mu\text{H} \times 48\,\text{kHz}} \approx 4.4\ \text{A}$$

This adds only about 0.03% to I_rms² and is negligible at 80 A.

**At 80 A, T_j = 125 °C:**

$$P_{\text{cond}} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.5 = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

**At 80 A, T_j = 25 °C (cold start of burst):** 12.8 W.

### 2.2 Switching losses at 48 kHz

Under complementary PWM with inductive load, only the **high-side switch in the PWM leg hard-switches**. The low-side switch commutates near zero voltage because its body diode clamps during dead time.

**(a) V–I overlap (hard-switched HS):**

$$P_{\text{VI}} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{\text{bus}}\,I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{\text{sw}} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery** (LS body diode, dissipated in HS):

$$P_{\text{rr}} = Q_{\text{rr}}\,V_{\text{bus}}\,f_{\text{sw}} = 50\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$

**(c) Output capacitance:**

$$P_{\text{oss}} = Q_{\text{oss}}\,V_{\text{bus}}\,f_{\text{sw}} = 60\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.073\ W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead-time intervals per period):

$$P_{\text{dt}} = V_{\text{SD}}\,I\,(2\,t_{\text{dt}})\,f_{\text{sw}} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive** (6 FETs per channel). This power is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs:

$$P_g = 6\,Q_g\,V_{GS}\,f_{\text{sw}} = 6 \times 80\,\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.23\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{\text{sw}} = 1.45 + 0.06 + 0.07 + 0.61 = \mathbf{2.19\ W\ per\ channel}\ (+0.23\ \text{W gate drive})$$
