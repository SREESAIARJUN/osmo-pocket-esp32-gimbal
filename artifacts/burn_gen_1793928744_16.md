# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #49): Power, Thermal, and DShot Design Package

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Feasibility

**Architecture.** In production 4-in-1 designs, the STM32H743 does not commutate the motors directly. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071, running AM32 or BLHeli_32) driving a 3-phase gate driver. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to the ESCs over bi-directional DShot600. This package models that topology. Commutating 4×3 phases from the H743 directly would exhaust its advanced timers (only TIM1 and TIM8 have complementary outputs with dead time) and is not supported by ArduPilot.

**Feasibility.** "80A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is a vendor-style rating. As shown below, it is physically achievable for only about 1 second if all four motors are loaded together. Treat any 80A rating as single-motor, short-duration.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths

In 6-step trapezoidal control with synchronous (complementary) PWM, two FETs are in series with the load at every instant:
- the high side, or its complementary low side, of the PWM'd phase;
- the static low side of the sinking phase.

Each FET position conducts for 1/3 of the electrical period, weighted by D or (1 − D).

### 1.2 Equations

Total conduction loss per motor:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Per-FET average loss:
- static low side: $P = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{ds}$
- PWM high side: $P = \tfrac{D}{3} I^2 R_{ds}$
- complementary low side: $P = \tfrac{1-D}{3} I^2 R_{ds}$
