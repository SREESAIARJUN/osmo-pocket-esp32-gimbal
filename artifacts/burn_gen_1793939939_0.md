## 1. Field-Oriented Control (Innermost Loop)

### 1.1 Electrical angle

$$
\theta_e = p\,(\theta_m - \theta_{\text{off}}) \bmod 2\pi
$$

θ_off is found at commissioning by applying a d-axis voltage vector at θ_e = 0 and reading the encoder.

### 1.2 Clarke and Park transforms (amplitude-invariant)

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \frac{i_a + 2 i_b}{\sqrt3}\quad (i_a+i_b+i_c=0)
$$

$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix} =
\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}
$$

### 1.3 Plant and current PI design

The d–q voltage equations are:

$$
v_d = R i_d + L\dot i_d - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad
v_q = R i_q + L\dot i_q + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e \lambda_m
$$

Each axis, after decoupling, is a first-order plant $G_i(s) = \frac{1}{Ls+R}$. Placing the PI zero on the plant pole (pole–zero cancellation) gives:

$$
K_{p,i} = L\,\omega_{ci},\qquad K_{i,i} = R\,\omega_{ci}
\;\Rightarrow\; \frac{i}{i^*} = \frac{\omega_{ci}}{s+\omega_{ci}}
$$

Choose $\omega_{ci} \approx 2\pi\cdot 1.5\text{ kHz}$, below about f_PWM/10 to leave margin for the 1.5-sample PWM/computation delay.

Decoupling and back-EMF feedforward:

$$
v_d^* = PI_d(e_d) - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad v_q^* = PI_q(e_q) + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e\lambda_m
$$

### 1.4 Voltage limiting and anti-windup

The voltage vector is limited to the circle inscribed in the SVPWM hexagon:

$$
|v_{dq}| \le V_{max} = \frac{V_{bus}}{\sqrt3}
$$

The d-axis has priority:
- $v_q$ is clamped to $\pm\sqrt{V_{max}^2 - v_d^2}$.
- The integrator uses back-calculation: $\dot x_I = K_i e + K_{aw}(v_{sat} - v_{unsat})$, with $K_{aw} = K_i/K_p$.

### 1.5 SVPWM (min-max zero-sequence injection)

$$
v_{abc} = T^{-1}(\theta_e)\,v_{dq},\qquad
v_0 = -\tfrac12\big(\max v_{abc} + \min v_{abc}\big),\qquad
D_x = \tfrac12 + \frac{v_x + v_0}{V_{bus}}
$$

This is mathematically equivalent to sector-based SVPWM and is branch-light.

## 2. Cascaded Velocity and Angle Control

### 2.1 Mechanical plant (per axis)

$$
J\dot\omega = K_t\, i_q - B\omega - \tau_{cog}(\theta_m) - \tau_{dist},\qquad K_t = \tfrac32\, p\,\lambda_m
$$

Because the current loop is about 10× faster, it is treated as unity gain, so $G_\omega(s) \approx \frac{K_t}{Js + B}$.

### 2.2 Velocity PI (with optional filtered D)

Design for crossover $\omega_{cv}$ with the PI zero one decade below:

$$
K_{p,\omega} = \frac{J\,\omega_{cv}}{K_t},\qquad
K_{i,\omega} = K_{p,\omega}\,\frac{\omega_{cv}}{4}
$$

The resulting phase margin is about 76°. Typical value: $\omega_{cv} = 2\pi\cdot 150$ Hz.

Velocity feedback comes from the encoder difference, passed through a PLL observer:

$$
\hat\theta_{k+1} = \hat\theta_k + T_s\hat\omega_k + T_s k_1 e_k,\quad
\hat\omega_{k+1} = \hat\omega_k + T_s k_2 e_k,\quad e_k = \text{wrap}(\theta_k - \hat\theta_k)
$$

with $k_1 = 2\zeta\omega_n$, $k_2 = \omega_n^2$, and $\omega_n \approx 2\pi\cdot 400$ Hz, at least 2.5× $\omega_{cv}$.

The current command is:

$$
i_q^* = \text{sat}\Big(K_{p,\omega}e_\omega + x_{I\omega} + i_{ff,cog}(\theta_m) + \frac{J}{K_t}\dot\omega^*\Big)
$$

### 2.3 Angle (stabilization) loop: P plus rate feedforward

The gimbal uses the IMU (camera-frame attitude) for the outer loop. The encoder serves only for commutation and velocity.

$$
\omega^* = K_{p,\theta}\,\text{wrap}(\theta^* - \hat\theta_{cam}) + \omega^*_{ff} - \omega_{base}
$$

- $K_{p,\theta} = \omega_{c\theta}$, with $\omega_{c\theta} \approx \omega_{cv}/5$ (≈ 2π·30 Hz).
- $\omega_{base}$ is the measured base rate projected onto this joint axis. This is the key stabilization feedforward: the motor counter-rotates base motion before any attitude error builds up.

A small integral term ($K_{i,\theta} = K_{p,\theta}\,\omega_{c\theta}/10$) can be added to remove steady error from cable drag.

Joint-axis projection for a yaw-roll-pitch gimbal: the rate needed at joint *j* is $\omega_{joint} = J_g^{-1}(q)\,(\omega^*_{cam} - \omega_{base})$, where $J_g$ is the gimbal kinematic Jacobian. Near home pose this reduces to a per-axis mapping.

### 2.4 Bandwidth hierarchy summary

| Loop | Rate | Crossover |
|---|---|---|
| Current | 20 kHz | 1.5 kHz |
| Velocity | 5 kHz | 150 Hz |
| Angle | 1 kHz | 30 Hz |

Each loop runs at least 6× faster than the crossover of the loop above it, and at least 5× faster than its own crossover.
