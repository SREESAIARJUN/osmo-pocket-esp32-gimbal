# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #16): Power Stage Thermal Model and Bidirectional DShot600 Architecture

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Flag

The STM32H743 cannot directly drive the motors. A 4-in-1 ESC needs a dedicated commutation MCU per motor, because sub-µs ADC/comparator timing for 4×3 phases is beyond the flight stack's real-time budget. The partitioning is:

| Block | Device | Firmware |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIH6 (480 MHz) | ArduPilot |
| ESC MCU ×4 | AT32F421K8U7 (or STM32G071) | AM32 / BLHeli_32-compatible |
| Gate driver ×4 | FD6288Q (3-phase, 20 V, bootstrap) | n/a |
| Power FETs ×24 | 40 V N-ch, PQFN 3.3×3.3 | n/a |
| Link | Bidirectional DShot600, 4 lines | n/a |

**Feasibility warning.** 80 A per channel on a 20×20 mm "nano" board is a **burst-only** rating. The model below shows that the burst is thermally limited to roughly 1 s at full load. Continuous ratings on this footprint land at 35–45 A. If 80 A sustained is required, move to 30.5×30.5 mm with 3 oz copper.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.5 × 1.60 = **2.40 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step with complementary (synchronous) PWM

During each 60° commutation sector:
- One leg switches: the high-side conducts for D, the synchronous low-side for 1−D.
- A second leg's low-side is held on continuously.

So exactly two FET channels are in series at every instant, regardless of duty:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) + I^2 R_{cu}$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2(80^2)(2.40\text{ mΩ}) + 80^2(0.50\text{ mΩ}) = 30.72 + 3.20 = \mathbf{33.9\ W}$$

Each FET conducts on average 1/3 of the electrical cycle, but the duty is split between switching and static roles. The averaged load is:

$$\bar P_{FET} = \frac{30.72}{6} = 5.12\ \text{W},\qquad P_{FET,peak} = I^2R_{DS} = 15.4\ \text{W (instantaneous)}$$

### 1.3 Electro-thermal stability criterion

Losses rise with temperature, so there is a runaway limit. With junction-to-reference thermal impedance Z_th:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + Z_{th}\,I^2 R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

$$\Rightarrow\quad T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + Z_{th}I^2R_{25}(1-25\alpha)}{1 - \alpha Z_{th} I^2 R_{25}}$$

Thermal runaway occurs when $\alpha Z_{th} I^2 R_{25} \ge 1$.

At 80 A, I²R₂₅ = 9.6 W, giving **Z_th,crit = 1/(0.006 × 9.6) = 17.4 K/W**.

A 3.3×3.3 PQFN on a via-stitched 8-layer 2 oz board has a steady-state R_th(j–board) of about 15–20 K/W. **Steady 80 A is therefore at or beyond runaway**, which confirms that the rating must be time-limited.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg per channel switches at a time: one hard-switched high-side edge pair per PWM period. The low-side syncs after dead time and switches softly at near-zero V_DS.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss

Gate-driver plateau current is I_g ≈ (V_drv − V_plateau)/(R_g,ext + R_g,int) ≈ (12 − 3.5)/(4.7 + 1.2) ≈ 1.4 A.

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_g} = \frac{9\text{ nC}}{1.4\text{ A}} \approx 6.4\text{ ns}$$

Layout-parasitic ringing roughly doubles the effective time, so use t_r + t_f ≈ 30 ns:

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(30\text{ ns})(48\text{ k}) = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Remaining switching-related terms

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse recovery | $V_{bus}Q_{rr}f_{sw}$ | 0.06 W |
| C_oss (2 nodes) | $2\cdot\tfrac12 Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}$ | 0.05 W |
| Dead-time diode | $2\,V_F I\,t_{dt} f_{sw}$ | 1.23 W |
| Gate drive (in FD6288Q) | $2\,Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw}$ | 0.035 W |

Dead-time loss is about as large as overlap loss. AM32 lets you trim dead time. Each 50 ns removed saves roughly 0.3 W per channel, but you must verify there is no shoot-through on the bench.
