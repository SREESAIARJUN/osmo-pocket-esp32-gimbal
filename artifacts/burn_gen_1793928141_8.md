> ⚠️ **Feasibility flag:** A 20×20 mm "nano" stack cannot sustain 4 × 80 A continuously. Below I show that 80 A on all four motors simultaneously is limited to roughly **2 seconds** by board thermal mass. Treat "80 A" as a **single-motor, ~5 s burst** rating, or move to a 30.5×30.5 mm footprint with paralleled FETs. The marketing rating must be derived from Section 3, not the other way around.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (Trapezoidal 6-Step, Synchronous Rectification)

### 1.1 Current path topology
In each 60° commutation sector, two legs are active:
- The **PWM leg** alternates: the high side conducts for D·T_sw and the synchronous low side conducts for (1−D)·T_sw.
- The **return leg** has its low side held on for the whole sector.

The motor current therefore always flows through **exactly 2 channel resistances in series**, independent of D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2\,\big[2\,R_{DS}(T_j) + R_{pcb}\big]$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At the iterated operating point T_j ≈ 100 °C:

$$R_{DS}(100) = 1.3\,\text{mΩ}\times(1+0.005\times75) = 1.79\ \text{mΩ}$$

### 1.3 Results at 80 A

$$P_{FET,cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.79\text{m} = 6400 \times 3.58\text{m} = \mathbf{22.9\ W}$$

$$P_{pcb} = 6400 \times 0.5\text{m} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution
Each switch conducts for 120° of the electrical cycle. Over that 120°, its role splits as follows:
- In PWM sectors it carries current for D (high side) or (1−D) (synchronous low side).
- In return-leg sectors it carries current for 100%.

Averaged across the six FETs:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx \frac{22.9}{6} = 3.8\ \text{W per FET}$$

This averaging is valid because commutation frequency (eRPM/60 × 6, typically 2–20 kHz) is much faster than the die thermal time constant (around 1–10 ms).

**Thermal-runaway check.** The loop gain is the fractional increase in loss per kelvin of junction rise, multiplied by the thermal resistance:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\cdot\frac{R_{th,ja}}{P}\approx\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha(T_j-25)}\,R_{th,ja}\cdot P_{FET}\Big/P_{FET}... \;\Rightarrow\; G = \frac{\alpha\,P_{FET}\,R_{th,ja}}{1+\alpha(T_j-25)}$$

With P_FET = 3.8 W, R_th,ja = 7 K/W (Section 3.2), α = 0.005 and the denominator 1.375:

$$G = \frac{0.005 \times 3.8 \times 7}{1.375} \approx 0.10$$

G is well below 1, so the system is stable against runaway.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Its high-side FET hard-switches, and its synchronous low side switches at near-zero voltage but incurs dead-time diode and reverse-recovery losses.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard switching)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

Check on t_r: with Q_gd = 6 nC and a plateau gate current of about 0.4 A, t ≈ Q_gd / I_g ≈ 15 ns.

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I_{ph}\,(2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss(er)} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} \times 2 = 0.5\times1\text{n}\times635\times48\text{k}\times2 = \mathbf{0.03\ W}$$
