## 0. Architectural Corrections and Assumptions

Three issues in the brief would sink the design if built literally. They are corrected here before any modelling.

**A. The H743 cannot commutate the motors.** ArduPilot does not run ESC firmware. The H743 also lacks the complementary timer outputs needed for 24 gate signals. Its usable sources are TIM1/TIM8 with 6 outputs each, HRTIM with 10, and TIM15/16/17 with a few complementary pins, and ArduPilot does not use them for this purpose.

The correct topology is an **AIO board**:
- The **H743 runs ArduPilot** as the flight controller.
- **Four dedicated ESC MCUs** each run AM32 at a 48 kHz PWM carrier. The reference part is the STM32G071GBU6 (an AT32F421 is an alternative).
- The two are linked internally by **bidirectional DShot600**.

**B. "Nano" and "80 A burst" are not compatible in a sustained sense.**
- A 20×20 mm board has roughly 4 cm² of copper per layer and about 2 J/K of heat capacity.
- The model in §3 shows that four motors at 80 A heat such a board by about 45 K/s.
- Therefore 80 A is treated as a **per-motor, ≤1 s burst** rating.
- I recommend a **30.5×30.5 mm** footprint. Results for both sizes are given.

**C. Operating point (all calculations use these values):**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Bus voltage | 6S, V_bus = 25.2 V max (22.2 V nominal) |
| Burst current | I_pk = 80 A per motor, 6-step trapezoidal (AM32), synchronous rectification |
| Continuous current | 45 A per motor |
| PWM frequency | f_sw = 48 kHz |
| MOSFET | 40 V N-channel, PDFN 5×6 / 3.3×3.3 class |
| R_DS(on) | 0.95 mΩ @ 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V |
| Gate charge | Q_g = 45 nC |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr = 35 nC |
| Output capacitance | C_oss ≈ 1.6 nF |
| Thermal resistance | R_θJC ≈ 1.2 K/W |
| Switching transitions | t_r = 18 ns, t_f = 15 ns (gate driver ~1 A source / 1.5 A sink) |
| Dead time | t_dt = 100 ns |
| Body diode | V_F = 0.8 V |

Swap in your chosen MOSFET's datasheet values. All equations are kept symbolic first so this is straightforward.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right]^{\,k}, \quad \text{linearised: } \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1},\ k=1$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

R_DS = 0.95 × (1 + 0.005 × 85) = **1.35 mΩ**

### 1.2 Six-step current path

In any commutation sector, current flows through one high-side FET of phase X and one low-side FET of phase Y.

- With synchronous rectification, phase X's high-side and low-side FETs share the current according to duty cycle D: the high side conducts for D and the low side for (1 − D).
- The return FET conducts 100% of the time.

Per sector, therefore, the effective conduction is always **two R_DS in series**:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j) + I^2 R_{Cu}$$

Copper resistance R_Cu:
- The path is roughly 6 squares of 2 oz (70 µm) copper at 0.25 mΩ/□.
- Paralleling over 3 inner layers gives about 0.5 mΩ.
- Pad and solder contributions add about 0.2 mΩ.
- Total: R_Cu ≈ 0.7 mΩ.

### 1.3 Per-FET loss

Each FET is active for one-third of the electrical cycle. When active as the return FET it carries the full I² for that sector, and the high-side/low-side pair splits the sector by duty cycle. Averaging over the 6 sectors:

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{1}{6}\,P_{cond,pair}\ \ \text{(per FET, uniform rotation)}$$

| Case | I (A) | 2·R_DS·I² (W) | R_Cu·I² (W) | P_cond per channel (W) | Avg per FET (W) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Burst @110 °C | 80 | 17.3 | 4.5 | **21.8** | 2.9 |
| Continuous @90 °C | 45 | 5.0 (R_DS = 1.26 mΩ) | 1.4 | **6.4** | 0.83 |
