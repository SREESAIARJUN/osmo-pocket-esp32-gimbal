## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

**Partitioning.** The STM32H743 is the **flight controller** MCU and runs ArduPilot. It generates bi-directional DShot600. The **4-in-1 ESC** commutates each motor with its own per-channel MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32 class firmware. The 48 kHz PWM, dead-time, and commutation are ESC-side. Driving 4×3-phase bridges directly from the H743 alongside ArduPilot is not a supported configuration.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Bridge topology during one 60° commutation sector

The drive is trapezoidal 6-step with complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM. In each sector the phase current I flows through two paths:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side (HS) conducts for fraction D. The same-leg low-side (LS) synchronously rectifies for (1 − D).
- **Sink leg:** the LS is held fully on, so it conducts 100% of the time.

$$
P_{cond,bridge} = I^2 R_{hot}\left[D + (1-D)\right] + I^2 R_{hot} = 2\,I^2 R_{hot}
$$

**Key result:** bridge conduction loss is **independent of duty cycle** when synchronous rectification is used.

### 1.2 Temperature-corrected on-resistance

$$
R_{hot} = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right] = 1.0\,\text{mΩ}\times[1+0.005\times100] = 1.5\,\text{mΩ}\quad(T_j=125^\circ\text{C})
$$

$$
P_{cond,FET} = 2\times 80^2\times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ channel}
$$

Because R_ds(on) rises with temperature, loss rises with temperature. This positive feedback is solved iteratively in §3.4.

### 1.3 Copper and shunt losses (often underestimated)

**Copper.** The sheet resistance of 2 oz (70 µm) copper is:

$$
R_\square = \frac{\rho}{t} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}}{70\times10^{-6}} \approx 0.25\ \text{mΩ}/\square \ (25^\circ\text{C})
$$

Assume an effective path of ~0.3 mΩ per conduction path, with layers paralleled and stitched by vias. At 80 °C (+22%):

$$
P_{Cu} = 2\times80^2\times0.3\times10^{-3}\times1.22 \approx \mathbf{4.7\ W\ per\ channel}
$$

**Shunt.** A single shared DC-link shunt at 4 × 80 A = 320 A is unworkable:

$$
P = 320^2 \times 0.25\ \text{mΩ} = 25.6\ \text{W}
$$

Use one of the following instead:
- per-channel 0.1 mΩ shunts (0.64 W each), or
- R_ds(on)-based / Hall current sensing.
