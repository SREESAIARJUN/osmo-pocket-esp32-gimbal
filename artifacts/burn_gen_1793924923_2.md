# 3-Axis Gimbal FOC and Dual-IMU Fusion: Equations and C++ Implementation

**Assumptions.** I don't have the Job #40 specification, so I've assumed a typical brushless gimbal: a 12N14P motor (7 pole pairs), 14-bit magnetic encoder, inline phase-current sensing, and an STM32G4/H7-class MCU with an FPU. Every value marked *[ASSUMED]* should be replaced with your measured data before closing any loop on hardware.

---

## 1. System Architecture and Loop Rates

| Loop | Rate | Feedback source | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Current (d/q PI) | 20 kHz | Phase currents and encoder θₑ | v_d, v_q to SVPWM |
| Velocity (PI with filtered D) | 4 kHz | Gyro rate projected onto the joint axis (primary); encoder rate (fallback) | i_q* |
| Angle (P with small I) | 1 kHz | Fused camera attitude, then joint-space error | ω* |
| IMU pipeline | 8 kHz ODR, 2× ICM-42688-P | — | Filtered ω, a |
| Attitude (Mahony) | 1 kHz | Fused IMU | q̂ |

**Bandwidth separation.** Each inner loop should be 4–10× faster than the loop around it. Typical crossover targets are:

- current loop: ω_c ≈ 2π·1500 rad/s
- velocity loop: ω_v ≈ 2π·150 rad/s
- angle loop: ω_θ ≈ 2π·30 rad/s

## 2. FOC Current Loop

### 2.1 Transforms

**Clarke transform** (amplitude-invariant, with i_a + i_b + i_c = 0):

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}(i_a + 2i_b)
$$

**Park transform**, with θₑ = p·θ_m − θ_offset:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix}=
\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}
$$

### 2.2 Motor model (surface PMSM, L_d = L_q = L)

$$
v_d = R i_d + L\dot i_d - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad
v_q = R i_q + L\dot i_q + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e \lambda
$$

$$
\tau = \tfrac32 p \lambda\, i_q = K_t i_q
$$

### 2.3 Current PI tuning (pole–zero cancellation)

The plant for each axis is G(s) = 1/(Ls + R). Choosing

$$
K_p = L\,\omega_c,\qquad K_i = R\,\omega_c
$$

cancels the electrical pole at R/L. The closed loop then becomes first order:

$$
\frac{i}{i^*} = \frac{\omega_c}{s+\omega_c}
$$

**Decoupling feedforward** removes the cross-coupling and back-EMF terms:

$$
v_d^{ff} = -\omega_e L i_q,\qquad v_q^{ff} = \omega_e (L i_d + \lambda)
$$

**Voltage limit.** The output vector is limited to the inscribed SVPWM circle, |v_dq| ≤ V_bus/√3. The limit is applied with q-axis priority:

- v_d is clamped first.
- v_q is limited to the remaining magnitude, √(V_max² − v_d²).

### 2.4 Discrete PI with back-calculation anti-windup

$$
u_k = K_p e_k + I_k,\qquad \bar u_k=\mathrm{sat}(u_k)
$$

$$
I_{k+1} = I_k + K_i T_s e_k + K_{aw} T_s(\bar u_k - u_k)
$$

A good default for the anti-windup gain is K_aw = K_i/K_p.

### 2.5 Delay compensation

The PWM update introduces about 1.5 Tₛ of delay. Compensate for it in the inverse Park transform:

$$
\theta_e^{inv} = \theta_e + 1.5\,\omega_e T_s
$$

### 2.6 Voltage-mode fallback (no current sensors)

Many gimbal motors (R ≈ 5–15 Ω) are driven without current sensing. In that case use

$$
v_q = R\,i_q^* + \omega_e\lambda,\qquad v_d = -\omega_e L i_q^*
$$

## 3. Cascaded Velocity and Angle Control

### 3.1 Mechanical plant

$$
J\dot\omega = K_t i_q - B\omega - \tau_{cog}(\theta) - \tau_{dist}
$$

### 3.2 Velocity PI (symmetric-optimum style)

Treat the plant as K_t/(Js) and choose

$$
K_{pv} = \frac{J\,\omega_v}{K_t},\qquad K_{iv} = \frac{K_{pv}\,\omega_v}{4}
$$

This gives about 50–60° of phase margin
