### Architectural note

The STM32H743 is the **flight controller**. It does not run the motors. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071), its own gate driver, and 6 MOSFETs. The H743 talks to those MCUs over bi-directional DShot600. This is the only architecture ArduPilot supports for bdshot. The netlist in §5 reflects it.

### Feasibility warning

80 A per motor (320 A aggregate) on a 30×30 mm board is **only achievable as a short, thermally-capacitive burst**. It is not a continuous rating. §3 quantifies this. Treat the result as a design constraint, not a marketing number.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous PWM

At any instant, two phases carry current. In each 60° sector:

- The **PWM leg** has its high-side FET switching at duty D. The complementary low-side FET synchronously rectifies for (1−D).
- The **return leg** has its low-side FET on for 100% of the sector.

Each FET is active in 2 of the 6 sectors, which is 1/3 of the electrical period. Assuming AM32 places PWM on the high side:

$$P_{cond,HS} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,\frac{D}{3}, \qquad P_{cond,LS} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,\frac{1}{3}\left[\tfrac{1}{2}(1-D) + \tfrac{1}{2}\right]$$

In practice the low-side FET serves both as synchronous rectifier and as the return switch in alternating sectors. The bridge total is the robust quantity:

$$\boxed{P_{cond,bridge} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)}$$

This holds independent of D, because two R_ds(on) are always in series with the motor.

### 1.2 Ripple correction

With phase inductance L_ph (two phases in series), the peak-to-peak ripple is:

$$\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{2 L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}, \qquad I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

Typical values for a 2207-class motor are L_ph ≈ 15 µH and V_bemf ≈ 0.85·V_bus at burst. This gives ΔI ≈ 2.4 A, so the correction term is about 0.5 A². That is negligible at 80 A, but include it for low-inductance motors.

### 1.3 Temperature dependence (requires iteration)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

Because R_ds depends on T_j and T_j depends on loss, the model must be iterated to convergence (fixed point, typically 3–4 iterations):

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_{board}(t) + P\!\left(R_{ds}(T_j^{(n)})\right)\cdot Z_{th,jb}$$

### 1.4 Worked example (T_j = 100 °C)

- R_ds = 1.2 × (1 + 0.0045 × 75) = **1.605 mΩ**
- P_cond,bridge = 2 × 80² × 1.605×10⁻³ = **20.5 W per motor**
- Instantaneous dissipation in one conducting FET: I²R = **10.3 W**. This is the figure that matters for junction transients, because the electrical period is about 1–2 ms, which is comparable to the die's thermal time constant.

### 1.5 PCB copper conduction (frequently forgotten)

Copper resistivity is ρ_Cu = 1.72×10⁻⁸ Ω·m and rises about 0.39 %/K.

Example: a 2 oz (70 µm) pour, 8 mm wide and 15 mm long, from the battery pad to the bridge:

$$R = \frac{\rho L}{wt} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\times 0.015}{0.008\times70\times10^{-6}} = 0.46\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P = 80^2 \times 0.46\times10^{-3} \approx 2.9\ \text{W per channel, per layer path}$$
