## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

Two points need to be settled before the math, because they change the design.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In ArduPilot, each of the four ESC channels runs its own MCU (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM and handles commutation. The H743 only sends DShot600 frames and receives eRPM telemetry. The loss model below therefore applies to the ESC power stage. The DMA model applies to the H743.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm) board is thermally capacity-limited, not steady-state.** The model below shows that four channels at 80 A can be sustained for only about 1.5–2 s. Continuous current is about 22 A per channel. This number should go on the datasheet.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths
In 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification (AM32/BLHeli default), one phase is PWM'd and another is held low:

- High-side FET conducts for D·T.
- The complementary low-side FET (synchronous rectifier) conducts for (1−D)·T.
- The low-side FET of the return phase conducts for the full period.

Therefore, at any instant, **two FET channels are always in series with the motor current**, regardless of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

### 1.2 Ripple contribution at 48 kHz
The worst case occurs at D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L\, f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\,\mu\text{H}\times 48\,\text{kHz}} = 6.56\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{rms} = \sqrt{I_{dc}^2 + \tfrac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}} = \sqrt{80^2 + 3.59} = 80.02\ \text{A}$$

Ripple is negligible at 80 A, so I_rms ≈ I_dc.
