# Job #8: STM32H743 Nano AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC) System Model

## 0. Architecture Corrections Before the Math

Three points in the brief would cause a design failure if taken literally.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot runs on the H743 as the flight controller and emits bidirectional DShot600. Each motor channel needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071, with a 3-phase gate driver. The 48 kHz PWM is an ESC-side setting, not an H743 timer.
2. **80 A per channel on a 20×20 "nano" board is a burst rating of seconds, not a continuous rating.** The thermal model below quantifies this as roughly 2 s with all four channels at 80 A, starting from 40 °C. Plan on 30.5×30.5 if the requirement is real.
3. **Component values are assumptions.** No MOSFET part number was specified, so every device parameter below is flagged as an assumption. Substitute datasheet values; all equations stay valid.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

**Current path.** With complementary PWM in any 60° sector, current always flows through exactly two FETs:
- During the on-time: high side of phase A plus low side of phase B.
- During the off-time: low side of phase A (synchronous freewheel) plus low side of phase B.

So conduction loss is nearly independent of duty cycle D.

**Temperature-dependent R_DS(on):**

$$R_{DS}(T_j)=R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.0 × 1.45 = **1.45 mΩ**

**RMS current with ripple** (ripple ratio r = ΔI/I; r ≈ 0.2 for typical motor inductance at 48 kHz):

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2\left(1+\frac{r^2}{12}\right) = 6400 \times 1.0033 = 6421\ \text{A}^2$$

**Silicon conduction loss per channel:**

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) = 2 \times 6421 \times 1.45\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{18.6\ W}$$

**Copper loss.** Phase-to-pad plus bus copper is about 0.5 mΩ on a 6-layer, 2 oz board:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{3.2\ W\ per\ channel}$$

**Per-FET average.** Each of the 6 FETs carries roughly 1/3 of the conducting time, so P_FET ≈ P_cond / 6 × 2 ≈ 6.2 W peak-sector average, and about 3.1 W long-run average.

## 3. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switched high side sees hard switching. The synchronous low side turns on after dead time at about V_f, so it switches softly.

**(a) Hard-switching V–I overlap:**

$$P_{sw}=\tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\times25.2\times80\times30\text{n}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery of the low-side body diode:**

$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}=40\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

**(c) Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss}=Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}=50\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.060\ W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction (2 edges per period):**

$$P_{dt}=V_f\,I\,(2t_{dt})f_{sw}=0.8\times80\times200\text{n}\times48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive (2 actively switched FETs per sector):**

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\times50\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$
This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET.

**Switching total per channel ≈ 2.2 W.**
