# STM32H743 AIO 4-in-1 ESC Nano Flight Controller: Architecture Model

**Scope note:** I don't have access to any prior "Job #49" specification, so this document states its baseline assumptions explicitly. Every equation is parameterized, so you can substitute your actual BOM values. Part numbers and pin assignments are representative and must be verified against current datasheets before layout.

---

## 0. Design Baseline (Assumptions)

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | V_bus | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V max (22.2 V nominal) |
| Burst current per motor channel | I_pk | 80 A (≤ 2 s) |
| Continuous per channel | I_cont | 40 A |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| Board format | — | 30.5 × 30.5 mm mount, ~36 × 36 mm outline, 8-layer, 2 oz outer / 1 oz inner (2 oz on power planes) |
| Topology | — | H743 flight controller → 4 × ESC MCUs (AT32F421, running AM32) → 4 × 3-phase bridges |
| MOSFET (per switch) | — | 40 V N-ch, 5×6 DFN, R_ds(on) = 0.9 mΩ @ 25 °C, Q_g = 45 nC, Q_rr = 40 nC, C_oss ≈ 1.2 nF |
| Gate driver | — | FD6288Q-class 3-phase half-bridge driver, ~1 A source / 1.5 A sink |
| ESC protocol | — | Bi-directional DShot600 (ArduPilot `BIDIR`) |

The architecture point that drives everything below is that the H743 does **not** drive the bridges directly. It emits DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs. The H743-side DMA problem is therefore purely a protocol and timer problem, decoupled from commutation.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ /\mathrm{K}$$

| T_j | R_ds |
|---|---|
| 25 °C | 0.90 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 1.20 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 1.31 mΩ |

Design at **T_j = 125 °C, R_ds = 1.31 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-step commutation with synchronous rectification

Under AM32 trapezoidal control, at any instant one phase is high, one is low and one is floating. With complementary PWM on the active leg, the current path is:

- **Active leg:** high-side conducts for D·T_s, and the low-side (sync rectifier) conducts for (1−D)·T_s.
- **Return leg:** low-side conducts 100 %.

There are always two R_ds in series, independent of D (dead time is ignored here and handled in §2.4):

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2\left[R_{ds}D + R_{ds}(1-D) + R_{ds}\right] = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

At 80 A:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.31\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{16.8\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-device distribution

Each switch conducts 120° out of 360° of the electrical cycle. Averaged over an electrical revolution, the 6 devices share the 16.8 W:

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} = \frac{16.8}{6} \approx 2.8\ \mathrm{W}$$

However, the electrical period at typical eRPM is about 0.3 to 3 ms, far below the die thermal time constant. The die therefore sees average power, with a peak-instantaneous ripple of about 3×.

### 1.4 Whole board

$$P_{cond,board} = 4 \times 16.8 = 67.2\ \mathrm{W}\quad(\text{all four motors at 80 A simultaneously})$$

**Copper losses must also be counted.** For 2 oz copper, R_□ ≈ 0.25 mΩ/□. A phase trace of 4 squares on two paralleled layers gives about 0.5 mΩ:

$$P_{Cu,phase} = 80^2 \times 0.5\,\mathrm{m\Omega} = 3.2\ \mathrm{W\ per\ active\ phase\ path}$$

That is roughly 6.4 W per channel, about the same magnitude as one MOSFET. **Do not neglect it.** Mitigations are via-stitched multilayer phase pours and solder-tinned pads.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing high-side device has hard switching. The sync low-side is soft-switched (ZVS-like, via the body diode during dead time).

### 2.1 V-I overlap loss

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

Current-rise and voltage-fall times are set by gate charge and driver current:

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd} + Q_{gs2}}{I_{drive}} \approx \frac{12\ \mathrm{nC} + 5\ \mathrm{nC}}{1\ \mathrm{A}} \approx 17\ \mathrm{ns}$$

Taking t_r + t_f ≈ 30 ns (turn-on is slower because of the 1 A source):

$$P_{VI} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\times10^{-9} \times 48\times10^3 = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (sync FET body diode → hard turn-on of high-side)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 40\,\mathrm{nC}\times 25.2 \times 48\,\mathrm{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

Q_rr scales with di/dt and I_F. At 80 A, use a derating of about 2×, giving **≈ 0.10 W**.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss,eff} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.2\,\mathrm{nF} \times 635 \times 48\,\mathrm{k} = \mathbf{0.018\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per switching period, each t_dt ≈ 50 ns (AM32 default range), with V_F ≈ 0.8 V at 80 A:

$$P_{dt} = V_F\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\,\mathrm{ns}\times 48\,\mathrm{k} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in driver and gate resistors, not the FET)

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 45\,\mathrm{nC}\times 10 \times 48\,\mathrm{k} = 21.6\ \mathrm{mW\ per\ FET}$
