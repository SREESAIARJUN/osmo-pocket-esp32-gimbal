## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Assumptions

Several assumptions shape the rest of this document.

**System partitioning.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does not commutate the motors. Each of the four ESC channels has its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32:
- AT32F421 or STM32G071 for the ESC MCUs.
- These MCUs generate the 48 kHz PWM.

Data flows in both directions over DShot:
- The H743 sends DShot600 commands.
- It receives bidirectional eRPM telemetry back on the same wire.

**Assumed baseline parameters.** Every value marked † must be replaced with your actual BOM datasheet values before release.

> **Headline finding (stated up front):** 80 A per motor is **not sustainable** on a nano-format AIO. Section 3 shows it is a **thermal-mass-limited burst of roughly 2–3 seconds**, starting from a 40 °C board. Marketing "80 A burst" ratings usually assume 10 s on 2–4 parallel FETs per switch. Size the firmware current limit accordingly (§3.5).

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step trapezoidal commutation

In 120° trapezoidal commutation, two phase legs carry current at every instant:
- One leg is PWM-ing: HS ↔ LS complementary.
- One leg is held on: LS on continuously, or HS on continuously, depending on the PWM scheme.

With synchronous rectification, the current in each active leg always flows through **exactly one** FET channel. Body-diode conduction occurs only during dead time and is treated separately in §2.3.

This gives:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

The result is independent of duty cycle D, which is a key property of synchronous complementary PWM.

The per-phase RMS current over one electrical revolution, used for copper sizing, is:

$$
I_{phase,rms} = I\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{3}} = 80 \times 0.8165 = 65.3\ \text{A}
$$

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)]
$$

| T_j (°C) | R_DS(on) (mΩ) | P_cond per motor @ 80 A (W) |
|---|---|---|
| 25 | 1.20 | 15.4 |
| 75 | 1.53 | 19.6 |
| 100 | 1.70 | 21.7 |
| 125 | 1.86 | 23.8 |

### 1.3 PCB copper and interconnect loss

Assume an effective series path of 0.35 mΩ per conducting leg. This covers battery pad → bus planes → FET → phase pad, through 2 oz planes and via arrays.

$$
P_{Cu,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{Cu} = 2 \times 6400 \times 0.35\times10^{-3} = 4.5\ \text{W}
$$

Copper also has a positive TCR of +0.393 %/K. At 100 °C this rises to about **5.8 W**.

### 1.4 Shunt loss (if per-channel low-side shunts are fitted)

A 0.25 mΩ shunt in the DC return path carries I·√D. Worst case at D → 1:

$$
P_{shunt} = 6400 \times 0.25\times10^{-3} = 1.6\ \text{W}
$$
