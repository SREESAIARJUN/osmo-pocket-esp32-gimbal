## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Flag

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does not commutate motors. Commutation is handled by one ESC MCU per channel, for example an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32. The H743 talks to those ESC MCUs over bi-directional DShot600. The ESC MCUs generate the 48 kHz PWM, so the switching-loss model below belongs to the ESC side.

**Feasibility flag: "nano" form factor vs. 80A burst.**
- An 80A × 4 burst produces roughly 140W of heat on the board (Section 3).
- A 20×20 mm nano board cannot sustain this for more than about 1 s.
- I recommend either:
  - (a) a 30.5×30.5 mm footprint with an 80A burst rating defined as ≤10 s on a single channel, or
  - (b) honestly rating the 20×20 mm board as roughly 45A continuous / 60A burst.

Calculations below use **30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz copper**.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

With complementary (synchronous) PWM in trapezoidal 120° commutation, the current path always passes through exactly two conducting FETs:
- one high-side FET, or its complementary low-side FET during the off-time, and
- the active low-side FET of the return phase.

**Per-channel conduction loss:**

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha_T (T_j - 25)\right]$$

Here α_T ≈ 0.005 /K, which gives 1.5× at 125 °C.

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond per channel |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.50 mΩ | 2·6400·0.0015 = **19.2 W** |
| 125 °C | 2.25 mΩ | 2·6400·0.00225 = **28.8 W** |

**Copper loss per channel:**

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.0005 = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

**Per-FET loss** (the important figure for junction temperature): 0.5 × 28.8 = **14.4 W per conducting FET at 125 °C**.

**Electrothermal feedback.** P_cond rises with T_j, and T_j rises with P_cond. Solve iteratively:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + Z_{th}\cdot I^2 R_{25}\left[1+\alpha_T(T_j-25)\right]$$

This converges only if:

$$Z_{th}\, I^2 R_{25}\, \alpha_T < 1$$

At 80A: 6400 × 0.0015 × 0.005 = 0.048 W/K. Thermal runaway therefore occurs if the effective Z_th exceeds about **20.8 K/W**.

- A DFN 3.3×3.3 on a nano board has a steady-state R_th(j-a) of about 35–50 K/W, so **steady 80A is thermally unstable**.
- Only transient (burst) operation is viable. This is the mathematical basis for the feasibility flag in Section 0.

### 2.2 Switching Loss (hard-switched FET only, 48 kHz)

**Gate currents during the Miller plateau:**

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{g,on}} = \frac{9}{16} = 0.56\ \text{A}, \qquad I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{g,off}} = \frac{3}{12} = 0.25\ \text{A}$$

**Transition times:**

$$t_{on} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = \frac{9\ \text{nC}}{0.56} \approx 16\ \text{ns}, \qquad t_{off} \approx \frac{9\ \text{nC}}{0.25} = 36\ \text{ns}$$

**V–I overlap loss:**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_{on}+t_{off}) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(52\times10^{-9})(48\times10^3) = \mathbf{2.52\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery loss:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

**Output capacitance loss:**

$$P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.030\ \text{W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period):**

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**Gate drive loss** (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, all 6 FETs):

$$P_g = 6\, Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 6\cdot30\text{n}\cdot12\cdot48\text{k} = 0.10\ \text{W}$$

**Frequency sensitivity.** Switching-dependent losses total about 3.2W at 48 kHz.
- Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.6W per channel.
- The cost is more current ripple and audible noise.
- The 48 kHz choice is acceptable but not free.
