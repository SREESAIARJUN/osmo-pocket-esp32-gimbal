**Job #49: Design Basis Document, Rev A**

## 0. Scope, Architecture Correction, and Assumptions

### 0.1 Architecture

The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. The architecture is:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──bidir DShot600 ×4──▶ 4× ESC MCU (AM32/BLHeli_32 class)
                                               │
                                               ├─ 3-phase gate driver
                                               └─ 6× N-MOSFET half-bridges per motor
```

- The 48 kHz switching frequency and the conduction/switching losses belong to the ESC power stages.
- The DMA and timer allocation belongs to the H743.

The two sections below are therefore decoupled, but they share the board thermally.

### 0.2 Feasibility Flag

"80A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm) AIO is extremely aggressive. The model below shows it is only survivable as a short transient (on the order of 1–2 s), not a sustained rating. Treat 80A as a burst figure and design continuous ratings separately.

No MOSFET part number was specified. Values are representative of a 40 V, DFN 3.3×3.3 / 5×6 class device. Substitute datasheet values before release.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Path Topology (6-Step, Complementary PWM)

During any 60° commutation sector, phase X is PWM'd and phase Y is held low.

- **On-state (D):** HS_X → motor → LS_Y. Two FETs carry I.
- **Freewheel (1−D):** LS_X (synchronous rectifier) → motor → LS_Y. Two FETs carry I.

So, regardless of duty cycle, exactly two R_ds,on conduct the phase current at every instant (ignoring dead time, which is accounted for in §2).

### 1.2 Per-Channel Conduction Loss

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{rms}^2 \,\frac{R_{ds,on}(T_j)}{n_p}
$$

where the current includes the PWM ripple:

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I_{dc}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12},
\qquad
\Delta I_{pp} \approx \frac{(V_{bus} - E_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}
$$

The temperature dependence of R_ds,on is linearized as:

$$
R_{ds,on}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + k(T_j - 25)],\qquad k \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

Each of the 6 FETs conducts for an equal share over an electrical revolution, so the average loss per FET is:

$$
\bar{P}_{cond,FET} = \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6} = \frac{I^2 R_{ds}}{3 n_p}
$$

### 1.3 Worked Values at 80A

The ripple term is neglected because, at high-current low-kV operation, ΔI_pp is much smaller than I.

| T_j | R_ds,on | P_cond per channel | Per FET (avg) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.50 mΩ | 2 · 6400 · 0.0015 = **19.2 W** | 3.2 W |
| 125 °C | 2.25 mΩ | 2 · 6400 · 0.00225 = **28.8 W** | 4.8 W |

**Electrothermal coupling.** R_ds,on is a function of T_j, so solve iteratively. The loop gain is:

$$
G = R_{\theta} \cdot \partial P/\partial T_j = R_\theta \cdot 2I^2 R_{25} k
$$

With R_θ,j-a ≈ 15 K/W effective per FET on a nano board, G is approximately 15 · 0.0192 · 6400 / 6 ≈ 0.3 per FET. This converges, but the steady-state answer exceeds T_j,max. That confirms 80A cannot be a continuous rating.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg (HS_X / LS_X) switches. LS_Y is static.

### 2.1 Hard-Switching V-I Overlap (HS Turn-On and Turn-Off)

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}
= 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 35\text{ ns} \cdot 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.69\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction (LS_X, Two Edges per Period)

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw}
= 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\text{ ns} \cdot 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Reverse Recovery (Dissipated Mostly in HS at Turn-On)

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{ nC} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

Q_rr itself scales with di/dt and I_F, so a datasheet curve lookup is required.

### 2.4 Output Capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = C_{oss(er)} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1\text{ nF} \cdot 635 \cdot 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.030\ W}
$$

This accounts for charge/discharge of both Coss in the leg. The ½CV² terms per FET sum to ≈ C·V².

### 2.5 Gate Drive (Dissipated in the Driver and R_g, Not the FET)

$$
P_{g} = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 50\text{ nC} \cdot 10 \cdot 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

### 2.6 Switching Total per Channel at 80A

$$
P_{sw,ch} \approx 1.69 + 0.61 + 0.05 + 0.03 = \mathbf{2.38\ W}\quad(+0.05\text{ W in driver})
$$

**Sensitivity.** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it is ≈ 1.2 W; at 96 kHz it is ≈ 4.8 W. At 80A, conduction dominates by more than 10×, so 48 kHz is not the thermal limiter. It is chosen for acoustic and current-ripple reasons.
