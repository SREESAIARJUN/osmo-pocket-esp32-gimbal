## 0. Architectural Corrections and Assumptions

Two points need settling before any math, because they change the design.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot runs on the H743 and emits bi-directional DShot600. Each motor needs a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, such as an AT32F421K8U7 (QFN-32) or STM32G071. Running 4× sensorless 6-step or FOC on the H743 alongside ArduPilot would take 24 complementary PWM outputs, 12+ fast ADC/comparator channels, and a non-ArduPilot firmware stack. That approach is not viable.

**Topology:** H743 (ArduPilot) → 4× DShot600 BIDIR → 4× AT32F421 (AM32) → 4× FD6288Q gate drivers → 24× N-MOSFETs

**2. "80 A burst" on a nano AIO is thermally aggressive.** Section 4 shows the board can only handle it for a few seconds. The rating must be qualified with a duration and a simultaneity condition.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation model (6-step, synchronous rectification)

In 6-step trapezoidal drive, two phases conduct at any instant:

- **Phase A (PWM leg):** high-side FET on for duty D. The complementary low-side FET (sync rect.) conducts for (1−D).
- **Phase B (sink leg):** low-side FET on continuously for the 60° sector.

The current path resistance always comprises one PWM-leg FET plus one sink-leg FET:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{rms}^2 \left[ R_{DS,hot}\,D + R_{DS,hot}(1-D) + R_{DS,hot} \right] = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS,hot}$$

The loss is independent of D with synchronous rectification. This holds as long as the ripple is small. With the low inductance of large-KV motors, add a ripple term:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph-ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent Rds

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ /\mathrm{K}$$

$$R_{DS}(125^\circ\mathrm{C}) = 0.80 \times 1.5 = 1.20\ \mathrm{m\Omega}$$

**Ripple check:** take L_ph-ph = 10 µH, D = 0.9, V_bemf ≈ 18 V.

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(25.2-18)(0.9)}{10\mu \cdot 48\mathrm{k}} = 13.5\ \mathrm{A} \Rightarrow \frac{13.5^2}{12} = 15.2\ \mathrm{A^2} \;(\text{+0.24\%, negligible})$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| Term | Calculation | Loss per motor |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (hot) | 2 × 80² × 1.20 mΩ | **15.36 W** |
| FET conduction (cold, start of burst) | 2 × 80² × 0.80 mΩ | 10.24 W |
| PCB copper + solder + bus (≈0.4 mΩ loop) | 80² × 0.4 mΩ | **2.56 W** |
| Shunt (if a 0.25 mΩ shunt sits in series) | 80² × 0.25 mΩ | 1.60 W |

There is a thermal runaway coupling: Rds rises with T_j, which raises P, which raises T_j. The fixed point exists only if

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T} \cdot R_{th} < 1 \;\Rightarrow\; 2I^2 R_{DS,25}\,\alpha\,R_{th,eff} < 1$$

At 80 A: 2 × 6400 × 0.8 m × 0.005 = 0.0512 W/K. The loop is stable for R_th,eff < 19.5 K/W, which is satisfied.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Hard switching occurs on the high-side FET, while the low-side FET soft-switches via the body diode.

### 2.1 V-I overlap (high-side FET)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I\, (t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\,\mathrm{ns} \times 48\,\mathrm{kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

A more accurate version uses the Miller plateau. With driver source/sink currents I_src and I_snk:

$$t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{src}} + \frac{Q_{gs2}}{I_{src}}, \quad I_{src}=\frac{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}{R_{g,int}+R_{g,ext}+R_{drv}}$$

With V_drv = 12 V, V_pl = 4.5 V, and ΣR_g = 8 Ω, I_src = 0.94 A. With Q_gd + Q_gs2 = 18 nC, t_r ≈ 19 ns, which is consistent with the table.

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (body diode of the low-side FET, at turn-on of the high-side FET)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 40\,\mathrm{nC}\times 25.2\times 48\,\mathrm{k} = 0.048\ \mathrm{W}$$

Q_rr scales with di/dt and I. At 80 A, derate ×2 to get **≈0.10 W**.
