## 1. Conduction Loss Model at 80A Burst

### 1.1 Topology

With trapezoidal 6-step commutation and synchronous rectification, two phases conduct at any instant:
- The active leg alternates between the high-side FET (duty D) and the low-side FET (1–D).
- The return leg's low-side FET conducts the full current 100% of the time.

### 1.2 Per-motor conduction loss

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds,hot}
$$

This result is **independent of duty cycle**, which is a key property of synchronous drive.

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.92\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{24.6\ W}
$$

### 1.3 Temperature-coupled solution

R_ds depends on T_j, so solve iteratively:

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.007\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$
$$
T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}(T_j)\cdot R_{\theta JC}
$$

This converges in 3–4 iterations. Thermal runaway occurs if

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\cdot R_\theta \ge 1
$$

which is not a risk at the per-FET R_θ values here. It becomes a real risk at the **board** level (Section 4).

### 1.4 Copper and shunt losses (often ignored, should not be)

- **Phase trace / pad resistance:** about 0.3 mΩ round trip, giving 80² × 0.3 m = **1.9 W**.
- **Shunt** (0.25 mΩ, per-ESC): 80² × 0.25 m = **1.6 W**, but only if it sits in the full-current path.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the active (PWM) leg switches. The return leg is static between commutations.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_f \, I \, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}
$$

### 2.4 Output capacitance

With Coss,er ≈ 1.5 nF:

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,er} V^2 f \times 2 = 0.046\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss lands in the driver and R_g, not the FET:

$$
P_{gate} = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} \times 2 = 60\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = 0.058\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.6 Loss budget per motor at 80A burst

| Loss term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 24.6 |
| Copper and shunt | 3.5 |
| Switching overlap | 1.94 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Q_rr + Coss + gate | 0.15 |
| **Total per motor** | **≈ 30.8** |
| **4-in-1 total (all motors at burst)** | **≈ 123 W** |

### 2.7 Frequency trade-off

Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw: about 2.6 W per motor at 48 kHz versus about 1.3 W at 24 kHz. Conduction loss dominates by roughly 10×. So 48 kHz costs only about 4% extra loss, and it buys lower current ripple and quieter motors.

## 3. Per-FET Thermal Distribution

Over one electrical revolution, each of the 6 FETs per motor spends about 1/3 of the time in the conducting set.

$$
\bar P_{FET} \approx \frac{P_{cond} + P_{sw} + P_{dt}}{6} = \frac{27.2}{6} \approx 4.5\ \text{W}
$$

The **worst-case instantaneous** load falls on the always-on return low-side FET:

$$
80^2 \times 1.92\text{m} = 12.3\ \text{W}
$$

This lasts for one commutation sector, which at high eRPM is about 50–200 µs. Use the datasheet transient curve Z_θJC(t):
- For a sector under 1 ms, Z_θJC ≈ 0.1–0.2 K/W.
- ΔT_j,ripple ≈ 12.3 × 0.15
