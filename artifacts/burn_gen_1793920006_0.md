# STM32H743 AIO / 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Thermal Model and Bi-Directional DShot600 Architecture

> **Feasibility first:** 80 A per motor on a nano (20×20 mm) 4-in-1 is a seconds-scale burst, not a sustainable rating. The model below quantifies this. The numbers are derived from stated assumptions because no MOSFET part number was specified. Replace the parameters in §1 with datasheet values from your BOM, then re-run the equations.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current path

In 6-step synchronous operation, at any instant current flows through exactly two FETs in series:
- During t_on, the PWM high-side FET (phase A) and the low-side FET of the sink phase (phase B) conduct.
- During t_off, the synchronous low-side FET (phase A) and the low-side FET of phase B conduct.

So, ignoring dead time, the motor current always sees 2·R_DS(on) regardless of duty cycle D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 2.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

R_DS(on) rises with junction temperature, which in turn raises the loss:

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0053\,/\text{K}$$

At T_j = 100 °C this gives R = 1.68 mΩ.

**Per motor:**

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.68\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{21.5\ W}$$

**Board total (4 motors):** 86 W.

### 2.3 Per-FET distribution

Over one electrical revolution, each phase leg carries current for 2/3 of the period: 1/3 sourcing and 1/3 sinking. Within the sourcing interval, the HS/LS split is D and (1−D).

- Worst-case single FET: an always-on sink LS conducts I² for 1/3 of the cycle.
  - P = 6400 × 1.68 mΩ / 3 ≈ **3.6 W**
- Worst-case HS FET at D = 1: also ≈ 3.6 W.

### 2.4 Copper loss

This term is frequently ignored but is significant at 80 A.

- Trace or pour resistance: 2 oz Cu, 10 mm long × 4 mm wide ≈ 0.43 mΩ per segment.
- Per motor, with roughly 3 segments in the path: 80² × 1.3 mΩ ≈ **8 W**.
- **Mitigation:** use 3–4 oz copper on inner layers, parallel the pours, and stitch with vias at roughly 1 A/via.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing leg hard-switches. The LS FET is the synchronous FET, so it turns on at near-zero V_DS.

### 3.1 Transition times

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{pl}}{R_{g,ext} + R_{g,int} + R_{drv,src}}, \quad I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{g,ext} + R_{g,int} + R_{drv,snk}}$$

With FD6288-class source/sink limits (roughly 1.0 A / 1.5 A):

- Turn-on: I_g ≈ 1.0 A, so t_r ≈ Q_sw / I_g = **10 ns**.
- Turn-off: I_g ≈ 3 V / 3.7 Ω ≈ 0.8 A, so t_f ≈ **12.5 ns**.

### 3.2 Loss components, per motor

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·22.5n·48k | **1.09 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 0.036 W |
| C_oss | ½·C_oss·V²·f_sw | 0.018 W |
| Dead-time body diode | V_f·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·100n·48k | **0.31 W** |
| Gate drive (driver side) | 2·Q_g·V_drv·f_sw | 0.034 W |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 1.49 W** |

**Conclusion:** at 80 A and 48 kHz, conduction (21.5 W) plus copper (≈8 W) dwarfs switching (1.5 W). Two consequences follow:
- Raising f_sw to 96 kHz for smoother low-throttle behaviour costs only about +1.5 W per motor.
- Lowering R_DS(on), for example by paralleling FETs, is the dominant design lever.

### 3.3 Bulk-capacitor ripple current

This is a critical constraint for a nano board:

$$I_{C,rms} = I_{ph}\sqrt{D(1-D)} \;\xrightarrow{D = 0.5}\; 40\ \text{A}_{rms}\ \text{per motor}$$

- Interleaving across four motors helps only statistically.
- On-board MLCCs alone cannot carry this. A typical bank is 8× 10 µF 50 V
