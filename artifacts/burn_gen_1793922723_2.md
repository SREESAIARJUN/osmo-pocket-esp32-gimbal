## 0. Scope and Assumptions

I don't have Job #46's BOM, stack-up or layout files. Every component value below is a **stated assumption**. The equations are general, so substitute datasheet values and the numbers will follow.

**Architectural point:** in a 4-in-1 ESC, the STM32H743 does **not** drive the MOSFETs. Each motor channel (or one shared MCU) runs its own ESC firmware, such as AM32 or BLHeli_32, on an AT32F421 or similar. That firmware generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 runs ArduPilot and only produces DShot600 frames and decodes bidirectional eRPM telemetry. The two domains are modeled separately below.

## 1. Conduction Losses at 80 A Burst

### 1.1 Topology

The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous (complementary) PWM. In each commutation interval, two phases carry current:

- **PWM'd phase:**
  - The high-side FET conducts for duty fraction D.
  - The low-side FET conducts for (1 − D) as the synchronous rectifier.
- **Return phase:** the low-side FET conducts 100 % of the interval.

### 1.2 Per-channel conduction loss

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{hot}\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I^2 R_{hot}$$

The result is **independent of duty cycle**, which is a useful property of synchronous rectification.

The hot on-resistance is:

$$R_{hot} = R_{25}\,\big[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\big] \approx k_T R_{25} = 1.35\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.35\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{17.3\ W\ per\ motor\ channel}$$

### 1.3 PCB copper loss (often larger than designers expect)

The phase path consists of the bus, the FETs, the phase pad and back. Model it as:

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho\, L}{w\, t}, \qquad \rho_{Cu,100°C} \approx 2.2\times10^{-8}\ \Omega\cdot m$$

For example, a 10 mm path that is 4 mm wide in 2 oz copper (70 µm) gives about 0.79 mΩ per layer. With three layers in parallel, that is about 0.26 mΩ, and the current traverses it twice:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 \times 2 R_{Cu} \approx 80^2 \times 0.52\times10^{-3} \approx \mathbf{3.4\ W}$$

### 1.4 Electro-thermal convergence

R_DS(on) depends on T_j, which in turn depends on the dissipated power. The model therefore iterates:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_a + P_{tot}\big(R_{hot}(T_j^{(n)})\big)\cdot Z_{\theta}(t_{burst})$$

If this does not converge (dT/dP grows unbounded), the design is in thermal runaway. In that case you must cap burst duration in the ESC firmware current limit.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

In each commutation interval, one half-bridge is hard-switched.

| Loss term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | ½·25.2·80·20 ns·48 k = **0.97 W** |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss·V·f_sw | 40 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.05 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 50 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.06 W** |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_F·I·2t_dt·f_sw | 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw | 2·50 nC·10·48 k = **0.05 W** (dissipated in the driver and gate resistor) |
| **Subtotal** | | **≈ 1.74 W** |

Two sensitivities matter:

- **Dead time is the second-largest loss.** Cutting it from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel. Verify against the driver's propagation skew first, so the change does not cause shoot-through.
- **Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw.** At 24 kHz the subtotal would be about 0.9 W, at the cost of more current ripple and audible noise.

Efficiency at the burst point: P_out ≈ 4 × 25.2 V × 80 A × D. At full throttle that is about 8 kW, so efficiency is roughly 98.9 %. The absolute 90 W is the problem on a 30×30 mm board.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Network (Cauer form, per FET)

```
P_FET ─► [R_θJC 1.5 K/W] ─► case/pad ─► [R_θ,spread ≈ 8–15 K/W per FET footprint] ─► board node
                                                                                     │
                                                              C_board ≈ 6 J/K ───────┤
                                                                                     │
                                                      [R_θ,board-air ≈ 3–6 K/W with prop-wash] ─► T_a
```

### 4.2 Peak per-FET dissipation

The return-phase low-side FET conducts for a full interval, so its instantaneous loss is I²R_hot = 8.6 W. Averaged over a 360° electrical cycle, each FET conducts about one-third of the time, giving roughly 2.9 W conduction plus about 0.4 W switching, or about 3.3 W average.

### 4.3 Junction rise above the board

$$\Delta T_{j-b} \approx P_{FET}(R_{\theta JC} + R_{\theta,spread}) \approx 3.3 \times (1.5 + 10) \approx 38\ \text{K}$$

At the electrical frequency (hundreds of Hz to kHz), the junction thermal time constant filters the 8.6 W pulses, so you only need to add a small ripple term. Use Z_θJC(t_pulse) from the datasheet transient curve.

### 4.4 Board burst budget (adiabatic bound)

$$C_{board} \approx m_{Cu}c_{Cu} + m_{FR4}c_{FR4} \approx 4\,\text{g}\times0.385 + 4\,\text{g}\times1.1 \approx 6\ \text{J/K}$$

$$t_{burst} = \frac{C_{board}\,(T_{b,max} - T_{b,0})}{P_{tot} - (T_b - T_a)/R_{\theta,b-a}}$$

Taking T_b,max = 110 °C (leaving margin for the ~38 K junction rise to stay under 150 °C) and T_b,0 = 50 °C:

$$t_{burst} \approx \frac{6 \times 60}{90 - \sim 15} \approx \mathbf{4.8\ s}\ \text{(all four motors at 80 A)}$$

Interpretation:

- **"80 A burst" is credible for roughly 5 s on all four motors only if:**
  - The FETs are at least 5×6 class.
  - Copper is at least 2 oz on four or more layers.
  - Prop-wash airflow is present
