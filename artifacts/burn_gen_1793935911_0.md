## 0. Architectural Clarification (Read First)

Two points shape everything that follows.

**1. Partitioning.** ArduPilot does not run six-step or FOC motor commutation on the STM32H743. The realistic architecture has two parts:
- **FC:** STM32H743 running ArduPilot. It generates bidirectional DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry.
- **ESC:** four independent ESC MCUs (e.g., STM32G071 or AT32F421 running AM32/BLHeli_32-class firmware). Each MCU drives one 3-phase bridge at 48 kHz PWM.

The 48 kHz switching analysis therefore applies to the ESC bridges, not the H743.

**2. "80A burst" on a nano form factor.** At 20×20 mm or even 30.5×30.5 mm, 80A per channel is achievable only as a short burst (a few seconds) with heat sinking into board copper and airflow. Section 3 shows the board is thermal-capacity limited, not junction limited. I model 80A per channel with all four channels loaded simultaneously, which is the worst case.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous PWM

At any instant, two phases carry current I.

- **During D·T_sw:** the high-side FET of phase A and the low-side FET of phase B conduct. Two R_ds are in series.
- **During (1−D)·T_sw:** the high-side of A turns off and the low-side of A synchronously rectifies, together with the low-side of B. Two R_ds are again in series.

The bridge therefore always has exactly two FETs in the current path:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Each FET is active for 1/3 of the electrical period. The per-FET RMS current is

$$I_{FET,rms} = \frac{I}{\sqrt{3}}, \quad I_{phase,rms} = I\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{3}}$$

### 2.2 Conduction loss

The on-resistance rises linearly with junction temperature:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 115 °C:

$$R_{ds} = 1.0 \times (1 + 0.0045 \times 90) = 1.405\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.405\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{17.98\ W}$$

### 2.3 Switching loss (hard-switched leg only)

Only one leg PWM-switches at a time, so per channel:

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.968\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode loss

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM cycle:

$$P_{dt} = 2\,V_f\,I\,t_{dt}\,f_{sw} = 2 \times 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.229\ W}$$

> **Design lever:** reducing dead time to 100 ns halves this term. Verify shoot-through margin against the driver's propagation-delay mismatch.

### 2.5 Reverse recovery, output capacitance, and gate drive

$$P_{rr} = V_{bus} Q_{rr} f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 50\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} Q_{oss} f_{sw} = 0.024\ \text{W}$$

Two FETs switch per cycle, so the gate-drive loss is:

$$P_{gate} = 2\,Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.058\ \text{W}$$

Most of P_gate is dissipated in the gate resistors and driver, not the FETs.

### 2.6 Summary at 80A burst, T_j ≈ 115 °C

| Loss term | Per channel | 4-in-1 total |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 17.98 W | 71.9 W |
| Switching | 0.97 W | 3.9 W |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 W | 4.9 W |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.08 W | 0.3 W |
| Gate | 0.06 W | 0.2 W |
| **Total semiconductor** | **≈20.3 W** | **≈81.3 W** |

**Copper loss (not negligible).** Assume a phase or bus trace of 25 mm × 6 mm in 2 oz copper (70 µm) over 4 layers in parallel. Sheet resistance is about 0.25 mΩ/sq per layer, giving R ≈ 0.26 mΩ effective for the whole path. The loss is then 2 × 80² × 0.26 mΩ ≈ 3.3 W per channel.

**Total burst heat load: ≈95 W for the 4-in-1.**

**Electrical efficiency check.** Input power is 25.2 V × 80 A × 4 = 8064 W. Losses of 95 W correspond to η ≈ 98.8% at the bridge. The loss is low as a percentage but high in power density.
