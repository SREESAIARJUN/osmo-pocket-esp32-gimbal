**Architectural clarification.** On a 4-in-1, the H743 does not switch the bridges. Each ESC channel has its own MCU, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32. That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 sends DShot600 commands and receives the bidirectional eRPM telemetry. Sections 1–3 model the power stage. Section 4 models the H743 timer and DMA side.

**Findings stated up front:**

1. **80 A per ESC is short-burst only on a 20×20 board.** Total stage dissipation is about 27 W per ESC at burst, or about 110 W with all four channels at burst. The board heats at roughly 50 K/s. Expect about 1–2 s on all four channels before reaching T_j limits. This must be enforced in firmware (Section 3.4).
2. **Conduction loss is the dominant term.** It is about 93% of the loss, not switching. Spend your design effort on R_ds(on), copper, and paralleling, not on gate-drive speed.
3. **The current-sense shunt is a hidden thermal problem.** Section 2.6 covers it.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Conduction topology (6-step, synchronous)

In any 60° commutation sector, exactly one phase is "high" (PWM), one is "low" (held on), and one floats.

- **PWM ON:** HS_A + LS_B carry I_ph.
- **PWM OFF:** LS_A (synchronous rectifier) + LS_B carry I_ph.

So exactly two FETs carry the full phase current at every instant, regardless of duty cycle D. This holds outside the dead-time intervals, which are handled in 2.4.

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 RMS including ripple

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\, D(1-D)}{L_{LL}\, f_{sw}}$$

The worst case is at D = 0.5. With L_LL = 15 µH, which is typical for a 2306–2810 class motor line-to-line:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{15\times10^{-6}\times 48\times10^{3}} = 8.75\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 6.4 = 6406\ \text{A}^2$$

Ripple adds only 0.1%, so it can be neglected at 48 kHz. It would become significant at 24 kHz with low-inductance motors.

### 1.3 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on) and self-consistency

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ /\text{K}$$

Using the max datasheet value (1.6 mΩ) for design margin:

| T_j | R_ds(on) | P_cond per ESC at 80 A | Per conducting FET (instantaneous) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.60 mΩ | 20.5 W | 10.2 W |
| 100 °C | 2.26 mΩ | 28.9 W | 14.5 W |
| 125 °C | 2.48 mΩ | **31.8 W** | 15.9 W |

Using the typical value (1.3 mΩ × 1.55 = 2.0 mΩ): **P_cond = 25.6 W per ESC.** This is the baseline figure used below.

Because R_ds rises with temperature, loss rises with temperature. That positive feedback converges only if:

$$\frac{dP}{dT} \cdot R_{th} < 1 \;\Rightarrow\; 2I^2R_{25}\,\alpha\, R_{th,ja,eff} < 1$$
