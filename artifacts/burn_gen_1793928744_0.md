# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #9): System Architecture and Analysis

## 0. Architecture and Scope Decisions

**The H743 does not commutate the motors.** Driving 12 half-bridges with field-oriented or 6-step control, and also running ArduPilot, is not practical on one MCU. ArduPilot has no ESC firmware. The partition is:

```
[STM32H743VIT6 – ArduPilot]
   │  4× Bi-dir DShot600 (single-wire, open-drain-ish, pull-up)
   ▼
[4× AT32F421K8U7 – AM32 firmware, 48 kHz PWM]
   │  3× (HIN/LIN) per ESC
   ▼
[4× FD6288Q 3-phase gate driver] → [24× N-MOSFET, 40 V] → Motors 1-4
```

**Feasibility flag on "nano" plus 80 A burst:** this combination is marginal. Section 3.4 shows that on a 20×20 mm board the burst must stay at 5 s or less, with active airflow. Every number below is parameterised so you can substitute final datasheet values.

**Baseline assumptions** (substitute your BOM values):

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation topology

In 6-step trapezoidal operation (AM32 default) with complementary (synchronous) PWM, at any instant:
- One leg is **PWM-switching**. Its high-side FET conducts for duty D, and its low-side conducts for (1–D) as the synchronous rectifier.
- One leg has its **low-side FET held ON**, carrying the return current.

The phase current I therefore always flows through exactly **two FET channels in series**:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \left[ D\,R_{HS} + (1-D)\,R_{LS} + R_{LS,return} \right] = 2\,I^2 R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

This assumes R_HS = R_LS and ignores the dead-time fraction, which is treated separately in §2.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence (the self-heating loop)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

$$T_j = T_{board} + P_{FET}\,R_{\theta JC}$$

These are coupled. Solve by iteration, or use the closed form for a single FET:

$$P_{FET} = \frac{I^2 R_{25}(1+\alpha(T_b-25))}{1 - I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_{\theta JC}}$$

**Thermal runaway criterion:** the denominator must stay positive, i.e. I²·R₂₅·α·R_θJC < 1.
With 80 A, 1 mΩ, 0.0045, 1.5 K/W: 6400 × 0.001 × 0.0045 × 1.5 = **0.043**. This is stable, with a large margin.

### 1.3 Numerical result at 80 A, T_j ≈ 125 °C

$$P_{cond,FET} = 80^2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 9.6\ \text{W per conducting FET}$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 9.6 = 19.2\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{copper,ch} = 2 \times I^2 R_{pcb} = 2 \times 6400 \times 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega = 3.84\ \text{W}$$

> Copper loss is about 20% of the FET loss. On a nano board, 2 oz minimum outer layers plus stitched inner planes is not optional.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. That gives one hard-switched transition pair per PWM period per channel.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard switching)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

Better estimate of t_r and t_f from gate charge:
$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2} + Q_{gd}}{I_{G}},\quad I_G = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}{R_{G,ext} + R_{G,int} + R_{drv}}$$

The FD6288Q sources about 1.5 A and sinks about 1.8 A. With R_G,ext = 4.7 Ω, t_sw ≈ 15–25 ns, which is consistent with the baseline.

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = V_{bus}\, Q_{rr}\, f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 50\text{nC} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.02\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors)

$$P_{gate} = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2_{FETs} = 60\text{nC}\times10\times48\text{k}\times2 = \mathbf{0.058\ W}$$

### 2.6 Frequency sensitivity

$$\frac{\partial P_{sw,total}}{\partial f_{sw}} = \frac{1.94+0.61+0.06+0.02}{48\text{k}} \approx 55\ \mu\text{W/Hz}$$

Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.3 W per channel. That is about 6% of the total, so conduction dominates at burst current. 48 kHz is justified for low-inductance motors (current ripple), but it is not a big thermal lever.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Per-channel and total loss budget (80 A burst, all four motors)

| Loss | Per channel | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 19.20 W | 76.8 W |
| PCB copper | 3.84 W | 15.4 W |
| Switching (overlap) | 1.94 W | 7.8 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W | 2.4 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.14 W | 0.6 W |
| **Total** | **25.7 W** | **≈103 W** |

**Efficiency at burst:** P_out ≈ 25.2 V × 80 A × 4 × ~0.9 (modulation) ≈ 7.3 kW. Losses are 103 W, so η ≈ 98.6% electrically. The problem is power density, not efficiency.

### 3.2 Steady-state junction rise (hottest FET)

The hottest FET is the low-side return FET, which conducts 100% for 1/3 of the electrical cycle. Its peak instantaneous dissipation is 9.6 W plus its switching share.

$$\Delta T_{jc} = P_{FET} \cdot R_{\theta JC} = 10.6 \times 1.5 = \mathbf{16\ K}$$

Time-averaged over commutation, each FET carries about 1/3 duty as the return FET plus PWM duty. Use the averaged value for the board model and the peak for the junction.

### 3.3 Foster network (transient)

$$Z_{th}(t) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} R_i\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau_i}\right)$$

| Node | R (K/W) | τ | Physical meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Junction→case | 1.5 | ~5 ms | Die/clip |
| Case→PCB | 0.8 | ~50 ms | Solder + thermal vias (≥9 × 0.3 mm vias per pad) |
| PCB spreading (per FET) | 6 | ~2 s | 2 oz / 6-layer |
| Board→air (whole board) | 3.5 K/W total | ~60 s | Prop wash ≈ 5 m/s |

### 3.4 Burst duration limit (board lumped capacitance)

Effective thermal mass of a 20×20 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz board with FETs, solder, and caps:

$$C_{th} \approx m c_p \approx 12\,\text{g} \times 0.7\,\text{J/(g·K)} \approx 8.5\ \text{J/K}$$

For burst t ≪ τ_board (≈ R·C = 3.5 × 8.5 ≈ 30 s), heating is near-adiabatic:

$$\Delta T_{board}(t) = P_{tot} R_{th}\left(1 - e^{-t/RC}\right)$$
