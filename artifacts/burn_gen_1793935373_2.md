## 0. Architecture Assumptions

These assumptions determine every number below, so they come first.

| Item | Assumption | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Form factor | 20×20 mm (M2 or M3 mounting), 8-layer, 2 oz copper on power layers | "Nano" class |
| Battery | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal | 40 V FETs give >1.5× margin on 25.2 V plus ringing |
| FC MCU | STM32H743VIH6 (TFBGA100) or VIT6 (LQFP100) running ArduPilot | Specified |
| ESC MCUs | **4× AT32F421 (or STM32G071) running AM32**, one per motor | See note below |
| ESC PWM | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous), 6-step trapezoidal | Specified |
| MOSFET | 40 V, DFN3.3×3.3, R_DS(on) = 1.0 mΩ at 25 °C, Q_g ≈ 50 nC, Q_rr ≈ 40 nC, R_θJC ≈ 1.5 K/W | Representative best-in-class part for this footprint |
| Gate driver | 3-phase half-bridge driver (FD6288Q class), ~1.5 A source / 1.8 A sink | Standard for this ESC class |
| 80 A rating | **Per-channel burst, 1 motor**; the 4-motor simultaneous case is analyzed separately | Industry labeling convention |

**Architectural note.** ArduPilot does not commutate BLDC motors. The H743 is the flight controller: it generates bidirectional DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry. Commutation at 48 kHz runs on dedicated ESC MCUs. Using the H743 as a direct 12-phase commutator is possible in custom firmware but is incompatible with the ArduPilot requirement. This document therefore models a split AIO, with the FC section and the ESC section on one board.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

| T_j | R_DS(on) |
|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 1.41 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 1.55 mΩ |

The design value is **1.5 mΩ**, which is the hot-burst condition.

### 1.2 Six-step synchronous conduction

During any commutation sector, two phases carry current I:
- **Active PWM leg:** the high-side conducts for duty D and the low-side synchronous FET conducts for (1−D). The leg conducts for 100% of the period.
- **Return leg:** the low-side is on for 100% of the period.

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

At I = 80 A and R = 1.5 mΩ:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 6400 \times 0.0015 = \mathbf{19.2\ W}
$$

**Per-FET distribution.** Each of the 6 FETs conducts about 1/3 of the electrical cycle.
- Instantaneous worst-case loss per FET: I²R = **9.6 W**.
- Cycle-averaged loss per FET: ≈ **3.2 W**.

### 1.3 PCB copper conduction

The path runs from the battery pad through the VBAT plane, the FET, the phase pour, the motor pad, back through the FET, the shunt, and the GND plane. On a 20×20 board with 2 oz power layers, the effective loop resistance is about R_cu ≈ 0.4 mΩ (hot), and the current-sense shunt is 0.25 mΩ.

$$
P_{cu} = I^2 (R_{cu} + R_{shunt}) = 6400 \times 0.00065 = \mathbf{4.2\ W}
$$

### 1.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

With t_dt = 200 ns per edge, two edges per period, and V_F ≈ 0.8 V at 80 A:

$$
P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2\,t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{ns} \times 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}
$$

This is a significant term at 48 kHz. Reducing dead time to 100 ns halves it, but only if the gate-drive layout allows it without shoot-through.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

In six-step operation only the active leg hard-switches.

### 2.1 V-I overlap

Assume t_r + t_f ≈ 20 ns at 80 A with a ~1.5 A driver and Q_gd ≈ 10 nC.

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12\, V_{bus}\, I\,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ns} \times 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 40\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance (E_oss)

$$
P_{oss} = E_{oss}\, f_{sw} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 50\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.030\ W}
$$

### 2.4 Gate charge

This loss is dissipated mainly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET. Two FETs switch per active leg.

$$
P_g = 2\, Q_g\, V_{GS}\, f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{nC} \times 10\text{V} \times 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

### 2.5 Switching sub-total

$$
P_{sw,ch} = 0.97 + 0.048 + 0.030 + 0.048 \approx \mathbf{1.10\ W}
$$

### 2.6 Frequency sensitivity

| f_sw | P_VI + P_rr + P_oss + P_g | P_dt | Sum |
|---|---|---|---|
| 24 kHz | 0.55 W | 0.61 W | 1.16 W |
| 48 kHz | 1.10 W | 1.23 W | 2.33 W |
| 96 kHz | 2.19 W | 2.46 W | 4.65 W |

At 48 kHz, frequency-dependent losses are about 10% of the total at 80 A. **Conduction dominates; R_DS(on) is the lever that matters.**
