## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and a Feasibility Verdict

These architectural facts determine the rest of the design:

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot on the H743 sends DShot600 frames to four ESC MCUs and receives bi-directional eRPM telemetry from them. The ESC MCUs (STM32G071 or AT32F421) run AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, which performs 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification at 48 kHz. The two domains are modeled separately.
2. **The "80A burst" rating is interpreted as 80 A phase current per motor, sustained for seconds.** It is not an instantaneous peak.
3. **The pack is 6S:** 25.2 V maximum, 22.2 V nominal. The loss model uses 25.2 V as the worst case.
4. **MOSFET parameters are representative** of a 40 V N-channel device in a 3.3×3.3 mm DFN package with the best available R_DS(on). Substitute your selected part's datasheet values into the equations. Do not use these numbers for sign-off.

**Feasibility verdict.** An 80 A burst on a "nano" board (20×20 or 30×30 mm) is physically limited by board heat capacity, not by the MOSFETs' current rating. The analysis below shows the following for a 30×30 mm board at 40 °C ambient:
- All four motors at 80 A: about 4 s.
- One motor at 80 A: considerably longer.
- Continuous rating: about 25 A per motor.

These numbers match what the commercial market ships at this size. Advertise 80 A as a short burst with an explicit time limit. Otherwise the board will fail in the field.
