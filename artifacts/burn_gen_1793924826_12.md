# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC "Nano" Stack (Job #36): System Architecture & Mathematical Model

**Document class:** Design basis, Rev A
**Scope:** Power-stage loss model (80 A burst, 48 kHz PWM), thermal transient model, ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 timer and DMA allocation, and reference netlists.

---

## 0. Architecture Decision and Feasibility Flags

Two findings shape the rest of the design. Review them before committing to the spec.

1. **The H743 cannot commutate the motors.** ArduPilot running on the H743 is a flight controller. It cannot also run four sensorless BLDC commutation loops at 48 kHz. The architecture is therefore:
   - **FC:** STM32H743VIH6, running ArduPilot.
   - **ESC:** 4× dedicated ESC MCUs (AT32F421 or STM32G071), running AM32 firmware.
   - **Link:** bidirectional DShot600 between the two.

   This matches every production "H743 + 4-in-1" stack.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is a thermal-capacity rating, not a continuous rating.** The model in §3 shows:
   - Four channels at 80 A dissipate about 80 W.
   - A nano board reaches T<sub>j,max</sub> in roughly **2–3 s**.
   - The spec must therefore define burst as "per-channel 80 A, ≤1 s, ≥10 s recovery," or the format must grow to 30×30.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R<sub>DS(on)</sub>

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\left(\frac{T_j + 273}{298}\right)^{\alpha},\quad \alpha \approx 2.0
$$

At T<sub>j</sub> = 125 °C, using the max datasheet value:

$$
R_{DS}(125) = 1.15 \times (398/298)^2 = 2.05\ \text{m}\Omega \;(\text{worst})
$$

For the typical device, R<sub>DS</sub>(125) ≈ 1.6 mΩ. The model uses **1.3 mΩ at T<sub>j</sub> ≈ 90 °C** as the burst design point.

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step synchronous PWM

In every commutation sector exactly two legs carry the phase current I:

- **PWM leg:** current flows through the high-side switch for duty D and through the synchronous low-side switch for (1−D), minus dead time.
- **Return leg:** current flows through the low-side switch for 100% of the sector.

Ignoring the dead-time interval (handled in §2.4), the current always sees **two R<sub>DS</sub> in series**:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

In 6-step operation each phase conducts for 2/3 of the electrical cycle, so I<sub>rms,phase</sub> = I·√(2/3). The bus-side two-switch formula above already accounts for this.

**Per-FET distribution.** Each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical period.

- Low-side, return role: I²R·(1/6).
- High-side or sync-low, PWM role: I²R·(1/6)·D or I²R·(1/6)·(1−D).

### 1.3 Numbers at the 80 A burst point (R = 1.3 mΩ)

| Quantity | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| P<sub>cond,ch</sub> | 2 × 80² × 1.3 mΩ | **16.6 W** |
| P<sub>cond</sub> per FET (average) | 16.6 / 6 | 2.77 W |
| P<sub>cond</sub> worst FET (low side) | (80² × 1.3 m)/3 | 2.77 W, with 8.3 W instantaneous while on |
| PCB copper | path ≈ 0.4 mΩ (2 oz, 6L, parallel planes): 80² × 0.4 m | 2.6 W |

The **8.3 W instantaneous per-FET pulse** sets the die-level ΔT (§3.3).
