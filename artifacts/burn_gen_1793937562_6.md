## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At a design-point T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{DS}(125) = 0.70 \times (1 + 0.5) = 1.05\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Six-step commutation with synchronous rectification

During each commutation sector, one high-side FET (H) and one low-side FET (L) on different legs carry the phase current I. The PWM leg alternates between H (on-time D) and its complementary L (off-time 1−D):

$$P_{cond,H} = D\,I^2 R_{DS},\qquad P_{cond,L,comp} = (1-D)\,I^2 R_{DS}$$

$$P_{cond,leg} = I^2 R_{DS}\quad\text{(independent of D)}$$

The return leg's low-side FET conducts 100% of the sector:

$$P_{cond,return} = I^2 R_{DS}$$

**Per channel (two current paths):**

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 1.3 Numerical result at 80 A

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.05\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{13.44\ W}$$

Hottest single FET (the return-leg low side, 100% conduction): **6.72 W**.

Add the PCB copper: a phase path of ~0.15 mΩ (trace + pads + solder joints) adds about 2 × 80² × 0.15 mΩ ≈ **1.9 W** per channel into the board.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-leg high-side FET hard-switches. Its complementary low side commutates near zero voltage, but body-diode conduction and reverse recovery during dead-time still apply.

### 2.1 Transition times

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,src}} = \frac{12\,\text{nC}}{1.0\,\text{A (effective)}} \approx 12\ \text{ns},\qquad t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,snk}} \approx 8\ \text{ns}$$

Use 15 ns each to allow for gate resistors and common-source inductance (t_r + t_f = 30 ns).

### 2.2 Loss components

| Term | Equation | Value @ 25.2 V, 80 A, 48 kHz |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | $\tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}$ | 0.5·25.2·80·30 ns·48k = **1.45 W** |
| Reverse recovery | $Q_{rr} V f_{sw}$ | 50 nC·25.2·48k = **0.060 W** |
| Output capacitance | $Q_{oss} V f_{sw}$ (effective) | 40 nC·25.2·48k = **0.048 W** |
| Dead-time body diode | $V_F\,I\,(2 t_{dt}) f_{sw}$, t_dt = 100 ns, V_F = 0.8 V | 0.8·80·200 ns·48k = **0.614 W** |
| Gate drive (driver IC) | $2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw}$ (H+L of active leg) | 2·60 nC·10 V·48k = **0.058 W** |
| **Total switching (per channel)** | | **≈ 2.23 W** |

**Optimization note:** dead-time is the second-largest term. Cutting t_dt from 100 ns to 50 ns (AM32 dead-time setting plus a fast driver) saves about 0.3 W per channel. Verify shoot-through margin on the scope first.

## 3. Thermal Budget

### 3.1 Total dissipation at 80 A burst

| Source | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 13.44 W | 53.8 W |
| FET switching | 2.23 W | 8.9 W |
| PCB copper I²R | 1.9 W | 7.7 W |
| Bulk cap ESR (see §3.4) | ~0.3 W | 1.2 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 17.9 W** | **≈ 71.6 W** |

Hottest FET: 6.72 W (conduction) plus at most ~1.0 W if it is also the switching leg in that sector. Sectors rotate, so time-averaged this is **≈ 5.5 W per FET (worst device)**.

### 3.2 Junction temperature (quasi-steady, with airflow)

$$T_j = T_{amb} + P_{FET}\,\big(R_{\theta jc} + R_{\theta c\text{-}pcb} + R_{\theta pcb\text{-}a,share}\big)$$

| Term | Value |
|---|---|
| R_θjc (5×6 package) | 1.0 K/W |
| R_θ case→PCB (solder + vias, 30 thermal vias Ø0.3 mm) | 2.5 K/W |
| R_θ PCB→air, per-FET share (12 FETs, prop-wash ≈ 5 m/s) | ~8 K/W |

$$T_j = 40 + 5.5 \times 11.5 \approx \mathbf{103\ °C}$$

This is the *steady-state* answer, but the board-level limit is reached first (see §3.3).

### 3.3 Burst duration: lumped thermal capacitance

Board thermal mass (30.5² mm, 8-layer, 1.6 mm, heavy copper, plus FETs and caps):

- Copper ≈ 4.5 g × 0.385 J/gK = 1.73 J/K
- FR4 ≈ 2.5 g × 1.1 J/gK = 2.75 J/K
- Components ≈ 2 g × 0.7 J/gK = 1.4 J/K
- **C_th ≈ 5.9 J/K**

Total board-to-air resistance with airflow: R_θ,ba ≈ 1.2 K/W. This gives τ = R·C ≈ 7 s.

$$\Delta T(t) = P\,R_{\theta}\,\big(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\big)$$

The steady-state ΔT would be 71.6 × 1.2 ≈ 86 K. Limit the board to 105 °C from 40 °C ambient (ΔT = 65 K):
