# STM32H743 FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC (Nano): Architecture, Loss Model, Thermal Model, DShot DMA Allocation

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Two Early Findings

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 cannot be the ESC commutation MCU here. ArduPilot on the H743 is the flight controller. Each motor channel needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, connected over bidirectional DShot600. This document therefore models:

- **FC:** STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100, 400 MHz) running ArduPilot.
- **ESC:** 4 × AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, with 3-phase gate drivers and 24 MOSFETs.

**Finding 1: 80A on a nano board is burst-only.** At 80A per channel on a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm board, total dissipation is about 85 W. No nano-class board can sink that continuously. The thermal model in §3 makes this explicit and gives the allowable burst duration.

**Finding 2: The netlist is architectural, not production.** The netlist in §5 is a reference netlist covering the critical nets. A production netlist must come from your capture tool after part selection is final.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

The ESC runs trapezoidal six-step commutation with synchronous rectification (AM32 complementary PWM). At any instant, the phase current flows through exactly two MOSFETs:

- During the PWM **on** interval: high-side of phase A, then the motor, then low-side of phase B.
- During the PWM **off** interval: low-side of phase A (synchronous freewheel), then the motor, then low-side of phase B.

Either way, two channels in series carry the current. With synchronous rectification, conduction loss is therefore approximately independent of duty cycle.

### 1.2 Equations

$$R_{ds,hot} = \alpha_R \cdot R_{ds,25} = 1.5 \times 0.95\,\text{m}\Omega = 1.425\,\text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds,hot} = 80^2 \times 2.85\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{18.2\ W}$$

**Per-FET distribution.** Each phase leg conducts for 240° of 360° electrical. The high-side FET carries D·I² for 120°. The low-side FET carries (1−D)·I² for 120° (sync rectification) plus I² for 120° (commutation return).

| Quantity | Formula | At D = 0.9 (burst at full throttle) |
|---|---|---|
| High-side FET | (1/3)·D·I²·R | 2.74 W |
| Low-side FET | (1/3)·(2−D)·I²·R | 3.34 W |

Instantaneous peak per FET while conducting is I²·R = 9.1 W. Over a commutation period of about 0.1–1 ms, the die (τ ≈ 1–5 ms) partially averages this. Use 1.3× the average as the peak-die-temperature correction.

### 1.3 Copper and shunt losses

These are frequently ignored and are significant at 80A.

| Element | Value | Loss at 80A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase pour, 2 oz copper, ~8 mm long, 3 mm wide, 2 layers paralleled | ≈ 0.25 mΩ hot | 1.6 W per channel |
| Bus path (pads → caps → FETs) | ≈ 0.1 mΩ | — |
| Current shunt | 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W **per shunt** |

The bus-path term is shared across channels. Because the bus current is about D·ΣI, at 4×80A this term is large; include it in the total.

$$P_{Cu,ch} \approx 1.6\ \text{W (phase)} + \tfrac{1}{4}(0.1\,\text{m}\Omega\times(0.9\cdot320)^2) \approx 1.6 + 2.1 = 3.7\ \text{W}$$

**Recommendation:** use phase-current sensing through R_ds(on) or a single 0.2 mΩ bus shunt. Do not use per-channel shunts on a nano board.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Hard-switching transition times

Gate current during the plateau:

| Edge | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Turn-on | (V_drv − V_pl)/R_g | (10 − 3.2)/4 = 1.70 A |
| Turn-off | V_pl/R_g | 3.2/4 = 0.80 A |

Transition times (Q_sw = Q_gs2 + Q_gd = 10 nC):

$$t_r = \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{g,on}} = 5.9\,\text{ns}, \qquad t_f = \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{g,off}} = 12.5\,\text{ns}$$

Common-source inductance and layout parasitics (about 1–2 nH in DFN) typically stretch these by 1.5–2×. Use t_r ≈ 10 ns and t_f ≈ 20 ns.

### 2.2 Loss components

Only the PWM-switching high-side FET hard-switches. The low-side sync FET performs zero-voltage switching (ZVS) after dead time.

| Mechanism | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V·I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = 0.5·25.2·80·30 ns·48k | **1.45 W** |
| Coss | Q_oss·V·f_sw = 40n·25.2·48k | 0.05 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 50n·25.2·48k | 0.06 W |
| Dead-time diode | V_f·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·200 ns·48k | **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (in driver IC) | 6·Q_g·V_drv·f_sw = 6·45n·10·48k | 0.13 W |
| **Total per channel** | | **≈ 2.3 W** |

### 2.3 Frequency sensitivity

Every switching term scales linearly with f_sw:

$$P_{sw}(f) = 2.3\,\text{W} \times \frac{f}{48\,\text{kHz}}$$

| f_sw | P_sw per channel |
|---|---|
| 24 kHz | 1.15 W |
| 48 kHz | 2.3 W |
| 96 kHz | 4.6 W |

At 80A, 48 kHz is a reasonable choice. The main lever is dead time. AM32 lets you trim it, and every 25 ns removed saves about 0.15 W per channel. Trimming must be validated against shoot-through on the bench.
