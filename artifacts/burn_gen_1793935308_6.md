## 0. Architecture Clarification and Design Assumptions

Three points shape everything below.

1. **Two separate processors handle control.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller. It generates DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry. The 48 kHz PWM commutation runs on a **separate ESC MCU per motor** (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32). The H743 never drives gates directly. This is how every production 4-in-1 stack works, and it keeps the 400 MHz H7 free for EKF3.

2. **80 A is a burst rating, not a continuous one.** Section 4 shows that a nano-class board (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm mounting) can sustain roughly 25–35 A per motor continuously in prop-wash. The 80 A figure is defensible only as a time-limited burst (about 1–3 s).

3. **Numerical assumptions.** These are placeholders. Substitute datasheet values when the BOM is frozen.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

With complementary (synchronous) PWM and 6-step trapezoidal commutation, motor current always flows through **exactly two FETs in series**:

- During the on-time: the high-side FET of phase A and the low-side FET of phase B.
- During the off-time: the low-side FET of A (synchronous freewheel) and the low-side FET of B.

The phase current therefore sees 2·R_DS(on) at every instant, independent of duty cycle (dead-time excluded, handled in §2).

### 1.2 Equations

R_DS(on)(T_j) = R₂₅ · [1 + k·(T_j − 25)], with k ≈ 0.0059 /K, giving the 1.5× factor at 110 °C.

P_cond,motor = I_rms² · 2 · R_DS(on)(T_j)

For trapezoidal drive the phase current during conduction is approximately flat, so I_rms ≈ I_pk = 80 A.

### 1.3 Results

| Condition | R_DS(on) | P_cond per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cold (25 °C) | 1.0 mΩ | 80² × 2 × 1.0 m = **12.8 W** | 51.2 W |
| Hot (110 °C) | 1.5 mΩ | 80² × 2 × 1.5 m = **19.2 W** | 76.8 W |

**Worst single device.** The freewheeling low-side carries current during both phases of the PWM cycle within its 120° commutation window. Its peak instantaneous dissipation is I²·R = 80² × 1.5 mΩ = **9.6 W**. Use this value for the junction-rise calculation in §4.

**Thermal runaway check.** Because R_DS(on) rises with temperature, iterate: P → T_j → R → P. With the R_θ values in §4 the loop converges below 125 °C for bursts under about 2 s. It does **not** converge under continuous 80 A, which confirms the burst-only rating.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd high-side hard-switches each cycle. The low-side commutates softly through its body diode during dead-time.

| Loss term | Equation | Value per motor |
|---|---|---|
| Hard-switching overlap | ½ · V_bus · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40n × 48k | **1.94 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_F · I · 2·t_dt · f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 200n × 48k | **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | V_bus · Q_rr · f_sw = 25.2 × 50n × 48k | **0.06 W** |
| C_oss discharge | ½ · C_oss · V_bus² · f_sw = 0.5 × 1.5n × 635 × 48k | **0.02 W** |
| Gate drive (lost in driver/R_g, not the FET) | Q_g · V_GS · f_sw × 2 = 60n × 10 × 48k × 2 | **0.06 W** |
| **Switching subtotal** | | **≈ 2.69 W** |

### 2.1 Total burst dissipation

| | Per motor | 4-in-1 total |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (hot) | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching | 2.7 W | 10.8 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, if in-line) | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| Bus copper and connectors (est.) | ~1.5 W | ~6 W |
| **Total** | **~25 W** | **~100 W** |

### 2.2 Design levers

- **Moving from 48 kHz to 24 kHz** saves about 1.3 W per motor in switching loss, at the cost of higher current ripple and audible noise. Switching loss is only about 11% of the total, so **R_DS(on) dominates; spend BOM budget on FETs, not on faster edges.**
- **Reducing dead-time** from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per motor. Verify against shoot-through margin using the driver's propagation-delay mismatch.

## 3. ArduPilot Bi-Directional DShot600: Timer and DMA Allocation

### 3.1 Protocol timing

| Item | Derivation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Bit rate | | 600 kbit/s |
| Bit period | 1/600k | 1.667 µs |
| Command frame | 16 bits | 26.7 µs |
| ESC turnaround | per spec | ~30 µs |
| Telemetry (GCR, inverted line) | 21 bits at 5/4 × 600k = 750 kbit/s | ~28 µs |
| **Full bidir transaction** | | **~85 µs** (+ guard) |

At a loop rate of 400 Hz–2 kHz this leaves ample headroom. Even an 8 kHz output rate (125 µs period) fits.

### 3.2 Timer clocking (H743 at 400 MHz, ArduPilot default clock tree)

APB1 and APB2 timer clocks run at 200 MHz. ArduPilot's `RCOutput` computes the prescaler so that each DShot bit spans a fixed number of timer ticks. The firmware calculates the prescaler and ARR automatically. You do **not** hand-set them in hwdef. The design constraint is that all channels on one timer share one DShot rate and one prescaler, so **group motors by timer.**

### 3.3 Pin and timer selection rules

1. **No complementary outputs (`TIMx_CHyN`) for bidir motors.** Input capture is impossible on N channels, so eRPM telemetry will fail.
2. **Use 2 motors per timer, on 2 timers.** This allows the two DShot bursts to overlap and isolates faults.
