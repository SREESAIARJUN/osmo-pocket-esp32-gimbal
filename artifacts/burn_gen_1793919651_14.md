# STM32H743 AIO (4-in-1 ESC) Design Package: Loss Model, Thermal Budget, and DShot/DMA Allocation

## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility Warning

"Job #40" isn't defined in this conversation, so the assumptions below are stated explicitly. Replace them with your actual BOM values and the equations stay valid.

| Parameter | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Bus voltage | 6S, 25.2 V full charge (worst case for switching) |
| "80 A burst" | 80 A **phase current per ESC channel**, all 4 channels simultaneously |
| Commutation / PWM | 6-step trapezoidal, synchronous (complementary) PWM, 48 kHz (AM32/BLHeli_32 style) |
| MOSFET (representative) | 40 V, R_DS(on) = 1.2 mΩ max @ 25 °C, Q_g = 50 nC, Q_gd + Q_gs2 ≈ 14 nC, Q_rr ≈ 40 nC, V_plateau ≈ 3 V |
| Gate drive | 10 V supply, ~10 Ω effective on-path, ~7 Ω off-path |
| Dead time | 200 ns |
| Target T_j | ≤ 110 °C |

**Architectural note:** in an AIO, the H743 does **not** switch the FETs. Each ESC channel has its own MCU (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32) driving a 3-phase gate driver. The H743 only emits bidirectional DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry. The 48 kHz switching losses therefore belong to the ESC MCUs' PWM, not to H743 timers.

**Feasibility warning:** the numbers below show that a continuous 4 × 80 A on a 20×20 mm "nano" is not physically achievable. At best it is a sub-few-second burst rating. Section 3 quantifies this.
