# STM32H743 Nano AIO with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #17): Power Stage, Thermal, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Architecture

## 0. Assumptions

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does not commutate motors. Each of the 4 ESC channels has its own MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32) driving a 3-phase gate driver and 6 MOSFETs at 48 kHz PWM. The H743 talks to the ESCs over bi-directional DShot600.

**Feasibility.** 80 A burst on all four channels of a nano (20×20 / 25.5×25.5 mm) board is achievable only for seconds. The math in Section 3 shows why. Treat "80 A" as a short-burst rating backed by a firmware thermal limiter, not a continuous rating.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous (complementary) PWM

In each commutation sector, current I flows from one phase to another:

- **Switching leg:** the high-side (HS) is on for duty D. Its complementary low-side (LS) carries freewheel current for (1−D), apart from dead time.
- **Return leg:** the LS is on for 100% of the sector.

The instantaneous conducting resistance is always one FET in the switching leg plus one FET in the return leg:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

This result is independent of D, which is the benefit of synchronous rectification. Note that I here is the **phase** current. Battery current is I_bat ≈ D·I_phase, so 80 A battery current at full throttle (D → 1) means roughly 80 A phase current. At partial throttle the same battery current implies higher phase current.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence and self-consistent T_j

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

Solve iteratively at T_j ≈ 110 °C:

$$R_{DS}(110) = 1.0\,\text{mΩ} \times (1 + 0.006 \times 85) = 1.51\ \text{mΩ}$$
