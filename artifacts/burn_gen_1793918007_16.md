## 0. Architectural Clarifications

These points constrain everything that follows.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** A production-viable 4-in-1 ESC uses one dedicated MCU per motor running AM32 (or BLHeli_32-class firmware). The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to those ESC MCUs. The 48 kHz switching frequency is set by the ESC MCUs, not the H743. Putting FOC/6-step for four motors on the H743 alongside ArduPilot is possible in principle, but unsupported by ArduPilot and not recommended.
2. **"80A burst" is interpreted as per-motor, ≤ 5 s, 6S (25.2 V max).** On a 30.5×30.5 mm or smaller board, the math below shows this is thermally capacity-limited, not resistance-limited. The continuous rating will be much lower, roughly 25–30 A per channel.
3. **Assumed component parameters must be replaced with datasheet values** once parts are frozen. All equations are kept symbolic so they can be re-evaluated.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Paths (6-step, synchronous rectification, complementary PWM)

In any commutation sector, two phases carry current:

- **PWM leg:** current flows through the high-side switch for a fraction D of the period, and through the low-side switch (synchronous rectification) for (1−D).
- **Return leg:** the low-side switch is on for 100% of the sector.

Therefore, at any instant, exactly two MOSFETs carry I_ph (ignoring dead time):

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph,rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)\,\big[D + (1-D)\big] + I_{ph,rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) = 2\,I_{ph,rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

### 1.2 Ripple Correction

The peak-to-peak ripple current is:

$$\Delta I_{pp}=\frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L\,f_{sw}} \;\xrightarrow{D=0.5}\; \frac{25.2\cdot0.25}{20\,\mu\text{H}\cdot48\,\text{kHz}} = 6.56\ \text{A}$$

The resulting RMS current is:

$$I_{rms}=\sqrt{I_{ph}^2+\tfrac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}}=\sqrt{6400+3.59}=80.02\ \text{A}$$

The ripple contribution is negligible at 48 kHz, which is one benefit of the higher switching frequency.

### 1.3 Electro-Thermal Fixed Point

Conduction loss raises T_j, which raises R_DS(on), which raises loss. Solve iteratively:

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + R_{\theta JC}\cdot I^2 R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

With T_pcb = 90 °C at the end of the burst:

| Iteration | T_j (°C) | R_DS (mΩ) | P per FET (W) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 100 | 1.30 | 8.32 |
| 1 | 102.5 | 1.31 | 8.38 |
| 2 (converged) | **102.6** | **1.31** | **8.38** |

The fixed point is stable when the loop gain is below 1:

$$R_{\theta JC}\,I^2R_{25}\alpha = 1.5 \cdot 6400 \cdot 0.001 \cdot 0.004 = 0.038 \ll 1$$

So there is no thermal runaway at the device level. Board-level runaway is a different question, covered in §3.

**Conduction loss per channel:**

$$P_{cond,ch} \approx 2 \times 8.38 = 16.8\ \text{W}$$
