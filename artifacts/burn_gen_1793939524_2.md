# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage, Thermal, and Bi-directional DShot600 Design Package

## 0. Architecture Assumptions

Some parameters in the request are ambiguous. These assumptions fix them; change them and the equations still hold.

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Topology | AIO board. The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot. Each of the 4 ESC channels has its own MCU (AT32F421 class running AM32) and a 3-phase gate driver. The H743 does **not** commutate motors; it sends bidirectional DShot600 to each ESC MCU. |
| Bus | 6S LiPo: 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal. Design at V_bus = 25.2 V. |
| "80 A burst" | 80 A motor (phase-path) current **per channel**, all 4 channels simultaneously, for a short duration. |
| ESC PWM | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous rectification), 6-step trapezoidal. |
| MOSFET (placeholder, 40 V DFN5x6 class) | R_DS(on) = 1.0 mΩ @25 °C (Vgs = 10 V); t_r + t_f = 20 ns at the operating gate drive; Q_g = 50 nC; Q_oss = 40 nC; Q_rr = 30 nC; V_SD = 0.8 V; R_θJC = 1.5 K/W. |
| Dead time | 50 ns per edge. |
| PCB | 30.5 × 30.5 mm, 8 layers, 2 oz copper. Lumped bus + phase copper path ≈ 0.3 mΩ per channel. |

> **Engineering flag:** At 80 A × 4 the stage dissipates roughly 90 W. No 30.5 mm board can dissipate that continuously. The results below show this is a **seconds-scale burst rating** limited by board heat capacity. A 20 × 20 mm "nano" form factor would roughly halve the available burst time again.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path
In 6-step drive, two phases conduct in each 60° sector. With complementary PWM there is always exactly one MOSFET in each of the two active legs:

- During the on-time: HS(x) → motor → LS(y)
- During the off-time: LS(x) (synchronous rectification) → motor → LS(y)

So the current always passes through **two** channels in series. That gives:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + I^2 R_{Cu}$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence
$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.0 mΩ × 1.5 = **1.5 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A
$$P_{FET,cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.5\text{ m}\Omega = 6400 \times 3\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W/ch}$$

$$P_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.3\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{1.92\ W/ch}$$

### 1.4 Per-device distribution
Each FET conducts for 120° of the electrical cycle. Over that window the HS/LS split depends on duty D. Averaged over a rotation, the 19.2 W spreads across 6 devices:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx 19.2/6 = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

- At high duty (D → 1), high-side FETs carry most of it.
- The instantaneous dissipation inside a FET's conduction window is I²R = 9.6 W.
- Electrical frequencies are in the kHz range, so the die thermal time constant (ms) averages this out.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing leg hard-switches, so there is **one hard-switched transition pair per channel per PWM cycle**.

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·20 ns·48 kHz | **0.968 W** |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss·V·f_sw = 40 nC·25.2·48k | 0.048 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 30 nC·25.2·48k | 0.036 W |
| Dead-time body diode | V_SD·I·2·t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·100 ns·48k | 0.307 W |
| Gate charge (mostly in driver and R_g) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw = 2·50 nC·10·48k | 0.048 W |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 1.41 W/ch** |

**Sensitivities:**
- Overlap loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it would be ≈ 0.70 W/ch.
- Dead-time loss dominates the second-order terms. Trimming dead time to 30 ns saves about 0.12 W/ch.
- The hottest device is the PWM-ing high side. It carries about 1.41/3 ≈ 0.47 W of switching loss averaged over rotation, on top of its conduction share.

## 3. Total Loss Budget at 4 × 80 A

| Source | Per channel | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (T_j = 125 °C) | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Copper | 1.92 W | 7.7 W |
| Switching | 1.41 W | 5.6 W |
| Gate driver, LDO, ESC MCU | ~0.15 W | 0.6 W |
| **Total** | **~22.7 W** | **~90.7 W** |

**Efficiency:** output power ≈ 25.2 V × D × 80 A. At D ≈ 0.9 this is about 1.8 kW per channel, so efficiency ≈ 98.7%. The efficiency is fine; the problem is power density.
