# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model (Job #61)

## 0. Architecture and Feasibility

Before the math, three constraints shape the design.

1. **The H743 should be the flight controller, not the ESC MCU.** ArduPilot runs on the H743. Motor commutation runs on four separate ESC MCUs (or one per pair) running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, e.g. STM32G071 or AT32F421. ArduPilot cannot commutate motors, and 48 kHz six-step control on four motors would compete with the flight loop. The design below follows this split.
2. **80 A burst on a nano footprint is limited by heat capacity, not by the FETs.** Section 3 shows that four channels at 80 A dissipate about 90 W. With about 6 J/K of board heat capacity, that buys only a few seconds. "80 A burst" must be specified with a duration and duty cycle.
3. **Component values are representative placeholders, not datasheet values.** They are flagged with †. Replace them with your chosen MOSFET's datasheet figures, and validate with thermocouple or IR testing.

**Operating assumptions**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal |
| f_sw | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous) PWM, trapezoidal six-step |
| Burst current | I = 80 A per channel (DC-link phase current during conduction) |
| MOSFET† (40 V, 3.3×3.3 or 5×6 mm) | R_ds(on),25 = 0.9 mΩ @ V_gs = 10 V; Q_g = 60 nC; Q_rr = 60 nC; Q_oss = 50 nC; t_r = t_f = 20 ns at 80 A with the chosen gate resistor; V_SD = 0.8 V |
| Dead time | t_dt = 100 ns |
| Board | 30.5×30.5 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz copper on all layers |

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\qquad \alpha\approx0.005\ /\mathrm{K}^\dagger$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_hot = 0.9 × 1.5 = **1.35 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-step current path

In each 60° commutation sector, current flows through exactly one leg's high-side path and another leg's low-side path. With complementary PWM at duty D:

- **Sourcing leg:** the HS FET conducts for D; the LS FET (synchronous rectification) conducts for (1−D).
- **Sinking leg:** the LS FET is held on for 100% of the sector.

At every instant, therefore, two FET channels are in series with the motor current.

$$P_{cond,ch}=2I^2R_{hot}=2(80)^2(1.35\,\text{m}\Omega)=\mathbf{17.3\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Over an electrical cycle, each leg sources for 1/3 of the time and sinks for 1/3:

$$P_{HS}=\tfrac{D}{3}I^2R,\qquad P_{LS}=\tfrac{1}{3}I^2R+\tfrac{1-D}{3}I^2R$$

| D | P_HS | P_LS | Leg total |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 (full burst) | 2.88 W | 2.88 W | 5.76 W |
| 0.5 | 1.44 W | 4.32 W | 5.76 W |

At part throttle, the **low-side FETs run hotter**. Place them nearest the ground-plane thermal vias.

### 1.4 Copper path loss

Assume R_Cu ≈ 0.5 mΩ† for the round trip: pads, pours, and the shared ground return.

$$P_{Cu}=I^2R_{Cu}=\mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

Copper resistance rises about 0.39 %/K, so recompute this term at operating temperature.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the sourcing leg switches, so there is one hard-switched commutation pair per channel at any time.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = ½ · 25.2 · 80 · 40 ns · 48 k | **1.94 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw = 60 nC · 25.2 · 48 k | 0.07 W |
| Coss | ½ · Q_oss · V · f_sw | 0.03 W |
| Dead-time body diode | V_SD · I · 2 · t_dt · f_sw = 0.8 · 80 · 200 ns · 48 k | **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2 · Q_g · V_drv · f_sw = 2 · 60 nC · 10 V · 48 k | 0.06 W (in the driver/regulator, not the FETs) |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.65 W** |

The overlap loss is distributed across all three HS FETs over the cycle, adding about 0.65 W to each.

**Effect of switching frequency.** Overlap and dead-time losses scale linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz they would total about 1.3 W per channel instead of 2.65 W. The 48 kHz setting is justified only if you need the lower current ripple for low-inductance motors.

### 2.1 Totals at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

| | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 17.3 W | 69.1 W |
| Copper | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| Switching + dead time | 2.65 W | 10.6 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 23.1 W** | **≈ 92.5 W** |

Efficiency at 80 A × 25.2 V × 4 ≈ 8 kW input is about 98.8%. The problem is not efficiency but where the 92 W goes in 9 cm².

### 2.2 Hottest single FET (D = 1)

P_FET ≈ 2.88 W (conduction) + 0.65 W (switching) ≈ **3.5 W**.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Junction-local rise (fast, milliseconds)

Use a Foster network from the MOSFET datasheet:

$$Z_{th,jc}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}R_i\left(1-e^{-t/\tau_i}\right)$$

With R_th,jc ≈ 1.0 K/W† and a pad-to-board spreading resistance of about 8 K/W† (2 oz copper, 8–12 vias of 0.3 mm under the pad):

$$\Delta T_{j-board,ss}\approx3.5\times(1+8)=31.5\ \text{K}$$

The local junction-to-board rise therefore settles at about 30 K above the board within roughly 100–500 ms.

### 3.2 Board bulk heat capacity (seconds)

| Mass | Calculation | C_th |
|---|---|---|
| Copper | 9 cm² × 6 × 70 µm = 0.378 cm³ → 3.39 g × 0.385 J/g·K | 1.31 J/K |
| FR4 | 1.44 cm³ × 1.85 g/cm³ × 1.1 J/g·K | 2.93 J/K |
| Components (FETs, caps, MCUs) | estimate | ~1.5 J/K |
| **C_board** | | **≈ 5.7 J/K** |

### 3.3 Board-to-air resistance

Use h = 50–100 W/m²K under propwash, A ≈ 18 cm² (both faces):

$$R_{ba}=\frac{1}{hA}=5.5\text{–}11\ \text{K/W},\qquad \tau=R_{ba}C\approx31\text{–}63\ \text{s}$$
