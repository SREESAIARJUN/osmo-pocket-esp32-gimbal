# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80 A ESC (Job #13): Architecture, Loss Model, DShot DMA Plan

## 0. Architecture Decisions

These decisions determine everything else, so they come first.

| # | Decision | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | **The H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller only.** Each ESC channel has its own commutation MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32-class firmware). | ArduPilot has no in-tree FOC/6-step motor commutation. Commutating 4 motors at 48 kHz on the FC core would fight the EKF/scheduler for CPU time. |
| A2 | **The FC→ESC link is bidirectional DShot600.** RPM comes back via eRPM telemetry for the harmonic notch. | This is native in ArduPilot (`BIDIR` hwdef tag, `SERVO_BLH_BDMASK`). |
| A3 | **The 80 A figure is a ≤5 s phase-current burst per channel on 6S.** It is not continuous. | The math below shows that 4×80 A continuous is physically impossible on a nano (≤36 mm) board without a heatsink. |
| A4 | **Footprint is a 30.5 mm mount, 36×36 mm board.** It uses 8-layer construction with 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner power planes, and 5×6 mm PQFN MOSFETs. | A true 20×20 "nano" cannot carry 80 A per phase. There is not enough copper cross-section or thermal mass. |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 115 °C: R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0055 × 90) = **1.495 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-step, synchronous (complementary) PWM

During any 60° commutation sector, two legs are active:
- The **PWM leg**: current alternates between the high-side (HS) FET for time D·T and the low-side (LS) FET for (1−D)·T, minus dead time.
- The **return leg**: the LS FET is on continuously.

Therefore, independent of duty cycle D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

At I_ph = 80 A:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 × 6400 × 1.495\text{ mΩ} = \mathbf{19.1\ W}$$

### 1.3 Copper (PCB) conduction

Assume an effective phase path of 25 mm long and 8 mm wide, using 4 layers of 2 oz copper (70 µm each), at ρ_Cu(80 °C) = 2.0×10⁻⁸ Ω·m:

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{w\,t_{eff}} = \frac{2.0\times10^{-8} × 0.025}{0.008 × 280\times10^{-6}} = 0.22\text{ mΩ}$$

The current crosses about two such paths (in and out):

$$P_{Cu,ch} \approx 2 × 6400 × 0.22\text{ mΩ} = 2.8\text{ W}$$

### 1.4 Thermal-runaway stability check

Loss rises with temperature through R_ds:

$$\frac{\partial P_{cond}}{\partial T_j} = 2I^2R_{ds25}\,\alpha = 2 × 6400 × 10^{-3} × 0.0055 = 0.070\text{ W/K}$$

The loop is stable when (∂P/∂T)·R_θ,total < 1. This means the total thermal resistance must satisfy R_θ,total < 14 K/W per channel. A board-level steady-state R_θ of about 20 K/W **fails** this test. This is the formal proof that 80 A cannot be continuous on this board. It is stable only as a time-limited transient (Section 4).

## 2. Switching Losses at f_sw = 48 kHz

Hard switching occurs on the PWM leg only:

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V_bus·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·40 ns·48 kHz | **1.94 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V_bus·f_sw = 50 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.06 W |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss·V_bus·f_sw (Q_oss charge/discharge across commutating pair) | 0.07 W |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_f·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k | **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver/R_g, not the FET) | Q_g·V_gs·f_sw·2 = 60 nC·10·48 k·2 | 0.06 W |

$$P_{sw,ch} \approx 2.68\text{ W (FET)} + 0.06\text{ W (driver)}$$

Rule of thumb: halving f_sw to 24 kHz saves about 1.3 W per channel at 80 A. However, it raises the current-ripple frequency into the audible range and increases ripple on low-inductance motors.

### Per-channel and board totals

| Component | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| MOSFET conduction | 19.1 W | 76.4 W |
| Switching + dead time | 2.7 W | 10.7 W |
| PCB copper | 2.8 W | 11.2 W |
| Gate drive + ESC MCU + LDO | ~0.3 W | 1.2 W |
| **Total** | **24.9 W** | **≈ 99.5 W** |

The efficiency check is that the per-channel input power is about V_bus·I_ph·D. At D ≈ 0.8 that gives ≈ 1.6 kW per channel, so efficiency is about 98.5%. The efficiency is good; heat **density** is the actual problem.

### 2.1 DC-link capacitor ripple

$$I_{C,rms} = I_{ph}\sqrt{D(1-D)}$$

This peaks at D = 0.5: 80 × 0.5 = **40 A_rms per channel**. The four channels are uncorrelated rather than phase-interleaved, so the worst case is about √4 × 40 = 80 A_rms combined.
