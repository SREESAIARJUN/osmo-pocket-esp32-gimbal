## 0. Assumptions and Two Design Issues

### 0.1 Architecture correction

ArduPilot does not commutate brushless motors. A "STM32H743 4-in-1 ESC" must be split into two parts:

| Block | MCU | Firmware | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743 (480 MHz, M7) | ArduPilot | Attitude control and bidirectional DShot600 master |
| ESC, ×4 channels | STM32G071 or AT32F421 (one per motor, or one per pair) | AM32 / BLHeli_32 | 6-step commutation, 48 kHz PWM, eRPM telemetry |

These can sit on one board (an AIO stack) or on two boards connected by a 30.5 mm stack connector. The rest of this document uses that split.

### 0.2 Feasibility: 80 A on a "nano" board

A 20×20 mm nano board only fits 3.3×3.3 mm PDFN MOSFETs, with R_DS(on) around 2.5–4 mΩ when hot.

- At 80 A, that gives 2·I²·R ≈ **38–51 W per channel**, or about 200 W across the board.
- This is not thermally survivable, even for a burst of a few seconds.

**Recommendation:** use a 30.5×30.5 mm ESC board with 5×6 mm PDFN MOSFETs. The model below assumes this.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 0.95 × 1.6 = **1.52 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Conduction loss per channel

During any 6-step commutation interval, two current paths are always active:

- The **static leg**: one low-side MOSFET held on.
- The **PWM leg**: the high-side MOSFET for fraction D of each period, and its synchronous low-side for (1−D).

So two MOSFETs always carry the phase current, regardless of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

At 80 A:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.52\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.5\ W}$$

### 1.3 Electro-thermal convergence

Because R_DS(on) rises with temperature, iterate:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_a + P(T_j^{(n)})\cdot R_{\theta JA,eff}$$

Without a heatsink this does not converge at 80 A in steady state, which is why 80 A must be treated as a time-limited burst (Section 3).

### 1.4 Shunt loss

Battery current is I_bat ≈ D·I. With a 0.25 mΩ shunt per channel at D ≈ 1:

$$P_{shunt} = 6400 \times 0.25\,\text{m}\Omega = 1.6\ \text{W}$$
