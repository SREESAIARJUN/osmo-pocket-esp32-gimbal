## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

### 1.2 Current paths in 6-step complementary PWM

At any instant, one high-side and one low-side path carry the phase current I. During PWM off-time, the complementary low-side FET (synchronous rectifier) carries the current instead of the high-side FET. Either way, **two FET channels are always in series with the motor current.**

Per FET, averaged over an electrical cycle:
- **Active high-side FET:** conducts for 1/3 of the cycle, at fraction D within that window.
- **Its complementary low-side FET:** conducts for 1/3 of the cycle, at fraction (1−D).
- **Static low-side FET:** conducts for 1/3 of the cycle, at 100%.

Summed over all six FETs:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

This total is independent of D. The split between FETs, however, is not. The worst single FET is the static low-side FET, which carries:

$$P_{cond,FET,max} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

averaged over the cycle. During its conduction window it dissipates the full I²R, and that window (~ms at typical eRPM) is longer than the die thermal time constant. **For junction temperature, use instantaneous I²R, not the averaged value.**

### 1.3 Numerical results at 80 A, T_j = 110 °C

$$R_{DS}(110) = 0.9\,(1+0.005\cdot 85) = 1.283\ \text{m}\Omega$$

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Instantaneous loss per conducting FET | 80² × 1.283 mΩ = **8.21 W** |
| Per motor (2 conducting) | **16.4 W** |
| 4 motors simultaneous burst | **65.7 W** |

### 1.4 PCB copper loss (often ignored, but it matters on a nano board)

Assume 2 oz (70 µm) copper, phase path 15 mm long × 4 mm wide, 2 parallel layers:

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{wt\,n} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot 0.015}{0.004\cdot 70\times10^{-6}\cdot 2} = 0.46\ \text{m}\Omega$$

Two paths in series per motor gives 2 × 80² × 0.46 mΩ ≈ **5.9 W per motor**. At 80 A, copper is roughly a third of the FET conduction loss. Use 3 oz copper with stacked vias, or bus-bar the B+/B− pads.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Per PWM period, one hard-switched FET makes one turn-on and one turn-off transition. The complementary FET switches with near-zero voltage (body diode already conducting), so it is soft-switched.

### 2.1 Loss terms

| Term | Equation | Value (25.2 V, 80 A, t_r = t_f = 15 ns) |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ V I (t_r + t_f) f_sw | ½·25.2·80·30 ns·48 k = **1.45 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr V f_sw | 40 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.048 W** |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss V f_sw (≈ E_oss·f, linear approx.) | 50 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.060 W** |
| Dead-time diode | V_F I (2 t_dt) f_sw | 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (in driver, not FET) | 2 Q_g V_GS f_sw | 2·50 nC·10·48 k = **0.048 W** |

**Switching subtotal ≈ 2.17 W per motor** (FET-side), plus 0.05 W in the driver.

### 2.2 Sensitivity

- **Ringing.** With 1 nH of loop inductance, di/dt = 80 A / 15 ns gives L·di/dt ≈ 5.3 V of overshoot on top of 25.2 V. That leaves a 40 V FET with only about 9 V of margin once ringing is included.
  - Mandatory: place 0402 100 nF + 1 µF ceramics within 2 mm of every half-bridge.
  - Mandatory: add bulk low-ESR capacitance at the battery pads.
- **Frequency scaling.** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz you save ~1.1 W per motor, but you get more current ripple and audible noise. 48 kHz is a reasonable compromise for the low-inductance motors typical here.
- **Dead-time.** Dead-time loss is the second largest switching term. Reducing dead-time from 100 ns to 50 ns saves 0.3 W per motor, if the gate driver's propagation matching allows it.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Total burst power

| Source | Per motor | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 16.4 W | 65.7 W |
| Switching | 2.2 W | 8.7 W |
| PCB copper | 5.9 W | 23.6 W |
| **Total** | **24.5 W** | **≈ 98 W** |

### 3.2 Electro-thermal fixed point (thermal-runaway check)

Let P₀ = I²R_{DS,25} and R_θ = junction-to-reference thermal resistance. From

$$T_j = T_{ref} + R_\theta P_0[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

the solution is:

$$T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + R_\theta P_0(1-25\alpha)}{1-\alpha R_\theta P_0}$$

**Stability requires α R_θ P₀ < 1.**

For the hottest FET (P₀ = 80² × 0.9 mΩ = 5.76 W), the runaway threshold is R_θ < 1/(0.005 × 5.76) = **34.7 K/W**. This is safe at die level. The board-level limit is the binding one (§3.3).

### 3.3 Junction rise above the board

$$\Delta T_{jc} = 8.21\ \text{W} \times 1.2\ \text{K/W} \approx 10\ \text{K}$$

This is small. **The board is the bottleneck, not the die.**

### 3.4 Board lumped transient (single-node capacitance model)

A 20×20 or 30×30 mm 8-layer 2 oz board with components has a mass of about 6–8 g, with c_p ≈ 0.5 J/(g·K). That gives **C_th ≈ 3.5 J/K**.

With prop-wash convection on a 30×30 board, R_θ,board-amb ≈ 6 K/W, so the time constant is τ = R·C ≈ 21 s.

$$T_{board}(t) = T_a + P R_\theta\,(1-e^{-t/\tau})$$

Steady-state at 98 W would be 588 K above ambient, which is obviously impossible. **80 A is burst-only.** The initial slope is

$$\frac{dT}{dt} \approx \frac{P}{C} = \frac{98}{3.5} \approx 28\ \text{K/s}$$
