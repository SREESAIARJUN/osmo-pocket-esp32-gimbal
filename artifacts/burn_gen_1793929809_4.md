# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Thermal Model and Bidirectional DShot600 Allocation

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

I don't have Job #41's BOM, stackup, or enclosure data, so this model is fully parametric. Every number below comes from the stated baseline, and you can substitute your datasheet values directly.

**Architectural correction.** In this class of board, the STM32H743 does not generate the 48 kHz power-stage PWM. ## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step complementary PWM

During any 60° commutation sector, current flows through two phase legs:

- **Active (PWM) leg:**
  - The high-side FET conducts for duty fraction D.
  - The low-side FET conducts for (1 − D) as the synchronous rectifier.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET conducts 100% of the sector.

So, at any instant, the current passes through exactly two R_ds(on) in series:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This is independent of D, which is a useful property of synchronous rectification. Phase-current ripple adds a factor $(1 + \tfrac{1}{12}(\Delta I_{pp}/I)^2)$. That factor is under 1% for typical motor inductance at 48 kHz, so I neglect it.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence and thermal runaway

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha(T_j - 25)]$$

The values at the baseline current:

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per ESC (80 A) | ×4 ESCs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.30 mΩ | 16.6 W | 66.6 W |
| 100 °C | 1.89 mΩ | 24.2 W | 96.6 W |
| 125 °C | 2.08 mΩ | 26.6 W | 106.5 W |

**Electro-thermal runaway criterion.** With an effective thermal resistance R_θ from junction to the sink, the feedback loop gain is:

$$G = 2 I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_\theta$$

The system is stable in steady state only if G < 1:

$$I_{max,SS} < \sqrt{\frac{1}{2 R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_\theta}}$$

For R_θ = 10 K/W (per-ESC share of a nano board in airflow), I_max,SS ≈ 80 A. That puts the design exactly on the edge. **80 A must therefore be treated strictly as a time-limited burst rating, not a continuous one.**

### 1.3 Copper (PCB) conduction

Copper loss is

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 \sum R_{trace}$$

with sheet resistance $\rho_{Cu}/t = 0.25\ \text{m}\Omega/\square$ for 2 oz copper at 25 °C, tempco 0.0039 /K.

A realistic path is battery pad → bus plane → FET → phase pad, about 4 squares with two parallel planes, giving roughly 0.5 mΩ. That yields:

- 80² × 0.5 mΩ ≈ **3.2 W per ESC**
- **12.8 W total**

This term is usually omitted, and it should not be.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the active leg switches. Per ESC, per switching period:

| Term | Expression | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | ½·25.2·80·30 ns·48 kHz = **1.45 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 40 nC·25.2·48 kHz = **0.048 W** |
| C_oss charge | ½·C_oss·V²·f_sw (×2 devices in leg) | **0.027 W** |
| Dead-time body diode | 2·t_dt·V_f·I·f_sw | 2·50 ns·0.8·80·48 kHz = **0.31 W** |
| Gate drive | Q_g·V_gs·f_sw (×2 FETs) | **0.038 W** (dissipated mostly in the driver) |
| **Total P_sw per ESC** | | **≈ 1.87 W** |

Two design notes follow from these terms:

- **Dead time and driver strength are the levers.** Overlap loss scales linearly with t_r + t_f. A 0.5 A driver (t_r ≈ 40 ns) raises the overlap term to about 3.9 W per ESC.
- **f_sw sensitivity.** All terms scale linearly with f_sw. Moving from 48 to 24 kHz saves about 0.9 W per ESC but adds current ripple and audible noise.

## 3. Total Burst Loss Budget (4 × 80 A, T_j ≈ 125 °C)

| Source | Per ESC | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| MOSFET conduction | 26.6 W | 106.5 W |
| MOSFET switching | 1.9 W | 7.5 W |
| PCB copper (hot) | 4.0 W | 16.0 W |
| Gate driver + ESC MCU + LDO | 0.3 W | 1.2 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 32.8 W** | **≈ 131 W** |

Electrical efficiency at this point: P_out ≈ 4 × 25.2 × 80 × D. At D ≈ 0.9 that is ≈ 7.3 kW, so η ≈ 98.2%. The efficiency is fine. The problem is power density: 131 W into roughly 9 cm² of board.
