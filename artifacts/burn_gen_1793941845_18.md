## 0. Scope and Engineering Caveats

**Architecture assumption.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and does not commutate motors. Each of the four ESC channels uses its own commutation MCU (AT32F421 running AM32 shown here). The ESC MCUs generate the 48 kHz PWM, and the H743 commands them over bi-directional DShot600. This is how production AIO and 4-in-1 boards are built: the H743 cannot also run 12 phase legs with sub-µs commutation timing alongside the ArduPilot scheduler.

**Parameter basis.** All MOSFET parameters below are *representative values for a 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm PDFN part*, not a specific datasheet. Before release, re-run every equation with the selected part's datasheet values, especially R_DS(on)(T_j), Q_gd, Q_rr and R_θJC.

**Headline result: 80 A per motor on a nano board is a burst rating, not a continuous one.**

| Condition | Result |
|---|---|
| 4 × 80 A phase-current burst | Total loss ≈ 160 W |
| Adiabatic board heating rate | ≈ 40 K/s |
| Usable burst duration | Roughly 1 s |
| Sustainable continuous current with propwash cooling | Roughly 20–25 A per motor |

The math in Section 3 supports these numbers. Marketing the board as "80 A" without a duration qualifier would be misleading.

**Topology.** Six-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. At any instant, the current path runs through exactly two MOSFETs: one high side (HS) and one low side (LS), either directly or via the synchronous-rectifying complement.

## 2. Loss Model per Motor

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

Temperature-dependent on-resistance:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)] = 2.0\,[1 + 0.005 \times 100] = 3.0\ \text{m}\Omega \quad (T_j = 125\,^\circ\text{C})$$

There are always two conducting devices in series, so per motor:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j) = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.003 = \mathbf{38.4\ W}$$

**Distribution among the six FETs.** In each 60° sector, the PWM'd leg splits its loss between HS (fraction D) and LS (fraction 1 − D). The other active leg conducts continuously. Averaged over a full electrical cycle, each leg is active for 2/3 of the time. Per-FET average dissipation is therefore about 38.4 / 6 ≈ **6.4 W**. The peak instantaneous dissipation in the continuously-on device is I²R = **19.2 W**.

**Self-consistency check.** Because R_DS(on) rises with temperature, solve iteratively:

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + R_{\theta JC}\, P_{FET}(T_j)$$

With T_pcb = 100 °C and 6.4 W per FET, T_j ≈ 110 °C. This is below the 125 °C assumption, so the estimate is conservative.

**Other copper losses.**

- **Shunt:** 0.25 mΩ × 80² ≈ 1.6 W (per-motor or common shunt, depending on topology).
- **PCB phase traces:** about 0.3 mΩ, adding roughly 1.9 W.

### 2.2 Switching Losses (Hard-Switched Leg)

At any time, only one leg per motor is PWM-switching.

**Gate currents:**

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{pl}}{R_{g,ext} + R_{g,int}} = \frac{6.5}{5.7} = 1.14\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{5.7} = 0.61\ \text{A}$$

**Transition times:**

$$t_{on} = \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{g,on}} = 8.8\ \text{ns}, \qquad t_{off} = \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{g,off}} = 16.4\ \text{ns}$$

**V–I overlap loss:**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_{on} + t_{off}) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 25.2\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{1.22\ W}$$

**Secondary terms:**

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| C_oss charge | ½ C_oss V² f | 0.009 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr V f | 0.024 W |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_SD I (2 t_dead) f = 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k | **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (dissipated mostly in driver and R_g) | 2 Q_g V_drv f | 0.024 W |

**Total switching loss per motor:**

$$P_{sw} \approx \mathbf{1.9\ W}$$

- **Dead time** is the second-largest switching term. Trimming t_dead from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per motor. Verify shoot-through margin on the scope at T_j,max before doing so.
