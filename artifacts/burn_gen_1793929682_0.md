> ⚠️ **Architectural flag:** In the drone market, a "nano" 4-in-1 normally means a 20×20 mm board. I modelled 30.5×30.5 mm because, as Section 1.5 shows, even that size cannot sustain 80 A beyond a few seconds. A 20×20 board has roughly 45 % of the thermal mass, so its burst window scales down accordingly. Treat 80 A as a burst rating with a defined duration, never as a continuous rating.

> **Architectural note:** The STM32H743 is the flight controller. Each ESC channel runs its own MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 class) with AM32 or BLHeli_32 firmware. The H743 generates DShot600 and decodes the bi-directional eRPM telemetry.

## 1. Electrothermal Model of the ESC Power Stage

### 1.1 Conduction Loss

**Derivation.** In 6-step complementary PWM, current at any instant flows through exactly two phase legs:

- **Leg A** (PWM leg): the high-side switch conducts for D·T_sw. The low-side switch carries freewheeling current, synchronously rectified, for (1−D)·T_sw.
- **Leg B** (return leg): the low-side switch is held fully ON.

The current therefore always passes through exactly two channel resistances in series, whatever the duty cycle. Instantaneous bridge conduction loss is:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

with the temperature dependence:

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx \frac{k_T - 1}{100} = 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

**Evaluation at Tj = 125 °C.** R_DS(on) = 1.2 mΩ × 1.5 = 1.8 mΩ, so:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.8\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{23.04\ W\ per\ channel}$$

**PCB copper loss (non-negligible at 80 A).** For one 2 oz layer:

$$R_{\square} = \frac{\rho_{Cu}}{t} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}}{70\times10^{-6}} = 0.246\ \text{m}\Omega/\square$
